# Round 1 publisher and printed originals, November 24, 2026

This review checked 59 exact claim and publication pairs from 28 publications. The [experiment receipts](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/followup/priority-five-legacy-originals-nov24/README.md) identify each frozen preparation and endpoint. Forty-nine checks closed at complete-original or authenticated original-section depth. Ten require additional original access.

The 1999 mortality article uses SAGE as the name of a geriatric database. The 2001 movement-sign article and 2005 pain review were published by Sage. These mentions do not identify use of the culinary herb. The occupational pesticide study reports parkinsonism as the examination outcome. It does not isolate idiopathic Parkinson disease. Midcentury cardiac publications were verified from their printed pages. Their pain endpoints concern cardiac presentations, and one cites Parkinson as an author's surname.

The [frontotemporal dementia review](../2005-2009/Source-Text/2007-PMID18166608-Frontotemporal-Dementia-Original-Access.txt) resolves 14 checks. It names olive oil within a Mediterranean dietary pattern discussed for dementia prevention. Its later urinary, behavioral and movement sections do not give an olive-oil intervention outcome. The older access account remains in the same local source record.

The [enteroendocrine review](../2020-2024/Source-Text/2023-PMID38037432-Enteroendocrine-Visceral-Pain.txt) links isolated cinnamaldehyde with a serotonin-release mechanism. Its constipation drug comparisons and prolonged cell-activation findings on anxiety are separate. The [ground cinnamon rat experiment](../2020-2024/Source-Text/2023-Ness-Ground-Cinnamon-and-Bladder-Nociception.txt) compared an oral culinary powder with saline and measured a distension-evoked muscle reflex. The higher dose gave opposite signs with and without bladder inflammation. The oral animal findings and local cinnamaldehyde infusion are one study family. The Parkinson pain score remains tentative and a culinary Parkinson comparison is still needed.
