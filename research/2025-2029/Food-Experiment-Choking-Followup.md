# Food experiment choking evidence

## Scalloped potato obstruction in an Oklahoma facility

An August 8, 2025 federal administrative decision details a February 17, 2020 incident in an Oklahoma nursing facility. Staff removed pieces of scalloped potato after a resident choked during a meal. An emergency department diagnosed aspiration pneumonia and transferred the resident to intensive care. The resident returned to the facility after five days. The decision records Down syndrome and vascular and unspecified dementia, without a Parkinson’s diagnosis. The regular menu listed scalloped potatoes and the adjudicator found the facility failed to supply the ordered mechanically softened food. The record does not establish the potatoes’ exact cooking method. [Primary decision CR6744, pp. 37–42](https://www.hhs.gov/about/agencies/dab/decisions/alj-decisions/2025/alj-cr6744/index.html)

## Potato and meat obstruction in an English restaurant

An English coroner’s September 2025 report records a fatal choking incident during a June 11, 2025 restaurant meal. Paramedics removed potato and meat from the airway using a laryngoscope and forceps. The finding records previous swallowing difficulties and a past medical history including schizophrenia and type 2 diabetes. It identifies no Parkinson’s diagnosis and gives no potato preparation or separate attribution of the obstruction to either food. [Primary coronial report 2025-0460, §§3–4](https://www.judiciary.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Stuart-Gilchrist--Prevention-of-future-deaths-report-2025-0460_Published.pdf)

## Mashed potato on a menu during a South Dakota meat obstruction

A South Dakota Department of Health report describes fatal choking during a July 27, 2025 lunch. The general menu included cheesy mashed potatoes and sliced pork with gravy. Paramedics removed meat from the epiglottic opening, and the report identifies the cause of death as foreign-body asphyxia and cardiopulmonary arrest. The clinical record describes dysphagia and stroke, without documenting Parkinson’s. It supplies no evidence of potato ingestion or a potato-related response. The survey ended August 1 and the public form is marked August 13, 2025. [Primary state CMS-2567, CCN 435042, F0678, pp. 2–5](https://doh.sd.gov/media/baydqxcy/08-01-25_aberdeen_avera-mother-joseph-manor-retirement-community_c.pdf#page=3)

## FDA review of manufacturer choking reports

The March 4, 2026 FDA De Novo decision for LifeVac reviewed two sets of voluntary reports from the manufacturer’s internal database. For cases meeting the indication and eligibility criteria, 735 earlier reports and 367 later reports entered the safety analyses, and 681 and 319 respectively had known outcomes for effectiveness analyses. Eligibility included complete airway obstruction by food or another object. The FDA identifies selection and reporting bias. The public review provides aggregate outcomes and does not identify obstructing foods by diagnosis or report potato preparation. It cannot link the Parkinson’s and potato counts in the 39-report dysphagia series, and overlap with that earlier publication is unverified. The review also describes future postmarket collection. ## Vanilla pudding and dry biscuit in endoscopic swallowing tests

Mozzanica and colleagues’ November 3, 2025 report describes 33 people with Parkinson’s. Endoscopy used room-temperature blue-dyed water, Crème Line vanilla pudding, and half of an eight-gram dry biscuit. The separate mastication test used a GranPavesi cracker. Figure 2 groups penetration and aspiration together for 20 of 28 people tested with water (71.4%), 15 of 33 with pudding (45.5%), and six of 27 with biscuit (22.2%). The bolus groups had different denominators, and the protocol omitted some consistencies. These descriptive rates combine penetration and aspiration and cannot isolate an ingredient effect. In the tongue-pressure regressions, only residue after water swallows showed an association with maximal pressure among the endoscopic outcomes. The article reports 33 enrolled and uses 34 as the denominator in its malnutrition summary. [Primary report, methods, Figure 2 and Tables 3–4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00455-025-10902-7) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC13457437.txt)

## Levodopa regimen and repeated tongue pressure

Hayasaka and colleagues’ 2026 MDS abstract `1296` reports an observational comparison of ten participants on intermittent levodopa and four receiving continuous dopaminergic delivery. Continuous treatment included two patients receiving intestinal gel and two receiving subcutaneous infusion. The protocol recorded maximal tongue-pressure efforts 20 times at 10-second intervals. Pressure waveform area declined with repetition in the continuous group (slope −0.837, p = .0005) and was stable in the intermittent group (slope −0.043, p = .71). The group-by-repetition interaction was significant for waveform area (p = .0027), with no significant interaction for peak pressure. The 10 seconds refers to spacing between measurement efforts. The abstract gives no meal schedule, food bolus, instrumental swallowing or speech outcome. Searches by title and author found no matching full article. [MDS abstract 1296](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/levodopa-dosing-regimen-influences-repetitive-tongue-force-maintenance-in-parkinsons-disease/) · [Official schedule, p. 10](https://www.mdscongress.org/Abstract-Schedule.pdf#page=10) · [Earlier Urayasu reports](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Choking-Followup.md#repetitive-tongue-pressure-studies-in-urayasu)

## Oropharyngeal freezing in progressive supranuclear palsy

Wang and colleagues’ MDS abstract `139`, scheduled for October 4, 2026, reports penetration or aspiration in 41 of 62 people with progressive supranuclear palsy and oropharyngeal freezing in six. Longer disease duration and higher Hoehn and Yahr stage were associated with freezing in Table 2 (p = .013 and .012). Of eight people who completed functional near-infrared spectroscopy, one had oropharyngeal freezing. ## Age subgroup of the Hiroshima water bolus cohort

Nakamori and colleagues published an age subgroup of `jRCTs062220013` on June 19, 2026. MDS abstract `747` was scheduled for October 7 in Seoul and reports the same 25 participants. The full paper specifies 3 mL water with 30% barium in the videofluoroscopic test and nebulized 1% citric acid inhaled through the mouth in a separate cough test. At baseline, penetration or aspiration occurred in three of nine people aged 75 or older and seven of 16 younger people (p = .610). Mean peak expiratory flow was 145.1 versus 226.1 L/min (p = .008). The full sample’s week-eight rates of penetration or aspiration were 10 of 25 at baseline and nine of 25 after cervical stimulation (p = .771). The age groups did not differ significantly in post-stimulation change. These publications reuse the 2023–2024 cohort and provide no dietary ingredient comparison. ## Water swallow screening in northern Tanzania

Haggie and colleagues’ 2026 MDS abstract `1273`, scheduled for October 6, reports home or near-home assessments of 28 people with Parkinson’s in Tanzania’s Hai district. The clinical screen used 150 mL water and recorded swallow number, time, coughing and wet voice. Five of ten participants with cough peak flow below 160 L/min showed a clinical sign of possible aspiration, versus zero of 17 with higher cough peak flow (Fisher p = .003). Higher motor UPDRS scores correlated with longer water swallow time (r = .46, p = .014). The study used clinical signs and reports no instrumental confirmation of aspiration or comparison across foods. Searches by title and investigators did not locate a full paper for the pilot. [MDS abstract 1273](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/pilot-feasibility-study-of-home-based-assessment-of-aspiration-risk-among-people-with-parkinsons-disease-in-rural-northern-tanzania/) · [Official schedule, p. 114](https://www.mdscongress.org/Abstract-Schedule.pdf#page=114)

Investigators measured maximal inspiratory and expiratory pressure with a handheld respiratory pressure meter and cough peak flow through a face mask and peak-flow meter. The report omits the mask and flow-meter models. Five of 28 could not complete pressure testing. One participant is absent from the reported cough-flow groups. The 2023 TraPCAf umbrella protocol documents Hai recruitment and clinical phenotyping, with separate enrollment criteria for its Mucuna trial. The protocol and registry do not describe this water-swallow substudy or establish overlap with the Mucuna trial. The abstract cites an ERS respiratory muscle-testing statement for its 160 L/min cutoff. Earlier Parkinson studies used related measurements with different methods. Miller and colleagues tested 150 mL water off medication in Newcastle, Pflug and colleagues checked timed 90 mL water against endoscopy, and Lee and colleagues derived a cutoff of 153 L/min against videofluoroscopic aspiration in a separate Korean sample. [TraPCAf protocol, RQ4 and RQ6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12883-023-03414-0) · [ERS statement](https://doi.org/10.1183/13993003.01214-2018) · [Miller primary study](../2005-2009/Nonmotor-and-Daily-Life.md#2008--timed-swallowing-revealed-problems-that-self-report-missed) · [Pflug primary study](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Choking-Followup.md#timed-water-swallowing-against-endoscopy-in-parkinsons-disease) · [Lee primary study](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Choking-Followup.md#yogurt-rice-porridge-and-boiled-rice-in-korean-cough-screening)

## Yogurt and wafer cookie in Brazilian videofluoroscopy

Gonçalves and colleagues’ September 28, 2026 paper analyzed existing videofluoroscopic exams from 16 patients with Parkinson’s and 16 controls. The protocol included water with contrast, thickened liquid, petit-suisse yogurt as a spoon-fed paste, and a contrast-soaked wafer cookie. Laryngeal displacement was lower in the Parkinson’s group with each analyzed consistency. Mean displacement during yogurt swallowing was 2.00 cm in 15 Parkinson’s participants and 2.80 cm in 16 controls (p = .011). For wafer cookie the values were 2.18 cm in 14 Parkinson’s participants and 2.98 cm in 16 controls (p = .008). The p-values are unadjusted and both findings remained significant after false discovery rate adjustment. The comparisons are between diagnostic groups for each bolus. Exams omitted some consistencies for safety or lacked usable images. The paper does not estimate the effect of switching foods within Parkinson’s or report baked potato or steamed asparagus. [Primary article, Procedures and Table 5, PMID 42803492](https://turkarchotolaryngol.net/article/10.4274/tao.2026.2026-5-6)

## Shortbread and pudding in respiratory swallowing tests

Rangwala and colleagues’ May 21, 2025 report describes videofluoroscopy in 31 people with Parkinson’s and dysphagia. Supplementary Table 4 records a Penetration–Aspiration Scale score of at least 3 in 6.5% of 31 tests using half a Lorna Doone shortbread cookie coated with pudding barium, compared with 22.6% of 31 thin-barium teaspoon tests. These are descriptive rates across bolus tasks that varied in volume and consistency. Supplementary Table 2 reports mean patient ratings of swallowing difficulty of 22.9 for hard foods and 23.0 for dry foods, compared with 7.6 for soft foods on separate 100-point items. The ratings refer to broad textures and provide no potato-specific outcome. [Primary May report, methods and results, PMID 40396298](https://doi.org/10.1002/mdc3.70129) · [Original Supplementary Tables 2 and 4, DOCX in Europe PMC ZIP](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12528960/supplementaryFiles)

The December 2, 2025 respiratory-phase analysis reports the same sample size, recruitment source, ethics approval, baseline demographics and prior swallowing history. Across 274 analyzed swallows, inspiration preceded one of 31 cookie swallows and 13 of 31 trials in each of the thin-liquid cup sip and sequential-swallow conditions. Across the analyzed swallowing trials, inspiration before swallowing was associated with greater odds of a Penetration–Aspiration Scale score of at least 3 (OR 3.77, 95% CI 1.27–11.18). The latter estimate pools boluses. The matching sample and protocol indicate a second analysis of the same 31 participants. [Primary December report, swallowing protocol and Tables 3–4, PMID 41329938](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12806011/)

The same research group’s October 8, 2026 report used respiratory monitoring in 20 people with Parkinson’s. Named materials included a half shortbread cookie and 5 mL of packaged pudding. Their nectar- and honey-thick liquids were modified-cornstarch products, with the terms referring to consistency. Cued bolus holding increased the odds of an exhale–swallow–exhale pattern for a 5 mL thin liquid, a thin cup sip and pudding. The pudding estimate lost statistical significance when the authors excluded one participant with a low cognitive screening score (p = .055). Shortbread showed no significant change in respiratory pause duration after the cue (p = .670). The report measured respiratory coordination without imaging airway invasion. Both reports cite the same Northwestern ethics approval (`STU00212981`), but participant-level overlap is unverified. ## Applesauce and biscuit in a respiratory training study

Nachalon and colleagues published a four-week elevation training mask study on February 25, 2025. Eleven of 13 enrolled men with idiopathic Parkinson’s and dysphagia completed the protocol. Endoscopic testing before and after training used colored water (IDDSI level 0), two 5 mL spoonfuls of applesauce (level 4), and two biscuit bites (level 7). Median Yale vallecular residue scores fell from 3 to 2 for applesauce (p = 0.025) and biscuit (p = 0.028). The changes in water residue and penetration–aspiration scores for all three foods did not reach statistical significance. ## Carbonated and noncarbonated water in Parkinson swallowing tests

Glinzer and colleagues’ September 11, 2026 DYSparkLE article reports within-person endoscopic tests in 83 people with Parkinson’s. The team compared unflavored carbonated mineral water (Fürst Bismarck Classic) with unflavored noncarbonated liquid at 5 and 90 mL. In the 80 paired 90 mL observations, the depth of airway invasion improved in 27, worsened in six and was unchanged in 47 with the carbonated liquid. The adjusted log-odds coefficient was −1.62 (p < 0.001). At 5 mL the corresponding counts were 14 improved, six worsened and 62 unchanged. The liquids differed in coloring, the noncarbonated liquid was given first and raters knew which was which. These were immediate instrumental swallowing outcomes for the named liquid contrast. The study does not assess baked potato or another frozen food exposure. ## Swallowing and taste tests in the UNIFESP saliva cohort
