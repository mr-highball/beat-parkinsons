# Food experiment evidence for soybean and rice bran oil

## Rice bran extract and depression scores

Huh and colleagues first published the NCT05180136 trial on May 21, 2025. Adults with mild to moderate depressive symptoms received rice bran extract or placebo for eight weeks. The authors report a lower PHQ-9 score at eight weeks in the per-protocol analysis (p = .026) with several other questionnaire and biomarker comparisons inconclusive. Apathy in Parkinson was not an endpoint, and the assigned product was rice bran extract. [Primary clinical report and tables](https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165%2825%2900276-X/fulltext) · [Dated registration](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Soybean-Rice-Bran-Oils.md#rice-bran-extract-depression-trial-registration)

## Fermented rice bran and bowel diary registration

UMIN000060916 was publicly disclosed March 13, 2026. It compares a fermented rice bran food with a matched placebo in adults with mild constipation and no chronic conditions, with target enrollment 100. Bowel movement counts are primary. Stool consistency, relief, straining and salivary assays are secondary. The record lists recruitment closed and no published results. It supplies a related ingredient and measured outcome, with a different preparation and population from the claim. [Official registration](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000069232) · [Local registry snapshot](Source-Text/Trials/UMIN000060916.txt)

## Soybean oil in a cinnamon nanosuspension

Lin and colleagues’ February 2, 2026 rat report gives 1% soybean oil in the active cinnamon leaf nanosuspension, along with lecithin and Tween 80. Relative to rotenone controls, catalepsy was reduced in the nanosuspension, hydrosol and powder groups. The study did not separately assign soybean oil. The 2025 amyloid-beta study uses a related emulsion with soybean oil and measures cognition and dopamine. Neither is a food-grade soybean oil clinical comparison.
