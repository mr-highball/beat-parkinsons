# Food experiment evidence for green beans, pinto beans, tempeh, and garlic

## Green bean menu option and Parkinson motor scores

Bedarf and colleagues’ 2025 study offered green beans among several recommended prebiotic foods and used apples and lactulose as other intervention components. Ten participants with Parkinson and ten household controls completed the four-week intervention. The aggregate motor change in Parkinson was inconclusive. The supplement confirms that the adherence score uses apples and a group-level fiber comparison, with no green bean portion, cooking form or rigidity, facial expression, fall, fatigue or executive-function estimate. The data-availability statement provides public metagenomic data and says other data are available upon request. ## Garlic-derived allicin and mouse motor assays

The August 4, 2025 report compared injected allicin with saline in 6-OHDA-lesioned mice. It measured pole climbing, rotation behavior and dopaminergic markers. Its results concern a single garlic-derived compound administered by injection. No cooked garlic, urination diary or bladder urgency measure was included. [Primary article text](Source-Text/PMC12348316.txt#L160)

## Cooked tempeh and cognition study endpoints

A May 2025 study assigned older participants with mild cognitive impairment mixed tempeh or soy tempeh, with steaming or stir-frying recommended. It analyzed 57 completers and reported within-group changes in cognitive tests. It did not measure walking, stride or Parkinson symptoms. The earlier 2020 soy-tempeh cognitive study also did not supply gait outcomes. [2025 primary article text](Source-Text/PMC12104678.txt) · [2020 primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33080604/)
