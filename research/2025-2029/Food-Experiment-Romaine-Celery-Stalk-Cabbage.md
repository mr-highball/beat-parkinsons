# Food experiment evidence for romaine lettuce, celery stalk, cabbage and Brussels sprouts

## 2025 Red cabbage microgreens and blood pressure

Lee and colleagues compared red cabbage microgreens and beet microgreens for two weeks each in healthy middle-aged and older adults. Table 6 gives red cabbage brachial systolic pressure of 118 mmHg both before and after the intervention. Brachial and central hemodynamic contrasts were not significant. Red cabbage microgreens differ from mature raw cabbage, and no orthostatic symptoms were assessed. [Primary report, Table 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11820112/) · [Local article](Source-Text/PMC11820112.txt)

## 2025 Sauerkraut and microbiome outcomes

Schropp and colleagues compared fresh and pasteurized sauerkraut for four weeks each. Of 87 healthy adults enrolled, 84 completed all parts. Global gut microbial diversity and stool frequency did not materially change, and several species and serum short-chain fatty acids differed by preparation. The 2026 companion paper uses the same registration and reports a small within-period reduction in systolic pressure after both preparations. Neither paper reports drooling. The 2025 paper names the supplier and product variants without specifying a cabbage cultivar. [2025 primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11817299/) · [Local article](Source-Text/PMC11817299.txt) · [2026 companion report](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41430-026-01776-5)

## 2026 Registered bioactivated glucoraphanin and Parkinson symptoms

NCT07360977 includes a Parkinson group alongside other neurologic groups and tracks UPDRS and nonmotor measures. The registration has no posted results. The earlier Parkinson sulforaphane registration NCT05084365 also has no posted results. Neither intervention is steamed Brussels sprouts. The records are already cataloged locally. [2026 registry and local assessment](Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#registered-bioactivated-glucoraphanin-study) · [2021 registry and local assessment](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#sulforaphane-registration)

## 2026 Finely shredded sauerkraut in older adults with dysphagia

Mayama and colleagues compared nonfermented pickles and finely shredded fermented sauerkraut in 13 older adults with dysphagia risk. The crossover found no significant change in global microbiome diversity and reported different taxon-level shifts with the two products. The primary report addresses feasibility and microbiome outcomes. The accessible source gives no Parkinson subgroup, drooling score or cabbage cultivar. It cites UMIN000046319, whose public description encompasses multiple food and swallowing interventions. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41539433/) · [Earlier registration](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Romaine-Celery-Stalk-Cabbage.md#2022-registry-for-fermented-food-and-dysphagia-research)

## 2026 Fermented red cabbage blend and depression measures

Bester and colleagues randomized healthy adults among fermented foods and a control in a preprint first posted January 7, 2026. The red cabbage product also contained beetroot and caraway. The cabbage group had lower within-group stress and total DASS-21 scores. The study reports no between-group depression difference (p = .607) and no POMS depression effect. It provides mood evidence for a fermented blend in healthy adults and leaves cooked red cabbage in Parkinson's unresolved. ISRCTN75418579 first registered February 4, 2026, after the preprint and the registry's March 2020 completion date. It lists the product ingredients and outcomes. The author's deposited dataset also identifies the same study. [Primary preprint, Section 3.4 and Figure 7](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202601.0406) · [Trial registry](https://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN75418579) · [Author dataset](https://reshare.ukdataservice.ac.uk/858349/)
