# Tea aromas, diagnostic tests and odor memory

## Open Essence odor cards in Japanese community samples

The first Nakashima report was available online April 12, 2025. Sixty-six older and 66 younger participants completed 12 odor cards. The older group had a median Open Essence score of 7.0 versus 10.0 (p=.001) despite reporting higher odor awareness. Recognition of the mandarin and rose cards was 24/66 versus 50/66 and 28/66 versus 59/66, respectively (p=.001 for each). The article discusses these cards because they share aroma constituents with tea. The study administered neither green tea nor a green-tea scent intervention. [Original Methods and Tables 1–3](https://doi.org/10.4091/iken.2025.001)

A second study was available online July 18, 2025. It classified 115 older day-service users into normal cognition (46), mild cognitive impairment (33) and cognitive decline (36) groups. Those with cognitive decline had lower scores on the same card test. The article examines the mandarin and rose items in particular. Clinical grouping and card identification do not test how tea consumption affects smell. [Original study](https://doi.org/10.4091/iken.2025.004)

## Mind Your Nose odor-memory trial

The July 9, 2025 primary report randomized 53 older adults with subjective cognitive decline to odor-memory training (36) or visual-memory training (17). Participants smelled tea sachets as cues in memory tasks. The report does not identify the tea varieties. It does not administer a tea beverage. The post-training group-by-time estimate for the Sniffin' Sticks TDI composite was 0.27 points (SE 1.69, p=.871). At follow-up, the abstract and results prose give −1.27, while Table 3 gives −1.78 (SE 1.69, p=.296). Keep both estimates pending clarification. The study was registered as ACTRN12622000116729 on January 24, 2022.
