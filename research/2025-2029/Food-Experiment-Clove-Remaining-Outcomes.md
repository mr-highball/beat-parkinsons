# Ground clove and Parkinson symptom evidence

## Registered clove oil lozenge comparison after intubation

The official entry first posted and last updated February 6, 2025. A recheck on November 15, 2026 returned Unknown status and no posted results. The protocol compares clove and menthol lozenges with a control after intubation and lists pain VAS and sore-throat ratings. Participants were instructed to let the lozenges dissolve in the mouth. No clove-oil dose or acoustic voice measure was listed. [ClinicalTrials.gov primary registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06811051).


## Registered mixed granola containing cloves and sleep quality

The protocol first posted May 20, 2026 and lists cloves among ingredients of Cinnamon Granola Bark. The randomized comparison changes snacks as whole products. Sleep quality is secondary and no specific daytime sleepiness instrument was identified. Clove preparation, isolated effect and Parkinson status are unestablished. The record was Recruiting without posted results. ## Registered odor training with a cognitive test

First posted February 12, 2025. The registered program targets older adults with idiopathic olfactory dysfunction and subjective cognitive decline. The protocol compares modified and conventional olfactory training and a control. The February registry snapshot still says not yet recruiting and shows no posted results. The August journal protocol describes March 2025 recruitment and planned Trail Making assessment. It uses clove odor with other scents and no culinary ingestion, separate clove arm or Parkinson outcome. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06821828) · [Local text](Source-Text/2025-NCT06821828-Olfactory-Training-Cognition.txt)

## Clove odor and Trail Making in a Beijing training protocol

Published August 13, 2025. The NCT06821828 publication plans allocation of 114 older adults to modified training, conventional training or control. Four odors include clove; conventional training uses eugenol for that odor. Trial methods plan cognition, olfactory testing and imaging, and identify Trail Making for executive function. The article gives procedures without results. A wording error labels the control description as conventional training, while the design and flow separate the two. No ground clove, Parkinson cohort or isolated scent comparison was assigned. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12352155/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2025-Lin-Clove-Odor-Trail-Making-Protocol.txt)

## Clove in a registered odor kit and cognitive follow up

First posted October 27, 2025. The BOOST trial registers conventional olfactory training with clove odor among its stimuli and lists Trail Making Test B among cognition measures. It targets people with HIV. The June 11, 2026 update lists recruiting and no posted results. Clove odor and the other components are not separated into ingredient-specific arms. The registry does not test ground clove ingestion or Parkinson executive impairment. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07221123) · [Local text](Source-Text/2025-NCT07221123-Clove-Odor-Executive-Proxy.txt)

## Clove odor in a multiodor diabetes cognition trial

First online September 5, 2025. Chao and colleagues randomized 60 adults with type 2 diabetes and mild cognitive impairment to six-odor training or routine care. Clove was one of six stimuli. The original abstract reports mean MoCA change +1.9 versus +0.4 after 16 weeks (p=.001) and describes executive-domain improvement. The abstract gives no executive-domain effect estimate and the publisher full text and supplemental file returned access errors. There was no separated clove arm, culinary ingestion or Parkinson population. NCT06327633 is a probable match based on the same center, sample size, randomization, six odors, training schedule and MoCA outcome. An explicit publication identifier and the executive-domain table remain open. The registry has no posted results, and its two references labeled as results were published before the trial started.
