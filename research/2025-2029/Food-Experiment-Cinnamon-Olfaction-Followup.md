# Cinnamon and olfactory testing, 2025–2029

## Cinnamon identification in Parkinson and other hyposmia

Mitchell and colleagues published on April 4, 2025. Supplementary Table 1 gives correct cinnamon UPSIT identification rates of 48% in Parkinson hyposmia and 29% in non-Parkinson hyposmia, with 194 per group and adjusted p = .0087. These groups combine the 155-per-group training and 39-per-group validation partitions described in the article. The result measures differential recognition conditional on hyposmia. It does not provide a dose, meal exposure or symptom change after cinnamon consumption. ## Larina early-onset cohort figure and companion abstract

The 2026 Larina congress figure shows about 56% cinnamon identification errors, consistent with 43.9% correct in the abstract. The overall cohort was 156 people with motor onset at or before age 50. The figure provides no item-level denominator, and 43.9% cannot be recovered by rounding a whole-number count over 156 to one decimal place. The report gives no cinnamon-specific duration estimate or food intake measure. A companion abstract with the same ten authors describes 93 women from an early-onset Parkinson registry. It offers a possible registry link, with no participant-level linkage to the smell cohort. Searches in English and Russian located the two congress records without finding a full Larina paper or registry data dictionary. [Primary congress report](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/odor-specific-olfactory-dysfunction-in-early-onset-parkinsons-disease/) · [Original figure](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/0988_1474_000891_1.jpeg) · [Companion registry abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/menstrual-cycle-influence-on-parkinsons-disease-symptoms-in-women-with-early-onset/)

## Six inhaled odors and olfaction in diabetes-related cognitive impairment

Chao and colleagues published online on 5 September 2025. The abstract reports improved overall olfactory function after 16 weeks of training with six odors including cinnamon, compared with the control group in an open-label randomized trial of 60 participants. Original arm-level smell-domain estimates and supplemental analyses remain inaccessible. NCT06327633 closely matches the study characteristics. The available abstract does not explicitly name the registry identifier. The report concerns a bundle of inhaled stimuli without a standalone cinnamon treatment contrast. ## Registered oral mixed oils and adverse smell symptoms

The first public posting was April 8, 2026. The official record lists completion on September 30, 2024 and no posted results. The respiratory illness trial compares an oral formulation containing cinnamon essential oil and six other essential oils with placebo. Follow-up asks about adverse symptoms including changes in smell. The protocol does not identify ground bark, an isolated cinnamon dose comparison, a validated olfactory impairment scale or a Parkinson subgroup. Planned symptom collection gives no result or oil-specific attribution. [Official API record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT07518394).
