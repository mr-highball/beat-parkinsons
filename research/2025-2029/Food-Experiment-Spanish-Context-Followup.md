# Spanish evidence follow-up, 2025–2029

## Lingual stimulation and reported swallowing in Barcelona

De Almeida Simão and colleagues first published qualitative findings on October 17, 2025. In 11 focus groups with 24 participants with Parkinson disease and 14 caregivers, some described easier swallowing of food or tablets and improved saliva control after intraoral stimulation plus conventional speech therapy. Others described unchanged symptoms. The interviews used solids and liquids as broad categories and supply no named food or preparation comparison. [Primary journal article, PMID 41105286](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12534625/) · [Licensed local article text](Source-Text/PMC12534625.txt#L163)

The linked February 20, 2026 Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona dissertation adds the quantitative phase. Thirty participants received stimulation plus conventional therapy in a single-arm pre/post design. The investigators measured tongue strength and endurance with a pressure instrument and used the Eating Assessment Tool and the Functional Oral Intake Scale for clinical assessment. At baseline, 2, 12 and 16 participants were classified at intake levels 4, 5 and 6, respectively. Table 11 reports mean maximum tongue pressures of 40.60 kPa at baseline, 45.40 after six sessions, 50.00 after 12 sessions and 44.08 a month later. The table gives larger model coefficients at six sessions and one month than the corresponding raw mean changes. The reported tongue-endurance contrasts were inconclusive. The full methods supply no standardized food challenge or effect of a named ingredient.
