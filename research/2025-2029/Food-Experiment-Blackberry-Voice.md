# Blackberry and voice outcomes

## Boysenberry apple powder and adult throat irritation

Lomiwes and colleagues reported a randomized crossover comparison in 20 healthy adults in September 2025. Participants consumed BerriQi boysenberry–apple powder or placebo daily for five days before exposure in an environmental chamber. The report's abstract gives p=0.04 for throat irritation and discusses recovery on separately scored respiratory symptoms through 48 hours. The accessible record does not give an acoustic result, laryngeal examination, or actual intervention dose. The earlier registry entry has a different target sample and records planned exposure. Whole frozen blackberry, Parkinson's disease and speech loudness were outside the reported trial scope. ## Pediatric boysenberry apple trial and composite talking item

Shrestha and colleagues posted the trial as a preprint on 3 August 2026. The study randomized 84 children aged 5–13 with acute upper respiratory infection, 42 per group. Eighty-one supplied a diary and 64 met per-protocol criteria. For 14 days, each arm received two daily tablets based on freeze-dried boysenberry and apple. The placebo boysenberry component was heat-treated before freeze drying, with the apple held constant. The trial reported 6.4 mg per day of combined measured anthocyanins, quercetin and procyanidins in the active product and 0.5 mg per day in the placebo.

The child or caregiver illness questionnaire included talking in a seven-item score for daily function, alongside thinking, sleeping, breathing, walking, school and play. The authors reported p=0.05 for the between-group composite function effect and p=0.57 for its interaction with day. The global illness item showed p=0.21 for a treatment effect. The body and four supplements supplied no isolated talking result or acoustic amplitude. Sore throat was a different item, and its reported nominal result did not survive the adjustment for multiple comparisons. ## Parkinson dietary questionnaires and exposure resolution

A 70-person Thai Parkinson cohort logged berries and frozen foods under different survey items. The recovered official questionnaire gives cups of unspecified berries over seven days in its berry module. The frozen-food module counts frozen or ready-made meals over seven days, with no intersection by fruit or preparation. The separate item-congruence table confirms how these exposures were asked. The report lists 20 participants eating berries weekly and 16 eating frozen foods weekly. Neither aggregate identifies frozen blackberry or pairs individual consumption with calibrated speech sound pressure. The Lithuanian Parkinson food-frequency and diet-recall papers provide survey-based food and motor information, with no published frozen blackberry-specific association with voice or acoustic outcome. These studies are useful to locate diet histories and indicate the limits of the published fields.
