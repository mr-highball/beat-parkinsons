# Round 1 saffron citations, dietary oil and clove source audit

The November 24, 2026 review resolved 95 exact claim–source checks across 60 originals, with 68 contextual exclusions and 27 related scopes. Thirty-two related assessments were added. The 25 rechecked scorecards retain their verdicts.

## Ground saffron and depressive symptoms

The 2019 saffron metabolite review points to reference 110 for safranal's antidepressant activity. The full original describes an induced-seizure experiment and cites an independent 2004 study for the mood finding. In that animal experiment, injected saffron preparations showed forced-swim and open-field signals. The highest safranal arm had less immobility and more swimming during the forced-swim assay, while overall open-field locomotion fell. The paper varies its dose-unit labels between figures and tables. The clinical evidence for claim 0063 still comes from the previously recorded Parkinson ground-stigma comparison. [2019 review](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2019-PMID31382514-Saffron-Metabolite-Review-and-Safranal-Citation.txt) · [2006 citation scope](../2005-2009/Source-Text/2006-PMID16707256-Safranal-Seizures-and-Citation-Scope.txt) · [2004 forced-swim experiment](../2000-2004/Source-Text/2004-ActaHortic-650-54-Saffron-Stigma-and-Forced-Swim.txt) · [Parkinson trial record](Source-Text/2025-Hajhashemy-Saffron-AMT-and-PDQ.txt)

The same seizure article points to a 1994 report that attributes customary saffron use for fear and low mood to a 1977 Chinese dictionary. The 1994 experiment reported a longer duration of drug-induced loss of righting reflex in one oral-extract arm. Its memory, sleep and customary-use observations have separate records. The dictionary pages remain uninspected. ## Olive oil and coconut oil

In the already cataloged 2017 study of 1,053 people with self-reported idiopathic Parkinson disease, each higher food-frequency category corresponded to −31.4 points for olive oil (95% CI −44.7 to −18.1) and −20.2 for coconut oil (95% CI −31.0 to −9.4) on total PRO-PD in the model that added household income. The 2019 gut review repeats the same cohort. The associations come from baseline, duration-adjusted data and do not isolate refining grades, individual symptoms, or change over time. Separate reviews name olive oil within a Mediterranean pattern and oats and barley as sources of prebiotic fiber. [2017 primary article and Table 2](../2015-2019/Source-Text/PMC5610862.txt) · [Gut dysbiosis review](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2019-PMID31327219-Parkinson-Gut-Review-Olive-and-Coconut-Oil-Cohort.txt) · [Diet and microbiota review](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2019-PMID31763115-Parkinson-Microbiota-Diet-Patterns-Oats-and-Barley.txt)

## Clove and abdominal pain reports

A randomized trial in 64 constipated postmenopausal women tested a powdered multi-herb capsule. It contained cloves, senna, raisins and other ingredients. Abdominal pain was reported by six of 32 people on the formula and two of 32 on placebo. The same composite intervention improved constipation. The pain counts track occurrence. The trial offers no clove-only comparison or pain severity measure and does not study Parkinson disease. The clove pain scorecard records the signal as indirect, separate from the earlier topical acute-pain comparisons.
