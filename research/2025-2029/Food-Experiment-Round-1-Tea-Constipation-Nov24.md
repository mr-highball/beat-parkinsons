## Claim 0072, extra-virgin olive oil and Parkinson constipation

A Spanish survey of adults over 50 found fewer cases of infrequent bowel movements among people reporting higher virgin olive oil use. Table II reports 16/397 versus 2/11 with three or fewer weekly movements, and Table IV reports a higher mean weekly frequency in the high-consumption group. The printed chi-square and reported p do not align, and the low-intake group is very small. A later review assigned the opposite direction to the same survey. The scorecard cites the original source and counts the observation once. [Original survey summary](../2005-2009/Source-Text/2006-PMID16948544-Lopez-Cara-Olive-Oil-and-Constipation.txt) · [Review scope and correction](../2005-2009/Source-Text/2008-PMID18269746-Constipation-Epidemiology-Review.txt)

Fernandez and colleagues studied Mediterranean diet counseling with a randomized three-month waitlist in Parkinson constipation. Both groups improved and there was no clear between-group difference. The preprint and final abstract have different descriptions of the timing of the quoted means. The preprint reports an inconclusive month-three group interaction on stool frequency and stool form. Olive oil was one component of an adherence score and the trial did not isolate extra-virgin olive oil. The preprint, final report and earlier registration count as one study. [Trial original review](Source-Text/2026-Fernandez-Mediterranean-Counseling-Preprint.txt) · [Registered design and version](../2020-2024/Source-Text/Trials/RBR-36q469s.txt)

Morvaridi's original data deposit is a new access route for the ulcerative-colitis crossover. The underlying participant data were not inspected and the numerical between-oil constipation result remains open. The 0072 rated direction still comes from a direct extra-virgin versus refined contrast outside Parkinson disease. ## Claim 0082, brewed black tea and bradykinesia

The Research Square article body identifies habitual caffeinated coffee as the exposure behind an unfavorable progression signal. Its bradykinesia comparison is distinct from its inconclusive rest-tremor result. The Singapore cohort compares the incidence of Parkinson diagnosis and supplies no established-Parkinson symptom endpoint. A six-week isolated-caffeine randomized trial reports a separate bradykinesia comparison, and the measured caffeine in brewed tea supports only a limited link. The evidence scorecards preserve the contradictory signal and separate unrelated underlying comparisons.
