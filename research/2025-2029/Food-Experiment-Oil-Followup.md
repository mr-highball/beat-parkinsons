# Edible oil evidence follow-up, 2025–2029

## Coconut oil vehicle and reported fatigue in healthy volunteers

Smith and colleagues published the PPI-1011 phase I study in March 2025 (PMID 40083139, DOI 10.1111/cts.70195). The drug and placebo both used oral liquid coconut oil with thioglycerol. The paper gives no virgin or culinary grade. The single-dose adverse-event table lists fatigue in one of six participants in the lowest drug-dose group and zero of ten in the pooled placebo group. The repeated-dose table has no fatigue entry across its 12 drug and four placebo participants. These are treatment-emergent adverse-event counts, with no fatigue severity scale, oil-free comparison or Parkinson cohort. The trial isolates adding the drug to a shared vehicle, so it does not estimate a virgin coconut oil effect. [Original Methods and Tables 2–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11906485/) · [Dated registration](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Oil-Followup.md#registration-for-the-coconut-oil-vehicle-in-the-ppi-1011-safety-study)

## Greek olive phenolic protocols in mild cognitive impairment

The Panhellenic meeting abstract 2.36 proposes two concentrations of an evaporated aqueous olive fraction (VITA P), placebo, or Mediterranean diet instructions, with cognition and Alzheimer’s biomarkers in mild cognitive impairment. No separate trial registration or results were located by the study title and product name. Abstract 2.85 describes the registered MeDi-SUPOL capsule and dietary-pattern trial. Abstract 2.49 describes use of extra-virgin olive oil among several products in MCI care without a controlled comparison. The Parkinson’s abstracts elsewhere in the book supply no olive-oil anxiety or apathy outcomes. [Primary abstracts 2.36, 2.49 and 2.85](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12472420/) · [Earlier pilot and MeDi-SUPOL registration](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Oil-Followup.md#extra-virgin-olive-oil-and-olive-phenolics-in-mild-cognitive-impairment)

## Medium-chain triglycerides in a worm model

Zhang and colleagues examined how alpha-synuclein altered lipid metabolism in *Caenorhabditis elegans*. Medium-chain triglyceride supplementation improved some model phenotypes. A human comparison reported plasma lipid concentrations in Parkinson’s cohorts. It did not test an oil intervention. The animal preparation and the human biomarker comparison leave the clinical effects of virgin and refined coconut oil unresolved. [Primary report, PMID 42811169](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44319-026-00954-7)

## Coconut-derived medium-chain triglycerides in a Mediterranean diet

In the KIM study, Tosefsky and colleagues randomized 52 people with Parkinson’s to an eight-week Mediterranean ketogenic diet and an eight-week Mediterranean diet supplemented with coconut-derived medium-chain triglyceride oil, in randomized order. Forty-eight started and 33 completed both periods. After the triglyceride-supplemented period, MDS-UPDRS Parts II and IV fell by 1.4 and 1.0 points on average in within-period comparisons. The study found no clear between-diet differences in any MDS-UPDRS part. The investigators documented two reports of diarrhea, one of fatigue, and one of exacerbated tremor during the triglyceride-supplemented period. They assessed the fatigue report as unlikely to be caused by the intervention. These data describe combined diets with an oil fraction and offer limited attribution to the ingredient, preparation, or symptom in a frozen statement. [Primary report, Tables 3–4, PMID 41637153](https://doi.org/10.1177/1877718X261418986) · [Local study registration](../2020-2024/Source-Text/Trials/NCT05469997.txt)

The April 2025 medRxiv preprint is an earlier report of the same study. Its local text includes the adverse-event and clinical-outcome tables. [Local preprint v1 text, Tables 3–4](Source-Text/2025-Tosefsky-KIM-Preprint-v1.txt) · [Publisher preprint](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/medrxiv/early/2025/04/03/2025.04.01.25325057.full.pdf)

The 2025 Movement Disorder Society abstract also reports the same registration and sample, with lower within-period MDS-UPDRS II and IV scores after the MCT-supplemented diet. It does not supply individual Part IV items or an independent cohort. [Primary conference abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/random-order-crossover-trial-assessing-the-safety-feasibility-and-exploratory-efficacy-of-two-mediterranean-ketogenic-nutritional-interventions-in-parkinsons-disease/)

## KIM diet interviews

The October 30, 2025 qualitative report analyzed 67 interviews from 44 participants in the same crossover. Fourteen interviewees described perceived constipation relief as a reason to adhere. Three interviewees reported reduced energy during the Mediterranean ketogenic period. Two participants attributed gastrointestinal effects that led them to withdraw to the MCT oil during the supplemented period. Another participant who withdrew from the ketogenic period described prolonged OFF time after a high-fat meal and suspected delayed gastric emptying and levodopa absorption. The report gives no measured gastric emptying, drug concentration, or separate oil exposure for that account. Apathy appears as an existing barrier to meal preparation and logging. The interviews do not estimate an isolated culinary oil effect. [Local primary full text](Source-Text/PMC12670100.txt#L346) · [Primary report, PMID 41341605](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cdnut.2025.107591)

## KIM bowel and fatigue outcomes

The October 28, 2026 iScience report adds gastrointestinal and immune outcomes from the same crossover. Bowel movement frequency (OR 1.03, 95% CI 0.82–1.28), stool form (OR 1.16, 95% CI 0.47–2.85), and laxative use (OR 1.03, 95% CI 0.80–1.33) showed no clear between-diet difference. Neither diet showed a clear within-period change on the MDS-UPDRS constipation item (ketogenic p = .763, triglyceride-supplemented p = .963). [Primary report, PMID 42992162](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13738181/) · [Local text](Source-Text/PMC13738181.txt#L182) · [Supplementary Tables S2 and S4](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC13738181.1/mmc1.pdf)

Among the 34 ketogenic and 37 triglyceride-supplemented periods with Patient Global Impression of Change responses, bowel symptoms improved in 15 (44%) and 20 (54%) and worsened in 8 (24%) and 8 (22%), respectively (p = .7). Fatigue improved in 10 (29%) and 9 (24%) and worsened in 13 (38%) and 9 (24%), respectively (p = .2). The report lists baseline counts meeting the Fatigue Severity Scale and Starkstein Apathy Scale thresholds and gives no separate post-intervention estimates for either scale. The results compare two dietary patterns and a fractionated triglyceride oil. They give no isolated effect for culinary coconut oil. [Supplementary Tables S2 and S4](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC13738181.1/mmc1.pdf) · [Primary report and local text](Source-Text/PMC13738181.txt#L117)

The Results report no multiple-testing-adjusted association between *Roseburia hominis* abundance and MDS-UPDRS Parts II or IV (q > .05). The Discussion states that higher abundance was associated with lower Part IV scores. A local replay of the public data found an inverse Part IV association in the ketogenic period before adjustment (normal Wald p = .024), with inconclusive adjusted results in both diets (q = .367 and .668). This could explain the difference in wording. The original model table is unavailable. The models compare visit-level abundance and Part IV scores and give no separate motor complication outcome for coconut oil. [Results](Source-Text/PMC13738181.txt#L214) · [Discussion](Source-Text/PMC13738181.txt#L254) · [Native replay and limitations](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/followup/kim-part-iv-audit.md)

The Results and Table S4 also give different within-period p-values for IL-1β (.014 versus .056) and IL-8 (.003 versus .001) during the triglyceride-supplemented diet. These are circulating biomarkers. The report says the changes did not exceed its reference-change thresholds. [Local results](Source-Text/PMC13738181.txt#L188) · [Supplementary Table S4](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC13738181.1/mmc1.pdf)

## KIM public dataset

Borealis first released the study dataset on October 26, 2026, two days before the iScience article appeared online. Version 1 includes a data dictionary and a 172-row, 54-column clinical file. ## Gua Sha and REM sleep behavior disorder

Chen and colleagues randomized 52 people with Parkinson’s disease and REM sleep behavior disorder to Gua Sha with usual care or usual care alone. At 12 weeks the adjusted between-group difference on the Hong Kong REM Sleep Behavior Disorder Questionnaire was −9.31 points (95% CI −10.69 to −7.92), favoring the Gua Sha group. The authors also reported that family members noticed less nocturnal shouting, without tabulating event counts. The accessible methods list scraping oil without identifying its composition. The trial leaves the use of grapeseed oil unverified and provides no estimate for [0441, ingested refined grapeseed oil and dream enactment frequency](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0441.txt). [Primary abstract, PMID 40608228](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40608228/) · [Available copy, Methods and Table 2](https://www.scribd.com/document/1066019934/s10072-025-08335-z-2) · [2023 registration note](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Oil-Followup.md#gua-sha-trial-registrations-at-changshu-hospital)

## Extra-virgin olive oil in the PRIME Parkinson diet protocol

The PRIME trial registration first posted on July 31, 2025. The March 25, 2026 protocol specifies extra-virgin olive oil as the main added fat in the Mediterranean diet intervention. The planned 80-participant factorial trial compares the diet, physical activity, both, and standard care. Its main endpoint concerns microbiome composition. Exploratory clinical outcomes include motor examination and complications, gastrointestinal symptoms and stool form. The protocol plans periodic food records. Participants prepare or purchase their own meals and change multiple components of their diet. The trial can estimate a dietary-pattern effect and its interaction with activity. It has no arm assigned to olive oil alone. [2025 registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07097103) · [2026 protocol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41959663/) · [Licensed protocol text](Source-Text/PMC13057374.txt)

The registry API checked November 16, 2026 lists its July 30, 2026 posting and no results. It still lists not yet recruiting and a September 1, 2026 estimated start, with no actual start date. The registry lists constipation and gait as other outcomes. The corrected March protocol designates a prespecified microbiome beta-diversity contrast, while the registry lists four primary microbiome measures. [Official API record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT07097103)

## Caregiver-reported stool changes after olive oil

A March 1, 2026 post in a Parkinson community describes softer stool after one week of drinking olive oil of unspecified grade. The caregiver also describes concurrent walking and says passage of the initial hard stool remained difficult. The post provides no formal diagnosis record, stool diary or comparator. It supplies a reported stool-consistency change and leaves the effect of extra-virgin olive oil on Parkinson constipation unresolved. [Caregiver account](https://www.reddit.com/r/Parkinsons/comments/1rhiuqy/a_shot_or_2_of_olive_oil/)

## Medium-chain triglycerides in a ketogenic neuromelanin mouse model

Gatti and colleagues reported a ketogenic diet containing medium-chain triglycerides in the October 2025 Bordeaux BrainBody poster P17 and the May 2026 World Parkinson Congress abstract P26.05. The Bordeaux report describes five months of feeding and reports improved cognition, more vocalizations and inconclusive anxiety- and depression-like behavior. The World Parkinson Congress report describes four months of feeding, reports greater anxiety, less depression-like behavior, better long-term memory and partial recovery of vocalizations, and describes larger effects in males. Both report a mixed ketogenic feed and do not identify the source oil or provide a separated oil comparison. The matching study team, model, starting age and diet suggest overlapping work. The differing treatment duration and behavioral results leave exact cohort and analysis overlap unresolved. [Bordeaux poster P17, October 1–3, 2025](https://brainconf.u-bordeaux.fr/assets/Group/24/brainbody---posters.pdf) · [World Parkinson Congress abstract P26.05, May 22, 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13360760/)

## Intravenous hydroxytyrosol in a later MPP rat report

Pérez-Barrón, Sánchez-González and colleagues published a related rat study on March 5, 2026. The primary abstract describes 1.5 mg/kg of isolated hydroxytyrosol by tail vein before a 10 μg intrastriatal MPP+ challenge. It reports forced-swim behavior, apomorphine rotations and striatal dopamine and serotonin. The university classifies the full article as restricted. The authors and procedures overlap the 2019 thesis. Whether they reused animals or results remains unresolved. The abstract gives no culinary olive oil, resting tremor or clinical bradykinesia comparison. ## KIM olive oil advice and individual symptom reporting

The feasibility report was first published February 4, 2026. Both eight-week phases emphasized olive oil alongside other Mediterranean foods. The article body gives no olive-oil grade, dose or separate participant-level intake estimates. The trial assigned the order of full dietary patterns. The Table 3 safety record includes one mild intermittent insomnia event judged possibly related to the ketogenic diet and one unrelated post-DBS serious insomnia and psychosis event during the MCT-supplemented diet. The investigators asked about adverse events at each check-in and visit. The paper reports 41 participants completed at least one phase and 33 both. Table 4 lists 39 and 41 post-visit observations. Event incidence by completed phase cannot be calculated reliably from those denominators without reconciling the difference. [Primary report, Methods and Tables 3–4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13347592/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/PMC13347592-KIM-Olive-Oil-Outcomes.txt)

A local count of the publicly released Borealis V1 file `KIM_clinical.tab` uses nonmissing post-visit anxiety ratings and the dictionary categories 1–3 improved, 4 unchanged and 5–7 worse. The ketogenic phase gives 10 improved, 20 unchanged and 4 worse (34 ratings). The MCT-supplemented phase gives 9 improved, 22 unchanged and 6 worse (37 ratings). These are unadjusted responses during overlapping crossover phases, without an olive-only comparator. The public file gives ordinal anxiety change ratings. Post-phase Parkinson Anxiety Scale estimates remain unverified. The later bowel report from the same cohort gives an imprecise bowel-frequency comparison between the full diets (OR 1.03, 95% CI 0.82–1.28). [Released clinical data and dictionary](https://doi.org/10.5683/SP4/EKV3YZ) · [Bowel outcomes](#kim-bowel-and-fatigue-outcomes)

The KIM protocol and the later primary report specify supply for MCT oil. The two PubMed Central article-version inventories contain no diet handouts. The SAGE and medRxiv supplement routes were blocked during review. The original handouts could clarify recommended olive-oil grade or amount. [Earlier protocol and status abstract](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Oil-Followup.md#public-kim-protocol-and-olive-oil-guidance) · [Publisher supplement route](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/suppl/10.1177/1877718X261418986)

## Lithuanian Mediterranean adherence and olive oil items

The dietary-history study included 59 people with Parkinson disease and 54 controls. Reported use of olive oil in cooking was more frequent in the Parkinson group (median 5.5 versus 3.0 times per week, p=.024). Within Parkinson disease, each additional point on the whole Mediterranean score was associated with lower odds of anxiety (unadjusted OR 0.88, 95% CI 0.78–0.98, p=.028) and constipation (OR 0.88, 95% CI 0.78–0.98, p=.027). The authors reported that associations persisted after adjustment. The models use the composite dietary score. Cooking frequency did not specify olive-oil grade, and the report gives no separate oil-specific symptom association. [Original clinical and dietary tables](Source-Text/PMC13006276.txt#L238) · [Original symptom models](Source-Text/PMC13006276.txt#L283) · [Original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13006276/)

## Shared olive oil in a Parkinson cannabis case series

First public article date February 24, 2025, despite a 2024 collection year. Six people with Parkinson disease were assigned to two doses of the same cannabis extract diluted in olive oil. The study had three people per dose and no oil-only or oil-free comparator. Grade was unreported. Table 1 lists tremor, postural instability and bradykinesia at baseline. The report supplies no separate serial change for those motor items. At day 60 the higher-dose group reported a lower Insomnia Severity Index than its own baseline (p=.03). This comparison does not isolate olive oil and does not supply a measured anxiety endpoint. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11891160/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2025-Ruver-Martins-Olive-Carrier-and-Insomnia.txt)
