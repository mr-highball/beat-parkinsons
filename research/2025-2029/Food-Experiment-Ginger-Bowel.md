# Ginger bowel outcomes, 2025–2029

## Steamed ginger extract in functional dyspepsia

Baek and colleagues assigned 80 adults with functional dyspepsia to standardized steamed ginger extract or placebo over 12 weeks. Constipation on the Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale changed by −1.5 ± 4.4 with extract and +1.4 ± 2.9 with placebo, reported p<.001 for the comparison. The preparation used dried and steamed ginger followed by ethanol extraction and spray drying. The separate constipation score supplies human outcome evidence with limited applicability to raw grated rhizome or Parkinson disease. ## Ginger-PD gastrointestinal outcomes

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted Ginger-PD (NCT07882108) on 20 October 2026, after the listed actual start on 17 December 2025. The randomized study compares two strengths of ginger root extract, both in capsules that also contain sunflower oil. The primary outcome is ghrelin. The Gastrointestinal Dysfunction Scale for Parkinson Disease, whole gut transit and breath methane are listed as other outcomes. The registry has no standalone constipation result and no inactive comparator. The 15 November 2026 retrieval found the trial recruiting and no posted results. [Official record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07882108) · [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07882108.txt)

## Isolated cinnamon-oil compounds in a constipation mouse model

Peng and colleagues assessed three isolated compounds in a mouse model of constipation-predominant irritable bowel syndrome. The original abstract names pellet counts and fecal water and reports overall improvement. Arm-specific pellet and water results remain inaccessible. The modeled outcome and isolated exposure leave culinary ground cinnamon effects open. [Original June 2026 abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00210-026-05614-0) · [Indexed abstract summary](Source-Text/2026-Peng-Cinnamon-Compounds-Abstract-Extract.txt)

## Cited ginger trial in the neurodegenerative constipation review

Park and colleagues first published this scoping review online July 16, 2026. The abstract counts 13 reports across multiple sclerosis, Parkinson disease and dementia, with one ginger intervention. It gives no identity or separate outcome for ginger. The [publisher-deposited bibliography](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177%2F01939459261451721) includes DOI 10.1186/s12906-023-04227-x, an earlier randomized trial of ginger tablets in relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis. The public supplementary outcome table now confirms that the review included the Foshati trial. The main article remains restricted. The separate Parkinson ginger registration NCT07882108 first posted in October 2026 after the review search and has no posted results. [Original source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42459041/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2026-Park-Neurodegenerative-Constipation-Citation-Extract.txt)

## Ginger trial identity in the published constipation review supplement

The July 2026 scoping review's Supplementary Table S1 names Foshati (2023) under constipation and records a 0–100 mm visual analogue scale. The corresponding publisher reference identifies the relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis randomized trial DOI 10.1186/s12906-023-04227-x. The supplement superscript is 45 and the final bibliography numbers this reference 46. The author, year and title establish the match. The primary report compares standardized ginger tablets with corn placebo. Its constipation frequency outcome was a participant rating on a visual analogue scale. The supplement confirms inclusion in the review without adding a separate trial, a Parkinson estimate or a fresh-grated ginger comparison.
