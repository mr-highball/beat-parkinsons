# Refined rice bran oil and Parkinson symptom evidence

## Rice oil inhalation control in older adults with dysphagia

Thirty-four older adults received brief odor exposure to hinoki cypress oil and to Boso Komeabura rice oil in a crossover. Six were classified as having neurodegenerative disease, without individual diagnoses or subgroup outcomes. Under rice oil control, median change in repetitive saliva swallowing was 0.0 (95% CI −0.5 to 0.0, P=.067), in 3 mL modified water swallowing 0.0 (P=.739), and in 30 mL water swallowing grade 0.0 (P=.257). Hinoki versus control favored hinoki for the first two swallowing tests. The modified water test incorporates cough or wet-hoarse voice in one grade, without separate hoarseness or voice loudness outcomes. The rice oil was on cotton for odor exposure, without ingestion. The manufacturer identifies its edible oil as rice bran oil and describes extraction and refining, including deodorization, without a documented match to the specific study lot. ## Perceived intensity of rice oil control in a healthy crossover

Twenty healthy adults inhaled Boso Komeabura rice oil and hinoki cypress essential oil separately. During exposure, mean odor intensity was 0.4 (SEM 0.1) for rice oil and 2.9 (SEM 0.2) for hinoki on a 0–4 scale, P<.001. Repetitive saliva swallow count change was +0.30 (SEM 0.30) under rice oil and +1.35 (SEM 0.30) under hinoki, paired P=.011. The participant's rating of a presented odor does not measure whether olfactory impairment changed. The trial tests short odor exposure with no food or Parkinson participants. The study methods and Figure 1 give different amounts applied to the mask cotton. Manufacturer information identifies the named oil family as refined edible rice bran oil but cannot verify the lot.
