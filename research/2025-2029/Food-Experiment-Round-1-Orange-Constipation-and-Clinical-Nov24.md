# Round 1 orange constipation and clinical source audit

This audit checked 79 ingredient–symptom scopes for 19 frozen claims across 60 original publications. It archived dated factual extracts and reports 73 scope exclusions and six related contexts. Forty-four source checks involve dried dates and either pain (39) or apathy (five). The original publications give no dried-date fruit effects for those Parkinson outcomes.

Three original-study lookups followed the raw-orange lead. The [2022 crossover](../2020-2024/Source-Text/2022-PMID36102329-Orange-Pomace-and-Bowel-Movement-Crossover.txt) gave an inconclusive primary weekly frequency interaction and a favorable secondary product main effect for avoiding a day without a bowel movement. The latter was reported as p=.002 in the prose and p=.004 in Table S7, with baseline imbalance and an inconclusive interaction. The [2026 blend trial](Source-Text/2026-PMID42369644-Citrus-Fibre-Blend-and-Constipation.txt) improved bowel frequency in functional constipation, but excluded Parkinson disease and combined four fibres. The [2024 UK Biobank author abstract](../2020-2024/Source-Text/2024-PMID39159603-Constipation-and-Incident-Parkinson-Cohort.txt) linked baseline constipation and future Parkinson diagnosis with no dietary assignment. These outcomes supply context for the very weak easing lean previously anchored to a separate orange-pomace study.

The nettle experiment used corn oil as a vehicle. The Parkinson prodromal cohort grouped chicken or turkey sandwiches with other ultraprocessed foods. Coffee restriction was part of a separate diagnostic comparison. The lemon balm trial administered an inhaled preparation of a different plant. The colonic model review discussed earlier citrus-related work. These sources preserve exposure and endpoint boundaries for the claim assessments.
