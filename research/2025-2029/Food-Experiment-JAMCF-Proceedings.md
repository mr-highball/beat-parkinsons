# Japan Chronic Phase Medicine meeting reports on feeding in Parkinson’s and vascular parkinsonism

## Thickened water and jelly during swallowing rehabilitation

Takahiro Izumi and colleagues described a man in his sixties with Parkinson’s disease who used gastrostomy feeding after aortic dissection and aspiration pneumonia. During multidisciplinary rehabilitation, the team adjusted formula type and timing and introduced direct training with moderately thick water and jelly. At final assessment, videofluoroscopy showed pharyngeal residue for both preparations, with no aspiration at boluses up to 4 mL when he swallowed repeatedly. He subsequently used thickened juice and jelly at home and progressed to code 4 texture with no clinical signs of aspiration. The report does not identify the juice or jelly ingredients or separate their effect from rehabilitation and nutrition management. [2025 original abstract 18-5, PDF page 263](https://gakkai.co.jp/jamcf33/files/abstracts_full.pdf#page=263)

## Frozen soda confection and videofluoroscopy in vascular parkinsonism

Saori Ōki and colleagues compared a soda-flavored ice confection with thickened water on videofluoroscopy in six patients in their seventies to nineties with vascular parkinsonism, pseudobulbar palsy and severe dysphagia. The named product was Garigari-kun soda flavor. Chewing and subsequent swallowing were observed with the confection in all six. Oral transit time, laryngeal elevation delay and swallow-reflex initiation delay were shorter in all six with the confection, and penetration–aspiration scores improved. The abstract gives directions without numerical values, and the boluses differed in texture, temperature and taste. The population had vascular parkinsonism with pseudobulbar palsy. The organizer published the abstract before the November 19–20, 2026 meeting.
