# Spice evidence follow-up, 2025–2029

## Nigella seed extract and cinnamaldehyde in a manganese rat model

Ahmed and colleagues published in the April 2025 issue. After manganese exposure, separate nigella seed extract and isolated cinnamaldehyde groups spent less time immobile in forced swimming and showed higher sucrose preference than the model group. Manganese withdrawal alone also reduced swim immobility, and Figure 1 assigns the same statistical grouping to withdrawal and treated arms. The nanoemulsion arm had no significant sucrose-preference difference from the model group. The study gives related rat behavioral evidence for `0019` and no patient outcome for culinary nigella seed or cinnamon. [Primary report, Figures 1–2, DOI 10.21608/avmj.2025.351081.1546](https://avmj.journals.ekb.eg/article_422090_c1fef4d29aae87d8917760770c6bf859.pdf)

## Cassia-containing prescription in a Parkinson rehabilitation case

Yun and colleagues published on May 25, 2025. A woman with Parkinson disease and severe thoracolumbar kyphosis received multimodal rehabilitation, including acupuncture, manual and thermal therapies, and exercise, while continuing levodopa. Table A1 lists cassia in the multicomponent Doinseunggi-tang routine medication. Back pain fell from 50 to 40 on the report's 0–100 scale over four weeks. The case supplies no cassia-only comparison and cannot attribute the change to cinnamon for `0161`. ## Isolated ginger constituent and depression-like behavior in mice

Kim and colleagues first published on March 10, 2025. The accessible abstract reports that oral gavage with isolated 6-shogaol during an MPTP regimen reduced motor deficits and depression-like behaviors in mice. It also reports changes in striatal and hippocampal monoamines. The full text was unavailable through the publisher’s public route. The abstract supplies preclinical evidence for a ginger constituent without a whole or grated ginger comparison or a clinical depression outcome. [Primary abstract, PMID 40063144](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00394-025-03639-4)

Cadi Ayyad University scheduled Mjid Oukhrib's doctoral defense for September 27, 2025. ## Injected carvacrol and haloperidol-associated motor deficits

Albaqami and colleagues published on February 5, 2025. Figure 2 reports mean catalepsy time of 114.72 seconds in female rats treated with haloperidol, and 51.50 and 73.08 seconds in the groups receiving the two tested levels of injected isolated carvacrol (p < .001 for each comparison). Figure 3 reports higher locomotor activity and less swim immobility in carvacrol groups. Nine animals that died during the experiment were replaced, including one in the higher carvacrol group and six in the carvacrol-plus-thalidomide group. The authors discuss swim immobility as a motility outcome in this model. No culinary oregano, stride length, clinical anxiety or daily off time was assessed.
