# Primary Portuguese-language diet research

## 2026 Projeto Reviver gastrointestinal and nutrition records

Souza and colleagues’ September 9, 2026 chapter reports 11 people followed by Projeto Reviver at the Federal University of Amapá in 2026. Table 1 gives constipation in three of seven participants with Parkinson disease and one of four with Alzheimer disease. The three Parkinson participants with constipation also separately reported reduced appetite, reflux, or difficulty chewing. The study recorded body mass index, comorbidities and gastrointestinal complaints. Its limitations explicitly state that fiber and liquid consumption and constipating medication use were unmeasured. A January 13 journal report from the project gives a descriptive clinical and nutritional profile, with constipation the most commonly reported gastrointestinal symptom and excess weight predominating. Its publisher PDF route returned an access error, so participant-level overlap and any additional measures remain unverified. ## Dietary protein and sarcopenia in Porto Alegre

Juliana Heitich Brendler’s 2024 UFRGS dissertation was accessioned on February 26, 2025, the earliest repository date verified. The cross-sectional study collected three nonconsecutive days of food records from 37 people with Parkinson’s disease and classified 12 as having sarcopenia using the SARC-F and handgrip strength. Total protein intake was similar by sarcopenia status. Estimated intake of vitamins B3 (p = .019) and B12 (p = .023) was lower in the sarcopenia group. The abstract does not report a named food comparison or treatment effect. [BDTD record and accession date](https://www.bdtd.ibict.br/vufind/Record/UFRGS-2_32335cccecc5840ab8d026f916218e56/Details), [UFRGS dissertation](https://hdl.handle.net/10183/287650)

## Combined prebiotic fibers and depressive-like behavior in mice

Ingrid Prata de Mendonça defended the UFPE thesis February 21, 2025. The university made it available September 15, 2025. The thesis describes combined oral fructooligosaccharides and galactooligosaccharides during rotenone induction in mice. It reports longer rotarod latency and more open-field crossings and rearing, along with greater sucrose preference and less tail-suspension immobility. The results overlap the [2024 UFPE motor report](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Portuguese-Primary-Followup.md#combined-prebiotic-fibers-and-motor-behavior-in-mice) and the 2026 article, including the same animal ethics protocol and some identical results. [UFPE thesis and availability date](https://repositorio.ufpe.br/handle/123456789/65911?show=full)

The journal version first appeared March 13, 2026. Relative to rotenone alone, combined prebiotic treatment increased rotarod latency (p = .0071), rearing (p = .0047) and crossings (p = .0349), and increased sucrose preference (p = .0154) and reduced tail-suspension immobility (p = .0120). The motor tests included 8–10 animals per group and the depressive-like behavior tests included 5–10 per group. These are motor and depressive-like behavior outcomes from a mouse model in which the preparation was given during induction. The experiment supplies no result for a specific food or for clinical depression in people with Parkinson’s disease.
