# Blackberry exposures and pain outcomes

Reviewed 2026-11-14 (US Eastern).

## Rubus ulmifolius extract confection and menstrual cramps

Manjarrez Camacho and Valdiviezo Campos published the questionnaire study on 2025-04-03. Twenty volunteers aged 15–30 received four 0.85 g gum or confection units containing a preparation described as *Rubus ulmifolius* extract. The paper gives the weight of the entire unit and does not quantify its blackberry extract content. It refers to both leaves and berries and labels the additional ingredient niacin in the abstract and thiamine in Results.

The report describes self-rated menstrual-cramp severity on a 1–5 scale and states that 75% perceived the product as working for their cramps. The pre/post response distributions are aggregate and the reported baseline 41.4% at level 4 does not reconcile with the 10 of 20 depicted in Figure 1. No concurrent untreated or placebo group, blinding or paired participant-level analysis is reported. The authors told volunteers before tasting that the confection was intended as an analgesic. This measurement concerns menstrual cramps after a formulated extract, without a freezing specification or Parkinson cohort. [Manjarrez Camacho primary article](https://ojs.eumed.net/rev/index.php/rilcoDS/article/view/14411mv), [local text](Source-Text/2025-Manjarrez-Blackberry-Extract-Cramps.txt).

## Food frequency and pain in Parkinson cohorts

Guk and colleagues found a pattern-level association between higher Mediterranean diet score and lower odds of pain (OR 0.86, 95% CI 0.75–0.98) in a case–control study with 59 PD participants and 54 controls. A companion publication with the same group sizes and investigators reports two dietary recalls and the MDS-UPDRS Part I pain item. The available analyses provide no specific blackberry consumption and pain severity contrast. Group sizes and site indicate a potentially reused study population. [Local diet-frequency report](Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md), [local recall report](Food-Experiment-Kiwi-and-Caneberries.md), [Guk diet-frequency primary article](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1773331/full), [Guk dietary recall primary article](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/18/8/1302).

The Thailand [cross-sectional Parkinson cohort](Food-Experiment-TCTR-Followup.md) records weekly intake of a grouped berry item and preference for food temperature at the participant level. Its published tables do not identify the species, preparation or a berry–pain analysis.
