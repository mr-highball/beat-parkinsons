# Round 1 olive oil, constipation and clinical source checks

This audit checked 91 exact scopes across 60 original publications and 30 frozen claims. It records 72 scope exclusions and 19 related contexts. One extra-virgin olive oil surgical trial entered the local catalog at original author-abstract depth. The review rechecked the previously cataloged Parkinson whole-diet trial as one family linking its preprint and journal abstract.

The [Bahrami perioperative randomized trial](Source-Text/2025-PMID40642904-Bahrami-EVOO-and-Postoperative-Constipation.txt) reports greater improvement in constipation and pain scores after assigned extra-virgin olive oil than after usual care in cardiac bypass patients. The public abstract does not provide arm estimates, analyzed counts or the conversion of Bristol stool form to the reported constipation score. The [NHANES analysis](Source-Text/2025-PMID40822527-Mediterranean-Score-and-Constipation-in-NHANES.txt) concerns composite diet scores and self-reported usual stool form in the general population. Its association does not isolate olive oil. The [Fernandez Parkinson diet trial](Source-Text/2026-Fernandez-Mediterranean-Counseling-Preprint.txt) reports improvement in both groups and an inconclusive three-month between-group comparison. These new records preserve the extra-virgin oil claim's minimal easing tier.

Thirty-eight pain scopes and eleven apathy scopes concern dried date fruit. The clinical originals distinguish apathy from mood, other neuropsychiatric features and measures of quality of life. None assigned dried date fruit for those Parkinson outcomes. [The resting and action tremor original](Source-Text/2025-PMID41038844-Rest-and-Action-Tremor-After-STN-Stimulation.txt) reports different responses at ten and fifteen years after treatment. Its outcomes are unrelated to raw fig intake.
