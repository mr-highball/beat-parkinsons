# Food experiment follow-up evidence

## Blueberry powder and constipation in older adults

Millar et al. published the Berries and Steps protocol on May 29, 2025. It planned to ask sedentary older adults with depressive symptoms about gastrointestinal symptoms at each visit. ClinicalTrials.gov posted NCT05735587 results on July 30. Constipation was reported in 1 of 15 participants receiving 48 g/day freeze-dried blueberry powder and 0 of 14 receiving placebo. Increased bowel movement frequency was reported in one person per arm. The results give no constipation severity scale or statistical comparison. The separately registered microbiome substudy NCT05817383 was nested in the same trial and does not provide another independent constipation comparison. [2025 protocol](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12937-025-01154-0) · [Local CC BY protocol text](Source-Text/PMC12123748.txt#L221) · [Official results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT05735587) · [Local registry results extract](Source-Text/Trials/NCT05735587-results.txt) · [Nested substudy record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT05817383)

The ABSORB crossover first posted results on December 15, 2025. The record gives zero nonserious adverse events among five participants reported under the 24 g dose and six under the 48 g dose, each given over three days. The safety table reports these denominators by arm. It gives no separate constipation score. Both trials used powder in people without Parkinson’s and provide indirect safety context for [0746, raw blueberry and Parkinson’s constipation](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0746.txt). [ABSORB official results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT05937165) · [2023 registrations](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Blueberry-Followup.md#blueberry-trials-with-planned-gastrointestinal-safety-checks)

## Whole blueberries and adverse events in a pharmacokinetics crossover

Santana et al. first published the NCT04175106 pharmacokinetics study on January 31, 2025. Eighteen healthy adults received a single 150 g serving of Elliott and Olympia blueberries, a blueberry-rich bar, and a matched control beverage in separate sessions. The February 9, 2026 registry results list no serious or nonserious adverse events in any period, with 18 at risk per condition and a 0% reporting threshold. The investigators measured blood and urine metabolites for 48 hours and reported no constipation symptom score. This shows what the short whole-fruit exposure study recorded without resolving [0746, raw blueberry and Parkinson’s constipation](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0746.txt). ## Manuka honey and propolis in a Parkinson’s crossover

Endo and colleagues presented a 12-person randomized crossover at the 2025 Movement Disorders Society congress. The study compared 14-day courses of Manuka honey, propolis, and placebo, with 14-day washouts. The authors reported no significant group differences in daily OFF time or total UPDRS part III. They did not give effect estimates for these outcomes. Cinnamon was outside the intervention, which limits comparison with the 2019 cinnamon and honey case. [Primary congress abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/honey-products-in-parkinsons-disease-randomized-placebo-controlled-double-blind-pilot-study/) · [2022 registration](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#registered-honey-and-propolis-crossover-in-parkinsons-disease) · [2019 cinnamon and honey case](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#cinnamon-powder-and-honey-in-a-parkinsons-case)

## Asparagus stem extract and awakening symptoms

Takahashi and colleagues reported the UMIN000052913 blinded crossover pilot in April 2025. Participants were healthy men with poor sleep. Standardized asparagus stem extract scored better than placebo on the OSA-MA sleepiness-on-rising and refreshment subscales. The article title includes fatigue, while Figure 7 reports sleep questionnaire subscales. The primary sleep EEG comparisons did not show clear differences. The study gives indirect evidence about symptoms on awakening and does not measure Parkinson fatigue after roasted asparagus. The publisher page gives April 9 and the PDF gives April 8, both in the same period. ## Fork-mashable potato and swallowing in older inpatients

Ismael-Mohammed and colleagues assessed six fork-mashable dishes with a modified volume–viscosity swallow test in 20 hospitalized older adults. Each participant tested three dishes. The potato dish produced fewer clinical signs of unsafe swallowing than thin liquid (p < .05), but more oral residue than thin or thickened liquid (p < .05). The test was an immediate assessment in older inpatients with swallowing problems. It supplies no isolated baked-potato result in Parkinson’s (`0290`). [Primary report, Figures 3–4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12111229/)

The potato dish used potato flakes, virgin oil, thickener and salt in 5, 10 and 20 g boluses. The graphs show about 90% safe swallowing for the potato dish and about 54% for thin liquid, with oral residue in about 60% for the potato dish and 0% in both liquid conditions. These values are readings from the figures. The supplement has aggregate graphs and food images. The authors did not report the number of Parkinson participants and make individual data available on request subject to privacy and ethics restrictions. [Recipe and data statement](https://www.mdpi.com/2304-8158/14/10/1723) · [Swallow safety figure](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC12111229.1/foods-14-01723-g003.jpg) · [Residue figure](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC12111229.1/foods-14-01723-g004.jpg) · [Companion dissertation and proposed Parkinson validation](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/tesis/2025/hdl_10803_695838/kmii1de1.pdf#page=159)

## Olive oil and Parkinson's incidence in the E3N cohort

The French E3N cohort analysis included 71,542 women and 845 incident Parkinson’s cases. In Table 4A, the overall hazard ratio for olive-oil intake at or above the median versus below was 0.95 (95% CI 0.83–1.09), and the hazard ratio before age 71 was 0.81 (95% CI 0.65–1.01). The analysis concerns incident diagnosis. Baseline constipation and depression are used in sensitivity analyses. The exposure lacks preparation detail and the study does not provide longitudinal symptom estimates for extra-virgin olive oil and constipation (`0072`) or cold-pressed olive oil and anxiety (`0343`). [Primary cohort report, Tables 4A–4B](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13011782/)

## MiniMeal mango or passion fruit drink and depression questionnaire

The MiniMeal study assigned adults aged 70 and older to two daily products made from bioprocessed oat bran and chickpea, lower-fiber reference products, or usual diet. The products were tomato soup and a mango or passion fruit drink. The drink's fruit and the products' other ingredients and nutrient content were not assigned separately. The protocol excludes diagnosed neurodegenerative disease and measures anxiety and depression with the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale. Physical tests include single-leg stance and a chair-stand test. Fall frequency is outside the planned battery. Data collection was complete by February 27, 2026, with 114 randomized and 106 completing all visits. The protocol and registry provide no outcome estimates. This is a dietary-program lead for dried mango and depression (`0232`) and a preparation and outcome near match for cooked oats and falls (`0095`). [Primary protocol, PMID 42752400, Table 1 and outcome schedule](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13631491/) · [2024 registration](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#minimeal-food-product-trial-registration)

## Fresh fruit frequency and constipation in Fox Insight

Fernandez and Lobato analyzed responses from 416 people with Parkinson’s disease. The adjusted odds ratio for the authors’ constipation category was 2.07 for daily versus less frequent fresh fruit intake (95% CI 1.36–3.15). The methods classified all non-daily bowel-frequency responses and missing responses as constipation, despite also citing a fewer-than-three-per-week threshold. The questionnaire pooled fresh fruit and did not identify orange or blueberry intake, assess constipation severity, or establish whether diet preceded symptoms. The association is indirect context for raw blueberry (`0746`) and raw orange (`0878`). [Primary paper, DOI 10.34024/rnc.2025.v33.20260](https://periodicos.unifesp.br/index.php/neurociencias/article/view/20260) · [Methods and Tables 3–4](https://periodicos.unifesp.br/index.php/neurociencias/article/download/20260/14372)

## Whole Ajwa dates and schizophrenia symptom ratings

Syamsuddin and colleagues compared risperidone plus whole Ajwa dates with risperidone alone in 60 men hospitalized with schizophrenia. The single-masked report found lower negative symptom subscale scores at weeks four, six, and eight in the added-date group. The subscale includes behaviors related to motivation and social withdrawal alongside other domains. Table 3 gives a mean eight-week drop of 5.87 points (SD 1.91) in the added-date group and 3.53 (SD 1.31) in the control group. Its p value is printed as .000. The paper supplies no separate PANSS N4 passive/apathetic social withdrawal result. The repository for the matching 2024 thesis shows public metadata from September 2025 and a full-text restriction through October 29, 2027. [Thesis metadata](https://repository.unhas.ac.id/id/eprint/49734/) · [Local thesis source record](Source-Text/2025-Firdaus-Ajwa-Thesis-Public-Metadata.txt) A separate 44-participant article from the same clinical setting reported quality of life and inflammatory markers. Participant overlap is unclear. These reports provide indirect context for `0758`, which predicts worse apathy from dried dates in Parkinson’s disease. [Negative symptom report, PMID 41070421](https://www.rivistadipsichiatria.it/archivio/4583/articoli/45902/) · [Quality of life report, PMID 40170205](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40170205/)

The 2026 Ketoflex 12/3 randomized Parkinson’s trial included the Starkstein Apathy Scale. The intervention combined personalized food choices with time-restricted eating. The published Methods give no dried-date intake or date-specific subgroup, and the apathy results contain discrepant comparisons in the text and table. ## Date palm and fig preparations in animal models

Henry and colleagues reported better motor performance and dopamine measurements after an oral date palm fraction in a 35-rat rotenone study. The 2025 conference abstract and journal supplement share authors, title, and model. Treat them as one study. The fraction and model leave pain and apathy after eating dried dates unresolved. [Primary conference abstract SP22_4](https://sona2025.uca.ma/abstract_sympo/SP22_4.pdf) · [Journal supplement DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroscience.2025.05.133)

Kehili and colleagues reported smaller paw diameter in mice after seven days of oral Degla or Homayra date syrup in a formalin-induced edema model. The study assessed swelling and histology. The acute toxicity observation monitored drowsiness and lethargy, with no apparent toxicity reported. Neither assay provides a clinical pain or apathy measure. A 2020 conference abstract by the group also describes the Homayra cultivar in a formalin model. Overlap is unverified. [2026 primary study, Methods, Table 2 and Figure 5](https://jjbs.hu.edu.jo/files/vol19/n1/Paper%20Number%209.pdf) · [2020 primary abstract PC500](https://mpm-2020dz.sciencesconf.org/data/MPM_2020.pdf)

The results section describes a bark formulation, while the discussion also describes a fruit-extract experiment. ## Lithuanian food-frequency and Mediterranean diet study

Guk and colleagues compared 59 people with Parkinson’s disease with 54 controls. The Parkinson’s group reported fewer monthly potato servings, with a median of 12 versus 13, and the food-frequency tables showed higher poultry and black tea intake and lower caffeinated coffee intake. The within-Parkinson symptom regressions used the overall Mediterranean diet score. They estimated lower odds of pain, urinary dysfunction, constipation, and anxiety at higher adherence. The design supplies case–control food frequencies and pattern-level symptom estimates. The individual preparation and symptom combinations in the potato, coffee, chicken, and black tea claims remain untested by these comparisons. [Primary paper, PMID 41878573, Table 3 and supplement](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1773331/full)

## Reported caffeine intake and Parkinson’s fatigue

Alshimemeri et al.’s January 17, 2026 cross-sectional survey assessed 131 Saudi adults with Parkinson’s using the Arabic Fatigue Severity Scale (FSS). Table 4 reports FSS ≥ 4 for 60.7% of 107 frequent caffeine users and 62.5% of 24 infrequent users (OR 0.94, 95% CI 0.37–2.35, p = 1.000). Frequent use meant daily or several days per week. Infrequent use meant once or twice per week. The table gives no beverage, brewing method, dose, or separate FSS means by exposure. The estimate is imprecise and does not isolate the brewed coffee or brewed green tea in `0121` and `0948`. Broad fruit and vegetable frequency also gave an inconclusive association (irregular versus regular OR 0.80, 95% CI 0.40–1.70, p = .755). [Primary article abstract, PMID 41546733](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41546733/) · [Original Table 4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10072-025-08597-7/tables/4)

Table 4 also reports FSS ≥ 4 in 67.4% of 95 participants in the lower water intake group versus 44.4% of 36 in the higher group (OR 2.5, 95% CI 1.16–5.57, p = .020). The lower reported intake was associated with higher fatigue prevalence in the same survey. The cross-sectional comparison gives no change in fatigue after changing water intake. Table 4 labels the higher group as more than four cups per day. The supplementary reporting checklist describes it as at least four cups. The checklist reports a 26.2% response rate (131 of 500 invited) and gives no additional beverage outcomes or questionnaire. Figure 1 plots fatigue by exercise frequency. [Original Table 4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10072-025-08597-7/tables/4) · [Supplementary reporting checklist](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1007%2Fs10072-025-08597-7/MediaObjects/10072_2025_8597_MOESM1_ESM.doc) · [Original Figure 1](https://media.springernature.com/full/springer-static/image/art%3A10.1007%2Fs10072-025-08597-7/MediaObjects/10072_2025_8597_Fig1_HTML.png)

## Annonaceae and motor severity in clinically defined Parkinson’s disease

A 2026 comparison included 74 Caribbean and 104 mainland French participants diagnosed with Parkinson’s disease. Nearly all interviewed Caribbean participants reported Annonaceae exposure, compared with none of the 20 interviewed mainland participants. In an adjusted comparison within the Caribbean group, participants with exposure above the study’s two fruit-or-cup-year threshold had motor examination scores 8.3 points higher on UPDRS-III (95% CI 1.2 to 15). The study also found cognitive differences across the regional groups. Retrospective diet reporting, strongly differing regional exposure, and residual confounding limit causal attribution. Cohort independence from the earlier Caribbean report is uncertain. This follow-up covers an exposure absent from the frozen ingredient pool. [Primary paper, PMID 42192274](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13212262/)

## Total protein intake and falls

Lima and colleagues compared dietary protein and recent falls in 117 people with mild to moderate Parkinson’s. In the lower-protein group, 17 of 41 participants reported at least one fall in the previous six months, compared with 29 of 76 in the normal-protein group (p = 0.727). For two or more falls, the counts were 8 of 41 and 22 of 76 (p = 0.265). This cross-sectional study supplies an inconclusive comparison of falls by total protein intake. It did not report a shrimp-specific exposure or a change in falls after modifying protein intake. The [boiled shrimp claim, 0322](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0322.txt) remains unresolved. [Primary report, Table 4, PMID 40693199](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12277168/)

A separate 2026 case–control study reported weekly shellfish consumption in 21 of 60 people with Parkinson’s and 12 of 60 controls. Table 2 defines shellfish as snails and clams. The study assessed hair metal levels and gives no result for shrimp preparation or falls. [Primary report, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2026.114942)

## Ajwa seed and fruit pulp extracts in mice under chronic stress

Asdaq and colleagues’ November 12, 2025 study tested separate oral methanolic Ajwa seed and fruit-pulp extracts in mice exposed to chronic mild stress. Figure 4 shows sucrose preference at 17% in stressed controls after three weeks, approximately 35% and 45% with the two pulp-extract doses, and approximately 30% and 46% with the two seed-extract doses. The Results report p < 0.01 for the lower seed dose and p < 0.001 for the other three comparisons with stressed controls. Sucrose preference is a proxy for anhedonia in this rodent stress model, and the preparations are solvent extracts. ## Freeze-dried Barhi and Khassab date flesh in a healthy crossover

Altuwairki and colleagues’ July 22, 2026 paper reports results from the same 35-person crossover first reported in the Newcastle dissertation in 2023. Participants ate freeze-dried pitted flesh equivalent to 115 g fresh fruit in yogurt or a sugar- and fiber-matched yogurt control. ## Coffee chlorogenic acid drink and fatigue on waking in healthy workers

## Dietary recalls and symptom subgroups in the Vilnius cohort

Guk and colleagues' April 20, 2026 report analyzed two 24-hour dietary recalls in 59 people with Parkinson’s and 54 controls. Among the Parkinson participants, the fatigue subgroup reported higher total fat (58.79 vs 47.39 g/day) and polyunsaturated fat (5.55% vs 4.39% of energy). The reported age-, sex- and disease-duration-adjusted p-values were .047 and .018. The depression subgroup reported lower dietary fiber (20.97 vs 29.91 g/day, adjusted p = .013).
