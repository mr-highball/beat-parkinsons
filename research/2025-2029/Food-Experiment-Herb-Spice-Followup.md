# Herb and spice evidence follow-up, 2025–2029

## Plasma piperine and motor scores in PPMI

Dong et al. first published on December 19, 2025. Their 455-person PPMI analysis included 303 people with Parkinson disease and 152 controls. In the original Table S2 workbook, the only listed piperine–symptom correlation is for MDS-UPDRS Part III in people with both GBA1 and LRRK2 variants (Pearson r = −0.466, p = .108, n = 13 observations as tabulated). The same subgroup’s additive model has p = .151. The paper reports lower baseline piperine in the LRRK2 Parkinson, GBA1 Parkinson and prodromal LRRK2 groups versus controls. The estimates concern plasma piperine and a composite motor examination or diagnosis. There is no reported black-pepper intake, specific symptom or preparation contrast. The earlier Xia reports also draw from PPMI and do not establish independent samples. [Primary report, Figure 4 and Methods](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27010016) · [Original Table S2 workbook in supplement archive](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC12785599.1/ijms-27-00016-s001.zip) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC12785599.txt#L228) · [Earlier PPMI reports](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Spice-Followup.md#plasma-piperine-and-curcumin-in-the-ppmi-cohort).

## Gingerol-enriched extract in neuropathic pain models

Mendóza and colleagues administered a gingerol-enriched ginger extract in a 27-male-rat spinal nerve ligation experiment. The treated group increased open-arm time, distance and entries on the elevated plus maze, a behavioral proxy for anxiety. The disease model is neuropathic pain and the 2026 paper's behavioral endpoint is the maze. Earlier reports by the same research group used sensory and pain-associated behaviors in the same type of nerve-injury model. These studies used enriched extracts in rats, which differs from grated ginger in people with Parkinson’s (`0725`, `0937`). [Primary 2026 report, Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12787805/) · [Earlier primary rat studies](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#ginger-root-extracts-in-rat-neuropathic-pain-studies)

## Isolated 6-shogaol and levodopa in a mouse follow-up

Kim and colleagues’ June 30, 2026 report describes 20 mice, five per group. The levodopa groups showed improved pole performance compared with untreated model mice, without a reported additional improvement from synthetic 6-shogaol. The combined group also had shorter bead expulsion time, while the levodopa-alone group showed no clear difference from the model group on that measure. Figure 1 labels comparisons with the untreated model and does not give the combination-versus-levodopa effect estimate. The 2024 report used 29 mice across five groups under the same ethics approval, and the papers do not specify whether any animals overlapped. The 2026 paper discusses levodopa-related dyskinesia as background. It reports no dyskinesia, stride-length, or anxiety measure. [Primary report, Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13324408/) · [Earlier study](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#isolated-6-shogaol-in-two-parkinson-animal-models)

## Pooled curcuminoid trials in knee osteoarthritis

The investigators pooled 123 participants from two randomized trials in a 2026 conference abstract. The trial of turmeric-rhizome curcuminoids plus acupressure contributed 63 participants and the trial of curcuminoids alone contributed 60. The between-group difference in pain change was −6.1 points (95% CI −12.2 to 0.1, p = .052). The abstract gives no pain contrast separately for the curcuminoid-only study. A 2025 Indonesian registration describes a curcuminoid-only study with the same intervention and outcomes, but the abstract gives no registration identifier for that trial. These studies used extracted curcuminoids in knee osteoarthritis, an indirect comparison for ground turmeric and Parkinson’s pain (`0812`). [Primary IHINC 2026 abstract ID 51, page 56](https://fliphtml5.com/llata/Abstrak-Book-IHINC-2026-HPHI/56/) · [Curcuminoid-only registry candidate, NCT07183215](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07183215) · [Combined intervention protocol and dissertation](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#curcuminoids-plus-acupressure-in-knee-osteoarthritis)

## Cinnamon in an abbreviated smell test

The 2025 analysis compared subsets of smell-identification stimuli using three existing cohorts. Cinnamon was among seven odors that approximated the diagnostic performance of the full kits for Parkinson’s disease or dementia with Lewy bodies versus healthy controls. The same report discusses coffee as an odor and cherry as an incorrect response option. The study assessed existing smell impairment. It did not assign cinnamon, coffee, or cherry ingestion and measure symptom change. [Primary report, PMID 40268961, Figures 3–6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12019603/) · [Local full text](Source-Text/PMC12019603.txt)

## Cardamom odor as an assessment stimulus

Nagaraj et al. compared graded and binary scoring of an Indian smell identification test in people with Parkinson’s and controls. In the first phase, 78 of 129 people with Parkinson’s identified cardamom odor, compared with 120 of 129 controls. The study used the scent to measure olfactory performance and did not measure olfactory change after ingestion. This is an assessment lead for [0550, ground cardamom and olfactory impairment](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0550.txt). [Primary report, Table 3, PMID 42262828](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13349266/)

## Cinnamon odor in early-onset Parkinson disease and other hyposmia

In the 2026 Larina congress report, 123 of 156 people with early-onset Parkinson disease met the Sniffin’ Sticks hyposmia criterion. Identification was correct for 43.9% on cinnamon, 41.0% on grapefruit, 42.9% on pineapple and 81.4% on orange. Orange, grapefruit, coffee and rose identification scores were associated with disease duration in this cross-sectional sample. The abstract reports no cinnamon-specific duration estimate, dietary intake or test after eating cinnamon. A search by title and authors found the congress record. [Larina primary abstract and figure](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/odor-specific-olfactory-dysfunction-in-early-onset-parkinsons-disease/)

The cited April 2025 Mitchell study compared existing hyposmia in participants with and without Parkinson disease. Methods specify 155 per group in the model discovery set and 39 per group in independent validation. Cinnamon and pine item differences survived multiple-comparison adjustment. In the validation set the best model correctly classified up to 63% of the balanced sample. Recognition is the measured response, and ground cinnamon ingestion was outside the design. [Mitchell primary report and local text](Source-Text/PMC11971265.txt#L117)


## Lemongrass essential oil and chronic Parkinson’s pain

Aoyama et al. reported a 2025 single-arm crossover case series of four people with Parkinson’s and chronic pain. Each participant received three weeks each of lemongrass essential-oil inhalation, massage with jojoba oil, and massage with lemongrass essential oil in jojoba oil. Both massage conditions reduced reported pain during and immediately after treatment. The investigators reported no significant change with inhalation alone and no significant difference between the two massage conditions during or immediately after treatment. Their exploratory model favored aroma massage over massage alone at one hour. Sample size, lack of blinding, and possible carryover substantially limit interpretation. [Primary report, Methods and Tables 11–14](https://www.mj-med-u-tokai.com/pdf/500303.pdf) · [PubMed, PMID 41250562](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41250562/)

The local [literature index](Literature-Index.tsv) listed the report without identifying lemongrass in the title. The ingredient and delivery route are reported in the full text. This is related clinical evidence for lemongrass as an aromatic exposure. The frozen lemongrass claims concern consuming brewed or minced preparations and cover other endpoints, including [0406, daytime sleepiness](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0406.txt) and [0753, insomnia](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0753.txt).

The 2025 series also reports unexpected daytime sleep in PDQ-39 item 30. One participant scored 2 before and after every condition, and the other three scored 0 throughout. Table 13 shows that nocturnal pain decreased from 8 to 2 in two participants during aroma massage and stayed at 6 in the other two. These observations concern aromatic and topical exposure. The daytime item gives four unchanged observations with three baseline floor scores for `0406`. Nocturnal pain and the separate PDQ-39 item combining distressing dreams and hallucinations do not provide a measure of insomnia severity for `0753`. [Primary report, Table 13 on printed p. 95 and Table 15 on printed p. 97](https://mj-med-u-tokai.com/pdf/500303.pdf#page=11)

The UMIN registry lists a 2026 open-label randomized comparison of aromatherapy massage with usual care, UMIN000062783. The primary outcome is change on a pain visual analogue scale at three weeks, and the secondary outcome field is empty. Both public versions identify jojoba as an example carrier oil in the eligibility text and give no essential-oil species or sleep endpoint. The current record lists 28 enrolled and September 30, 2026 as the scheduled end of follow-up. The results fields were empty at review on November 12, 2026. [Official bilingual UMIN registration](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000065252) · [Registry history](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/icdr/ctr_his_list.cgi?recptno=R000065252) · [Japanese ministry record](https://rctportal.mhlw.go.jp/detail/um?trial_id=UMIN000062783)

J-GLOBAL and the nursing journal index also list a March 2025 Japanese report by Aoyama, Nagata, and Okubo about aromatherapy massage and chronic pain in Parkinson’s. A 2025 composition paper cites earlier lemongrass work from the group and reports the same pain means as the Tokai report. The March report’s full text was unavailable through the indexed routes checked, so its participant overlap with the December report could not be determined. It supplies no independently counted effect estimate here. [March report metadata](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=202502247924611955) · [Composition paper, DOI 10.11425/sst.14.37](https://doi.org/10.11425/sst.14.37)

The Kyorin University project report for fiscal year 2023 describes the same three three-week crossover periods and the same modeled mean pain scores of 6.42 cm before aroma massage and 0.25 cm during or immediately after. The matching design and estimates link the university report to the published case series. [Kyorin University report, printed pp. 8–12](https://www.kyorin-u.ac.jp/univ/area/activities/archives/pdf/2023chikisogo_kiyou.pdf)

## Repeated capsaicin in older adults with oropharyngeal dysphagia

Tomsen et al. reported a double-blind randomized study with 51 older adults with oropharyngeal dysphagia, 32 allocated to capsaicin solution and 19 to placebo. In the capsaicin arm, the mean penetration–aspiration score changed from 4.7 to 3.9 over two weeks, with a within-arm p-value of 0.02. The placebo arm showed no significant improvement in the reported swallowing measures. The accessible abstract does not give a direct between-group effect estimate or a Parkinson-specific analysis. A 2023 conference abstract with the same authors, group sizes and reported values appears to describe the same study. [Primary trial abstract, PMID 41147275](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41147275/) · [Conference abstracts, record NGS18153-90](https://doi.org/10.1111/nmo.14637)

This supplies a swallowing physiology lead for [0724, ground cayenne and swallowing difficulty](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0724.txt). The different preparation and unverified Parkinson subgroup leave the culinary claim unresolved. The earlier capsaicin wafer trial separately reported no statistically significant swallowing-timing effect in its Parkinson subgroup. [Earlier Parkinson subgroup summary](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#capsaicin-wafer-and-swallowing)

## Oral cayenne extract and suppression of aerosol-induced cough

Wüst studied 25 healthy adults in a repeated-measures bachelor’s project submitted in September 2024 and posted publicly in March 2025. All 25 initially coughed at their individually established aerosol threshold. After drinking a liquid cayenne extract, 23 of 25 temporarily stopped responding at that same threshold. Twelve participants had suppression for less than five minutes and two had none. The stated mean of 16.25 minutes conflicts with the distribution in Figure 7, so the count and interval data are more interpretable. The study used oral liquid extract and measured cough provocation in healthy participants. The clinical effects on swallowing after eating ground cayenne in Parkinson’s (`0724`) are unresolved. [Primary bachelor’s thesis, Methods, Table 5 and Figure 7](https://dysphagie-netzwerk-suedwest.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/BA_Wuest_CPS.pdf) · [Host publication metadata](https://dysphagie-netzwerk-suedwest.de/wp-json/wp/v2/media/2497)

## Pharmaceutical butylphthalide and composite motor outcomes

Chen and Hu retrospectively compared 100 older adults with mid- to late-stage Parkinson’s receiving levodopa/benserazide, 50 of whom also received DL-3-n-butylphthalide soft capsules. Both groups improved on motor measures over one year. The authors reported lower UPDRS Part III and Hoehn and Yahr scores in the adjunct group at follow-up. Treatment selection and other confounding limit interpretation. The abstract reports aggregate motor outcomes and no separate bradykinesia estimate. This is related to [0275, fresh celery leaf and bradykinesia](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0275.txt) through the compound’s historical identification in celery seed. The study used a pharmaceutical capsule. [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.11669/cpj.2025.02.012) · [Earlier primary report describing the compound’s origin](https://doi.org/10.3892/etm.2019.7397)

A 2026 study retrospectively compared 119 people with Parkinson’s-related cognitive impairment who took donepezil and levodopa/benserazide, 64 of whom also took pharmaceutical butylphthalide. The adjunct group had higher overall MoCA and lower UPDRS II and III scores at follow-up, with comparable reported adverse-event incidence. Exploratory analyses found no clear differences by baseline MoCA or Hoehn and Yahr stage. The study did not report executive function or bradykinesia separately. It adds indirect evidence for [0164, dried celery leaf and executive function](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0164.txt) and [0275, fresh celery leaf and bradykinesia](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0275.txt). [Primary report, Figures 1–2, PMID 42923556](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42923556/)

## Eugenol as one component of olfactory training

Dou et al. randomized participants with Parkinson’s to six months of olfactory training or standard care. Seventy completed the study. The training used four separate odorants, including eugenol as clove scent. The trial reported a more favorable composite smell score and higher general cognition scores with training, and no clear difference on total MDS-UPDRS or motor Part III. Figure 5 shows lower anxiety scores after training on HAMA and GAD-7, consistent with the abstract and discussion. The results text describes the direction as higher. This multi-odor intervention gives no estimate for the effect of eugenol by itself or of eating ground clove, as specified by [0788, ground clove and anxiety](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0788.txt) and [0648, ground clove and executive function](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0648.txt). The cognitive outcomes were general scales. [Primary report, Figures 4–6, PMID 41477097](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12750363/) · [NLM Figure 5](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC12750363.1/gr5.jpg)

## Perilla and ginger formula use in Korean insurance records

A 2025 analysis identified 10,060 people with Parkinson’s who received insured herbal medicines during 2014–2020. The perilla and ginger formula Banhahubak-tang was among frequently prescribed preparations in the group diagnosed more than seven years earlier. The study’s administrative outcomes were dispensing and prescription duration. It does not estimate effects of that formula or its ingredients on any frozen symptom. ## Oral coriander leaf preparation in mouse models

The 2025 coriander study tested an oral *Coriandrum sativum* leaf preparation in two mouse models. The investigators reported reduced motor deficits and inflammatory measures in both models. The design supplies animal evidence for this preparation, adjacent to [0679, dried cilantro and walking slowness](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0679.txt), with no human data or separate estimate for culinary dried cilantro. ## Distilled Japanese pepper in experimental Parkinson models

A July 17, 2026 study by Kim and colleagues tested a distilled extract of Japanese pepper or Korean chopi (*Zanthoxylum piperitum*) in toxin-based worm and mouse models. The mouse intervention was administered intranasally and began before toxin exposure. It prolonged rota-rod latency and shortened pole descent time compared with the toxin-only group. These are model outcomes for coordination and movement speed. The study used a related *Zanthoxylum* species and a distilled preparation administered by a different route from culinary Sichuan pepper. It provides indirect leads for [0654, bradykinesia](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0654.txt) and [0827, postural instability](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0827.txt). ## Stepped herbal formulas and insomnia outside Parkinson’s

Tang and colleagues’ 2025 protocol lists cinnamon bark among the ingredients of the insomnia tisane and one standardized combination. Fresh ginger rhizome appears in the standardized Gui Pi Tang formula. Assignment and escalation were by clinical presentation and district start time. Yu and colleagues presented insomnia outcomes from the same registration at World Sleep in September 2025. Among 366 adults with insomnia, the reported Insomnia Severity Index change after the initial combined herbal treatment was −3.38 points (95% CI −4.03 to −2.73). Among 49 patients escalating to personalized herbs with psychotherapy, the reported change was −0.90 (−2.49 to 0.69). The abstract gives no separate cinnamon or ginger estimates and does not identify the reported changes as between-cluster contrasts. The trial excludes Parkinson’s. It offers formulation context for `0243` and `0937`, with no reported ingredient-specific anxiety outcome. [2024 registration](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#registered-stepped-herbal-care-for-sleep-and-mental-health) · [February 2025 primary preprint protocol](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5126538) · [Protocol Tables 1 and 2](Source-Text/PMC12536410.txt#L183) · [Primary World Sleep 2025 results, printed pp. 291–292](https://worldsleepsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/World-Sleep-2025_Abstracts_02032026.pdf)

## Cinnamon leaf pretreatment and catalepsy in rats

In 2026, Lee and colleagues compared *Cinnamomum osmophloeum* leaf nanosuspension, hydrosol, and powder in water, starting each before rotenone exposure. The week-nine Figure 10 bars show 26.1 seconds in the model group and 23.7 in the powder group. The same statistical letter marks both, so the reported 9.5% reduction is inconclusive. The nanosuspension and hydrosol groups have different statistical letters from the model group at week nine. A distinct 2025 amyloid-beta infusion study used cinnamon leaf products and reported memory and striatal dopamine findings. These preparations and animal endpoints are indirect for ground cinnamon and muscle rigidity (`0146`). [Primary 2026 report, Figure 10, PMID 41750576](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12938807/) · [2025 amyloid-beta rat report, PMID 41012536](Source-Text/PMC12473313.txt#L67) · [2023 rotenone study](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#cinnamon-leaf-preparations-and-catalepsy-in-rats)

## Isolated curcumin in fly and mouse movement assays

In a 2025 rotenone fly study, seven-day curcumin co-exposure produced similar climbing activity and lethality to rotenone alone. The separate longer pretreatment experiment had high mortality and reported dopaminergic cell counts among survivors. In a 2026 mouse study with six animals per group, end-of-study rotarod latency was 76.7 seconds with MPTP alone, 150.3 with curcumin, 179.2 with resveratrol, 210.7 with both compounds and 243.0 with levodopa/carbidopa. These studies tested isolated constituents. They supply model locomotor outcomes and do not establish clinical bradykinesia (`0507`) or rigidity (`0359`, `0881`) effects of turmeric or grape. [2025 primary fly report, Results and Supplementary Figure S4](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0322640) · [2026 primary mouse report, Tables 1 and 2](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.103681) · [Human nanomicelle curcumin trial](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#nanomicelle-curcumin-as-an-add-on)

## Curcumin-associated microbiota transplant and motor assays

Li and colleagues’ *Acta Anatomica Sinica* report lists an April 6, 2025 publication date and June 16 online release. The authors compared a fecal microbiota transplant from curcumin-treated MPTP-model mice with other transplant and model groups. The original abstract reports a shorter pole descent time (p < .05), longer rotarod retention (p < .01) and longer time moving in the center of the open field (p < .05) for MPTP-model recipients of the curcumin-associated microbiota. The original abstract gives no numerical effect sizes or group sample sizes. The manipulated exposure in those recipients was the transplant. These movement outcomes leave the culinary turmeric statements unresolved. [Primary original, DOI 10.16098/j.issn.0529-1356.2025.02.003](https://www.aats.ac.cn/CN/10.16098/j.issn.0529-1356.2025.02.003) · [Earlier direct isolated-curcumin model experiments](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#purified-curcumin-in-gut-microbiota-and-movement-experiments)

## Sweet basil extract in a rotenone model

Gayakwad et al. compared injected *Ocimum basilicum* extract with no added treatment in a rotenone rat model. On day 28 the higher extract group stayed on the rotarod for a mean 144.6 seconds versus 57.3 seconds in the rotenone-only group and climbed the stairs in 29.3 versus 35.8 seconds. The paper's narrative and Tables 1–2 disagree on statistical significance at the lower extract level. The paper calls the tasks a measure of motor impairment and gives no separate rigidity or clinical postural assessment. The results are indirect for [0292, fresh basil and rigidity](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0292.txt) and [0696, fresh basil and postural instability](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0696.txt). [Primary report, Methods and Tables 1–2](https://www.ijam.co.in/index.php/ijam/article/download/6636/1615/13600)

## Rosemary extract and depression during methadone maintenance

Heidarzadeh and Banna Zadeh Mahani reported a double-blind rosemary extract capsule versus placebo trial in adults receiving methadone maintenance. The paper describes 54 enrolled and 47 completing. The full report found no clear between-group difference on the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale depression subscale in Figure 1B, despite the abstract’s favorable wording. Figure 1C shows a favorable anxiety comparison. The depression result is indirect for [0128, fresh rosemary and depressive symptoms in Parkinson’s](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0128.txt). [Primary report, Results and Figure 1](https://www.mental-wellness-journal.net/mw/article/download/36/32)

The report was first published March 19, 2026 as DOI 10.4081/mw.2026.36. It gives 26 rosemary and 21 placebo completers and describes dried, standardized rosemary powder in capsules. Its abstract states 500 mg daily for eight weeks, while its methods state 500 mg twice daily for four weeks. Methods call the study a crossover, but the results present separate groups without describing treatment periods or washout. Figure 1B displays bars without exact numerical depression scores or confidence intervals; the results text reports no significant between-group difference before or after treatment. These reporting conflicts and the small, non-Parkinson sample limit interpretation. [Primary report, Methods, Results and Figure 1](https://www.mental-wellness-journal.net/mw/article/download/36/32)

## Formulated turmeric and post-exercise muscle soreness

Schönenberger and colleagues' October 3, 2025 original report is PMID 41040018, PMCID PMC12498372, DOI 10.1080/15502783.2025.2568048. The NCT04946981 registration first posted July 1, 2021 before the reported July 12 start. Registry enrollment was 45. The article reports 44 moderately active adults (34 men and 10 women) and documents one additional randomized male participant who withdrew before product intake. OAP36_02 repeats this cohort in the Cambridge IUNS supplement published in July 2026. [Primary article, Sections 2.3 and 3.1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12498372/) · [Trial registration](../2020-2024/Source-Text/Trials/NCT04946981.txt) · [Original conference collection, OAP36_02](https://doi.org/10.1017/jns.2026.10126)

In the double-blind crossover, adults took a 300 mg daily capsule of Turmipure Gold or placebo for five days. The capsule was a dried colloidal suspension of turmeric extract with quillaja extract, sunflower oil and acacia gum, with a nominal minimum 30% curcuminoids. The paper reports a mean of 101.2 mg measured total curcuminoids and 86.6 mg curcumin per capsule in a ten-capsule assay. Participants ran downhill during each period. The main male analysis was powered for the 72-hour area under the curve for quadriceps soreness after squatting. The AUC and comparisons at individual time points did not clearly differ. The paper identifies the maximum-to-72-hour recovery contrast as post hoc. It favored the formulation by 10.7 percentage points in men (SE 4.3, p=.0184) and 7.9 percentage points overall (SE 3.6, p=.0346). The published results use sensitivity models without the original analysis plan's pre-exercise covariate, because those baseline values followed the initial dose. [Primary article, Sections 2.3.2, 2.4, 2.5.3–2.5.4 and 3.2, Figure 2 and Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12498372/)

Givaudan France Naturals funded the trial. Company authors contributed to design and writing. The report says independent contract research organizations conducted the study and analyses. Randomization and visually matched capsules strengthen the comparison. The post hoc contrast, industry involvement, few women with exploratory analysis, acute induced-soreness setting and proprietary formulation limit interpretation for claim 0812. The trial gives indirect pain context without estimating the effect of culinary ground turmeric in Parkinson disease. Count the registry, journal article and conference abstract once.
