# Bay leaf symptom evidence

## Fatigue during probable liver injury associated with laurel leaf infusion

Soare and colleagues’ January 22, 2026 case report describes a 55-year-old woman who started regularly drinking an infusion made from crushed dried *Laurus nobilis* leaves. After approximately three months she had asthenia and fatigue, anorexia, abdominal symptoms and abnormal liver enzymes. Clinical and biochemical improvement followed cessation. The authors judged the overall association with liver injury probable on updated RUCAM (8), with continued abnormalities on later monitoring. The case gives no fatigue severity score, isolated culinary ground-leaf exposure, or Parkinson diagnosis. The adverse symptom occurred in the setting of probable liver injury and provides an opposing lead for 0992. ## Four-week randomized safety study of standardized leaf extract

Kobayashi and colleagues reported a July 20, 2026 double-blind placebo-controlled study in healthy adults. The accessible study abstract gives 12 participants assigned to LAURESH leaf extract at 540 mg/day and 10 assigned to placebo for four weeks. It reports zero adverse events in both groups and no between-group difference in the frequency of blood or urine values moving outside reference ranges. No fatigue instrument or item-specific results appear in the accessible abstract. The exposure was a standardized extract and the small sample can miss uncommon adverse effects. The study gives context for the prolonged crushed-leaf tea case and for claim 0992. Three similar August 2024 registrations from the same organizations use unnamed products. Their linkage to this study remains unconfirmed. ## Laurel leaf extract safety comparison in healthy adults

Kobayashi and colleagues published a separate blinded four-week safety comparison on July 20, 2026. The available abstract reports 12 active and 10 placebo participants and a primary adverse-event occurrence outcome, with no adverse events reported. Blood and urine outcomes were secondary.
