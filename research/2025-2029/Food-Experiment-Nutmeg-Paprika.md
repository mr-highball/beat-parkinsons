# Nutmeg and paprika evidence

## Swiss poison-center series and somnolence after nutmeg overdose

Bleisch and colleagues published a case and a 2014–2023 poison-center analysis in May 2026. Their case describes a man taking long-term fluoxetine and quetiapine who became somnolent about 14 hours after deliberate nutmeg overdose and recovered within 26 hours. Of 236 enquiries, 33 had documented medical follow-up. Eight of those had no effects, 16 had minor effects, and nine had moderate effects that included stupor, hallucinations, agitation or myoclonus. The study concerns acute poisoning and gives no usual culinary or Parkinson daytime-sleepiness estimate. [Primary full text](Source-Text/PMC13206746.txt)

## Spicy foods and urgency urinary incontinence

Ha and colleagues compared 651 cases of urgency urinary incontinence with 8,890 controls. Their reported adjusted associations did not identify spicy foods as a statistically significant factor. The exposure grouped spicy foods, and the comparison concerned urinary incontinence rather than the number of urgency episodes after eating smoked paprika. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1097/SPV.0000000000001652)

## Registered paprika xanthophyll and exercise blood pressure

UMIN000056794 first disclosed on January 27, 2025 a double-blind trial of a soft capsule containing 9 mg daily of paprika xanthophyll versus placebo for eight weeks in healthy adults aged 30–59. A fatigue visual analog scale is primary and ramp-test blood pressure is secondary. UMIN lists 23 enrolled, with no arm counts. The ministry mirror's 30 is the target. The listed last follow-up was April 11, 2025, and the December 2 update still records unpublished results. Targeted identity, sponsor, dose and investigator searches did not verify a matching publication. The intervention differs from culinary paprika and the test does not measure orthostatic lightheadedness. [Official UMIN record](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000064785) · [Ministry mirror](https://rctportal.mhlw.go.jp/detail/um?trial_id=UMIN000056794)

## Paprika xanthophyll and athlete endurance and sleep

Nishimura and colleagues published online December 26, 2025 and in print in 2026, DOI 10.7600/jpfsm.2024.049. In UMIN000050969, 219 collegiate male athletes were randomized to placebo, nicotinamide mononucleotide, paprika xanthophyll or both. Full-group comparisons for xanthophyll alone against placebo found no clear differences in lactate-threshold pace, lactate-accumulation pace or heart-rate slope (Table 3). Early combined-arm and low-baseline-NAD subgroup signals concern endurance. The paper records a sleep questionnaire with a favorable within-combination sleep-quality result at four weeks, while the xanthophyll-only scores were 0.91 before intake and 0.88 in weeks 0–4. The report does not give isolated paprika effects on a clinical insomnia scale or a patient-reported everyday fatigue outcome. [Primary paper, Tables 3 and 7](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpfsm/15/2/15_2024.049/_pdf/-char/en) · [Official registered results](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/icdr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000058095)

## Registered nutmeg-containing snack and perimenopausal mood and sleep

NCT07599930 was first posted May 20, 2026. The planned double-blind crossover compares a granola snack containing nutmeg, cinnamon, cloves, ginger, seeds, nuts, fruit, oil, cocoa and fiber with a calorie-matched chocolate cereal snack in perimenopausal women. The registry estimates enrollment of 20 and lists mood measured by the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale and sleep quality measured by the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index among the secondary outcomes. The current record lists recruiting and has no posted results. A product-level effect would leave nutmeg attribution and Parkinson symptoms unresolved. [Official study](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07599930) · [Selected registry fields](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07599930.txt)

## Nutmeg infusion with sleep hygiene in a student study

Riyanni’s July 2026 university thesis abstract reports a purposive sample of 30 students with baseline Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index above five. Seven days of ground-seed infusion and 21 days of sleep-hygiene education were combined. The mean score changed from 7.50 to 4.97 (within-group p < .001). The single-arm design cannot isolate the infusion effect or separate it from the education, and did not include Parkinson disease.
