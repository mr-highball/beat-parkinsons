# Mixed fruit trial and abdominal symptom reporting

## Mixed dried or juiced fruit and PAC-SYM

The 2025 BSG abstract O22 and the 2026 IUNS presentation SOAP08_01 describe 150 adults with Rome IV functional constipation. The former links NCT04086134 and the latter matches the three interventions, sample size, age and sex summary. The fruit arms used a dried combination of prunes, raisins and apricots or a plum, grape and apricot juice blend. The comparison arm used cordial. The change in primary stool weight differed across groups, with P = .027 in the 2025 abstract and P = .047 in the 2026 report. Both give the same rounded arm changes and report greater stool weight with dried fruit than cordial. The difference between reported analyses remains unresolved. [2025 original abstract](https://gut.bmj.com/content/74/Suppl_1/A16.1) · [2026 original conference collection](https://doi.org/10.1017/jns.2026.10126) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2025-Steenson-Mixed-Dried-Fruit-Factual-Extract.txt)

PAC-SYM includes an abdominal pain item. The public abstracts report aggregate clinical scores without a separate pain estimate or an isolated apricot contrast. They give related context for 0604 with different preparation, population and outcome. Count the original registry, the thesis abstract and the two conference results as reports of one trial. The King's College record dates the thesis award to July 1, 2023, and gives the same protocol. Its public metadata leaves thesis-body access and first posting date unresolved. A separate May 2025 abstract title reports dried fruit and stool frequency. Its bibliographic identity is verified, and its original body and sample details remain open.
