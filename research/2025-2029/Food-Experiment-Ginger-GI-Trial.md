# Ginger capsules and gastrointestinal symptoms in pulmonary tuberculosis

## Epigastric pain in the ginger trial heatmap

Latif and colleagues first published May 25, 2025. Of 200 enrolled across four groups, 189 completed, including 47 in the ginger group and 48 in the placebo group. The intervention section describes capsules of dried ginger powder. Its separate analytical methods describe an ethanol extraction. The preparation does not verify fresh grated ginger consumption. Figure 4 and Supplementary Table S1 include epigastric pain among seven gastrointestinal items. Constipation appears in the introduction without a displayed symptom result. The article and registry disagree on masking and placebo dose. [Original report, Methods, section 3.5 and Figure 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12104075/) · [Earlier registration](../2020-2024/Source-Text/Trials/NCT06157034.txt)

The official supplement reports the counts under none, mild and moderate epigastric pain. Ginger counts were 12/25/10 at baseline, 17/23/7 during intervention and 18/23/6 at washout. Placebo counts were 12/24/12, 9/25/14 and 7/29/12. These are descriptive counts with phase-wide Cramér's V for the four groups. The supplement gives no paired or ginger-versus-placebo change estimate. The table also lists moderate vomiting during intervention and washout in the ginger group, differing from the body narrative, and its ginger washout vomiting cells total 57 against the stated group size of 47. The pain comparison remains indirect for Parkinson disease and the frozen preparation.
