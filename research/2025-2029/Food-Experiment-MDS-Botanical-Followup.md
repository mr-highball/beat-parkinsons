# Botanical preparations traced through the 2026 Movement Disorder Society congress

## Eclipta-derived silver nanoparticles in mice

Rajput, Tiwari and Sinha reported six groups of 12 male mice each. The intervention groups received oral silver nanoparticles produced from *Eclipta prostrata* at 50, 100 or 200 mg/kg/day concurrently with intraperitoneal haloperidol 1 mg/kg/day for a week. The 100 and 200 mg/kg groups had longer hanging-wire latency (p < .005), fewer vacuous chewing movements (p < .001), and more hole-board head dips and line crossings (p < .001) than haloperidol alone. A levodopa group served as positive control. The abstract gives no plain-plant or silver-only arm, particle characterization, plant part, or numerical group estimates. Haloperidol-induced oral movements differ from levodopa-induced dyskinesia in idiopathic Parkinson disease. Exact title and author searches located the congress abstract without an attached figure or full report. [2026 original congress report 36540](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/neuroprotective-and-antiparkinsonian-effects-of-green-synthesized-silver-nanoparticles-from-eclipta-prostrata-in-a-haloperidol-induced-murine-model/) · [2014 oral leaf extract](../2010-2014/Food-and-Nutrition.md#2014--eclipta-leaf-extract-and-drug-induced-movement-in-rats) · [2016 and 2018 oral preparations](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-MDS-Botanical-Followup.md#oral-eclipta-and-holy-basil-extracts-in-drug-induced-models) · [2024 injected preparation](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-MDS-Botanical-Followup.md#injected-aqueous-eclipta-and-catalepsy).

## Pueraria extract and larval locomotion in Friedreich ataxia

Yadav et al. mixed *Pueraria tuberosa* extract into larval feed at 2.5 or 5 mg/mL for the frataxin S136R model of Friedreich ataxia. The congress report gives 2.10 cm/min for untreated mutant larvae and 2.35 cm/min after treatment. ## Cannabis extract in a shared MCT carrier for Parkinson pain

The August 7, 2026 Kubota et al. paper and MDS archive record 35632 report the same University of São Paulo trial (RBR-3tvjjkx). The trial randomized 101 people with Parkinson disease and chronic pain, analyzed 99 who attended at least one follow-up and retained 87 through nine weeks. The oral extract contained CBD 96 mg/mL and THC 2.1 mg/mL. Both study arms contained the same medium-chain triglyceride oil and ascorbyl acetate. The paper does not identify the oil’s botanical source. Figure 1 reports a day-63 effect size for the Parkinson Disease Pain Classification System of 0.09 (95% CI −0.44 to 0.61, p = .757). The team stopped recruitment for futility. Irritability was reported more often with the extract (p = .005). [Primary article, PMID 42563548](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.70449) · [Registration first posted January 25, 2023](https://ensaiosclinicos.gov.br/rg/RBR-3tvjjkx) · [July 2025 statistical analysis plan](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/BQRWF).

The conference's original Table 2 gives day-63 between-group p-values of .285 for freezing of gait, .683 for UPDRS Part III, .885 for Timed Up and Go, .148 for MoCA and .643 for the combined autonomic score. The paper and abstract give p = .776 for total Brief Pain Inventory, and the conference table gives p = .376 for the separate pain-intensity row. These comparisons estimate the added cannabinoid formulation while holding the carrier oil constant. They provide no independent refined or virgin coconut-oil effect for [0031, orthostatic lightheadedness](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0031.txt), [0110, postural instability](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0110.txt), or [0598, executive impairment](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0598.txt). Wiley's supplementary table endpoints returned HTTP 403 during retrieval, and the conference attachment was accessible. Crossref reports CC BY 4.0 for the journal version. [MDS archive record](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/cannabis-based-oil-for-pain-and-other-non-motor-symptoms-in-parkinsons-disease-a-randomized-controlled-trial/) · [Original Table 2](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/0988_1474_000411_3.jpg) · [Crossref license record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1002/mds.70449) · [Earlier Canadian dose-finding study](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-MDS-Botanical-Followup.md#olive-oil-carrier-in-a-canadian-cannabinoid-pain-study).

## Reported velvet bean use in Argentina

Bacigalupe, López, and Merino surveyed 71 people with Parkinson disease from messaging groups in February to May 2025. Ninety-seven percent were from Argentina, 29 currently used *Mucuna pruriens*, 16 had formerly used it, and 26 had never used it. Respondents most often took the powder in liquid with or before standard medication. They reported perceived benefit for tremor and other motor symptoms and some reported pain, mood or gastrointestinal benefit. Reported reasons for stopping included gastrointestinal intolerance and no perceived effect. The study had no validated symptom-change scale, symptom-specific denominators, untreated comparator, or known dose standardization. Velvet bean is a different species from broad bean. The earliest located outcome record is the MDS abstract, whose archive page gives September 1, 2026. A July 2026 local program names a same-investigator session about velvet bean without reporting these results. [MDS archive record 35476](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/use-of-mucuna-pruriens-m-pruriens-in-people-with-parkinsons-disease-pwpd/) · [July event program](https://www.lanueva.com/nota/2026-7-9-5-0-23-jornadas-federales-de-parkinson-en-darregueira-y-villa-ventana) · [Earlier Mexican case and Argentine seed analysis](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-MDS-Botanical-Followup.md#velvet-bean-with-levodopa-in-a-mexican-case).

## Injected curcumin and catalepsy in Armenian rats

Darbinyan et al. injected male albino rats daily with rotenone (2 mg/kg) and/or isolated curcumin (200 mg/kg) for nine weeks. The September 15, 2025 journal article reports decreased catalepsy duration and increased rearing with curcumin. Figure 6 plots values of about 2, 13 and 5 for control, rotenone and rotenone plus curcumin, respectively. Figure 7 plots roughly 10, 4 and 12 rearing events for those groups. Values are approximate readings from the figures. The publisher provides the abstract and figures, and the full article is subscription access. The 2026 Yerevan proceedings and MDS record 35680 report the same nine-week doses, target regions and 100 Hz pedunculopontine stimulation with overlapping authors. Count these reports as one source family. The group's distinct 2017 cylinder test used 21 days of rotenone and its March 2022 substantia nigra paper used a 35-day toxin regimen and reported electrophysiology and histology. [2025 original, PMID 40952533](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40952533/) · [Publisher and Figures 6–7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11011-025-01698-4) · [May 2026 Yerevan proceedings](https://pureportal.spbu.ru/files/154982411/ISBS_2026_Yerevan_Abstracts.pdf) · [MDS archive record](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/neuroprotective-effects-of-curcumin-on-hippocampal-ppn-circuitry-in-a-rotenone-induced-rat-model-of-parkinsons-disease/) · [July 2017 original](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11011-017-0060-y) · [March 2022 original](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11011-022-00941-6).

## Oral curcumin with and without levodopa in Turkish rats

The April 10, 2025 Belviranlı, Okudan, and Sezer study is a separate 35-day experiment in Wistar rats. Two of 42 rats died early, leaving six in each of the healthy vehicle and healthy curcumin arms and seven in each of four rotenone arms. The authors injected rotenone subcutaneously in sunflower oil and gavaged isolated curcumin (200 mg/kg) in corn oil. The treatment arms took curcumin, levodopa, or both for the final 15 days. The vehicle control received both oils and saline. The study has no randomized comparison isolating either edible oil and reports no oil grade. [Primary study, DOI 10.1007/s11011-025-01602-0](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11011-025-01602-0) · [Local licensed article text](Source-Text/2025-Belviranli-Curcumin-Levodopa-Rats.txt).

The isolated-curcumin treatment arm improved pole-test descent time and time mobile relative to the rotenone group. The authors interpreted longer time staying on the pole as better motor control. Open-field speed differed between the rotenone arm and the combined curcumin–levodopa arm, and the post-hoc comparisons did not identify a speed difference for curcumin alone. The groups did not differ on the anxiety index (p = .14). The combination, with no detected effect for curcumin alone, improved the sucrose-preference measure of depressive-like behavior. The results are indirect for [0507](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0507.txt) and [0642](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0642.txt). Sunflower oil served as a carrier, leaving the effect of refined sunflower oil on depression [0033](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0033.txt) unresolved. [Primary Figures 2 and 3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11011-025-01602-0#Sec16).

Figure 1 plots hot tail-flick latency near 1.7 seconds with reserpine alone and 4.5 seconds with added CBD 30 mg/kg (p < .01), while Figure 3 plots mechanical threshold near 2.4 g with reserpine alone and 14 g and 17 g with added CBD 60 mg/kg and THC 6 mg/kg, respectively (both p < .001). These values are approximate readings from the figures. Figure 2 cold tail-flick, Figure 4 MDA and Figure 5 SOD align closely in group order, point estimates, and comparisons with the corresponding graphs in the August 31, 2026 journal article. Its abstract describes three weeks, while its Methods give the sequential schedule. Figure 1 and its Results report cold tail-flick latency of 18.50 ± 2.87 seconds for reserpine, 46.75 ± 4.01 for CBD 60 mg/kg and 57.75 ± 10.06 for THC 6 mg/kg (mean ± SEM, both p < .05 versus reserpine). The combination was 40.25 ± 2.14 seconds and did not differ significantly from reserpine. The 2025 control bar appears slightly above the journal Methods' stated 60-second cutoff. The published study states distilled water for the healthy control and lists Tween 80 among materials. It provides no named edible-oil intervention. The publisher's open-access policy and site footer specify different reuse licenses, so the original PDF is linked here. ## XJN010 nasal spray and fed oral comparator

Guangzhou Novaken's December 24, 2025 Hong Kong Exchange application proof is the earliest located public source for the healthy-volunteer Phase I results later presented by Tang et al. The relative-bioavailability study used a three-period double Latin-square design with oral tablets under fasting and fed conditions and intranasal XJN010. Relative bioavailability for the 40 mg spray was 118% versus fasting oral tablets and 98.3% versus fed oral tablets. The poster calls the fed condition a high-fat meal, with ingredients and preparation unspecified. Figure 1 I–N shows healthy-volunteer drug concentrations and times. The abstract reports motor results for 6-OHDA rats only. Panel H shows fewer rotations with the nasal spray and no significant difference for the oral reference versus the model. Panel G displays rotarod values without significance brackets. [Primary company filing, pp. 285 and 290–291](https://www.hkexnews.hk/app/sehk/2025/107993/a128565/sehk25122400165.pdf) · [MDS archive record 36403](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/intranasal-spray-for-nose-to-brain-drug-delivery-emergency-treatment-of-off-episodes-in-parkinsons-disease/) · [Original Figure 1](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/0988_1474_001536_1.jpg).
