# Printed pharmacology and food-context evidence in Round 1

Twenty-three original records resolved 184 exact claim–source comparisons. The archived scopes record ingredient identity, preparation, source member and measured outcome. Reviews and conference abstracts can document a material or context without supplying a measured Parkinson symptom result. ## Drug-study vehicles in the 1988 proceedings

The National Library of Medicine scans contain 64 distinct pages. The original crossover on pp. 621P–622P used decaffeinated coffee as a caffeine vehicle after hypnotics. Several tests showed impairment for zopiclone alone against placebo, with no statistically significant impairment reported for zopiclone with caffeine. Nitrazepam-related memory impairment remained. The printed table marks comparisons against placebo and does not give a direct test of the difference between the two zopiclone arms. The corn-oil study on pp. 666P–667P measured animal drug-metabolism endpoints. Orange juice was shared by both arms in a separate ethanol experiment on p. 668P. These are separate experiments and none isolates an ordinary dietary exposure for the frozen Parkinson outcome. [Printed proceedings notes](../1985-1989/Source-Text/1988-PMID3207568-BPS-Clinical-Pharmacology-Proceedings-Food-Scopes.txt)

## Distinct botanical and clinical trails

The polyphenol review's saffron attribution for curcumin conflicts with the cited Goel article, which identifies turmeric. Authentic Crocus sativus studies take a different route. The food chemistry analysis describes measured constituents in 11 certified saffron samples. The Rao experiment compared a saffron extract and crocin in flies and reported favorable locomotor results. Neither supplies a ground saffron and human Parkinson rigidity comparison. [Review citation](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2018-PMID30081787-Polyphenol-Diabetes-Alzheimer-Review-Food-Scopes.txt) · [Turmeric identification](../2005-2009/Source-Text/2007-PMID17900536-Curcumin-and-Turmeric-Identity.txt) · [Saffron composition](../2005-2009/Source-Text/2007-DOI-FoodChem-Saffron-Eleven-Samples.txt) · [Fly study](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2015-PMID26705857-Saffron-Extract-and-Crocin-in-Flies.txt)

The dietary microbiota review cites a randomized twelve-week green-tea extract supplement comparison. Its 58 analyzable participants provided no statistically significant treatment-by-time interaction in microbiota diversity or body composition. This comparison used capsules and did not measure Parkinson fatigue. [Dietary microbiota review](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2018-PMID29886183-Diet-Microbiota-Neuroimmune-Review-Food-Scopes.txt) · [Original trial](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2016-DOI-PLOS-Green-Tea-Extract-and-Microbiota.txt)

The traditional food review names pepper, cinnamon, clove, cumin and onion in separate customary mixtures. Its diarrhea and stomachache reports do not supply a constipation result or isolate the effect of a named spice.
