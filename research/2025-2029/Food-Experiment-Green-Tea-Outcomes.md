# Green tea constituents and physical performance

## Tea catechins and ornithine during cycling

The crossover in 16 healthy men compared tea catechins plus ornithine with placebo. The subjective rating of perceived exertion during fixed-intensity cycling was lower after the acute combination by 0.5 points (95% CI 0.2 to 0.8 points lower) and after 14 days by 0.6 points (0.2 to 0.9 lower) against the respective placebo periods. During the separate 15-minute performance test, power and exertion showed no clear difference. This is a supplement combination under exercise, so neither ingredient-specific fatigue nor the brewed tea Parkinson outcome is resolved. [Original article and Figures 3–4](Source-Text/PMC12528257.txt) · [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-025-05833-y)

## Powdered green tea and exercise trial results

The Makizako trial enrolled 78 community-dwelling older adults, 45 assigned to tea powder and 33 to control, with exercise in both arms. The April 15, 2026 publication and the August 4, 2026 registry update report no statistically clear overall physical-performance or muscle-mass comparisons in the intention-to-treat mixed models. A separate knee-extension percentage-change comparison gave p=.016. Grip strength was a registered outcome, but the available results give no independently verified handwriting size, upper-limb dexterity or fatigue estimate. The registered formulation was a powder and participants were outside Parkinson disease. [Primary publication abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42030620/) · [Local registry results](Source-Text/Trials/UMIN000055115-results.txt) · [First registration](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Green-Tea-Outcomes.md#powdered-green-tea-plus-exercise-trial-registration)

## SleepLift formulation and fatigue subscales

Singh and colleagues’ May 20, 2026 publication analyzed SleepLift, an insomnia crossover with ten enrolled adults aged 50 or older and one loss to follow-up. The publication calls the supplement proprietary. The public protocol and ClinicalTrials.gov arm descriptions specify 5 g green tea extract, 1 g chicory extract, and 5 g collagen peptides per planned dose. The protocol specifies decaffeinated green tea containing EGCG but gives no quantified EGCG or caffeine dose. The protocol and registry differ in reported beverage volume and excipient percentages. [Local 2022 registration and recipe sources](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Green-Tea-Outcomes.md#sleeplift-registration-and-public-supplement-protocol)

The journal reports a general-fatigue model contrast of +0.05 (95% CI −0.4 to +0.5, p=.79) and a physical-fatigue contrast of −0.67 (95% CI −1.3 to −0.1, p=.03), with lower physical fatigue in the supplement period. Mental fatigue was inconclusive and daytime sleepiness favored placebo. Registry results were first posted in 2024. The current record lists least-squares means for general, physical and mental fatigue separately. These are publications of one small crossover trial in adults with insomnia. The whole supplement was compared with placebo, so the results leave the effect of consuming brewed green tea on Parkinson fatigue unresolved. [Primary article and Table 2](Source-Text/PMC13189356.txt) · [2024 posting and current registry fatigue results](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Green-Tea-Outcomes.md#sleeplift-posted-fatigue-results-by-crossover-condition)

## Tea consumption and odor identification in the CHRIS cohort

Hantikainen and colleagues, first published October 6, 2026, analyzed a cross-sectional general population cohort of 6,690 adults in South Tyrol. They used Sniffin’ Sticks for 16-odor identification and defined hyposmia as a score of 12 or less. Self-reported tea consumption was coded as any versus none. Supplement Table S2 defines this category as black tea and herbal tea. Tables S3–S4 report an adjusted odor-identification ordinal OR of 1.07 (95% CI 1.02 to 1.12) and hyposmia odds ratio of 0.90 (95% CI 0.84 to 0.96) for consumers. Both survive the reported false discovery rate correction. The study gives no green-tea estimate, Parkinson subgroup or change in smell. Measured smell could influence food choice. The primary publisher confirms October despite a conflicting PubMed date.
