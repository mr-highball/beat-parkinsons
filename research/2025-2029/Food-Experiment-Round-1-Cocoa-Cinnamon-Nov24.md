## Cocoa, dark chocolate and anxiety

A 2009 dark-chocolate study grouped 30 healthy volunteers by baseline anxiety trait and tracked metabolites during two weeks of intake. The reported chocolate contrasts did not measure change in anxiety severity and did not expose a non-chocolate comparison. Urinary cortisol and catecholamine changes describe stress metabolism. [Martin study](../2005-2009/Source-Text/2009-PMID19810704-Martin-Dark-Chocolate-Stress-Metabolism.txt)

A separate 30-person masked crossover compared three drinks with different cocoa-flavanol amounts and closely matched other constituents. State anxiety showed no clear treatment effect, with a treatment-sequence difference that limits interpretation. Task-evoked mental fatigue fell at the lower added flavanol dose. The later review's broader phrasing applies that result to both enriched drinks. The original report supports the lower dose alone. [Scholey crossover](../2005-2009/Source-Text/2009-PMID19942640-Scholey-Cocoa-State-Anxiety.txt) · [Katz review](../2010-2014/Source-Text/2011-PMID21470061-Katz-Cocoa-and-Chocolate-Outcomes.txt)

The scorecard records these as two separate families. The original review links to both papers without contributing another independent effect. The strongest competing solid-chocolate evidence remains the acute healthy-adult snack trial. ## Cinnamon and caffeine context

The cinnamon article assessed isolated sodium benzoate and molecular outcomes. Parkinson rigidity and rest tremor describe clinical background. The separate caffeine article observed rodent wakefulness, which leaves the clinical brewed-coffee fatigue question open. [Cinnamon-metabolite study](../2010-2014/Source-Text/2011-PMID21701815-Khasnavis-Cinnamon-Metabolite-and-DJ1.txt) · [Caffeine wakefulness article](../2010-2014/Source-Text/2011-PMID21734299-Lazarus-Caffeine-and-Rodent-Wakefulness.txt)
