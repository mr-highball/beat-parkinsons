## Coffee and clinical movement context

The 1981 essential-tremor review differentiates action tremor and resting tremor. Page 1560 describes possible worsening after coffee or tea in some people with essential tremor. It also names physical fatigue as a separate circumstance that may aggravate shaking. No specified coffee preparation, controlled comparison or Parkinson fatigue estimate is given. A later controlled caffeine test and historical reports are already covered in the Parkinson caffeine review records. The printed 1994 Parkinson review discusses motor and nonmotor fluctuations from levodopa treatment. Its cited Sage references and the broken word passage on p. 675 supply no botanical exposure. ## Traditional recommendations and compound formulas

The public Chinese excerpt names a cinnamon-twig formula with kudzu for a patterned upper-body or limb pain context. The later Chinese primary-care consensus table retains that formulation. The 2024 English review shortens the wording to a cinnamon-twig option. The source trail contains a professional recommendation and supplies no independently measured effect of culinary ground cinnamon. The original 2021 PDF remains unverified. [Dated traditional record](../2020-2024/Cultural-and-Experiential/Traditional-Practices/2021-TCM-Consensus-Cinnamon-Twig-and-Kudzu-Parkinson-Pain.txt) · [Clinical mini review](../2020-2024/Source-Text/2024-PMID39723118-Parkinson-Nonmotor-Alternative-Therapies-Food-Scopes.txt)

A 2021 history review and its 2010 cited source attribute distinct customary uses to sweet basil, cardamom and turmeric. The two documents form a citation chain and contain no measured Parkinson outcomes or a verified original manuscript passage for each claim. ## Oil preparations and study outcomes

The registered coconut-oil beverage trial concerned Alzheimer cognition and ended early. The completed coconut gel trial uses a topical oral preparation and measures an oral lesion score. Neither registry posts results. The animal oil records concern an olive/peanut oil metabolic experiment and a separate sesame oil Alzheimer model with learning and memory outcomes. None measures falling or the other mapped Parkinson symptoms.
