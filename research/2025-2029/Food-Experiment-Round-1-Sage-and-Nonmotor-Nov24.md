## Sage, rosemary and independent clinical outcomes

The Parkinson extended-release amantadine safety study enrolled 223 people and reported 56 (25.1%) with falls and 16 (7.2%) with insomnia. The extension was uncontrolled and had no sage exposure. A similar clinical-language error occurred where rosemary identified an author in a nerve-block paper. A Parkinson cognition paper distinguished akinetic-rigid and tremor-dominant participants without a sage exposure. Preserve these studies in the catalog under their actual clinical contexts. [Amantadine](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2017-PMID28777755-Amantadine-Extension-Visual-Adverse-Events.txt) · [Nerve block](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2017-PMID28494088-Hip-Fracture-Nerve-Blocks-and-Pain.txt) · [Parkinson cognition](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2017-PMID28612623-Akinetic-Rigid-and-Tremor-Dominant-Parkinson-Cognition.txt)

Dates in the patent highlight mark the invention's filing and publication. Figure references, place names and instrument terms also produced incidental matches. By contrast, hen egg-white lysozyme and curcuminoids were genuinely studied. The lysozyme experiment measured protein aggregation, one curcumin paper measured exocytosis, and the other curcumin paper measured maze performance after sleep deprivation. None supplied a fall observation. ## Ground turmeric and falls

The original solid-lipid curcumin trial reports rapid-step time group-by-time p=.9, step errors p=.4 and four-meter gait speed p=.9 in healthy adults. Some rounded test scores numerically improved more with the formulated product. The study measured balance and mobility tasks, not fall events. The closer Parkinson formulated-curcumin reports still lack an individually verified fall-frequency estimate. ## Poached egg and falls

An older-adult study supplied whole eggs as part of a higher-protein diet and measured body composition. In a separate randomized study of recipes and spice packets, the recipe arm began with lower mean short physical performance scores (7.81 vs 9.32). The adjusted analyses found no clear arm effect on that class of outcomes. An egg-white supplement trial specifically registered recalled falls, fall-related injury and confidence about falling. The registry has no posted results. The author abstract gives an inconclusive comparison for its reported strength and function outcomes and does not report the fall comparison. Its full report remained restricted and the registry and report use different enrollment counts. These three families leave the earlier near-neutral Parkinson figure and unadjusted fracture case-control result as indirect signals without a validated link to poached-egg fall frequency. ## Ground rosemary and constipation

The rosemary preprint reports wet stool percentages of 19.71% on vehicle and 31.57%, 41.50% and 68.01% across lower levels of oral hydroethanolic extract in unchallenged mice. A separate induced-diarrhea model used higher levels and an injection route and reported reduced transit and secretions. The distinct conditions matter when interpreting the two signs. The older human records combined rosemary-containing oil with massage or other ingredients. The more recent rosemary-extract registry is for people with type 2 diabetes, has no posted results and groups diarrhea and constipation in its adverse-event question. No preparation bridge determines how either finding translates to culinary rosemary.
