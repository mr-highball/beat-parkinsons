# Isfahan saffron trial registration history

## The 2025 registration revisions and manuscript chronology

IRCT history dates revision 350954 to July 30, 2025. The revision page gives August 9 as its last update. It raises the target from 86 to 92 and adds fatigue measured with the 16-item Parkinson Fatigue Scale, appetite and blood pressure. It specifies Mojtahedi as the saffron supplier, replaces superoxide dismutase with catalase and drops protein carbonyl from the listed biochemical outcomes. The reason field refers to possible dropout and correction of omitted items. Revision 354286 retains the revised outcomes. The history dates it September 6 and its page gives September 7 as the last update. [July revision](https://irct.ir/trial/75378?revision=350954) · [September revision](https://irct.ir/trial/75378?revision=354286) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/IRCT20121216011763N61-2025-Outcome-Revisions.txt)

BMJ Open marks September 2, 2024 as the date it received the protocol, September 25, 2025 as acceptance and October 29, 2025 as first publication. The recovered September 2, 2024 journal submission omits the standalone PFS-16, and the August 9, 2025 R1 draft identifies it as a secondary outcome. The first internal use of the scale remains unresolved. Food & Function received the outcomes manuscript on April 27, 2025 and first published it on December 24. The first archived IRCT revision listing fatigue follows the reported manuscript receipt. Actual recruitment and study completion dates remain unresolved. The record documents public outcome-list changes and leaves the reason for the changes open. [Original registry version](https://irct.ir/trial/75378?revision=296350) · [Protocol and article history](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/15/10/e093167) · [Outcome report and editorial dates](https://doi.org/10.1039/D5FO01924A)

The published fatigue comparison was 9.20 points lower with saffron powder tablets than placebo, with a 95% confidence interval from 4.65 to 13.76 points lower. The delayed public registration of this outcome, the fixed tablet regimen and the absence of independent matched replication weaken the claim-level inference. The depression scale was already listed in the original public registration. [Trial outcomes](Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#saffron-powder-and-fatigue)

## Protocol peer review and the timing of the fatigue outcome

The protocol's public peer-review file contains dated reviewer comments on October 12, 2024 and May 15, 2025. The comments do not name the Parkinson Fatigue Scale. The undated author response on PDF page 3 lists fatigue, blood pressure and appetite among secondary outcomes. The dated draft bundle now ties this addition to the August 9, 2025 R1 manuscript. The original IRCT registration listed a broad MDS-UPDRS measure, which includes a fatigue item in Part I. Its list did not specify the separate Parkinson Fatigue Scale comparison used in the reported fatigue result. [Primary peer-review archive](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12574437/supplementaryFiles) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2025-Hajhashemy-Peer-Review-Revision-Extract.txt)

The public journal version archive also contains R2 dated August 27 and R3 dated September 12. Both retain the standalone fatigue scale. The initial submission already lists general motor and nonmotor scores, sleep quality and mental health. The earliest released submission and registry thus agree on the absence of the standalone PFS-16 in the initial listed outcomes.
