# Rosemary exposure and clinical outcomes

## Oral rosemary extract and general safety

Kuwata and colleagues studied an oral rosemary extract in a four-week open-label safety study in 22 healthy adults and a separate placebo-controlled crossover with 40 participants. The article reports no clinically relevant adverse events in the safety trial and reports stress measures. This is an extract in healthy adults, with no fresh culinary leaf intervention or Parkinson visual-hallucination frequency measure; a general safety statement is not a hallucination-specific result. [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.3164/jcbn.25-157).

## Nurse interviews about topical rosemary care

A qualitative study interviewed 49 intensive-care nurses about implementing topical herbal care. Its protocol table lists rosemary essential oil as a 10% body oil or bath milk for tiredness and circulation, while the delirium entry concerns a different plant, Oxalis acetosella. The study measured nurse perceptions, not patient clinical effects, and gives no Parkinson fresh-leaf or hallucination-frequency comparison. [Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12906-026-05423-1).

## Depression screen and anxiety outcomes for Kuwata’s crossover

The September 10, 2025 paper reports the UMIN000054368 crossover, with 40 assigned and 38 analyzed. The capsules used extract from post-distillation rosemary residue. Table 5’s 42.9 and 44.5 values are week-four state anxiety scores (p=.153). BDI-II was an eligibility and baseline measure, without a reported treatment comparison. The schedule, population and registry distinguish the trial from Araki’s earlier cohort, and the test does not contribute a depression direction for fresh rosemary. [Primary full text, Methods and Table 5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12646844/).
