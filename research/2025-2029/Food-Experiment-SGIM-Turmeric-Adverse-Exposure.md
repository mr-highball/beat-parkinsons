# Turmeric, contaminated spice and adverse-event pain reports

The original Society of General Internal Medicine meeting collection was first published on July 17, 2025. It contains two distinct turmeric-related presentations. [Journal supplement](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-025-09516-4)

## Headache in an adolescent with lead exposure

Gonehal and Biswas reported headache, fatigue and elevated blood lead in an adolescent. A public-health investigation attributed the contamination to imported turmeric. Headache frequency and blood lead improved during follow-up after discontinuation and supplementation, while headache intensity and cognitive complaints persisted. The exposure under investigation was lead in the spice, and the report has no Parkinson pain comparison. [Primary case abstract, Control ID 4271805](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12572470/#Par186)

## Abdominal pain in selected turmeric supplement injury reports

Singh and colleagues reviewed 17 selected adult reports of liver injury associated with turmeric use. Among cases with symptom data, 37.5% had right upper quadrant pain. This is a symptom frequency within selected adverse-event reports. It provides no incidence estimate, independent replication count or comparison of culinary ground turmeric in Parkinson disease. [Original report synthesis, Control ID 4267863](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12572470/#Par9673)
