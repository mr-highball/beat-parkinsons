# Mango in the FruktBAR smoothie studies

## Pilot smoothie feasibility and fatigue in opioid agonist treatment

The July 2025 paper reports a 2021 feasibility study with 24 people receiving opioid agonist treatment. The Bergen clinic supplied bottled purée and juice blends, some containing mango, while Stavanger ran fresh-smoothie workshops with different ingredients. At the two time points, Table 2 gives pre- and post-intervention medians for 14 people with paired FSS-3 records of 4.3 and 4.5 and a median paired change of 0.33 (IQR −0.67 to 2.33). There was no control group or inferential significance test. Qualitative reports of increased energy use a different outcome. No dose or exposure-specific association was reported for mango. ## Randomized mixed fruit smoothies and fatigue severity

The August 2025 main report studied commercial 250 ml smoothies offered daily for 16 weeks with standard opioid agonist treatment versus standard treatment alone. Supplementary Table S1 confirms two of four options contained mango, with passion fruit or pineapple. The other two did not include mango in their names. There was no randomization by flavor, quantified mango amount or isolated mango estimate. The Methods and flow diagram give 319 randomized and 311 analyzed, while the abstract calls 311 randomized. Among 127 people in the intervention arm who received more than 75% of planned bottles, mean self-reported consumption was 3.9 bottles per week (SD 1.49).

Table 2 gives a between-arm fatigue-score change of −0.1 (95% CI −0.6 to 0.4) in the reported intention-to-treat analysis and −0.2 (−0.8 to 0.3) per protocol. The paper terms the outcome the sum FSS-3 score, while the registry describes a 0–18 range and the paper describes three 1–7 items. The comparison remains on the published scale. It evaluates a mixed intervention in an opioid-dependent population without selection for Parkinson disease. The registry showed completed status and no posted outcome tables when checked on November 14, 2026 US Eastern.
