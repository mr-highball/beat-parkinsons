# Parkinson swallowing and food in the 2025 and 2026 JSDR meetings

The Japanese Society of Dysphagia Rehabilitation held its 31st meeting September 19–20, 2025 and published abstracts in journal volume 29(3). Its 32nd meeting took place September 11–13, 2026. The notes below preserve original summaries and links to exact pages. No permissive redistribution license was verified for the proceedings or program. [Society meeting archive](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/meeting/meeting_annual.html)

## Thin and honey-thick liquid in Parkinson swallowing CT

Bayona and colleagues, ENS2-02, used 10 mL thin and honey-thick liquids in 17 patients with Hoehn and Yahr stage II–IV Parkinson disease and 19 controls. Multiple swallows with thin liquid were more frequent in Parkinson disease (65% vs 16%, p < .01). The comparison for penetration was inconclusive (17.7% vs 5.3%, p = .12). Thick liquid was associated with a larger upper esophageal sphincter opening area in controls (p = .02), while the estimate in the Parkinson group showed no clear difference (p = .89). The conference abstract describes honey-thick viscosity and does not identify honey as a test ingredient. [2025 proceedings, ENS2-02, S67](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no31_abstracts.pdf#page=68) · [Continued conclusions, S68](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no31_abstracts.pdf#page=69)

On November 3, 2026, Fujita's UMIN000050721 record posted swallowing-CT results for 22 eligible Parkinson participants from 27 who consented and underwent CT. Three exclusions had atypical parkinsonism and two were imaged upright. The results describe more penetration–aspiration and multiple swallows with thin liquid and more residue with both consistencies than in matched controls. Aspiration occurred in one of 43 Parkinson swallows and cleared with a cough. The investigators reported no serious adverse events and no medication ON/OFF data. The registered investigators and methods align with the 2025 meeting report, which gives no registry ID. They should be treated as a likely study family with a larger registry analysis. The registry provides no ingredient-specific outcome or linked journal article. [Original UMIN registry, Results and Participant flow](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/icdr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000057788) · [Initial 2023 registration](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-JSDR-Recent-Abstracts.md#fujita-parkinson-swallowing-ct-registration)

## Water and jelly between porridge swallows

Okada and colleagues, O50-04, reported two people in their 80s with stage IV Parkinson disease, low appetite and esophageal impairment. Porridge stayed in the esophagus during videofluoroscopy. Water alternation helped clear the porridge in case A, who also received two high-energy jellies and three Meibalance Mini drinks per day. His reported BMI increased from 16.1 to 20.3. In case B, water silently aspirated and jelly alternation helped with porridge retention. He took one high-energy jelly per day, and BMI fell from 20.0 to 17.4 even though the report says no further aspiration pneumonia occurred during the described follow-up. These are individual observations under care with different medical events, boluses and outcomes. The matching team and similar presentation leave possible overlap between case A and the group's 2024 case. [2025 proceedings, O50-04, S210](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no31_abstracts.pdf#page=211) · [2024 report](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-JSDR-Recent-Abstracts.md#porridge-retention-and-nutrition-supplements-in-a-2024-parkinson-case)

## Chewing check gum and swallowing assessments at home

Watanabe and colleagues, O20-02, reported a 74-year-old person with Parkinson disease whose chewing-check-gum score rose from 3.0 to 6.4 after two months. Weekly oral, voice and articulation exercises continued, and side-to-side jaw movement and attention to chewing were added after the initial gum assessment. The Parkinson case reports a chewing test result only. BMI, voice quality and diet-texture changes presented with it belong to a separate patient with Alzheimer disease. [2025 proceedings, O20-02, S128](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no31_abstracts.pdf#page=129)

## Diet texture and assisted feeding in advanced Parkinson disease

P-060 reported continued pleasure feeding using a blended diet alongside posture changes, meal timing in the medication ON state, bite-size and pace control, and suction after meals. P-080 described a different person who initially ate regular food and moderately thick fluids at home when alert, then developed inconsistent intake and dehydration and was admitted for gastrostomy. P-090 described an older woman with Parkinson disease whose dysphagia worsened after COVID-19. Endoscopy found no penetration or aspiration in a complete side-lying position. The team used assisted puréed meals and thickened fluids, then later observed reduced residue and some return to sitting intake. These cases report clinical feeding strategies without named ingredients or food-specific estimates. [2025 proceedings, P-060, S255](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no31_abstracts.pdf#page=256) · [P-080, S264](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no31_abstracts.pdf#page=265) · [P-090, S268](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no31_abstracts.pdf#page=269)

## Sweet potato mousse in care-facility feedback

Kim and colleagues, EP-003, described mousse-type meals for older people with dysphagia. The preparations included sweet-potato mousse and items made with soybean, spinach, cabbage, meat or fruits. Results describe care-facility feedback about satisfaction and intake for the menu. The abstract gives no diagnosis-stratified results, individual food outcomes or baked-potato comparison for `0290`. [2025 proceedings, EP-003, S223](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no31_abstracts.pdf#page=224)

## NCNP carbonation study status

The original UMIN000051942 record was updated August 19, 2025 and lists the study as terminated. Its notes say it was discontinued before it began because participants could not be recruited. The NIPH mirror has shown a different status. The original trial record carries the explanation and has no posted results. UMIN000051163, the related but separately registered NCNP study, was still accepting participants by invitation in the May 29, 2026 Japanese update. Its results fields were empty when checked November 12, 2026. These records provide no observed swallowing outcome for their carbonation contrasts. [Terminated UMIN000051942](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/icdr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000057259) · [UMIN000051163 Japanese original](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000058349) · [Initial 2023 registry records](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-JSDR-Recent-Abstracts.md#ncnp-carbonation-and-swallowing-registrations)

## Public 2026 JSDR program and abstract access

The official public program lists presentation titles and authors. EL29, Toshiyuki Yamamoto, covers Parkinson dysphagia. O25-3, Marina Aoyama, addresses meal selection before formal swallowing assessment. O25-4, Yasuhiro Okada, covers esophageal impairment and aspiration pneumonia risk in two advanced cases. O26-4, Azusa Kimura, addresses bite-size adjustment and swallowing strategies for self-feeding. O26-5, Minako Terasawa, describes rapid dysphagia in a person with Parkinson-associated dementia and possible rheumatoid arthritis exacerbation. The titles provide no named dishes or quantified outcomes. [Public program, PDF p. 11](https://www.congre.co.jp/jsdr32/dl/program.pdf#page=11) · [O25-3 and O25-4, p. 83](https://www.congre.co.jp/jsdr32/dl/program.pdf#page=83) · [O26-4 and O26-5, p. 84](https://www.congre.co.jp/jsdr32/dl/program.pdf#page=84)

P2-A6-5, Tomoo Ogino, describes pneumonia after aspiration-prevention surgery in two Parkinson cases. P2-A6-6, Masako Sato, gives a title-level direction of greater silent aspiration in Parkinson disease than in matched progressive supranuclear palsy patients, with no counts, bolus definitions or effect estimates in the program. P2-C5-5, Hiromi Yoshino, describes adapting oral conditions for self-care. P2-C7-1, Naoki Fukumura, describes six years of home care with oral nutrition in a complete side-lying position after recurrent pneumonia and gastrostomy in Hoehn and Yahr stage V Parkinson disease. The title supplies no named foods or serial swallow scores. It cannot be confirmed as the same person as any prior report. The same author's 2024 paper on long-term side-lying feeding involved a case described as having Wallenberg syndrome, sarcopenia and Lewy body dementia. [Public program, PDF p. 137](https://www.congre.co.jp/jsdr32/dl/program.pdf#page=137) · [P2-C5-5, p. 152](https://www.congre.co.jp/jsdr32/dl/program.pdf#page=152) · [P2-C7-1, p. 154](https://www.congre.co.jp/jsdr32/dl/program.pdf#page=154) · [2024 Fukumura case](https://doi.org/10.2490/prm.20240008)

## Capsaicin pilot in dysphagic patients

The September 2026 program lists O8-5, an Asahi University pilot on capsaicin, chewing and swallowing in patients with dysphagia. The public Confit presentation identifies Yuji Kajikawa as presenter and Akira Tada, Mana Oshio, Kota Amano, Saki Mizutani and Hiroshige Taniguchi as coauthors. Taniguchi led the earlier mochi study in healthy volunteers. The 2026 public title does not identify diagnoses, formulation, dose, results or linkage to the earlier cohort. The individual abstract requires an attendee password.
