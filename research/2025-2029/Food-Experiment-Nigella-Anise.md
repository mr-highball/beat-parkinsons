# Nigella seed and anise seed evidence

## Ground nigella seed in a premenstrual symptom trial

Farjana and colleagues randomized 40 participants to capsules containing ground seed or psyllium for two menstrual cycles. Table 3 reports lower mood and behavioral domain scores after nigella, with a post-treatment mood difference of 9.14 points favoring nigella (95% CI 5.16 to 13.13). The physical domain comparison was inconclusive (p = .187). The depression inventory was used in screening. The paper does not supply a fatigue item result or a Parkinson measure. [Licensed primary text, Table 3](Source-Text/PMC12628082.txt#L182)

## Registered nigella-containing mixture and lupus fatigue

NCT07398885 first posted in February 2026 after completing enrollment. The trial assigned 59 adults with systemic lupus erythematosus to EVACUR or placebo and specified FACIT-F fatigue. EVACUR contains dry nigella extract alongside ginger, several other botanicals and micronutrients. The registry lists completion and has no posted results. [Official registry snapshot](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07398885.txt)

## Nigella oil extract and melatonin in a sleep crossover

Twenty-four adults with nonrestorative sleep completed seven-day periods of the proprietary oil extract and melatonin in randomized order. Both arms had favorable within-arm actigraphy changes. Melatonin had a larger improvement in latency and total sleep time. The oil arm had less daytime sleepiness in the reported comparison. The trial used an active comparator and did not test ground seed or clinical Parkinson insomnia. The paper reports a November 2024 Indian registration. The direct registry endpoint timed out during retrieval. [Licensed study](Source-Text/PMC13625438.txt) · [Registry URL cited by the investigators](https://ctri.nic.in/Clinicaltrials/pmaindet2.php?EncHid=MTExNTY4&Enc=&userName=)

## Nigella and flax extracts in relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis

Naseri and colleagues randomized 50 adults and analyzed 35 completers, 17 with the two extracts and 18 with placebo. Each active capsule contained 0.75 mL each of nigella and flax hydroalcoholic extract alongside routine multiple sclerosis treatment. The full 2026 paper reports no clear group difference across CFAB-MS mood or fatigue measures. A companion October 2026 conference abstract reports an inconclusive BDI-II comparison (p > .05) without arm estimates. The matching investigators, preparations, allocation and completer counts point to one cohort. Ingredient-specific effects and toasted seed in Parkinson disease remain unmeasured. ## SONA Nigella oil plan and reported stress experiment

The 2025 SONA proceedings describe a restraint-stress experiment in a 6-OHDA animal model and list Nigella oil for future evaluation. The reported motor findings concern stress and the model. The proceeding supplies no administered Nigella oil comparison or result. It is retained as a lead for later source and study-result follow-up. ## Isolated thymoquinone in wild-type zebrafish

The July 8, 2026 Research Square preprint studies separate oral exposures in adult wild-type fish. Section 3.3 reports day-14 swimming velocity of 7.1 cm/s in the intermediate thymoquinone group. Section 3.1 reports 50% survival at that level, in contrast with the abstract’s description of intermediate exposure as well tolerated. The highest exposures were dropped from the behavioral study after acute toxicity. Differential survival and handling stress limit interpretation of the swimming and freezing measures. The authors propose future testing in a Parkinson model. The pilot leaves the ground-seed and clinical bradykinesia question open.
