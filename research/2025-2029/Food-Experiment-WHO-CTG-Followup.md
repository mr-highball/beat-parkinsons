# Dietary trial follow-up from WHO ClinicalTrials.gov records, 2025–2029

## Ketoflex trial registration and linked outcomes

NCT06892418 first posted on March 24, 2025, after the registry's reported 2024 completion. The February 2026 article identifies the registration explicitly. It compares a personalized plant-rich, time-restricted diet plus conventional care with conventional care. The paper does not assign steamed broccoli or report a separate fall-frequency outcome. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06892418) [Primary report, PMID 41666212](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13347589/) [Local outcome assessment](Food-and-Nutrition.md#2026--personalized-plant-rich-and-time-restricted-intervention)

## Mediterranean diet program registrations

NCT07097103 first posted on July 31, 2025. PRIMES registers a factorial comparison of Mediterranean diet guidance, intensive activity, both, and control. NCT07838675 first posted on September 24, 2026. MediPD tests a 12-week online Mediterranean diet program, with dietary adherence at 24 weeks as the primary measure and gastrointestinal, nonmotor, quality-of-life, and cognitive outcomes. Neither trial had registry results in the November 2026 archive. The programs combine foods. [PRIMES registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07097103) [MediPD registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07838675)

## Full-spectrum cannabidiol oil and mood registration

NCT07651930 first posted on June 16, 2026 after the reported November 2024 completion. It compared three daily doses of full-spectrum cannabidiol oil in 27 people with Parkinson disease and anxiety or depressive symptoms. It specifies Beck anxiety and depression instruments and no untreated or placebo group. The November 2026 registry extract gives no posted results, and an exact identifier search located no linked primary outcome. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07651930) [Local registration](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07651930.txt)

## PARKA conference outcome and supplement discrepancy

The April 2–3, 2025 AD/PD conference abstract SHIFT 01-102 explicitly identifies NCT04322461. Ten Parkinson participants received 24 g/day D-beta-hydroxybutyrate and 1 g/day nicotinamide riboside in two doses, with supervised aerobic exercise three days per week for two months. The assessments compared a baseline visit after a single supplement dose with the visit after the two-month combined intervention. The abstract reports a 17% improvement in CANTAB attention and psychomotor speed (p < .05), a 1.5-fold global brain ketone uptake relative to the single-dose visit, and no change in physical performance. The June 13, 2025 PIVOT presentation title independently identifies the same supplement combination and exercise. The latest registry still describes commercial MCT. [Official AD/PD abstract, PDF p. 45](https://info.kenes.com/Flip/ADPD25_ADPD25%20-%20Orals%20on%20Demand%20-%2027%20March/PDF.pdf#page=45) [Hospital 2025 presentation list, p. 85](https://www.cdrv.ca/clients/SanteEstrie/Sous-sites/Centres_de_recherche/CDRV/Recherche/Publications-scientifiques/Publications_2025.pdf#page=85) [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04322461)

## PARKA ketone salts and brain connectivity at PIVOT 2026

Roy and colleagues' June 12, 2026 PIVOT abstract explicitly cites NCT04322461 and describes ten people with Parkinson disease receiving beta-hydroxybutyrate salts twice daily and supervised moderate aerobic exercise for two months. This report does not specify nicotinamide riboside or mention MCT. It reports a 2.4-fold global brain ketone uptake relative to an acute single dose (p < .001), no change in cerebral glucose uptake, and increases of 52% and 53% in somatomotor and limbic network connectivity (p = .005 and .013). It reports a 16% improvement in CANTAB attention and psychomotor speed without naming individual tasks or providing uncertainty for that estimate. The participant count, accession and intervention timing overlap the 2025 report. The different ketone-uptake ratios and cognitive percentages cannot be reconciled from the abstracts. They report one combined intervention without an independent exercise or oil group. No result isolates executive function or refined coconut oil for claim 0598. [2026 primary abstract, p. 14](https://doi.org/10.17118/11143/25386) [Institutional PDF](https://usherbrooke.scholaris.ca/bitstreams/122648a6-8090-41fa-936a-f234ee7408c7/download) [Registration and preserved version](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-WHO-CTG-Followup.md#parka-supplement-and-exercise-registration)

## Ketone ester beverage and cycling outcomes

NCT05948956 first posted results on April 28, 2026. At five cycling sessions the posted mean endurance time went from 26.31 to 31.89 minutes with ketone ester (n = 8) and from 25.84 to 33.21 minutes with electrolyte control (n = 10). At twelve sessions each posted outcome arm had two participants. The results table shows baseline and follow-up means without a between-group contrast, and its outcome denominator counts exceed the overall enrollment field when summed across the four arms. The registry gives no food-specific Parkinson fatigue or mobility estimate. [Official results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05948956?tab=results) [Local result table](../2020-2024/Source-Text/Trials/NCT05948956.txt#L313)

## NOPARK nicotinamide riboside outcome

Brakedal and colleagues published the NCT03568968 randomized trial on October 8, 2026. At 52 weeks, nicotinamide riboside had a 2.72-point greater worsening than placebo on the combined MDS-UPDRS outcome (95% CI 0.47 to 4.98), and a 4.22-point greater worsening on the Non-Motor Symptoms Scale (95% CI 1.07 to 7.37). The trial evaluates an isolated supplement and aggregate scales. It provides no frozen culinary ingredient or individual symptom estimate. [Primary abstract, PMID 42848371](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42848371/) [2018 registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03568968)

## Twelve-month gluten-free diet outcomes in Prague

The October 2026 late-breaking abstract LBA 14 reports 57 people with Parkinson disease in the Prague randomized, rater-blinded comparison. Thirty were assigned to the gluten-free diet and 27 to usual diet. The abstract reports no clear motor effect at one year. It gives a MoCA p-value of .046 without an effect size or a clearly specified comparison. PDQ-39 improved over time (p = .005) and versus control (p = .035). These are whole-diet results. The report gives no separate grain consumption, standing lightheadedness, anxiety or multiple-system-atrophy result. A 2025 Parkinsonism & Related Disorders conference item from the same group is indexed by title and DOI, with the results body unavailable on the publisher route. The initial one-arm Timed Up and Go signal in the 2024 abstract was followed by a larger randomized report with an inconclusive overall motor result. [MDS late-breaking report, PDF p. 33](https://mdscongress.org/IC26-LBAs.pdf#page=33) · [Earlier registry and reports](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-WHO-CTG-Followup.md#gluten-free-diet-in-parkinson-disease-and-multiple-system-atrophy) · [2025 conference item](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.parkreldis.2025.107638)

The Czech Ministry of Health amendment for grant NU21-04-00443 was first published October 29, 2025. It extended the project through December 31, 2026 for final analysis and publication, and reported that the last patient visit was planned for February 2026. The latest ClinicalTrials.gov record still shows the April 8, 2022 posting, unknown status and no posted results. The grant document gives a later project timeline and no outcome table. The 2025 IAPRD abstract is in volume 134 of *Parkinsonism & Related Disorders*, article 107638. Elsevier’s article API labels it closed access and returned metadata only. The official abstract HTML and PDF routes blocked access to the results, and the IAPRD ePoster information page had no public poster link. ## Synthetic glabridin analogue in early Parkinson disease

The October 2026 LBA 22 reports a randomized trial with 99 participants and a per-protocol efficacy set of 77. The 24-week mean MDS-UPDRS III changes were −1.37 and −2.83 in the two vutiglabridin arms and +0.73 with placebo. The reported placebo-minus-higher-dose difference was 3.56 points (p = .03). The abstract supplies no confidence interval for that contrast, and the official record had no posted results as of November 12. The candidate is a synthetic analogue of glabridin, a licorice constituent. The earlier An et al. study tested the analogue in models and reported motor and biological effects. Neither study tested dietary licorice. [MDS late-breaking report, PDF p. 53](https://mdscongress.org/IC26-LBAs.pdf#page=53) · [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06329141) · [2025 model paper and local text](Source-Text/PMC12535076.txt)

KoNECT lists the phase 2b follow-up for early Parkinson disease with 126 planned participants and an approval date of August 13, 2026. The public record shows that date as the most recent change and supplies no separate first posting date. It projects a September 2026 through December 2029 study period and contains no results. The intervention is vutiglabridin. [KoNECT trial record](https://trialforme.konect.or.kr/clnctest/view.do?clncTestSn=202600637)
