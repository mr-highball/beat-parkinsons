# Dietary trial follow-up, 2025–2029

## Wild blueberry powder in a motor performance trial

NCT07720869 enrolled 24 people with Parkinson’s and compared 22.5 g daily freeze-dried wild blueberry powder with matched placebo powder for eight weeks. Both groups used pineapple juice as the vehicle. The registry was first posted July 22, 2026 and records completion on April 18, 2025. Its November 12, 2026 API record gives no summary results or linked publications. Registered endpoints include gait velocity, gait variability, standing balance, and UPDRS Part III motor scores. [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07720869.txt) · [Primary registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07720869)

An LSU announcement on January 15, 2026 stated that Hondzinski’s lab observed a possible slowing of decline in walking variability associated with fall risk after daily wild blueberry intake. The lab, exposure, population and endpoint align with this trial, although the announcement gives no trial identifier. It provides no arm counts, arm estimates, uncertainty, walking conditions or fall events. The report supplies no results for facial expressiveness, constipation, voice volume or speech intelligibility, and the study used a powder beverage rather than the frozen hypotheses’ raw or frozen fruit. A quantitative outcome report and any separately reported UPDRS items remain open for claims `0300`, `0746`, `0830`, and `0912`. [LSU announcement](https://apa.lsu.edu/chse/news/2026/hondzinski-parkinsons-award.php) · [Investigator’s grant listing](https://www.lsu.edu/chse/kinesiology/files/curriculum-vitae/hondzinski-jan.pdf#page=15)

The registry gives an August 31, 2018 actual start and a July 17, 2026 first submission, after trial completion and the LSU announcement. A second submission on July 22 was posted on July 24. The archived version lists gait velocity, center-of-pressure ellipse area, step count and timed up-and-go as primary. OFF-medication UPDRS Part III and gait variability are secondary. ON-medication UPDRS Part III is listed among other outcomes. The Part III motor scales contain facial expression and a composite speech rating. A total motor score cannot supply separate loudness or intelligibility estimates. Constipation is absent from the registered outcome list. The two historical versions could not be compared through the public routes checked. ## Preliminary observations from the MCT crossover

Tidman, White and White’s recruitment deck uses the same scientific title, 36-week crossover structure and three diet phases as ISRCTN13221188. The PDF metadata date is October 8, 2024, and the publicly served asset has a July 2025 version timestamp. The deck describes up to five tablespoons daily of either coconut oil or MCT oil on the usual diet, and continued oil in the ketogenic phase. Its preliminary-results slide describes improvements in daily fatigue and sleep quality and reports less perceived cognitive fog in the two oil phases. It also mentions tremor and mobility improvements. The deck provides no analyzed counts, oil grade or product use by participant, phase-specific scores, comparator contrasts or uncertainty. Its sleep quality and perceived cognitive fog observations do not supply the registered Epworth Sleepiness Scale or cognitive scale results. The fatigue observation runs opposite to claim `0229`, but the oil identity and severity estimate are unresolved. Claim `0513` requires daytime sleepiness with virgin coconut oil, and `0598` requires executive function with refined coconut oil. The registry has no posted final results. ## Multi-ingredient fiber bars and bowel function

NCT07914387 was first posted on November 3, 2026. The protocol follows people with Parkinson’s and household members before, during, and after a fiber bar containing multiple plant ingredients. The recorded ingredients include oats, cherries, cranberries, cocoa, green tea polyphenols, and date paste in both flavors. The study plans to measure bowel function and gut inflammation. The registry snapshot lists the study as not yet recruiting and contains no posted results. ### Earlier NeuroFiber pilot

The Stanford protocol cites the separate Sorridi-sponsored pilot NCT07127120. The May 22, 2026 World Parkinson Congress abstract P26.07 reports 18 people with Parkinson’s receiving NeuroFiber daily for four weeks without a comparator. The trial register lists 20 enrolled. The sponsor separately reports that constipation improved in 63–75% of participants with constipation at baseline. The registry and congress abstract identify the bar by name and omit its complete ingredient list. The abstract gives composite findings and no separate food or pain and apathy results. The full American Academy of Neurology abstract P8-17.016 remains inaccessible through the routes checked. NCT07682532 was first posted on July 6, 2026. The registry snapshot lists it as recruiting with no posted results. L-theanine occurs in tea leaves and is supplied as a separate supplement in the study. ## Apples and lactulose in a high-fiber diet

Bedarf and colleagues reported the completed DRKS00034528 study in April 2025. Ten people with Parkinson’s and ten unaffected partners completed the four-week open-label intervention. It combined raw apples or dried apple rings and other high-fiber foods, including olive oil, with lactulose. The Parkinson’s group used a higher lactulose dose than the household controls. Motor scores decreased slightly in the Parkinson’s group with a paired p value above 0.05. The difference in the overall gastrointestinal symptom score between groups narrowed, which the authors note may partly reflect worsening among the unaffected controls. The between-group difference in stool frequency persisted. The study did not isolate rest tremor, the contribution of apples, the olive oil grade, or the effect of oil on constipation. This study maps to the raw apple and rest tremor hypothesis `0833` and the extra-virgin olive oil and constipation hypothesis `0072`. ## Australian Mediterranean meal-box pilot

The provisional Brain Bites PD entry ACTRN12625000607471p was posted June 12, 2025. It describes a two-week, uncontrolled meal-box study with Mediterranean-style meals and optional ingredients such as olive oil, quinoa, tomato, and lentils. The primary outcome is feasibility. Cooking and eating behaviors and quality of life are secondary. The available record lists no posted results and excludes people with diagnosed dysphagia. ## MIND diet feasibility in ALS and healthy controls

Morgan and colleagues reported preliminary results from seven weeks of MIND diet education in the October 6, 2025 NEALS proceedings. The abstract included 11 people with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and 20 healthy controls who completed the intervention. Average dietary adherence scores increased from 6.6 to 9.0 in the ALS group and from 8.0 to 9.32 in controls on a 13-point scale. The authors described it as part of a microbiome study. The investigators, participating institutions, diet education materials, and dietary tracking align with the Meals Study, registered in June 2026 as NCT07625605 after its May 2025 completion. The abstract gives no trial ID and no Parkinson’s outcomes. The registry lists 43 enrolled across ALS, Parkinson’s, and healthy control groups and records olive oil as one component of the intervention. [NEALS proceedings abstract 68](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mus.70018) · [Local Meals registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07625605.txt) · [Meals registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07625605)

## MED-PARK modified Mediterranean diet protocol

Pirovano and colleagues published the MED-PARK protocol in October 2025. NCT06705517 assigns a six-month modified Mediterranean diet or a control diet in people with Parkinson’s, with combined MDS-UPDRS Parts I and II as the primary outcome. Olive oil is one diet component. As checked on November 12, 2026, the registry lists recruitment and no posted results. This is a separate trial from the published five-week Mediterranean diet pilot with constipation outcomes. ## Ginger-PD supplement trial

NCT07882108 compares two doses of ginger extract in capsules for 12 weeks in groups with Parkinson’s or REM sleep behavior disorder and in controls. The study plans to measure ghrelin, tolerability, and gastrointestinal symptoms. The UK Health Research Authority summary links the Cambridge study to IRAS 359423 and records a favorable ethics opinion in November 2025. The November 12, 2026 registry check showed recruitment and no posted results. The capsules provide indirect context for the grated ginger and constipation claim `0581`. ## Swallowing difficulty in the oral supplement arm

The December 15, 2025 results posting for NCT02445651 lists nine people who started and completed the subsequently discontinued oral arm. The combination included 500 mg curcumin phytosome twice daily along with baicalin, reishi extract and omega-3. The sponsor notes difficulty swallowing multiple supplements, gives no count of participants who reported it and supplies no swallowing severity measure. This is a regimen tolerability observation related to grated turmeric and swallowing difficulty (`0225`). The registry gives no separate effect of turmeric, curcumin or another component. [Primary posted results and limitations](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02445651?tab=results) · [Local registry results](../2015-2019/Source-Text/Trials/NCT02445651.txt#L917)

The results table includes descriptive caudate and putamen transporter-binding changes for the oral arm of −0.031 (95% CI −0.381 to +0.232) and −0.032 (95% CI −0.463 to +0.190), respectively. The results description says the oral arm was left out of the analysis, and the posted statistical comparisons use the N-acetylcysteine and standard care groups. These descriptive imaging data supply no supplement-arm symptom estimates. ## RESET resveratrol trial

NCT07592767 assigns a trans-resveratrol formulation or placebo in people with Parkinson’s and primarily measures safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetics. On October 6, 2026 the sponsor announced that the first participant had received a dose. A 2022 pharmacokinetic study of the same formulation, NCT04668274, enrolled healthy volunteers. The two studies use a formulation rather than raw or frozen grapes, and no RESET outcome report was located on November 12, 2026. ## Apple adherence and motor outcomes in the Bedarf supplement

The original paper used food diaries to track raw apples or dried rings as a dietary adherence measure. Supplementary Figure 1A reports four participants below its adherence criterion, with two in each group. Figure 1D shows total Part III by overall fiber intake, and its higher-fiber result is inconclusive. The 114-page supplement, public script listing and published report give no individual rest-tremor scores. The authors describe other data types as available upon request. The diet had multiple foods and lactulose, leaving apple and olive oil effects on rest tremor unresolved.
