# Odor testing, fatigue education and food texture at the World Parkinson Congress

## Odor-panel stability in PREDICT-PD

The May 2026 proceedings report that 1,519 PREDICT-PD participants aged 50–80 completed a six-odor test between October 2024 and April 2025. Participants with diagnosed Parkinson disease, REM sleep behavior disorder, or pure autonomic failure were excluded. Cinnamon and watermelon served as scratch-and-sniff odors. Correct identification changed over the sampling period for both after adjustment in both models, as it did for petrol and lemon. The observations measure test stability over time, with no food administration or intervention on olfaction. [P38.06 in the original proceedings](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13360760/)

## Parkinson fatigue management sessions

Wan-Albert, Flynn and Ng reported on 16 community-dwelling people with Parkinson fatigue who participated in group education on exercise and sleep hygiene with follow-up over 12 weeks. Seventy-five percent reported improved exercise self-efficacy, and 56% improved on the Sleep Hygiene Index by more than the minimal detectable change. Participants' feedback mentioned avoiding caffeine and sweets in the evening. The abstract reports no measured improvement in fatigue attributable to caffeine or coffee. [P25.48 in the original proceedings](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13360760/)

## Watermelon as a swallowing education example

Scott's Parkinson presentation uses watermelon and cereal with milk as examples of foods with mixed consistencies. The original text describes a possible coordination problem when liquid separates from solids. The presentation is educational and supplies no participant-level watermelon exposure, aspiration or choking frequency. [P45.31 in the original proceedings](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13360760/)
