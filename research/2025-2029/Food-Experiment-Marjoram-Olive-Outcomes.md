# Marjoram and olive oil symptom outcomes

## Combined olive oil and dietary counseling for fall risk

VIVI-OLIVE, NCT07829484, first posted September 21, 2026, plans individualized exercise plus Mediterranean dietary counseling and extra-virgin olive oil versus exercise alone in older adults at high fall risk. Usual gait speed is the primary measure and falls are secondary. The exercise-only comparator leaves diet counseling bundled with the oil. The record was recruiting with no posted results. It does not specify cold pressing or a clinical Parkinson postural-instability outcome. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07829484)

## Refined and extra-virgin olive oil and gait speed

The FOOP-Sarc randomized trial first published March 5, 2026. Thirty-eight participants with probable sarcopenia received refined oil, extra-virgin oil or extra-virgin oil plus prebiotic, alongside common dietary and activity advice. The four-meter gait-speed comparison showed no clear difference among treatments at 12 weeks or at the 12-week follow-up. It measured speed and did not quantify stride length or enroll a Parkinson cohort. ## Refined olive oil taste and exploratory electroencephalography

The March 9, 2026 pilot compared oral perception of commercial refined and extra-virgin oils in six healthy young adults. The authors described different activation patterns and greater high-beta and gamma activation during extra-virgin olive oil perception. The acute sensory experiment did not measure attention impairment, motivation or hallucinations. [Primary paper, Methods and Results](https://doi.org/10.1002/fsn3.71636) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC12971614.txt)

## Marjoram extract and neuronal excitability

De Pascual and colleagues first published September 19, 2026. The hydroalcoholic extract reduced selected ionic currents and repetitive firing in bovine chromaffin cells. In primary rat cortical neuron cultures, the comparison on mitochondrial metabolic activity did not remain clear after multiplicity correction. The authors state that the assay does not establish cytoprotection. The paper cites the clinical marjoram pilot and gives no behavioral or patient symptom outcomes. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/ph19091493) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC13610231.txt)

## Mediterranean diet and intake monitoring after deep brain stimulation

NCT07187739 first posted September 23, 2025. The Turkish trial promotes olive oil as the main fat in Mediterranean diet counseling after deep brain stimulation. It plans food records and selected nutrition-related motor items, including chewing and swallowing. The registry does not assign an oil grade, isolate olive oil or list a separate stride-length, facial expressiveness or attention outcome. It has no posted results. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07187739) · [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07187739.txt)

## Marjoram oil massage and jaw ultrasound in bruxism

Tekçe, Alagöz and colleagues report a randomized 30-person comparison of marjoram oil manually applied as massage versus botulinum toxin. They report no clear between-group masseter thickness or elastography difference at any observed time. The study did not give dream-enactment or bruxism episode counts in the abstract. Its design, author and enrollment closely fit NCT06674733, but the conference record omits a registered identifier. ## Marjoram blend by inhalation and massage in a bruxism protocol

First posted 2026-06-24. The proprietary blend contains sweet marjoram, lavender and vetiver and is compared with sesame carrier by twice-daily inhalation and daily extraoral massage. Masseter thickness and elasticity by ultrasound are primary measures. Other measures include pain, headache, tooth sensitivity, fatigue and perceived stress or anxiety. No dream-enactment, polysomnographic REM without atonia or dietary exposure is specified. Status was active, not recruiting; no registry results were posted as captured 2026-11-16. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07665398) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2026-NCT07665398-Marjoram-Blend-Bruxism.txt)

## Common olive oil intake and longitudinal attention scores

Ni and colleagues published January 24, 2026. The prospective PREDIMED-Plus analysis followed 656 older adults with overweight or obesity and metabolic syndrome for two years. The baseline questionnaire recorded refined oil and olive-pomace oil separately, then combined them as common olive oil in the reported exposure. Attention was a standardized composite of cognitive test outcomes. In Table 2 the fully adjusted attention change per 10 g/day of common olive oil was −0.006 z-score units (95% CI −0.047 to 0.035, p=.771), and the top versus bottom tertile estimate was −0.073 (95% CI −0.209 to 0.062). The basic top-tertile estimate was −0.137 (95% CI −0.271 to −0.003) and attenuated with adjustment. Total olive oil had a different fully adjusted continuous association, +0.046 (95% CI 0.008 to 0.084). The study was observational and leaves the refined-only association and Parkinson symptom outcome unresolved. ## Posted olive oil placebo attention and reaction time results

First results posted May 26, 2026. In the olive-oil placebo group of 12, mean psychomotor vigilance response time changed from 400.0 to 406.6 ms and mean task-switching response time from 948.0 to 830.6 ms. The control incongruent-flanker mean changed from 570.3 to 545.4 ms. Across outcomes the within-placebo changes vary in direction. The other arm combined astaxanthin with olive oil. The trial gives no oil-free arm, verified oil grade, or Parkinson attention comparison.
