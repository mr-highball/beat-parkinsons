# Lemongrass preparation and symptom evidence

## Parkinson aroma oil case series and distinct questionnaire items

The same four-case Parkinson series already used in the archive gave inhalation of lemongrass oil, massage with plain jojoba and massage with jojoba plus 3% lemongrass essential oil over three sequential conditions. Table 15's columns for each case are Pre, inhalation, massage and aroma massage. General speech difficulty had fixed scores 2, 2, 2 and 0 across participants. Clear writing difficulty stayed at 0, 0, 1 and 1 and fear of falling in public stayed at 1, 0, 0 and 0. One participant reported less difficulty walking half a mile during massage and aroma massage and two participants improved on the 100-yard item, with no standardized postural challenge or observed falls count. For the combined item on distressing dreams or hallucinations, one participant had a score of 2 during baseline and the first two phases and 0 in the aroma-massage phase. The other three were 0 throughout. The questionnaire does not isolate low voice volume, character size, actual falls, dream enactment or Parkinson postural instability, and the intervention was nonoral. The report labels the oil lemongrass without an unambiguous botanical species assignment in the Methods consulted. ## Inhaled lemongrass and sleep quality in smokers

One hundred smokers aged 18–30 received a 3% Cymbopogon citratus essential-oil inhaler or a sunflower-oil placebo four times daily for 60 days. The abstract gives Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index means changing from 7.84 to 4.42 versus 7.68 to 7.54 (p < .001). Global sleep quality is distinct from witnessed dream enactment and the intervention was inhaled essential oil. The sample lacked a Parkinson cohort. The early-view article did not report a separate dream enactment result. Related nicotine-dependence registration NCT07366294 was screened, but cohort linkage was not assumed. ## Registered lemongrass extract and inhalation comparisons

NCT07195240 was first posted September 26, 2025. The completed 34-person study compares an oral extract with placebo and records aerobic capacity, exercise lactate, and heart rate. Its structured results remain unposted. The registered population and endpoints do not specify Parkinson symptoms or a culinary preparation. [Local registry extract](Source-Text/NCT07195240-Lemongrass-Aerobic-Registry-Extract.txt)

NCT07366294 was first posted January 26, 2026. The completed 200-person smoking study has control, inhaler, vapor and combined delivery arms. Sleep quality appears among the secondary outcomes. Its structured results remain unposted. The separately published two-arm report gives 100 participants and a different schedule, so the relationship between the studies is unresolved. Neither registry specifies a dream-enactment measure. [Local registry extract](Source-Text/NCT07366294-Lemongrass-Inhalation-Registry-Extract.txt)

## Inhaled lemongrass and insomnia scores in adult smokers

Kumboyono and colleagues reported a January 2026 quasi-experimental comparison of lemongrass oil inhalation and a sunflower oil comparator. Table 2 shows mean Insomnia Severity Index scores of 14.64 to 12.00 in the lemongrass group and 15.00 to 14.08 in control. It labels the posttest comparison p=.035. The Abstract and Methods state 100 participants. Table 1 counts and percentages imply 25 per group, and the reported ages need clarification against the eligibility description. A June report with four shared authors describes a similar inhalation formulation and schedule with a younger sample and PSQI outcomes. Participant overlap remains unresolved. Neither paper examines eating minced lemongrass, dream enactment or Parkinson disease. ## Lemongrass essential oil and separate first-fall latency and counts

First published September 30, 2026. Figure 4 shows shorter first-fall latency and more falls after 100 and 500 mg/kg oral lemongrass essential oil in healthy mice. The smaller dose had no marked contrast. Graphical readings are about 12, 8 and 7 seconds for vehicle, 100 and 500 mg/kg, respectively. The figure marks p < .05 at 100 and p < .001 at 500, while the Results prose says latency increased and describes both as p < .01. The Discussion describes a motor impairment signal. This is concentrated essential oil and a rod assay outside Parkinson disease. An earlier oral essential-oil experiment gave an inconclusive result for total accumulated time on a rotating rod.
