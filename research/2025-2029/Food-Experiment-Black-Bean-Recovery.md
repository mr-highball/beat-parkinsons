# Recovered black-bean evidence

## Canned black beans and rice in healthy adults

[Zupančič et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13494084/) was first published 28 May 2026 (PMID 42214492, DOI 10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101380; trial NCT06781723). In a randomized crossover study, 11 healthy physically active young adults were assigned two conditions; ten completed both. The food condition was 143 g canned black beans, drained and microwaved, plus 108 g rice cooked in a rice cooker. The comparison was a matched mixture of isolated amino acids and other nutrients. Postexercise myofibrillar protein synthesis was 0.057 ± 0.013 versus 0.052 ± 0.013 %/h, respectively (between-condition difference 0.005, 95% CI −0.008 to 0.018; p=0.260). The meal was tested as a combined food matrix, not beans alone.

This is actual human ingestion of canned black beans, but participants were healthy young adults and measured outcomes were muscle protein synthesis and related physiology, not Parkinson disease, insomnia, daytime sleepiness or postural instability. It does not favor either frozen claim direction. [Factual source extract](Source-Text/2026-Zupancic-Canned-Beans-Extract.txt).

## Registration of the black bean and rice crossover

The registry first posted January 17, 2025. Participants were assigned cooked rice and black beans or an isolated nutrient mixture after resistance exercise in a crossover comparison. The planned outcomes include skeletal muscle protein fractional synthesis and plasma amino acid concentrations. The registered meal arm lists 123 g each of rice and black beans. The 2026 report describes the served weights as 108 g rice and 143 g drained canned black beans. Neither record tests Parkinson disease or a sleep or postural outcome.
