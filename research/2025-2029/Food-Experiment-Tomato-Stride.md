# Tomato and stride follow-up

## Carotenoid Raman peaks and step length during VIRTREAD-PD

Gualerzi and colleagues, August 22, 2025. The VIRTREAD-PD analysis used paired serum samples from 30 people with diagnosed Parkinson's disease over eight weeks of conventional or augmented treadmill rehabilitation. Supplementary Table 1 compares baseline Raman signal areas of circulating particles at 1154 and 1514 cm⁻¹ with gait outcomes, controlling for age and disease duration. Each baseline peak area correlated −.49 with baseline maximum step length (p = .01 for each). The corresponding correlations with post-rehabilitation maximum step length were −.42 and −.41 (both p = .03). At baseline self-selected speed, step-length correlations were −.34 (p = .09) and −.37 (p = .05). The table states p < .05 as its criterion. The intervention was treadmill rehabilitation. The assay used lutein and β-carotene standards and the reported carotenoid peaks do not identify lycopene or cooked tomato intake. ## PRIME Parkinson diet and wearable stride protocol

The original protocol for NCT07097103 plans 80 participants across Mediterranean diet, physical activity, their combination and standard care. It explicitly defines same-foot stride length, height-normalized stride length and walking speed from a wearable inertial sensor. Methods plan baseline and repeated three-day food records and personalized seven-day meal plans with ingredient quantities. The recovered materials do not specify a cooked tomato arm or an individual-food stride analysis. The official register currently gives no results. ## Isolated lycopene and open field and pole outcomes

The July 2026 mouse paper compared 5 mg/kg lycopene by oral gavage with MPTP model controls. Fig. 2 reports open-field movement distance and speed, pole turn and downward arrival times, tail-suspension immobility and rotarod fall latency, with five animals per group used for the behavior analyses. Lycopene increased several open-field activity measures, shortened pole turn and descent times and improved rotarod retention. No footprint or stride endpoint appears among the reported measures. [Local licensed article and Fig. 2 caption](Source-Text/PMC13416145.txt) · [Primary paper, PMID 42514303](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13416145/)

## Tomato sauce in an Italian Parkinson dietary questionnaire

Gigante and colleagues' 2025 case–control study included pasta or rice with tomato sauce and tomatoes in season as separate questionnaire rows. Neither questionnaire row has a displayed loading in the reported table of dietary pattern factors. The modeled clinical outcome is case status. The report does not estimate either food item's association with a gait or stride outcome. [Primary report, PMID 41148145, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13347557/)
