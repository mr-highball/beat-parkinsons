# Wild blueberry powder motor trial recheck

The primary registry NCT07720869, first posted July 22, 2026 after its reported April 18, 2025 completion, describes 24 people with Parkinson's assigned 22.5 g daily freeze-dried wild blueberry powder or matched placebo in pineapple juice for eight weeks. OFF-medication UPDRS Part III is registered as a secondary outcome and ON-medication Part III as another outcome. Facial expression and speech are scale components, not reported separately in the available registry; speech combines voice and intelligibility dimensions. No study results or individual-item tables were posted in the November 15, 2026 04:59 UTC official API recheck. A prior institutional announcement describes preliminary walking variability but has no quantitative facial, voice or intelligibility result and no study identifier. The planned powder drink differs from raw or frozen whole fruit, and registration does not verify individual consumption. ## Infant blueberry powder and unreported constipation items

2025-08-28. Venter and colleagues randomized infants to freeze-dried highbush blueberry powder or matched placebo. Of 76 enrolled, 30 active and 31 placebo participants completed the study. Caregiver symptom questions included constipation. Table 2 and the supplement provide no separate constipation symptom count or between-arm estimate. The separate September microbiome article explicitly reuses the same 61 completers. See NCT05006989. [Primary report, section 2.6 and Table 2](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu17172795)

## Nested blueberry powder microbiome analysis

2026-08-21. Millar and colleagues reported 18 participants from NCT05817383, nested in the Berries and Steps parent NCT05735587. Stool samples supplied microbial genes and short-chain fatty acids, without a separate reported bowel severity result. The parent trial’s adverse events are retained separately and count once. ## Live blueberry kombucha and stool form

2026-09-18. The authors assigned adults to live blueberry-flavored kombucha or sterilized kombucha for 28 days. Production used boiled blueberry syrup and prepared both live and sterilized formulations with and without syrup. The clinical Methods describe the control as sterilized kombucha, while the clinical product comparison is presented as a live-versus-sterilized comparison. The live group had fewer hard stool types and more type 4 stools within the group. The day-28 cross-group type 2 difference (p = .012) is described as lower in the control group. Chronic constipation was an exclusion criterion. The text gives no raw blueberry contrast or clean separation of blueberry from the other formulation differences. [Primary report, sections 2.2, 2.8 and 3.6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13587112/) · [Local source text](Source-Text/PMC13587112.txt)

## Hoarseness initially attributed to blueberries

Published December 2, 2025. Hoarseness and swelling in a young adult were initially attributed to frequent blueberry intake and briefly improved after avoidance. Symptoms progressed despite avoidance. Imaging found an anterior mediastinal mass encasing major vessels and partially compressing the trachea. Histology indicated a round cell neoplasm, with exact subtype unconfirmed because immunohistochemistry was declined. The case gives no measured vocal intensity, controlled rechallenge, verified berry preparation or Parkinson diagnosis. The clinical timeline does not establish blueberry causation. ## Blueberry powder conference report on stool microbiota and mood

The Millar conference report describes a 12-week randomized comparison of freeze-dried blueberry powder and placebo in sedentary older adults with mild depressive symptoms. Twenty-two completed, and ten blueberry and eight placebo participants supplied stool for microbiome analysis. The report describes no clear within-group change in inflammation, motivation, steps or microbial diversity. It reports an exploratory change in selected microbial enzymes and preliminary mood results. Stool analysis did not provide stool frequency or constipation severity. The later paper identifies the microbiome substudy NCT05817383 and parent NCT05735587; these reports should not be counted as independent trials.
