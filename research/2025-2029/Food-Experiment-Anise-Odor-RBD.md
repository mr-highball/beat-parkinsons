# Anise odor and REM sleep behavior disorder screening

## Aniseed as an odor in Chinese Parkinson and RBD screening

An [October 1, 2026 diagnostic study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13677288/) (PMID 42898076, DOI 10.1016/j.prdoa.2026.100507) compared odor identification in discovery and validation cohorts of people with Parkinson disease, REM sleep behavior disorder (RBD), and healthy controls. A culturally adapted odor panel included a scent labeled aniseed. The validation cohort contained 48 people in each of the Parkinson, RBD and control groups. This was odor identification, not consumption of ground *Pimpinella anisum*; the paper does not establish that scented material's botanical identity or estimate an effect on dream-enactment frequency. The original 2021 three-odor model (PMID 33627500) likewise used anise, licorice and banana as smell-test labels. [2026 source extract](Source-Text/2026-Anise-Odor-RBD-Extract.txt).

## Sesame among the diagnostic odors

In the 2026 diagnostic discovery cohort, the odor labeled sesame had AUC 0.655 (95% CI 0.566–0.738) for discriminating Parkinson disease and healthy controls. The source separately cites earlier diagnostic adaptations using sesame oil as a replacement scent. The discovery test is an odor stimulus and does not supply cold-pressed oil identity, oral intake or a treatment effect.
