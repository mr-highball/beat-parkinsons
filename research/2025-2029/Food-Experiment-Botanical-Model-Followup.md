# Botanical preparations in Parkinson’s models, 2025–2029

## Clove oil and oxidative markers in cell culture

Hamdi and colleagues published this cellular study December 15, 2025. Clove essential oil reduced oxidative DNA damage in SH-SY5Y cultures in Figure 1 and was associated with higher antioxidant protein expression in Figure 3. The reported oil composition differs from the 2023 report, with 84.49% eugenol compared with 85.61% in the earlier paper. The later paper gives additional gene and protein measurements. The shared investigators and similar cell model make the degree of experimental overlap uncertain. The paper gives no behavioral measurements or oral exposure. ## Black pepper essential oil and zebrafish behavior

Silva and colleagues published the study on February 11, 2025. They exposed zebrafish embryos and larvae to *Piper nigrum* essential oil, rotenone and levodopa in the water. The manufacturer reported β-caryophyllene (25.5%), limonene (19.8%), β-pinene (13.8%) and eugenol (7.2%) among the oil constituents. Behavioral testing at 144 hours after fertilization included only larvae without visible malformations. The authors report no detectable change in thigmotaxis or touch response in Figure 6. Its caption gives omnibus F(6, 34) values of 2.03 and 1.92 alongside p < .05 for both. Those F values correspond to p values above .05. [Journal record](https://journalacri.com/index.php/ACRI/article/view/1082) · [Original article, Table 1 and Figures 6–7](https://www.journalacri.com/index.php/ACRI/article/download/1082/2453/1625) · [HAL record](https://hal.science/hal-05049440v1) · [OpenAlex W4407375428](https://openalex.org/W4407375428)

For Figure 7, the Results describe impaired optomotor response with the highest oil concentration alone and a retained response with the lowest oil concentration plus rotenone. The tested changes were initial-to-final within each group. The paper nevertheless concludes that the oil had no general behavioral or protective effect. The study did not assess the frozen black pepper outcomes or administer ground or cracked pepper as food. The publisher PDF carries a CC BY 4.0 notice on its final page. ## High-fat feed and beam walking in a multiple system atrophy model

Arotcarena and colleagues published this mouse study on June 29, 2026. The four groups compared wild-type and transgenic mice modeling multiple system atrophy under standard chow and D12451 feed with 45% of calories from fat. The supplier lists lard and soybean oil in D12451, with several other ingredients. In Figure 4F the authors report more beam-walking errors in transgenic mice on high-fat feed than in wild-type mice on the same feed. This result compares genotypes under the same exposure. The two feed formulas differ in several components, and the report gives no isolated soybean oil effect, refining grade, or human walking speed comparison. [Primary report, Methods and Figure 4F](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.70424) · [Feed formulation](https://www.researchdiets.com/en/formulas/d12451)


## Pea lectin and motor and memory proxies in rats

The July 19, 2026 Peshattiwar preprint describes a longer-duration paraquat and isolated lectin rat model derived from the 2018 *Pisum sativum* protein experiments. The methods assign 32 animals to the combined exposure and six to each of three comparison groups. The combined group showed progressive deficits in forelimb placement and lower Y-maze spontaneous alternation at week 12. The isolated lectin group had no clear Y-maze change over follow-up. The preprint explicitly names pea as the lectin source for the earlier model. Its new Methods calls the reagent lectin and gives no product number. The April 2026 Swain paper used a separate rat experiment to test chronic levodopa after the combined exposure. The October late-breaking abstract LBA 13 describes a separate mechanistic comparison of four successfully targeted animals and four controls. These reports examine toxin exposure and disease mechanisms in rats. Cooked-pea intake and human executive function, walking speed and falls remain untested. ## Squalamine in the expanded rat model

The November 6, 2026 Swain report describes 93 rats in the paraquat and lectin model and 37 receiving squalamine in drinking water. The abstract reports fewer motor, gastrointestinal motility and sleep abnormalities with squalamine. It gives no cognitive effect estimate. An earlier report from the same team used a 33-rat comparison. The shared model and intervention make overlap plausible, and the accessible reports do not identify individual animals. The 2026 abstract names lectin without species or preparation details. The related 2018 model specifies an isolated pea protein. These comparisons provide no cooked-pea, fish or fish-oil intake outcome.
