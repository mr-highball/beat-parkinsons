# Step-length learning under localized capsaicin and heat

## Step lengths during gait learning and retention across pain contexts

Jackson and colleagues randomized 30 young healthy adults into no pain, pain during training, and pain during both training and retesting. The two pain conditions used localized capsaicin cream and heat. Figure 4 shows no clear group difference at baseline or late training. At early next-day retesting, learned step-length asymmetry was 7.1% in controls versus 3.4% and 3.6% in the two pain groups. The journal report followed an apparently matching 30-person three-arm poster. The manipulation concerns learning retention and does not estimate a culinary spice effect on natural stride length. ## Step-length task in younger and older adults with localized pain

A separate 60-person trial published in 2026 linked to NCT05471557 compared capsaicin-plus-heat pain with no stimulus in healthy younger and older adults. The reported asymmetry during learning was 6.6% under pain and 8.1% in controls (p=.02), and next-day retention was 2.6% and 5.9% respectively (p<.01). This differs from the earlier young-adult finding of no clear learning effect. Age did not interact with pain at retention. The task concerns retention of an asymmetry produced by distorted visual feedback and excludes participants with neurological conditions. ## Paprika oleoresin as a colorant in a clinical sleep trial

Lavender and colleagues first published February 16, 2026. Twenty participants with insomnia completed a randomized three-period crossover of two cannabinol conditions and matched placebo. The liquid preparation lists paprika oleoresin as a colorant and the paper describes placebo as excipient only. Its polysomnography found inconclusive wake after sleep onset after either cannabinol dose versus placebo. Some secondary sleep measures favored the higher cannabinol arm. The Methods print concentrations inconsistent by a factor of ten with the reported dose totals and volumes. The publication gives no separately assigned paprika exposure, paprika amount, ground culinary preparation or Parkinson subgroup.
