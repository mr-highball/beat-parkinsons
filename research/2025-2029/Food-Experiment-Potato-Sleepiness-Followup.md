# Potato-containing meals and daytime sleepiness

## Dietary processing and daytime sleepiness in a community cohort

Wang et al. analyzed NHS/HPFS participants without a history of Parkinson disease, with repeated dietary questionnaires and later Epworth Sleepiness Scale screening, defining excessive daytime sleepiness as a score above 9. Potato/corn chips were treated as non-ultraprocessed in the primary analysis because classification evidence was insufficient. The paper reports extreme quintile comparison for ultraprocessed food intake was not statistically significant for excessive daytime sleepiness, though an overall trend was reported. It does not isolate boiled potato or assess treatment in diagnosed Parkinson disease. These are overlapping NHS/HPFS cohorts with the Molsberry dietary-pattern analysis, not independent populations. [University-hosted full primary report](https://edepot.wur.nl/694829).

## Registered potato salad meal and postprandial sleepiness

UMIN000062693, disclosed 3 September 2026, describes a single-meal comparison in healthy Japanese males aged 20–59, target 40. One condition is 73.7 g white bread; the other is 56.7 g white bread plus 80 g potato salad containing 6.8 g reduced digestible dextrin. Outcomes include postprandial visual analogue and Stanford Sleepiness Scale scores alongside continuous glucose monitoring. The registration title calls it randomized crossover, while structured fields label it nonrandomized, single-arm and self-controlled. It says results are unpublished and recruitment preinitiation. Actual ingestion, potato boiling, Parkinson disease and results are not established; the altered bread amount and added dextrin further prevent a potato-only interpretation. [Official UMIN registry](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/icdr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000071097).

## Dietary processing and questionnaire-defined probable REM behavior

First published May 7, 2025. The Nurses’ Health Study and Health Professionals Follow-Up Study used repeated food questionnaires and later questionnaire-defined probable REM sleep behavior disorder. Higher cumulative ultraprocessed-food consumption was associated with increased odds of probable REM sleep behavior disorder. The analysis overlaps the cohorts used in the earlier Molsberry diet-quality paper. Different food class definitions and estimands preclude treating their results as repeated estimates of the same contrast. The reported analyses do not isolate raw carrot or count episodes. ## Potato chips in a midlife sleep trial

First posted January 13, 2026. The planned 72-participant study in midlife women with poor sleep compares potato chips and pistachios for 30 days under matched healthy-habits coaching, with a separate sleep-coaching arm. Primary PSQI and secondary PROMIS Sleep Related Impairment, vigilance testing and Insomnia Severity Index include daytime effects. The current record is recruiting without posted results and contains no boiled-potato or Parkinson arm. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07337915) · [Local text](Source-Text/2026-NCT07337915-Potato-Chip-Sleep-Registration.txt)

## Registered baked and fried potato experiment A

First posted November 17, 2025. Institutional identifier REB 25-172 [A] compares baked home fries, baked russet potato, baked fries, quick-serve fried fries, baked cauliflower and no side in healthy adults. An acute secondary emotion inventory includes sleepiness. Recruiting without posted results. The registry does not assign boiled potato or measure Parkinson daytime sleepiness. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07230951) · [Local text](Source-Text/2025-NCT07230951-Potato-Emotions-Registration.txt)

## Registered baked and fried potato experiment B

First posted October 2, 2026. Institutional identifier REB 25-172 [B] specifies a distinct second experiment of the same program. Baked and fried potato or baked cauliflower or no side accompany an experimental meal. Sleepiness contributes to an acute emotion inventory and next-morning sleep quality is assessed with five visual analogue items. Recruiting without posted results. No boiled-potato or Parkinson arm and no confirmed participant overlap with experiment A.
