# Risk and prevention

### 2026 — Mexican crop-growing regions and Parkinson prevalence

Martinez et al.'s May 28, 2026 primary report compares annual average Parkinson case counts across Mexican states from 2015 to 2024. Northern and western groups averaged 13.8 and 8.6 cases per 100,000, respectively, versus 4.9 in the rest of the country. The reported Poisson prevalence ratios were 2.57 (95% CI 2.42–2.74) for the north and 1.54 (1.44–1.63) for the west. The original congress abstract lists cultivation of agave, avocados and limes in the west and corn, tomatoes, beans and apples in the north. The analysis has no participant-level food intake, pesticide measurements or symptom outcomes. [Original journal report, Figure 1 and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnagi.2026.1832314) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC13253685.txt) · [Related congress report 36309](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/the-green-parkinsons-belt-an-ecological-study-of-agricultural-regions-linked-to-increased-disease-burden-in-mexico/).

### 2026 — Caffeinated drinks as a background variable in PPMI

Martinez et al.'s April 14, 2026 preprint compares 8,795 self-reported Parkinson cases and 22,861 controls on occupational solvent history. The matching congress poster's Table 1 reports weekly caffeinated-drink frequency (23.16 ± 18.80 with Parkinson disease and 24.45 ± 18.98 in controls, p < .001). The preprint includes caffeine as a covariate in the solvent models. The poster also reports depression and MDS-UPDRS scores as group characteristics without testing a caffeine-to-symptom relationship. The report provides no named beverage intake or Parkinson symptom estimate by caffeinated drink. [Primary preprint](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-9294981/v1) · [Local licensed preprint text](Source-Text/2026-Martinez-PPMI-Occupational-Solvents.txt) · [Congress record 37436 and Table 1](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/occupational-solvent-exposure-and-parkinsons-disease-an-observational-study-in-ppmi-online/).

### 2025 — Incidence and survival across eight parkinsonian syndromes

A UK observational study used linked healthcare records from 2003 to 2023 to compare diagnosis rates and survival across Parkinson’s disease and rarer parkinsonian conditions. Recorded incidence of rarer syndromes rose in the years before the pandemic, while adjusted Parkinson’s incidence initially fell and then stabilized. Recognition and coding changes can influence apparent trends. First published online in 2025. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41125074/)
