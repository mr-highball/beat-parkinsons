# Seafood and Parkinson’s symptom evidence

## Dysarthria after shrimp scampi

Abusharif and colleagues’ April 2025 conference vignette reports acute dysarthria and limb weakness after shrimp scampi in a 51-year-old woman with fever and confusion. The authors suspected paralytic shellfish poisoning clinically and reported no toxin confirmation. Blood cultures identified Klebsiella pneumoniae. The vignette does not identify how the shrimp was cooked or report a speech intelligibility score, falls, or visual hallucinations. [Primary conference report](https://shmabstracts.org/abstract/toxins-at-the-table-a-rare-case-of-paralytic-shellfish-poisoning-and-klebsiella-bacteremia/)

## Pooled fish exposure and overactive bladder

Wang and colleagues analyzed 23,104 NHANES participants under 65 years of age. In adjusted component analyses, higher pooled fish intake was associated with lower odds of overactive bladder (OR 0.979, 95% CI 0.967–0.991). The outcome combined urge incontinence and nocturia using a composite threshold. The survey analysis did not isolate grilled sardines, urgency without leakage, or Parkinson’s. [2025 primary report](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41043-025-01100-0)

## Shrimp and krill surimi and constipation in mice

Tan and colleagues published online on December 24, 2025, ahead of the January 2026 issue. The surimi included whiteleg shrimp and Antarctic krill. The loperamide experiment randomized 12 mice per group, and Figure 3 reports six mice per group for the stool outcomes. Relative to the model group, six-hour stool pellet counts increased by 127.82% with the high-protein gel diet, 211.75% with the high-protein and dietary-fiber gel diet, and 183.69% with the dietary-fiber diet containing inulin and sodium alginate without surimi. The latter two groups also had wetter stools. The study provides neither a boiled-shrimp comparison nor a Parkinson’s constipation outcome. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41517127/) · [Local CC BY article text](Source-Text/PMC12786157.txt)

## Pooled sardine and seafood variables in Parkinson cohorts

Gigante and colleagues first published an Italian case–control analysis on October 28, 2025. The food questionnaire grouped tuna and sardines in oil and grouped boiled or broiled fish and mollusks. The analysis addressed case–control diagnosis status through dietary patterns and provided no separate sardine or shrimp symptom effect. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41148145/)

The 2026 UK and Italian microbiome cohort data include an oily-fish frequency item with sardines among its examples, HADS anxiety and depression scores, daytime sleepiness, the total SCOPA urinary score, and MDS-UPDRS parts I–IV totals. The article reports associations across microbiome change groups without a food-to-symptom comparison. The dataset combines fish species and preparations and omits individual MDS-UPDRS item scores. The 2026 discordant-twin cohort grouped canned sardines with other preserved fish. These cohorts have no separate sardine estimates for fatigue, speech intelligibility or urinary urgency.
