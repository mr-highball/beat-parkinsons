# Food and Nutrition

### 2025 — Filtered coffee crossover in Parkinson’s and stroke

Sachett and Nepomuceno’s March 13 paper reports a randomized, single-blind crossover in six people, three with Parkinson’s and three with stroke. The investigators used paper-filtered caffeinated and decaffeinated coffee. Table 4 gives pooled mean Timed Up and Go results of 11.3 versus 11.2 seconds (p = 0.391) and six-minute walk results of 379.2 versus 380.0 meters (p = 0.962). Post-walk systolic pressure was 119.0 versus 111.7 mm Hg (p = 0.028). The MoCA comparison was inconclusive under both the table and narrative p values, which differ. The study reports no Parkinson-only estimate and no fatigue outcome. [Journal record](https://www.rbpfex.com.br/index.php/rbpfex/article/view/2978) · [Full paper, Methods and Table 4](https://www.rbpfex.com.br/index.php/rbpfex/article/download/2978/2028/) · [Earlier university record](../2020-2024/Food-and-Nutrition.md#2024--repository-record-for-the-brazilian-filtered-coffee-crossover)

### 2025 — Mixed-meal blood pressure response in autonomic failure

A retrospective comparison examined 13 patients with Parkinson’s disease and 13 with multiple system atrophy who had autonomic failure. All participants received the same 450-kcal mixed meal. The median largest systolic drop within two hours was 41.7 mm Hg in the Parkinson group and 28.6 mm Hg in the multiple system atrophy group. The authors report the meal’s macronutrient proportions and measure post-meal blood pressure with participants seated and their feet elevated. The comparison gives no effect estimate for steamed brown rice or lightheadedness on standing. [Local primary full text, online May 13, 2025](Source-Text/PMC12325557.txt)

### 2025 — Four-strain probiotic in people with constipation

A placebo-controlled randomized study reported changes in some microbial taxa and inflammatory measures. Improvements in levodopa time-to-on and nonmotor scores were reported within the active group, with the strength of inference depending on the between-group analysis for each endpoint. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41126787/)

### 2026 — Mediterranean-ketogenic diet crossover feasibility

Two eight-week Mediterranean-based interventions compared a ketogenic version with one supplemented with medium-chain triglycerides. The ketogenic version produced ketosis more often, while preliminary patient-reported motor changes appeared after the triglyceride-supplemented version. A 37% dropout rate and modest adherence limit feasibility and efficacy conclusions. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41637153/) · [Local methods, safety and anxiety extract](Food-Experiment-Oil-Followup.md#kim-olive-oil-advice-and-individual-symptom-reporting)

### 2026 — Personalized plant-rich and time-restricted intervention

A six-month randomized trial of 40 people with Parkinson’s reported a larger improvement in motor examination scores under a personalized plant-rich diet and time-restricted eating program. Mean Starkstein Apathy Scale scores moved from 13.65 to 9.0 in the intervention group and from 13.05 to 13.6 in controls. The reported time-by-group analysis favored the intervention, while the post-intervention comparison in Table 2 gives p = 0.333 and a separate comparison in the narrative gives p < 0.001. Those conflicting endpoint comparisons need clarification. The report provides no apathy estimate for an individual food, including dried dates. [Primary full text, Table 2 and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13347589/) · [Trial registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06892418)

### 2026 — Prebiotic and short-chain fatty acids for constipation

The 2026 bowel report is a secondary analysis of the same three-arm cohort reported in 2025. Figure 1 describes within-group constipation-score improvements with 2-fucosyllactose at six months and with the combined regimen at three and six months. Its legends list 24, 23 and 24 participants. The main text gives no numerical outcome table or inactive control. The retrospective registry describes allocation as nonrandomized and lists 80 final participants, in contrast with the published randomized design and 72 assigned. No shiso exposure is reported. ### 2026 — Mediterranean diet counseling for constipation

A single-center randomized trial in 38 people with Parkinson’s and constipation compared immediate Mediterranean diet counseling with delayed counseling. Both groups reported more bowel movements during the first three months. The preprint and ReBEC results report a group-by-time odds ratio of 0.85 (95% CI 0.5–1.3) for bowel movement frequency and an inconclusive Bristol Stool Form Scale contrast. Olive oil was one component of the diet adherence measure. The comparison gives no separate estimate for extra-virgin olive oil (`0072`) or another food. The source XML dates preprint v1 to May 10, 2026. The registry cites May 7 and the journal report followed in September. ### 2026 — Nicotinamide riboside phase 3 results

NOPARK randomized 410 people with early Parkinson’s to the NAD precursor nicotinamide riboside or placebo for 52 weeks. The total clinical scale favored placebo by an adjusted 2.72 points, and one prespecified nonmotor scale also favored placebo. The result addresses this regimen and population, and the total-score difference was below the trial’s stated threshold for a clinically important worsening. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42848371/)

### 2025 — Diet quality and nutrition assessment in a Buenos Aires clinic

Pérez and colleagues’ June 1, 2025 report compared 45 participants with Parkinson’s and 36 comparison participants. The median Argentine diet-quality score was 55 versus 52 (p = 0.82). The report found no clear difference in its individual component scores. The Subjective Global Assessment placed 16 of 45 and 3 of 36 in category B. Dietary measures pool foods and the authors say the study did not measure constipation. The paper provides no individual-food symptom estimate. The publisher displays the complete article under CC BY 4.0. Direct PDF downloads from the journal and university repository returned HTTP 403 during archiving. ### 2025 — Food-frequency and symptom scores in a Brazilian case report

Lima and colleagues’ January 6 report records baseline food choices and clinical scores for one person with Parkinson’s. Table 3 names apple, watermelon, tomato and coffee with sugar among the foods eaten. The frequencies are for food groups. Baseline motor impairment is described in the clinical history and UPDRS section. The conclusion reports qualitative improvement after nutritional counseling and physiotherapy, with no post-intervention symptom scores or specific food changes. The measured exposures and outcomes cannot be paired into an estimate for any one food. ### 2025 — Smell scores and constipation in a Parkinson microbiome cohort

Yu and colleagues reported an observational study of 173 people with Parkinson disease. At baseline 172 completed Sniffin’ Sticks testing. In baseline multiple regression, a higher smell test total was associated with a lower constipation score (coefficient −0.333, SE 0.123, p=.007). The investigators collected food-frequency questionnaires and described diet mediation as a future analysis. The abstract gives no measured food or preparation effect on olfactory function. It cites the Canadian gut-microbiome study as the parent cohort. [Original society abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/the-role-of-diet-in-the-relationship-between-hyposmia-and-constipation-in-patients-with-parkinsons-disease/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2025-Yu-Parkinson-Hyposmia-Constipation-MDS-Extract.txt)

## Brazilian counseling handout and three-month bowel outcomes

The May 10, 2026 preprint for RBR-36q469s reports 38 people with Parkinson disease and constipation, 18 assigned to immediate Mediterranean diet counseling and 20 to delayed counseling. The primary outcome was days in the previous week with at least one bowel movement, on a scale from 0 to 7. At three months, the main analysis gives group-by-time OR 0.85 (95% CI 0.5–1.3, p=.490), and stool form beta 0.10 (95% CI −0.4 to 0.6, p=.732). Supplementary Table S1 gives the same OR and CI with p=.513 for the longitudinal model. Table S2 gives OR 0.85 (95% CI 0.5–1.4, p=.540) after excluding six waitlist participants with increased dietary adherence. The preprint abstract labels approximately 2.91 to 4.14 and 2.68 to 4.18 as six-month values. Table S1 shows approximately those changes at three months and larger values at six months. The September journal abstract agrees with three-month timing. Its full body remained inaccessible through the tested routes. The handout recommends olive oil, including on a sample salad. The examples give no dose or processing grade, and the results provide no participant-specific olive oil comparison.
