Title: Parkinson's UK forum discussions on cooked sweet potato for blended dysphagia meals, daytime low-protein timing, and an unverified nurse levodopa claim
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/broad-beans/44138
Source kind: Conversation
Publication date: 2025-03-11
Date basis: Opening post timestamp on Parkinson's UK public forum topic 44138 (2025-03-11; sweet-potato post 2025-03-16), cross-referenced with topic 29827 (opened 2021-10-16; sweet-potato blending reply 2024-02-19) and topic 30602 (opened 2021-12-29).

Across three public Parkinson's UK Community Forum threads inspected during Round 7 review, participants and caregivers discussed sweet potatoes in practical PD dietary contexts. In topic 29827 ("Blending food", opened 2021-10-16 by a caregiver whose mother struggled to eat solid food), a 2024-02-19 reply specifically listed cooked sweet potatoes alongside cooked carrots, spinach, bananas, and avocado as soft, creamy vegetables well suited for pureeing into dysphagia-friendly soups and smoothies. In topic 30602 (opened 2021-12-29), a member with an 8-year PD history noted that dietary protein routinely triggered 1-2 hour OFF episodes and sought satisfying immediate protein-free daytime meals—leading peers to share plant-based vegetable-anchored options. In topic 44138 ("Broad beans", opened 2025-03-11), a participant posted on 2025-03-16 that their Parkinson's nurse had recommended both broad beans and sweet potatoes as dietary sources of levodopa within an 80% vegetable/fruit low-protein diet; another member replied on 2025-03-18 cautioning that overly strict low-protein dieting accelerates age-related skeletal muscle loss. Reviewer annotation: unlike Vicia faba (broad beans), Ipomoea batatas (sweet potato) is NOT an established pharmacological source of dietary L-DOPA—the secondhand nurse attribution is unverified and illustrates why community nutrition claims require primary biochemical verification. All account names and handles are omitted.
