Privacy-Safe Summary of Observations:
A caregiver posting on the Parkinson's UK public forum reported that their spouse with Parkinson's disease experienced a sudden, severe episode of immobility ('legs completely frozen', unable to move) shortly after taking Bronchostop cough pastilles (an OTC UK herbal cough remedy whose active herbal extract is Thymus vulgaris / T. zygis herb extract, alongside marshmallow root in syrup variants and excipients such as gum arabic, sorbitol, maltitol, and berry/menthol flavorings) for a severe cough. Crucially, in a follow-up post on 2025-01-23, the caregiver stated that a similar abrupt freezing reaction occurred a second time after taking Bronchostop (informal positive rechallenge), that they reported the event to their GP, and that they contacted the Parkinson's UK research team to flag a potential interaction. The caregiver also speculated informally about thyme and thyroid hormone pathways (unverified).

Critical Appraisal & Confounders:
1. Acute intercurrent respiratory infection / severe cough is itself a well-documented physiological trigger of abrupt Parkinson motor OFF periods and freezing of gait.
2. Commercial OTC cough lozenges/pastilles contain concentrated thyme herb extract plus multiple excipients and are not equivalent to culinary fresh or dried thyme.
3. Unverified single-case anonymous caregiver report (n=1) without medical confirmation, levodopa timing records, or validated freezing scales.
Nonetheless, this repeated-exposure PD caregiver observation is preserved transparently as a safety/pharmacovigilance note requiring clinician discussion before PD patients use concentrated OTC thyme cough remedies.
