Title: Parkinson UK forum discussion on slow-motility PD constipation: cautionary account of persistent constipation despite whole-wheat biscuits and wheat bran
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/slow-motility-constiption/50272
Source kind: Conversation
Publication date: 2026-10-15
Date basis: Opening post date on public Parkinson UK Discourse forum (2026-10-15; community replies dated 2026-10-19 to 2026-10-21; inspected 2026-11-26 UTC).

Anonymized thread summary (opened 2026-10-15; replies 2026-10-19):
1. Opening post (person living with PD for 15 years post-diagnosis, with constipation beginning a decade before diagnosis): Described severe, relentless slow-motility constipation with absent bowel contractions, early satiety, bloating, and gas that failed to respond to daily exercise, high dietary fiber, continuous hydration, cutting out most refined bread and flour products, over-the-counter laxatives, suppositories, and enemas.
2. Caregiver reply (2026-10-19; spouse of a person with PD): Explicitly cautioned against assuming bulk cereal fiber resolves slow-motility constipation, reporting personal experience eating two whole-wheat breakfast biscuits plus two tablespoons of wheat bran and two tablespoons of milled flaxseed daily alongside 3 liters of water while STILL remaining constipated. Noted that insoluble wheat bran bulks out stool and can make it harder to move when transit is sluggish, whereas daily prune juice proved effective for both themselves and their spouse with PD.
3. Additional community replies (2026-10-19): One member with structural pelvic/spinal comorbidity described severe tenesmus and cramping requiring careful daily laxative titration; another (diagnosed 2008) reported success only with a strict multi-component regimen combining psyllium husk, olive oil, 3 L water, daily exercise, heel drops, and morning suppositories, while noting marked flatulence.

Interpretive limits:
Accounts reflect wheat biscuits and wheat bran rather than boiled intact wheat berries, involve severe multifactorial PD dysmotility and co-interventions, and provide no controlled comparison, yet they mirror clinical guideline caution (Van Der Schoot 2022 / BDA 2025) favoring soluble/osmotic strategies over insoluble wheat bran in refractory slow-transit constipation.
