Community/context: The survey speaks with 248 informants including 36 key informants and records zongzi-related preparation choices across the studied regions. Zongzi is associated with the Dragon Boat Festival and exhibits distinct local formulations.

Exposure: The study distinguishes culinary turmeric coloring and Zanthoxylum bungeanum seasoning from aromatic lemongrass ash in Tengchong rice and cinnamon-branch ash in other regions. It records duck yolk and quail eggs as fillings, and cites historic glutinous millet. Lanxangia tsao-ko is specifically Chinese cardamom, distinct from culinary Elettaria cardamomum. Different preparation types should not be collapsed.

Reported direction: The documented uses concern flavor, color, texture, ceremonial association and regional knowledge. No Parkinson symptom change is reported.

Authenticity: Published observations, original plant tables and literature sources are individually identified. The same research group separately studied wrapper leaves in 2019.

Efficacy evidence: This is food and local-knowledge provenance. There are no analyzed Parkinson outcomes, treatment comparisons or within-person symptom changes.

Open trail: Preserve plant species, plant part and whether material is a filling, seasoning, wrapper or processed ash when comparing this record with other ceremonial, dietary or medical sources.
