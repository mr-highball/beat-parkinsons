Reviewed: 2026-11-21 UTC
Location inspected: PubMed bibliographic record and full abstract (PMID 41071416).

Community/context: Interprofessional support-group focus sessions in the United States involving adults with dysphagia (caused by Parkinson disease, cancer, and cervical surgery) and their caregivers examining religious, spiritual, and cultural food participation.
Exposure and reported symptom: Examined how swallowing difficulty (dysphagia) disrupted participation in religious food rituals (explicitly citing communion), faith-based communal meals/social events, and consumption of traditional cultural foods, while religious/spiritual communities and beliefs supported coping, resilience, and caregiving decisions.
Reported direction: Unspecified for any single culinary ingredient; documents lived disruption of ceremonial and cultural eating by dysphagia alongside psychosocial resilience from religious/spiritual communities.
Evidence and limits: Qualitative support-group focus groups mixing multiple dysphagia etiologies (Parkinson disease alongside cancer and post-surgical causes); abstract does not report total participant count or isolate Parkinson-specific quotes.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed Journal of Religion and Health qualitative study (DOI 10.1007/s10943-025-02477-2).
Efficacy evidence: Documents ceremonial and cultural food-access impacts of dysphagia rather than therapeutic symptom efficacy of any food.
Related research: Pairs with existing ceremonial food records (research/2015-2019/Cultural-and-Experiential/Ceremonial-Accounts/2018-PMID29801593-Riyadh-Ramadan-Iftar-Dates.txt, research/2025-2029/Cultural-and-Experiential/Ceremonial-Accounts/2025-PMID39787165-Bradford-Iftar-Dates-and-Meal-Experiences.txt) and Parkinson swallowing/drooling qualitative accounts.
Access and follow-up: Full article behind Springer paywall; abstract reviewed in full. Parkinson-specific participant narratives and specific adapted communion/cultural food strategies remain behind paywall.
