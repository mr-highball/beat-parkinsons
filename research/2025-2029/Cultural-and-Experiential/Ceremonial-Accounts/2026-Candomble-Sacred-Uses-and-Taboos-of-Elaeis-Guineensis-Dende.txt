Title: Ceremonial role and dietary taboos (euo/quizila) of Elaeis guineensis and dende oil in Afro-Brazilian Candomble
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12844919/
Source kind: Ethnographic ceremonial record
Publication date: 2026
Date basis: Publication year of Azevedo et al. 2026 ethnographic study in Plants (PMID:41600067, PMC12844919); records longstanding Afro-Brazilian Candomble ritual practices.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Symbolic spiritual protection, ancestral mediation, and sacred offerings—alongside explicit ritual avoidance (euo/quizila) for specific deities; no biomedical Parkinson symptom claim.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed participant-observation ethnobotanical study (Azevedo et al., Plants 2026; PMID:41600067, PMC12844919).
Efficacy evidence: Ceremonial and cultural symbolism only; does not test or establish physiological or clinical efficacy for Parkinson disease symptoms.

Title and source: Ceremonial and ritual uses of Elaeis guineensis Jacq. (dende / oil palm) in Afro-Brazilian Candomble Nacao Angola, inspected via Azevedo et al. 2026 (Plants 15(2):260; PMID:41600067, PMC12844919).
Community/context: Candomble Nacao Angola communities in Brazil preserving West and Central African (Bantu/Yoruba) botanical heritage across liturgical food offerings (comidas de santo), doorway protection (mariwo palm fronds), and funerary/ancestral transition rites (Azerin).
Exposure and reported symptom: Whole oil palm plant parts and unrefined red palm oil (azeite de dende) used as ritual binding media and food offerings; notably balanced by strict ritual prohibition (euo/quizila) for deities such as Oxala, where dende is avoided in favor of honey or white foods. Spiritual protection and ancestral harmony are ceremonial concepts rather than clinical Parkinson diagnoses.
Evidence and limits: Documents biocultural significance and both positive ritual integration and explicit cultural avoidance boundaries without any clinical comparator, denominator, or Parkinson symptom measurement.
