Title: Cross-Cultural Ethnobotany and Adverse-Event Boundaries of Apium graveolens Leaves (Indonesia, Denmark, Italy, Trinidad & Tobago)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13186234/
Source kind: Traditional-practice and ethnobotanical record
Publication date: 2026
Date basis: Publication year of Indonesian Anak Dalam ethnobotanical review (Muhaimin et al., 2026, PMID:42163992 / PMC13186234), integrating Danish folk mood-herb screening (Jager et al., 2013, PMID:23266274), Bologna leaf ethnobotany (Chiocchio et al., 2024, PMC10936038), Trinidad & Tobago ethnomedicine (Lans 2006, PMID:17040567), and clinical dried-leaf hyperthyroidism (PMID:28458701) and allergy (PMID:6734454) reports.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: Mixed favorable traditional use and documented adverse safety cautions: Southeast Asian, Caribbean, Italian, and Danish folk traditions use celery leaf decoctions/infusions for hypertension, urinary/diuretic support, and low mood, balanced against clinical hyperthyroidism from heavy dried-leaf dosing (4 g/d x 45 d) and celery-mugwort-spice allergy.
Authenticity: Traceable to peer-reviewed ethnobotanical surveys (PMC13186234; PMC10936038; PMID:23266274; PMID:17040567) and peer-reviewed clinical safety reports (PMC5403020; PMID:6734454).
Efficacy evidence: Ethnobotanical and clinical-safety context only; distinguishes aerial leaf culinary/tea traditions from celery seed extracts and synthetic DL-NBP drugs without proving human Parkinson's symptom efficacy.

Ethnobotanical surveys across four continents record distinct folk uses of Apium graveolens leaves and aerial herb separate from concentrated celery seed medicine: Indonesia's Anak Dalam tribe in Jambi prepares celery herb as a traditional antihypertensive remedy (Muhaimin et al., 2026, PMC13186234); Caribbean ethnomedicine in Trinidad and Tobago uses celery infusions for hypertension and urinary/diuretic complaints (Lans 2006, PMID:17040567); rural Bologna traditions record culinary and digestive aerial use (Chiocchio et al., 2024, PMC10936038); and Danish folk medicine lists celery leaves among traditional remedies for depressed mood (screened in vitro for MAO-A inhibition by Jager et al., 2013, PMID:23266274). Crucially, human safety case reports counterbalance heavy supplemental leaf use: Rouhi-Boroujeni et al. (2016, PMC5403020) documented overt hyperthyroidism after 4 g/day dried celery leaves for 45 days, and Wuthrich & Hofer (1984, PMID:6734454) characterized IgE-mediated celery-mugwort-spice allergy capable of causing acute oropharyngeal swelling.
