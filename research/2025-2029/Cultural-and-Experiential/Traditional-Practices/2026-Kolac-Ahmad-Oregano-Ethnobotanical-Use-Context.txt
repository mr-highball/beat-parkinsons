Title: Eastern Anatolian, Moroccan, and South Asian ethnobotanical uses of wild and culinary oregano (Origanum vulgare L.)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12899098/

Publication date: 2026

Reported direction: High community informant consensus for traditional Origanum vulgare aerial-part infusions/decoctions in colds, stomach pain, digestive disorders, inflammation, and asthenia/insomnia inventories; no Parkinson's symptom efficacy measurement.

Efficacy evidence: Community citation inventories without clinical comparison, dosing standardization, or Parkinson's cohort; establishes traditional culinary/infusion context distinct from concentrated carvacrol-rich essential oils.

Field ethnobotanical surveys across Eastern Anatolia, North Africa, and northern Pakistan consistently rank Origanum vulgare L. among the most frequently cited Lamiaceae herbs in household traditional medicine. In Konak (Eastern Anatolia; 68 local informants, Kolaç et al., 2026, PMC12899098), Origanum vulgare subsp. gracile was among the top-cited species within high-consensus categories for colds (FIC 0.95), stomach pain (FIC 0.92), and inflammation (FIC 0.90). In Chail Valley, Pakistan (142 informants, Ahmad et al., 2014, PMC4022037), Origanum vulgare achieved among the highest relative frequency of citation values, primarily prepared as leaf decoctions for digestive and respiratory complaints. In Fez-Meknes, Morocco (120 herbalists, Beniaich et al., 2022, PMID:34031820), Origanum vulgare was listed alongside Origanum majorana in combined traditional inventories for asthenia, insomnia, and oral conditions. Across these traditions, household culinary leaves and aqueous decoctions differ sharply in carvacrol concentration and mucosal tolerability from concentrated commercial oregano essential oil capsules.
