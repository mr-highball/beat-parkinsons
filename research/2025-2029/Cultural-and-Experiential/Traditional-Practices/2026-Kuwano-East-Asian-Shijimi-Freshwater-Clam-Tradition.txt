Title: East Asian shijimi (Corbicula spp.) freshwater clam hot-water soup traditions for fatigue, liver recovery, and urination
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13256729/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2026
Date basis: Publication year of Kuwano et al. 2026 (Foods 15(11):1870, PMC13256729); explicitly distinguished from 1578 Ming-era Bencao Gangmu historical attribution.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional restorative dietary use of cooked freshwater clam broth for hepatic dysfunction, heat dispersal, urination, and physical fatigue; no traditional PD-specific efficacy claim.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed 2026 review (Kuwano et al., PMID:42279657 / PMC13256729) corroborated by Yu et al. 2020 (PMID:32061848).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional culinary-medicinal context only; modern clinical sleep RCT (Shimazoe 2022) and rat exhaustion study (Huang 2013) tested concentrated Corbicula extracts rather than whole cooked marine or freshwater clams in PD.

Published documentation (Kuwano et al. 2026, Foods 15(11):1870, PMID:42279657 / PMC13256729; corroborated by Yu et al. 2020, PMID:32061848) documents longstanding East Asian culinary and traditional-medicine use of cooked freshwater clams (Corbicula spp., known as shijimi in Japan and xian in Chinese materia medica) prepared as hot-water soup or broth.

Historical attribution (distinct from 2026 publication date): Kuwano et al. cite Li Shizhen's Ming-era Compendium of Materia Medica (Bencao Gangmu, 1578), which describes freshwater clams as dispersing acute heat, brightening the eyes, promoting urination, and easing damp-heat conditions, alongside Japanese folk practice consuming shijimi miso soup to support hepatic recovery and ease fatigue after alcohol or physical strain.

Culinary-biochemical bridge and boundaries: Boiled Corbicula soup releases ornithine, taurine, and phytosterols in protein-polysaccharide nanoparticles (Yu 2020), providing culinary context for modern freshwater clam extract studies. However, East Asian shijimi traditions concern freshwater Corbicula species rather than all culinary marine clams (e.g., Mercenaria, Venerupis, Ruditapes), and traditional folk use does not establish efficacy for Parkinson's symptoms.
