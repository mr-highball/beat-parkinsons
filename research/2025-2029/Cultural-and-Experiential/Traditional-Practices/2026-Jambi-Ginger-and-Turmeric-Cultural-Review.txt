Exposure and reported symptom: The authors attribute blood-pressure uses of a set of plants to prior reports about the Anak Dalam community in Jambi. Ginger is identified as Zingiber officinale and turmeric as Curcuma longa in the list. Other table entries distinguish Jamaica cherry, Muntingia calabura, and Indonesian bay, Syzygium polyanthum, from the similarly named culinary species in Round 1.
Reported direction: Lowering blood pressure is the reported intended use in the review. There is no measured Parkinson symptom response or clinical anxiety direction from the cultural account.
Efficacy evidence: This publication synthesizes literature and references earlier field work. The available 2012 provincial scan contains the survey summary and one result-table page, which does not include the ginger and turmeric blood-pressure entries. Those uses remain attributed to the later review and are not counted as newly observed field accounts.
Summary: Jambi ethnobotanical review describes ginger and turmeric in traditional blood-pressure contexts. It does not measure Parkinson anxiety or establish equivalent species for common-name matches.

Authenticity: Original published secondary synthesis. The available field report independently covers the survey context, with the specific blood-pressure entries read through the review.
