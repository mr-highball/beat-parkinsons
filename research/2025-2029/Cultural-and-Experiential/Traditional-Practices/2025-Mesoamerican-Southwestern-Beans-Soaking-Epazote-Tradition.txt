Title: Traditional Mesoamerican and Southwestern common-bean (Phaseolus vulgaris) preparation: soaking water disposal and carminative epazote pairing
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12251798/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2025-06-20
Date basis: Publication date of peer-reviewed open-access review of epazote (Dysphania ambrosioides) in traditional Latin American culinary/medicinal practice (PMC12251798, 2025-06-20; cross-linking common-bean soaking oligosaccharide assay PMID:12489819, 2002).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional culinary strategy aimed at reducing flatulence and bloating from common beans (including pinto beans) via soaking and carminative epazote; no claim for Parkinson disease symptoms.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed open-access ethnobotanical/phytochemical review in NCBI PMC (PMC12251798) cross-linked to domestic Phaseolus vulgaris soaking assay (PMID:12489819) and human pinto bean flatulence trials (PMC3228670), inspected during Round 6 review.
Efficacy evidence: Traditional culinary preparation and food-chemistry context only; explains real-world oligosaccharide management and thorough boiling (which also denatures raw PHA lectins) without establishing clinical PD symptom efficacy.

Title and source: Traditional Latin American epazote and common-bean preparation documented in PMC12251798 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12251798/, 2025-06-20), cross-linked with domestic Phaseolus vulgaris soaking oligosaccharide study (PMID:12489819, 2002) and pinto bean feeding trials (Winham & Hutchins 2011, PMC3228670).
Source kind: Peer-reviewed ethnobotanical and food-science documentation of traditional culinary practice.
Publication date and date basis: Published 2025-06-20 (documenting longstanding Mesoamerican and Southwestern domestic culinary traditions; retrieved 2026-11-26).
Community/context: Indigenous Mesoamerican, Mexican, and US Southwestern household cuisines where Phaseolus vulgaris (including pinto beans) serves as a daily staple.
Exposure and reported symptom: Dry pinto and common beans are soaked and drained before prolonged boiling (reducing raffinose-family oligosaccharides without impairing protein nutritive value per PMID:12489819, and thoroughly heat-denaturing raw phytohemagglutinin lectins) and traditionally simmered with small culinary sprigs of epazote (Dysphania ambrosioides) as a carminative to reduce flatulence and abdominal cramping. Safety literature notes epazote essential oil should not be consumed in excess.
Reported direction: Customary mitigation of bean-associated flatulence/bloating; no clinical claim for Parkinson disease.
Evidence and limits: Culinary ethnobotany and food-chemistry assays do not test Parkinson disease motor or non-motor symptoms.
Authenticity and efficacy: Authentic peer-reviewed documentation; complements human pinto bean feeding trials (PMC3228670) showing initial week-1 flatulence/bloating largely adapts within 2-3 weeks of regular intake.
Related research: Cross-linked to Winham & Hutchins 2011 (PMC3228670), Hutchins & Winham 2020 (NCT04003194), Bell 2024 (PMC11600120), and Zheng 2016 (PMC4780318).
Access and follow-up: Open-access PMC text and PubMed record inspected directly.
