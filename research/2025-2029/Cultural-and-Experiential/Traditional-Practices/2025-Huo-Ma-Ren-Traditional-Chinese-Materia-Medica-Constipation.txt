Title: Traditional Chinese materia medica use of Huo Ma Ren (Fructus Cannabis, decorticated hemp seed) for intestinal dryness and elderly constipation
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40796348/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2025
Date basis: Peer-reviewed herbal-history and phytochemistry review published in 2025 (Kong et al., Phytochemical Analysis); distinguishes modern publication date from historical Chinese materia medica attributions.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional moistening-laxative benefit for dry stools and elderly/fluid-deficiency constipation; no controlled Parkinson disease monotherapy efficacy claim.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed ethnopharmacological review (Kong et al., 2025, PMID:40796348, DOI:10.1002/pca.70014), cross-referenced with Xie et al. (2023, PMC10485653) and Taiwan NHIRD PD prescription records (Lin et al., 2020, PMC7873047).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional textual and historical usage record only; documenting classical moistening-laxative practice does not establish controlled clinical efficacy for culinary hulled hemp hearts in Parkinson disease.

Community and practice context: Classical Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) and East Asian medicinal-food ("medicine and food homology") traditions.

Historical attributions vs publication date: Kong et al. (2025, Phytochemical Analysis, PMID:40796348) and Xie et al. (2023, Horticulture Research, PMC10485653) summarize classical Chinese sources naming decorticated Cannabis sativa L. fruit (Huo Ma Ren / Fructus Cannabis) as a food in the Zhou Li (Book of Rites) and as a top-grade medicinal substance in the Shennong Bencao Jing, later elaborated in the Compendium of Materia Medica (Bencao Gangmu) and paired as the monarch herb in Zhang Zhongjing's Shanghan Lun formula MaZiRenWan (~200 CE). Publication dates of these modern reviews (2023-2025) are tracked separately from those ancient attributions.

Exposure, preparation, and reported symptom: Huo Ma Ren denotes dried ripe decorticated (hulled) hemp seeds rich in fixed polyunsaturated oils, traditionally crushed or decocted to "moisten the intestines and relieve dryness" for dry, hard stools in elderly, frail, or blood/fluid-deficient individuals rather than harsh purgation. Importantly, the widely used modern proprietary formula MaZiRenWan (prescribed in 2.12% of TCM formula visits among 9,117 Taiwanese Parkinson disease patients in Lin et al., 2020, PMC7873047) combines Huo Ma Ren with five additional herbs including stimulant anthraquinone-containing rhubarb (Radix et Rhizoma Rhei), so clinical trials of MaZiRenWan cannot be attributed solely to culinary hulled hemp hearts.

Evidence limits and follow-up: No modern controlled trial isolates culinary hulled hemp hearts for Parkinson disease constipation; single-ingredient rodent constipation models (Li et al., 2022, PMC9755208) align with the traditional moistening-gut hypothesis but require human PD clinical confirmation.
