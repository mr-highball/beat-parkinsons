Title: Mesoamerican historical heritage of Persea americana and contemporary culinary/anti-aging interest in avocado oil
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11858862/
Source kind: Scholarly ethnobotanical and nutritional review
Publication date: 2025
Date basis: Peer-reviewed journal publication date (Pharmaceuticals 2025; PMID 40006059, PMC11858862); discusses archaeological Mesoamerican origin (~10,000 BP) alongside modern preclinical anti-aging research.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Historical Mesoamerican cultures (Aztec, Maya, Olmec) revered Persea americana fruit as food and offering; modern Mexican and global interest promotes culinary avocado oil for healthy aging, without traditional or clinical Parkinson's symptom claims.
Authenticity: Verified via open-access PubMed Central full text (Torres-Isidro et al., Pharmaceuticals 2025; PMC11858862).
Efficacy evidence: Cultural/historical background and preclinical narrative review only; does not establish clinical Parkinson's disease efficacy.

Torres-Isidro and colleagues (2025, PMC11858862) summarize the Mesoamerican cultural history of Persea americana (archaeological records dating back ~10,000 years in Coaxcatlan, Puebla, Mexico; Nahuatl name ahuacatl; revered by Aztec, Maya, and Olmec societies) alongside contemporary Mexican and global culinary and healthspan interest in avocado oil due to oleic acid, tocopherols, and phytosterols. While traditional whole-fruit consumption is millennia old, refined and cold-pressed culinary avocado oils are modern industrial/nutritional preparations whose proposed neuroprotective or anti-aging roles rest almost entirely on cell and rodent studies rather than traditional Parkinson's remedies or human neurological trials.
