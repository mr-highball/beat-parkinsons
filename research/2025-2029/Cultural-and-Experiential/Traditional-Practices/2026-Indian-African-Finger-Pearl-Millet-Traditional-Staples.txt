Finger millet (Eleusine coracana, known as ragi in India and wimbi/ugali grain across East Africa) and pearl millet (Pennisetum glaucum, known as bajra) are ancient drought-resilient cereals central to traditional Sub-Saharan African and South Asian foodways. As reviewed by Bham et al. (2026, PMC13536604) and Shobana et al. (2013, PMID:23522794), communities traditionally prepare these grains as fermented or unfermented porridges, cooling ragi malt beverages, and unleavened flatbreads (roti/bhakri) to sustain physical energy among laborers, support weaning infants, and nourish older adults. Traditional culinary practices do not document Parkinson-specific motor or cognitive treatment effects; modern controlled feeding trials have examined young healthy Indian adults (Kalpana 2025; Tanwar 2025; Saifi 2026) and school-age adolescents (Scott 2018; Stephenson 2023) rather than people living with Parkinson disease.
