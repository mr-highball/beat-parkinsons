Title: Mediterranean and Traditional Persian/Unani medicinal and dietary uses of Vitis vinifera (grape)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11939682/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2025
Date basis: Publication year of Acero et al. 2025 (PMC11939682), cross-referencing Traditional Persian/Iranian Medicine review Dehdari et al. 2018 (PMID:29526242).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional dietary and humoral uses describe fresh/dried grapes, unfermented grape juice, and vine leaves for general nourishment, digestive/anorectal relief, vascular comfort, and inflammation; no traditional system isolates Parkinson motor symptoms.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed ethnopharmacological reviews indexed in PubMed Central (Acero et al. 2025, Antioxidants, PMC11939682; Dehdari et al. 2018, J Integr Med, PMID:29526242).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional and ethnobotanical context only; does not establish controlled clinical efficacy for frozen or raw grapes in Parkinson disease.

Historical and ethnopharmacological literature documents millennia of culinary and traditional medicinal use of Vitis vinifera across the Mediterranean basin, Unani, and Traditional Persian Medicine (TPM). Fresh and dried berries, unfermented juices, and vine leaves were traditionally consumed or applied for general vitality, mild laxative and anorectal/hemorrhoidal relief (Dehdari et al. 2018, PMID:29526242), vascular tone, and inflammatory discomfort (Acero et al. 2025, PMC11939682; EMA well-established venous use applies specifically to dried aqueous vine-leaf extract, not table grapes). Traditional culinary use of grapes does not address Parkinson neurodegeneration or controlled PD symptom outcomes.
