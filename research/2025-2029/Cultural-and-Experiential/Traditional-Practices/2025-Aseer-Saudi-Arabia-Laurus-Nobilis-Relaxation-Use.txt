Title: Traditional Oral Infusion of Bay Leaf (Alghar / Laurus nobilis) for Relaxation in Aseer Province, Saudi Arabia
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12125768/
Source kind: traditional_practice
Publication date: 2025-05-31
Date basis: Peer-reviewed ethnobotanical survey publication date (Alshaqhaa et al., 2025, J Ethnobiol Ethnomed, PMID:40450274, PMC12125768) documenting contemporary folk herbal use
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: favorable_relaxation_traditional_context
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed field ethnobotanical record specifically identifying Mediterranean Laurus nobilis L. (local Arabic name: Alghar, UV=0.01), distinct from Caribbean bay rum tree (Pimenta racemosa) and Indian tejpat (Cinnamomum tamala)
Efficacy evidence: Customary traditional practice only; lower evidential weight than clinical trials and does not independently establish Parkinson's anxiety efficacy

In the Aseer Province ethnobotanical survey (Alshaqhaa et al., 2025, J Ethnobiol Ethnomed, PMID:40450274, PMC12125768), true Mediterranean bay leaf (Laurus nobilis L., local name: Alghar; Use Value UV=0.01) is documented as an oral leaf infusion used traditionally for relaxation and immune support. This record complements Mediterranean and North African folk traditions (e.g., Tunisian herbal medicine noted in Chbili et al., 2022/2026 and Smach et al., 2024) while botanically separating Laurus nobilis from West Indian bay (Pimenta racemosa) documented in existing Caribbean archive entries. Customary infusion use for relaxation provides cultural context only and does not establish efficacy for ground bay leaf in Parkinson's disease.
