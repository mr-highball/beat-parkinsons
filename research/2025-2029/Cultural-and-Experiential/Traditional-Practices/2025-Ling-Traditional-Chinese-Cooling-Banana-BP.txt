Title: Traditional Chinese dietary classification of banana as a cooling ("yin") food tested for acute blood pressure effects
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39531559/
Source kind: Traditional-dietary concept tested in clinical crossover
Publication date: 2025
Date basis: Peer-reviewed journal publication date (Journal of the American Nutrition Association 2025; PMID 39531559, DOI 10.1080/27697061.2024.2426563).
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional Chinese food philosophy classifies banana as a cooling ("yin") food counterbalancing heat/blood pressure elevation; however, the authors' 16-adult crossover trial found no acute postprandial blood pressure change after banana.
Authenticity: Verified via PubMed abstract (Ling et al., Journal of the American Nutrition Association, 2025; PMID 39531559).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional dietary classification paired with a null 3-hour postprandial human crossover test; provides no evidence of orthostatic lightheadedness effect in Parkinson's disease.

Ling and colleagues (2025, Journal of the American Nutrition Association, PMID 39531559) documented the Traditional Chinese dietary philosophy classifying banana as a cooling ("yin") food in contrast to "heaty" ("yang") durian believed to raise body temperature and blood pressure. When tested directly in a randomized crossover of 16 healthy young Chinese adults (367.5 kcal isocaloric portions over 3 hours), durian acutely lowered blood pressure and elevated thermic effect of food and triglycerides, whereas banana produced no significant acute change in postprandial blood pressure. This preserves both traditional culinary framing and a null acute physiological test.
