Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Location inspected: Publisher full article, sections 2.2.8, 2.2.9 and 3.3.

Community/context: The authors combine the five-neighborhood inventory with a literature review of frequently cited species. The field list overlaps with the original 2024 report.
Exposure and reported symptom: Common sage is identified as Salvia officinalis, with dried leaves reported. The authors discuss antispasmodic reputation and digestive uses. Their true cinnamon section attributes use for susto, insomnia and fatigue, among other indications. The original field inventory uses a broader altered-nerves expression. The follow-up does not isolate culinary ground bark for Parkinson anxiety or identify symptom trajectories after sage.
Reported direction: Intended relief appears in reported use. There is no measured symptom direction for a frozen Parkinson claim.
Evidence and limits: Susto is a cultural illness category. It cannot be assumed to equal clinical Parkinson anxiety. Antispasmodic language does not distinguish clinical rigidity, dystonia or rest tremor. Constituents and other species in the paper's literature discussion must be evaluated from their own evidence.
Authenticity: Original peer-reviewed account, with the earlier clinical-terminology and community-inventory boundary documented.
Efficacy evidence: The report supplies preparation and cultural leads without isolating an ingredient effect in Parkinson disease.
