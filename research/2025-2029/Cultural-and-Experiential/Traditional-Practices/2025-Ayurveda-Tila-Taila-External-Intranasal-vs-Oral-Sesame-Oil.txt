Reported direction: Classical Ayurvedic texts prize sesame oil (Tila Taila) as the primary base oil for external warm-oil massage (Abhyanga), daily intranasal instillation (Pratimarsha Nasya), oral swishing/oil pulling (Gandusha/Kavala), and Vata-pacifying medicated oils used traditionally for joint stiffness, tremor-like Vata disorders (Kampavata), and mucosal dryness; importantly, most therapeutic Ayurvedic applications are external, intranasal, rectal (Basti), or polyherbal rather than plain oral culinary sesame oil.
Authenticity: Traceable to peer-reviewed clinical literature on traditional intranasal sesame oil (PMID:40716461) and Traditional Persian Medicine oil records archived in this repository (2025-PMID40271504-Mohammadian-Dameski-TPM-Almond-Oil.txt).
Efficacy evidence: Customary multi-route traditional practice only; clearly separates non-oral routes (dermal massage, intranasal drops, oil pulling, rectal enema) and polyherbal oils from oral culinary cold-pressed or toasted sesame oil, supplying no clinical efficacy sign for frozen oral PD claims.

In Ayurveda, Traditional Persian Medicine, and East Asian culinary traditions, sesame oil (Sanskrit: Tila Taila) occupies a central cultural role both as an everyday cooking/finishing oil and as the canonical carrier oil for traditional therapeutics. Classical Ayurvedic regimens employ sesame oil primarily externally for warm whole-body or joint massage (Abhyanga), intranasally as daily nasal oil drops (Pratimarsha Nasya, examined in Chaturvedi et al. 2025, PMID:40716461), orally for non-swallowed oil pulling (Gandusha/Kavala), or rectally as medicated enemas (Sneha Basti) formulated with multiple herbs for Vata disorders (including classical Kampavata descriptions). Because these practices rely on non-oral routes or multi-herb decoctions, they cannot be conflated with swallowed culinary cold-pressed or toasted sesame oil in evidence scoring for Parkinson disease symptoms, and sesame remains a major IgE-mediated food allergen.
