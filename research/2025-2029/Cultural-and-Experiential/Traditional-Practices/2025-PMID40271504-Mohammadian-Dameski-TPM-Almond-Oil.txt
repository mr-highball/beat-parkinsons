Title: Traditional Persian Medicine topical anointment practices using sweet almond oil (Prunus amygdalus)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12013975/
Source kind: Traditional-practice scholarly review
Publication date: 2025
Date basis: Peer-reviewed journal publication date (Avicenna J Phytomed 2025; PMID 40271504, PMC12013975); summarizes classical Persian Medicine texts.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Classical Persian texts widely recommend TOPICAL sweet almond oil anointment for body strengthening, skin hydration, and pediatric care, contrasting with violet oil for insomnia and olive/chamomile oils for pediatric constipation.
Authenticity: Verified via open-access PubMed Central full text (Mohammadian-Dameski et al., Avicenna J Phytomed 2025; PMC12013975).
Efficacy evidence: Customary traditional-practice documentation only; primarily documents external anointment rather than oral cold-pressed almond oil in Parkinson's disease.

Mohammadian-Dameski and colleagues (2025, PMC12013975) surveyed classical Traditional Persian Medicine (TPM) texts on medicinal-oil anointment in pediatric care alongside modern clinical reports. Sweet almond oil (Prunus amygdalus / P. dulcis) is among the most widely prescribed TPM topical oils for daily body massage/anointment to support skin barrier hydration and general robustness, whereas classical pediatric insomnia anointment specifically emphasized violet oil (frequently extracted in almond or sesame carrier oil) and functional constipation anointment emphasized olive and chamomile oils. This distinction highlights why many modern Iranian trials employ sweet almond oil externally as a massage carrier or control vehicle rather than an oral therapeutic agent.
