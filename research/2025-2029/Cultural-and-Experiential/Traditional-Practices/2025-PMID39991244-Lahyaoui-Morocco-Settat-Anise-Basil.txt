Title: Settat Province, Morocco ethnobotanical survey documenting traditional uses of anise seed (Habat hlawah) and sweet basil (Lhbaq)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11846941/
Source kind: Peer-reviewed ethnobotanical field survey
Publication date: 2025
Date basis: Peer-reviewed journal publication date (Heliyon 2025; PMID 39991244, PMC11846941).
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Informants report dried Pimpinella anisum seed infusions (FI=0.22) for digestive disorders/bloating and fresh or dried Ocimum basilicum leaf infusions/steam powder (FI=0.065) for headache, rheumatism, bronchitis, diarrhea, and nervous/digestive complaints.
Authenticity: Verified via open-access PubMed Central full text (Lahyaoui et al., Heliyon 2025; PMC11846941).
Efficacy evidence: Ethnobotanical field documentation only; does not establish controlled clinical efficacy in Parkinson's disease.

Lahyaoui and colleagues (2025, PMC11846941) conducted an ethnobotanical survey in Settat Province, Morocco. Pimpinella anisum L. (local name: Habat hlawah; dried seeds prepared as a post-meal infusion) tied with Verbena as the most familiar medicinal species (Familiarity Index = 0.218 / 0.22), cited primarily for digestive disorders and bloating/meteorism. Ocimum basilicum L. (local name: Lhbaq; fresh or dried leaves prepared as infusion or steam powder, one cup daily; Familiarity Index = 0.065) was cited for headache, rheumatism, bronchitis/lung infection, and diarrhea, with comparative Moroccan ethnobotanical references noting Fez-Meknes nervous-system and digestive uses (complementing Beniaich 2022, PMID 34031820). Neither species was linked to diagnosed Parkinson's disease.
