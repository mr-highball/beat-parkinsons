Park et al. (2026, PMC13503140) trace yogurt as an ancient fermented milk staple produced by lactic acid bacteria across pastoralist and agricultural foodways of Central Asia, the Mediterranean, the Balkans, and South Asia (including traditional dahi and strained/concentrated yogurts). The authors note that yogurt gained modern Western popularity in the early 1900s after Elie Metchnikoff popularized fermented milk from rural Bulgarian communities as a putative secret to longevity and gut health, before entering decades of debate in the 1950s over dairy saturated fat. Modern scientific interest has refocused on fermented-dairy matrices, live cultures, and gut-microbiota metabolites in aging populations, while emphasizing that mechanistic plausibility and historical reputation remain distinct from controlled clinical proof. This historical synthesis documents traditional digestive and healthy-aging expectations surrounding plain and strained yogurt without supplying controlled Parkinson symptom outcomes.
