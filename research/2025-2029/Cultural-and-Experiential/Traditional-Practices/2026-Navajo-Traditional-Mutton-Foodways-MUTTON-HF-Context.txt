Title: Navajo (Dine) traditional mutton and lamb foodways reclaimed in culturally tailored Indigenous meals
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13409115/
Source kind: Traditional-Practice
Publication date: 2026-09-01
Date basis: Publication date of the MUTTON-HF randomized trial in JAMA Internal Medicine (PMID:42507456, PMC13409115; companion feasibility report JAMA Network Open 2026, PMID:41649816).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Culturally central nourishing staple in Navajo (Dine) community foodways; in MUTTON-HF (n=206), low-sodium culturally tailored Indigenous meal delivery reduced heart-failure hospitalizations/ED visits (RR 0.72, p=0.02) and improved KCCQ QoL and resting BP, without isolating mutton/lamb or evaluating Parkinson disease symptoms.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed community-based participatory clinical research conducted at two Indian Health Service sites in rural Navajo Nation (2025-2026).
Efficacy evidence: Documents deep cultural significance and acceptability of traditional mutton stew and Indigenous dishes, while providing no isolated evidence of cooked lamb efficacy for Parkinson disease symptoms.

In Navajo (Dine) pastoral culture, sheep (dibe) and mutton/lamb stews hold enduring nutritional, social, and ceremonial importance across generations. In the 2025-2026 MUTTON-HF community-based participatory trials (Eberly et al., JAMA Intern Med 2026, PMC13409115; JAMA Netw Open 2026, PMC12881988), rural Navajo Nation clinicians and community partners reclaimed traditional Navajo recipes—including culturally tailored low-sodium mutton-based meals—into an 8-week medically tailored meal intervention for adults with heart failure (n=206). The bundled program reduced 90-day hospitalizations/ED visits and improved quality of life and blood pressure relative to usual advice. Because the intervention delivered a multi-component low-sodium DASH-aligned Indigenous meal package to heart-failure patients rather than isolated cooked lamb in Parkinson disease, it documents cultural heritage and meal acceptability rather than isolated lamb symptom efficacy.
