Ahmed et al. (2026, South African Journal of Botany, PMC12931810) review traditional cross-cultural uses of Solanum species for pain and inflammatory complaints, summarizing ethnobotanical records alongside phytochemical studies of melongenamides A-D from Solanum melongena roots, chlorogenic/caffeic acids, and steroidal glycoalkaloids in LPS-stimulated macrophage and rodent models. The authors explicitly caution that analgesic and anti-inflammatory evidence for these Solanum constituents remains restricted to in vitro and animal models, with no controlled human pain trials validating cooked S. melongena fruit.
