Title: Khadka et al. — reported cultural, food and medicinal uses of Prunus cerasoides in Nepal
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Ethnobotanical field survey of reported plant uses
DOI: 10.1016/j.tfp.2025.100919
Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666719325001451
Date basis: DOI registered in June 2025 and the journal issue is September 2025. An exact first public date has not been verified.
Publication date: 2025
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC
Location inspected: Original publisher abstract, checked against the abstract supplied by the research organization's public page and FAO AGRIS record. The detailed plant-part and preparation tables remain to be inspected.

Community/context: The authors surveyed 713 participants from eastern, central and western Nepal about Prunus cerasoides. Their sample included several ethnic and religious groups. The paper reports distinct use categories and differences in use patterns across the surveyed groups.
Exposure and reported symptom: The species was reported in cultural, food and medicinal settings. The abstract does not specify a dried-cherry preparation or identify Parkinson apathy as an indication.
Reported direction: The survey reports uses. No symptom improvement or worsening is measured.
Evidence and limits: The reported use fidelity levels were 79.38% for cultural use, 44.32% for food and 26.37% for medicine. These are use-report measures from one survey. They supply neither treatment response rates nor independently replicated accounts.
Authenticity: The same title, DOI and author abstract appear in the publisher and public institutional records.
Efficacy evidence: The available report does not establish a clinical symptom effect or equivalence between this species and the culinary cherry claim.
Related research: The 2026 Jeong extract and prunetin vascular dementia report and its 2025 proceedings precursor concern experimentally studied preparations. They do not measure the reported practices in this survey.
Open trail: Check the full use table for plant parts and preparations, and keep regional reporting and assigned laboratory exposures separate.
