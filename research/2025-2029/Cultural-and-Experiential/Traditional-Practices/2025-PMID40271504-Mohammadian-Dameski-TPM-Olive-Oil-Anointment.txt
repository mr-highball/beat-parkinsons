Reported direction: Traditional external oil anointment for comfort/musculoskeletal soothing in Traditional Persian Medicine (distinct from oral consumption) alongside Spanish pharmacy review noting folk beliefs around oral olive oil for constipation (PMID:42382140, PMC13310362).
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed ethnomedical and pharmacy reviews indexed in PubMed/PMC (PMID:40271504, PMID:42382140).
Efficacy evidence: Historical and folk practice documentation only; separates claimed historical antiquity from modern clinical proof and carries no causal efficacy weight for Parkinson's symptoms.

A 2025 comparative review in Avicenna Journal of Phytomedicine (PMID:40271504, PMC12013975) documents the historical role of medicinal plant-oil anointment—including olive oil—in Traditional Persian Medicine for health preservation and soothing physical discomfort, explicitly a topical practice distinct from oral intake. Separately, a 2026 Spanish community pharmacy review (Díaz López, PMID:42382140, PMC13310362) notes persistent folk home remedies around constipation management. Together with the earlier archived Cypriot iatrosophia record on olive oil and headache (2026-John-the-Physician-Cyprus-Olive-Oil-Headache.txt), these sources document centuries-old cultural practices without establishing clinical efficacy in Parkinson's disease.
