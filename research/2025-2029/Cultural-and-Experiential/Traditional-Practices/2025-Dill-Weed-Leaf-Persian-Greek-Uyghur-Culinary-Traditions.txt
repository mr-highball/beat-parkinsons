Title: Traditional culinary and digestive uses of fresh and dried dill weed foliage (Anethum graveolens L.) across Mediterranean, Persian, Uyghur, and Asian traditions

Feathery green leaves and young aerial stems of Anethum graveolens L. (dill weed; Persian shevid, Greek anithos) have a distinct culinary and ethnobotanical tradition separate from mature carvone-rich dill seeds/fruits. Across Greece and the Eastern Mediterranean, tall leafy dill landraces are cultivated specifically for fresh and dried foliage used generously in soups, rice dishes, salads, and sauces (PeerJ 2023, PMC10066692). In Persian culinary and medical heritage, fresh and dried dill weed (shevid) is cooked into rice dishes (baghali polo) and stews and valued for digestive ease, while in Uyghur traditional medicine the aerial herb of dill is recorded for hiccups, food stagnation, intestinal obstruction, and anorectal complaints (Qiao et al. 2025, PMID:39667686). In Thailand and Southeast Asia, dried and fresh dill leaves are consumed in soups and hot-water herbal teas (PeerJ 2025, PMC12164813). Traditional records associate dill foliage primarily with gastrointestinal comfort rather than Parkinson's motor or neuropsychiatric symptoms.
