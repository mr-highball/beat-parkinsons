Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Millennial East Asian culinary and dietary-therapy heritage prizes cooked/simmered shiitake fruiting bodies (Chinese xianggu; Japanese shiitake named after Castanopsis shii hardwood) for umami broths, stamina/Qi nourishment, and general wellness; classical texts do not document controlled Parkinson disease efficacy and apply to whole fruiting bodies rather than modern submerged-fermented mycelial supplements (AHCC/LEM).
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed open-access mycology and medicinal-mushroom reviews (Song et al. 2025, PMC11897120; Sadowska et al. 2026, PMC13209540; Berger et al. 2022, PMC9315710).
Efficacy evidence: Historical ethnomycology and culinary context only; traditional Qi/stamina claims do not establish clinical Parkinson efficacy and must be separated from injectable pharmaceutical lentinan and cultured mycelial AHCC.

Title and source: East Asian cultivation history and food-as-medicine (shiliao) ethnomycology of Lentinula edodes (Chinese xianggu, "fragrant mushroom"; Japanese shiitake, from shii Castanopsis hardwood + take mushroom), documented via Song et al., Appl Microbiol Biotechnol 2025 (PMID:40067479, PMC11897120), Sadowska et al., Molecules 2026 (PMC13209540), and Berger et al., Microorganisms 2022 (PMC9315710).
Community/context: Domestication of L. edodes began in China during the Song dynasty (traditionally linked in folklore to Wu San Kwung and formally recorded in Wang Zhen's 1313 AD Yuan-dynasty Nong Shu agricultural treatise using notched hardwood logs) before spreading to Japan, where dried shiitake became foundational to plant-based shojin ryori Buddhist cuisine and umami dashi broths. Classical Chinese dietary materia medica (including Ming-era editions of Wu Rui's Riyong Bencao) classified xianggu as a nourishing dietary tonic thought to support Qi, dispel cold, aid sluggish digestion, and counter weariness.
Matrix separation and limits: Traditional culinary preparations soak and thoroughly simmer, braise, or roast whole or sliced fruiting bodies—and often consume the soaking/simmering broth (retaining water-soluble compounds such as L-ergothioneine and guanylate umami nucleotides). Modern commercial extracts such as intravenous pharmaceutical lentinan or cultured mycelial AHCC/LEM are biochemically distinct industrial products (Berger et al. 2022) and do not represent traditional culinary shiitake. Historical tonic attributions supply no verified Parkinson disease symptom outcome.
