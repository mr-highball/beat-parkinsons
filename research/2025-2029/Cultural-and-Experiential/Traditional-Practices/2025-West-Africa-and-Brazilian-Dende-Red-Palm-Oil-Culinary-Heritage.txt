Date basis: Publication year of the 2025 Brazilian crude palm oil consumer perception study (PMID:41008214, PMC12469376), cross-referenced with 1997 Nigerian preschool dietary vitamin A survey (PMID:9403878) and 2017 meta-analysis (PMID:29186779).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Culinary staple and traditional vitamin A food source across West Africa and Northeastern Brazil (Bahia); consumer survey shows largely neutral/mixed modern perceptions with regional culinary familiarity in Northeast/Southeast Brazil and no traditional Parkinson efficacy claim.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed Brazilian consumer survey (Foods 2025; n=1,065; PMC12469376) cross-referenced with Nigerian dietary survey (Adelekan 1997, PMID:9403878).
Efficacy evidence: Cultural culinary and nutritional-history context only; establishes longstanding dietary use and carotenoid/tocotrienol richness without clinical evidence for Parkinson symptoms.

Title and source: West African and Afro-Brazilian culinary traditions of unrefined red palm oil (Elaeis guineensis; Portuguese azeite de dende), inspected via Alves et al. 2025 (Foods, PMC12469376, n=1,065 Brazilian consumers) and cross-referenced with Adelekan et al. 1997 (PMID:9403878, rural Osun State, Southwest Nigeria).
Community/context: Native to West African rainforests (Nigeria, Ghana, Sierra Leone, Benin) where minimally processed red palm oil anchors traditional stews, soups (e.g., egusi), beans, and rice dishes, and carried via the transatlantic diaspora to Northeastern Brazil (especially Bahia), where azeite de dende defines iconic dishes such as moqueca, acaraje, and vatapa. In rural Southwest Nigeria (Adelekan 1997), >=43% of preschool children consumed carotene-rich red palm oil six or more times weekly, maintaining low clinical vitamin A deficiency despite broader undernutrition.
Exposure and reported symptom: Unrefined culinary fruit-mesocarp red palm oil (distinct from bleached/deodorized industrial palm oil and distinct from pharmaceutical tocotrienol capsules). Ethnomedical literature notes topical emollient/wound uses and general food-based tonic roles against weakness or night blindness; NO traditional claim targets diagnosed Parkinson disease motor or non-motor symptoms.
Evidence and limits: Contemporary Brazilian survey (Alves 2025, n=1,065) found 61.1% could not accurately define hybrid crude palm oil and overall perception was neutral (more positive in Northeast/Southeast culinary heartlands), underscoring tension between ancestral culinary value and modern saturated-fat/sustainability concerns. Cross-linked with clinical vitamin A RCT meta-analysis (Dong 2017, PMID:29186779) and cardiometabolic RCT (Teng 2024, PMID:38372798).
