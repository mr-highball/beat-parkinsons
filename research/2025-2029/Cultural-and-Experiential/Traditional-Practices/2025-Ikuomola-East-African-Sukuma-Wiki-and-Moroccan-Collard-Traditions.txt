Title: East African sukuma wiki staple foodway and Ugandan/Moroccan ethnomedicinal uses of collard greens (Brassica oleracea var. viridis)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12519067/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2025
Date basis: Publication year of Ikuomola et al. 2025 (F1000Research, PMID:41098508, PMC12519067); also cites Amssayef & Eddouks 2021 (PMID:32990547).
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: Everyday East African culinary staple (sukuma wiki) plus local non-PD ethnomedicinal uses (metabolic/reproductive); no Parkinson-specific efficacy claim.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed publications documenting Brassica oleracea var. viridis traditional uses in Uganda (Ikuomola et al. 2025, PMID:41098508 / PMC12519067) and Morocco (Amssayef & Eddouks 2021, PMID:32990547).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional culinary and non-neurological ethnomedicinal context only; zero evidence for Parkinson's motor or non-motor symptoms.

Peer-reviewed documentation (Ikuomola et al. 2025, F1000Research, PMID:41098508 / PMC12519067; Amssayef & Eddouks 2021, PMID:32990547) records widespread dietary and ethnomedicinal use of collard greens (Brassica oleracea var. viridis) outside North America, including as "sukuma wiki" ("stretch the week")—a cornerstone sautéed/cooked daily leafy green across Kenya, Tanzania, and Uganda—alongside local Ugandan traditional use for male infertility and Moroccan folk use of aqueous leaf preparations for blood-glucose control.

Evidentiary context: Confirms global culinary ubiquity of cooked collard greens (complementing Mediterranean Brassica domestication history in Mabry et al. 2021 archived locally), while revealing no traditional Parkinson's symptom claims or controlled neurological trials.
