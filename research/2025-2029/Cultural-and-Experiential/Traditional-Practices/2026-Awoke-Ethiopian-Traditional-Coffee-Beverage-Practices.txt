Title: Ethiopian traditional Coffea arabica beverage practices and perceived restorative use (Awoke et al. 2026)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12977837/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2026-02-01
Date basis: Journal publication year/month (Journal of Ethnobiology and Ethnomedicine 2026; PMID:41656248, PMC12977837; fieldwork conducted August-October 2025).
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: Customary cultural hospitality and perceived warming/restorative household use of roasted/boiled Coffea arabica leaf beverage (chemo); explicitly distinct from brewed or decaffeinated coffee beans and non-clinical.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed open-access mixed-methods ethnobotanical fieldwork study (64 households, 16 key informants in Tepi Town, Southwestern Ethiopia; PMID:41656248, PMC12977837).
Efficacy evidence: Ethnographic cultural context only; uses spiced coffee leaves rather than roasted coffee beans and does not assess clinical Parkinson's disease symptoms.

Awoke, Gizaw, & Tilahun (2026, Journal of Ethnobiology and Ethnomedicine, PMID:41656248, PMC12977837) documented traditional preparation and cultural roles of chemo—an indigenous hot beverage made from roasted, ground, and boiled Coffea arabica leaves with local herbs, spices, butter, and salt—across 64 households and 16 key informants in Southwestern Ethiopia. Community members valued the beverage for hospitality, social cohesion, and perceived warming and restorative effects. As a cultural record, it illustrates the deep traditional roots of Coffea arabica preparations in Ethiopia while clearly distinguishing traditional spiced coffee-leaf infusions from modern brewed or decaffeinated coffee beans and offering no clinical evidence for Parkinson's disease symptom modification.
