Title: Cross-Cultural Ethnobotany (Amazon, Italy, Romania) and Traditional Persian Renal Caution for Chives (Allium schoenoprasum)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12383013/
Source kind: Traditional-practice and ethnobotanical record
Publication date: 2025-08-05
Date basis: Publication date of Amazonian medicinal-leaves profiling study (Mendez et al., 2025-08-05, PMID:40867861 / PMC12383013), integrating Bologna ethnobotany (Chiocchio et al., 2024, PMC10936038), Romanian folk anti-inflammatory context (Parvu et al., 2014, PMID:24781739), and unfavorable Traditional Persian Medicine renal-toxicity listing (Kolangi et al., 2018, PMID:29623832).
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: Mixed favorable culinary/folk use and unfavorable traditional caution: Amazonian and European ethnobotanical records describe Allium schoenoprasum leaves as phenolic-rich culinary/folk digestive and mild anti-inflammatory greens, whereas Traditional Persian Medicine texts flag chives among potentially nephrotoxic/renal-irritant herbs without modern clinical confirmation.
Authenticity: Traceable to peer-reviewed ethnobotanical and traditional-medicine publications (PMC12383013, HTTP 200; PMC10936038; PMID:24781739; PMID:29623832).
Efficacy evidence: Ethnobotanical and historical traditional-medicine context only; strictly distinguishes culinary Allium schoenoprasum from Chinese/garlic chives (Allium tuberosum), onion, and garlic without proving Parkinson's symptom effects.

Peer-reviewed ethnobotanical surveys document modest culinary and folk-medicinal roles for common chives (Allium schoenoprasum) across disparate regions: an August 2025 Amazonian medicinal-leaf study (Mendez et al., PMC12383013, PMID:40867861) noted traditional culinary/medicinal leaf use and measured high total phenolic content (11,351.6 mg/100 g DW) while cautioning that traditional medicinal leaves require safety evaluation; Italian Bologna ethnobotany (Chiocchio et al., 2024, PMC10936038) and Romanian folk traditions (Parvu et al., 2014, PMID:24781739) document mild culinary, appetite, and folk anti-inflammatory uses. Preserving unfavorable traditional perspectives, a Traditional Persian Medicine review (Kolangi et al., 2018, PMID:29623832) catalogs Allium schoenoprasum among classical herbs historically cautioned for potential renal irritation/nephrotoxicity (unconfirmed in modern clinical trials). None of these traditions document standalone Parkinson's disease efficacy.
