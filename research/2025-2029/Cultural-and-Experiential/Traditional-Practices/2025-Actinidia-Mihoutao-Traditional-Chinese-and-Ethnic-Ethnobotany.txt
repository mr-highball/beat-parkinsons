Origin and ethnobotanical context:
Actinidia species (Chinese: Mihoutao) are native to central and southern China, where wild and cultivated fruits have long served as seasonal edible fruits alongside distinct medicinal uses of Actinidia chinensis roots (Mihoutaogen) in Traditional Chinese Medicine.
A 2025 ethnobotanical field survey of Guangxi's Longsheng Terraces Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System (PMID:41287094, PMC12642339) documented traditional Zhuang, Yao, and Dong knowledge valuing native wild and terrace flora—including Actinidia taxa—across food, household medicine, and ecological management.
Distinction from modern raw kiwifruit claims: Historical TCM materia medica oncology/heat-clearing citations overwhelmingly target A. chinensis root decoctions rather than raw commercial A. deliciosa (Hayward) or A. chinensis (SunGold) fruit, and do not describe controlled outcomes for modern Parkinson's disease symptoms. Traditional food consumption therefore serves purely as botanical and cultural context.
