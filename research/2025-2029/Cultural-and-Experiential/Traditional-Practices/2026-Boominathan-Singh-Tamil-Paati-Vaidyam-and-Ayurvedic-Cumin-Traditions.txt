Title: Traditional South Asian Ayurvedic (Jiraka), Persian (Zira), and Tamil Paati Vaidyam uses of true Cuminum cyminum
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38030481/
Source kind: cultural
Publication date: 2026-01-01
Date basis: Peer-reviewed publication dates (Boominathan et al., J Voice, PMID:38030481, 2026; Singh et al., PMID:33893678, 2021); documented oral and textual traditions span centuries.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: favorable traditional digestive/vocal comfort uses, with no controlled clinical efficacy for Parkinson's symptoms
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed ethnomedicinal survey of school teachers and peer-reviewed pharmacognosy review.
Efficacy evidence: Customary dietary and vocal-care practices only; explicitly separates true culinary Cuminum cyminum from Nigella sativa ('black cumin').

In Ayurveda (Jiraka / Jeera), Unani/Persian medicine (Zira-e-Safaid), and South Indian Tamil household traditions ('Paati Vaidyam'), ground or dry-toasted seeds of true culinary cumin (Cuminum cyminum L.) are widely prepared as jeera water, tempered spice infusions, or warming decoctions for bloating, abdominal cramping, postpartum recovery, and throat/voice comfort. A 2026 cross-sectional survey of Tamil school teachers (Boominathan et al., J Voice, PMID:38030481) documented cumin seeds among recurring home remedies used for vocal strain alongside black pepper, ginger, and honey. Pharmacognosy reviews (Singh et al., 2021, PMID:33893678) emphasize that true Cuminum cyminum must not be conflated with Nigella sativa ('black cumin' / kalonji) or Bunium persicum ('black jeera'). Traditional household practices do not demonstrate clinical efficacy for Parkinson's hypophonia, dyskinesia, or motor symptoms.
