Title: Traditional Oral Use of Raw Arugula (Jarjeer / Eruca sativa) for Hypertension in Aseer Province, Saudi Arabia

Source kind: traditional_practice
Publication date: 2025-05-31
Date basis: Peer-reviewed ethnobotanical field survey publication date (Alshaqhaa et al., 2025, J Ethnobiol Ethnomed, PMID:40450274, PMC12125768) documenting contemporary regional folk knowledge
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: blood_pressure_lowering_traditional_context
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed ethnobotanical survey with quantitative Use Value (UV=0.0442) and verified botanical identification of Eruca sativa Mill.; publication date distinct from regional herbal heritage
Efficacy evidence: Customary traditional practice only; does not establish clinical efficacy or safety for Parkinson's neurogenic orthostatic lightheadedness

In an ethnobotanical field survey of medicinal plants used in Aseer Province, Southwestern Saudi Arabia (Alshaqhaa et al., 2025, J Ethnobiol Ethnomed, PMID:40450274, PMC12125768), Eruca sativa Mill. (local Arabic name: Jarjeer) is documented with a Use Value (UV) of 0.0442. Informants report consuming the fresh raw leaves orally as a traditional hypotensive remedy to lower high blood pressure. This regional practice parallels South Asian traditional antihypertensive use of E. sativa noted in Salma et al. (2018, PMID:29913298) and acute nitrate-mediated resting blood-pressure lowering in healthy young adults (Jonvik et al., 2016, PMID:27075914). However, customary blood-pressure-lowering use in the general community does not test Parkinson's disease or standing orthostatic lightheadedness and carries lower evidential weight than controlled clinical trials.
