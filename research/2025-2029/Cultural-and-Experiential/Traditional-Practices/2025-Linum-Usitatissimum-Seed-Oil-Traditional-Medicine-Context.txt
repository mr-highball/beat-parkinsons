Reported direction: Contextual traditional dietary and medicinal use for gastrointestinal, metabolic, cardiovascular, and skin complaints; no traditional Parkinson's neurological claim.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed summary inspected during Round 4 review (PMID:39803256, PMC11717047, 2025).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional dietary/herbal context only; does not establish clinical efficacy for Parkinson's symptoms and strictly distinguishes oil from mucilage-rich whole seed.

Traditional context:
Cold-pressed oil expressed from Linum usitatissimum L. seeds has a multi-century culinary and traditional medicinal record across Eurasia, Mediterranean, Persian, and South Asian foodways. As summarized in a 2025 Food Science & Nutrition review (PMID:39803256, PMC11717047), traditional medicine systems have employed flaxseed oil alongside olive oil for general gastrointestinal, cardiovascular, inflammatory, skin, and metabolic wellness.
Matrix and diagnostic boundaries: Traditional oil pressing yields alpha-linolenic acid (ALA) and minor lipophilic phenolics while leaving behind the seed coat mucilage fiber and most water-soluble lignans responsible for whole-seed bulk laxation. Historical sources do not isolate modern Parkinson's disease phenotypes or verify cold-pressed oxidation stability.
