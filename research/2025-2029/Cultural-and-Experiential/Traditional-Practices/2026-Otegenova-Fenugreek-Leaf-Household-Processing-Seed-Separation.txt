Title: Household drying, cooking, and ethnobotanical separation of fenugreek leaves (Barg-e-Hulba / Methi Patta) from fenugreek seeds
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13388466/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2026
Date basis: Peer-reviewed open-access review publication year (Otegenova et al., 2026, PMC13388466; Salam et al., 2023, PMC10148852; Khatoon et al., 2024, PMID:37143276).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Contextual traditional leafy-vegetable preparation and nutritional-retention record; no Parkinson's disease efficacy claim.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed open-access phytochemistry and Unani ethnobotanical reviews inspected during Round 4 review (PMC13388466; PMC10148852; PMID:37143276).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional household-processing and botanical-separation context only; confirms absence of leaf-specific human clinical trials.

In South Asian, Unani (Barg-e-Hulba), and Egyptian household food traditions, fresh fenugreek leaves are commonly blanched or sautéed in vegetable dishes, or shade-/air-dried to extend shelf life and concentrate culinary aroma (Otegenova et al., 2026, PMC13388466; Salam et al., 2023, PMC10148852). Published household-processing analyses demonstrate that sun- or oven-drying fenugreek leaves degrades ~84-85% of fresh ascorbic acid while air-dried leaves retain substantially higher total phenolics (15.27 vs 6.54 mg GAE/g dw) and flavonoids (7.71 vs 4.23 mg QE/g dw) than raw seeds, yet lack the ~45-50% galactomannan fiber matrix and seed-specific 4-hydroxyisoleucine profile tested in fenugreek seed clinical trials. Public Parkinson's UK forum searches also yielded zero threads discussing fenugreek leaves. Consequently, seed-extract trial findings cannot be transferred to cooked or dried fenugreek leaves.
