Title: Tarragon pain-related practices documented in the Cayoose Creek Band book
Source: https://indigenousmedicinescayoosecreek.pressbooks.tru.ca/chapter/tarragon/
Publication date: 2025
Date basis: 2025 copyright of the published open book. The individual chapter date and original oral dates are unavailable.
Reviewed: 2026-11-27
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Traditional practice attributed to community elders and members
Community/context: Public chapter 66 in Indigenous Medicinal and Food Plants of the Cayoose Creek Band of Sekw’el’was, hosted by Thompson Rivers University.
Exposure and reported symptom: The chapter identifies Artemisia dracunculus and attributes softened leaf contact or oil infusion for toothache to community knowledge. The authors also recount tea for menstrual discomfort in the same attributed section.
Reported direction: Intended relief for particular pain contexts, without measured outcomes.
Authenticity: The inspected chapter gives a collective community source in reference 3 and does not date the original oral accounts or provide independent testimonial details.
Efficacy evidence: The chapter describes customary practice. It does not report a comparison of oral fresh leaf with a placebo, Parkinson pain or a reproducible clinical effect.
Access and follow-up: Use the section headed Traditional Indigenous Uses. Original interview or oral-history dates and individual preparation details were unavailable.
