Title: Energy, fatigue and symptom variability reported in a Parkinson diet crossover
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://doi.org/10.4102/jmh.v9i1.154
DOI: 10.4102/jmh.v9i1.154
Source kind: Firsthand accounts gathered in post-study interviews
Publication date: 2026-11-13
Date basis: Electronic publication of the original article
Source locator: Qualitative results, including symptom variability and cognitive clarity and energy
Review depth: full_text
Reviewed: 2026-11-24
Reported direction: Mixed. Some volunteers reported less fatigue or more stamina, while others described variability or early worsening of certain symptoms.
Authenticity: The original study's qualitative interview results were inspected. The interviewees came from the same clinical crossover cohort.
Efficacy evidence: Unblinded, voluntary post-study reports about intervention phases. The interviews do not establish named culinary oil effects.

Participants: Eight of the sixteen completers gave voluntary interviews about a sequence of usual-diet, MCT-supplement and combined ketogenic/MCT phases.
Reported outcomes: Some participants described better stamina, less fatigue or improved mental clarity, particularly in later phases. Others noted symptom variability, including early worsening of certain symptoms, and difficulty following the diet. The interviews give no separate comparative estimate for named oils or Epworth result.
Efficacy context: These firsthand but unblinded reports come from the cohort that supplied the measured fatigue, sleep and cognition scores. Preserve one study lineage. There was no assigned virgin or refined coconut oil.
