Title: Sauzet et al. — Qualitative focus groups on functional food literacy, cooking fatigue and caregiver support in Parkinson disease and multiple sclerosis
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Qualitative focus-group study
DOI: 10.1080/09638288.2026.2661486
PMID: 42047406
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42047406/
Publication date: 2026
Date basis: Disability and Rehabilitation publication in 2026 reporting semi-structured focus groups.
Reviewed: 2026-11-21 UTC
Location inspected: PubMed bibliographic record and full structured abstract (PMID 42047406).

Community/context: Four semi-structured qualitative focus groups with 10 total participants comprising individuals living with Parkinson disease or multiple sclerosis and their caregivers, analyzed using the International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF) and a functional food literacy framework.
Exposure and reported symptom: Explores practical meal planning, grocery shopping, kitchen-tool handling, recipe simplification, and food preparation rather than a specific nutrient; participants highlighted cognitive and physical fatigue, reduced strength, and mobility limits as major barriers to cooking and dietary autonomy, relying heavily on caregiver support and compensatory sequencing strategies.
Reported direction: Unspecified for any individual food; documents lived bidirectional impact of fatigue and motor impairment on food preparation and dietary adherence.
Evidence and limits: Small qualitative sample (n = 10 across both Parkinson disease and multiple sclerosis) without subgroup breakdown by diagnosis in the abstract and without testing a specific food's symptom effect.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed Disability and Rehabilitation study (DOI 10.1080/09638288.2026.2661486).
Efficacy evidence: Qualitative experiential record informing real-world dietary feasibility and preparation burden; supplies no efficacy estimate for individual ingredients.
Related research: Contextualizes food-preparation feasibility across culinary claims in Round 1 (particularly fatigue severity and hand dexterity impairment).
Access and follow-up: Full publisher text paywalled; structured abstract reviewed. Separate Parkinson-only participant themes and the eight intervention recommendations remain behind publisher paywall.
