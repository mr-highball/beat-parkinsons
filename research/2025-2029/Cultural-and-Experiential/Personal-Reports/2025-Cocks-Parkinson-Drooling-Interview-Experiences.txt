Title: Food preparation and oral cueing in participant interviews about Parkinson drooling
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17549507.2025.2544784
DOI: 10.1080/17549507.2025.2544784
Source kind: Participant experiences gathered through a qualitative clinical study
Publication date: 2025-08-25
Date basis: Publisher first online date. The journal issue appeared in October 2026.
Source locator: Cocks and colleagues, The impact of drooling on the lives of people with Parkinson's disease, Factors that make drooling worse and Management of drooling.
Community/context: Eleven adults recruited through a Parkinson research group and community support settings in Australia.
Reported direction: Several participants recalled more drooling around food preparation. Some participants described chewing gum as helpful, even when they disliked using it.
Authenticity: Traceable original publication and interviewer-reported themes. No individual identifying details are reproduced.
Efficacy evidence: Self-reported perceived changes, without assigned food, standardized gum, a control period or independent measurements of gum effects.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20, original paper and public author abstract.

People also described concentration, fatigue, social discomfort and medication off periods as contexts. Experiences and perceived burden varied. The food context includes preparation and being around food, with no raw fig ingestion substantiated. The reported gum experience and the 2010 South swallowing-frequency study address different observations. No frozen food effect is assigned from these interviews.
