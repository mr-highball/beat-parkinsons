The survey describes self-administered approaches to mental health among students in Wrocław. In the herb-use section 260 of 491 respondents (53.0%) reported using lemon balm. The figure counts herb use. The separate satisfaction ratings refer to complementary and alternative medicine overall. They do not provide an ingredient-specific improvement rate.

Reported direction: Unspecified for lemon balm. The study reports use and treatment-seeking context, with no assigned preparation, dose or measured change attributable to lemon balm.
Authenticity: Original publisher Methods, Results section 3.3 and tables were inspected.
Efficacy evidence: Self-selected cross-sectional reports outside Parkinson disease. Different response denominators apply to the herb-use and overall satisfaction questions. The data do not establish a Parkinson diagnosis, symptom effect or a causal direction.
Related research: Earlier controlled extract studies and brewed-tea comparisons are indexed separately in the food experiment.
Open trail: The report does not establish how often the herb was brewed or what happened to depression symptoms after its use. The published results preserve local use as a lead alongside the distinct intervention studies.
