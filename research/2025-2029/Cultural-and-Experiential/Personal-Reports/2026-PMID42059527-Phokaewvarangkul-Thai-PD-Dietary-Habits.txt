Reviewed: 2026-11-21 UTC
Location inspected: PubMed bibliographic record and full structured abstract (PMID 42059527 / PMC13435194).

Community/context: Seventy patients with Parkinson disease attending a tertiary movement disorders center in Thailand evaluated with a culturally adapted Thai Parkinson dietary questionnaire alongside dietitian interviews and UPDRS Parts III–IV ratings.
Exposure and reported symptom: Examined real-world Thai dietary textures (soft or liquid diets), dietary variety, and lower meat and vegetable intake in relation to underweight status (BMI < 18.5), Mini Nutritional Assessment-Short Form (MNA-SF), dysphagia (swallowing difficulty), and dyskinesia / UPDRS Part IV motor complications.
Reported direction: Observational association rather than single-food treatment effect: underweight Parkinson patients more often shifted to soft or liquid diets and reported narrower dietary variety with lower meat and vegetable intake despite similar total calories/fluids; dysphagia (OR 5.97), dyskinesia (OR 2.03), and female sex (OR 18.51) were independently associated with underweight status, with higher UPDRS Part IV scores (p = 0.015).
Evidence and limits: Cross-sectional observational design (n = 70) cannot determine whether dietary adaptations precede or follow worsening dysphagia and dyskinesia, and does not isolate individual culinary ingredients.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed Journal of Parkinson's Disease study (DOI 10.1177/1877718X261445952) with standardized clinical movement-disorder ratings and dietitian evaluation.
Efficacy evidence: Provides culturally specific experiential evidence on how swallowing difficulty and dyskinesia reshape food choices and nutrition in Thai Parkinson patients; does not test a dietary intervention for symptom modification.
