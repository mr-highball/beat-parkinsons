Title: Pacific narratives of care and coping with PINK1-associated Parkinson disease
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Qualitative Talanoa interviews and group discussion
Source: Fa’alau et al., Parkinson's Disease 2026:4024943. PMID: 42787988. PMCID: PMC13602813. DOI: 10.1155/padi/4024943. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13602813/
Publication date: 2026-09-24
Date basis: Electronic publication date in the official archive
Reviewed: 2026-11-17

Context: The New Zealand study included 11 people with PINK1-associated Parkinson disease and five caregivers. It used Talanoa, a Pacific method of open, participant-led conversation. The study gives an account of this small group.

Exposure: Narratives describe faith, family, spiritual explanations, traditional healers and massage. Separate accounts concern an apology ritual and shaking when medication was unavailable.
Reported direction: Faith and family were described as helpful for coping. A caregiver questioned perceived improvement with massage and described stopping it. The separate apology account describes no perceived change, and the report does not establish the person's Parkinson diagnosis.
Authenticity: The official CC BY PMC archive text was inspected, including Methods 2.1–2.3 and the cultural struggles and adaptation themes. Primary archive text: https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC13602813.1/PMC13602813.1.txt
Efficacy evidence: These accounts describe coping and perceived experiences. No controlled comparison, botanical exposure or separate rest-tremor response is reported. Participant identifiers are omitted.
Open trail: The report leaves the longitudinal course of the practices, exposure detail and a measurable symptom comparison open.
