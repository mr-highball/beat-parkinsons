Title: Ethnomedicinal use of raw/powdered banana fruit for constipation and black pepper seed for stomach pain in Sahiwal District, Pakistan
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12370920/
Source kind: Peer-reviewed ethnomedicinal field survey
Publication date: 2025
Date basis: Peer-reviewed journal publication date (Scientific Reports 2025; PMID 40841421, PMCID PMC12370920, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-15632-2); interviews conducted Aug 2021-Jan 2022.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Local informants report raw or powdered banana fruit (Musa x paradisiaca, Keela) for relieving constipation (highest survey Use Value, 2.25) and powdered or raw black pepper seed (Piper nigrum, Kaali-mirch) for stomach pain (UV 1.33).
Authenticity: Verified directly from open-access full text and Table 2 of Harun et al., Scientific Reports (2025), PMC12370920, surveying 125 local informants.
Efficacy evidence: Traditional local-knowledge ethnomedicinal documentation only; informant consensus does not establish clinical efficacy in Parkinson's disease.

Harun and colleagues (2025, Scientific Reports, PMID 40841421, PMC12370920) interviewed 125 local respondents in Sahiwal District, Punjab, Pakistan (August 2021 to January 2022) to document ethnomedicinal plants used for gastrointestinal disorders (74 species across 35 families; constipation ICF 0.64). In Table 2, Musa x paradisiaca L. (local name Banana/Keela, voucher UOBH00485) fruit prepared as powder or eaten raw was cited specifically for constipation (4 informant reports, Use Value 2.25 — tied with Cymbopogon citratus for the highest Use Value in the entire survey). In the same survey, Piper nigrum L. (local name Kaali-mirch, voucher UOBH00491) seed prepared as powder or raw was cited for stomach pain (6 reports, Use Value 1.33). These records reflect community ethnomedicinal traditions in a general population rather than controlled Parkinson's clinical data.
