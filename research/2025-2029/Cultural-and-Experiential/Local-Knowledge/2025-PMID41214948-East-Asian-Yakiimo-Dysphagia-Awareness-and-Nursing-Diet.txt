Title: East Asian practical culinary contrast: Japanese yaki-imo roasted sweet potato swallowing awareness vs Chinese moist boiled/steamed inpatient constipation diets
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41214948/
Source kind: Regional culinary-practice and clinical-geriatric foodways record
Publication date: 2025
Date basis: Publication year of Chang et al. 2025 (Food Res Int, PMID:41214948), integrating Japanese yaki-imo older-adult swallowing survey (Ogoshi 2000/2007; FSCJ 2010, archived locally) and Chinese inpatient constipation nursing diets (Ren 2012; Zou 2016).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Mixed preparation-dependent observations: dry roasted/baked sweet potato (Japanese yaki-imo) is widely recognized by Japanese older adults as a dry, moisture-absorbing bolus difficult to swallow (#1/#2 ranked among 601 adults in Ogoshi 2007/FSCJ 2010), prompting IDDSI Level 3 texture-modified purple sweet potato gels (Chang 2025), whereas moist steamed/boiled whole sweet potato (200 g/day) is traditionally and clinically used in Chinese hospital nursing care to promote bowel regularity (Zou 2016, Ren 2012).
Authenticity: Grounded in peer-reviewed East Asian food-science and nursing publications (Chang et al. 2025 PMID:41214948; Ogoshi 2007 & FSCJ 2010 verified in local archive; Zou 2016 PMID:25881813; Ren 2012 PMID:22847287).
Efficacy evidence: Preparation-texture and regional food-practice context highlighting how cooking method (dry oven-roasting vs moist boiling/steaming/pureeing) radically changes bolus safety and gastrointestinal utility; does not by itself establish Parkinson clinical dysphagia or constipation efficacy.

Title and source: Regional East Asian culinary contrast between dry roasted sweet potato swallowing awareness and moist boiled/steamed bowel-care practice, traced through Ogoshi 2007 / Food Safety Commission of Japan 2010 (local archive), Chang et al., Food Res Int 2025 (PMID:41214948), Zou et al., Cancer Nurs 2016 (PMID:25881813), and Ren et al., Gastroenterol Nurs 2012 (PMID:22847287).
Community/context: In Japan, stone-roasted sweet potato (yaki-imo) is a beloved autumn/winter street food, yet geriatric food-safety surveys of 601 Japanese adults ranked yaki-imo #1 among older adults living alone and middle-aged adults and #2 among institutional residents as a food perceived difficult to swallow due to its dry, powdery, saliva-absorbing flesh—prompting contemporary Taiwanese and Japanese food scientists (Chang et al. 2025, n=67 older sensory panelists) to develop moist, xanthan-stabilized IDDSI Level 3 purple sweet potato gels that retain cultural palatability while reducing choking risk. By contrast, in Chinese household foodways and hospital nursing care, moist boiled or steamed sweet potato (200 g/day) is widely used as an inexpensive, palatable dietary bowel aid for hospitalized patients.
Limits: Illustrates why frozen preparation wording ("roasted" vs "boiled" vs "baked" vs pureed/IDDSI-modified) matters clinically for people with Parkinson's who often experience coexisting constipation and xerostomia/oropharyngeal dysphagia, while remaining contextual rather than a Parkinson clinical trial.
