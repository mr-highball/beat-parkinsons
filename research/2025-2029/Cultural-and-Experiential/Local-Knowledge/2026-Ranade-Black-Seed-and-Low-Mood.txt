Title: The cultural and medicinal importance of tulsi, rosemary, and black seed
Source: https://doi.org/10.1177/1934578X261471121
Original article: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1934578X261471121
DOI: 10.1177/1934578X261471121
Publication date: 2026-07-27
Date basis: First online publication. Interviews were conducted between June 2023 and March 2024.
Source type: Qualitative local knowledge
Exposure: Black seed (Nigella sativa)
Symptom topic: Depressive symptom severity
Reviewed: 2026-11-27

The study used structured interviews from two UK convenience samples, 20 members of the public and 10 herbal practitioners. Public accounts describe black seed in cooking and cultural practice. The practitioners' thematic table lists low mood and depression among professional applications for black seed. The table does not give the frequency of that theme, an individual preparation, a Parkinson diagnosis or a measured mood change. The separate culinary accounts do not establish that the professional mood use involved toasted seeds.

This is documentation of reported professional use. It supplies cultural context for Round 1 claim 0019 and leaves the original preparation and outcome attribution uncertain.
