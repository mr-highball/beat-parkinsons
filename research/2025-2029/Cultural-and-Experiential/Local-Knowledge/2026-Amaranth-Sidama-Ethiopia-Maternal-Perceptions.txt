Title: Qualitative Community Perceptions of Amaranth-Chickpea Flatbread Among Pregnant Women in Sidama, Ethiopia
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13173720/
Source kind: local_knowledge
Publication date: 2026
Date basis: Peer-reviewed mixed-methods cluster RCT publication date (Mussie et al. 2026, BMC Nutrition, PMC13173720)
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: favorable_qualitative_bundled_food
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed qualitative focus-group data collected within a registered cluster RCT (NCT06536153) with anonymized participants
Efficacy evidence: Qualitative experiential perceptions of a bundled 70% amaranth / 30% chickpea flatbread in maternal anemia; does not establish isolated amaranth efficacy for Parkinson's fatigue or GI symptoms

In the qualitative acceptability component of Mussie et al. (2026, BMC Nutrition, PMC13173720, NCT06536153), pregnant women in rural Sidama, Ethiopia participating in focus-group discussions reported that daily 70% amaranth grain / 30% chickpea flatbread fit naturally alongside familiar fermented injera diets and described feeling stronger and less tired while consuming the iron-rich flatbread during pregnancy. Minor transient gastrointestinal complaints were rare and comparable to maize controls (7.8% vs 5.9%). Because amaranth was co-formulated with 30% chickpea, fatigue was not scored on a standardized instrument, and participants were pregnant women with iron deficiency anemia rather than adults with Parkinson's disease, this local-knowledge record is non-directional context for frozen Parkinson's claims.
