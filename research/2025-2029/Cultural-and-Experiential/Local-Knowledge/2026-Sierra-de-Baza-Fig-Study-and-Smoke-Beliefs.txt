The common fig profile describes a reputed former practice among students of consuming dried fig fruit prepared with milk before studying. Its stated purpose is concentration or memory. The account gives no frequency of reported benefit. The purported older provenance is unverified.

The same page separately records a folk belief about inhaling smoke from burning figwood. It reports unusual sensations in broad language and speculates about their cause. This is an inhalation narrative about wood, with no characterized visual symptom or verified event. It has no measured relationship to consumption of fig fruit.

Open trail: Find an earlier dated edition or independent source for the study custom and the smoke belief. The organization's 1998 guide dates a cheesemaking example, rather than these two accounts. Personal details from unrelated anecdotes are omitted.
