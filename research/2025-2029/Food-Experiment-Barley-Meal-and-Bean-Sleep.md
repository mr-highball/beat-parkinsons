# Barley in a post-meal pressure trial and bean intake and sleep

## Pearled barley in a low glycemic home dinner

Farrow and colleagues, Topics in Spinal Cord Injury Rehabilitation, February 14, 2025. DOI 10.46292/sci24-00044. Eleven adults with chronic spinal cord injury were randomized in a crossover comparison. Table 3 lists pearled barley as a component of the low-glycemic home dinner alongside black and pinto beans and other ingredients, without its preparation method. In the home comparison, maximal post-meal systolic decline showed no clear diet effect (p = .994), with nine participants and incomplete measurements across meals. The laboratory breakfast contained no barley and showed a separate 14 ± 12 versus 24 ± 25 mm Hg maximum decline (p = .056). This does not estimate a barley effect or test orthostatic symptoms in Parkinson disease. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11848139/)

## Pooled bean intake and self reported sleep

Lin and colleagues, 2026, DOI 10.1002/fsn3.71475. The 6,885-person Chinese cohort analysis assessed self-reported sleep quality and duration, with bean consumption recorded as an ordinal food frequency. The original results report favorable pooled bean associations with the two sleep measures. Other analyses in the paper address insomnia under food preferences and genetic proxies. This does not establish an insomnia-specific estimate for regularly eating canned black beans. The study's licensed full text was already cataloged for the separate potato analysis. [Original source](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/fsn3.71475)
