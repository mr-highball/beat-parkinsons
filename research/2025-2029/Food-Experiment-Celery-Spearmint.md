# Food experiment evidence for celery leaf and spearmint

## 2025 Inconsistent mint identity in a tuberculosis trial

Latif and colleagues report the study registered as NCT06157034. The paper labels the treatment peppermint in the title and abstract, calls it *Mentha spicata* in the study design and clinical intervention sections, and calls it *Mentha piperita* in Table 1 and its analytical discussion. The source was a local market and the paper supplies no voucher number or reported species-resolving authentication. The study used a mint oil capsule, with 46 analyzed in the mint arm and 48 in placebo. In Figure 4 and Supplementary Table S1, the mint group reported no epigastric pain during washout. The narrative does not give a controlled fresh-spearmint pain effect for 0728. Parkinson’s was outside the study population. [Primary report, Sections 2.1, 2.4.3 and 3.5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12104075/) · [Existing local full text](Source-Text/PMC12104075.txt) · [Earlier registration](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Celery-Spearmint.md#2023-registered-mentha-spicata-oil-in-a-tuberculosis-trial)

The registry reports no masking. The article reports masking participants, caregivers and outcome assessors. The registry lists 1.5 mL twice daily with a 2% concentration field and elsewhere states 30 mL per day. The article gives 1.5 mL twice daily and 3 mL per day without resolving the concentration. The exclusion ages overlap the stated inclusion ages in both sources. The registry and article also give different placebo amounts. Protocol execution and the actual amount of essential oil remain unresolved. [Original registry JSON](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT06157034) · [Original report, section 2.3.2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12104075/)

## 2025 and 2026 Mint-flavored gum and Parkinson gait and balance

Durr’s dissertation and Durr, Kushnick and Cottone’s conference report describe 27 participants with Parkinson’s and 27 matched controls. A single session compared mint-flavored sugar-free gum chewing with no gum. The Parkinson group improved on the Functional Gait Assessment (22.96 to 25.56) and Mini-BESTest (21.23 to 23.22), both p < .001. The 10-meter walk, Timed Up and Go and forward reach did not change significantly. The conference report does not identify the botanical flavor. No prospective falls or stride-length outcome was reported. This is a Parkinson motor comparison for 0040, 0494 and 0525, with an unidentified flavor and chewing exposure. The dissertation carries a 2025 publication date, the conference occurred in October 2025 and the proceedings article appeared in May 2026. ## 2026 Aqueous spearmint leaf preparation and rat urine output

A University of Makati project in the Philippine health research registry tested aqueous *Mentha spicata* leaf preparations in 15 rats. The summary reports higher short-term urine output for the medium preparation than the other extract concentrations, with furosemide described as the strongest comparator. The summary and results sections use different numerical descriptions of the medium-group volume (6.06 and 2.53). The study measured urine output and gives no count of urgency episodes. This is an autonomic-adjacent study for 0135. The registry identifier is PHRR260302-009740 and the project completion field gives May 2, 2025. [Official research record](https://www.herdin.ph/index.php/registry?cid=9740&layout=details&view=research)

## Topical menthol registration for Parkinson motor and sleep outcomes

NCT07294469 was first posted December 19, 2025. The registry specifies menthol-containing gloves and socks versus plain-lotion gloves and socks for four weeks in a planned 80-person Parkinson cohort. Primary measures include UPDRS I–III and PSQI, with PDSS-2 and PDQ-39 among secondary measures. No daily OFF diary is specified. The menthol is an isolated topical exposure. The registry gives neither fresh spearmint nor any posted result. [Official trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07294469) · [Local registry fields](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07294469.txt)

## 2025 Celery leaf decoction and pain in a gout case report

Berwulo and colleagues published December 5, 2025, an uncontrolled celery decoction report. Its abstract says two older patients and pain score 5 to 3; Methods, Results and Table 1 instead describe one 65-year-old woman with pain score 5 to 2 over seven days, alongside low-purine and hydration advice. Fresh leaves were boiled and the liquid consumed. Parkinson disease was not reported. These internal contradictions prevent a reliable estimate of treatment effect. [Publisher record](https://journal.ipm2kpe.or.id/index.php/JOTING/article/view/17638) · [Primary PDF, Methods and Table 1](https://journal.ipm2kpe.or.id/index.php/JOTING/article/download/17638/11404/152441) · [Factual extract](Source-Text/2025-Berwulo-Celery-Decoction-Pain.txt)

A 2023 report names different authors, study dates, and a distant site, but uses a closely similar preparation and overlapping pain and urate values. The available texts do not establish whether participants or data overlap. Keep the two reports distinct while treating their independence as unresolved; neither supplies a controlled fresh-leaf Parkinson comparison.
