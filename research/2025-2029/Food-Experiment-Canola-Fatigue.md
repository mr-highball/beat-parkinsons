# Canola-containing comparators and fatigue

## Canola-containing comparator and physical fatigability in older adults

First published November 27, 2025. The pilot randomized 30 older adults to ketone ester or a comparison beverage containing canola oil. Twenty-three completed. Reported final physical Pittsburgh Fatigability Scale values were 8.45 for ketone ester and 11.0 for the comparison, with p = .352 for the reported contrast. Leg-press repetitions to fatigue were 26.2 and 40.4, respectively, with reported p = .759. Both comparisons were inconclusive. The oil's grade was unverified and the design did not isolate an oil effect. This is the pilot discussed in two 2024 conference presentations and is distinct from the later frailty trial NCT06645847. ## Canola comparator sleep outcomes and report version

The same 23 completers contributed to the 2024 exploratory preprint and 2025 article. The preprint assessed a broad daytime dysfunction component and reported changes +0.09 with the canola comparator versus +0.08 on ketone ester. Its global PSQI comparison was p = .047. The final peer-reviewed report found no clear longitudinal PSQI differences across global or component measures. The estimates come from one healthy-older-adult cohort. The comparison does not isolate the effect of canola, establish the oil grade or measure Parkinson daytime sleepiness. ## Walking distance in the canola comparator pilot

The published exploratory comparison reports six-minute walking distance at follow-up of 487.8 m for ketone ester and 470.5 m for the canola-containing comparison (p = .461). Mean Short Physical Performance Battery changes were 0.00 and +0.36 points, respectively (p = .099 for the reported contrast). Four-meter gait speed was a component of the battery. No standalone result for it or freezing-event frequency was reported. The pilot recruited older adults without active neurologic disorders. Canola oil was in the comparison drink, and the design provides no isolated oil effect.
