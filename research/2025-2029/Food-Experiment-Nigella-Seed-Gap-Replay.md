# Nigella seed and oil in human oral and facial studies

## Chewed nigella seeds and salivary acidity in healthy women

Sennan and colleagues randomized 60 healthy women among *Nigella sativa* seed, carom, fennel and paraffin. Participants assigned Nigella chewed and swallowed the seeds. Toasting and grinding were unreported. Table 1 gives mean salivary pH of 6.53 at baseline, 8.80 immediately and 7.07 after 30 minutes for the Nigella group. The corresponding paraffin values were 6.53, 6.67 and 6.27. The study collected a small saliva sample for pH strips and did not measure secretion rate or clinical drooling. The methods identify the comparator seed as fennel even though the introduction also names anise. ## Cold pressed nigella oil rinse and salivary volume

In Mustapha and colleagues' repeated-measures pilot, ten healthy adults completed saline first and then a cold-pressed *Nigella sativa* oil rinse. Participants spat both the 5 mL rinse and saliva into a pre-weighed tube. The report labels the resulting outcome salivary volume and gives no procedure for accounting for retained rinse fluid. At one minute, mean volume was 4.89 mL with oil versus 1.43 mL with saline (adjusted p < .0001). Comparisons at five and ten minutes did not differ clearly. The fixed order, small sample and absence of a different oil control limit attribution. The procedure involved rinsing and spitting out the oil. The study had no patients with Parkinson disease and no repeated drooling outcome. ## Facial palsy and prior use of nigella and rosemary

A 2026 case report describes a woman with progressive unilateral facial weakness ultimately attributed to congenital petrous cholesteatoma. The timeline records prior use of *Nigella sativa* and rosemary without symptom improvement. It gives neither the preparation nor a pretreatment grade for the herbs. At tertiary assessment facial weakness was House–Brackmann grade V. It improved to grade II three days after surgery. The report records concurrent herbs and peripheral weakness in a different condition. ## Isolated thymoquinone and movement outcomes in a mouse study

Kusumawati and colleagues compared several preparations in groups of three rotenone-exposed mice. The single-agent oral thymoquinone arm at the highest of the tested single-agent doses improved open-field distance and mean velocity against rotenone control (p < .05 for each). The corresponding beam and hanging-wire comparisons were inconclusive. Active constituent, small groups and exploratory movement measures limit transfer to ground seed in Parkinson bradykinesia. ## Nigella seed extract alone and an inconclusive catalepsy test

Rathod and Aswar first published December 2, 2025. Their original Results and Figure 1 identify the Nigella sativa hydroalcoholic seed extract single arm as T6. Neither tested dose showed a significant catalepsy comparison in six mice per group. The broader discussion groups several chosen extracts together. The final combined preparation was evaluated for in vitro outcomes and toxicity without a reported separate motor comparison. This source adds a distinct, inconclusive seed-extract proxy alongside favorable isolated-compound experiments.
