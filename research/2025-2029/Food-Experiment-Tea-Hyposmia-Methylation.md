# Tea and hyposmia in a Parkinson methylation study

## Tea and hyposmia among cfDNA phenotypes

Zhu and colleagues' study was first published online on 10 June 2025. The author abstract describes 20 people with Parkinson's and nine healthy participants and cell-free DNA methylation analyses in cerebrospinal fluid and/or blood. Tea consumption and hyposmia were among separate phenotypes associated with methylation signatures and listed among candidate blood diagnostic phenotypes. The abstract reports no direct tea–hyposmia estimate and does not specify tea type, preparation or smell-assessment method. This was not a tea intervention. Full methods and tables have not been verified.

This study differs in stated setting and design from the [GBA1 family survey](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-GBA1-Tea-Hyposmia.md#tea-exposure-and-clinical-symptoms-in-a-gba1-family-survey). Their accessible abstracts do not establish whether any participants overlapped.
