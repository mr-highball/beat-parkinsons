## Turmeric and Cochrane study membership

The Cochrane review team's public supplement places Thanawala 2025 in an 11-study publication-bias dataset and the corresponding pain risk-of-bias workbook. Its arm counts match the clinical paper. The published abstract reports a four-study meta-analysis in 349 people, with an average 0.72-point reduction on a 0–10 knee-pain scale. The supplement supplies no verified final membership for those four studies. The report's PMC embargo date is November 19, 2027. [Supplement and study identity](Source-Text/2026-CD014683-Curcuma-Pain-Supplement-Membership.txt) · [Published review](Source-Text/2026-PMID43051256-Cochrane-Turmeric-Knee-Pain.txt) · [Thanawala primary trial](Source-Text/2025-PMID39882178-Thanawala-Turmeric-Extract-Knee-Pain.txt)

Two older reviews point to the same diabetic mouse experiment involving isolated curcumin with insulin. The primary abstract supplies a pain-related direction for the bundled treatment but lacks a numerical incremental curcumin comparison. A separate historical review reports Ayurvedic turmeric use for skin and muscle pain. It does not give a controlled comparison or confirmed preparation. The ground-turmeric scorecard retains its minimal easing tier pending direct evidence in Parkinson pain. ## Black pepper and preparation fit

The 1998 randomized crossover reported an approximately 20-fold difference in serum curcumin exposure when isolated piperine was added. The reported human methods and abstract agree on total dose, while the table header applies inconsistent weight-based units. Eight participants were analyzed. This supplies constituent context and no measured direction for culinary black pepper, pain or Parkinson disease.
