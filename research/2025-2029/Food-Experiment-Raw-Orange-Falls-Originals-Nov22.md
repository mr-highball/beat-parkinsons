# Raw orange and Parkinson fall frequency evidence gaps, November 22, 2026

Claim 0259 asks whether raw orange worsens the frequency of falls in diagnosed Parkinson disease. The v1 review remains Unknown at the open anchor of 50. Five underlying source families document related findings and planned measurements. The reviewed evidence still needs a measured and attributable raw sweet-orange comparison with prospective all-cause fall counts in diagnosed Parkinson disease.

## Evidence recovered and remaining scope

- **Grouped citrus and hospital admissions.** Sim and colleagues followed 1,429 older women for first fall-related hospitalization. The adjusted hazard ratio per 20 g/day of oranges and other citrus was 1.01 (95% CI 0.99–1.04, p=.345). The 1,468-participant fracture analysis by Blekkenhorst and colleagues draws on the same CAIFOS parent cohort. It reports an adjusted hazard ratio of 1.01 (0.97–1.04) for all fracture-related admissions per 20 g/day grouped citrus. Each analysis pools oranges with other citrus and uses hospital admissions. A separate raw sweet-orange estimate and repeated falls in Parkinson disease were outside these analyses. [2018 falls original](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2018-PMID30153877-Sim-Fruit-Injurious-Falls.txt) · [2017 fracture companion](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2017-PMID28524097-Blekkenhorst-Citrus-Fractures.txt)
- **Combined supplement and fall risk.** The 36-person Altemor pilot outside Parkinson disease studied diosmin, hesperidin and proanthocyanidins together. The public results report a favorable change in a test-based fall-risk measure. The publisher and institutional records yielded no complete results table. The official registry lists no deposited results. Retrieve arm-level Hunova estimates and investigate whether actual fall events were recorded. [Article, registry replay and exact access route](Source-Text/2025-PMID40694049-Giovannini-Altemor-Elderly-Falls-Cognition.txt)
- **Combined fruit and vegetable intake.** Cross-sectional SAGE data associated insufficient combined fruit and vegetable consumption with retrospectively reported injurious falls, adjusted OR 1.41 (95% CI 1.05–1.90). Exposure and diagnosis were pooled too broadly to determine what raw orange does to falls in Parkinson disease. [Original SAGE methods and Table 2](Source-Text/2025-PMID40095277-SAGE-Fruit-Vegetables-Injurious-Falls.txt)
- **Kampo prescription context.** The traditional formula Yokukansankachimpihange contains dried Citrus unshiu peel. In a four-week dementia with Lewy bodies sleep study, patients or bed partners reported no falls linked to oversedation or muscular relaxation. The design provides no all-cause fall count or raw sweet-orange comparison. The two notes are from one observation family. [Original clinical record](../2020-2024/Source-Text/2020-PMID32923452-Raw-Orange-Falls-Scope.txt) · [Traditional-practice record](../2020-2024/Cultural-and-Experiential/Traditional-Practices/2020-Yokukansankachimpihange-Dried-Citrus-Peel.txt)
- **Parkinson falls diaries.** The PRIME diet-and-exercise protocol plans prospective falls safety capture at visits and through a diary. The official registry replay still has no posted results. The intervention is a whole Mediterranean diet and exercise design, with no isolated raw-orange arm. Check the registry and full results for whether any separate citrus exposure was measured against those falls. [Protocol original](Source-Text/PMC13057374.txt) · [Registry replay and outcome gap](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07097103.txt)
