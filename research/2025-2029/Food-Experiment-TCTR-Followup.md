# Thai Clinical Trials Registry and citation follow-up

## Thai Mucuna crossover trial

Boonmongkol and colleagues first published results on March 26, 2025, from a single-blind randomized crossover study in Thailand. Eleven of 12 participants completed both sessions comparing *Mucuna pruriens* powder with dispersible levodopa and benserazide. The powder had greater systemic levodopa exposure (AUC ratio 155.67%, 90% CI 134.59% to 180.04%) and longer ON time without dyskinesia (232.2 versus 161.8 minutes, p = .01). Mild nausea and dizziness were more frequent with the powder. This was an acute medicinal preparation of velvet bean, a species distinct from broad bean (*Vicia faba*). A TCTR identifier did not surface in the title and author search. [Primary report, PMID 40137945](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40137945/)

## Twelve-week Buriram cannabis extract outcome

Mitarnun and colleagues first published the journal results of TCTR20230204001 online on April 16, 2025. The team randomized 60 people and analyzed 51 (27 extract, 24 placebo). The primary MoCA delayed recall outcome showed no clear difference. A secondary naming subscore favored the extract (+0.37 points, 95% CI +0.01 to +0.73). The report also describes a language score increase in the placebo arm and no clear motor, affective, or inflammatory marker differences. Both sublingual study products contained olive oil according to the registration, so the randomized contrast leaves the effect of olive oil unresolved. [2023 registration and earlier conference reference](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-TCTR-Followup.md#twelve-week-buriram-cannabis-extract-registration) · [Primary report, PMID 40267585](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40267585/)

## Food habits and symptoms in Thai Parkinson outpatients

Phokaewvarangkul and colleagues first published this cross-sectional study of 70 Thai outpatients on April 30, 2026. Table 1 reports food intake and symptoms in the same cohort. Twelve participants reported daily olive oil, 19 reported dysphagia, and 45 reported constipation. Table 2 stratifies by weight category. Daily olive oil intake was reported by 2 of 13 underweight and 10 of 57 normal-to-overweight participants (p = .609). The paper reports higher dysphagia and dyskinesia measures in the underweight group but gives no estimate of symptom change after any individual food exposure. No TCTR identifier surfaced. [Primary report, PMID 42059527](https://doi.org/10.1177/1877718X261445952)
