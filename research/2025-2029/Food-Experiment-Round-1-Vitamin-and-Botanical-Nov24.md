## Sesame and turmeric citation trail

The Huntington model review links sesame oil through isolated sesamol and turmeric through standardized curcumin. Reference 180 reports rat motor coordination and locomotion. Reference 150 uses an elevated plus maze and water maze to measure memory. Anxiety remains unverified in that experiment. Both papers share authors and model class, so the scorecards count one provisional lineage. Reference 181 is a mouse kidney study. Reference 114 reports broader animal motor and cognitive outcomes using standardized curcumin. Exact movement speed and culinary turmeric exposure remain open. [Huntington botanical review](../2010-2014/Source-Text/2013-PMID24347915-Huntington-Botanical-Review-Ingredient-Scope.txt)

The 2023 oral oil abstract reports 60 randomized and 53 completing. Both arms received dietary energy restriction. The published conclusion favors sesame over sunflower for fatigue, anxiety and depression, while the reported p values cannot be assigned to specific contrasts from the accessible abstract. The 2022 companions specify refined and odorless oils. The 2025 metabolic report matches the registration and participant flow and contributes no further mood or fatigue endpoint. The combined family gives a minimal easing lean outside Parkinson disease with a different oil grade. Clinical cold-pressed and toasted preparations remain open. [Local trial registration](../2020-2024/Source-Text/Trials/IRCT20140208016529N6.txt)

## Green tea preparations and fatigue

The fibroid proceedings discuss EGCG. Their separate drug adverse-event table describes a different intervention. The primary fibroid trial used decaffeinated extract and reports a symptom composite, quality of life and hemoglobin without a standalone fatigue result. Its oncology citation reports fatigue under uncontrolled caffeinated concentrate dose escalation. These preparations, settings and outcomes differ from the small uncontrolled brewed-green-tea Parkinson observation used in the claim scorecard.
