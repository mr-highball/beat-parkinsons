# Corn oil and Parkinson’s symptom follow-up

## Corn oil vehicle in a mouse olfactory training experiment

The 2026 experiment assigned 30 mice to vehicle control, 3-methylindole-induced olfactory impairment, or impairment plus olfactory training. The injections used corn oil in all arms, including the matched-volume control. The control mice and toxin-exposed mice were assessed using buried-food-pellet latency. That outcome tests olfactory behavior following toxin exposure or training on a shared oil background. The oil was injected and refinement was unreported. Use this study as an exposure and endpoint boundary for the proposed worsening of Parkinson olfaction after dietary refined corn oil (0823). [Primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13417942/)

## Corn oil vehicle in a lycopene pole test

The July 2026 study used corn oil for oral gavage in the lycopene experiment. Figure 2 includes pole-turn and descent time in mice. The methods paragraph describes a same-volume corn-oil gavage in one sentence while descriptions of the nominal blank control and treatment allocation conflict elsewhere. The experiment does not provide a reliable isolated oil estimate, a verified refining grade or an instance of Parkinson clinical bradykinesia.
