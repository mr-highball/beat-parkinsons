# Macadamia oil and hazelnut preparation follow-up

## Postmeal sleepiness after oral macadamia oil

Ikeguchi and colleagues published two single-blind crossover experiments in 2025. Their journal abstract reports that macadamia oil with bread reduced acute postmeal sleepiness and fatigue relative to bread alone or bread with soybean oil. The author’s companion report describes 16 and eight healthy students receiving 8 g of macadamia oil or soybean oil in bread-matched meals and a higher-carbohydrate bread control. Figure 3 describes a tendency toward less sleepiness with macadamia oil than soybean oil at 30 minutes in the larger sample. Figure 5 gives the smaller sample's acute ratings. It reports that fatigue contrasts range from trends to statistical significance. The accessible sources do not establish cold pressing. Prior-night sleep timing is baseline information and these results provide no intervention-period dream enactment, insomnia or manual dexterity measure. The grant report, companion paper and journal abstract cover the same two experiments. ## Subsequent macadamia oil dose and vigilance registrations

UMIN000057570, disclosed April 9, 2025, lists bread alone and bread with 2, 4 or 8 g of macadamia or soy oil. Primary outcomes include acute sleepiness, fatigue, blood glucose and core temperature. UMIN000059952, disclosed December 3, 2025, additionally lists powder and liquid oil formulations and a psychomotor vigilance task to measure alertness. It collects previous-night sleep timing and sleep-quality scores separately. Neither public record supplies outcome results, a Parkinson cohort, dream enactment observation, or a validated measure of hand dexterity. ## Macadamia oil and rat brain injury biomarkers

Salama and colleagues first published their rat study on August 14, 2025. Macadamia oil treatment was associated with changes in oxidative and inflammatory markers and lower caspase-3 and GFAP immunostaining in cerebellar cortex after chemically induced brain injury. The accessible primary abstract gives no motor or bulbar behavioral results, no confirmed cold pressing and no Parkinson model. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40818780/) · [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fitote.2025.106821)

## Hazelnut challenge and pooled allergy symptoms in children

Brindisi and colleagues first published January 29, 2026. Among 60 pediatric hazelnut oral food challenges, 41 were positive. Table 2 reports a combined dysphonia, pruritus or throat-constriction category in 22 of 88 children and hypotension in four of 88, pooled over the cohort of multiple food allergies. The authors do not assign these four hypotension cases or the combined voice and throat category to individual hazelnut challenges. The exposure and result cannot verify either cold-pressed hazelnut oil or an orthostatic or voice effect in Parkinson disease.
