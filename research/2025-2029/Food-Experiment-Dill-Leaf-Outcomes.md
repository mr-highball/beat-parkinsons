# Dill extract and motor behavior

## Oral sowa extract and motor performance during alcohol withdrawal

Talreja and colleagues used a shade-dried, defatted ethanolic *Anethum graveolens* sowa extract in an alcohol-withdrawal model, with five groups of six male Wistar rats. The Methods identify the plant and extraction procedure. The specific plant part is unclear. Table 3.5 gives mean rotarod retention at 24 hours of 62.7 and 66.2 seconds with the two extract doses, 33.02 seconds under withdrawal alone and 60.03 seconds in the nonwithdrawal control. The study reports p<.01 for each extract dose versus withdrawal alone. Table 3.2.2 gives open-arm entry proportions of 40.70% and 43.72% with extract versus 19.21% under withdrawal alone at 24 hours. The different motor performance across groups limits attribution of exploration changes to anxiety alone. The study measured whole-body coordination under induced withdrawal. It supplies no clinical bradykinesia, rest tremor, dystonia, precision hand task or fresh leaf comparison. [Primary report, Methods and Tables 3.2.2 and 3.5](https://storage.googleapis.com/innctech/ejpmr/article_issue/1749199475.pdf)

## Dill extract and offspring reflexes after perinatal nicotine exposure

Maodaa and colleagues gave oral dill extract to pregnant mice and later measured developmental reflexes and anxiety-like behavior in the newborn offspring. The publisher abstract reports improvements in righting, rotation and cliff avoidance under extract and reductions in anxiety-like behavior after developmental nicotine exposure. The abstract does not identify the clinical equivalent of these motor measures, the plant part or individual test estimates. The study compares maternal exposure and development. The 2021 paper from the research group shares a developmental nicotine model but reports a different dill dose and examines liver and kidney tissue. Animal reuse has not been established. The institutional record is a bibliographic deposit without public full text. [Primary article, June 2, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10735-025-10457-9) · [Institutional record](https://hdl.handle.net/10779/mmu.32554629) · [2021 primary organ study](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6412/11/7/838)

The public figure and supplement identify elevated-plus-maze arm entries and time. Nicotine plus extract shows higher open-arm values than nicotine alone, with about 22 versus 16 entries and 118 versus 82 seconds read from the graph. The figure caption and pairwise tests remain gated. The supplement omits n and degrees of freedom and has apparent F and p-value inconsistencies. The tested extract organ and relevance to dried dill leaf remain unclear.
