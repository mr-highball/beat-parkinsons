# Round 1 olive-oil anxiety, sage fatigue and banana source checks

The review checked 103 exact scopes across 60 authenticated publications and 31 frozen claims. It records 78 exclusions and 25 related scopes. A cited acute fatigue trial was reused and a banana biomarker study entered the earlier publication bucket. One verified already indexed Parkinson diet original was reused for the exact sage questions.

The [UK Biobank cohort](Source-Text/2025-PMID41245532-Dietary-Patterns-Olive-Oil-and-Incident-Brain-Disorders.txt) gives an adjusted olive oil estimate of HR 0.905 for incident anxiety comparing the 90th and 10th percentiles (p=.00234, FDR q=.020475). Table S23 separately gives an inconclusive estimate for incident Parkinson disease (HR 1.01, p=.878). Oil grade is unavailable, and the endpoint is a first diagnosis in people recruited without major brain disorders. It does not estimate anxiety severity in patients with Parkinson disease. The abstract and body describe different main cohort sizes.

The [Jackson crossover](../2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC7468953.txt) reports lower acute task fatigue for apple and coffee-berry blends against placebo (p=.002 and .003). The three active drinks share beetroot, ginseng and sage extract. Placebo contains no active ingredients. The observed effects belong to the combinations and do not isolate sage.

The [Lee study](../2005-2009/Source-Text/2009-PMID19951298-Banana-Plasma-and-Rat-Tissue-Salsolinol.txt) verifies increased circulating salsolinol after banana in six healthy volunteers. Its separate rat analysis found no clear corresponding difference in striatum and nucleus accumbens. The tissue result is limited to the sampled model and the study does not measure anxiety. The frozen banana, olive-oil and sage directional decisions remain at their existing minimal tier.

Forty-three exact scopes concern dried date fruit and pain. They cover neurologic, orthopedic, palliative and other clinical settings, with no verified exposure comparison for dried date fruit in Parkinson pain.
