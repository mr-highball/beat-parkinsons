## Rice bran oil and rigidity

The 2016 review cites the 2014 Naz and Shireen experiment. Figure 4.4 shows reduced catalepsy with oral red rice bran oil compared with saline in the haloperidol groups. The water groups show a smaller opposite separation. The Results record some higher measurements in the oil-only group and the Discussion describes that group's response differently. The paper also leaves the refining process, precise significance by week and per-arm denominators unresolved. Later experiments with an enriched rice bran oil formula or isolated rice constituents address related hypotheses with different exposure limitations. [2014 original comparison](../2010-2014/Source-Text/2014-Naz-Shireen-Red-Rice-Bran-Oil-Catalepsy.txt) · [2016 companion review](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2016-PMID27540314-Drug-Induced-Movement-Rice-Bran-Oil.txt) · [Later animal models and enriched formulation](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Soybean-Rice-Bran-Oils.md)

The drug-induced group is the more relevant animal model for this comparison. Oral oil was isolated in that contrast. The result therefore supports a minimal easing signal on the catalepsy proxy, with substantial uncertainty about transferring it to refined oil and human Parkinson rigidity. The frozen 0476 claim predicts worsening, and the new rubric-checked verdict is Maybe False. The original did not separately assess clinical dystonia or constipation.
