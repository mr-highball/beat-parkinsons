# Off-time item in an oral cumin-containing Parkinson case

## Cumin in a combined Ayurvedic regimen with a reported OFF item

In a 2025 single-patient report, Mohandas, Sharma and Shetty describe an inpatient regimen for a person with Parkinson’s. It included one oral Anuloma DS combination tablet at bedtime, with *Cuminum cyminum* (Jeeraka) listed among the formulation ingredients. The study gives no cumin mass or preparation within the tablet. Multiple concurrent interventions included oral Zandopa granules (*Mucuna pruriens*, a levodopa source) and therapeutic enemas. The report lists levodopa/carbidopa at presentation. It leaves the conventional dosing schedule during treatment and follow-up unspecified.

Table 3 prints the OFF item as 2 (50%) on February 9, 11 and 14, and 2 (25%) on February 16 and March 17. The integer did not change. The paper supplies no diary, percentage calculation, or hours per day, and its first table measurement was after admission. The discharge medication list retains oral *Mucuna* and omits the cumin-containing tablet. The percentage annotations suggest improvement during the overall treatment course, weakly connecting cumin exposure with the intended symptom. The design cannot isolate cumin, its formulation, a levodopa interaction, or a change in daily OFF hours. Separate Anuloma DS studies report bowel endpoints in functional constipation. They give no motor or levodopa results and no evidence that the later case used the same batch. A controlled Parkinson crossover shows that the case's concurrent levodopa-containing *Mucuna* is independently capable of altering acute motor response. The crossover does not estimate its contribution to the case or verify a dietary effect.
