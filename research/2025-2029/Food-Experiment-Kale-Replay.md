# Kale seed extract and registered neurological study

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT07360977 on 22 January 2026. It describes a randomized, parallel, open-label study in PD, multiple sclerosis and pediatric neurologic cohorts. The PD experimental arm receives bioactivated glucoraphanin (GRA), with adult dose 50 mg/day for six months, alongside a PD standard-care arm. PD eligibility includes age 45–75, UK Brain Bank clinical diagnosis, three months of clinical stability, and stable antiparkinsonian drugs for at least three months before entry; common requirements include no drug-treatment changes during the six-month study and consent. Listed exclusions include MRI contraindication, major confounding neurological disease or severe comorbidity, MMSE below 24, another clinical trial, and pregnancy or lactation. These are eligibility criteria, not observed patient characteristics. The captured description does not specify route. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07360977) · [Local selected primary registry fields](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07360977.txt)

The primary publication by Gugliandolo et al. on 16 September 2026 reports a laboratory experiment in MPP+-treated differentiated SH-SY5Y cells. It identifies the GRA extract as made from Tuscan black kale (*Brassica oleracea*) seeds and the myrosinase extract from *Sinapis alba* seeds using hydrodynamic cavitation; material was spray-dried and treated by gamma radiation. The authors say these formulations were intended for the registered clinical trial. This is a processed seed-derived compound preparation, not a steamed, roasted, or raw kale leaf intervention; intent is not confirmation of participants receiving or consuming the specific batch. The experiment measured cell viability, gene expression and protein rather than the frozen human symptoms. [Primary laboratory paper, Methods and Discussion](Source-Text/PMC13607179.txt) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27188232)

## White mustard enzyme in the intended glucoraphanin formulation

The companion laboratory team identifies white mustard seed as the source of processed myrosinase for the formulation intended for NCT07360977. A current registry check showed active, not recruiting and no posted results. The registered Parkinson motor outcome uses a composite and supplies no isolated rest-tremor estimate. The study record does not confirm that trial participants received the exact lab material. The enzyme preparation and kale seed glucoraphanin combination cannot isolate the effect of toasted culinary mustard seed.
