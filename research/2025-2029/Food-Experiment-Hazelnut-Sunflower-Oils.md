# Hazelnut and sunflower oil evidence

## Sunflower oil vehicle in a fortified cognitive model

Tang and colleagues published January 31, 2025. The scopolamine mouse experiment compared diets with and without added vitamins or sunflower lecithin. Table 1 specifies 40 g/kg of sunflower oil in all diets. The behavioral improvements measure the difference between additive regimens at equal oil exposure. The design provides no independent sunflower contrast or Parkinson model. [Local primary text](Source-Text/PMC11819866.txt) · [Published article, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11819866/)

## Sunflower oil comparator in a teenage attention trial

ANZCTR first registered ACTRN12625000047493 on January 20, 2025, and linked the journal results on August 25, 2026. Lopresti and Smith first published June 15, 2026. Eighty-two healthy adolescents received a sunflower-oil placebo or lutein and zeaxanthin for six months. Attention and processing speed favored carotenoids (p = .026 and .034), while visual reasoning, nonverbal memory, self-reported attention and sleep showed no clear group difference. The registry describes a matching placebo containing all ingredients except the carotenoids, and neither accessible source specifies oil grade. The design did not measure a Parkinson subgroup or independent oil effect. [Official ANZCTR registration](https://anzctr.org.au/ACTRN12625000047493.aspx) · [Local registry summary](Source-Text/Trials/ACTRN12625000047493.txt) · [Original results abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42413424/)

## Sunflower oil emulsion gels for dysphagia food design

The October 1, 2025 primary study used a named commercial sunflower oil in inulin and psyllium emulsion gels added to meat and fish purées. The formulations met IDDSI Level 4 in fork pressure and spoon tilt tests. The experiments evaluated texture, cohesion, and enzymatic properties and did not measure swallowing in a patient. A distinct 2025 surimi study also tested sunflower-oil gels with bench-level IDDSI criteria. The public reports do not establish the refining status or a direction for clinical swallowing symptoms. [Local emulsion study](Source-Text/PMC12526442.txt) · [Surimi study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40597497/)

## Injected sunflower oil in a Parkinson depression and memory model

Ramos and colleagues first published June 25, 2026. The sunflower-oil vehicle group was compared with an intraperitoneal rotenone group. Compared with vehicle, rotenone increased forced-swim immobility (p = .0059), and worsened social recognition ratio (p = .0101). No untreated or alternative-oil arm isolates an effect of sunflower oil. The exposure was injected, the oil grade is not given, and the behavioral assays differ from clinical depression and executive impairment. [Local primary article and Figures 3–4](Source-Text/PMC13377602.txt) · [Published article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13377602/)

## Sunflower oil excipient in the Ginger PD registration

ClinicalTrials.gov posted Ginger-PD, NCT07882108, October 20, 2026. The Parkinson, REM sleep behavior disorder and control groups receive one of two doses of the same ginger capsule formula, whose ingredient list includes sunflower seed oil. The study plans ghrelin and gut outcomes and exploratory MDS-UPDRS I–IV, MoCA and semantic fluency measurements. It reports neither the amount or grade of the excipient nor an oil-specific comparison. The trial was recruiting without posted results. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07882108) · [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07882108.txt)
