# Pomegranate oral interventions and nonmotor outcome follow-up

## Pomegranate juice and sleep quality during opioid treatment

Leventelis and colleagues’ July 25, 2025 sleep publication reports 58 people treated for opioid use disorder. Forty drank pomegranate juice and 18 received no juice. The article reports improved global sleep quality after 120 days only in the methadone subgroup and higher measured salivary melatonin in both medication groups. A separate daytime dysfunction score or sleepiness adverse-event count was not verified from the accessible report. The January craving paper is a companion from the same registration, with drug craving and blood redox outcomes. These studies did not test raw arils, Parkinson daytime sleepiness or apathy. [Sleep publication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40709794/) · [Craving publication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39798867/) · [2023 registration and protocol note](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Pomegranate.md#registered-pomegranate-juice-and-sleep-during-opioid-treatment)

## Swallowed pomegranate extract and pediatric oral outcomes

Ramesh and Syam’s January 15, 2026 trial reports 500 young children enrolled for a dental caries intervention. The intervention used a daily swish-and-swallow extract and the comparison group received standard oral hygiene guidance. The methods describe measuring salivary flow. The outcome tables report salivary pH and dental measures without a separate flow estimate or a drooling outcome. [Primary article, Methods and Tables 2–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12855020/) · [Publication metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41624364/)

## Registered pomegranate acai blend and self reported motivation

NCT07766564 first posted in August 2026. It lists self-reported motivation among secondary outcomes in 30 adults using two commercial liquid blends sequentially, including a pomegranate-acai product. There is no parallel control or isolated aril comparison. The public record contains no posted results. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07766564)
