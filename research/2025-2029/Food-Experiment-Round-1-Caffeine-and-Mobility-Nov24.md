# Round 1 caffeine, mobility and withdrawal comparisons

Forty-two exact checks across 30 original publications now have dated source notes. The review distinguishes treatment goals, exposure terms, measured fatigue and fatigue discussed only as background. [Exact evidence packet](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/followup/priority-five-caffeine-and-mobility-nov24/README.md)

## Fatigue during withdrawal and usual use

The 1992 two-day blinded cessation comparison reported abnormally high POMS fatigue in 8% after placebo compared with 0% at baseline or with capsule replacement. This was a thresholded observation. The 2009 caffeine-maintenance crossover found the highest fatigue item after placebo challenged active maintenance, with a narrower finding on the fatigue subscale. A separate 2009 crossover found a tiredness effect after 30-hour abstention and did not establish the same benefit after usual intake. Attention and arousal measurements address separate outcomes. [1992 threshold comparison](../1990-1994/Source-Text/1992-PMID1528206-Blinded-Caffeine-Cessation-Fatigue.txt) · [2009 maintenance trial](../2005-2009/Source-Text/2009-PMID19241060-Caffeine-Maintenance-and-Fatigue.txt) · [2009 usual-use comparison](../2005-2009/Source-Text/2009-PMID19777214-Caffeine-Usual-Use-and-Abstention.txt)

Earlier low-dose substitution and maintenance studies report variable withdrawal symptoms. A 1991 comparison describes a tiredness measure, but its accessible abstract omits the item-specific estimate. The two 1997 Lane publications had different comparison designs and sample sizes with unresolved overlap. Follow the original tables before counting their fatigue findings as independently replicated. [1990 substitution](../1990-1994/Source-Text/1990-PMID2262896-Low-Dose-Caffeine-Withdrawal.txt) · [1991 timing comparison](../1990-1994/Source-Text/1991-PMID22282364-Caffeine-Abstinence-Tiredness.txt) · [1997 fixed-capsule report](../1995-1999/Source-Text/1997-PMID9402612-Blinded-Caffeine-and-Brief-Deprivation.txt) · [1998 reinforcement and symptoms](../1995-1999/Source-Text/1998-PMID9784073-Caffeine-Dependence-and-Fatigue.txt)

## Caffeine reduction trials and separate symptoms

The early treatment trial comes from a documented baseline intake cohort. It tracks caffeine from multiple sources. The investigators explicitly excluded prospective withdrawal trajectories from their analyses. In the later manual-only trial, the highest rating across five withdrawal domains made up one composite. Fatigue or drowsiness was one possible contributor, and standalone fatigue results were unavailable. [2012 intake cohort](../2010-2014/Source-Text/2012-PMID22369218-Caffeine-Dependence-Intake-Cohort.txt) · [2015 trial](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2015-PMID26501499-Manualized-Caffeine-Reduction-and-Fatigue-Scope.txt) · [2018 Table 1](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2018-PMID30580203-Caffeine-Taper-and-Withdrawal-Composite.txt)

The remote reduction study's registry was posted in 2020 with results in 2025. It provides a 19-item standardized total plus named anxiety, sleep and gastrointestinal measures. The 2026 publication gives matching enrollment and group counts, while its public abstract omits the NCT identifier. The paired records are provisionally treated as one program. Separate fatigue data and a direct identifier link remain open until study tables and full text can be inspected. [Registry with posted results](../2020-2024/Source-Text/Trials/NCT04560595.txt) · [2026 publication scope](../2025-2029/Source-Text/2026-PMID41864111-Remote-Caffeine-Reduction-Outcomes.txt)

A dated public account describes more fatigue and lighter sleep approximately a month after stopping several caffeine sources, with later improvement in restfulness. Other commenters discuss changes at different times. The account has no verified Parkinson diagnosis, separate beverage effect or validated fatigue measure. ## Application to Parkinson fatigue

The symptom-definition review distinguishes subjective Parkinson fatigue, performance fatigability and related apathy. Reassessments of the two brewed-beverage hypotheses give greater transfer weight to actual beverage exposure and clinically relevant fatigue results. The short withdrawal observations remain relevant context and some general-use endpoints are inconclusive. No controlled brewed-beverage Parkinson fatigue comparison was recovered here.
