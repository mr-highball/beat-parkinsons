# Bean Sleep Followup

## Registered Mucuna and soybean tea sleep comparison

First public registry date October 30, 2025. The planned crossover compares roasted Mucuna and black soybean tea with black soybean tea in healthy adults. Planned primary assessments include sleep quality and mood. The last inspected registry update lists recruiting, with results unavailable. Mucuna and soybean are separate genera from common bean, and the exposure is a tea rather than boiled pinto beans. ## Grouped bean score in the MIND cardiometabolic companion

First published December 29, 2025, with a January 30, 2026 version of record. The report uses the same 44 participants and registration IRCT20181111041611N8 as the sleep trial. Table 2 gives a grouped bean score changing from 0.23 to 0.70 in the MIND arm and 0.23 to 0.45 in control, with adjusted follow-up p=.003. The baseline difference is printed as zero with a confidence interval spanning zero, alongside p=.007. That discrepancy remains unresolved. The supplement groups beans, lentils, chickpeas and soybeans. Food diaries were collected and participant data are unavailable publicly. The report gives no pinto identity, preparation or ingredient-specific sleep response. ## MIND trial third report on quality of life and biomarkers

First public accepted version September 26, 2026. It explicitly identifies the same 44 enrolled women, registration IRCT20181111041611N8 and three-month comparison as the earlier sleep and cardiometabolic reports. The modified MIND program had adjusted improvements in SF-36 energy/fatigue and emotional well-being. The reported comparisons concern the assigned diet and biomarkers. The third paper does not add a separate sleep/insomnia result or participant pinto intake. The accepted article is not yet the final version of record on the inspected route. ## Black soybean seed coat extract and sleepiness on rising

First published June 11, 2025. Akagi and colleagues randomized 64 healthy Japanese adults, 32 per arm, to black soybean seed coat extract capsules or placebo for 12 weeks. OSA-MA factor I was measured immediately upon waking. In the full analysis set, scores favored extract at weeks eight and twelve, with higher scores denoting a more favorable waking state. The trial concerns Glycine max extract and a waking-time measure. Ordinary boiled Phaseolus vulgaris beans, clinical daytime sleepiness, and Parkinson disease are outside its scope. Fujicco employees were among the authors. The paper identifies the earlier task-day crossover as a separate study. ## Daytime dysfunction in the MIND trial sleep report

Table 4 gives the daytime dysfunction component separately from the global PSQI score. The adjusted 12-week contrast was −0.41 points (95% CI −1.00 to 0.18, p=.163). Both arms improved within group. The component captures daytime function through the sleep questionnaire and the randomized comparison covers the full diet. The study does not identify black bean consumption or a Parkinson daytime sleepiness effect.
