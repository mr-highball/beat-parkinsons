# Parkinson swallowing studies and food textures

## 2025 conference abstract of the French medication swallowing survey

Rittié-Burkhard and colleagues reported the [2024 thesis survey](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-DUMAS-Swallowing.md#2024-french-survey-of-medication-swallowing-practices) as JNLF abstract P222 in the April 2025 issue of *Revue Neurologique*. The title, authorship overlap and response counts match the thesis. The abstract repeats professional practice findings and gives no patient-level food or swallowing outcomes. Its DOI was registered March 18, 2025. The Crossref license records are for text and data mining. [Publisher abstract](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1730906/article/la-dysphagie-medicamenteuse-chez-le-patient-parkin) · [Crossref date and license metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/j.neurol.2025.01.229)

## 2025 French questionnaire for swallowing and oral motor symptoms

Jessica Dassaud and Anne Giffon defended this thesis on June 4, 2025. DUMAS records public release on December 17, 2025. They evaluated the French Radboud Oral Motor inventory for Parkinson’s disease in 19 people with Parkinson’s. In Table 9, the swallowing score correlated with the Dysphagia Handicap Index (Spearman ρ = .869) and MDS-UPDRS II item 2.3 (ρ = .872), both p < .001. The swallowing subscale’s internal consistency was α = .690. [Thesis, Tables 9–10](https://dumas.ccsd.cnrs.fr/dumas-05414027/document#page=37) · [HAL release metadata](https://api.archives-ouvertes.fr/search/?q=halId_s:dumas-05414027)

Annex 5 gives meat and peanuts as examples of foods that are hard to chew. One response to the tablet-swallowing item groups using compote with using a special technique. The thesis provides no counts for those individual responses, identifies no fruit in the compote, and tested no food or potato preparation. The cover specifies CC BY-NC-ND 4.0.
