- The 1911 BMJ supplement records organizational business, notices and health statistics. Orange and Saffron Walden occur in administrative or geographical contexts. Later issue indexes similarly use ingredient-like names for authors and other contributors. The 1972 index has a real coffee entry about instant coffee and caffeine, and the 1985 index points to an infant egg hypersensitivity paper. These are bibliographic clues with different exposures and endpoints from the frozen propositions. - The Nurses' Health Study original names string beans in its intake-measurement discussion and reports an inverse association between total vegetable intake and occurrence of the fatigue component of frailty. The item-specific tables are still needed. A separate nonrandomized pediatric comparison reported favorable cognitive fatigue for a green-vegetable category and an inconclusive general-fatigue estimate. A public meal account names cooked string beans as an option alongside other ingredients. Each contributes at its own scope without an isolated effect for claim 0561.
