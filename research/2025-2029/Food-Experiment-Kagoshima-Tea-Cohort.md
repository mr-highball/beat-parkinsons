# Kagoshima green tea and Parkinson nonmotor outcomes in 2025–2029

## Kagoshima green tea conference analysis

Harutoshi Sakakima and colleagues presented an observational report on 31 October 2025. It describes 100 older people with Parkinson's in Kagoshima Prefecture and compares the extremes of reported habitual green tea volume. Methods give >1,000 mL/day (n=22) and ≤500 mL/day (n=37). Results label the upper boundary as ≥1 L/day. The higher-volume group had higher Barthel Index scores (p=.019) and fewer moderate or severe sleep and constipation symptoms (p=.047 and .044). The abstract gives neither group event counts nor logistic effect estimates. The seven listed nonmotor items exclude fatigue and smell.

The conference report, the [2024 sarcopenia analysis](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Kagoshima-Tea-Cohort.md#sarcopenia-and-forward-trunk-posture-in-the-kagoshima-project), and [2026 green tea paper](#green-tea-preparation-and-outcome-reporting-in-the-2026-cohort) share Kagoshima approval 220137 and investigators. The two tea reports provide closely related analyses under that project and count as one evidence trail. The public reports give no participant crosswalk.

## Green tea preparation and outcome reporting in the 2026 cohort

The 30 September 2026 report describes recruitment in Kagoshima from October 2022 to May 2025. It screened 114 people, excluded nine, and compared 105 by reported intake frequency. Among 78 daily users, volume groups were <500 mL/day (42), 500–1,000 mL/day (12) and >1,000 mL/day (24). The Methods names sencha, gyokuro and hojicha. Participants reported usual intake. Household versus commercial preparation, steep conditions and beverage-specific caffeine were unspecified. The 80–100°C passage refers to an external caffeine extraction figure.

Table 1 gives hyposmia in 4/5 nonusers, 16/22 nondaily users and 60/78 daily users (p=.854). Table 2 gives 32/42, 8/12 and 20/24 by increasing daily volume (p=.519). These are cross-sectional prevalence counts. The Methods does not identify an olfactory test or provide a severity score. The seven specified MDS-UPDRS Part I items were cognition, hallucinations and psychosis, depressed mood, anxious mood, apathy, sleep problems and constipation. The listed analyses omit the fatigue item and fatigue-specific scales.

The conference used different volume groups. The journal's Table 3 gives p=.029 for the distribution of sleep ratings and p=.144 for constipation. Tables 4–5 report inverse correlations and adjusted associations for both outcomes. The publisher lists no supplemental attachment. The data statement directs readers to the corresponding author. The analysis differs in population and design from the Kagoshima older-adult exercise and catechin trial UMIN000055115.
