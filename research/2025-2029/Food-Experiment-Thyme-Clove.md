# Thyme and clove evidence, 2025–2029

## Thyme extract and behavior in a paraquat model

Akhter and colleagues published this mouse study online April 2, 2025. The author abstract lists ladder climbing, footprints, wire hanging, an elevated plus maze, Y-maze and open-field testing. It reports improvements in neurobehavioral measures with thyme extract collectively. Task-level effects, sample sizes, exposure route and timing remain to be verified from the full methods and figures. These tests are model proxies for coordination and anxiety. They cannot establish effects of dried thyme on clinical postural instability or anxiety in Parkinson’s. The available abstract supplies no dyskinesia count. [Primary author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40188599/) · [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2025.151740)

## Nasal eugenol and human salivary flow

Faruque and colleagues’ 2025 nasal-spray report tested four odors in 12 healthy adults. Eugenol had no clear salivary-flow increase in the published comparison. Their January 2026 inhaler paper used a different delivery device, with 12 healthy adults again. With eugenol the median flow changed from 0.29 to 0.34 mL/min without a statistically clear difference. The investigators also reported no clear change in saliva stretchiness or perceived oral moisture after inhaled eugenol. The 2026 paper explicitly compares its findings with the previous nasal-spray experiment. Participant overlap remains unverified. Both studies measure salivary secretion, with no direct drooling endpoint, Parkinson’s subgroup or oral ground clove exposure. [2025 primary spray study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0003996925001104) · [2026 inhaler study, Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12789157/) · [Local 2026 CC BY article](Source-Text/PMC12789157.txt)

## Thyme essential oil and isolated guinea pig bladder strips

The 2026 study compared essential oils from thyme, oregano and cinnamon in isolated guinea pig bladder tissue. Table 3 reports a 58 ± 0.9% decrease in potassium-evoked tension at the concentration giving thyme oil’s maximum tested effect. In the longitudinal layer, the authors report increased spontaneous contraction variability at the lower tested thyme concentration and a decrease at a higher concentration. Different contraction measures and concentrations can therefore give different directional results within this one study. The study measured tissue contractility without patients, urinary urgency counts or ingestion of fresh thyme. [Primary report, section 2.2 and Table 3](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-6382/15/9/909) · [Local CC BY article](Source-Text/PMC13603988.txt)

## Clove aromatherapy and postoperative headache

Dehghan and colleagues’ 2025 randomized trial used separate inhaled clove, inhaled lavender and control groups in urological surgery. Table 3 gives mean headache scores of 0.60 with clove and 0.80 with control at eight hours and 0.23 with clove and 0.57 with control at 24 hours. The overall three-arm contrasts were inconclusive at both times (p = .20 and .31). Headache decreased within all three groups. The paper’s prose and Table 3 give different eight-hour standard deviations for clove (1.66 and 1.16). The comparison is relevant to pain but has different formulation, route and population from claim 0956. [Primary report, Table 3](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1002/hsr2.70392) · [2022 registration](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Thyme-Clove.md#registered-clove-aromatherapy-for-postoperative-headache)

## Registered topical clove oil for dental procedure pain

ANZCTR registered ACTRN12625001207404 prospectively on October 31, 2025. The trial plans to compare topical clove oil with olive oil for pain during restoration in 132 participants with occlusal caries. The record still lists recruitment as not started and gives projected dates only, with no results. Even a completed comparison would address a local dental procedure and topical oil. [Official record](https://www.anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=389791) · [Local registry fields](Source-Text/Trials/ACTRN12625001207404.txt)

## Registered clove and citrus aromatherapy in older adults

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT07368374 on January 26, 2026. The record plans four-week comparisons of separate inhaled clove and citrus oils and placebo in older adults. Fatigue inventory and a basic insomnia and sleep quality scale are registered as primary outcomes. The record lists estimated dates and no posted results. The sleep instrument addresses a different construct from the daytime sleepiness outcome in claim 0150. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07368374) · [Local registry fields](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07368374.txt)

## Three topical anesthetics for pediatric dental injections

Awasthi and colleagues randomized children aged seven to ten to topical clove oil, EMLA or lignocaine gel. The primary author abstract reports inconclusive overall between-group differences on the two pain scales. All arms received active preparations and no equivalence margin was reported. This dental challenge gives a different comparison from the older ground-clove placebo study. [Primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13457260/) · [Older ground-clove placebo comparison](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Thyme-Clove.md#ground-clove-gel-and-dental-needle-pain)

## Clove-derived injection and evoked pain in rats

2026-09-20. The clove pharmacopuncture study evaluated a distilled injected formulation in cell and rat experiments. It increased thermal tail-flick latency and attenuated mechanical hypersensitivity after experimental inflammation. This records a local and preclinical analgesic signal with an exposure and outcome distinct from consuming ground clove for Parkinson pain. [Primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13591119/) · [Local source text](Source-Text/PMC13591119.txt)
