# Feeding and nutrition reports from the 2025 NHO meeting and related research

## 2025 candy during oral care and feeding support

Saito described a man in his 70s with Parkinson’s who had a feeding tube and wanted to suck candy. During nursing care that included oral motor exercises and massage, he began moving his lips and tongue toward offered candy. After a month, he voiced preferences such as melon and Kanro candy more often. This observation was made during a combined intervention. The abstract gives no product packaging, measured swallowing change or comparison by flavor or food. Kanro’s original candy formula lists sugar, starch syrup, soy sauce and salt. The case does not establish that melon was served. [Primary abstract P1-10-5](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000252185.pdf#page=332) · [Manufacturer’s description of original Kanro candy](https://www.kanro.co.jp/kanroameshokudo/index.html)

Koide and colleagues reported increased oral intake in an older Parkinson patient following medication adjustment, oral care, nutrition support and rehabilitation guided by a feeding assessment chart. The abstract gives no named preparation. [Primary abstract P1-49-1](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000252185.pdf#page=398)

## 2025 predictors of gastrostomy and recovery of oral feeding

Shiroma and colleagues reviewed 80 Parkinson patients who underwent videofluoroscopy, including 20 who later received a gastrostomy. The adjusted odds ratio for gastrostomy was 3.55 per point of Hoehn and Yahr stage (95% CI 1.48–8.52) and 0.63 per point of meal intake score (95% CI 0.49–0.81). The abstract provides no test bolus composition or named food analysis. [Primary abstract P1-10-4](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000252185.pdf#page=332)

Oike and colleagues reviewed 42 Parkinson patients who received nasogastric nutrition. Seven later discontinued tube feeding. Five of those seven had an interval of combined oral and tube feeding, with a mean 70.8 days to discontinuation reported for this transition. Thirteen of all 42 had been admitted with aspiration pneumonia. Sixteen of the 35 who remained on tube feeding received gastrostomy and 11 died. The report does not identify the oral preparations or compare individual food effects. The authors’ professional society lists the presentation on November 7, 2025. [Primary abstract P1-116-7](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000252185.pdf#page=513) · [Presentation date](https://kokueikyo.jp/gyouseki/gyouseki-01-06.html)

## 2025 diet diversity protocol and 2026 GLIM results

A hospital ethics record approved on March 27, 2025 identifies Ueno’s planned study of malnutrition risk in older Parkinson patients. The protocol describes a Food Intake Diversity Score and an oral frailty checklist alongside GLIM nutritional assessment. The public protocol gives no item-level data. [Hospital ethics record 6-22](https://saishun.hosp.go.jp/files/000235635.pdf)

Ueno and colleagues reported cross-sectional nutritional assessment in 99 Parkinson outpatients aged 65 or over with Hoehn and Yahr stages I–III. GLIM classified 33% as malnourished. On the Mini Nutritional Assessment–Short Form, 47% were at risk and 2% were classified as malnourished. The abstract reports age, sex and living arrangement comparisons and gives no dietary diversity or oral frailty results. A March 6, 2026 Kumamoto meeting lists a second presentation by Ueno on GLIM. The study’s overlap and diet-item results remain unverified. The earlier tube-feeding report concerns a different clinical setting and research question. [Primary abstract O-254, printed p. S68](https://www.eiyou.or.jp/gakujutsu/pdf/jsmcn_Vol29.Supplement_vo.pdf) · [February 1 and March 6 presentation records](https://kokueikyo.jp/gyouseki/gyouseki-01-06.html)
