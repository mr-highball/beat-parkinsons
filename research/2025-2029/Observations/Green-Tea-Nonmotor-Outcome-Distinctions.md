# Green tea and separate Parkinson’s symptom outcomes

The 2026 Japanese cross-sectional analysis reports smaller odds for mild-or-worse nighttime sleep problems and constipation with greater volume among daily green-tea drinkers. The constipation group comparison and the selected adjusted model use different tests and yield different p-values. Five participants reported no green tea. Other fluids, brewing parameters, and the scaling of the model’s tea term remain gaps. [Primary study extract](../Source-Text/2026-PMID42816813-Sakakima-Green-Tea-Nonmotor-Symptoms.txt)

The previous period’s MVP baseline figure gives an inverse correlation for a fatigue item with a poorly specified tea preparation. An earlier uncontrolled brewed-tea pilot measured fatigue severity directly and reported an increase. The 2026 survey supplies new associations for separate symptoms. Its binary hyposmia findings are nonmonotonic by both frequency and volume, and the authors did not report fatigue severity, quantitative olfactory severity, daytime sleepiness, or a handwriting item.
