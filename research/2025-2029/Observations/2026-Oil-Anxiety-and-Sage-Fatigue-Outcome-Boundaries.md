# Olive-oil incidence and sage-containing blend outcomes

A [2025 prospective cohort](../Source-Text/2025-PMID41245532-Dietary-Patterns-Olive-Oil-and-Incident-Brain-Disorders.txt) reports a lower hazard of incident anxiety in people who reported higher olive-oil intake. Its separate incident Parkinson comparison is inconclusive. Earlier [Parkinson diet comparisons](../Food-Experiment-Oil-Followup.md) examine whole diets and actual symptoms. These questions remain distinct because the population, exposure definition and outcome differ.

The [2020 sage-containing blend trial](../../2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC7468953.txt) reports less short-term mental fatigue for two active blend combinations than placebo. That result differs in exposure and setting from the earlier documented [fresh sage-tip extract](../../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#fresh-sage-tip-extract-and-menopausal-fatigue). The blend does not establish any individual contribution from sage. Neither study measures the frozen fresh culinary sage comparison in Parkinson disease.

The [clinical batch](../Food-Experiment-Round-1-Olive-Anxiety-Sage-and-Banana-Nov24.md) also rechecked the personalized Parkinson diet trial. Its apathy contrast concerns a program of diet changes, timing and counseling, with no dried sage exposure estimate and inconsistent reporting of the final apathy comparison.
