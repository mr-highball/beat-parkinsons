# Visual episode counts, clinical settings and conference reports

An earlier clinical record may say that a person experienced visual hallucinations or give an interview severity score. The critical-care journal study adds a different measure. Its review of clinical notes found hallucinations in 625 of 7,525 patients, with more than one recorded episode in 281 of the 625 and median time to first resolution of 15 hours. The repeat count covers all modalities. Abstract 000132 in the ESICM proceedings reports the same underlying sample, including the proportion classified as visual only. Neither report tracks a relevant culinary exposure. A family-care trial called FICUS appears in the same supplement and has separate participants and endpoints. [Critical-care original](../Source-Text/PMC11783843.txt) · [ESICM proceedings](../Source-Text/PMC12640885.txt).

The general internal medicine supplement has several different visual outcomes. One report describes nightly hallucinations after retinal surgery and severe vision impairment. Another describes visual hallucinations during encephalopathy and multiple nutrient deficiencies after gastrectomy and greatly reduced intake. A separate report links clinical timing to medication changes. The Parkinson case with acute loss of vision reports a different symptom. Combining these observations would create an exposure and outcome no single member measured. [General internal medicine meeting summaries](../Source-Text/PMC12572470.txt).

In the Lewy body and Parkinson cognitive syndrome microbleed comparison, the unadjusted association with visual hallucination presence was inconclusive. It became detectable after adjustment. Both comparisons concern presence in a clinical cohort, without counting food-timed episodes. In ELEGANCE, the seven people with any hallucination reports represent patients with adverse events during medication treatment. The original tabulates visual events and serious visual events using different reporting fields. Avoid adding the serious counts a second time. [Microbleeds](../Source-Text/PMC12016122.txt) · [ELEGANCE results](../Source-Text/PMC12371452.txt) · [ELEGANCE registry](../../2020-2024/Source-Text/Trials/NCT05043103.txt).

Earlier contrast acuity tests, the newer medication or stimulation visual task comparisons, and stimulation-evoked percepts in epilepsy all give distinct measurements. They supply useful alternatives and controls for defining visual symptom endpoints. Dried fig and recurrent Parkinson visual episode frequency remain an open exposure and outcome comparison. [Parkinson reading and contrast](../Source-Text/PMC12455113.txt) · [Stimulation-evoked visual sensations](../Source-Text/PMC12433393.txt).
