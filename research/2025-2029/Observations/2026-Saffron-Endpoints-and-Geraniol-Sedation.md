# Saffron endpoints and geraniol sedation

The 2026 depression network meta-analysis includes a favorable saffron estimate in the main continuous-symptom network. The heterogeneity and inconsistency were high, and saffron did not remain among the interventions confirmed in the restricted depression analysis. An anxiety comparison is a separate outcome. The report concerns major depressive disorder and reuses cohorts such as the [2016 curcumin–saffron trial](../../2015-2019/Source-Text/2016-PMID27723543-Curcumin-Saffron-and-Depression-Contrasts.txt). The Parkinson depression rating still rests on the ground-stigma comparison and its limitations.

The saffron extract model gives a longer rotarod time to fall and stronger grip, with an inconclusive wire-hang comparison. Those endpoints add animal motor context. The earlier ground-stigma Parkinson studies and these animal tests measure different outcomes. The outcome needed to rate the falls claim remains a count or frequency of falls after the relevant exposure.

The 2026 insomnia review identifies geraniol as a lemongrass oil constituent. In the preceding period, the [2022 mouse report](../../2020-2024/Source-Text/2022-PMID35144136-Geraniol-Mouse-Sedation-and-Access.txt) measured pharmacologic sedation after nonculinary administration. The separate [2018 rat report](../../2015-2019/Source-Text/2018-PMID30580793-Geraniol-Sedation-and-Cortical-Spectra.txt) found longer drug-induced sleep with inconclusive sleep latency. Neither result resolves clinical insomnia severity after eating lemongrass.
