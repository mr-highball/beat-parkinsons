# Modifiable Variables in Parkinsonism food and symptom follow-up, 2025–2029

These analyses follow earlier work in the [MVP cohort](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-MVP-Followup.md). The analyses use overlapping participant pools with different exposures, endpoints and follow-up windows.

## PRO-21 adherence and composite progression

Mischley and Murawska’s *Beyond MIND and Mediterranean Diets* was published 16 July 2025. Table 4 gives adjusted baseline component estimates for total PRO-PD. These include fresh vegetables (−165.335, SE 21.636), olive oil (−93.201, SE 20.154), liberal spice use (−61.746, SE 20.031, p = 0.002) and coffee (−70.401, SE 38.186, p = 0.065). Table 4 labels fresh vegetables as a half cup at least five times per week and olive oil as two tablespoons daily. The Methods give different thresholds for the corresponding PRO-21 adherence score, and the Table 4 caption reports N = 2,788 while the abstract describes 2,290 people with PD. [Primary paper, Tables 4 and 5](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu17142330) · [Local source text](Source-Text/PMC12299618.txt)

Table 5 follows repeated total PRO-PD by **whole** baseline PRO-21 diet adherence. Its table labels the four mixed-model slope estimates as unadjusted, at 4.451, 4.480, 3.691 and 2.889 in ascending adherence groups. The highest group included 109 people and 383 observations. The Results also describe covariate-adjusted estimates and give a 45% reduction for the highest group, while the abstract says 43%. The individual food estimates are baseline composite associations, and the subsequent slopes apply to the whole diet score. Neither analysis quantifies fresh vegetables or another food versus subsequent motivation. [Primary paper, Results and Table 5](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu17142330)

## Individual foods and one-year composite symptoms

Mischley, Farahnik and Murawska’s 2025 MDS congress abstract *Food-Related Predictors of Parkinson Progression* was scheduled for 8 October 2025 as e-poster 1307. It fitted a gamma model to 2,788 baseline assessments and applied it to 774 assessments approximately one year and one month later, with cross-validation. Outcomes were total PRO-PD. The abstract identifies greater subsequent overall symptom burden in association with milk, juice, bread, fried food, cheese, butter and soda, and lower overall burden in association with beef, nuts and seeds, olive oil and spices. This prospective analysis predicts subsequent severity from baseline diet. The abstract does not provide an ingredient-specific estimate of within-person symptom change. [Congress abstract and methods](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/food-related-predictors-of-parkinson-progression/) · [Congress schedule](https://www.mdscongress.org/E-Poster-Schedule-updated.pdf#page=21)

In the first figure, the fresh-vegetable row shows a small negative mean correlation, low reliability by the authors’ cross-validation categories, and a plotted bar spanning zero. Coffee is close to zero, also low reliability. In the frequency-stratified second figure, the regular fresh-vegetable marker is negative with high reliability. The heavy, occasional and rare vegetable bins have bars that span zero. Coffee varies by bin, with a positive mean for rare intake, a negative mean for occasional intake and bars spanning zero for regular or heavy intake. The figures do not specify the thresholds for those labels or define the plotted bars. They give no individual fatigue, motivation or other symptom result. The olive oil and spices results pool the oil grades or individual ingredients. ## Alcohol and individual symptom scores

Mischley and Farahnik’s research letter, published 29 April 2026, used self-reported six-month alcohol consumption and PRO-PD from 517 MVP participants. This cross-sectional analysis associated the highest versus lowest quartile of pooled wine, beer and liquor with 23% lower total scores and 24% lower non-motor scores. Figure 1C plots 892 versus 687 for total and 550 versus 418 for non-motor, in the lowest and highest plotted groups. The four group labels sum to 440. The plot title and data show lower scores with higher intake, while the figure caption reverses the direction. The text names fatigue, gait difficulty and apathy among the symptoms with the largest quartile differences. Figure 1B separately plots unlabelled individual symptom magnitudes for daily versus never wine intake. Neither comparison supplies numerical estimates or uncertainty intervals for those symptoms. The exposure is alcoholic beverages, with no corresponding frozen ingredient claim.
