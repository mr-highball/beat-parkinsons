# Bell pepper source follow-through

## Thai Parkinson diet questionnaire and mobility

Phokaewvarangkul et al. first published their Thai Parkinson dietary study on April 30, 2026. Seventy patients with Parkinson disease were assessed for diet and outcomes such as mobility. The original 118-item questionnaire names added chili powder in section 8, item 8.3, and asks about leafy and root vegetable intake in sections 9–10. Both supplement versions were inspected. The questionnaire did not identify bell pepper intake separately. Published mobility analyses give no bell-pepper-specific coefficient. The article and supplements are CC BY-NC and are recorded here as original summaries. [Publisher report and selected questionnaires](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/1877718X261445952) · [Bilingual questionnaire](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC13435194.1/sj-docx-2-pkn-10.1177_1877718X261445952.docx)

## Capsiate and readiness to invest effort in cycling

Leal et al.'s publisher abstract available in November 2026 reports a randomized, double-blind crossover in 11 physically active men. Twelve mg capsiate before repeated maximal cycling did not change readiness to invest effort or perceived exertion. No relevant arm estimates are given. Readiness during exercise is an adjacent behavioral measure, and the study did not use a clinical apathy rating or roasted bell pepper. The publisher and Coventry University give November 2, 2026 as acceptance and describe the formatted paper as in press at review. [Publisher's public abstract](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1992677/abstract) · [Author institution record](https://pureportal.coventry.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effects-of-acute-capsiate-supplementation-on-energy-systems-c/)

## Chilean older-adult recall of red bell pepper

The July 9, 2026 cross-sectional Chilean PACAM study included 181 adults aged at least 60. Interviewers collected three nonconsecutive 24-hour diet recalls. Red bell pepper was a named contributor to estimated dietary polyphenols. The reported model uses total dietary polyphenols. It does not estimate the pepper's individual association with handgrip. The study did not measure walking performance, fine hand dexterity or grilled bell pepper exposure in Parkinson disease.
