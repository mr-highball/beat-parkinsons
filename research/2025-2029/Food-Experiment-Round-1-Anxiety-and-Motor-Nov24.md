## Clinical thresholds and measurements

For the older-adult Mediterranean diet analysis, the US model's 2,352 participants contribute a measured 20-foot walk. High versus low adherence gives odds of slower walking of 0.706 (95% CI 0.511–0.974) before including cognition, and 0.753 (0.540–1.049) after. The continuous model text describes increased risk while Table 3a shows an odds ratio below one. The exposure is a dietary score with a fat ratio, and the Israeli cohort has a disability outcome. The clinical effect of cold-pressed olive oil remains unmeasured in the study. The Alzheimer neuroinflammation review discusses curcumin in animal diets and separately cites three clinical dementia cases using turmeric capsules. The case results concern observed behavior and caregiver distress, with variable cognition and no separate falls. The glucagon formulation study evaluates stability and glucose outcomes after using curcumin. Those two scopes address different preparations and clinical questions. ## Two caffeine citation trails

The 2014 tea review's immediate reference 106 is the 2004 chapter by Smith and colleagues. Its nine-person caffeine crossover citation records less subjective tiredness after caffeine abstinence, which allows withdrawal relief as an alternative. The other cited trial has 24 participants and records sleep latency and vigilance. Separate ratings of fatigue are unverified. The 2014 microglia experiment instead cites the 2002 A. Smith review, which discusses alternative interpretations of regular and decaffeinated coffee studies. Its own animal outcomes concern microglia. ## Green tea leaf and tiredness

The EMA final monograph places oral green tea leaf infusion and powdered leaf under traditional use for fatigue and perceived weakness. It documents a longstanding practice. The agency's supporting assessment groups the Dimpfel comparison under an uncontrolled heading, while the original abstract identifies a randomized placebo-controlled crossover. The enriched drink measured EEG and evoked potentials. The Park supplement trial measured memory and attention, with subgroup results and no separately reported fatigue. The Giesbrecht crossover compared a two-constituent drink with a matched placebo and found lower acute task-tiredness ratings (p<.05). These preparations and participants differ from the diagnosed Parkinson brewed tea comparison.
