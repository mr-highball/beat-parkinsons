# Food experiment evidence for 2025–2029

## Saffron powder and depressive symptoms

In a 12-week triple-blind randomized trial of 92 people with Parkinson’s, the Isfahan investigators compared orally administered saffron powder with placebo. The adjusted difference on the prespecified DASS-21 depression measure favored saffron by 4.58 points, with a 95% confidence interval from 0.81 to 8.34 points in the favorable direction. The protocol specifies first-class Sargol saffron powder from Mojtahedi Company, delivered in a 100 mg study tablet. It planned to check crocin content by HPLC before and after tablet preparation. Sargol describes the dried red-stigma grade, while the trial used a specific supplier and formulation. Depressive symptoms and fatigue were secondary outcomes, and the investigators based the sample-size calculation on C-reactive protein. The tablet and the trial’s multiple symptom endpoints limit how confidently the direction applies to ordinary culinary use. A separate Tehran trial at a different dose reported a favorable Hamilton depression comparison and no clear differences on two secondary depression measures. [Trial report](https://doi.org/10.1039/D5FO01924A) · [Protocol](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/15/10/e093167) · [Tehran trial](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#saffron-capsules-and-depressive-symptoms)

## Saffron powder and fatigue

The same trial, first published online on December 24, 2025, found lower fatigue scores with saffron powder than placebo. The adjusted difference was −9.20 points (95% CI −13.76 to −4.65). The published protocol describes the 16-item Parkinson Fatigue Scale and a tablet containing Sargol saffron powder. The original 2024 registration omitted the standalone fatigue scale, and the first archived revision listing it follows the outcomes manuscript receipt. Sargol is a grade made from the dried red stigma. This is close exposure and matching outcome evidence against frozen claim `0485`, which predicts worsening fatigue after consuming dried saffron. The tablet regimen, one study population, and multiple outcomes limit transfer to culinary use. The trial also reported results for overall sleep quality and cognitive status. Those measures are broader than the insomnia and executive function endpoints in `0218` and `0469`. [Primary report and publication date](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2026/fo/d5fo01924a) · [Protocol and fatigue measure](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12574437/) · [Sargol composition study](https://doi.org/10.15237/gida.GD18018) · [Registry history and peer review](Food-Experiment-Saffron-Registry-History.md)

## Marjoram tea and oxidative stress biomarkers

Chbili and colleagues’ October 4, 2025 report links to PACTR202205801626909. The Results describe 61 people with idiopathic Parkinson’s and 55 completers (30 marjoram infusion, 25 placebo) after 30 days. The abstract also mentions healthy volunteers. The reported group-by-time interactions concern superoxide dismutase, catalase and carbonylated proteins. The registry plans biomarker outcomes and supplies no separate anxiety measure. The publisher lists CEFMS 23/2019 and gives a window of May 4, 2023 to August 31, 2024, described as three months. The official record reports May 9 to June 1, 2022. The 2021 symptom pilot lists a different ethics identifier. The protocols and reports address distinct outcomes, and individual participant overlap is unverified. [Primary report](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0306452225009777) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41052623/) · [Official registration](https://pactr.samrc.ac.za/TrialDisplay.aspx?TrialID=23704)

## Black tea and motor function in Parkinson’s cohorts

A longitudinal analysis of participants from PPMI-Online and Fox Insight associated reported black-tea consumption with a less severe composite motor-experiences score in the smaller LRRK2-related Parkinson’s group. The study did not find a corresponding association in idiopathic Parkinson’s. Brewing details and effects on individual bradykinesia, walking, or rest-tremor endpoints were unresolved. [Lüth et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41531-025-01010-2)

## Bay leaf infusion and stress in healthy adults

Chbili and colleagues’ September 28, 2026 paper reports the second component of a bay-leaf infusion project in healthy adults. Of 62 enrolled, 55 completed ten days, 30 with strained infusion of dried *Laurus nobilis* and 25 with depleted *Origanum syriacum* stem infusion. Both perceived stress and state-trait anxiety scores were lower in the bay-leaf arm at the day 11 assessment (reported p = .001). The registration’s outcome table lists blood counts and inflammatory markers and does not name the mood scales. This second publication shares the sample totals and regimen of the 2024 lipid report. Participant overlap has not been established from individual data. The clinical outcomes are stress and anxiety in healthy adults. [Primary report and Table 3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-73555-y) · [2022 registration and 2024 project report](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#bay-leaf-infusion-registrations-and-lipid-outcomes)

The day 11 visit also asked about tolerability. The paper reports no event that substantially affected the protocol. It gives no adverse-event table by arm or standardized fatigue measure. [Primary report, Assessments and Results](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-73555-y)

The 2024 article dates its randomized study to August 2019 through February 2020 (CEFMS 29/2019). The 2026 article gives May 2022 (CEFMS 17/2019). Their Table 1 values match for women per arm (26 of 30 and 20 of 25), age, weight and the three associated comparison p values to rounding. The 2026 authors call the reports parts of the same project. Count the reports as potentially overlapping while the different dates and approval numbers remain unresolved. Table 3 reports group-by-time p = .0011 for stress and p = .0027 for anxiety, with day 11 comparisons listed as .0001 rather than the abstract’s .001. [2024 study design and Table 1](https://www.najfnr.com/home/article/download/415/277/2738) · [2026 Table 1](https://pubblicazioni.unicam.it/retrieve/95338d97-5710-416d-9925-1de561eb4647/s41598-026-73555-y-2.pdf#page=6) · [2026 Table 3](https://pubblicazioni.unicam.it/retrieve/95338d97-5710-416d-9925-1de561eb4647/s41598-026-73555-y-2.pdf#page=9)

## Bay leaf infusion lipid findings re-reported in 2025

Abdellaoui and colleagues’ September 16, 2025 conference abstract reports a ten-day pre-post study in 30 healthy volunteers in Sousse in 2019. HDL cholesterol increased (p = .01), and LDL cholesterol and triglycerides had no clear change. The abstract additionally reports associations of HDL change with age and weight and lower blood urea (p = .008). Shared investigators, setting, intervention and outcomes suggest overlap with the 2020 lipid report. The abstract describes three dried leaves and the earlier report specifies leaf weight. The reports give no Parkinson symptom outcome. [Primary conference abstract](https://www.em-consulte.com/es/article/1762778/article/effet-de-l-infusion-de-laurus-nobilis-sur-le-chole) · [Earlier reports](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#earlier-sousse-bay-leaf-infusion-reports)

## Habitual green tea and nonmotor symptoms

A 2026 comparative analysis of 105 community-dwelling older people with Parkinson’s examined habitual tea use and nonmotor features. The comparison between tea-use groups showed no clear difference in the prevalence of hyposmia. Binary hyposmia prevalence and habitual exposure do not measure a change in olfactory impairment severity after brewed green tea. The study’s sleep and constipation associations address other outcomes. [Sakakima et al. 2026, Tables 1–2](https://doi.org/10.1177/02601060261493252)

## Cassia oil fragrance and winter mood symptoms

A randomized study tested cassia oil fragrance in people with Parkinson’s and winter-related mood symptoms. The investigators reported sleep and mood signals in that setting. The intervention used fragrance exposure, which differs from consuming ground culinary cinnamon. [Trial abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40840152/)

## Extra-virgin olive oil and constipation outside Parkinson’s

A randomized comparison reported more favorable constipation scores with extra-virgin olive oil than with refined olive oil in people with constipation. The trial did not recruit a Parkinson’s cohort. A separate local trial tested a Mediterranean dietary pattern in Parkinson’s and did not isolate extra-virgin olive oil. These two studies address different populations and intervention comparisons. [Olive-oil trial abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41383802/) · [Parkinson’s dietary-pattern trial](../2020-2024/Food-and-Nutrition.md#2024--mediterranean-diet-added-to-standard-constipation-care)

The official registry covers this same cohort and outcomes. The March 2021 version and its May revision differ in their written dose, and the article reports two tablespoons daily. The article describes a high-polyphenol oil but acknowledges that no polyphenol assay was performed in the study. [Registration and revision history](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Olive-Oil-Constipation-Comparators.md#registered-comparison-of-extra-virgin-and-ordinary-olive-oils)

## Virgin coconut oil in experimental models

A study of a traditional virgin coconut oil preparation reported findings in cultured cells and an animal model, with additional computational analysis. The study did not measure symptom changes in people with Parkinson’s. It supplies no direct human evidence for the frozen coconut oil claims. [Study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42217587/)

## Olive oil within a dietary-pattern cohort

A Lithuanian dietary questionnaire study recorded olive oil use and analyzed relationships between a Mediterranean dietary-pattern score and motor and nonmotor outcomes. The reported comparisons did not isolate an extra-virgin, cold-pressed, or refined oil effect on any of the frozen symptoms. [Study report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13006276/)

## Lemon balm use in a Parkinson’s survey

Pignataro and colleagues’ October 2026 conference abstract reports a cross-sectional survey of 47 Brazilian outpatients with Parkinson’s. Of these, 89.4% reported using at least one unconventional product. The abstract reports lemon balm use at 66.7% and perceived symptom improvement at 23.8%, and lists commercial tea and fresh products as common formulations. Preparation and perceived improvement are pooled across the products. The abstract gives no lemon-balm-specific depression outcome or exposure comparison. [Primary conference abstract eCBN260617](https://www.arquivosdeneuropsiquiatria.org/article/use-of-non-prescribed-unconventional-therapeutic-products-in-parkinsons-disease-a-cross-sectional-study/) · [Earlier brewed lemon balm trial in burn patients](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#brewed-lemon-balm-and-depression-after-burn-injury)
