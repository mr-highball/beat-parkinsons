# Longitudinal coconut-oil association in MVP

An unreviewed preprint by Mischley and Murawska posted October 13, 2026 analyzed the Modifiable Variables in Parkinsonism cohort (2,612 at baseline). Self-reported consistent complementary-therapy use in the preceding six months was modeled against overall patient-reported PRO-PD symptom scores. Coconut oil users had no statistically significant baseline association in the separate model; coconut oil was not retained in the multivariable baseline model; baseline use was associated with slower progression in an adjusted mixed model. The authors caution that multiple comparisons were uncorrected and differences between analytic approaches, co-use and selection can confound interpretation. The preprint does not specify virgin or refined oil or report separate balance, dream-enactment or listener-intelligibility associations. The same MVP cohort generated earlier coconut-oil analyses, so these are not independent clinical trials. The progression models adjust for age, gender, income and time since diagnosis. Concurrent therapies were included only in the separate baseline model, where coconut oil was not retained. Publisher full HTML was verified. The exact coconut-oil coefficient and confidence interval are available only graphically in Figure 6 and were not confirmed through the available image route. The baseline individual-item heatmap is an earlier exploratory analysis of the same cohort.
