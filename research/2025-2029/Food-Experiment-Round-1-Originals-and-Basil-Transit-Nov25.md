## Sweet basil, bowel transit and traditional use

The 2006 experiment reported faster marker progression in rats given an oral water extract made from dried sweet basil leaves compared with saline. The preparation is a filtered extract and the endpoint is acute intestinal transit, with no diagnosed Parkinson participants. A separate small Parkinson study connects delayed colonic transit to one aspect of bowel dysfunction and shows weak agreement with self-reported constipation. These sources support a limited hypothesis about the symptom mechanism. [Sweet basil transit original](../2005-2009/Source-Text/2006-Abbah-Sweet-Basil-Intestinal-Transit.txt) · [Parkinson colonic transit study](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2017-PMID28157109-Knudsen-Parkinson-Colonic-Transit-Abstract.txt)

The 2004 clove basil study had the opposite direction in a related species. A 2026 sweet basil study measured relaxation in isolated ileum, which leaves whole-organism transit unmeasured. Manobo and Dominican reports describe customary bowel uses with preparation and identity limits. Neither cultural account measures bowel response. [Clove basil study](../2000-2004/Source-Text/2004-Owulade-Clove-Basil-Intestinal-Transit.txt) · [Isolated ileum study](../2025-2029/Source-Text/2026-PMID41977738-Sweet-Basil-Ileal-Contraction.txt) · [Manobo account](../2020-2024/Cultural-and-Experiential/Traditional-Practices/2020-PMID32138749-Manobo-Sweet-Basil-Bowel-Practice.txt) · [Dominican account](../2010-2014/Cultural-and-Experiential/Traditional-Practices/2011-Dominican-Albahaca-Bowel-Practice.txt)

The zongzi field survey records different culinary and ceremonial roles for fillings, spices, colorants and ash. It expands the 2019 wrapper study and keeps the cooking method distinct from a symptom observation.
