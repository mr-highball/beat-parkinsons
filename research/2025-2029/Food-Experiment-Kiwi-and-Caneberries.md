# Kiwifruit, blackberry and raspberry studies

## Oregon blackberry powder and cognition in older adults

Park and colleagues’ 2025 Linus Pauling Institute poster reports 29 adults aged at least 55 who underwent NIH Toolbox and virtual Morris water maze testing. The intervention was freeze-dried Oregon blackberry powder in water, with water as control. The abstract reports improved fluid, crystallized and total cognition composites after 12 weeks (p = .04, .02 and .0009), alongside increased depression scores (p = .047). The virtual water maze showed no clear treatment effect on memory or cognitive flexibility. The abstract does not specify the depression contrast, raw estimates, fruit cultivar, or corrections for multiple testing. The investigator’s university profile confirms use of the virtual maze in the blackberry study. The study enrolled older adults outside a Parkinson cohort and tested powder. [Original conference abstract, poster 22](https://lpiconference.org/2025-poster-abstracts/) · [University investigator profile](https://lpi.oregonstate.edu/faculty-staff/kathy-magnusson)

## Red raspberry powder and post meal spatial working memory

Xiao and colleagues first published January 15, 2026. In a randomized crossover, 36 adults aged 55–70 with overweight or obesity consumed a breakfast with or without 25 g of freeze-dried red raspberry powder. The primary abstract reports fewer Paired Associates Learning attempts, fewer Spatial Working Memory errors and better strategy use after the raspberry meal (p < .05), and no clear vascular differences. Exact numerical estimates and the other cognitive and mood outcomes listed in the registry remain open. The 2019 registration and earlier investigator reports are the same study family. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41536265/) · [USDA author record](https://www.ars.usda.gov/research/publications/publication/?seqNo115=423095) · [2019 registration](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Kiwi-and-Caneberries.md#red-raspberry-meal-and-cognition-registration)

The publisher supplement’s recipe table specifies 12.5 g freeze-dried raspberry powder in the active drink and another 12.5 g in the breakfast component. The control recipe uses fructose and dextrose in the drink and differs in some flavorings and ingredient quantities. The supplement contains no cognitive or mood outcome estimates. The investigators’ 2023 report shows a six-hour spatial working memory contrast and gives a group statement about the remaining measured outcomes. [Publisher supplement](https://static.cambridge.org/content/id/urn%3Acambridge.org%3Aid%3Aarticle%3AS0007114525105497/resource/name/S0007114525105497sup001.docx) · [Earlier investigator report](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Kiwi-and-Caneberries-Source-Followup.md#red-raspberry-spatial-working-memory-and-other-measured-outcomes)

## Black raspberry powder and a global dementia rating

Tandoro and colleagues (February 12, 2025) assigned older adults with mild Alzheimer-type dementia, overweight and Helicobacter pylori infection to black raspberry powder or dextrin. Figure 2A reports a Clinical Dementia Rating change from 0.5 to 0 in 11 of 11 powder participants and 0 of 10 controls. This is a global clinical category and the paper did not give separate executive test outcomes. The public registry’s listed primary outcomes are glycemic indices and anthropometry, which differ from the paper’s outcome description. [Primary report, Figure 2A](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41538-024-00356-w) · [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05680532)

## Combined kiwi strawberry and dragon fruit extracts in mice

Fatima and colleagues’ February 2025 article used combined Soxhlet ethanolic extracts of kiwi, strawberry and dragon fruit in a haloperidol mouse model. It reports less catalepsy and better hanging-wire performance without detailed numerical group results. There was no kiwi-only comparison and no direct gait freezing or postural instability measurement. The journal landing page labels the report IJSDR2502170 while the PDF footer labels it IJSDR2502169. [Primary paper, Sections 2 and 3](https://ijsdr.org/papers/IJSDR2502170.pdf) · [Journal record](https://ijsdr.org/viewpaperforall.php?paper=IJSDR2502170)

## Fresh SunGold kiwifruit mood trial and adverse effects

Billows and colleagues’ 2025 GoKiPH crossover enrolled 26 adults with mood disturbance. Two SunGold kiwi daily for four weeks reduced the total mood disturbance score over time relative to usual diet (time-by-treatment p < .001, treatment main effect p = .07). The study reports flatulence and mild bloating in one participant. It did not conduct objective attention testing. [Local primary text](Source-Text/PMC12030757.txt#L143) · [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12030757/)

## Parkinson diet recalls and symptom fields in Lithuania

Guk and colleagues (April 20, 2026) obtained two item-level diet recalls from 59 people with Parkinson’s and 54 controls. They measured MDS-UPDRS III, MoCA, HADS anxiety and depression, sleep outcomes and MDS-UPDRS I pain and orthostatic symptoms. The article reports nutrients and symptom summaries. The supplementary files are tables of nutrients and outcomes and provide no food-level kiwi, blackberry or raspberry contrast. Publicly available data cannot link food preparation to a symptom. [Primary article, Methods and data availability](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/18/8/1302)

## Investigator follow-up to the hardy kiwi mouse study

Suzuki and colleagues’ Japanese Society for Hygiene symposium report was released February 20, 2025. Kitamura’s section gives group sizes of nine controls, nine MPTP mice and eight mice receiving hardy kiwi juice plus MPTP. It describes attenuation of motor impairment in pole, catalepsy and beam tests (reported p < .01), with a tyrosine hydroxylase comparison at p < .05. The text points to Kitamura’s 2021 experiment. It places motor testing 21 days after final MPTP versus 22 days in the earlier publisher preview. The study used juice pressed from previously frozen hardy kiwi and gives no clinical estimate for raw kiwi, freezing or postural instability. [Investigator report, pp. 2–3](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjh/80/0/80_24009/_pdf) · [Dated original study trail](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Kiwi-and-Caneberries-Source-Followup.md#hardy-kiwifruit-original-report-access)

## Kiwi juice and acute anaphylactic shock

Vijayachandra and colleagues reported giddiness, breathlessness and swelling after street-vendor kiwi juice in a 51-year-old man. Initial systolic pressure was 60 mm Hg. Clinicians diagnosed anaphylactic shock followed by acute myocardial infarction. The report gives no orthostatic measurements or defined juice ingredients. [Original source](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/26324636251319844) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2025-Vijayachandra-Kiwi-Juice-Shock-Case-Extract.txt)

## Himalayan raspberry case series and blood pressure

Mani and Gautam describe five hypertensive adults who reported long-term Himalayan raspberry consumption. Table 2 lists mild asymptomatic low readings in three and medication reductions in two. Some case-specific systolic declines exceed the 8–12 mm Hg range in the abstract. Species, freezing, posture and symptom measurements remain unverified. The uncontrolled series provides context for blood pressure searches. [Original case series](https://9vom.in/journals/index.php/htnj/article/download/707/897/3366) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2025-Mani-Gautam-Himalayan-Raspberry-Blood-Pressure-Extract.txt)

## Journal bioamine report from the Kanon parent experiment

November 9, 2026. Kanon and colleagues describe 48 pigs consuming green Hayward or gold Hort16A fruit with soda bread. They report different post-meal trajectories for GABA and hippocampal serotonin and a plasma histidine contrast. Chapter 5 of the 2024 author thesis supplies the matching primary time-course comparison. Cultivars differ in more than actinidin. No food-free arm is described for this analysis, and the study reports biochemical endpoints without attention behavior. The standalone journal tables remain unverified because the publisher body was inaccessible.
