# Fig fruit exposure and symptom outcomes, 2025–2029

## Drooling in a fig and almond syrup ADHD trial

Mohammadi Kenari et al. randomized children aged 6–14 with ADHD to commercial fig-and-almond syrup, a separate sweet-almond syrup, or placebo syrup. All received methylphenidate. The 2022 registry identifies the commercial NIAK product by Iranian FDA registration 8953162594580741 and describes the university comparator as sweet almonds plus raisins. The published trial names the commercial syrup Tinlose. These were separate formulations, and the public sources do not specify the fresh or dried state of the fig ingredients. The planned and reported adverse-event method was a checklist based on CTCAE v4.03 every two weeks. Table 3 reports drooling in 17/40, 15/40 and 5/40 in the fig-containing, almond comparator and placebo groups, respectively. The published p = 0.008 is omnibus. The report gives event counts and no event-specific grading rule or pairwise comparison. These outcomes concern pediatric safety during ADHD treatment. The article identifies an Iran University of Medical Sciences thesis, which remains unverified. ## Sweet almond syrup and parent rated attention

The same 2025 Mohammadi Kenari trial gives baseline-to-week-12 cognitive problems and inattention means of 11.57 to 6.20 for sweet-almond syrup and 8.05 to 8.10 for placebo. All children received methylphenidate. The almond-versus-placebo Tukey p values were .003 at baseline and .011 at follow-up. The Methods mention ANCOVA and Table 2 prints the raw visits with no clearly identified adjusted treatment estimate for this contrast. The preparation contained sweet almonds and raisins. The report gives no cold-pressed almond oil or Parkinson subgroup.
