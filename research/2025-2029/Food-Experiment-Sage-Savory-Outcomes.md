# Sage and summer savory symptom studies

## Dried common sage tea and primary insomnia

Mokogwu and colleagues published April 18, 2026. Thirty adults were assigned to dried common sage leaf tea or chamomile tea for 14 days. In Table 2, mean Insomnia Severity Index scores in the sage group fell from 13.36 to 6.04 by day 14. The chamomile group fell from 16.14 to 6.36. The day-14 group difference was inconclusive (reported p = .78). There was no inert control. Methods specify ages 20–60 and a baseline Insomnia Severity Index eligibility score of 18–21. Both reported arm means fall below the stated score range. The preparation used coarsely ground dried leaves brewed in water, and the participants had primary insomnia. [Publisher PDF, Methods and Table 2](https://currentsci.com/images/articlesFile/1778339879.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2026-04-18-Mokogwu-Sage-Chamomile-Insomnia.txt)

## Common sage extract and recognition memory in mice

Sefah and colleagues published March 20, 2025. After lipopolysaccharide exposure, mice given hydroethanolic sage leaf extract performed better in a novel-object recognition test and, at the two higher doses, reached a closed arm sooner on a retention task. Brain TNF-α differences were inconclusive at the sage doses. In a separate acute screen the authors reported increased vocalization and irritability at the two highest extract doses. These behavior tests address recognition, task retention and acute observations. They do not quantify apathy, hypomimia or Parkinson motor symptoms. [Primary paper, Results and Tables 2–3](https://doi.org/10.1155/tswj/2198542) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC11949616.txt)

## Summer savory essential oil in a cholinesterase assay

Çoban and colleagues published September 15, 2025. The report measured 31.85% acetylcholinesterase inhibition and 40.20% butyrylcholinesterase inhibition for summer savory essential oil at the tested concentrations. It gives separate measurements for summer and winter savory and for extracts and essential oils. The assay gives no human or animal executive-function endpoint. [Primary paper, cholinesterase results](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0332178) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC12435695.txt)

## Registered common sage extract trial in middle-aged adults

NCT07757529 was first posted August 11, 2026. The recruiting placebo-controlled parallel-group trial tests a named sage extract in middle-aged adults. The registration lists a computerized cognitive test battery and physical performance, with no posted results. It has no enrolled Parkinson cohort or named apathy outcome. [Official trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07757529)

## Citation trail and later insomnia results

Table 2 provides day-21 insomnia means of 6.36 and 6.12 (P=.83) and global sleep-quality means of 5.98 and 4.42 (P=.41), sage and chamomile respectively. The report describes the follow-up timing inconsistently. An additional public protocol or adverse-event table remains unverified. The companion citation requires DOI 10.26538/tjnpr/v9i5.14. The Thai registration identifies dried pepper leaves and chamomile, and the original reports different settings, investigators and numeric outcomes from the sage trial. The similar designs do not establish participant overlap. ## Sleep measurements after multicomponent aromatherapy

The published NCT06208800 study reports improved PSQI and wearable-measured sleep after a combined inhalation program in older residential-care adults. Its abstract and Table 2 give different standardized sleep estimates. The article treats clary sage and musk sage as names for one of several oils. The untreated comparison and inhaled mixture leave the contribution of the sage oil unresolved. [Original report summary](Source-Text/2026-PMID42011111.txt) · [Official trial entry](../2020-2024/Source-Text/2024-NCT06208800.txt)

## Registered mixture with protein and a sleep-quality measure

NCT07806838 plans to compare usual care, protein plus placebo and protein plus a six-spice mixture in adults using GLP-1 medications. PSQI is secondary. The formulation does not specify sage species or preparation, and no results were posted at the November 17, 2026 check. [Dated registry summary](Source-Text/2026-NCT07806838.txt)
