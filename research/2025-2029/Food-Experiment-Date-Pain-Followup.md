# Additional date palm and pain research, 2025–2029

## Date remedies and menstrual pain in a university survey

Tahir and colleagues’ May 19, 2025 report includes 202 students. It describes date use among home remedies, and its supplement provides one survey row per participant. Among 155 rows reporting menstrual pain, an exploratory check of the supplement found 24 with the exact dates remedy label. Mean WaLIDD scores were 6.67 for those rows and 6.07 for 131 rows with other or unrecorded remedies. The highest of four recorded pain-intensity codes occurred in 11 of 24 and 48 of 131, respectively. The preparation, dose and sequence of remedy use and pain were not recorded. The comparison is descriptive and may reflect people with more pain choosing a remedy. It supplies no estimate of pain change after dates or Parkinson’s symptoms. ## Dried fruit phenotypes in later Parkinson genetic analyses

Su and colleagues’ January 18, 2025 Supplementary Table S1 includes two UK Biobank dried-fruit genetic phenotypes, `ukb-b-16576` and `ukb-b-15321`. Dried fruit was absent from the reported retained associations. Zhao and colleagues first published May 13, 2026 and reused `ukb-b-16576` with a Parkinson’s susceptibility GWAS. They reported IVW OR 1.70 (95% CI 1.13–2.56) and an inconclusive MR-Egger estimate (OR 1.324, 95% CI 0.282–6.204). The reused exposure data limit independence across analyses. Neither study assessed pain or apathy severity in people with Parkinson’s or isolated dates in the exposure. [Su primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11742879/) · [Su Supplementary Table S1](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41598-025-85668-x/MediaObjects/41598_2025_85668_MOESM1_ESM.xlsx) · [Zhao primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42132746/) · [Zhao supplementary data, Tables S3 and S8](https://www.rsc.org/suppdata/d5/fo/d5fo02160j/d5fo02160j1.pdf)

## Date paste in a Parkinson fiber bar and its pilot

For the accompanying cherry source check, the official NCT07914387 record offers Dark Chocolate Cherry and Lemon Cranberry flavors. The food-form description does not establish dried cherry content or participant-level intake. The registered Part IB total covers a fatigue item through its composite, while the specific planned item comparison concerns constipation. There is no identified cherry-only arm or separately registered fatigue-item result. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07914387)

The Stanford SUNs registration first posted November 3, 2026 and explicitly lists date paste in both flavors of the NeuroFiber bar. The study gives the same multi-ingredient bar to participants with Parkinson’s and to cohabitating controls without Parkinson’s. It records the pain-containing MDS-UPDRS Part IB and the constipation symptoms questionnaire. The listed Part IB comparisons are the total and constipation item. The public record gives no planned separate pain or apathy analysis and had no posted results as of November 12, 2026. [Primary registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07914387) · [Local ingredient list](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07914387.txt#L211) · [Local outcomes](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07914387.txt#L306)

Smith and colleagues’ original May 22, 2026 World Parkinson Congress abstract P26.07 identifies the earlier Sorridi pilot as `NCT07127120`. Eighteen people with Parkinson’s consumed one NeuroFiber bar daily for four weeks without a parallel comparator. The investigators report a calprotectin decrease (p = .021), digestive symptom improvements in 78%, and improved overall motor and nonmotor scores. The abstract gives no pain or apathy subitem estimate. The pilot registration lists gastrointestinal cramping among questionnaire topics and total MDS-UPDRS, and gives no individual symptom result. The sponsor’s outcome charts and summaries give no pain or apathy estimate. The registry and primary abstract identify the product by name and leave the exact pilot excipients unstated. [Primary WPC abstract P26.07, PMID 42175618](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13360760/) · [Pilot registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07127120) · [Local pilot registration](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07127120.txt) · [Sponsor summary](https://www.neurofiberhealth.com/pages/medical-professionals) · [Later AAN abstract citation](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0000000000212978)

Todd and colleagues’ April 14, 2026 preprint separately tested a NeuroFiber whole-food bar matrix with stool microbiota from six Parkinson’s donors. Supplementary Table S2 confirms date paste among its excipients. The experimental comparison changed the full bar matrix and measured fermentation, metabolites and bacterial composition. It provides formulation context for the branded bar and no symptom response data. [Primary preprint, Table 1 and Supplementary Table S2](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.04.13.718214) · [Public author copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/403829641_Fiber_Formulation-Dependent_Modulation_of_Gut_Microbial_Metabolism_in_Parkinsons_Disease)

## Whole date feeding and premenstrual symptoms

Al-Sayyed and colleagues’ January 8, 2025 article describes 36 volunteers in two groups, with 20 receiving date fruit. The abstract reports earlier onset for three symptoms and a correlation between prolactin and onset of abdominal cramps. Its conclusion describes improvement in cramps, but the accessible Results give no between-group estimate for cramp severity. The overlapping authors, sample size, and references suggest the 2018 proceedings and 2020 nutrient paper concern the same feeding project. The published symptom report’s title gives five cycles, and the proceedings describe feeding over four cycles. The earlier reports therefore supply context for the preparation and design. A later narrative review misidentifies the journal and DOI and makes stronger claims about pain intensity than the accessible primary report supports. [2025 primary article](https://www.benthamscience.com/public/article/145562) · [2018 proceedings](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Date-Pain-Followup.md#whole-barhi-dates-and-menstrual-characteristics-conference-report) · [2020 nutrient report](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Date-Pain-Followup.md#barhi-date-feeding-and-nutrient-intake) · [Narrative review, Table 1 and reference list](https://newinera.com/index.php/JournalLaMedihealtico/article/download/2721/2229)

## Date palm distillate and migraine registration

IRCT prospectively registered IRCT20260702070048N1 on July 19, 2026. The recruiting study plans 80 adults with migraine and compares oral date-palm distillate with placebo alongside usual treatment for eight weeks. It specifies visual analogue pain severity and migraine disability. The study has no posted results. Migraine and a distillate differ from the frozen dried-fruit and Parkinson’s pain comparison. [Primary registration](https://irct.ir/trial/91429)

## Date palm sap and nociception

Salisu and colleagues reported reduced acetic acid-induced writhing and longer hot-plate response times after oral date palm sap in mice. Table 2 reports a mean of 3.00 writhes in the highest-dose sap group versus 11.00 with water and 1.50 with piroxicam, with six mice per group. The separate rat assay measured paw edema. The preparation was tree sap and the outcomes were evoked responses in animals. [Primary report, Tables 2–3, DOI 10.4314/dujopas.v11i2d.11](https://www.ajol.info/index.php/dujopas/article/view/300271)

## Purified date seed lectin and nociception

Elmubarak and colleagues administered an isolated date seed lectin by intraperitoneal injection to mice. They reported fewer writhes after a chemical stimulus and longer response latency on a hot plate. The purified protein and injection route differ from consuming dried date fruit. The endpoints concern acute experimental nociception. [Primary paper, Sections 2.9–2.10 and 3.6, DOI 10.1016/j.phyplu.2025.100767](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667031325000405)

## Avocado, pineapple, and date juice in adolescents with menstrual pain

Universitas Bina Sehat PPNI made Atulmuniroh’s bachelor’s thesis available on October 29, 2025. The repository lists October 10 as the issue date. Seventeen adolescents with dysmenorrhea received avocado, pineapple, and date juice each morning for three days starting on the first day of menstruation. Table 4.4 shows 15 with moderate or severe pain on day one and four on day three. Chapter 4 reports only the treated participants’ before-and-after counts and says the control group lacked observation sheets. The results therefore leave the effect of date fruit alone and the contribution of the usual course of menstrual pain unresolved.
