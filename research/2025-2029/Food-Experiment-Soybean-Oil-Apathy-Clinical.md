# Soybean-oil and apathy follow-up

## 2026 — Whole-blood fatty acids and motivational symptoms in Parkinson’s disease

Roberts, Murawska and Mischley reported a retrospective cross-sectional analysis of 235 people with Parkinson’s disease at Seattle Integrative Medicine. Baseline blood samples and PRO-PD ratings were paired from 2019–2024. The preprint was posted October 15, 2026 and has not undergone peer review in the version checked. Blood fatty acids were expressed as a percentage of total whole-blood fatty acids, with the omega-6 to omega-3 ratio expressed without units. Loss of interest and motivation or initiative were modeled as separate PRO-PD symptom items. The study used no dedicated multi-item apathy scale. [Local Methods and outcomes extract](Source-Text/2026-Roberts-Fatty-Acids-Apathy-Extract.txt) · [Primary preprint, Methods](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202610.1439/v1) · [Crossref record and publication date](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.20944/preprints202610.1439.v1)

In models adjusted for age, gender and years since diagnosis, DHA was associated with lower loss-of-interest scores (β −5.37, p < .001, n = 191) and lower motivation difficulty (β −4.97, p = .001, n = 190). The omega-6 to omega-3 ratio was associated with higher loss-of-interest scores (β 1.57, p = .001) and higher motivation difficulty (β 1.43, p = .002). Adjustment for the mean of the self-reported memory and comprehension items attenuated the DHA associations to β −2.47 (p = .031) and β −2.20 (p = .070), respectively. The corresponding ratio estimates attenuated to β .74 (p = .037) and β .65 (p = .083). Arachidonic acid, total omega-6 and linoleic acid did not reach nominal p < .05 on either motivational item in the base model. These symptom-item p values are nominal. Separately, the panel-wide screen used total PRO-PD and had no individual marker with false-discovery-rate q < .05. [Primary preprint, Table 4 and Appendix Table A1](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202610.1439/v1)

The models use a blood measurement and provide no soybean-oil consumption, refinement, dose or comparison group. The authors did not distinguish omega-3 from food and supplements and list medication data and diet quality among unmeasured confounders. The MVP parent study is also named in the 2022 MEDI and MIND report. The papers describe distinct eligibility and ascertainment and do not identify shared participants. ## 2026 — Apathy outcome in the Ketoflex trial and registry

Tağraf and colleagues reported a dedicated Starkstein Apathy Scale outcome for 40 people randomized to personalized plant-rich nutrition plus standard care or standard care. The change favored the diet package in the reported time-by-group analysis. Table 2 and its preceding prose give p = .333 for the post-intervention arm comparison, while separate Results prose describes a significant post-intervention comparison. The study monitored self-prepared meals qualitatively with recalls and menu reviews, with participant-specific fat-quality guidance. The publication gives no soybean-oil assignment, intake dose, source or refinement. The current public registry outcomes module lists change in motor symptoms alone. It has no separate listed apathy outcome or public results. The article lists apathy as secondary. The registry's actual start and completion dates in 2024 also precede the March 2025 ethics approval reported in the paper. The article and registry do not explain these discrepancies. Participant data are available on request to the authors, subject to privacy and ethics restrictions.
