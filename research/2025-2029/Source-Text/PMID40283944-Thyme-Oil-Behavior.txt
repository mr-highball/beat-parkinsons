Injected thyme essential oil and rodent anxiety proxies
Source: https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8247/18/4/509
PMID: 40283944
PMCID: PMC12030012
DOI: 10.3390/ph18040509
Coverage: Full original March 31, 2025, Figures 2 and 5 and behavioral Results

Kourat and colleagues studied injected Thymus ciliatus essential oil in BPA-exposed rats. Oil-treated rats increased open-field center visits and had lower center-entry latency compared with BPA alone. The article interprets the BPA animals spending more time in light as worse anxiety and their spending more time in darkness after oil as improvement. This interpretation and movement differences limit the use of the behavioral proxies. Species, preparation, route, toxicant and endpoints differ from dried thyme and Parkinson anxiety.
