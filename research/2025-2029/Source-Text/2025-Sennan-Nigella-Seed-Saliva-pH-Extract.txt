2025 | Sennan et al. | Salivary pH after chewing Nigella sativa seeds

Source: primary published full report, DOI 10.22271/flora.2025.v13.i4c.1011, 2025 volume 13 issue 4.

The four randomized groups included 60 healthy women. The methods specify carom (Trachyspermum ammi), fennel (Foeniculum vulgare), Nigella sativa and paraffin. One portion of the introduction names Pimpinella anisum where the intervention list describes fennel. The Nigella condition involved chewing and swallowing seeds. The article gives no ground or toasted preparation. Saliva collecting was for pH strip assessment, without volume per time or drooling.

Table 1 gives mean pH for Nigella of 6.533 at baseline, 8.800 immediately, 8.333 at five minutes, 7.133 at 15 minutes and 7.067 at 30 minutes. The paraffin group values were 6.533, 6.667, 6.467, 6.333 and 6.267. Table 2 reports p less than .001 for the Nigella/paraffin comparisons at the four post-exposure timepoints. Saliva acidity is materially different from output and retention in the mouth.
