Title: Acute pain impairs retention of locomotor learning, regardless of the context of retention testing
Authors: Samuel R Jackson, Ryan T Pohlig and Susanne M Morton
Date: 2025 journal report; matching design presented in 2024 poster PSTR408
Identifiers: PMID 40366338; PMCID PMC12416618; DOI 10.1152/jn.00502.2024
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12416618/
Coverage: Original factual extract of Methods and Figure 4.

Thirty healthy adults learned a deliberately asymmetric walking pattern, ten per group. One group received a capsaicin-plus-heat skin exposure during learning, and one received the same exposure during both learning and next-day testing. Control participants received no pain stimulus. The group comparisons were p=.63 at late baseline and p=.59 at late practice. At early retesting, group means were 7.1% for control, 3.4% for pain during learning (p=.02 versus control), and 3.6% for pain during learning and retesting (p=.03 versus control). Neither ordinary stride shortening nor gait freezing was measured.
