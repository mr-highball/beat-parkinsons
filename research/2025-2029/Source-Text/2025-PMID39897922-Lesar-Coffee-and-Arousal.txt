Title: The complexity of caffeine's effects on regular coffee consumers

Twenty healthy habitual coffee drinkers received decaffeinated coffee with added caffeine powder or the same decaf without added caffeine in a randomized double-blind comparison. Both groups consumed the coffee base and believed it was regular coffee. Brew method and a coffee-free arm were absent.

Investigators measured cardiovascular variables, mental subtraction, an auditory reaction-time task and EEG. Some before-to-after outcomes changed in both groups, including shorter average reaction times. The resting eyes-open alpha-power result differed between conditions. The study did not use a fatigue severity or fatigue-rating measure. A performance task and a physiological arousal marker cannot supply an estimate of how the participant's fatigue felt.

Round 1 claim 0121. This study informs the limits of using a caffeine-added beverage as a proxy for brewed coffee. Its exposure, outcome and population do not support either sign of a Parkinson fatigue effect.
