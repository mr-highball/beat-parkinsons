Wild thyme extract and measured anxiety domain
Source: https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3271/14/6/611
PMID: 42930530
PMCID: PMC13707104
DOI: 10.3390/medsci14060611
Registration: NCT06639126
Coverage: Full original September 28, 2026, Table 3, official supplements and registry identity

López-Zambrano and colleagues report 178 adults with gastrointestinal discomfort randomized to a dry aqueous extract of Thymus serpyllum aerial parts or cellulose placebo for eight weeks. The exploratory FDDQL anxiety domain favored extract by +3.21 points (95% CI −4.17 to 10.60, p=.410), on a 0–100 scale with higher scores representing better quality of life. The anxiety-domain contrast was inconclusive. Observed week-eight outcomes were available for 65 of 90 and 66 of 88, with imputation for intention-to-treat estimates. The cited registry lists a 75-person crossover, investigator-only masking and primary microbiome outcome. The paper describes parallel allocation, double blinding and primary gastrointestinal symptom score. Public supplements do not resolve these differences or establish prespecification of the anxiety analysis.
