Mixed omega-3 supplements and cognitive trajectories in ADNI
First published online 2026-04-17. PMID 42000571. PMCID PMC13099475. DOI 10.1016/j.tjpad.2026.100569.
Original source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13099475/
Coverage: Original Methods 2.1–2.3 and primary longitudinal comparisons.
Liao and colleagues matched 273 older adult omega-3 supplement users to 546 non-users in the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative. The pooled self-reported exposure includes fish, flaxseed, and krill oils. The study did not separate a flaxseed oil estimate or specify its processing.
The adjusted annual group-by-time estimates were −0.266 MMSE points (95% CI −0.360 to −0.173), +0.823 ADAS-Cog13 points (0.562 to 1.080), and +0.205 CDR-SB points (0.146 to 0.264), associating supplement use with faster measured decline. The outcome scales are broad measures of cognition and functional impairment. The observational comparison leaves confounding possible and does not establish the effect of cold-pressed flaxseed oil or an executive-function effect in Parkinson disease.
