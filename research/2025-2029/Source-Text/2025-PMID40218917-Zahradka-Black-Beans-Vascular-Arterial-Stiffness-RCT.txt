Title: Distinct Effects of Wheat and Black Bean Consumption on Postprandial Vascular Responses in People with Arterial Stiffness: A Pilot Randomized Cross-Over Study.
PMID: 40218917
PMCID: PMC11990509
DOI: 10.3390/nu17071159
Registry ID: NCT05818358
First published: 2025
Authors: Zahradka P, Perera D, Charney J, Taylor CG
Journal: Nutrients
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40218917/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Single-blind randomized crossover trial (Zahradka et al., 2025, Nutrients, PMC11990509, NCT05818358) in 9 adults aged 50-64 years (3 men, 6 women) with established arterial stiffness comparing 3/4 cup cooked black beans, 3/4 cup whole wheat kernels, and 3/4 cup white rice at breakfast. Unlike white rice (which raised systolic BP by 4% at 2 h), cooked black beans caused no postprandial hypotension or systolic spike (and a small +2.9 mmHg peripheral DBP shift in resting hemodynamics), without improving augmentation index. Limits: tiny non-PD cohort (n=9) measuring resting postprandial hemodynamics without standing orthostatic challenge or lightheadedness ratings.
