Title: Fruit and vegetable consumption and injurious falls among adults aged 50 years or older
PMID: 40095277
PMCID: PMC11913954
DOI: 10.1007/s40520-025-02966-0
First published: 2025-03-17
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11913954/
Coverage: original methods and Table 2. Local factual extract.

The authors analyzed 34,129 adults aged 50 or older in WHO SAGE baseline data from China, Ghana, India, Mexico, Russia and South Africa. Dietary adequacy required both at least two daily fruit servings and at least three daily vegetable servings. The outcome was a reported fall-related injury in the preceding 12 months. Dietary data and the fall outcome were assessed cross-sectionally.

Fully adjusted odds ratio for inadequate combined intake was 1.41 (95% CI 1.05–1.90), p=.023. The female estimate was 1.96 (1.35–2.85) and the male estimate was 0.95 (0.62–1.43), Table 2. Gait speed was not a significant proposed mediator. These observational associations do not separate fruit from vegetables or any strawberry subtype and do not demonstrate a Parkinson-specific dietary effect. Prior injury, current diet and confounders have uncertain temporal ordering.
