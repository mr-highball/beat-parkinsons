Naseri et al., 2026. Factual extract from two reports on a likely shared cohort.
Original article: DOI 10.34172/hpp.44662, https://hpp.tbzmed.ac.ir/Inpress/hpp-44662.pdf
Conference abstract: DOI 10.1016/j.msard.2026.107648, https://www.msard-journal.com/article/S2211-0348%2826%2900683-8/abstract
Trial identity: the article cites IRCT20140617018126N6, registered November 1, 2022. Fifty adults with relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis were randomized, 25 per arm, with completers of 17 and 18. Both reports present the same split. Study treatment lasted three months.
Intervention: twice-daily capsules with 0.75 mL each of Nigella sativa and Linum usitatissimum hydroalcoholic extracts per capsule, compared with placebo. Participants continued routine multiple sclerosis treatment.
Outcomes: the full article reports no clear group difference in the CFAB-MS mood or fatigue domains. The October 2026 conference abstract reports an inconclusive between-arm BDI-II comparison (p > .05) without an arm-level estimate.
Relevance: the contrast estimates the combined supplement in a different neurologic disease. It cannot isolate nigella or establish a result for toasted seed in Parkinson disease.
