The review separates infusions, concentrated extracts, isolated chemical markers and carrier formulations. Its depression discussion revisits the Safari type 2 diabetes trial and Bano phospholipid-extract trial. Its raw calculations are secondary analyses of the same participants. They must not be counted as new treatment arms or independent trials.

For Safari, the authors report a calculated crude difference of −3.87 BDI-II change points, with 95% CI −6.10 to −1.64, using 23 active and 21 placebo completers. This is separately labeled from the investigator's covariate-adjusted model. Sixteen of the 60 randomized participants were missing from the outcome analysis. The caption of Table 4 says eight weeks while the methods say 12, which the review retains as a source discrepancy.

The food-matrix section refers to Scholey's distinct beverage and yoghurt experiments in healthy people. Those use incorporated extract, acute stress measures and different sweetener controls. The acute capsule trial on calming reports an inconclusive primary outcome. None of these sources verifies clinical depression outcomes with brewed leaf tea in Parkinson disease.
