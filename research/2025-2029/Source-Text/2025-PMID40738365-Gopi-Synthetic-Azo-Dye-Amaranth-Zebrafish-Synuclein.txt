Title: Synthetic azo dye amaranth disrupts neuronal plasticity causing alpha-synuclein aggregation and cognitive disability in an in vivo zebrafish model.
PMID: 40738365
DOI: 10.1016/j.fct.2025.115669
First published: 2025
Authors: Gopi S, Prethiba S, Madesh S, Almutairi BO, Choi KC, Arockiaraj J
Journal: Food and chemical toxicology : an international journal published for the British Industrial Biological Research Association
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40738365/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Zebrafish toxicology study (Gopi et al., 2025, Food Chem Toxicol) testing the synthetic petroleum-derived azo food dye amaranth (Acid Red 27 / FD&C Red No. 2 / E123) at 100 mg/L, reporting oxidative stress, GFAP elevation (2.42-fold, p<0.0001), alpha-synuclein accumulation (2.63-fold, p<0.0001), and altered locomotion/cognition. Critical homonym disambiguation: synthetic azo dye Acid Red 27 is chemically unrelated to botanical amaranth grain (Amaranthus spp.) and provides zero evidence about culinary amaranth.
