Title: Date palm pollen extract in an experimental colitis study
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13558286/
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42635914/
PMID: 42635914
PMCID: PMC13558286
DOI: 10.1007/s10787-026-02370-8
Publication date: 2026-08-24
Date basis: NLM electronic publication.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Review depth: Full structured original, Methods and Results, including Figure 2.

The structured original reports five groups of ten rats. It measures body weight, semiquantitative fecal occult blood, leukocytes, colonic tissue findings and inflammatory and oxidative markers. Figure 2 reports less fecal occult blood across the extract groups at days 10 and 14 compared with untreated colitis at the paper's p < .05 threshold. This measures intestinal bleeding. The methods list no clinical pain score, evoked pain assay or equivalent behavioral outcome. The authors list missing phytochemical characterization and absence of an active comparator as limitations.

The exposure is a date palm pollen extract. The results cannot establish pain severity after eating dried date fruit or replicate the earlier swish-and-swallow oral mucositis pain pilot.
