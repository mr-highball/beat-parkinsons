Primary Abstract:
BACKGROUND: There is considerable variation in the anabolic action of ingesting protein-dense foods on the stimulation of postprandial myofibrillar protein synthesis rates (MPS) despite ingesting similar amounts of protein and essential amino acids (EAA). OBJECTIVES: This study aims to determine the effects of consuming high-fat pork (HFP), low-fat pork (LFP), or a carbohydrate control (CHO) on the MPS response. METHODS: In a semicrossover design, 16 physically active adults (25 ± 5 y; 25.0 ± 2.3 kg/m2; 12M, 4F) received primed-constant infusions of L-[ring-13C6]phenylalanine and performed an acute bout of resistance exercise. Following exercise, participants ingested either HFP (266 kcal, 20 g protein, 20.6 g fat), LFP (120 kcal, 20 g protein, 4.4 g fat), or CHO (266 kcal, 0 g protein, 0 g fat, 73.3 g carbohydrate). Repeated blood and muscle samples were collected at rest and throughout 0-5 h postexercise recovery to measure plasma variables and MPS. Linear mixed-effects models with time and condition as fixed factors were used to detect differences. RESULTS: Both HFP and LFP conditions increased MPS above postabsorptive values (P = 0.028 and P < 0.001, respectively), with LFP eliciting a greater postexercise MPS {0.106 ± 0.026 %/h [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.088, 0.118]} than HFP [0.072 ± 0.027 %/h (95% CI: 0.057, 0.087); P = 0.030] and CHO [0.056 ± 0.035 %/h (95% CI: 0.41, 0.71); P < 0.001]. The absolute change in MPS from basal was significantly correlated with peak EAA and leucine concentrations (r = 0.4638, P = 0.017; r = 0.4211, P = 0.032). CONCLUSIONS: LFP stimulated MPS more than HFP or CHO conditions. Our work demonstrated that other nutrients in the food matrix, beyond total ingested protein or EAAs, can impact the regulation of MPS in healthy adults. This trial was registered at clinicaltrials.gov as NCT05876299.

Lean cooked pork produced rapid, robust postprandial rises in plasma essential amino acids (EAAs) and leucine/BCAAs (substantially higher and faster than high-fat pork matrix, p<0.01), demonstrating direct human cooked-pork LNAA bioavailability capable of competing with levodopa LAT1 transport.

[Discussion] This is the first study to characterize the anabolic properties of ingesting ground pork containing higher or lower amounts of lipids in healthy adults. Particularly, the production of a lipid-rich meat matrix is distinct from other protein-dense foods such as chicken eggs [10] and salmon [11]. Although the latter are naturally formed, the meat matrix is created by blending lean and fat trimmings to achieve a specific fat-to-lean ratio. Here, we demonstrate that ingestion of LFP leads to a more rapid and pronounced peak in plasma EAA and leucine concentrations, resulting in a greater stimulation of postprandial myofibrillar protein synthesis rates when compared with ingesting HFP and CHO. Moreover, the postexercise stimulation of myofibrillar protein synthesis rates did not differ between the HFP and CHO conditions, suggesting ingestion of a lipid-rich meal immediately postexercise may blunt the stimulation of muscle protein synthesis, potentially due to the blunted aminoacidemia (e.g., EAA/leucine Cmax) when compared with the ingestion of LFP.
