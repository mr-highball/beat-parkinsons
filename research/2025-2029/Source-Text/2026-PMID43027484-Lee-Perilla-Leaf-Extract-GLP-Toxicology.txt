Primary Abstract:
Perilla frutescens var. frutescens leaf extract (PFE) has previously demonstrated clinical efficacy in relieving knee joint discomfort. To establish its safety profile, good laboratory practice (GLP)-compliant nonclinical evaluations were conducted in accordance with the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development (OECD) test guidelines. In the acute oral toxicity test, the acute oral LD50 cutoff was estimated to be above 2000 mg/kg. In 28-day and 90-day repeated-dose oral toxicity studies of up to 5000 mg/kg/day, no mortalities, toxic signs, or adverse changes in body weight were observed. Minor hematological/biochemical shifts and organ weight increases at 5000 mg/kg/day lacked histopathological correlates and were largely reversible following a 28-day recovery period, establishing the no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) at 5000 mg/kg/day. Furthermore, PFE showed no mutagenic, clastogenic, or aneugenic potential across the bacterial reverse mutation, in vitro chromosomal aberration, or in vivo bone marrow micronucleus assays. These results demonstrate a broad safety margin, supporting the safe application of PFE as a functional food ingredient and nutraceutical agent.

MH710687.1) and psbA-trnH (GenBank accession no. OR567250.1) DNA sequences with 100% identity to the NCBI GenBank database. P. frutescens var. frutescens leaves used in this study was obtained from cultivated plants at a local farm in Mangyeong-eup, Gimje-si, Republic of Korea (acquired in September 2023 and July 2024). This cultivation region consists of agricultural alluvial plains with an elevation below 50 m. The climate features warm, humid summers, with mean annual temperatures ranging from 13.7 °C to 14.6 °C and annual precipitation between 1390 mm and 1980 mm. Dried leaves of P. frutescens var. frutescens were extracted with 70% ethanol at a 1:10 (w/v) ratio for 48 h. The extract was filtered through a 1 μm filter, concentrated under reduced pressure at 65–70 °C to 10–15° Brix, and freeze-dried at −40 °C followed by vacuum drying to obtain the standardized powder (yield: 7.0%). To properly characterize the phytochemical profile and ensure the reproducibility of the study, the chemical composition of PFE was quantitatively analyzed. The analysis confirmed that the extract is rich in major phenolic and flavonoid compounds. Results] Sequential administration of PFE at a limit dose of 2000 mg/kg body weight (n = 3 per step, total n = 6) resulted in no mortality, moribund state, or treatment-related systemic toxicity signs throughout the 14-day observation period. Although a slight, transient decrease in body weight was noted on Day 4 in two animals from the second group, normal weight gain resumed by Day 8, indicating no toxicological significance. At terminal sacrifice on day 15, gross pathological examination revealed no macroscopic lesions or abnormal changes in any organ. On the basis of these findings (zero mortality, absence of clinical toxicity, normal weight gain, and no gross pathology), PFE was classified as Category 5 or unclassified according to the GHS criteria. Daily oral administration of PFE at doses up to 5000 mg/kg/day for 28 consecutive days resulted in no mortality or moribund state in male or female Sprague–Dawley rats. Treatment-related clinical observations revealed dark-brown stool (compound-colored stool) in all the animals across all the PFE-treated groups (800, 2000, and 5000 mg/kg/day) from day 2 through day 29, which showed dose-dependent intensity. Slight watery salivation was observed in high-dose (5000 mg/kg/day) animals (3/5 males from Days 15–28 and 5/5 females from Days 6–28). These clinical observations were considered nonadverse physiological responses related to test substance excretion and sensory properties (taste/smell) rather than systemic organ toxicity. Compared with those of the vehicle control group, the mean daily body weights and overall body weight gain of the male rats decreased in a dose-dependent manner. [4. Discussion] Perilla frutescens var. frutescens is a traditional culinary and medicinal plant with established antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties [2,3,22]. In particular, P. frutescens leaf extract (PFE) has recently demonstrated significant clinical promise in improving joint function and alleviating knee discomfort in clinical settings [3]. However, despite its extensive history of dietary use and demonstrated therapeutic efficacy, the systematic nonclinical safety profile of PFE has not been previously investigated in full compliance with rigorous international regulatory guidelines. Establishing a comprehensive toxicological baseline is essential for defining a definitive safety margin for human application and supporting the commercial development of PFE as a functional dietary supplement or therapeutic agent.
