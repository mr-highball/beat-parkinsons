Title: Patient-reported turmeric and pain in Ben Tekaya et al. axial spondyloarthritis survey
Publication year: 2025.
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12457634/#Sec4277
Parent DOI: 10.1007/s40520-025-03119-z
Member: P1266, World Congress on Osteoporosis, Osteoarthritis and Musculoskeletal Diseases.
Parallel French report: https://www.litar.org.tn/public/uploads/files/posters_event/Litar-poste-1-merged-1-compressed-67af596516966.pdf
Coverage: Original published English poster and the French poster 12 on PDF page 14. Same study family.

The investigators surveyed 39 people with axial spondyloarthritis at the Farhat Hached rheumatology service in Tunisia. Mean age was 45 years and 88.5% received biologic therapy. Both reports describe respondents perceiving less pain more frequently with turmeric, vegetables, fruit and fatty fish. The same reports describe perceived worsening more often with dairy, red meat and sugary foods. The French and English versions match on investigators, cohort size, age, biologic use, disease activity summaries and diet-group comparisons. The French methods call the study multicenter while the English description specifies the rheumatology department. Recruiting sites beyond the documented service remain unclear.

Preparation, turmeric intake, ingredient-specific respondent counts, a turmeric-specific pain scale estimate and a comparator are unreported. The 50% figure describes diets overall and cannot be assigned to turmeric. The named BASDAI, BASFI and ASDAS comparisons contrast people following a diet with those who did not. None measures turmeric independently or identifies clinical Parkinson pain. Preserve the patient-perceived signal in its observed population and count the reports once.
