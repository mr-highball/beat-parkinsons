Coverage: Factual extract from the full PDF, Methods, Tables 11–13 and Discussion.

The methods formulate phytosomes from hydroalcoholic fig bark extract. Wistar rats were assigned to distilled water, F1 or F2, at six per group. Both formulations were administered orally at 100 mg/kg daily for 14 days. The stated experimental assignment does not describe a haloperidol challenge or a fruit-extract arm. Tables 11–13 give biochemical measurements for the two formulations, including catalase 34.3 and 15.8 U/mg protein for F1 and F2 respectively. Sample units and denominator for each reported biochemical value cannot be recovered from the tabulation.

The discussion separately recounts ethanolic fig fruit extract, haloperidol and catalepsy at two doses. This does not establish a fruit arm in the stated design. Neither the methods nor the results supply a resting-tremor comparison. Keep the bark formulation results, the fruit-extract discussion and the effect of eating raw fig separate.

November 27 original and citation check

The official publisher PDF was authenticated as a 16-page original. The article's discussion of fruit extract and catalepsy follows reference 29, a general antioxidant review. Reference 30 describes a separate Elaeocarpus animal study. These reference identities and the stated bark-only group assignments leave the fruit-extract paragraph's attribution unresolved. The separately archived Gafoor paper tests dried fig-fruit extract and reports animal motor tasks. The relationship between that paper and this paragraph remains unverified.
