Title: Almonds and Their Impact on Mental Health and the Gut-Brain Axis in Adults with Symptoms of Anxiety and Depression: A 12-Week Randomized Controlled Trial.
PMID: 42463057
DOI: 10.1016/j.tjnut.2026.101732
Registry ID: NCT06087471
First published: 2026
Authors: Sartori, Argomaniz, Farsi, Bajka, Chrysostomou
Journal: The Journal of nutrition
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42463057/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

12-week double-blind parallel RCT (NCT06087471) in 84 adults (mean age 25 y, 75% female) with mild-to-moderate anxiety and depression randomized to whole almonds (56 g/d) or isocaloric control snack. Primary outcome PHQ-ADS at week 12 showed no between-group difference (almond 11.6 +/- 5.3 vs control 12.5 +/- 5.3, P = 0.945). Functional impairment (WSAS) was borderline improved in ITT (9.6 +/- 7.0 vs 12.2 +/- 8.7, P = 0.055) and significant per-protocol (9.0 +/- 6.4 vs 12.5 +/- 9.6, P = 0.015). Whole almonds significantly reduced frequency of hard stools vs control (median [IQR]: 0.0 [0.1] vs 0.1 [0.4] stools/d, P = 0.002) with no impact on gut symptoms, gut microbiota composition, or fecal SCFAs. Limits: young non-PD cohort, abstract does not specify raw vs roasted state.
