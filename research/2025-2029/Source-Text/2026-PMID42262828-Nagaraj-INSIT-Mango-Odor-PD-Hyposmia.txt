Primary Abstract:
BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Olfactory dysfunction is a common nonmotor symptom and prodromal marker of Parkinson's disease (PD). Despite its early occurrence, hyposmia is frequently underrecognized, emphasizing the need for objective, culturally appropriate tools. The Indian smell identification test (INSIT) is widely used but relies on binary scoring, which may underestimate residual olfactory function. This study determines the prevalence of olfactory dysfunction in Indian persons with PD (PwPD) using INSIT and evaluates a modified version (mINSIT) with graded scoring as a preliminary proof-of-concept. METHODS: Two independent cohorts were recruited. Phase 1 included 129 PwPD and 129 age- and sex-matched healthy controls (HCs) assessed with INSIT. Phase 2 included 108 PwPD and 50 HCs tested with INSIT and mINSIT. INSIT used standard binary scoring, while mINSIT captured gradations: nonperception, perception without recognition, cued recognition, and spontaneous recognition. RESULTS: INSIT identified hyposmia in 62% of PwPD versus 32.5% of HCs in phase 1, and 59.3% versus 30% in phase 2 ( P < 0.001). mINSIT revealed that many PwPD who failed odor identification still perceived odors, demonstrating perception-identification dissociation in 19-86% of cases. Graded scoring provided a more nuanced assessment of olfactory function, with a broader distribution of scores compared with binary scoring. The odors most discriminative between groups were mango, cardamom, and lemon. CONCLUSIONS: INSIT is limited by its binary outcome design, whereas mINSIT provides a potentially useful, culturally relevant preliminary tool that captures gradations of olfactory function and may improve detection. It addresses limitations of conventional binary scoring; however, formal validation in larger cohorts is required before it can be recommended for clinical use.

[Methods] Subjects included in this study were prospectively recruited between July 2022 and December 2024, in a young and late onset PD (YOPD and LOPD, respectively) study funded by the Scientific Knowledge for Ageing and Neurological ailments (SKAN) research trust and conducted at the Department of Neurology, National Institute of Mental Health and Neuro Sciences, Bengaluru, India. Recruitment was carried out following ethical approval (NIMHANS/34th IEC (BS&NS DIV)/2022). Consecutive persons with PD (PwPD) who consented to participate in the study were recruited from the neurology outpatient and inpatient departments and movement disorder clinics. The diagnosis of PD was based on the Movement Disorders Society clinical diagnostic criteria for PD[16] and confirmed by trained neurologists and movement disorder specialists (PKP, RY, NK, RRM, PM, and VVH). Two independent cohorts were recruited. The first consisted of 129 PwPD and 129 age- and sex-matched HCs, all of whom underwent testing with INSIT. Analysis of this cohort revealed both high prevalence of hyposmia and limitations of binary scoring, motivating the design of the second cohort. This second group included 108 PwPD and 50 HCs, who were tested using both INSIT and modified INSIT (mINSIT; with graded scoring). A formal pre-specified sample size calculation was not performed for this exploratory study; the cohort size was determined by the availability of consecutive patients recruited within the study period. The second cohort was a pilot evaluation of mINSIT and was similarly not powered by a formal sample size calculation. We acknowledge these as limitations of the current study. [Discussion] The present study investigated olfactory dysfunction in Indian PwPD using INSIT and introduced mINSIT, which incorporates graded scoring. Our findings reaffirm that hyposmia is a highly prevalent NMS of PD, with approximately two-thirds of patients meeting INSIT criteria for olfactory impairment. Beyond replicating global observations in a relatively large Indian cohort, this study addressed a fundamental limitation of conventional smell identification tests: their reliance on binary scoring. The pilot implementation of mINSIT demonstrated that graded scoring uncovers a broader spectrum of olfactory deficits, offering potentially greater clinical and research utility; however, given the modest size of the second cohort and the absence of formal psychometric data, mINSIT should currently be regarded as a preliminary, proof-of-concept tool requiring validation. Olfactory dysfunction is well established as a pre-motor symptom in PD.
