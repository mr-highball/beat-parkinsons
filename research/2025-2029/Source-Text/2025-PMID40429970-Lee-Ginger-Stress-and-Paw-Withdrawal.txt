The six-arm rat experiment compares chronic mild stress and sham or ovariectomy conditions. Two separate arms received ginger water extract or Cornus officinalis water extract. The comparison for ginger used the ovariectomized stressed group without extract. Methods lists 50 ovariectomized and 20 sham procedures, then six groups of 10. That passage leaves ten animals outside the listed groups. The text describes treatment before and during the stress period and gives conflicting wording about when concurrent treatment started.

Thermal withdrawal and behavioral findings

Figure 5B shows a week-seven heat-evoked paw withdrawal latency of approximately 9.7 seconds in the ginger-extract group and 11.5 seconds in the stressed ovariectomized control, read from the plot. Sham rats without stress were near 7.6 seconds. The ginger and stressed control points carry different post hoc letters. Under the authors' stated interpretation of the assay, shorter latency reflects greater thermal sensitivity or a faster withdrawal response. It also moves toward the unstressed sham value. This is an evoked reflex measure in a non-Parkinson stress model. Clinical pain severity and eating grated ginger were outside the experiment.

Figure 5A shows increased sucrose consumption relative to the stressed ovariectomized control. Its axis is in mL, while the Methods describes a sucrose preference percentage. Figure 5C shows less forced-swim immobility with ginger. These are stress-related behaviors. The experiment reports no dedicated anxiety scale, plus-maze outcome or Parkinson diagnosis.
