Title: Avocado (Persea americana Mill.) consumption during pregnancy and lactation induces anxiogenic-like behavior, cerebral oxidative stress and compromises fecal microbiota in rat offspring.
PMID: 40024360
DOI: 10.1016/j.brainres.2025.149544
First published: 2025
Authors: Frazão Tavares de Melo MF, Moura RL, da Silva EB, Pereira DE, Alves MDC, Gomes Dutra LM, Guerra GCB, Araújo DFS, Estevez Pintado MM, Correia Sales GF, Bruno de Oliveira CJ, Barbosa Soares JK
Journal: Brain research
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40024360/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Preclinical developmental study in Wistar rat dams and male offspring fed avocado oil (AO) or avocado pulp (AP) during gestation and lactation. Both AO and AP reduced EPM open-arm time in dams and offspring (p < 0.001); adolescent and adult AO offspring spent less time in the open-field center (p < 0.05), and adult AO offspring ambulated less (p < 0.001) alongside increased maternal MDA, altered brain/milk PUFAs, and altered fecal microbiota. Maternal/developmental rodent model; neither anxiety nor walking slowness is among the 22 Round 2 avocado-oil claims, while it provides cautionary preclinical context for neural/microbiota effects of chronic high-dose avocado oil.
