Reviewed: 2026-11-19

Tağraf and colleagues assigned 40 participants with Parkinson disease to an individualized plant-rich, time-restricted diet and counseling or control. Assessors were blinded. Participants and the nutritionist knew assignments, and the paper reports no allocation concealment. Meals were self-prepared. Adherence checks included dietary recalls, menu reviews and sometimes meal photos or diaries. The protocol describes dietary principles without a banana-specific assignment, intake summary or reported exposure–outcome analysis.

In Table 2 the intervention group's mean Starkstein Apathy Scale score changed from 13.65 to 9.0 and control changed from 13.05 to 13.6. The descriptive difference in changes is −5.20 points, favoring the intervention. The article reports a time-by-group F(1,38) of 99.30, p<.001. The tabulated post-treatment comparison is p=.333. A later Results sentence describes both a within-arm and post-treatment benefit beside one t(38) of 12.60. That sentence does not reconcile the reported post-treatment comparison. Keep the interaction and endpoint comparison separate and seek a clarified contrast or underlying data.

ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06892418 lists motor UPDRS as the only structured endpoint and has no posted summary results. Its actual completion is November 30, 2024, before the March 24, 2025 first public posting. The article lists an ethics approval dated March 6, 2025. The timeline discrepancy needs clarification. The registry does not establish prospective specification of the separately reported apathy endpoint. The result concerns a whole intervention, with uncertain contribution from individual foods.
