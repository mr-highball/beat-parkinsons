Title: Dopaminergic therapy selectively amplifies hallucination susceptibility in patients with Parkinson's disease with cortico-striatal hyperconnectivity
Source: https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.01.26361921v1.full
DOI: 10.64898/2026.09.01.26361921
Publication date: 2026-09-03
Source depth: complete original medRxiv JATS body, version 1 preprint; locally distilled

Of 22 recruited people with Parkinson disease, 20 completed the analyzed medication on/off crossover. Eleven reported spontaneous hallucinations in daily life and nine did not. The behavioral experiment provoked sensed presence with eyes closed using a somatomotor task. The reported interaction among medication state, delay and prior hallucination phenotype had p = 0.044. Robot-induced sensed presence was more frequent on medication within the 11-person prior-hallucination group (p = 0.008), with an inconclusive comparison in the nine-person group (p = 0.918). The clinical interview separately identified two people reporting complex visual hallucinations at baseline. The authors acknowledge that an overnight medication break may leave residual drug effects.

The July 2026 preprint used 53 participants and a different clinical grouping with a common experimental paradigm. Its ethics protocol is 2019-02275. The September crossover lists Bern 2021-01608 and Geneva 2017-01852 with different eligibility and smaller analysis groups. Participant overlap remains unverified. The comparison concerns a prescribed medication state and experimentally induced sensed presence. It does not measure an ingestion-linked change in spontaneous visual episode frequency.
