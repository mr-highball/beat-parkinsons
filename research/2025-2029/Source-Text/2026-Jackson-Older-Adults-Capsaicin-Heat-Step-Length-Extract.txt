Title: Acute pain impairs locomotor learning and retention in older adults
Authors: Samuel R Jackson and colleagues
Date: 2026-07-11 online
Identifiers: PMID 42435877; PMCID PMC13426079; DOI 10.1016/j.jpain.2026.106384; NCT05471557
Source: https://udspace.udel.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e094e195-f2a1-4c7a-a196-ad5db04c4fa3/content
Coverage: Original factual extract of Methods and step-length Results.

Thirty younger and thirty older healthy adults learned a visual-feedback-guided step-length asymmetry. The stimulus combined topical capsaicin with local heat, compared with a no-stimulus control. Across age strata, learning magnitude was 6.6% with pain versus 8.1% for controls (p=.02). Next-day retention was 2.6% versus 5.9% (p<.01). The article reports no pain-by-age interaction for retention. People with neurological conditions were excluded. The outcome was learned left–right step-length asymmetry, not a culinary paprika exposure or clinical stride-length endpoint.
