Title: Acute non-alcoholic caffeinated beverage consumption as a trigger for cryptogenic ischemic stroke in the young: findings from the SECRETO study
PMID: 42274784
PMCID: PMC13260097
DOI: 10.1007/s00415-026-13882-2
Publication date: 2026-06-11
Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00415-026-13882-2
Registered study: NCT01934725
Accessed: 2026-11-19
Text coverage: Curated original methods, main results and limitations

Ferdinand and colleagues analyzed 598 adults aged 18–49 with a first cryptogenic ischemic stroke, enrolled from 19 European centers. A structured questionnaire collected usual intake and the timing of the last drink. The case-crossover analysis compares reported recent exposures within a participant.

The reported Mantel–Haenszel estimates for coffee were relative risk 1.73 (95% CI 1.06–2.83) in the one-hour period and 2.15 (1.40–3.29) in the two-hour period. Tea was pooled in the reported beverage comparisons. For two hours the estimates were 4.89 (1.92–12.50) for tea and 3.70 (1.18–11.62) for cola. Alternative regression gave wider intervals. The authors identify recall and generalizability limitations.

The 2025 conference member ESOC25-2386 reports the same study and sample. This analysis concerns stroke onset after self-reported drinks. It does not estimate a Parkinson fatigue result or a coffee preparation-specific treatment effect.
