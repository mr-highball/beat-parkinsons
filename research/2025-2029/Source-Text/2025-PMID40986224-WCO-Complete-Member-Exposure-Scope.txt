Coverage: 1,738 complete original third-level presentation headings accessed in the official HTML, with section anchors. The publisher correction, PMID 41417439, DOI 10.1007/s40520-025-03275-2, was reviewed separately. It supplies the originally omitted abstract 245 about music and osteoarthritis pain without a food comparison.

P628 (Sec2429) and P629 (Sec2432) use the same investigators, institution and November 2024 to January 2025 recruitment window for two spondyloarthritis diet analyses. Their samples are 43 and 40. Treat these as overlapping unless participant identity is resolved. P628 reports dietary-score correlations with inflammation and disease activity. P629 reports a higher olive-oil intake score in people with normal versus reduced bone density (1.71 versus 1.10, p=.043). These studies give no oil grade, clinical Parkinson effect, or isolated causal intervention.

P1266 (Sec4277) reports an independent 39-person survey of diet beliefs and practices in axial spondyloarthritis. Some people attributed less pain to turmeric. Olive oil was a questionnaire item. Neither turmeric preparation nor a turmeric-specific count, numerical effect size or controlled comparison is published. The French and English reports are the same family. See the separate dated member note for the attribution audit.

NSS2 (Sec445) discusses topical cayenne in a low-back-pain guideline review, with disagreement among guidelines. P267 (Sec1385) assigns isolated caffeine in a rat cartilage experiment. P1005 and P1063 report beverage beliefs and grouped beverage consumption. NSS93 includes eggs grouped with meat, poultry and fish. P1300 (Sec4368) measures beliefs about broccoli and caffeine for bone health without a food intervention or falls measure. A cherry-red spot is retinal color and P1480 studies Cornus mas, not Prunus cherry. Salmon calcitonin is a pharmaceutical exposure. Egg preservation in P626 concerns fertility. None supplies a direct culinary comparison for the frozen Parkinson outcomes.
