Primary Abstract:
Turnip (Brassica rapa L.) is widely cultivated for food and has documented ethnomedicinal uses. This scoping review mapped the international literature on turnip, with detailed synthesis focused on turnip materials associated with selected regional food and ethnomedicinal traditions in China. Searches covered seven bibliographic databases. The bibliographic map comprised 771 records, of which 397 were selected for detailed synthesis. Regional uses varied in plant part and preparation method. The clearest evidence concerned composition and processing: constituent profiles differed by provenance, plant part, and analytical method, whereas changes in composition and product quality depended on the product and process. Animal and cell studies reported effects of specific extracts, fractions, and multicomponent formulations on hypoxia tolerance, endurance, tissue injury, inflammation, redox regulation, metabolism, and the gut microbiota, but mechanistic findings did not converge across preparations and models. The few human studies addressed mainly hypoxia or exercise and supportive care during chemotherapy; differences in design, preparations, and outcomes precluded reproducible conclusions about any defined turnip food or ethnomedicinal preparation. Overall, this review clarifies the current evidence on turnip as a food resource, documents its ethnomedicinal contexts, and identifies priorities for future research and product development.

[2. Materials and Methods] This scoping review incorporated an evidence-mapping component. The design drew on guidance from the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) [23]. Reporting followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) and the PRISMA extension for literature searches (PRISMA-S) [24,25]. The protocol was documented during review conduct and deposited in a private Open Science Framework (OSF) project on 5 July 2026, before final eligibility verification and synthesis. Targeted supplementary checks followed in August 2026. Eligibility criteria were structured using the Population–Concept–Context framework. The Population included Brassica rapa materials identifiable as turnip and preparations derived from them, including the fleshy root and other organs explicitly obtained from turnip plants. The Concept encompassed traditional uses, composition and nutrition, extraction, food processing and food products, biological activity, and health-related research. The Context covered food, nutrition, functional-food research, and ethnomedicinal uses of edible plants. Other B. rapa crop groups and records using only a broad B. rapa designation were excluded unless turnip-specific results could be identified separately. When regional terminology, plant part, local use, and provenance collectively indicated turnip but botanical verification remained incomplete, the source terminology was retained and the material was classified as having uncertain identity. All records meeting the species and topic criteria were included in the global bibliographic map. [3. Results and Discussion] After initial relevance screening within each source, records were merged and obvious duplicates removed, leaving 5049 records for title and abstract screening. Screening excluded 4089 records that did not meet the species or topic criteria, leaving 960. Reconciliation of 42 cross-language duplicate groups removed a further 56 records, leaving 904 for eligibility and target-material verification; 152 were not retained. Supplementary checks assessed 20 additional records and retained 19, yielding a final bibliographic map of 771 records (Figure 1). All 771 records were included in bibliographic mapping of terminology, publication patterns, and the geographic distribution of the literature. Among them, 397 records underwent detailed data charting within the regional-material scope described in Section 2.1 and supported the syntheses of composition, processing, biological activity, and human evidence. The other 374 records were used for contextual mapping; those with verifiable species identity, plant part, preparation, and regional practice also informed the regional-use synthesis (Table S1; Figure 1). The final bibliographic map comprised 647 non-review journal articles, 36 reviews, 61 theses or dissertations, 18 conference papers or abstracts, and 9 other sources or notices (Figure 2; Table S1). Records published from 2019 onward totaled 415, representing 53.8% of the full record set; 248 were included in the detailed synthesis and accounted for 62.5% of the 397 detailed records. Publications from 2019 onward therefore accounted for more than half of both the full record set and the detailed-synthesis set. [4. Conclusions] The international literature documents turnip as both a food and an ethnomedicinal resource. For the regional materials examined in detail, the clearest findings concern material-specific composition and process-dependent changes in food quality. Fermentation, pickling, heating, and drying alter constituent profiles and sensory or storage properties. Extracts, fractions, and multicomponent formulations have shown biological activity in experimental models, but these findings do not establish health benefits from consuming turnip foods. Human studies have not yet established a reproducible benefit for a defined turnip food or preparation. Linking documented uses with characterized raw materials and finished products offers a practical basis for further processing and health research. This review supports the development and evaluation of turnip foods while distinguishing established compositional findings from health effects that require further testing.
