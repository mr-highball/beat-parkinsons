Primary Abstract:
Mental health issues are prevalent among athletes as well as non-athletes due to various psycho-physiological and environmental stresses. Finger millet being rich in antioxidants, polyphenols and high fibre, can be potentially effective in maintaining and improving mental health and cognition. Hence, the present study aims to evaluate the effects of the finger millet diet on mental health and meta-cognitive skills in athletes and non-athletes. 57 participants (males; 27 athletes and 30 non-athletes; 18 to 25 years) were randomly divided into experimental (EGA: experimental group athletes; n = 15; EGNA: experimental group non-athletes; n = 15) and control (CGA: control group athletes; n = 12; CGNA: control group non-athletes; n = 15) groups. The experimental group was provided with a finger millet diet (replacing 1/3rd of daily regular cereals) and a control group with a regular (rice/wheat) diet for 60 days. Mental health and meta-cognitive skills were measured via validated questionnaires. The SMHAT scores showed a significant decrease in sleep disturbance (p = 0.008) and a declining trend in depression (p = 0.059) and anxiety (p = 0.087) in EGA and no significant difference in CGA. The MHI scores showed significantly increased anxiety (p = 0.050), depression (p = 0.010) and psychological distress (p = 0.012) in CGNA while EGNA showed no significant difference in pre and post-test. MCSS subscales viz., implementation (p = 0.040), monitoring (p = 0.013) and total meta-cognitive skills scores showed significant improvement in EGA (p = 0.050) and no significant changes in CGA, EGNA and CGNA. Finger millet helps in improving mental health, sleep quality and meta-cognitive skills in athletes and help in the maintenance of mental health in non-athletes.

Verified Round 5 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
60-day randomized controlled feeding trial (CTRI/2023/10/059332; PMC12783109; same Manav Rachna research group as Kalpana 2025, separate CTRI cohort) in 60 randomized / 57 analyzed young males aged 18-25 y: athletes (EGA n=15 vs CGA n=12) and non-athletes (EGNA n=15 vs CGNA n=15). Experimental groups replaced 1/3 daily cereals (100 g/d) with cooked culinary finger millet (Eleusine coracana) foods (roti, paratha, chilla, porridge, upma, malt, snacks) vs rice/wheat. Critical full-text audit: Authors ran ONLY within-group Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (ZERO between-group comparisons!). Results: (1) MCSS: EGA improved within-group on Implementation (p=0.040), Monitoring (p=0.013), and Total MCSS (166.3->173.5, p=0.050; CGA 163.8->164.9, p=0.272), while Planning (p=0.313), Evaluation (p=0.266), and ALL non-athlete MCSS scores (EGNA p=0.140-0.469) were NULL; (2) Sleep: EGA SMHAT-1 sleep disturbance fell within-group (6.93->5.00, p=0.008; CGA 5.83->6.00, p=0.857), BUT non-athlete PSQI was NULL (EGNA 4.33->4.67, p=0.605) and ESS daytime sleepiness was NULL (EGNA 9.13->9.07, p=0.919); (3) Anxiety/Depression: Athlete EGA anxiety (p=0.087) and depression (p=0.059) were NS (and CGA dropped by a comparable magnitude), while non-athlete EGNA was NULL (anxiety p=0.148; depression 10.4->11.2 p=0.213) and CGNA worsened during university exams.

[Research methodology] For this randomised control trial 100 participants were screened at Manav Rachna campus and based on exclusion and inclusion criteria 60 participants were finalised (Fig. 1). The purpose of the study, complete study procedure and guidelines were explained to all the participants. Informed consent was obtained from them and confidentiality of data was ensured throughout the study. 30 athletes and 30 non-athletes were selected who were non-smokers, did not consume drugs and alcohol and millets, absence of health disorders or chronic illnesses and injury and were not on any kind of medication or supplementation. All the selected participants were males and aged between 18 and 25 years. Athletes selected were P3 level (training > 5 h/week; training frequency > 3 sessions/week)29 team sports players who engaged in regular training for at least 2 years. Non-athletes were selected to have a comprehensive overview and study the comparative effect of finger-millet supplementation in athletes. Based on G power analysis minimum sample size recommended was 47 with an effect size of 0.5, alpha at 0.05 and power at 0.9. Based on 20% dropout rate i.e. 10 samples were added to recommended sample size making the finalised sample size to 57 but 60 were still recruited to have a minimum of 30 participants in the experimental and control groups. [Discussion] This study explored the impact of 60 days of finger millet supplementation (replacing 1/3 of regular cereals) on mental health and meta-cognitive skills in athletes and non-athletes. The results of this study revealed distinct benefits of finger millet consumption. The high physical and psychological demands placed on athletes necessitate optimal nutrition to support both performance and mental health. Similarly, non-athletes of same age group face sudden life changes as they also transient from adolescence to adulthood with added responsibilities of academics, personal life and career development that affects their mental health. Inclusion of complex carbohydrates, unsaturated fats, proteins, fibre, B vitamins, magnesium, calcium, phytonutrients, polyphenols and melatonin help in improving sleep duration and quality36. Whereas, unhealthy dietary patterns, processed foods, saturated and trans fats reduce the gut microbiota and increases inflammation that leads to poor sleep quality, neurodegenerative diseases and decline in mental and cognitive health36.
