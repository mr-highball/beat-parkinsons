Inspected extent: Original methods, Table 3, and the narrative reporting the separate Health Professionals Follow-up Study analysis.

Bondonno and colleagues followed 62,743 women in the Nurses' Health Study and 23,687 men in the Health Professionals Follow-up Study. The exposure was estimated using food-frequency questionnaires. The article's narrative describes the relevant orange exposure jointly as oranges and orange juice. Table 3 abbreviates the row as Orange. In the women's cohort, the fully adjusted hazard ratios for the highest intake category (median 3 servings per week) versus the lowest (median zero) were 0.92 (95% CI 0.87–0.96) for incident composite frailty and 0.90 (95% CI 0.86–0.93) for impaired physical function. Across reported foods the authors did not see frailty or physical impairment associations in the male cohort.

The reported endpoints are frailty and physical function. The study does not report a fall count. The study provides a pooled orange exposure comparison in older adults. Exact raw fruit exposure, preparation and Parkinson fall-frequency outcomes remain open for claim 0259. NHS participants overlap with the source cohort used by Fung et al. for fruit and vegetable frailty outcomes. Keep that study lineage explicit.
