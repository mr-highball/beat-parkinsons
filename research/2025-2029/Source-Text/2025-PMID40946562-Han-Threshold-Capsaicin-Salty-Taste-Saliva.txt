Title: The threshold concentration capsaicin enhanced salty taste and reduced sodium: Sensory assessment and analysis of physiological and biochemical characteristics of saliva.
PMID: 40946562
DOI: 10.1016/j.foodchem.2025.146375
First published: 2025
Authors: Han T, Liu Q, Wang Z, Wang M, Hou Y, Wang S
Journal: Food chemistry
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40946562/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

PubMed abstract:
The health concerns associated with a high-salt diet and the desire for salty taste have led to the search for new approaches to salt reduction that could safely reduce the sodium content in foods without compromising the salty taste. This study explored capsaicin's impact on salty taste perception and sodium reduction using sensory evaluation (2-AFC and GLMS). Capsaicin at threshold levels enhanced saltiness perception in low-to-medium NaCl solutions (1.26-4.50 g/L), increasing saltiness intensity by 5.78 % and reducing NaCl mass concentration by 5.67 % (about 0.26 g/L). It also raised the saltiness difference threshold. Capsaicin-stimulated saliva showed increased flow rate, pH, endopeptidase, and saliva viscosity, while cystatin SN, carbonic anhydrase VI, Na+, K+, and total protein concentrations decreased. These findings suggest capsaicin could enhance saltiness perception, offering a strategy for developing lower-sodium foods without compromising taste. The study also provides insights into the mechanisms of salty taste perception and its link to saliva's physiological and biochemical properties.
