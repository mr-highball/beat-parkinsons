Primary Abstract:
INTRODUCTION: Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a common childhood disorder often managed with stimulant medications such as methylphenidate. Around 30% of patients with ADHD do not respond to treatment or cannot tolerate its side effects, which necessitates the consideration of alternative options. This study evaluated the effectiveness of adding the saffron extract to methylphenidate on ADHD symptoms and working memory in children aged 6-12.
METHOD: In a randomized clinical trial, 58 children diagnosed with ADHD were randomized into two groups: a methylphenidate (0.5-1 mg/kg/day, divided into 2-3 doses)/saffron (20-30 mg/day based on weight: 20 mg for <30 kg, 30 mg for >30 kg) group and a control group receiving only methylphenidate (0.5-1 mg/kg/day, divided into 2-3 doses) for six weeks. The severity of ADHD was evaluated using subjective (Conners' Parent Rating Scale-revised) and objective (Corsi block-span task and continuous performance test) tests at the start and end (in the sixth week) of the study.
RESULTS: 22 participants in the methylphenidate/saffron group and 21 in the control group completed the study. The changing trend of total ADHD scores (p = 0.002, Cohen's d = 0.85), hyperactivity score (p = 0.024, d = 0.62), impulsivity score (p = 0.031, d = 0.58), and attention score (p = 0.001, d = 0.92) was significantly different between groups based on subjective parent reports. However, no significant differences were found in impulsivity, inattention, or sustained attention on the CPT, though reaction time improved significantly (p = 0.023, d = 0.45). Scores on the Corsi Block Span Task indicated no significant changes in working memory between groups. The combination treatment was well-tolerated, with no reports of severe adverse effects.
CONCLUSION: The findings suggest that the combination of saffron with methylphenidate may enhance subjective reports of ADHD symptoms, particularly hyperactivity and impulsivity; however, objective cognitive measures showed limited changes. Given the favorable safety profile of saffron and study limitations, this supports its potential role as a complementary option, pending confirmation in placebo-controlled trials with larger samples and long-term evaluations.

[Methods] All experimental procedures adhered to ethical standards established by the institutional and national research committees, which were in line with the 1964 Helsinki Declaration and its later amendments. The study protocol received approval from the Ethics Committee of “details omitted for double‐anonymized peer review” (Ethics Number: 1402.386). The study is registered in the Iranian Clinical Trial Registration Center (Registration Number: “details omitted for double‐anonymized peer review”). Before participation, parents (either father or mother) were thoroughly informed of the intervention procedures, potential side effects, and benefits and provided written informed consent. This study was designed as a randomized, parallel‐group, superior clinical trial to evaluate the impact of saffron extract supplementation on ADHD symptoms and working memory in children aged 6 to 12. The study targeted children aged 6 to 12 years diagnosed with ADHD according to DSM‐5 criteria, who were referred to “details omitted for double‐anonymized peer review.” Demographic information, such as age and gender, was recorded for all participants. Inclusion criteria were children aged 6 to 12, confirmed diagnosis of ADHD by a child and adolescent psychiatry specialist based on DSM‐5 criteria, parents provided written informed consent for participation, and normal IQ levels (assessed via a standardized clinical evaluation). Exclusion criteria were non‐cooperation from parents at any stage of the study, presence of any other significant psychiatric disorders or systemic diseases requiring medication, substance dependence identified in the participant, and inability to complete detailed examinations. [Discussion] Saffron has been reported as an effective adjuvant therapy for several psychiatric disorders (Han et al. 2024; Matraszek‐Gawron, Chwil, Terlecki, and Skoczylas 2022). However, there is limited evidence regarding the efficacy of saffron extract when combined with methylphenidate for managing ADHD in children. This study aimed to evaluate the improvement in ADHD symptoms in children aged 6–12 years through both subjective and objective assessments after six weeks of treatment with saffron extract alongside methylphenidate. Our findings indicated a significant improvement in subjective assessment of hyperactivity, impulsivity, and inattention reported by parents. Additionally, enhancements were observed in working memory and sustained attention (objective assessments) following treatment, but the changes were not statistically significant. Our results demonstrated that children in the methylphenidate/saffron group showed statistically significant reductions in total ADHD scores and inattention after the intervention compared to the control group receiving only methylphenidate.
