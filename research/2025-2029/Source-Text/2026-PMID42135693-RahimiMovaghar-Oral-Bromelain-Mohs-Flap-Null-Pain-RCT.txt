Primary Abstract:
INTRODUCTION: Non-melanoma skin cancers (NMSCs) are the most common malignancies and Mohs micrographic surgery (MMS) with flap reconstruction is the treatment of choice. This study aims to investigate the effect of bromelain on the healing of skin flaps following MMS for NMSCs. METHODS: In a single-center, parallel-group, single-blinded randomized clinical trial, 60 patients with NMSC who underwent MMS and flap reconstruction were assigned (1:1) to either an intervention group receiving bromelain (1500 mg/day, Rataheal™) for 15 days postoperatively or a control group receiving standard care alone. The primary outcome was time to suture removal; the early wound healing score (EWHS) was recorded supportively. Secondary outcomes included pain, inflammation, keloid/scar formation, cosmetic satisfaction, and traction quality. RESULTS: Baseline characteristics (age: 69.2 ± 13.5 years; 71.7% male; flap size: 116.1 ± 7.0 mm2) were analyzed. Bromelain significantly shortened time to suture removal compared with controls (2.6 days, 95% CI: 0.74-4.11; p = 0.006). The EWHS was also significantly improved at the day 7 (7.2 vs. 5, p < 0.001) and day 14 (8.56 vs. 7.3, p = 0.014) of follow up. Other outcomes, including inflammation, pain, and cosmetic satisfaction, showed non-significant trends favoring bromelain. No major complications were reported. CONCLUSION: Bromelain supplementation is associated with earlier postoperative wound healing of skin flaps following MMS for NMSC, leading to 2.6 days earlier suture removal plus improvement in the EWHS without adverse effects. Our findings support the potential of bromelain as a safe and effective adjunct therapy for reconstructive skin cancer surgery. TRIAL REGISTRATION: Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT20240903062943N1|| https://irct.behdasht.gov.ir/trial/79056 ), 2024.10.28, enrollment started after registration.

[Method] The authors conducted the current randomized, single-blinded clinical trial prospectively at the surgical oncology department of Farhikhtegan Hospital, Tehran, Iran, from October 2024 to September 2025. Sixty patients with definitive diagnosis of NMSCs in the head and neck region who were surgically treated followed by skin flap reconstruction were held eligible for inclusion. Patients with any disturbing factor to wound healing (i.e., uncontrolled diabetes, malnutrition, immunosuppression, corticosteroid regimens, and radiotherapy), allergy to pineapple, any other diagnosis rather than SCC or BCC, active infections, and warfarin administration (due to possible contraindication with bromelain) were excluded from the study. The authors designed checklists and a standardized Likert-scale questionnaire to gather demographic and clinical data regarding variables, surgical approach and summary of the procedure, follow-up outcomes, and post-surgical complications based on clinical visits and patient interviews. An independent collaborator (who was not involved in surgery, postoperative assessment, or suture removal) assigned each patient to either the intervention or the control group (1:1 ratio) via online randomization [20]. The group assignments were placed in sequentially numbered, opaque sealed envelopes and concealed from the surgical and follow-up teams to minimize detection bias. The intervention group received 1500 mg/day of Rataheal™ (Pooyesh Darman Rata, Iran), orally prescribed in three divided doses (500 mg) for 15 days after surgery as adjunct therapy. Adherence was assessed by self-report and pill count in the absence of biochemical objective measurements. The control group received standard postoperative wound care. [Discussion] Wound healing occurs through a complex and tightly regulated cascade comprising hemostasis/coagulation, inflammatory, proliferative, and remodeling phases. This process begins almost immediately following surgery [26]. This matter represents a major public health concern due to its substantial economic and psychosocial burdens [27]. Therefore, appropriate treatment is critical to enhance wound healing and prevent further complications. Despite the numerous advantages of MMS and advances in flap design, complications such as wound dehiscence, partial or total flap failure, hematoma, infection, flap necrosis, scarring, and delayed wound healing remain significant challenges, particularly in sensitive anatomical regions like the face and neck [28–30]. Therefore, thoughtful decision-making is particularly important to accelerate the healing of skin flaps following MMS. Current approaches to improve outcomes primarily focus on surgical technique optimization. However, pharmacological adjunct interventions remain underexplored.
