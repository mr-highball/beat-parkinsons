Title: Acute aqueous lemon balm and assay-specific mouse immobility results
Source: https://biomedad.ae/PDF/bma-70
DOI: 10.34172/bma.70
Publication date: 2026-06-28
Text coverage: Publisher full article, pages 105 to 110. Methods, results, Figures 1 to 3 and discussion inspected.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18

The study allocated 66 male mice to 11 groups of six. The herb arms received separate concentrates of Melissa officinalis or Nepeta menthoides. The lemon balm concentrate used dried aerial parts extracted in hot water and concentrated after filtering. The study used one oral dose and compared immobility and open-field activity against saline and reference drug groups. No Parkinson model or reserpine enrollment appears in the protocol.

The 550 mg/kg lemon balm group showed shorter tail-suspension and forced-swim immobility compared with saline. Open-field grid crossings at this dose did not show a clear difference. The forced-swim results describe increased immobility in the 350 mg/kg group. The highest tested group showed more immobility relative to lower effective groups, and higher groups tended toward fewer crossings. The published interpretation allows motor suppression as an alternative explanation for some high-dose results. Omnibus ANOVA probabilities do not provide every requested pairwise estimate, and the animal assay does not measure clinical depressive symptoms.

The different dose directions qualify any claim that increasing exposure uniformly helps. The hot-water concentrate and animal dosing cannot establish the effect of a household leaf infusion in Parkinson disease. The earlier reserpine experiment tested pretreatment and emphasized a mixture. Its group results are separate from this acute design.
