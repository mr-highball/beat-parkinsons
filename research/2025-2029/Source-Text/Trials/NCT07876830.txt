BRIEF SUMMARY

The aim of this study is to evaluate the effect of artificial intelligence-supported
training and the Benson relaxation exercise on the care burden and stress levels of
caregivers of people with Parkinson's disease. In the study, caregivers of people with
Parkinson's disease will receive training on caring for people with Parkinson's disease
and on the Benson relaxation exercise. The training sessions will be conducted using an
artificial intelligence-based module. Participants will be randomly assigned to three
groups: a training group, an exercise group, and a control group. A caregiver information
form, the Modified Hoehn and Yahr Scale, the Zarit Caregiver Burden Scale, and the
Caregiver Stress Scale will be used to collect pretest data from all participants during
the initial interview. The training sessions for the experimental group will last 4
weeks. No training will be provided to the control group. Four weeks later, the Zarit
Caregiver Burden Scale and the Caregiver Stress Scale will be used to collect final test
data from the experimental and control groups.

The hypotheses related to this study are as follows:

  -  Artificial intelligence-supported training provided to caregivers of people with
     Parkinson's disease affects the burden of care.

  -  The Benson relaxation exercise provided to caregivers of people with Parkinson's
     disease affects the burden of care.

  -  Artificial intelligence-supported training provided to caregivers of people with
     Parkinson's disease affects stress levels.

  -  The Benson relaxation exercise administered to caregivers of people with Parkinson's
     disease affects stress levels.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Given the progressive nature of Parkinson's disease, individuals experience a progressive
decline in their ability to perform daily activities. In such cases, they require support
and assistance. People with Parkinson's are generally cared for by spouses, adult
children, close relatives, or other family members. Caregivers are exposed to stress and
the burden of care. It has been noted that providing caregivers with regular training on
managing the disease process, along with follow-up support after the training, can reduce
the caregiver's anxiety and facilitate the management of the patient's care. The use of
complementary and alternative methods has been steadily increasing in recent years. The
Benson relaxation technique, one of the most useful and well-known non-pharmacological
interventions, is a reliable method that can alleviate various physical and mental
symptoms such as anxiety, stress, and pain.

The aim of this study is to evaluate the effect of artificial intelligence-supported
training and the Benson relaxation exercise on the care burden and stress levels of
caregivers of people with Parkinson's disease.

In this study, caregivers in the experimental group will receive training on Parkinson's
disease care and the Benson relaxation exercise. 1. The experimental group will receive
training on Parkinson's disease and on managing common issues associated with the
disease. The training content was prepared by the researcher through a review of the
literature and consultation with experts in the field. 2. The training for the
experimental group, which includes the Benson relaxation exercise, will cover the
definition, benefits, and steps for performing the Benson relaxation exercise.

To enable participants to continue the training and the Benson relaxation exercise module
at home, the AI-based module will be downloaded to their phones via a link sent by the
researcher. The training provided to the experimental groups will last 4 weeks.
Throughout the 4 weeks, the researcher will monitor participants' engagement with the
training via the AI module. No training will be provided to the control group.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Zarit Caregiver Burden Scale
    Description
      This is a measure used to assess the stress experienced by caregivers of individuals
      requiring care or the elderly. The measurement tool consists of 22 items. The items are
      Likert-type, and each item is rated on a scale of 0 to 4, using the response options
      "never," "rarely," "sometimes," "often," or "almost always." A score ranging from 0 to 88
      is possible on this scale. A high scale score indicates a high level of distress.
    Time frame: The scale will be completed at the start of the study and 4 weeks after the completion of training and exercise.
  -
    Measure: Caregiver Stress Scale
    Description
      It was developed to measure the caregiving burden. It consists of 13 items. There is at
      least one item for each category: physical, social, work, financial, and time. On the
      scale, a score of 7 or higher indicates a higher level of stress.
    Time frame: The scale will be completed at the start of the study and 4 weeks after the completion of training and exercise.

  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  Be 18 years of age or older

    -  Be a family member of the patient (spouse, daughter, son, etc.)

    -  Living in the same household as the patient

    -  Having cared for the patient for at least 6 months

    -  Being the primary caregiver responsible for the patient's care

    -  There being no paid staff member in the home to assist the caregiver

    -  Having no health issues that prevent communication

    -  Being able to read and write

    -  Having access to a phone for communication

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  Having visual, auditory, or cognitive communication barriers
