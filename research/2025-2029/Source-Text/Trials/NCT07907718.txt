BRIEF SUMMARY

Cognitive-motor training combines thinking tasks with physical movements, encouraging
participants to choose appropriate actions and adjust their movements according to
different task demands. Training that involves stopping or changing a movement may help
people with Parkinson's disease respond to changing situations. These abilities may also
support walking in challenging environments, such as stepping over obstacles. However,
the effects of this type of training on cognitive function, walking performance, and
brain activity remain unclear. Therefore, this study aims to compare the effects of
cognitive-motor co-dependent training with conventional exercise training on these
outcomes in people with Parkinson's disease.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Gait speed
    Description
      Gait speed during obstacle crossing was measured using the GAITRite system. Continuous
      obstacle-crossing walking performance was assessed using the GaitUp system.
    Time frame: Before and after 8 weeks of intervention, as well as 4 weeks after intervention
  -
    Measure: Step length
    Description: Using GAITRite system to measure step length
    Time frame: Before and after 8 weeks of intervention, as well as 4 weeks after intervention
  -
    Measure: Step width
    Description: Using GAITRite system to measure step width
    Time frame: Before and after 8 weeks of intervention, as well as 4 weeks after intervention
  -
    Measure: Single support time
    Description: Using GAITRite system to measure single support time
    Time frame: Before and after 8 weeks of intervention, as well as 4 weeks after intervention
  -
    Measure: Gait Variability
    Description
      Gait variability during continuous obstacle crossing will be assessed using the GaitUp
      system and expressed as the coefficient of variation (CV = standard deviation / mean ×
      100%) of step time and stride length.
    Time frame: Before and after 8 weeks of intervention, as well as 4 weeks after intervention
  -
    Measure: Stride length
    Description
      Using the GaitUp system to measure stride length during continuous obstacle-crossing
      walking.
    Time frame: Before and after 8 weeks of intervention, as well as 4 weeks after intervention
  -
    Measure: Swing phase
    Description
      Using the GaitUp system to measure swing phase during continuous obstacle-crossing
      walking.
    Time frame: Before and after 8 weeks of intervention, as well as 4 weeks after intervention
  -
    Measure: Four Square Step Test
    Description
      The Four Square Step Test is a test used to assess the ability to step over objects and
      measures the time in seconds to navigate all four squares.
    Time frame: Before and after 8 weeks of intervention, as well as 4 weeks after intervention
  -
    Measure: Event-related potential - N2
    Description: Measure event-related potential through electroencephalography during the Go/No-go test
    Time frame: Before and after 8 weeks of intervention, as well as 4 weeks after intervention
  -
    Measure: Event-related potential - P300
    Description: Measure event-related potential through electroencephalography during the Go/No-go test
    Time frame: Before and after 8 weeks of intervention, as well as 4 weeks after intervention
  -
    Measure: Stroop color and word test
    Description: Record numbers of corrected color words which the subject responds within 45 sec.
    Time frame: Before and after 8 weeks of intervention, as well as 4 weeks after intervention
  -
    Measure: Trail making test
    Description
      Trail making test contain part A and part B. Faster completing the test indicates better
      executive function.
    Time frame: Before and after 8 weeks of intervention, as well as 4 weeks after intervention
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Part III of the Movement Disorder Society-Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS)
    Description
      Motor symptoms will be assessed using Part III of the Movement Disorder Society-Unified
      Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS). Scores range from 0 to 132, with higher
      scores indicating greater motor impairment.
    Time frame: Before and after 8 weeks of intervention, as well as 4 weeks after intervention
  -
    Measure: Single-Leg Standing Balance
    Description
      Balance will be assessed by measuring the time, in seconds, that participants can stand
      on one leg. Longer standing times indicate better balance.
    Time frame: Before and after 8 weeks of intervention, as well as 4 weeks after intervention
  -
    Measure: Montreal Cognitive Assessment
    Description
      Montreal Cognitive Assessment ranges from 0 to 30. Higher scores reflect better cognitive
      performances.
    Time frame: Before and after 8 weeks of intervention, as well as 4 weeks after intervention

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  A clinical diagnosis of idiopathic Parkinson's disease

    -  Stable use of prescribed antiparkinsonian medication for at least two weeks

    -  Modified Hoehn and Yahr stage I to III

    -  Age between 18 and 80 years

    -  Ability to ambulate independently for at least 30 meters without assistive devices

    -  A Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score of ≥ 24

    -  Corrected vision and hearing sufficient to effectively receive external stimuli

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  The presence of any neurological, musculoskeletal, or cardiovascular disorders that
       could affect participation in this study

    -  The presence of psychiatric disorders that could interfere with the ability to
       follow instructions or comply with study procedures

    -  A history of brain surgery, such as deep brain stimulation (DBS)
