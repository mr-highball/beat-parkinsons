BRIEF SUMMARY

Compulsive eating behaviors represent a significant issue across various disorders such
as Parkinson's disease, bipolar disorders, and obesity.

Despite their apparent differences, these conditions share common underlying disturbances
involving the brain's reward system, particularly within dopaminergic networks.6,7
Previous neuroimaging studies have consistently reported reward system connectivity
impairment associated with compulsive eating, in each of these disorders. However, few
studies have simultaneously explored the neurobiological similarities and specificities
of this common symptom, eating behavior, when occurring in different populations and
disorders.

The Bi-POTATOS project proposes an original and innovative approach by adopting a
transdiagnostic perspective, employing brain imaging and comprehensive psycho-behavioral
assessments to investigate common behavioral characteristics and neural circuit
alterations associated with compulsive eating across these three conditions. For the
first time, this study will allow a direct comparison of the cerebral signatures linked
to compulsive eating behavior, clearly distinguishing between transdiagnostic
vulnerabilities and features unique to each clinical condition.

The central hypothesis is that similar dysfunctions in brain regions involved in
motivation and impulsive decision-making underpin compulsive eating behaviors,
irrespective of the initial diagnosis. However, the underlying pathology may modulate
these mechanisms, introducing certain specificities.

By clarifying these common mechanisms, the Bi-POTATOS project will not only enhance our
understanding of the neurobiological etiology of these disorders but also pave the way
for innovative, targeted therapeutic interventions. This project will precisely identify
whether specific neural alterations constitute a universally relevant therapeutic target,
or conversely, determine which alterations necessitate tailored therapeutic strategies
based on the underlying pathology.

    Measure: Motor impulsivity (Go/No Go task)
    Description
      Motor impulsivity will be assessed with the Go/No-go task. It is a simple experimental
      paradigm that requires participants to respond as quickly as possible (Go) to a
      particular class of stimuli, for example by pressing a key on a keyboard, and not to
      respond to other classes of stimuli (No Go). The key behaviour measured with this
      experiment is the participants' ability to withhold a response on No-go trials.
    Time frame: at visit 2, between 1 and 12 weeks after selection visit
  -
    Measure: Motor inhibition (Stop Signal Task (SST))
    Description
      The Stop Signal Task (SST) is a test that measures motor inhibition. The participant must
      respond quickly to Go stimuli but interrupt their response when a stop signal appears.
      The key index, the Stop Signal Reaction Time (SSRT), reflects the time required to stop
      an ongoing action. This test will be performed by neuropsychologist.
    Time frame: at visit 2, between 1 and 12 weeks after selection visit
  -
    Measure: Iowa Gambling Task (IGT)
    Description
      IGT assesses risk-based learning by relying on a system of rewards and punishments within
      a simulated context of uncertainty, representing a real-life decision-making situation.

      Participants choose cards from four decks, each associated with varying amounts of
      monetary gains and losses. Over time, advantageous decks yield higher net rewards, while
      disadvantageous decks lead to net losses.

      Performance on the task reflects an individual's capacity to balance immediate rewards
      against long-term outcomes, thereby providing insight into adaptive decision-making and
      risk evaluation processes.

      This test will be performed by neuropsychologist.
    Time frame: at visit 2, between 1 and 12 weeks after selection visit.
  -
    Measure: Cognitive impulsivity (Stroop)
    Description
      The Stroop Test is a cognitive task designed to evaluate attentional control and the
      ability to inhibit automatic responses when faced with conflicting information.
      Participants are asked to name the color of the ink in which words are printed, even when
      the word itself refers to a different color (e.g., GREEN printed in red ink). This
      requires suppressing the automatic tendency to read the word and instead focusing on the
      ink color. The difference in performance between congruent and incongruent conditions,
      known as the Stroop interference effect, is a key indicator of cognitive inhibition and
      executive control. This test will be performed by neuropsychologist.
    Time frame: at visit 2, between 1 and 12 weeks after selection visit
  -
    Measure: Time perception (delay discounting reward task)
    Description
      Participants make choices between two alternatives: a larger reward available after a
      delay (across seven delay intervals) and a smaller reward available immediately. The
      value of the immediate reward is systematically adjusted, either increasing or
      decreasing, in separate blocks. The task assesses preferences for immediate versus
      delayed gratification using two reward types: money and food. This test will be performed
      by neuropsychologist.
    Time frame: at visit 2, between 1 and 12 weeks after selection visit
  -
    Measure: Balloon analog Risk task
    Description
      Participants are given 30 balloons (by default). For each balloon, they can choose either
      to inflate it further or to collect their earnings. With each successful inflation, they
      can earn money. However, if a balloon bursts before the participant collects the
      potential earnings, they lose all potential gains associated with that balloon.

      The average number of pumps on balloons that do not burst is the primary dependent
      variable in this paradigm. A higher number of pumps reflects increased risk-taking
      behavior. This test will be performed by neuropsychologist.
    Time frame: at visit 2, between 1 and 12 weeks after selection visit

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  1-Common Criteria (Parkinson disease, Obesity, Bipolar patients)

    -  Men or women, right-handed, 18 to 80 years old

    -  With or without compulsive eating according to the QEWP-R

    -  No changes to long-term treatments in the previous month

    -  Cooperation and understanding to strictly comply with the conditions set out in the
       protocol

    -  Acceptance of the protocol and signing of a written informed consent

    -  Affiliation to the social security system

       2-Specific criteria Parkinsonian patients (n=25 with CE, n=25 without CE)

    -  Diagnosis of Parkinson's disease according to the Movement Disorders Society
       criteria

    -  BMI<30

  Obese patients (n=25 with CE, n=25 without CE)

    -  Obese patients according to HAS 2022 classification

    -  BMI≥30

  Bipolar patients (n=25 with CE, n=25 without CE)

    -  Diagnosis of bipolar disorder according to DSM 5 criteria

    -  BMI<30

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  1-Common Criteria

    -  Dementia (MoCA <22)

    -  Severe depression (Beck Depression Inventory>30)

    -  Contraindications to MRI (implanted stimulator, insulin pump, intraocular metallic
       foreign bodies or close to the nervous and vascular systems, claustrophobia,
       non-removable dental appliance, tattoo on the face or neck, …)

    -  Refusal to be informed in case of incidental finding on MRI

    -  History of stroke or brain trauma that may impact MRI findings

    -  Patients under guardianship, deprived of freedoms or protection of justice.

    -  Pregnant women, breastfeeding women

       2- Specific criteria

  Parkinsonian patients:

    -  People suffering from neurological pathologies other than PD

    -  Patient suffering from psychiatric disorder

    -  Treated with deep brain stimulation

    -  Patients with severe tremor (> 3 for a tremor sub-item of the MDS-UPDRS III) or
       excessively severe dyskinesia contraindicating MRI

  Obese patients:

    -  Suffering from progressive neurological pathology and psychiatric disorder

    -  Weight >220kg contraindicating MRI

    -  Body (Shoulder) diameter > 220 cm contraindicating MRI

    -  History of bariatric surgery

    -  GLP1 treatment

  Bipolar patients:

    -  Suffering from neurological pathology and psychiatric disorder other than bipolar
       disorder

    -  Current acute depressive or hypomanic episode according to DSM 5

    -  Treated with deep brain stimulation or vagus nerve stimulation

    -  Electroconvulsivotherapy < 6 months
