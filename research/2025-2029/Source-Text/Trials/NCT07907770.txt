BRIEF SUMMARY

The study examines how individuals with Parkinson's disease, compared to healthy older
adults, evaluate their ability to cross an obstacle, and the association between this
evaluation, motor and cognitive status, and self-awareness.

A total of 120 participants aged 65-85 years will be recruited: 60 with Parkinson's
disease and 60 healthy adults. Following a telephone screening, eligible participants
will be invited to a single session lasting 1.5 to 2 hours at the University of Haifa, a
Maccabi Healthcare Services institute, or the participant's home.

During the session, following an explanation and the signing of an informed consent form,
demographic and clinical data will be collected, anthropometric measurements will be
taken, and questionnaires addressing walking confidence and emotional state will be
completed. Furthermore, mobility will be assessed using the Timed Up and Go test, motor
symptom severity will be assessed using the UPDRS-III (for individuals with Parkinson's
disease only), and cognitive function will be evaluated via a computerized test. Finally,
participants will perform the research task. In this task, while standing in front of the
obstacle, they will be asked to estimate the maximum obstacle height they believe they
can safely cross. Following this, they will be asked to actually cross it. Based on these
data, the gap between perceived and actual ability will be calculated. The task will be
performed under two conditions: without a backpack and with a weighted backpack (carrying
approximately 10% of the participant's body weight). The performance of the research task
will be video-recorded for the analysis of crossing data.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Background: Falls are a major cause of morbidity, loss of independence, and reduced
quality of life in individuals with Parkinson's disease (PD). Crossing obstacles is a
common daily task strongly associated with falls in this population. Before the physical
movement of crossing occurs, individuals are involved in a perceptual phase in which they
evaluate their capacity to perform the task (affordances). The accuracy of this
perceptual estimation is critical: over-estimation can lead to falls, while
under-estimation can unnecessarily limit independence. While literature shows healthy
older adults tend to over-estimate their abilities in obstacle crossing, this has rarely
been investigated in PD patients, who suffer from a unique combination of motor,
cognitive, and self-awareness impairments. Furthermore, carrying an external object (such
as a backpack) alters physical dimensions and further challenges this perceptual
estimation, a common real-world scenario that may exacerbate the gap between perceived
and actual ability.

Aims: The primary objective is to characterize the perception of action opportunities for
obstacle crossing with and without a weighted backpack (carrying approximately 10% of the
participant's body weight) in individuals with PD compared to healthy older adults.
Secondary objectives include investigating the relationships between this perception and
motor and cognitive status, and self-awareness.

Methods: The study is a cross-sectional observational study with experimental procedure.
It involves a single meeting where participants will undergo a computerized cognitive
assessment (NeuroTrax™), clinical motor tests (Timed Up and Go, MDS-UPDRS-III), and
complete standardized questionnaires (NFOG-Q, SAFRM, GDS-15, ABC). The experimental task
involves judging the maximum crossable height of an obstacle and then attempting to cross
it, allowing for the calculation of the perceptual error and its type
(over/under-estimation).

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: 1. Judgment error
    Description
      Calculated as the absolute difference (in cm) between the estimated maximum obstacle
      height and the actual maximum height successfully crossed
    Time frame: Baseline (single session, up to 2 hours)
  -
    Measure: 2. Normalized Estimated Maximum Obstacle Crossing Height
    Description
      The estimated maximum obstacle height the participant believes they can cross, normalized
      to the participant's leg length
    Time frame: Baseline (single session, up to 2 hours)
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: 1. Perceptual Error type (Over-estimation / Under-estimation)
    Description
      Categorized as over-estimation (actual performance < estimated) or under-estimation
      (actual performance > estimated)
    Time frame: Baseline (single session, up to 2 hours)
  -
    Measure: 2. Movement Disorder Society - Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS) Part III
    Description
      The evaluator assesses components such as bradykinesia, tremor, posture, and muscle
      rigidity. Higher scores indicate greater motor impairment. Assessed only in participants
      with Parkinson's disease
    Time frame: Baseline (single session, up to 2 hours)
  -
    Measure: 3. Levodopa Equivalent Dose (LED)
    Description
      A calculated value reflecting the weighted medication dosage for Parkinson's disease,
      serving as an indicator of disease severity. Assessed only in the Parkinson's disease
      group.
    Time frame: Baseline (single session, up to 2 hours)
  -
    Measure: 4. Timed Up and Go (TUG) Test
    Description
      The time (in seconds) it takes to rise from a chair, walk 3 meters, turn, return, and sit
      down is measured. Faster times indicate better functional mobility and lower fall risk
    Time frame: Baseline (single session, up to 2 hours)
  -
    Measure: 5. NeuroTrax™ - Global Cognitive Score (GCS)
    Description
      Assessed using the NeuroTrax™ computerized cognitive testing battery. Participants
      perform tasks testing attention, visuospatial processing, and executive functions.
      Results yield a GCS, where higher scores indicate better cognitive function
    Time frame: Baseline (single session, up to 2 hours)
  -
    Measure: 6. Self-Awareness of Falls Risk Measure (SAFRM)
    Description
      A measure assessing self-awareness of falls risk, consisting of identical patient and
      clinician versions across three sub-scales: Intellectual (15 items), Emergent (6 items,
      completed immediately after the Timed Up and Go test), and Anticipatory (10 items). The
      awareness score is calculated as the sum of the patient's scores minus the sum of the
      clinician's scores. A score of 0 indicates good awareness. A positive score indicates the
      patient overestimates their ability or underestimates the risk, while a negative score
      indicates the patient underestimates their ability or overestimates the risk
    Time frame: Baseline (single session, up to 2 hours)
  -
    Measure: 7. Geriatric Depression Scale (GDS-15)
    Description
      A 15-item questionnaire. Scores range from 0 to 15, with scores greater than 5 indicating
      a probability of depression
    Time frame: Baseline (single session, up to 2 hours)
  -
    Measure: 8. Activities-specific Balance Confidence (ABC) Scale
    Description
      Participants rate their confidence in performing daily activities without losing balance
      from 0% (no confidence) to 100% (complete confidence). The total score is the average of
      all items, with higher scores indicating greater confidence and less fear of falling
    Time frame: Baseline (single session, up to 2 hours)
  -
    Measure: 9. Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI)
    Description
      Quantified based on number and type of comorbidities as recorded in the participant's
      medical records. Higher scores indicate a heavier burden of comorbid conditions
    Time frame: Baseline (single session, up to 2 hours)

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria (Parkinson's Disease Group):

    1. Diagnosis of Parkinson's disease according to medical records.

    2. Ability to walk independently outdoors without walking aids in daily life.

    3. Fluent in reading and speaking Hebrew.

  Inclusion Criteria (Healthy Control Group):

    1. Ability to walk independently outdoors without walking aids in daily life.

    2. Fluent in reading and speaking Hebrew.

    3. No diagnosis of Parkinson's disease or any other neurological condition.

  Exclusion Criteria (Both Groups unless specified):

    1. Freezing of gait (Score > 0 on the NFOG-Q), for PD group only.

    2. Severe visual impairment that cannot be corrected with glasses (self-report).

    3. Dementia (Telephone Mini-Mental State Examination score ≤ 18).

    4. Any orthopaedic condition, pain, or health condition (other than PD) that affects
       walking (self-report).
