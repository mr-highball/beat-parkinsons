BRIEF SUMMARY

This study tests whether lemborexant, an approved sleep medicine, can improve sleep and
recovery in people with Parkinson's disease undergoing deep brain stimulation (DBS)
surgery. Sleep problems are common around surgery and may worsen pain and slow recovery.
Participants will be randomly assigned to take lemborexant or a placebo the night before
surgery. Researchers will compare anesthesia use, wake-up time, sleep quality (objective
and subjective), pain, recovery, and blood markers. The study will show whether
lemborexant is safe and helpful for this group.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Total Sleep Time (TST) on the Night Before Surgery
    Description
      Total sleep time measured by a wrist-worn sleep monitoring device (Huawei smart band 8)
      during the night before deep brain stimulation surgery. TST is defined as the total
      duration of sleep from sleep onset to final awakening, reported in minutes.
    Time frame: Night before surgery
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Perioperative subjective sleep quality
    Description
      Perioperative subjective sleep quality will be measured using the Richards-Campbell Sleep
      Questionnaire (RCSQ) score. The RCSQ is a 5-item visual analog scale assessing sleep
      depth, latency, awakenings, return to sleep, and sleep quality. Each item scored 0-100,
      total score is the mean of 5 items, range 0-100, higher scores indicate better sleep.
    Time frame: Preoperative night (assessed at 8:00-10:00 AM on the day of surgery), Postoperative days 1-3 (assessed at 8:00-10:00 AM)
  -
    Measure: Postoperative objective sleep quality
    Description
      Sleep parameters, including total sleep time and the duration of each sleep stage, will
      be measured using a wrist-worn sleep monitoring device.
    Time frame: Postoperative night 1 and night 3 (20:00 to 08:00 next day)
  -
    Measure: Time to Extubation
    Description
      Time from the end of surgery to extubation, defined as removal of the endotracheal tube.
      Reported in minutes.
    Time frame: Immediately after surgery (single assessment)
  -
    Measure: Time to Awakening
    Description
      Time from the end of anesthesia to the first response to verbal command. Reported in
      minutes.
    Time frame: Immediately after surgery (single assessment)
  -
    Measure: Perioperative Anxiety Visual Analog Scale (VAS-A) Score
    Description
      Anxiety Visual Analog Scale (VAS-A) score. The VAS-A is a 100-mm horizontal line anchored
      at 0 (no anxiety) and 100 (worst possible anxiety). The participant marks a point on the
      line corresponding to their anxiety level. Higher scores indicate greater anxiety. VAS-A
      score assessed upon entry to the operating room (before induction of anesthesia) and 1
      hour after surgery.
    Time frame: Perioperative [Upon entry to the operating room (before induction of anesthesia) and 1 hour after surgery]
  -
    Measure: Postoperative Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) Score
    Description
      The Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) is a 14-item self-rated questionnaire
      comprising two 7-item subscales: anxiety (HADS-A) and depression (HADS-D). Each item is
      scored from 0 to 3. Each subscale score ranges from 0 to 21, with higher scores
      indicating greater symptom severity.
    Time frame: postoperative day 1 and postoperative day 3.
  -
    Measure: Postoperative pain measured by Pain Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) score
    Description
      Pain Numeric Rating Scale (NRS) score. The NRS is an 11-point scale ranging from 0 (no
      pain) to 10 (worst possible pain). Higher scores indicate greater pain intensity.
    Time frame: 1 hour, 24 hours, 48 hours and 72 hours after surgery
  -
    Measure: Postoperative Quality of Recovery-15 (QoR-15)
    Description
      Quality of Recovery-15 (QoR-15) score. The QoR-15 is a 15-item questionnaire assessing
      postoperative recovery across physical comfort, emotional state, and physical
      independence. Each item scored 0-10, total range 0-150, higher scores indicate better
      recovery quality.
    Time frame: Postoperative day 1
  -
    Measure: Postoperative delirium
    Description
      Number of participants who develop postoperative delirium, assessed using the 3-Minute
      Diagnostic Interview for CAM-defined Delirium (3D-CAM). The 3D-CAM is a structured
      instrument for delirium assessment.
    Time frame: Within 3 days after surgery (assessed twice daily)
  -
    Measure: Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) Score at 1 Month After Surgery
    Description
      Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) score. The PSQI is a 19-item self-rated
      questionnaire assessing sleep quality over the past month. Seven component scores are
      summed to yield a global score ranging from 0 to 21. Higher scores indicate worse sleep
      quality.
    Time frame: 1 month after surgery
  -
    Measure: Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire-39 (PDQ-39) Score at 1 Month After Surgery
    Description
      Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire-39 (PDQ-39) score. The PDQ-39 is a 39-item
      disease-specific quality of life questionnaire for Parkinson's disease. Scores are
      transformed to a 0-100 scale, with higher scores indicating worse quality of life.
    Time frame: 1 month after surgery
Other outcomes
  -
    Measure: Total Sleep Time (TST) as Measured by Polysomnography (PSG)
    Description
      Total sleep time, defined as the total duration of sleep from sleep onset to final
      awakening, measured by 8-channel polysomnography. Reported in minutes.
    Time frame: Preoperative Day 1 (the night before surgery)
  -
    Measure: Sleep Efficiency (SE) as Measured by Polysomnography (PSG)
    Description
      Sleep efficiency, defined as total sleep time divided by total time in bed, expressed as
      a percentage. Measured by 8-channel polysomnography.
    Time frame: Preoperative Day 1 (the night before surgery)
  -
    Measure: Sleep Onset Latency (SOL) as Measured by Polysomnography (PSG)
    Description
      Sleep onset latency, defined as the time from lights off to the first epoch of any sleep
      stage, measured by 8-channel polysomnography. Reported in minutes.
    Time frame: Preoperative Day 1 (the night before surgery)
  -
    Measure: Percentage of Rapid Eye Movement (REM) Sleep as Measured by Polysomnography (PSG)
    Description
      Percentage of total sleep time spent in REM sleep, measured by 8-channel polysomnography.
      Reported as a percentage.
    Time frame: Preoperative Day 1 (the night before surgery)
  -
    Measure: Percentage of Non-REM Stage N3 Sleep as Measured by Polysomnography (PSG)
    Description
      Percentage of total sleep time spent in N3 (slow-wave) sleep, measured by 8-channel
      polysomnography. Reported as a percentage.
    Time frame: Preoperative Day 1 (the night before surgery)
  -
    Measure: Wake After Sleep Onset (WASO) as Measured by Polysomnography (PSG)
    Description
      Wake after sleep onset, defined as the total time spent awake after initial sleep onset,
      measured by 8-channel polysomnography. Reported in minutes.
    Time frame: Preoperative Day 1 (the night before surgery)
  -
    Measure: Plasma Orexin-A Level
    Description
      Fasting plasma orexin-A concentration measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay
      (ELISA). Reported in pg/mL.
    Time frame: Preoperative day 1, day of surgery (before anesthesia induction), Postoperative day 1
  -
    Measure: Number of Participants with Treatment-Emergent Adverse Events
    Description
      Number of participants experiencing any adverse event during the perioperative period,
      including but not limited to falls, nocturnal hypoxemia, abnormal dreams, somnolence,
      constipation, arthralgia, and headache.
    Time frame: Perioperative period (from study drug administration on the night before surgery through hospital discharge, approximately 5 days)

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  Patients with Parkinson's disease scheduled for elective DBS surgery under general
       anesthesia

    -  Age 50-80 years

    -  ASA physical status I-III

    -  Preoperative sleep disturbance, defined as Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI)
       score > 5

    -  Signed informed consent

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  Hoehn-Yahr stage ≥ 4

    -  Chronic use of sedative-hypnotic medications

    -  Acute psychiatric illness

    -  Severe sleep apnea, COPD, severe daytime sleepiness (Epworth Sleepiness Scale [ESS]
       ≥ 16), or history of narcolepsy and/or cataplexy

    -  Severe hepatic or renal impairment

    -  Use of systemic moderate or strong CYP3A inhibitors and/or inducers within 1 week
       before surgery

    -  Any condition that, in the opinion of the principal investigator, precludes
       participation in the study
