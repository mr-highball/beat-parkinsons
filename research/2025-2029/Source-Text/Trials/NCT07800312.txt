BRIEF SUMMARY

This study compared the effects of PechaKucha and traditional PowerPoint presentation
techniques used for Parkinson's disease education on nurses' knowledge and awareness
levels. The study used a two-group pretest-posttest quasi-experimental design. A total of
72 nurses were assigned to the PechaKucha intervention group (n=36) or the PowerPoint
control group (n=36) using 1:1 block randomization. Both groups received Parkinson's
disease education with identical educational content and learning objectives. The
PechaKucha presentation consisted of 20 slides automatically advancing every 20 seconds,
whereas the PowerPoint presentation was delivered using a conventional flexible-paced
format. Parkinson's disease knowledge and awareness were assessed before and immediately
after the educational intervention. The study aimed to determine whether the presentation
technique influenced nurses' knowledge and awareness following Parkinson's disease
education.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change in Parkinson's Disease Knowledge Score
    Description
      Parkinson's disease knowledge was assessed using the 20-item Parkinson's Disease
      Knowledge Test. Each correct answer was scored as 1 and each incorrect answer as 0,
      yielding a total score ranging from 0 to 20. Higher scores indicate greater knowledge of
      Parkinson's disease. Change in knowledge was evaluated by comparing pre-intervention and
      immediate post-intervention scores.
    Time frame: Baseline and immediately after the educational intervention
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change in Parkinson's Disease Awareness Score
    Description
      Parkinson's disease awareness was assessed using the 17-item Awareness Assessment Form.
      Items were rated on a 5-point Likert scale, yielding a total score ranging from 17 to 85.
      Higher scores indicate greater awareness of Parkinson's disease. Change in awareness was
      evaluated by comparing pre-intervention and immediate post-intervention scores.
    Time frame: Baseline and immediately after the educational intervention
