BRIEF SUMMARY

This study aims not only to develop a standardized protocol for performing the targeted
BBT but also to assess synchronized parameters: kinematic (using IMUs) and
electrophysiological (EMG) data. In this context, the pilot study aims to standardize the
instrumented BBT for the objective evaluation of joint kinematics and muscle activations
in the upper limb of healthy subjects. After establishing normative movement data, the
study will focus on characterizing pathological upper limb movements in individuals with
Parkinson's disease and post-stroke, comparing the obtained data with normative
references.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Upper limb impairment can result from various conditions, including stroke, Parkinson's
disease, musculoskeletal disorders, and cerebral palsy. Patients undergoing upper limb
rehabilitation are typically assessed using functional and motor scales to evaluate
therapy effectiveness and disease progression over time. These assessments focus on daily
living activities (ADLs), dexterity, and strength.

Several validated tests exist for evaluating upper limb disability. The Jebsen-Taylor
Hand Function Test measures ADL performance, such as eating, picking up small objects,
and card turning , where faster completion times indicate better function. The Action
Research Arm Test assesses grasping, moving, and releasing objects across 19 tasks,
scored from 0 to 3 by an observer. The Box and Blocks Test (BBT) is widely used to assess
manual dexterity across different clinical populations, including post-stroke, multiple
sclerosis, traumatic brain injury, and upper limb amputation. The BBT score is determined
by the number of wooden cubes (2.5 cm³) moved over a partition in one minute. Despite its
ease and ecological validity, traditional clinical tests only partially assess movement
quality.

Motion Capture (MoCap) technology enables a detailed, quantitative kinematic analysis of
upper limb movement, typically using marker-based stereophotogrammetry and infrared
cameras. Studies have demonstrated MoCap's utility for analyzing upper limb movement in
post-stroke patients and ADL performance. However, few studies have integrated MoCap into
clinical tests to analyze kinematics during repetitive motor tasks. These studies found
high trajectory variability in the BBT due to random block placement and unrestricted
selection.

To address this variability, has been introduced two standardized BBT versions:

Modified BBT (mBBT): Subjects pick up 16 blocks from a 4x4 grid in a set order, transport
them over the partition, and drop them.

Targeted BBT (tBBT): Blocks must be placed precisely onto a grid beyond the partition,
mimicking real-world object manipulation.

Early results from MoCap-based analysis of these BBT variants indicate feasibility and
standardized movement trends in healthy young adults. However, limitations exist, such as
the study's focus on young adults (29.9±8.3 years) and testing in a standing position.

An alternative kinematic analysis approach uses Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs), which
provide accurate motion tracking with minimal setup outside lab environments. IMUs have
been used in upper limb movement analysis, including clinical assessments for multiple
sclerosis, post-stroke, and Parkinson's disease.

Beyond kinematics, superficial electromyographic (sEMG) analysis evaluates muscle
activation and co-contraction during motor tasks highlighting the importance of
multimodal evaluations combining biomechanical and electrophysiological data.

This study aims to develop a standardized protocol for the targeted BBT, integrating
synchronized kinematic (IMU) and electrophysiological (sEMG) measurements. The pilot
phase will define normative movement patterns in healthy individuals before analyzing
pathological upper limb movements in Parkinson's disease and post-stroke patients,
comparing them against normative data.

Interventions
  -
    Type: OTHER
    Name: Inertial Measurement Unit and Box and Block test
    Description
      Subjects will be assessed during Standard Box and Block Test and Targeted Box and Block
      Test with Inertial Measurement Units.

      The subject is seated on a stable stool (without backrest and armrests) adjustable in
      height so that the hip and knee angles are formed at 90°. A height-adjustable table is
      placed in front of the subject: the heights of the table and the seat are adjusted so
      that the subject forms 90-degree elbow angles by resting the forearms on the table. The
      standardized BBT box (53.7 x 25.4 x 8.5 cm) is placed on the table so that the 15.2 cm
      high division is in correspondence with the medio-lateral axis of the subject and at a
      distance such that the subject reaches the (X0,Y0) of the box with the distal point of
      the metacarpal bone of the middle finger. Once the system has been calibrated and the
      inertial sensors applied, the patient will be asked to perform the task

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: IMU-based targeted Box and Block Test (t-BBT)
    Description
      Instrumental assessment of manual dexterity will be conducted using a sensorized and
      targeted BBT (tBBT): 16 wooden blocks, 2.5cm3 in size, are placed on a 4x4 grid in the
      container closest to the limb to be assessed. The subject must grasp one block at a time
      with a pre-established order (numbering in figure 2), transfer it over the dividing wall
      and release it on the corresponding position of a 4x4 grid present in the other
      container, as quickly as possible. Acquisition duration: time necessary to complete the
      exercise. After 2 minutes the acquisition is however interrupted.
    Time frame: Baseline
Other outcomes
  -
    Measure: Standard BBT
    Description
      The Box and Block Test (BBT) measures unilateral gross manual dexterity. It is a quick,
      simple and inexpensive test. It can be used with a wide range of populations, including
      clients with stroke. The BBT is composed of a wooden box divided in two compartments by a
      partition and 150 blocks. The BBT administration consists of asking the client to move,
      one by one, the maximum number of blocks from one compartment of a box to another of
      equal size, within 60 seconds. The box should be oriented lengthwise and placed at the
      client's midline, with the compartment holding the blocks oriented towards the hand being
      tested. In order to practice and register baseline scores, the test should begin with the
      unaffected upper limb.
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Passive Range of Motion (ROM)
    Description
      Range Of Motion (ROM) is the arc of motion available at a single joint or series of
      joints. It is the angle through which the joint moves from the anatomical position to the
      extreme limit of its motion in a particular direction. In essence, it is the extent to
      which a part of the body can be moved around a joint or a fixed point.
    Time frame: Baseline

      Parkinson's disease. It helps in describing the progression of the disease through
      various stages, thus allowing us to measure the severity of the case. 1 Unilateral
      involvement only usually with minimal or no functional disability Unilateral involvement
      only 1.5 - Unilateral and axial involvement 2 Bilateral or midline involvement without
      impairment of balance Bilateral involvement without impairment of balance 2.5 - Mild
      bilateral disease with recovery on pull test 3 Bilateral disease: mild to moderate
      disability with impaired postural reflexes; physically independent Mild to moderate
      bilateral disease; some postural instability; physically independent 4 Severely disabling
      disease; still able to walk or stand unassisted Severe disability; still able to walk or
      stand unassisted 5 Confinement to bed or wheelchair unless aided Wheelchair bound or
      bedridden unless aided
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS): UPDRS (2) e (3)
    Description
      Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS) is a rating tool used to gauge the the
      severity and progression of Parkinson's disease in patients. Unified Parkinson's Disease
      Rating Scale (UPDRS) is a rating tool used to gauge the the severity and progression of
      Parkinson's disease in patients[1]. The UPDRS scale consists of the following six
      segments: 1) Mentation, Behavior, and Mood, 2) ADL, 3) Motor sections, 4) Complications
      of Therapy (in the past week) 5) Modified Hoehn and Yahr Scale, and 6) Schwab and England
      ADL scale. The first four segments are made up of 42 items grouped into four subscales.
      The UPDRS was developed in 1987 as a gold standard by neurologists for monitoring the
      response to medications used to decrease the signs and symptoms of Parkinson's.
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Freezing Of Gait Questionaire (FOGQ)
    Description
      The FOGQ assesses Freezing of Gait (FOG) severity unrelated to falls in patients with
      Parkinson's Disease (PD), FOG frequency, disturbances in gait, and relationship to
      clinical features conceptually associated with gait and motor aspects (e.g., turning).
      The NFOG-Q doesn't require any equipment and is simple to administer. When rating the
      frequency of FOG over the previous month, patients independently complete the
      questionnaire, reporting both ON and OFF medication states. Responses to all but item 1
      are added up to determine the final score, which ranges from 0 to 28. Higher ratings
      indicate greater impact and intensity of freezing episodes. Patients who respond
      negatively to item 1, which functions as a screening question, may be categorised as
      non-freezers.
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Frenchay-Activities-Index (FAI)
    Description
      The aim is to record activities which require some initiative from the patient. It is
      important to concentrate upon the patient's actual frequency of activity over the recent
      past, not distant past performance nor potential performance. One activity can only score
      on one item. The frequency with which each item or activity is undertaken over the past
      3-6 months (depending on the nature of the activity) is assigned a score of 1 - 4 where a
      score of 1 is indicative of the lowest level of activity. The scale provides a summed
      score from 15 - 60.
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Fugl-Meyer Assessment Upper Limb (FM-UL)
    Description
      The Fugl-Meyer Assessment (FMA) is a stroke-specific, performance-based impairment index.
      it is designed to assess motor functioning, balance, sensation and joint functioning in
      patients with post-stroke hemiplegia. It is divided in Upper and Lower limb assesment. It
      is applied clinically and in research to determine disease severity, describe motor
      recovery, and to plan and assess treatment. Scoring is based on direct observation of
      performance. Scale items are scored on the basis of ability to complete the item using a
      3-point ordinal scale where 0=cannot perform, 1=performs partially and 2=performs fully.
      The total possible scale score for the Fugl-Meyer Assesment for Upper Limb is 126
      points.. Points are divided among the domains as follows: Motor score: ranges from 0
      (hemiplegia) to 66 points (normal motor performance); Sensation: ranges from 0 to 12
      point; Joint range of motion: ranges from 0 to 24 point; Pain: from 0 to 24 point.
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Modified Ashworth Scale (MAS)
    Description
      The Modified Ashworth Scale (MAS) is used to assess spasticity.The Modified Ashworth
      Scale (MAS) is used to assess spasticity. The test is performed by extending the patients
      limb's first from a position of maximal possible flexion to maximal possible extension
      (the point at which the first soft resistance is met). Afterwards, the modified Ashworth
      scale is assessed while moving from extension to flexion. Joint angles are measured in
      degrees.
    Time frame: Baseline

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  All participants who have signed the informed consent for the study and meeting the
  inclusion and exclusion criteria will be considered eligible. Each of the participants
  belonging to the three groups has to meet the following inclusion and exclusion criteria:

  Control group (CG)

  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  Age between 35 and 85 years old;

    -  Right-handed.

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  having pathologies affecting the upper limb function;

    -  having cognitive and/or severe visual deficit.

  Parkinson group (PG)

  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  diagnosis of idiopathic PD by UK Brain Bank criteria;

    -  Hoehn and Yahr (H&Y) stage 2-3;

    -  aged between 35 and 85 years old;

    -  moderate disease-related upper limb motor performance deficit (i.e., Unified
       Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS) Part II, items 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 16 =2-3;
       Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS) Part III, items 20, 21, 22, 23, 24,
       25 =2-3).

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  inability to understand study instructions;

    -  severe visual deficit;

    -  coexisting disabling neurological or orthopedic disorders at upper limb;

    -  previous brain surgery.

  Stroke group (SG)

  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  hemiparesis evaluated

    -  Fugl-Meyer Assessment Upper Limb (FMA-UL) ranging between moderate (22<FMA-UL<44) to
       mild (FMA>44);

    -  site of the cortical/subcortical lesion according to Bamford's classification: total
       anterior circulation infarct (TACI) and partial rior circulation infarct (PACI).

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  motor and cognitive inability to maintain the correct position during the execution
       time of the motor task;

    -  severe visual impairment;

    -  upper limb pain;

    -  site of the lesion according to Bamford's classification: posterior circulation
       infarct (POCI) and lacunar infarct (LACI).
