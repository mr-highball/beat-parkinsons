BRIEF SUMMARY

Parkinson's disease (PD) is a leading neurodegenerative health condition in Sweden and a
public health concern. Although no cure exists, functional independence and participation
in society remain top priorities, and can be achieved through targeted physical activity
(PA) interventions. To date, few longitudinal cohort studies have been conducted, using
PA as main outcome in therapy research, partly due to the complex nature of predicting
and controlling this phenomenon. To help address this knowledge gap, the overall purpose
of the future long-term (ActivPARK) study is to enhance knowledge of the evolution of PA
behavior, and how it is influenced beyond the disease characteristics using a broad
explanatory model, in persons with PD from a diversity of settings in Sweden with the aim
of prescribing tailored and personalized interventions to enhance functioning, health,
and wellbeing throughout the disease progression and recommend potential health care
pathway modifications.

The investigators have just established an expert group, comprising researchers,
healthcare professionals, and PD organisations (including persons with PD) and reached
consensus on essential priority clinical therapy research questions on PA and
methodological considerations. The next phase entails performing a multicentre
feasibility/pilot study, further developing and refining the assessment battery and
research questions linked to the improved explanatory model for PA and methodology, to
inform the definite larger clinical cohort study.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Feasibility of the assessment battery - length of the clinical examination and questionnaires
    Description
      The pilot/feasibility study aims to evaluate feasibility of the assessment battery
      measured the time it takes to complete the examination and questionnaires.
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Feasibility of the assessment battery - perception of the clinical examination
    Description
      Feasibility of the assessment battery will also be evaluated through semi-structured
      questions regarding fatique, concentration, pain/discomfort to the participants after the
      clinical examination.
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Feasibility of the assessment battery -Compliance to questionnaires sent via REDCap
    Description: Compliance will be measured with percentages of completed questionnaires in REDCap.
    Time frame: Baselline
  -
    Measure: Feasibility of the assessment battery - Suitability of the primary outcome
    Description
      Suitability of the accelerometers in the home environment as the primary outcome for the
      targeted patient group will be assessed by the percentage of participants that are able
      to wear the accelerometer for at least 4 days days and render valid data.
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Physical activity levels
    Description
      The ActiGraph accelerometer (GT3X+, ActiGraph, Pensacola, FL, US) will be used to measure
      absolute and relative time spent in sedentary behavior, low intensity physical activity
      (LIPA) and moderate to vigorous intensity physical activity (MVPA), by total time in
      bouts.
    Time frame: Baseline
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Physical activity - self rated
    Description: Frändin Grimby scale, ranging from 1 to 6, higher score=better.
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Disease severity, Parkinson symptoms
    Description
      Movement Disorders Society- Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (MDS- UPDRS) parts 1
      to 4.

      Higher scores = worse/more symptoms
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Gait
    Description: Gait speed in m/s measured with the 10 meter walking test or through gait analysis.
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Balance performance
    Description
      Assessed with the Mini-BESTest. Mini-Balance Evaluation Systems Test a rating scale for
      dynamic balance incorporating 14 different balance and gait items that were assessed by a
      physical therapist on a scale from 0-2. Maximum points 28. 0-28 points with higher scores
      indicating better balance control
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Anxiety and depression
    Description
      Assessed with Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS), 0-24 on the depression and
      anxiety part respectively. Lower score=better
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Cognitive performance
    Description: Assessed with the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), 0-25, higher score = better
    Time frame: Baseline and 3 year follow-up
  -
    Measure: Self assessed cognitive function
    Description
      Assessed with Executive function questionnaire (DEX), 20 items scored 1 to 4, max 80,
      more scores= worse.
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Non-Motor Symptoms
    Description
      Non-Motor Symptoms Questionnaire (NMSQ), 30 questions with yes/no answer. More yes
      answers = more non-motor symptoms
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Walking ability- self assessed
    Description
      WALK-12G questionnaire, 0 and 42 points, with higher scores reflecting greater perceived
      walking difficulties (higher=worse)
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Freezing of gait - self-assessed
    Description: Freezing of gait questionnaire (FOGQsa), 6 questions/items, scored 1 to 5 (higher=worse)
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Balance confidence
    Description
      Activities specific balance confidence (ABC scale), 16 items which is scored 1 to 10 and
      then divided by 16. 0-100%, higher % = better
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Motivation
    Description
      Behavioural Regulation in Exercise Questionnaire (BREQ 4), 28 items score 1 to 7 and
      divided into subcomponents: Amotivation, External regulation, Introjected avoidance,
      Introjected approach, Identified regulation, Integrated regulation, Intrinsic regulation
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Fatigue
    Description
      Parkinson's Fatigue Scale (PFS-16), 16 items scored 1 to 5 from strongly disagree to
      strongly agree . more points/score= worse.
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Disability
    Description
      World health organization (WHO) disability assessment schedule (Whodas 2.0), 12
      self-assessed questions (12 to 60) more points=worse
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Self-efficacy
    Description: Self-efficacy of exercise/PA (ESES), 10 questions, scored 1-4. higher=better
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Sleep
    Description
      Scales for Outcomes in Parkinson's disease - Sleep (SCOPA-SLEEP), 4 parts, A-2 questions,
      B-5 questions , C-1 question and D- 6 questions. Higher= worse
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Self rated pain
    Description: Assessed with visual analog scale, VAS from 0 to 100
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Health related quality of life
    Description
      Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire (PDQ39). The Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire (PDQ-39)
      assesses how often people with Parkinson's experience difficulties across 8 dimensions of
      daily living (mobility, activities of daily living, emotional well-being, stigma, social
      support, cognitions, communications and bodily discomfort). The sum score is as a
      percentage score ranging between 0 and 100. Higher is better
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Wellbeing
    Description
      The WHO- Five Well-Being Index (WHO-5), 5 questions ranging from 0 to 5, 0-25,
      higher=better
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Nutrition
    Description: Mini Nutritional Assessment, max 14. higher = better
    Time frame: Baseline
Other outcomes
  -
    Measure: Working och long-term memory
    Description: Rey-Auditory Verbal Learning Test (RAVLT), higher= better
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Parkinson related pain
    Description: Assessed with the Kings Parkinsons Pain Scale (KPPS), max 168, higher= more pain
    Time frame: Baseline
