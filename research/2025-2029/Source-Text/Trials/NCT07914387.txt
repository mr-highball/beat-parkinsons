BRIEF SUMMARY

Parkinson's disease often causes constipation and changes in gut bacteria, but it is not
known how quickly a daily fiber supplement changes gut physiology in people with
Parkinson's, or how long any change lasts after stopping. This study will test how a
daily fiber bar affects bowel function and gut inflammation in people with Parkinson's
disease and in household members who live with them. Participants will eat one fiber bar
(11 grams of fiber) each day for 3 months, then stop and be followed for one more month,
for about 4 months in total. At three points (before starting, after 3 months of fiber,
and one month after stopping), participants will collect a stool sample at home and mail
it to Stanford in a provided kit, so no clinic visits are needed. Every week,
participants will also fill out a short questionnaire about how often they have bowel
movements, their bowel regimen, and they will complete a questionnaire about how bowel
symptoms affect their daily life. In the lab, we will measure proteins in the stool that
are signs of gut inflammation. We want to learn how fast these proteins change when fiber
is added, whether changes last after fiber is stopped, and whether people with
Parkinson's respond differently than their household partners. We expect that fiber will
change gut inflammation and bowel function during the fiber period, and that these
changes will differ between people with Parkinson's and their partners after the fiber
bars are stopped. Results may help guide future research on diet and gut function in
Parkinson's disease.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Rationale:

Parkinson's disease (PD) is a progressive neurological disorder, and the biological
drivers of disease progression remain poorly defined. Gastrointestinal (GI) dysfunction
is among the earliest and most common non-motor features of PD, and constipation,
dysbiosis, and an altered gut microbial composition are reported in up to ~80% of people
with PD (PWP). PWP show reduced abundance of commensal taxa that produce metabolites,
notably short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), that are involved in intestinal motility,
epithelial barrier integrity, and mucosal immune homeostasis. SCFAs are generated by
microbial fermentation of dietary fiber. SCFA supplementation has been associated with
cognitive changes in animal models and in a small pilot cohort of PWP. Greater non-motor
symptom burden, as measured by MDS-UPDRS Part I, is associated with increased risk of
dementia in PD, which is one reason the gut is of interest in PD research.

In the general population, fiber supplementation is associated with increased stool
frequency and bowel regularity, and it has been proposed as a way to support bowel
function and the gut microbiome in PWP. Response to fiber depends on baseline microbiome
composition and function, but the responsiveness (flexibility) of the PD microbiome to
fiber has not been characterized, and the time course over which fiber changes bowel
function or gut inflammation in PWP has not been systematically studied. This study
addresses that gap.

Animal studies support a role for the gut microbiome in Parkinson's disease. In a
genetically modified mouse model of Parkinson-like disease, germ-free mice showed fewer
movement problems and less brain inflammation than mice with a normal microbiome, even
though both groups had the same genetic risk. When germ-free mice received gut bacteria
from people with Parkinson's disease, their symptoms worsened, whereas bacteria from
healthy human donors did not worsen movement symptoms (Sampson et al., 2016). This
suggests that gut bacteria may be involved in the disease process and not only accompany
it.

Study design:

SUNs is a 4-month, open-label, two-arm interventional study with three longitudinal
timepoints: baseline (no supplementation), the end of a 3-month fiber supplementation
period, and one month after cessation. PWP are enrolled together with a cohabitating
control (household partner), and both receive the same intervention. Dyad-based
enrollment is intended to reduce environmental and dietary confounding of the gut
microbiome. Participants are recruited from an existing Stanford biobank of 79
PWP/cohabitating control dyads, so baseline characterization already exists for the
candidate pool.

The intervention is a prebiotic fiber bar containing 11 g of fiber, manufactured by
Sorridi Therapeutics, at a dose of one bar per day as recommended by the study dietitian.
Participants may choose one flavor (Lemon Cranberry or Dark Chocolate Cherry) or receive
an equal shipment of both. Tolerability was previously assessed in an open-label,
single-arm pilot (NCT07127120); in a sponsor-reported summary, 18 adults with Parkinson's
disease ate one bar daily for 28 days, all completed the study, and no serious adverse
events were reported. SUNs extends daily exposure to 90 days. The NeuroFiber bars are
donated by Sorridi Therapeutics.

Specific aims and hypotheses:

Aim 1: Determine whether fiber supplementation changes fecal inflammatory protein levels
over time in PWP. We hypothesize that Neurofiber supplementation will change fecal
inflammatory protein levels (e.g., TNF-α, IL-6, CRP), and that the persistence of these
changes after cessation will differ between controls and PWP.

Aim 2: Determine the rate at which initiation and cessation of fiber supplementation
affects bowel function in PWP. We hypothesize that bowel function (Bristol Stool Form
Scale, defecation frequency, and bowel symptoms) will change in both PWP and controls
during supplementation, and that these measures will return toward baseline at different
rates in PWP and controls after cessation. Bowel symptoms and bowel-related quality of
life (PAC-SYM and PAC-QoL) are assessed longitudinally in parallel.

Biospecimen collection and processing:

Stool is collected remotely by participants using commercial stool collection kits (DNA
Genotek, Zymo, and OMNImet Gut collection tubes) and returned by mail to Stanford, where
samples are preserved for downstream protein isolation and quantification. Collecting at
home enables the repeated longitudinal sampling this design requires and supports
retention. We will also preserve these samples for downstream metagenomic and metabolomic
analysis.

Protein quantification:

Fecal proteins will be isolated and quantified in collaboration with the Stanford Human
Immune Monitoring Center (HIMC). Inflammatory protein levels will be measured using the
NULISA (NUcleic acid Linked Immuno-Sandwich Assay) platform, a proximity-ligation
immunoassay with DNA-barcoded antibodies and sequencing or qPCR readout that allows
highly sensitive, multiplexed protein detection from very small sample volumes.

Analysis:

Longitudinal changes in fecal inflammatory proteins and in questionnaire-based bowel
measures will be compared across the three timepoints within participants and between PWP
and cohabitating controls.

Significance and future directions:

The study will define the extent and time course of PD gut microbiome adaptation to fiber
and test whether adaptation is associated with changes in bowel function and gut
inflammation. These data are intended to inform future research on diet, the gut
microbiome, and the gut-brain axis.

STUDY GROUPS AND INTERVENTIONS

Arm groups
  -
    Label: People with Parkinson's disease + daily fiber
    Type: EXPERIMENTAL
    Description
      Participants with a clinical diagnosis of Parkinson's disease who live with a
      participating household partner. Participants eat one NeuroFiber bar (11g fiber) daily
      for 3 months, then stop for a 1-month washout. Stool samples are collected at baseline,
      after 3 months of supplementation, and 1 month after stopping. Bowel function is tracked
      with weekly questionnaires throughout the 4-month study. This arm is the primary
      population for testing how quickly fecal inflammatory proteins and self-reported bowel
      function change with fiber initiation and cessation.
    Intervention names
      - Drug: Prebiotic fiber bar
  -
    Label: Household control + daily fiber
    Type: OTHER
    Description
      Participants who live with a participating household partner that has been diagnosed with
      Parkinson's disease. Participants eat one NeuroFiber bar (11g fiber) daily for 3 months,
      then stop for a 1-month washout. Stool samples are collected at baseline, after 3 months
      of supplementation, and 1 month after stopping. Bowel function is tracked with weekly
      questionnaires throughout the 4-month study. This arm is the control population for
      testing how quickly fecal inflammatory proteins and self-reported bowel function change
      with fiber initiation and cessation.
    Intervention names
      - Drug: Prebiotic fiber bar
Interventions
  -
    Type: DRUG
    Name: Prebiotic fiber bar
    Description
      NeuroFiber® prebiotic fiber bar (Sorridi Therapeutics): a vegan, plant-based whole-food
      bar with a proprietary blend of 14 prebiotic and whole-food fibers, formulated as a
      source of prebiotic fiber to support short-chain fatty acid-producing gut bacteria. Each
      bar is one serving (50 g) with 11 g of fiber. Participants eat one bar daily and may
      choose one flavor (Lemon Cranberry or Dark Chocolate Cherry) or receive an equal shipment
      of both. Ingredients include a prebiotic fiber blend, date paste, almonds, walnuts,
      whole-grain oats, pumpkin and chia seeds, fruit, coconut, green tea extract, and vitamins
      D3, B12, and E; Lemon Cranberry also contains cashews and flax seeds, and Dark Chocolate
      Cherry also contains cocoa products. No inulin or gums. Contains tree nuts. Donated by
      the manufacturer. Tolerability previously assessed in open-label pilot NCT07127120.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Fecal Inflammatory Protein Quantification
    Description
      Fecal inflammatory proteins will be quantified with a multiplex immunoassay of 250
      cytokines, chemokines, and other inflammation-related proteins. The primary analyses are
      the 1) protein level change from baseline to Month 3, 2) protein level change after a one
      month wash out at month 4 in a pre-specified set of key proteins (eg., TNF and IL-6).
      Levels will be compared between people with Parkinson's disease and cohabitating
      controls.
    Time frame: Baseline (Day 0), end of fiber supplementation (Month 3 or 90 days, whichever is first), and end of 1-month washout (Month 4 or 120 days, whichever is first)
  -
    Measure: Bristol Stool Form Scale (BSFS) score
    Description
      Participants will complete a weekly bowel regimen questionnaire that includes the Bristol
      Stool Form Scale (BSFS), a validated 7-point scale of stool consistency (types 1-2
      indicate constipation, 3-5 normal stool, and 6-7 diarrhea). Participants take one 11 g
      prebiotic fiber bar daily for 3 months, followed by a 1-month washout. The primary
      analysis is the rate of change in weekly BSFS score after starting supplementation (Weeks
      1-12) and after stopping it (Weeks 13-17). Changes are compared between people with
      Parkinson's disease and cohabitating controls. Defecation frequency and other bowel
      symptoms from the same questionnaire are reported as separate secondary outcomes.
      Increase in BSFS score = decrease in constipation symptom report.
    Time frame: Weekly from baseline through Month 4 (Weeks 1-17).
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change in weekly defecation frequency during fiber supplementation and washout
    Description
      Self-reported number of bowel movements per week from the weekly bowel regimen
      questionnaire. Rate of change is evaluated during supplementation and during the 1-month
      washout and compared between people with Parkinson's disease and cohabitating controls.
    Time frame: Weekly from baseline through Month 4 (Weeks 1-17); supplementation phase ends at Day 90 or 3 months, whichever is first.
  -
    Measure: Change in Patient Assessment of Constipation Symptoms (PAC-SYM) score across fiber supplementation and washout
    Description
      The PAC-SYM is a validated 12-item self-report questionnaire with abdominal, rectal, and
      stool symptom subsections. Total scores are collected at baseline and weekly through
      Month 4 (Weeks 1-17). Changes from baseline to end of supplementation and from end of
      supplementation to end of washout are compared between people with Parkinson's disease
      and cohabitating controls. Higher total score indicates worse symptoms. A negative change
      from baseline = less constipation-related burden, positive change = more
      constipation-related burden.
    Time frame: Weekly from baseline through Month 4 (Weeks 1-17); supplementation phase ends at Day 90 or 3 months, whichever is first.
  -
    Measure: Change in Patient Assessment of Constipation Quality of Life (PAC-QoL) score
    Description
      AC-QoL total scores will be assessed at baseline and weekly through Month 4 (Weeks 1-17).
      A negative change from baseline = less constipation-related burden, positive change =
      more constipation-related burden.
    Time frame: Weekly from baseline through Month 4 (Weeks 1-17); supplementation phase ends at Day 90 or 3 months, whichever is first.
  -
    Measure: Change in gut microbiome composition and function across fiber supplementation and washout
    Description
      DNA extracted from stool will undergo shotgun metagenomic sequencing. Gut microbe
      composition (ie., total abundance) will be assessed. Changes from baseline to end of
      supplementation and from end of supplementation to end of the 1-month washout are
      compared between people with Parkinson's disease and cohabitating controls. Increase in
      complexity (different types of gut microbiota) = generally healthier outcome.
    Time frame: Baseline (Day 0), end of fiber supplementation (Day 90 or 3 months, whichever is first), and end of 1-month washout (Day 120 or 4 months, whichever comes first).
  -
    Measure: Change in fecal metabolite profile (metabolomics) across fiber supplementation and washout
    Description
      Stool metabolites will be profiled using mass spectrometry, including microbial
      metabolites such as short chain fatty acids. Changes in individual metabolite abundance
      and pathway-level profiles from baseline to end of supplementation and from end of
      supplementation to end of the 1-month washout are compared between people with
      Parkinson's disease and cohabitating controls. Multiple-comparison correction will be
      applied.
    Time frame: Baseline (Day 0), end of fiber supplementation (Day 90 or 3 months, whichever is first), and end of 1-month washout (Day 120 or 4 months, whichever comes first).
  -
    Measure: Change in autonomic symptoms (SCOPA-AUT total and gastrointestinal subscale score) across fiber supplementation and washout
    Description
      The SCOPA-AUT is a validated self-report questionnaire of autonomic symptoms over the
      past month, with gastrointestinal, urinary, cardiovascular, thermoregulatory,
      pupillomotor, and sexual domains. Each item is scored 0 (never) to 3 (often). Total score
      (0-69) and gastrointestinal subscale score (0-21) are collected at baseline, at the end
      of supplementation, and at the end of the 1-month washout. Changes from baseline to end
      of supplementation and from end of supplementation to end of washout only by participants
      with Parkinson's disease. Higher scores indicate worse autonomic symptoms. A negative
      change from baseline = less burden, positive change = more burden.
    Time frame: Baseline (Day 0), end of fiber supplementation (Day 90 or 3 months, whichever is first), and end of 1-month washout (Month 4)
  -
    Measure: Change in participant-reported non-motor experiences of daily living (MDS-UPDRS Part IB) in people with Parkinson's disease across fiber supplementation and washout
    Description
      MDS-UPDRS Part IB is a validated 7-item questionnaire covering non-motor symptoms over
      the past week, including constipation, urinary problems, lightheadedness on standing,
      fatigue, sleep problems, daytime sleepiness, and pain. Each item is scored 0 (normal) to
      4 (severe), for a total of 0-28. Completed only by participants with Parkinson's disease.
      Total score and the constipation item are compared within participants from baseline to
      the end of supplementation and from the end of supplementation to the end of the 1-month
      washout. Higher scores indicate worse symptoms. Higher score = increased burden.
    Time frame: Baseline (Day 0), end of fiber supplementation (Day 90 or 3 months, whichever is first), and end of 1-month washout (Month 4).
  -
    Measure: Change in participant-reported motor experiences of daily living (MDS-UPDRS Part II) in people with Parkinson's disease across fiber supplementation and washout
    Description
      MDS-UPDRS Part II is a validated 13-item participant questionnaire on the impact of motor
      symptoms on daily activities over the past week (for example speech, eating, dressing,
      hygiene, walking, tremor). Each item is scored 0 (normal) to 4 (severe), for a total of
      0-52. Completed only by participants with Parkinson's disease. Total scores are compared
      within participants from baseline to the end of supplementation and from the end of
      supplementation to the end of the 1-month washout. Higher scores indicate greater motor
      disability. A negative change from baseline = less burden, positive change = more burden.
    Time frame: Baseline (Day 0), end of fiber supplementation (Day 90 or 3 months, whichever is first), and end of 1-month washout (Month 4).
Other outcomes
  -
    Measure: Association of baseline Parkinson's disease risk factors (PD-RFQ) with fecal inflammatory protein, microbiome, and metabolite response to fiber supplementation
    Description
      Participants will complete the Parkinson's Disease Risk Factor Questionnaire (PD-RFQ) at
      baseline to capture environmental, lifestyle, and medication exposures. At Month 4,
      participants will confirm whether their residential address or employment has changed
      since baseline, to verify that exposures were stable during the study. Exploratory
      analyses will test whether baseline risk factors are associated with changes in fecal
      inflammatory proteins, gut microbiome composition, and fecal metabolites from baseline to
      the end of supplementation and through the 1-month washout. Participants with a change in
      address or employment will be flagged and examined in sensitivity analyses. Higher score
      = higher estimated PD risk.
    Time frame: Baseline (Day 0) for PD-RFQ; Month 4 for confirmation of address and employment

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria Arm 1:

    -  Diagnosed with Parkinson's disease by a movement disorders specialist

    -  Age 18+

    -  Lives with a participant in this study enrolled or planning to enroll in Arm 2

    -  Not currently consuming a fiber supplement

  Inclusion Criteria Arm 2:

    -  No history of neurodegenerative disease, including Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's
       disease, Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS), Frontotemporal dementia, Multiple
       Sclerosis

    -  Age 18+

    -  Lives with a participant in this study enrolled or planning to enroll in Arm 1

    -  Not currently consuming a fiber supplement

  Exclusion Criteria Arm 1:

    -  Allergy or intolerance to any ingredient in the study bar, including tree nuts,
       seeds, oats, coconut, or green tea extract

    -  Difficulty swallowing (dysphagia) that makes eating a dense, solid food bar unsafe

    -  History of bowel obstruction or major gastrointestinal surgery that makes a daily
       fiber supplement unsafe
