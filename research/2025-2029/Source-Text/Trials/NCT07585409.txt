BRIEF SUMMARY

This project aims to identify why some people with Parkinson's disease (PwPD) become less
physically active, and which factors support or hinder activity. Understanding these
factors is essential for developing person centred interventions and effective support
that can be implemented in routine healthcare. A national, multicentre longitudinal
cohort study will be conducted including approx 450 PwPD from five Swedish regions
(including the internal pilot NCT06901869). Physical activity will be measured
objectively with activity monitors, combined with clinical assessments and digital
questionnaires over four years. The primary outcome is physical activity level
(accelerometer measured decline), and exposure variables include physical, cognitive,
disease specific, social, environmental, motivational, and personal factors. Data
collection involves clinical tests, questionnaires, accelerometer data, and patient
reported experiences.

This is a continuation of an internal pilot study NCT06901869. The study will enable
early identification of those at risk for declining activity and guide development of
person centred, evidence based interventions. Long term, it aims to integrate activity
monitoring and risk factor screening into routine care to improve health and quality of
life for PwPD.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Physical activity - low and moderate intensity
    Description
      The primary outcome is changes over time in PA (i.e. low + moderate-vigorous intensity PA
      in time spent per day) measured with accelerometers (Actigraph GT3X+, Pensacola, FL, US)
      during 7 days in people with Parkinson's disease everyday life.
    Time frame: Baseline and yearly follow-ups for 3 years
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Physical activity - total vector magnitude
    Description
      The secondary outcomes are change over time in total vector magnitude measured with
      accelerometers (Actigraph GT3X+, Pensacola, FL, US) during 7 days in people with
      Parkinson's disease everyday life.
    Time frame: Baseline and yearly follow-ups for 3 years
  -
    Measure: Physical activity - low intensity
    Description
      The secondary outcomes are change in time spent in low intensity physical activity per
      day measured with accelerometers (Actigraph GT3X+, Pensacola, FL, US) during 7 days in
      people with Parkinson's disease everyday life.
    Time frame: Baseline and yearly follow-ups for 3 years
  -
    Measure: Physical activity - moderate high intensity
    Description

Other outcomes
  -
    Measure: Disease severity, Parkinsons symptoms
    Description
      Disease severity measured with the Movement Disorders Society -Unified Parkinson's
      Disease Rating Scale) parts 1 to 4.

      Higher scores = worse/more symptoms
    Time frame: Baseline and 3 year follow-up
  -
    Measure: Gait
    Description: Gait speed in m/s measured with the 10 meter walking test or through gait analysis.
    Time frame: Baseline and 3 year follow-up
  -
    Measure: Balance performance
    Description
      Assessed with the Mini-BESTest. Mini-Balance Evaluation Systems Test a rating scale for
      dynamic balance incorporating 14 different balance and gait items that were assessed by a
      physical therapist on a scale from 0-2. 0-28 points with higher scores indicating better
      balance control
    Time frame: Baseline and 3 year follow-up
  -
    Measure: Anxiety and depression
    Description
      Assessed with Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS), 0-24 on the depression and
      anxiety part respectively. Lower score=better
    Time frame: Baseline and yearly follow-ups for 3 years
  -
    Measure: Cognitive performance
    Description: Assessed with the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), 0-25, higher score = better
    Time frame: Baseline and 3 year follow-up
  -
    Measure: Motivation
    Description
      Assessed with the Behavioural Regulation in Exercise Questionnaire (BREQ 4), a 28-item
      survey that measures exercise motivation. The questionnaire uses a 7-point Likert scale,
      ranging from one to seven. Maximum 196. Higher scores = higher motivation
    Time frame: Baseline and yearly follow-ups for 3 years
  -
    Measure: Self-efficacy
    Description
      Assessed with the Self-efficacy and motivation for exercise/physical activity (ESES). 10
      questions rated on a 4 point Likert scale. Maximun 40 points, higher scores= better
      self-efficacy
    Time frame: Baseline and yearly follow-ups for 3 years
  -
    Measure: Physical activity history and preferences,
    Description: Self-made questions on physical activity history and preferences
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Self assessed cognitive function
    Description
      Assessed with Executive function questionnaire (DEX), 20 items scored 1 to 4, max 80,
      more scores= worse
    Time frame: Baseline and yearly follow-ups for 3 years
  -
    Measure: Non-Motor Symptoms
    Description
      Non-Motor Symptoms Questionnaire (NMSQ), 30 questions with yes/no answer. More yes
      answers = more non-motor symptoms
    Time frame: Baseline and yearly follow-ups for 3 years
  -
    Measure: Walking ability- self rated
    Description
      WALK-12G questionnaire, 0 and 42 points, with higher scores reflecting greater perceived
      walking difficulties (higher=worse)
    Time frame: Baseline and yearly follow-ups for 3 years
  -
    Measure: Freezing of gait - self-assessed
    Description: Freezing of gait questionnaire (FOGQsa), 6 questions/items, scored 1 to 5 (higher=worse)
    Time frame: Baseline and 3 year follow-up
  -
    Measure: Balance confidence
    Description
      Activities specific balance confidence (ABC scale), 16 items which is scored 1 to 10 and
      then divided by 16. 0-100%, higher % = better
    Time frame: Baseline and yearly follow-ups for 3 years
  -
    Measure: Fatigue
    Description
      Parkinson's Fatigue Scale (PFS-16), 16 items scored 1 to 5 from strongly disagree to
      strongly agree . more points/score= worse.
    Time frame: Baseline and yearly follow-ups for 3 years
  -
    Measure: Disability
    Description
      World health organization (WHO) disability assessment schedule (Whodas 2.0), 12
      self-assessed questions (12 to 60) more points=worse
    Time frame: Baseline and 3 year follow-up
  -
    Measure: Sleep
    Description
      Scales for Outcomes in Parkinson's disease - Sleep (SCOPA-SLEEP), 4 parts, A-2 questions,
      B-5 questions , C-1 question and D- 6 questions. Higher= worse
    Time frame: Baseline and 3 year follow-up
  -
    Measure: Self-rated pain
    Description: Assessed with visual analog scale, VAS from 0 to 100
    Time frame: Baseline and yearly follow-ups for 3 years
  -
    Measure: Health related quality of life
    Description
      Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire (PDQ39). The Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire (PDQ-39)
      assesses how often people with Parkinson's experience difficulties across 8 dimensions of
      daily living (mobility, activities of daily living, emotional well-being, stigma, social
      support, cognition, communications and bodily discomfort). The sum score is as a
      percentage score ranging between 0 and 100. Higher is better
    Time frame: Baseline and yearly follow-ups for 3 years
  -
    Measure: Wellbeing
    Description
      The WHO- Five Well-Being Index (WHO-5), 5 questions ranging from 0 to 5, 0-25,
      higher=better
    Time frame: Baseline and yearly follow-ups for 3 years
  -
    Measure: Nutrition
    Description: Mini Nutritional Assessment, max 14. higher = better
    Time frame: Baseline and 3 year follow-up
  -
    Measure: Socioeconomic status
    Description: Education and income level.
    Time frame: Baseline
