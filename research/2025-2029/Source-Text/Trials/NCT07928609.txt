BRIEF SUMMARY

Patients with Parkinson's disease experience progressive deconditioning of the
respiratory muscles, leading to weakness and potential ventilatory impairments. Evidence
indicates that physical exercise positively affects these muscles in people with various
clinical conditions. This study will evaluate the effects of multicomponent and
resistance training on respiratory parameters and physical and functional capacities in
individuals with Parkinson's disease. A total of 40 patients will be randomly allocated
to two groups: 20 individuals in the multicomponent training group and 20 in the
resistance training group. Both groups will undergo a three-month intervention with two
sessions per week. All participants must continue standard pharmacological treatment for
Parkinson's disease. Respiratory parameters will be assessed using respiratory pressure
measurement and spirometry. Physical and functional capacities will be assessed using the
Rikli and Jones test battery.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Inspiratory pressure
    Description
      Respiratory muscle strength will be assessed using a manovacuometer with a range of -300
      to +300 cmH2O. Respiratory muscle strength during inspiration will be assessed.
    Time frame: three weeks
  -
    Measure: Forced Vital Capacity
    Description
      Forced vital capacity, expressed in liters, will be assessed based on the maximum volume
      of inspired air, which will be measured in liters using a spirometer
    Time frame: three weeks

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  Hoehn and Yahr stages 1-3

    -  Physical and mental requirements,

    -  No history of exercise training in the preceding three months.

    -  Standard pharmacological treatment for PD throughout the 12-week study period.

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  Mini-Mental State Examination score <24

    -  Unstable cardiovascular disease or any other uncontrolled chronic condition
