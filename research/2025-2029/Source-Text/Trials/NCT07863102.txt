BRIEF SUMMARY

This study investigates how different types of extended reality, Virtual Reality (fully
immersive) and Mixed Reality (partly real-world), can be used in rehabilitation for
people with Parkinson's disease. Participants will attend a single 45-minute session at
the Rehabilitation Center of UZ Ghent, where they will perform a simple balloon-popping
task twice using a VR headset, once in each environment. During the exercises, they will
be safely supported to prevent falls, while a wearable device measures physical responses
such as heart rate and skin conductance. After each task, participants will complete
short questionnaires about their experience and mental effort. The study aims to
determine which type of extended reality is preferred and how each affects physical and
mental responses, supporting the development of more accessible rehabilitation
approaches.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Extended reality is being used increasingly within neurological rehabilitation. However,
there are different forms of extended reality that can be used while wearing a
head-mounted display (a VR headset). For example, sometimes the patient no longer sees
the environment at all and is thus completely immersed in virtual reality (Virtual
Reality), or sometimes the patient still sees part of the environment alongside the
virtual reality (Mixed Reality). There are therapeutic arguments for using both forms,
but to date, it is not known which is preferred for individuals with Parkinson's disease.

In a previous study (ONZ-2022-0404), a new type of therapy was tested in individuals with
Parkinson's disease: visual perturbation training. This study took place in a large VR
lab at Ghent University Hospital. To make this therapy accessible to more patients, this
study will investigate the effect using a VR headset. Therefore, first the subjective
preference in a small group of individuals with Parkinson's disease will be examined, and
this study will also investigate whether there is a difference in the use of various
forms of extended reality regarding a number of physical parameters (heart rate, heart
rate variability, changes in skin conductance, etc.) measured in the patient using a
wearable device, as well as mental load via questionnaires.

The participants will perform an exercise twice in which they have to pop balloons in a
virtual world (Virtual Reality or Mixed Reality). During the exercise, participants will
be secured in the Rysen, a weight-bearing system that ensures the participant cannot fall
in the event of loss of balance. After each exercise, participants will complete the
questionnaires on Redcap via a tablet provided by the department.

One test session lasts approximately 45 minutes.

All devices used (VR headset, Rysen weight-bearing system to guarantee patient safety,
Shimmer to measure heart rate variability and skin conductance) are CE marked and are
used within their scope. All tests will take place at the Rehabilitation Center of UZ
Ghent. The VR headset and Rysen are devices owned by UZ Ghent. The Shimmer wearable is
being made available by UGent for the duration of the study.

This information can help us with targeted follow-up research and applying for the
necessary funding.

STUDY GROUPS AND INTERVENTIONS

Arm groups
  -
    Label: Within-Subject XR (VR and MR)
    Description
      Participants complete two conditions during a single study session using a head-mounted
      display, a Virtual Reality (fully immersive) and Mixed Reality (real-world view combined
      with virtual elements) tasks. The order of conditions is varied. During both conditions,
      participants perform a standardized balloon-popping task while seated in a weight-support
      system to ensure safety. Physiological responses (heart rate, heart rate variability, and
      skin conductance) are recorded, and subjective experience and mental load are assessed
      via questionnaires after each condition.
    Intervention names
      - Device: Extended reality balance training
Interventions
  -
    Type: DEVICE
    Name: Extended reality balance training
    Description
      Participants perform a balloon-popping task for balance training using a VR headset in
      two conditions: Virtual Reality (fully immersive) and Mixed Reality (real-world combined
      with virtual elements). The task includes visual changes to challenge how participants
      see and respond to their environment, including eye movements. The goal is to compare how
      these two formats affect mental effort and experience.
    Arm group labels
      - Within-Subject XR (VR and MR)

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Score on the Igroup presence questionnaire (IPQ)
    Description
      The Igroup Presence Questionnaire (IPQ) is a scale for measuring the sense of presence
      experienced in a virtual environment. It will be used as one of the measures to identify
      the subjective preference during Virtual Reality (fully immersive) and Mixed Reality
      (real-world combined with virtual elements). This questionaire consists of 14 items where
      participants indicate their spatial presence, involvement or realness on a Likert scale
      (1-7). Max score is 98. Higher score is higher feeling of presence, immersion and
      engagement.
    Time frame: Immediately after each condition (within one study session)
  -
    Measure: Score on the NASA Task Load Index (Nasa-TLX)
    Description
      The NASA-TLX is a questionnaire to examine the subjective workload. It contains 6 items
      where participants again score each item on a 7 point Likert scale. A higher score
      indicates more cognitive workload.
    Time frame: Immediately after each condition (within one study session)
  -
    Measure: Score on the Virtual Reality Sickness Questionnaire
    Description
      Questionnaire to document potential side effects of using extended reality (e.g.
      cybersickness). Participants are asked to indicate for 9 symptoms on a 4 point Likert
      scale if they experience side effects and how intense they are present. A higher score
      indicates more adverse events.
    Time frame: Immediately after each condition (within one study session)
  -
    Measure: Hartrate (variability) during the extended reality sessions
    Description
      Physiological responses during VR and MR conditions are measured continuously using a
      wearable device. These measures reflect autonomic nervous system activity and objective
      load during task performance. Differences between VR and MR conditions will be analyzed.
    Time frame: During the MR and VR condition (within one study session)
  -
    Measure: Skin conduction during the extended reality sessions
    Description
      Physiological responses during VR and MR conditions are measured continuously using a
      wearable device. These measures reflect autonomic nervous system activity and objective
      load during task performance. Differences between VR and MR conditions will be analyzed.
    Time frame: During the MR and VR condition (within one study session)
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Eye tracking during the extended reality task as a measure of visual function
    Description
      Exploration of the relationship between visual function and subjective cognitive load
      scores obtained after the MR condition, to assess whether eye movement behavior
      contributes to perceived mental effort.
    Time frame: During the MR session
  -

    Description
      Participants will be questioned to know their highest education level. This will be used
      to assess if this a co-variate for the primary outcome measures
    Time frame: Before the intervention
  -

    Time frame: Before the intervention
