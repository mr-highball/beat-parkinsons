BRIEF SUMMARY

This is a single-center, prospective observational study of patients with
moderate-to-advanced primary Parkinson's disease (PD). PD is the second most common
neurodegenerative disease. Current clinical evaluation mainly depends on motor symptoms,
and there is a lack of quantitative biomarkers that can dynamically show nigrostriatal
neurodegeneration.

This study aims to explore the changing pattern of neurodegeneration by combining
¹⁸F-DOPA PET imaging and blood biomarkers such as neurofilament light chain,
alpha-synuclein, GFAP, Tau and inflammatory factors. Thirty-six eligible participants
will be enrolled. At baseline, clinical scales, lab tests, blood collection and ¹⁸F-DOPA
PET-CT will be performed. Follow-up assessments will take place at 6 and 12 months.

The primary outcome is the change in ¹⁸F-DOPA PET measurements from baseline to 12
months. Secondary outcomes include changes in clinical rating scales and blood biomarkers
over 1 year. Participants can receive standardized medication advice for PD during the
study.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Primary Parkinson's disease (PD) is the second-most common neurodegenerative disease.
Current clinical assessments rely heavily on motor-based rating scales, lacking
quantitative biomarkers for dynamic evaluation of nigrostriatal neurodegeneration.
Existing large PD cohorts focus mostly on early-stage patients, while longitudinal
evidence combining ¹⁸F-DOPA PET molecular imaging and circulating blood biomarkers for
moderate-to-advanced PD remains limited. This single-center, prospective observational
cohort study is performed at Xuanwu Hospital, Capital Medical University. Thirty-six
participants with moderate-to-advanced PD (Hoehn-Yahr stage 3-4) will be enrolled. The
study schedule includes baseline assessments and repeated follow-up evaluations at 6-,
and 12-months after enrollment. Study-related procedures cover clinical rating scales,
physical and laboratory examinations, levodopa challenge test, ¹⁸F-DOPA PET-CT scans,
blood sample collection and PD-related genetic testing. Adverse events and concomitant
medications are collected throughout the study period. Early-withdrawn participants will
complete required safety assessments. Potential risks are mainly associated with PET-CT
radiation exposure and gastrointestinal side-effects of carbidopa. All participants will
receive standardized medication counseling for PD during follow-up. Part of imaging and
genetic testing will be completed by collaborative institutions. Conducted in accordance
with the Declaration of Helsinki, the study requires written informed consent prior to
any study-related procedures. Subjects may withdraw freely at any time without impact on
routine medical care. This work aims to develop an integrated imaging-blood biomarker
model to quantify PD-related neurodegeneration and explore potential surrogate endpoints
for future disease-modifying trials. Both positive and negative findings will be publicly
reported.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change in Putamen 18F-DOPA PET Specific Binding Ratio (SBR) from Baseline to 12 Months
    Description
      Change in 18F-DOPA PET specific binding ratio (SBR) in the putamen from baseline to 12
      months after enrollment. SBR is calculated as the ratio of radiotracer uptake in the
      putamen to that in a reference region. Higher SBR values indicate greater presynaptic
      dopaminergic function.
    Time frame: Baseline and 12 months after enrollment
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change in MDS-UPDRS Part III Score from Baseline to 12 Months
    Description
      Change in the Movement Disorder Society-Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale
      (MDS-UPDRS) Part III score from baseline to 12 months after enrollment. MDS-UPDRS Part
      III is a clinician-rated motor examination composed of 18 items yielding 33 individual
      ratings. Each rating is scored from 0 to 4 (0 = normal, 4 = severe).
    Time frame: Baseline and 12 months after enrollment
  -
    Measure: Change in Hoehn-Yahr Stage from Baseline to 12 Months
    Description
      This study enrolls patients with Hoehn-Yahr stage 3 to 4. Higher stages indicate more
      advanced disease.
    Time frame: Baseline and 12 months after enrollment

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  Confirmed diagnosis of primary Parkinson's disease;

    -  Age 21 to 75 years, no sex restriction;

    -  Hoehn-Yahr (H&Y) stage 3-4;

    -  OFF-state MDS-Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale Part III (MDS-UPDRS-III)
       motor score > 33, with a positive Madopar challenge test (MDS-UPDRS-III improvement
       rate > 30%);

    -  Willingness to participate in the study and signed informed consent form.

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  Secondary parkinsonism / atypical parkinsonian syndromes;

    -  Genetically confirmed Parkinson's disease;

    -  Prior surgical treatment for Parkinson's disease;

    -  Contraindications to ¹⁸F-DOPA PET-CT scanning, including but not limited to cardiac
       pacemakers/defibrillators, ferromagnetic metallic implants (excluding skull-based
       and cardiac devices with MRI safety approval);

    -  Comorbid dementia, severe cognitive impairment, or severe psychiatric disorders.
