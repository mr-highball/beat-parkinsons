BRIEF SUMMARY

This study aimed to investigate the relationship between instrumented balance
measurements and clinical balance assessments in individuals with Parkinson's disease and
to evaluate changes in balance and functional mobility following a 12-week multimodal
rehabilitation program.

Eighteen individuals with Parkinson's disease participated in this prospective,
single-group study. All participants received a rehabilitation program that included
balance exercises, aerobic exercise, and balance training using the Korebalance system.
The program was conducted twice weekly for 12 weeks.

Balance was assessed using the Korebalance system and the Berg Balance Scale (BBS).
Functional mobility was evaluated using the Timed Up and Go (TUG) test. Assessments were
performed before and after the rehabilitation program.

The study examined whether instrumented balance measurements were associated with
clinical balance performance and whether balance and functional mobility changed
following rehabilitation.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

This prospective, single-center, single-group pre-post interventional study investigated
instrumented balance assessment and changes in balance and functional mobility following
multimodal rehabilitation in individuals with Parkinson's disease.

A total of 18 participants with Parkinson's disease were included. Eligible participants
were adults aged 18 years or older with Hoehn and Yahr stages 1-3 who were able to
participate in the rehabilitation program. Exclusion criteria included advanced
Parkinson's disease (Hoehn and Yahr stages 4-5), significant cognitive impairment, other
neurological disorders affecting balance, recent surgery within the preceding six months,
and severe or uncontrolled systemic diseases that could interfere with participation.

All participants underwent a 12-week multimodal rehabilitation program consisting of two
supervised sessions per week. The program included progressive static and dynamic balance
exercises, functional mobility training, dual-task activities, moderate-intensity aerobic
exercise, and balance training using the Korebalance system.

Balance training included tandem stance, single-leg stance when safely achievable,
multidirectional weight shifting and stepping, repeated sit-to-stand exercises, obstacle
negotiation, turning, and functional walking. More advanced activities included
figure-of-eight walking, step negotiation, reactive postural control, and dual-task
exercises combining motor and cognitive tasks. Aerobic training was performed using
treadmill walking or stationary cycling at moderate intensity, generally corresponding to
a Borg Rating of Perceived Exertion of 11-14.

The primary outcome measures were static and dynamic balance scores obtained using the
Korebalance Premiere-19 system. Lower Korebalance scores indicated better balance
performance.

Secondary outcome measures included the Berg Balance Scale (BBS), which assessed clinical
balance, and the Timed Up and Go (TUG) test, which assessed functional mobility.

Outcome assessments were performed at baseline and after completion of the 12-week
rehabilitation program.

Baseline instrumented balance measurements were examined in relation to clinical balance
and functional mobility measures. Changes in instrumented balance, clinical balance, and
functional mobility were evaluated by comparing measurements obtained before and after
rehabilitation.

The study did not include randomization or a control group. All participants received the
same multimodal rehabilitation intervention.

    Intervention names

Interventions
  -
    Type: BEHAVIORAL
    Name: Multimodal Rehabilitation Program
    Description
      Participants received a supervised multimodal rehabilitation program twice weekly for 12
      weeks (24 sessions). The program included approximately 10 minutes of warm-up exercises,
      20 minutes of progressive balance training, and 20 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic
      exercise using a treadmill or stationary bicycle. Balance exercises included static and
      dynamic balance tasks, multidirectional stepping, functional mobility activities,
      turning, and dual-task training. Additional balance training was performed using the
      Korebalance Premiere-19 system. Exercise intensity was generally maintained at a Borg
      Rating of Perceived Exertion of 11-14. Exercises were individually progressed according
      to participant ability and safety.
    Arm group labels
      - Multimodal Rehabilitation Group

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change in Static Korebalance Score
    Description
      Static balance was assessed using the Korebalance Premiere-19 system. Measurements were
      obtained at baseline and after the 12-week multimodal rehabilitation program. Lower
      scores indicate better balance performance. Change was calculated as the
      post-intervention score minus the baseline score.
    Time frame: Baseline and Week 12
  -
    Measure: Change in Dynamic Korebalance Score
    Description
      Dynamic balance was assessed using the Korebalance Premiere-19 system. Measurements were
      obtained at baseline and after the 12-week multimodal rehabilitation program. Lower
      scores indicate better balance performance. Change was calculated as the
      post-intervention score minus the baseline score.
    Time frame: Baseline and Week 12
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change in Berg Balance Scale Score
    Description
      Clinical balance was assessed using the Berg Balance Scale (BBS), which consists of 14
      items evaluating static and dynamic balance during functional activities. Each item is
      scored from 0 to 4, resulting in a total score ranging from 0 to 56. Higher scores
      indicate better balance performance. Change was calculated as the post-intervention score
      minus the baseline score.
    Time frame: Baseline and Week 12
  -
    Measure: Change in Timed Up and Go Test Time
    Description
      Functional mobility was assessed using the Timed Up and Go (TUG) test. Participants were
      instructed to stand up from a chair, walk 3 meters, turn around, return to the chair, and
      sit down. The time required to complete the test was recorded in seconds. Lower values
      indicate better functional mobility. Change was calculated as the post-intervention time
      minus the baseline time.
    Time frame: Baseline and Week 12

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  Adults aged 18 years or older.

    -  Diagnosis of Parkinson's disease.

    -  Hoehn and Yahr stages 1-3.

    -  Sufficient cognitive function to understand and follow instructions during
       assessments and rehabilitation.

    -  Ability to participate in the rehabilitation program.

    -  Provision of written informed consent.

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  Advanced Parkinson's disease (Hoehn and Yahr stages 4-5).

    -  Significant cognitive impairment that could interfere with participation or
       assessment.

    -  Other neurological disorders affecting balance or mobility.

    -  History of surgery within the preceding six months.

    -  Severe or uncontrolled systemic diseases that could interfere with safe
       participation in the rehabilitation program.

    -  Refusal or inability to provide informed consent.
