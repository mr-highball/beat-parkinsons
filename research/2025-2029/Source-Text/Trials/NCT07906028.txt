BRIEF SUMMARY

The goal of this clinical trial is to learn whether adding visual biofeedback to an
effortful swallow exercise program improves tongue strength and swallowing function in
adults with Parkinson's disease.

The main questions it aims to answer are:

  -  Does effortful swallow exercise with visual biofeedback increase tongue strength and
     tongue endurance more than the same exercise without visual biofeedback?

  -  Does visual biofeedback lead to greater improvements in swallowing pressure,
     swallowing muscle activity, swallowing symptoms, and swallowing-related quality of
     life?

Researchers compared two groups that performed the same effortful swallow exercise
program. In one group, participants saw real-time feedback on a tongue pressure device
(Iowa Oral Performance Instrument, IOPI) showing when they reached their target
swallowing effort. In the other group, participants did the same exercises with verbal
instructions only and did not see the device display.

Participants:

  -  Attended supervised exercise sessions with a speech-language therapist 3 times a
     week for 4 weeks (12 sessions in total)

  -  Performed repeated hard saliva swallows in each session, with rest breaks between
     sets

  -  Completed tongue pressure tests, a surface electromyography test of the muscles
     under the chin, and swallowing questionnaires before the program and within one week
     after it ended.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

This single-center, randomized, parallel-group study was conducted at the Neurology
Clinic of University of Health Sciences Istanbul Training and Research Hospital. Adults
with idiopathic Parkinson's disease (Modified Hoehn and Yahr stages 1-3) were randomly
assigned to one of two groups using a computer-generated block randomization sequence.

Both groups received supervised effortful swallow therapy three times per week for four
consecutive weeks (12 sessions). Each session consisted of five sets separated by
2-minute rest intervals. Participants performed 16 effortful saliva swallows per set at
15-second intervals during the first two weeks and 20 effortful swallows per set at
10-second intervals during the final two weeks. The IOPI bulb was positioned at the
posterior tongue throughout the intervention in both groups. Correct execution of each
swallow was confirmed by observation of hyolaryngeal movement.

In the visual biofeedback group, the IOPI was operated in Target Mode, with the target
set at 80% of each participant's baseline maximum posterior tongue pressure; participants
received real-time visual feedback when the target was reached. In the non-biofeedback
group, the IOPI was operated in Peak Mode and the display was visible only to the
therapist; participants received identical verbal instructions without visual feedback.

All assessments were performed during the ON phase of antiparkinsonian medication, at
baseline and within one week after completion of the intervention. Surface
electromyography assessments were performed by a neurologist blinded to group allocation.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Maximum Tongue Strength
    Description
      Maximum anterior and posterior tongue pressure (kPa) measured with the Iowa Oral
      Performance Instrument (IOPI). Participants pressed the bulb against the hard palate as
      forcefully as possible for 3 seconds; three trials were performed at each location and
      the highest value was used. Higher values indicate greater tongue strength.
    Time frame: Baseline and within 1 week after completion of the 4-week intervention
  -
    Measure: Tongue Endurance
    Description
      Anterior and posterior tongue endurance (seconds) measured with the IOPI as the time
      participants maintained 50% of their maximum tongue pressure. Three trials were performed
      at each location and the longest duration was used. Longer duration indicates greater
      endurance.
    Time frame: Baseline and within 1 week after completion of the 4-week intervention
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Swallowing pressure
    Description
      Typical and effortful swallowing pressures (kPa) measured with the IOPI bulb at the
      posterior tongue during 3 mL water swallows. The mean of three trials for each task was
      used. Higher values indicate greater tongue pressure during swallowing.
    Time frame: Baseline and within 1 week after completion of the 4-week intervention
  -
    Measure: Suprahyoid Muscle Activity
    Description
      Peak amplitude (µV) of suprahyoid muscle activity during typical and effortful saliva
      swallows, measured by surface electromyography. The mean of three peak amplitude values
      for each task was used. Assessments were performed by a neurologist blinded to group
      allocation.
    Time frame: Baseline and within 1 week after completion of the 4-week intervention
  -
    Measure: Swallowing-Related Quality of Life (T-SWAL-QOL)
    Description
      Turkish version of the Swallowing Quality of Life Questionnaire, a 44-item questionnaire
      assessing swallowing-related quality of life across 11 domains. Scores range from 0 to
      100, with higher scores indicating better swallowing-related quality of life.
    Time frame: Baseline and within 1 week after completion of the 4-week intervention
  -
    Measure: Dysphagia Symptom Severity (T-EAT-10)
    Description
      Turkish version of the Eating Assessment Tool, a 10-item self-report questionnaire. Each
      item is scored from 0 (no problem) to 4 (severe problem); total score ranges from 0 to
      40, with higher scores indicating more severe swallowing symptoms. A score of 3 or higher
      indicates clinically relevant swallowing difficulty.
    Time frame: Baseline and within 1 week after completion of the 4-week intervention
  -
    Measure: Functional Oral Intake (FOIS)
    Description
      Functional Oral Intake Scale, a 7-level scale rating oral intake from 1 (nothing by
      mouth) to 7 (total oral diet with no restrictions). Higher levels indicate better
      functional oral intake.
    Time frame: Baseline and within 1 week after completion of the 4-week intervention

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  Age 18 years or older

    -  Neurologist-confirmed diagnosis of idiopathic Parkinson's disease

    -  Stage 1 to 3 on the Modified Hoehn and Yahr Scale

    -  Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) score of 21 or higher

    -  Able to understand and follow study instructions

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  History of head and neck cancer

    -  Neurological disorder other than idiopathic Parkinson's disease

    -  Stage 4 or 5 on the Modified Hoehn and Yahr Scale

    -  Previous deep brain stimulation

    -  Cognitive impairment interfering with understanding study instructions

    -  Speech, language, or swallowing therapy within the previous 6 months
