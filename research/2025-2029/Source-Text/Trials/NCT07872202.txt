BRIEF SUMMARY

This interventional study aims to assess self-sufficiency in patients with Parkinson's
disease in the spa setting using the new Czech Functional Efficiency of Self-sufficiency
Test (FEST) and to validate the test in this population. It also aims to evaluate changes
in cognition, stability, fear of falling, and gait after specialized spa care and to
analyze correlations between self-sufficiency and these outcomes.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The research includes the validation of a Czech self-sufficiency test designed primarily
for patients after a stroke. The aim is to validate this tool in patients with
Parkinson's disease (PD). The research will also include the possible modification of
certain categories that are characteristic of PD patients, as well as the scoring of the
test. The study will include patients undergoing comprehensive spa treatment at a Czech
spa facility. The inclusion criteria are: 1/idiopathic PD, 2/disease severity 1-3
according to the Hoehn and Yahr scale, 3/age 50-80 years, 4/ability to walk
independently, 5/minimum score ≤76 in Adenbrooke's Cognitive Examination. Exclusion
criteria: 1/moderate to severe dementia, 2/other comorbidities affecting the gait (e.g.
post-stroke condition, vertigo,...), 3/psychiatric disorders, 4/severe sensory
impairments that would limit the implementation of the rehabilitation plan. The research
will also evaluate changes in selected parameters, namely: cognition, stability, gait,
and fear of falling. The research will also examine whether there is a correlation
between self-sufficiency and these factors. Patients will be tested upon admission to
care, then undergo a 3-week rehabilitation program, followed by a follow-up measurement.

Standardized tests were selected for research purposes:

Self-sufficiency: Barthel Index, FEST Fear of falling: Short Falls Efficacy Scale -
International Stability: Mini-Balance Evaluation Systems Test, measurement on a
stabilometric platform Cognition: Adenbrooke's Cognitive Examination (ACE-III) Gait:
6-minute walk test Descriptive statistics and correlation will be used for data
processing.

STUDY GROUPS AND INTERVENTIONS

Arm groups
  -
    Label: Parkinson's disease patients
    Type: EXPERIMENTAL
    Description
      Patients with Parkinson's disease undergo comprehensive spa treatment, which includes:
      individual therapeutic physical education, group exercises - exercises on balance balls,
      exercises in the pool, balance training on Nintendo Wii, Nordic walking, Qi Gong. In
      addition to active therapy, the treatment also utilizes the effects of natural resources
      on the body - iodine-bromine water in the form of compresses and baths, massages -
      aromatherapy, classic massages.
    Intervention names
      - Other: EXERCISE TRAINING WITH OR WITHOUT MEDICATION
Interventions
  -
    Type: OTHER
    Name: EXERCISE TRAINING WITH OR WITHOUT MEDICATION
    Description
      Patients undergo therapies led by clinical experts in the field of physical therapy.
      Individual therapies include soft tissue therapies, exercises aimed at
      increasing/maintaining range of motion, increasing muscle strength, balance, cognition,
      and self-sufficiency. The comprehensive rehabilitation stay will also include the use of
      natural healing resources - iodine-bromine brine, combined with active therapy.
    Arm group labels
      - Parkinson's disease patients
    Other names
      - Active exercising, rehabilitation care

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change in Self-Sufficiency Test Score
    Description
      Change in the total score of the Functional Efficiency of Self-Sufficiency Test from
      baseline to completion of the rehabilitation program. The total score ranges from 0 to
      100 points, with higher scores indicating greater functional independence.

      Unit of measure:

      points

      Measurement tool:

      Functional Efficiency of Self-Sufficiency Test
    Time frame: 3 weeks
  -
    Measure: Change in Addenbrooke's Cognitive Examination III (ACE-III) Score
    Description
      Change in the total Addenbrooke's Cognitive Examination III (ACE-III) score from baseline
      to completion of the rehabilitation program. The ACE-III total score ranges from 0 to 100
      points, with higher scores indicating better cognitive performance.

      Unit of measure:

      points

      Measurement tool:

      Addenbrooke's Cognitive Examination III (ACE-III)
    Time frame: 3 weeks
  -
    Measure: Change in Short Falls Efficacy Scale-International (Short FES-I) Score
    Description
      Change in the total Short Falls Efficacy Scale-International (Short FES-I) score from
      baseline to completion of the rehabilitation program. The total score ranges from 7 to 28
      points, with higher scores indicating greater concern about falling.

      Unit of measure:

      points

      Measurement tool:

      Short Falls Efficacy Scale-International (Short FES-I)
    Time frame: 3 weeks
  -
    Measure: Change in Mini-BESTest Score
    Description
      Change in the total Mini-Balance Evaluation Systems Test (Mini-BESTest) score from
      baseline to completion of the rehabilitation program. The total score ranges from 0 to 28
      points, with higher scores indicating better dynamic balance.

      Unit of measure:

      points

      Measurement tool:

      Mini-Balance Evaluation Systems Test (Mini-BESTest)
    Time frame: 3 weeks
  -
    Measure: Change in Weight-Bearing Asymmetry Measured Using a Force Platform
    Description
      Change in weight-bearing asymmetry during 30-second quiet standing from baseline to
      completion of the rehabilitation program, measured using a force platform. Weight-bearing
      asymmetry is expressed as the absolute difference between the percentage load on the left
      and right sides. Lower values indicate more symmetrical weight distribution.

      Unit of measure:

      percentage points

      Measurement tool:

      Force platform
    Time frame: 3 weeks
  -
    Measure: Change in Six-Minute Walk Test Distance
    Description
      Change in the distance walked during the Six-Minute Walk Test (6MWT) from baseline to
      completion of the rehabilitation program. A greater walking distance indicates better
      functional exercise capacity.

      Unit of measure:

      meters (m)

      Measurement tool:

      Six-Minute Walk Test (6MWT)
    Time frame: 3 weeks
