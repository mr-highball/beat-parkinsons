BRIEF SUMMARY

The goal of this clinical trial is to learn if the Ronnie Gardiner Method (RGM) works to
improve cognitive functions in adults with Parkinson's disease (early to mid-stage). It
will also learn about the secondary effects on speech, mobility, balance, and mood.

The main questions it aims to answer are:

  -  Does a 10-week RGM intervention improve cognitive aspects, speech (indirectly
     through improved breathing), mobility, and balance in participants with Parkinson's
     disease?

  -  How do participants experience the intensity, difficulty, and emotional impact of
     the RGM sessions?

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The Ronnie Gardiner Method (RGM) is a cheerful, structured, multi-sensory brain exercise
method that utilizes rhythm and music to stimulate movement, speech, and cognition. RGM
makes playful use of rhythm, music, movement, speech, hearing, sight, and touch. During
practice, the participant reads the symbols and 'translates' them into the corresponding
movement, which is performed to the rhythm of the music. Preliminary research suggests
RGM may have added value in promoting mobility, cognition, and well-being in neurological
patients (https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09638288.2024.2392037#d1e1192).

The RGM exercise series for which participants are registering is integrated in the "Fit
en Actief group classes" organized by the Ghent Flemish Parkinson League - in
collaboration with Ghent University Rehabilitation Sciences and Physiotherapy.

The classes will take place from October to December 2026 (10 weeks), with exercises
taking place once a week for 1.5 hours (on Thursdays) in the practical classrooms of the
Rehabilitation Sciences and Physiotherapy program (UH Ghent, B3). In addition to the
exercise sessions themselves, participants will be asked to perform home exercises twice
a week for 15 minutes.

Before and after the exercise series, participants will be invited to take a pre- and
post-test including administrative data, cognition, motor symptoms, balance, QOL. The
training process (home exercises, experiences) will be inventoried by temporary
questionnaires.

Afterwards, participants will be surveyed regarding changes in medication during the
exercise series, changes in the frequency or content of (physio)therapy, and perceived
changes in attention, memory, and planning.

Interventions
  -
    Type: OTHER
    Name: Ronnie Gardiner Method
    Description
      1. Warm-up (10 min): light rhythmic movements to music; activation of coordination and
           attention.

        2. RGM Block 1 (30 min): cognitive-motor exercises incorporating color, sound, and
           movement elements (bilateral).

        3. Strength or interval segment (10 min): short strength/conditioning exercises (Tabata
           style) alternating between 20 seconds of activity and 20 seconds of rest. The
           exercises focus on strengthening postural muscles, improving rotation, encouraging
           weight shifting, and initiating movement quickly (the 4 principles of the PWR!
           concept).

        4. 10-minute break

        5. RGM Block 2 (30 min): cognitive-motor exercises incorporating color, sound, and
           movement elements (bilateral).

        6. Cool-down (10 min): relaxation, breathing, evaluation.

      Cognitive component During the sessions, executive functions, attention, and processing
      speed are challenged and practiced through dual-task situations, pattern recognition, and
      auditory response tasks.

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  diagnosis of idiopathic Parkinson's disease

    -  safe, independent mobility and participation (Hoehn and Yahr stages 1-3)

    -  a stable dopaminergic medication regimen for at least 4 weeks prior to study
       participation

    -  willingness and ability to participate in a 10-week intervention program

    -  willingness to participate in the study (signing of informed consent)

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  Presence of other neurological or severe psychiatric conditions that could interfere
       with participation or the interpretation of results (e.g., substance use disorder,
       severe depression, psychosis)

    -  Severe visual or hearing impairments that limit the ability to follow group
       instructions as determined by a physician.

    -  Physical limitations that inhibit safe participation in moderate-to-vigorous
       exercise or group rehabilitation.
