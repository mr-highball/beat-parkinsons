BRIEF SUMMARY

A promising approach to combat the protein aggregation, oxidative stress, and
neuroinflammation occurring with PD is consistent aerobic exercise. Dr. Alberts' recently
completed Phase III Randomized Clinical Trial2,3 demonstrating that a specific aerobic
exercise prescription was effective at slowing the progression of motor symptoms in mild
to moderate Parkinson's disease patients. This project extends our positive findings by
evaluating the effectiveness of this PD-specific exercise intervention in individuals
identified as high-risk of converting to PD. The underlying hypothesis is that aerobic
exercise can delay or prevent motor and cognitive decline associated with PD, as aerobic
exercise is known to reduce oxidative stress and neuroinflammation and enhance
mitochondria function.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Parkinson's disease is associated bradykinesia, resting tremor, rigidity and postural
instability. Currently, there is no cure for PD. However, the field is transitioning from
symptom management to "disease modification", developing treatments that slow or stop the
underlying damage to neurons. It is theorized that disease modification approaches would
be most effective very early in the disease, even before symptoms are visible, due to the
potential for neuroplasticity at the stage of the disease when cell death is minimal.
Identifying individuals at risk for developing a disease is challenging.

Our 2024 study, Explainable machine learning for predicting conversion to neurological
disease: Results from 52,939 medical records,1 represents a major step forward in using
routine healthcare data to identify risk of converting to neurodegenerative disease years
before a formal clinical diagnosis. Briefly, by analyzing more than 52,000 electronic
health records we developed a model to predict who is likely to convert to PD. The
project is unique as it did not simply evaluate a "snapshot" of a patient's current
health; it analyzed their longitudinal history to see how aspects of health changed over
time. The models were effective at identifying risk even years before symptoms became
obvious, suggesting that PD does not have a silent phase, rather changes are occurring
that may be subtle and not observable through routine primary care visits. The model is
currently active in EPIC in three Cleveland Clinic Primary Care locations.

One hundred individuals, otherwise healthy between 55-65 years of age, who are identified
as at high risk for converting to PD based on our Explainable AI models will be
recruited. Following a 30-day control period to establish baseline physical activity,
individuals will be randomized to a UCC group (e.g., control group) or an AEX group. The
control group will not engage in any formal intervention. Those randomized to AEX will
complete aerobic exercise three times a week, 30+ minutes, for 24 months. The aerobic
exercise intervention will be completed in the home of the individual using a
commercially available Wi-Fi connected exercise cycle. All participants will complete
Comprehensive Assessments (Baseline, 12, 24 months) and Interim assessments (6 and 18
months).

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    1. Adult identified as high-risk for developing PD within the next 24 months

    2. Age 55-65 years

    3. Demonstrate the ability to safely mount and dismount the Peloton stationary cycle

    4. In-home WiFi (Peloton system requires WiFi to transmit exercise data)

  Exclusion Criteria:

    1. Diagnosis of neurocognitive impairment that compromises one's ability to provide
       informed consent.

    2. A musculoskeletal issue (arthritis, osteoporosis, back problem) that would limit
       one's ability to engage in aerobic exercise

    3. Neurological disease (i.e. multiple sclerosis, PD, stroke, etc.)

    4. Current cardiac arrhythmia

    5. Greater than 3 weeks of consecutive time away from home that would render the
       individual unable to complete the cycling protocol

    6. Recommendation for medical clearance using the American College of Sports Medicine
       (ACSM) Preparticipation Health Screen4

         -  If the ACSM screen recommends medical clearance, the subject must obtain
            medical clearance by their health care provider prior to participation.

         -  Those who choose not to obtain physician clearance will not be eligible for
            participation. Those who do not receive physician clearance for high intensity
            exercise will not be eligible.
