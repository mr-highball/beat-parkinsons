BRIEF SUMMARY

The primary objective of this study is to determine whether data acquired from a remote
monitoring approach can reliably identify and track motor symptoms in prodromal PD and
clinical PD.

The study is a one-year longitudinal biomarker study recruiting idiopathic RBD patients,
Parkinson's Disease patient and healthy controls. All participants receive a smartphone
with the Roche PD Mobile Application, complete a number of active tests daily and their
movements will be remotely monitored. Participants will undergo additional neurological
exams and DaT-SPECT and MRI imaging in the study.

A second objective of this study will be to validate an immunohistochemistry skin biopsy
assay for the detection of pathological alpha-synuclein diagnostic of PD, in the
peripheral nerves of the skin.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Digital Biomarkers
    Description
      Changes in continuous sensor-based measurements derived from Roche PD MobileApplication,
      with active testing and passive monitoring in idiopathic RBD and clinical PD (1 month in
      Healthy Controls)
    Time frame: 12 months
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: alpha-synuclein deposition in skin biopsy
    Description
      Frequency of pathological alpha-synuclein deposition in the skin biopsy assay in
      idiopathic RBD and clinical PD and HC (optional study procedure)
    Time frame: 12 months
  -
    Measure: Changes in sensor-based measurements and passive monitoring
    Description
      Relationship between changes in continuous sensor-based measurements derived from Roche
      PD Mobile Application active testing and passive monitoring over 12 months in idiopathic
      RBD, clinical PD and HC and changes in clinical assessments
    Time frame: 12 months
  -
    Measure: Correlation of sensor-based measurements and imaging outcomes
    Description
      For idiopathic RBD patients only, correlation between sensor-based measurements derived
      from Roche PD Mobile Application active testing and passive monitoring and changes in MRI
      and DaT-SPECT outcome measures.
    Time frame: 12 months
