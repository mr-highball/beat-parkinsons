BRIEF SUMMARY

This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of tTIS treatment in improving motor function in
patients with Parkinson's disease (PD), as well as its effects on mood, activities of
daily living, sleep, cognition, and other functional domains. It also seeks to assess
differences in treatment response among PD subtypes (tremor-dominant, rigidity-dominant,
and mixed) and across disease stages (early stage, Hoehn and Yahr [H&Y] stages 1-2, and
mid-to-late stage, H&Y stages 2.5-3). The study will enroll eligible PD patients and
evaluate their condition using the Movement Disorder Society-Unified Parkinson's Disease
Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS), the 39-item Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire (PDQ-39), the
Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), the Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAMA), the
Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD), and a visual analog scale (VAS) for fatigue.
Assessments will be performed before routine tTIS treatment, immediately after treatment,
and at two and four weeks post-treatment, in order to analyze the therapeutic effect of
tTIS in PD. This is an observational study without any intervention; all enrolled
participants will receive standard medical care and health education.

STUDY GROUPS AND INTERVENTIONS

Interventions
  -
    Type: DEVICE
    Name: transcranial temporal interference stimulation (tTIS)
    Description
      Participants in this group will receive transcranial temporal interference stimulation
      targeting the subthalamic nucleus using a frequency pair of 2000 Hz and 2130 Hz.
      Stimulation will be delivered twice daily at the same time for 30 minutes per session
      over five consecutive days（only once on the 5th day）. Stimulation intensity will be
      individually titrated starting from a low level and gradually increased to the maximum
      tolerable level. All treatments will be administered during the participant's medication
      "on" state.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change in Score on Part III (Motor Examination) of the Movement Disorder Society-Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS)
    Description
      The motor function of patients was assessed using Part III of the Movement Disorder
      Society-Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS-III), which has a total score
      range of 0 to 132. In this scale, a higher score indicates greater severity of motor
      symptoms and therefore a worse outcome, with 0 representing no impairment and 132
      representing the most severe impairment. Changes in the score reflect the degree of
      improvement in motor function.
    Time frame: at baseline(before treatment), immediately after the patient receives tTIS treatment, and and at the two-week and four-week follow-ups after the completion of treatment.
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: MDS-UPDRS Scale Part I Non-Motor Function Score Changes
    Description
      The MDS-UPDRS Scale Part I Non-Motor Function score was used to assess the patient's
      Cognitive impairment, hallucinations and psychiatric symptoms, depression, apathy,
      impulse control disorders/dopamine dysregulation syndrome, sleep disorders, pain and
      other sensory symptoms, and urinary symptoms, with changes in the score reflecting the
      degree of improvement in those functions. The score for Part I ranges from 0 to 52
      points. A higher score indicates greater impairment or more severe non-motor symptoms.
    Time frame: at baseline(before treatment), immediately after the patient receives tTIS treatment, and at the two-week and four-week follow-ups after the completion of treatment.
  -
    Measure: MDS-UPDRS Scale Part II: Activities of Daily Living Score Changes
    Description
      The MDS-UPDRS Scale Part II score was used to assess the patient's Activities of Daily
      Living, with changes in the score reflecting the degree of improvement in daily
      functions. The score for Part II ranges from 0 to 52 points. A higher score indicates
      greater impairment in daily activities.
    Time frame: at baseline(before treatment), immediately after the patient receives tTIS treatment, and at the two-week and four-week follow-ups after the completion of treatment.
  -
    Measure: MDS-UPDRS Scale Part IV: Motor Fluctuations, Dyskinesias, and Dystonia Score Changes
    Description
      The MDS-UPDRS Scale Part IV score was used to assess the patient's Motor Fluctuations,
      Dyskinesias, and Dystonia. The score for Part IV ranges from 0 to 24 points. A higher
      score indicates more severe motor complications caused by long-term levodopa therapy.
    Time frame: at baseline(before treatment), immediately after the patient receives tTIS treatment, and at the two-week and four-week follow-ups after the completion of treatment.
  -
    Measure: Change in Score on the 39-item Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire (PDQ-39)
    Description
      The 39-item Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire (PDQ-39) is used to assess the
      disease-specific health-related quality of life in patients with Parkinson's disease. The
      summary index score ranges from 0 to 100 points. A higher score indicates a poorer
      quality of life.
    Time frame: at baseline(before treatment), immediately after the patient receives tTIS treatment, and at the two-week and four-week follow-ups after the completion of treatment.
  -
    Measure: Change in Score on the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD)
    Description
      Change in Score on the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD) is used to evaluate the
      severity of depressive symptoms. This assessment consists of 17 items with a total score
      range from 0 to 54 points; a higher score indicates a worse outcome. With common clinical
      thresholds (e.g., 0-7 = Normal, 8-16 = Mild Depression, 17-23 = Moderate Depression, ≥24
      = Severe Depression).
    Time frame: at baseline(before treatment), immediately after the patient receives tTIS treatment, and at the two-week and four-week follow-ups after the completion of treatment.
  -
    Measure: Change in Score on the Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAMA)
    Description
      The Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale (HAMA) is a 14-item scale to evaluate the severity of
      anxiety symptoms, where each item is rated on a scale from 0 to 4. The HAMA total score
      ranges from 0 to 56, with lower scores indicating fewer anxiety symptoms.
    Time frame: at baseline(before treatment), immediately after the patient receives tTIS treatment, and at the two-week and four-week follow-ups after the completion of treatment.
  -
    Measure: Change in Score on the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI)
    Description
      the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) is used to assess sleep quality and
      disturbances over the past month. With changes in the score reflecting the multiple
      factors of daily function. Each component is scored from 0 to 3. The global PSQI score is
      the sum of the 7 component scores, ranging from 0 to 21 points. A higher total score
      indicates poorer sleep quality
    Time frame: at baseline(before treatment), immediately after the patient receives tTIS treatment, and at the two-week and four-week follow-ups after the completion of treatment.

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    1. Age ≥18 years, any gender.

    2. Diagnosis of idiopathic Parkinson's disease according to the Movement Disorder
       Society (MDS) clinical diagnostic criteria: the patient must (a) exhibit
       parkinsonism (bradykinesia plus rest tremor and/or rigidity); (b) not meet any of
       the MDS absolute exclusion criteria; and (c) have at least two MDS supportive
       criteria.

    3. Disease duration ≥1 year, with stable clinical status.

    4. Hoehn and Yahr stage 1.5-3; on a stable dose of levodopa or other dopaminergic
       medications, with a documented response to levodopa; treatment regimen remains
       unchanged throughout the study period.

    5. The patient is scheduled to receive tTIS treatment.

    6. The subject or legally authorized representative is able to provide written informed
       consent.

  Exclusion Criteria:

    1. Severe cognitive impairment, defined as a Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score
       ≤17 for illiterate individuals, ≤20 for those with primary school education, and ≤24
       for those with junior high school education or above; poor compliance due to
       dementia and/or inability to provide informed consent.

    2. History of severe psychiatric disorder, or a Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD)
       score >24.

    3. History of use of antipsychotics, antidepressants, or other medications that may
       affect dopamine levels.

    4. History of seizures within the past 1 year, or a family history of epilepsy.

    5. Inability to complete follow-up visits as required during the study.
