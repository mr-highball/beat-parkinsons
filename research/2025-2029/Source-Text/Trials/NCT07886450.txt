BRIEF SUMMARY

The goal of this prospective observational study is to investigate whether
oculomotor-pupillary abnormalities detected using a novel edge-AI eye-tracking device can
serve as objective and quantitative biomarkers for the early screening and clinical
evaluation of Parkinson's disease (PD). The study will include individuals with
early-to-moderate PD and healthy controls. The main question it aims to answer is:

Can oculomotor-pupillary abnormalities detected by a novel edge-AI eye-tracking device
serve as objective and quantitative biomarkers for the early screening of Parkinson's
disease?

Researchers will compare oculomotor-pupillary parameters between participants with PD and
healthy controls and evaluate the ability of these parameters to distinguish PD from
healthy controls. Participants with PD will also be followed for 12 months to assess
longitudinal changes in eye-tracking measures and their associations with clinical
changes.

Participants will:

  1. Complete standardized eye-tracking tasks to assess oculomotor and pupillary function
     using the study eye-tracking device.

  2. Undergo clinical assessments of motor function, cognitive function, and non-motor
     symptoms.

  3. For participants with PD, attend follow-up assessments over 12 months to evaluate
     changes in eye-tracking and clinical measures.

The study will also evaluate the feasibility and clinical application of the
independently developed edge-AI eye-tracking system and develop and validate lightweight
real-time models for PD classification and prediction of disease progression.

Interventions
  -
    Type: DEVICE
    Name: Edge-AI Eye-Tracking Assessment
    Description
      Participants will undergo standardized oculomotor and pupillary assessments using the
      study-developed edge-AI eye-tracking device. The assessment includes standardized visual
      tasks designed to evaluate fixation, saccadic eye movements, smooth pursuit, and
      pupillary responses. Eye movement and pupil data will be recorded and analyzed to
      identify oculomotor-pupillary biomarkers associated with Parkinson's disease. The device
      is used for research assessment only and does not alter participants' routine clinical
      treatment.
    Arm group labels
      - Healthy Control Group
      - Parkinson's Disease Group

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Differences in mean prosaccade gain between early-to-moderate PD patients and healthy controls.
    Description
      Prosaccade gain is defined as the ratio of the primary saccade amplitude to the target
      displacement amplitude, measured using a head-mounted infrared eye-tracking device. The
      mean gain across valid trials will be calculated for each participant. Mean prosaccade
      gain will be compared between patients with early-to-moderate PD and healthy controls.
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Differences in antisaccade directional error rate between early-to-moderate PD patients and healthy controls.
    Description
      Antisaccade directional error rate is defined as the percentage of valid antisaccade
      trials in which the initial saccade is incorrectly directed toward the visual target
      rather than in the opposite direction, measured using a head-mounted infrared
      eye-tracking device. The error rate will be calculated for each participant as the number
      of directional error trials divided by the total number of valid antisaccade trials,
      multiplied by 100. Antisaccade directional error rate will be compared between patients
      with early-to-moderate PD and healthy controls.
    Time frame: Baseline
  -
    Measure: Differences in mean prosaccade peak velocity between early-to-moderate PD patients and healthy controls.
    Description
      Prosaccade peak velocity is defined as the maximum angular eye velocity during the
      primary prosaccade, measured in degrees per second (°/s) using a head-mounted infrared
      eye-tracking device. The mean peak velocity across valid trials will be calculated for
      each participant. Mean prosaccade peak velocity will be compared between patients with
      early-to-moderate PD and healthy controls.
    Time frame: Baseline
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Correlation Between Changes in Prosaccade Gain and MDS-UPDRS Part III Scores Over 12 Months
    Description
      The correlation between changes in mean prosaccade gain and changes in motor symptom
      severity will be assessed in patients with PD over 12 months. Mean prosaccade gain will
      be measured using a head-mounted infrared eye-tracking device, while motor symptom
      severity will be assessed using the Movement Disorder Society-Unified Parkinson's Disease
      Rating Scale Part III (MDS-UPDRS III). Changes in both measures will be calculated from
      baseline to 12 months, and their association will be quantified using a correlation
      coefficient (r).
    Time frame: Baseline to 12 months
  -
    Measure: Correlation Between Changes in Antisaccade Directional Error Rate and MoCA Scores Over 12 Months
    Description
      The correlation between changes in antisaccade directional error rate and changes in
      cognitive function will be assessed in patients with PD over 12 months. Antisaccade
      directional error rate is defined as the percentage of valid antisaccade trials in which
      the initial saccade is incorrectly directed toward the visual target, measured using a
      head-mounted infrared eye-tracking device. Cognitive function will be assessed using the
      Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA). Changes in both measures from baseline to 12 months
      will be calculated, and their association will be quantified using a correlation
      coefficient (r).
    Time frame: Baseline to 12 months
  -
    Measure: Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUC) for PD Classification
    Description
      The diagnostic performance of an edge-AI model based on oculomotor-pupillary features
      measured using a head-mounted infrared eye-tracking device will be evaluated for
      distinguishing patients with PD from healthy controls. Model performance will be assessed
      using AUC, with values ranging from 0 to 1, where higher values indicate better
      discriminative performance.
    Time frame: Baseline

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

  PD Group:

    -  Age ≥ 45 years old.

    -  Diagnosed with Parkinson's disease (PD) by a neurologist.

    -  Hoehn-Yahr (H&Y) is staged as stage 1-3 (early-to-moderate PD).

    -  Newly diagnosed or drug-naive patients will be given priority, or patients who are
       already on medication but can stop taking it for at least 12 hours before testing
       ("Off" status).

    -  Participants who need to take medication must be accompanied by a caregiver, bring
       their own medication, and take it immediately after the assessment.

    -  Informed consent and voluntary participation in this study.

  HC Group:

    -  Age ≥ 45 years old.

    -  No history of neurological or mental illness or prodromal symptoms.

    -  No family history of PD or prodromal symptoms of PD.

    -  Not taking any medications that may affect eye movement or nervous system function.

    -  Informed consent and voluntary participation in this study.

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  Habitual visual acuity less than 20/63 (LogMAR 0.5) in either eye.

    -  Combined with eye diseases that affect eye movement or vision, such as severe
       cataracts, glaucoma, macular degeneration, oculomotor paralysis, etc.

    -  Other neurological disorders, such as progressive supranuclear palsy, multiple
       system atrophy, Huntington's disease, cerebellar disorders, etc.

    -  Illiteracy or severe cognitive dysfunction.

    -  Severe mental diseases, such as major depressive disorder or schizophrenia.

    -  Unable to cooperate in completing the eye-tracking test.

Study population
  The study population will consist of adults aged 45 years or older, including
  participants with early-to-moderate Parkinson's disease and age-comparable healthy
  controls. Participants with Parkinson's disease will be recruited from clinical
  populations and will undergo baseline eye-tracking and clinical assessments followed by
  longitudinal assessments over 12 months. Healthy controls without Parkinson's disease or
  other major neurological disorders will undergo baseline assessments for comparison with
  the Parkinson's disease group. All participants must be able to complete the required
  eye-tracking tasks and clinical assessments.
