BRIEF SUMMARY

This prospective, randomized, assessor-blinded, parallel-group clinical trial aims to
compare the effectiveness of early add-on opicapone versus levodopa dose escalation in
patients with Parkinson's disease experiencing emerging motor fluctuations.

A total of 46 patients are randomly assigned (1:1) to receive either opicapone 50 mg once
daily in addition to their existing dopaminergic treatment or an additional daily dose of
levodopa/dopa-decarboxylase inhibitor (100-200 mg).

Treatment effects are assessed over approximately four weeks through standardized
clinical evaluations, patient-reported outcome measures, and continuous home-based
monitoring using wearable sensors. The primary outcome is the change in daily OFF time
objectively estimated by wearable technology. Secondary outcomes include gait performance
and variability, motor symptom severity, patient-perceived improvement, quality of life,
and treatment safety.

The study aims to determine whether early COMT inhibition provides greater control of
motor fluctuations than levodopa dose escalation and to investigate the utility of
wearable-derived digital biomarkers for evaluating treatment response under real-world
conditions.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Motor fluctuations represent a major complication of Parkinson's disease (PD) and may
emerge during the early stages of levodopa treatment. These fluctuations commonly
manifest as wearing-off episodes, characterized by the reappearance of motor symptoms
before the next scheduled levodopa dose. Conventional therapeutic approaches include
increasing or fractionating levodopa doses or introducing adjunctive treatments aimed at
prolonging levodopa availability.

Catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT) inhibitors, such as opicapone, may improve the
stability of dopaminergic stimulation by increasing levodopa bioavailability and
prolonging its therapeutic effect. However, the comparative effectiveness of early COMT
inhibition and levodopa dose escalation on motor fluctuations and everyday motor
performance remains incompletely established. In addition, conventional clinical scales
may not adequately capture the temporal variability of motor symptoms during daily
activities.

This prospective, randomized, assessor-blinded, parallel-group study was designed to
compare early add-on opicapone with levodopa dose escalation in patients with PD
experiencing emerging motor fluctuations. The study also investigates the usefulness of
wearable-derived digital biomarkers for objectively quantifying treatment-related changes
in real-world motor performance.

Forty-six patients with idiopathic PD and emerging wearing-off symptoms were enrolled at
the Movement Disorders outpatient clinic of Sapienza University of Rome, Italy.
Participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio using computer-generated allocation to one of
two treatment strategies:

Opicapone group: Opicapone 50 mg once daily added to the existing dopaminergic treatment.

Levodopa escalation group: An additional 100-200 mg/day of levodopa combined with a
dopa-decarboxylase inhibitor, administered as an additional dose or distributed across
existing daily administrations.

Treatment adjustments were permitted during the initial two weeks according to clinical
response and tolerability. Clinical assessments were performed by a movement disorders
specialist blinded to treatment allocation.

Participants underwent standardized neurological and clinical evaluations at baseline and
after approximately four weeks of treatment. Assessments included motor symptom severity,
motor fluctuations, global clinical improvement, patient-perceived treatment response,
and health-related quality of life.

Objective motor monitoring was performed using the STAT-ON wearable sensor (Sense4Care,
Barcelona, Spain), positioned at the waist. Participants wore the device during their
usual daily activities for seven consecutive days at baseline and again after treatment.
The device uses accelerometry and validated signal-processing algorithms to estimate
medication-related motor states and quantify spatiotemporal gait characteristics,
including step length, gait velocity, cadence, and their variability.

The primary objective was to compare the change in wearable-derived daily OFF time
between treatment groups from baseline to the four-week follow-up. Secondary objectives
included evaluating changes in gait performance and variability, clinical motor severity,
wearing-off symptoms, patient-reported improvement, quality of life, and treatment
tolerability.

Safety assessments included neurological and physical examinations, vital signs,
concomitant medication review, and monitoring of treatment-emergent adverse events.

The study was designed to investigate whether early COMT inhibition offers advantages
over levodopa dose escalation in controlling emerging motor fluctuations and improving
motor stability during everyday activities. Furthermore, it aims to explore the
complementary role of wearable-derived digital biomarkers alongside conventional clinical
and patient-reported assessments in evaluating therapeutic response in Parkinson's
disease.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change in Wearable-Derived Daily OFF Time
    Description
      Percentage change in daily time spent in the OFF motor state from baseline to the
      four-week follow-up, objectively assessed using the STAT-ON wearable sensor during seven
      consecutive days of home-based monitoring. The device automatically classifies motor
      states using accelerometry-based algorithms. OFF time is expressed as a percentage of
      daily monitored time. The outcome is calculated as the difference between follow-up and
      baseline OFF time percentages, with negative values indicating a reduction in OFF time
      and clinical improvement.
    Time frame: Baseline and Week 4 (7 consecutive days of wearable monitoring at each time point)
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change in Step Length Variability
    Description
      Change from baseline to Week 4 in the coefficient of variation of step length (%),
      measured using wearable sensors during daily-life monitoring. Lower values indicate more
      consistent gait performance.
    Time frame: Baseline and Week 4 (7 consecutive days of wearable monitoring at each time point)
  -
    Measure: Change in Gait Velocity Variability
    Description
      Change from baseline to Week 4 in the coefficient of variation of gait velocity (%),
      measured using wearable sensors during daily-life monitoring. Lower values indicate more
      consistent gait performance.
    Time frame: Baseline and Week 4 (7 consecutive days of wearable monitoring at each time point)
  -
    Measure: 3. Change in MDS-UPDRS Part III Score
    Description
      Change from baseline to Week 4 in motor symptom severity assessed using Part III of the
      Movement Disorder Society-Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale. Scores range from 0
      to 132, with higher scores indicating greater motor impairment.
    Time frame: Baseline and Week 4
  -
    Measure: Change in WOQ-19 Score
    Description
      Change from baseline to Week 4 in wearing-off symptoms assessed using the 19-item
      Wearing-Off Questionnaire. Scores range from 0 to 19, with higher scores indicating more
      wearing-off symptoms.
    Time frame: Baseline and Week 4
  -
    Measure: Change in PDQ-8 Score
    Description
      Change from baseline to Week 4 in health-related quality of life assessed using the
      8-item Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire. The Summary Index ranges from 0 to 100, with
      higher scores indicating poorer quality of life.
    Time frame: Baseline and Week 4
  -
    Measure: Patient Global Impression of Change (PGI-C)
    Description
      Patient-reported global change in clinical condition at Week 4 compared with baseline,
      assessed using the Patient Global Impression of Change scale. Scores range from 1 (very
      much improved) to 7 (very much worse), with higher scores indicating a worse outcome.
    Time frame: Baseline and Week 4

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  Diagnosis of idiopathic Parkinson's disease according to established clinical
       diagnostic criteria.

    -  Presence of wearing-off motor fluctuations, including early morning akinesia, with
       at least 1 hour of daily awake OFF time persisting for a period ranging from 4 weeks
       to 2 years.

    -  Stable treatment with levodopa/dopa-decarboxylase inhibitor (DDCI), administered 3-4
       times daily, for at least 30 days before enrollment.

    -  Concomitant treatment with dopamine agonists or monoamine oxidase-B inhibitors
       permitted.

    -  Levodopa equivalent daily dose (LEDD) ≥300 mg.

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  Dementia, defined as a Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score below 24.

    -  Diagnosis of atypical parkinsonism.

    -  Inability to walk independently.

    -  Known intolerance to catechol-O-methyltransferase (COMT) inhibitors.

    -  Previous treatment with COMT inhibitors.
