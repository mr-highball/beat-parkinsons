BRIEF SUMMARY

Parkinson's disease can reduce facial expression and change the voice, making it harder
to communicate emotions. This study examined whether practising facial and vocal
imitation through speech and singing could improve facial expression and emotional
communication in people with Parkinson's disease.

Participants were assigned by chance to one of two 13-week programs. In Singing Imitation
Therapy, participants watched and listened to recordings of emotional speech and song and
copied the facial and vocal expressions. In Singing Observation Therapy, participants
watched and listened to the recordings without being instructed to copy them.

The main hypothesis was that imitation would lead to greater improvements in facial
expression and automatic facial responses to other people's emotional expressions than
observation alone.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Reduced spontaneous facial expression, or hypomimia, can affect emotional communication
and social interaction in Parkinson's disease. Facial mimicry refers to subtle, automatic
facial muscle responses that occur when observing another person's emotional expressions.
The study investigated whether deliberate practice of emotional facial and vocal
expression could improve this spontaneous responsiveness.

The intervention was based on the proposed relationship between perceiving emotional
expressions and producing the associated facial and vocal movements. Repeated intentional
imitation was hypothesized to strengthen facial motor responsiveness and support
expressive function. The observation comparator was included to examine whether active
imitation offered additional benefits beyond repeated exposure to emotional speech and
song. The broader rationale was that improved expressive function might support emotional
communication and well-being.

Additional audio and video recordings were collected for exploratory analyses of vocal
production, facial movement, and facial-vocal coordination. Vocal assessments using LSVT
assessment tasks were recorded at Week 1, Week 13, and two months after treatment
completion. Therapy sessions were video-recorded weekly during Weeks 1-13.

The original protocol proposed exploratory analyses of vocal frequency and intensity,
including their ranges; speech rate; voice perturbation measures, including jitter and
shimmer; formant frequencies; and harmonics-to-noise ratio. Planned facial movement
analyses included displacement, velocity, and acceleration, together with analyses of
coordination between facial movement and vocal acoustics.

Further analysis of instructed facial imitation is proposed within the Singing Imitation
Therapy group because the treatment groups performed different activities during therapy.
These analyses would characterise imitation performance and changes within that group
over time. Additional features and computational methods selected after trial completion
will be distinguished from those specified in the original protocol.

The trial was completed before public registration. It was retrospectively registered on
the Open Science Framework (OSF) on May 25, 2026. The OSF record documents the study
design and a later plan for longitudinal treatment-effect analyses comparing Singing
Imitation Therapy with Singing Observation Therapy. The present ClinicalTrials.gov record
provides an additional retrospective registration of the same completed trial.

Design info
  Allocation: RANDOMIZED
  Intervention model: PARALLEL
  Intervention model description
    Two-arm, parallel-group randomised controlled trial comparing Singing Imitation Therapy
    with Singing Observation Therapy, an active comparator. Twenty-eight participants with
    Parkinson's disease were allocated individually using a coin toss, with a target
    allocation ratio of 1:1 and a maximum of 14 participants per group. Once one group
    reached this maximum, subsequent participants were assigned to the other group.
    Allocation was not stratified. Twenty caregivers provided separate consent for
    questionnaire assessments; they were not independently randomised and did not receive a
    study intervention.
  Primary purpose: TREATMENT
  Masking info
    Masking: DOUBLE
    Masking description
      Participants were masked to whether their intervention was experimental or comparator and
      to the superiority hypothesis. They received instructions about their assigned activities
      and potential benefits without disclosure of the Singing Imitation Therapy or Singing
      Observation Therapy labels or experimental/control status. The MDS-UPDRS assessor was
      blinded to treatment allocation. The researcher recording surface electromyography was
      not involved in therapy delivery but inadvertently learned the allocation of a few early
      participants. Therapists knew treatment allocation but were unaware of the study
      hypotheses, as were recruitment and randomization personnel. Researchers responsible for
      data processing and statistical analysis were not blinded to group assignment.
    Who masked
      - PARTICIPANT

Interventions
  -
    Type: BEHAVIORAL
    Name: Singing Imitation Therapy
    Description
      Participants received approximately 20-minute, therapist-delivered home sessions once
      weekly for 13 weeks. Sessions used 43-45 emotional speech and song videos, with
      metronome-supported song presented before speech. Each video was presented twice:
      participants observed the first presentation and synchronously imitated the actor's
      facial and vocal expression during the second. After each sequence, participants
      identified the emotion and rated its intensity on a 1-5 scale. Therapist feedback
      encouraged facial and vocal expressiveness. Participants received DVDs of
      song-with-metronome videos for weekly home practice using the same imitation
      instructions; practice completion was not formally monitored.
    Arm group labels
      - Singing Imitation Therapy
  -
    Type: BEHAVIORAL
    Name: Singing Observation Therapy
    Description
      Participants received approximately 20-minute, therapist-delivered home sessions once
      weekly for 13 weeks, matched to the imitation intervention in structure and duration.
      Sessions used 43-45 emotional speech and song videos, with metronome-supported song
      presented before speech. Each video was presented twice. Participants watched and
      listened without instructed imitation, attending to the actor's facial and vocal emotion
      and its intensity. After each sequence, participants identified the emotion and rated its
      intensity on a 1-5 scale. Therapist feedback directed attention to the actor's voice or
      facial expression. Participants received DVDs of song-with-metronome videos for weekly
      home practice using the same observation instructions; practice completion was not
      formally monitored.
    Arm group labels
      - Singing Observation Therapy

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change in Standardized Facial Muscle Activity From Week 1 to Week 13
    Description
      Change in standardized facial muscle activity from Week 1 to Week 13. Surface
      electromyography is recorded from the zygomaticus major, corrugator supercilii, and
      medial frontalis during emotional speech and song observation. Mean activity 1-4 seconds
      after stimulus onset is expressed as percentage change from a 2-second prestimulus
      baseline, then standardized within participant and muscle, then within participant across
      muscles. Both recordings at each visit are included. Activity is retained by muscle and
      stimulus emotion. Analyses assess overall responsiveness across emotional trials,
      excluding neutral/calm, and emotion-congruent responses: happy versus neutral/calm for
      zygomaticus; negative emotions (sad, angry, fearful, disgust) versus neutral/calm for
      corrugator; and surprise versus neutral/calm for frontalis. Values are unitless with no
      fixed limits; positive changes indicate increased activity.

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

  Participants with Parkinson's disease:

    -  Clinical diagnosis of idiopathic Parkinson's disease.

    -  Aged 50-75 years at enrolment.

    -  Parkinson's disease diagnosed at age 50 years or older.

    -  At least eight years of English-speaking experience.
