BRIEF SUMMARY

This is a Phase 1 clinical trial to evaluate the safety and tolerability of a new cell
therapy called NCR201. The study will enroll adults with moderate-to-advanced primary
Parkinson's disease who have previously undergone deep brain stimulation (DBS) surgery.

NCR201 is an investigational cell therapy derived from hypoimmune human induced
pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs). Participants will receive a one-time surgical injection
of NCR201 directly into a specific brain region (the putamen) on both sides. The study
will use a standard "3+3" design to test two different doses: a low-dose group (4 million
cells per side) and a high-dose group (8 million cells per side).

The primary goal of this study is to check for any severe, dose-limiting side effects
within the first 8 weeks after surgery, and to determine the maximum tolerated dose or
the recommended dose for future Phase 2 studies. Researchers will also look for any
initial signs of clinical benefit. Participants will be closely monitored through regular
safety and symptom assessments for up to 96 weeks, with an optional long-term safety
follow-up lasting an additional 3 to 5 years.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Parkinson's disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disease caused by the loss of
dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra of the brain. Current treatments, including
oral levodopa medication and deep brain stimulation (DBS), can only relieve symptoms but
cannot restore the damaged dopaminergic neurons. For patients with moderate-to-advanced
primary PD whose symptoms continue to progress after DBS surgery, there remains a
critical unmet clinical need.

NCR201 is an off-the-shelf, hypoimmune dopaminergic progenitor cell product derived from
human induced pluripotent stem cells. Preclinical studies demonstrated that NCR201 cells
can survive in the brain, differentiate into functional dopaminergic neurons, and secrete
dopamine, with no evidence of tumorigenic risk. Previous investigator-initiated clinical
data showed favorable safety signals and symptom improvement in PD subjects, including
patients with prior DBS.

This is a multi-center trial planned to enroll 6-12 participants. Eligible subjects must
have a confirmed diagnosis of primary PD for at least 5 years, and their DBS surgery must
have been performed at least 3 years before study consent. After stereotactic bilateral
intraputaminal cell injection, participants will receive a 6-month immunosuppressive
regimen to prevent graft rejection. Existing anti-Parkinson medications may be maintained
and adjusted as needed during the trial, while DBS settings will be kept stable whenever
possible.

Study assessments include motor and non-motor symptom scales, daily OFF/ON time,
dyskinesia evaluation, quality of life questionnaires, and brain MRI and ¹⁸F-DOPA PET-CT
scans. Safety surveillance will focus on adverse events including intracranial
hemorrhage, brain infection, seizure, and graft overgrowth. The trial will also collect
exploratory data on immune responses and changes in the levodopa equivalent daily dose.

STUDY DESIGN

Study type: INTERVENTIONAL
Phases
  - PHASE1
Design info
  Allocation: NA
  Intervention model: SEQUENTIAL
  Intervention model description
    This is a multi-center, open-label, sequential-assignment, dose-escalation Phase 1 trial
    following a standard 3+3 design. Participants are enrolled into successive dose cohorts.
    Two dose levels will be assessed: 4×10^6 cells per side and 8×10^6 cells per side. All
    participants will receive a single stereotactic bilateral putaminal injection of NCR201.
    The dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) observation period is 8 weeks post-dosing. A safety
    review must be completed before advancing to the next higher-dose cohort. No
    intra-participant dose escalation is permitted. Planned total enrollment is 6-12
    participants.
  Primary purpose: TREATMENT
  Masking info
    Masking: NONE
    Masking description
      This is an open-label study. No masking is performed. All participants and study
      personnel know the intervention received.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Incidence of Adverse Events
    Description
      To evaluate the safety and tolerability of stereotactic intracerebral injection of NCR201
      by assessing the type, frequency, and severity of adverse events (AEs) and serious
      adverse events (SAEs).
    Time frame: From the time of cell infusion up to 12 months after NCR201 transplantation
  -
    Measure: Maximum Tolerated Dose (MTD) of NCR201
    Description
      Change from baseline in UPDRS Part III motor score to evaluate motor function improvement
      following NCR201 cell injection.
    Time frame: Within 12 months after NCR201 transplantation
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change in Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS) Part III Score
    Description
      Change from baseline in UPDRS Part III motor score to evaluate motor function improvement
      following NCR201 cell injection.
    Time frame: Baseline, 3 months, 6 months and 12 months after NCR201 transplantation
  -
    Measure: Survival and engraftment of grafted cells assessed by brain PET/MRI imaging
    Description
      Brain PET and MRI imaging to evaluate the survival, location and engraftment of
      transplanted NCR201 dopaminergic progenitor cells in the putamen.
    Time frame: Baseline, 6 months and 12 months after NCR201 transplantation
  -
    Measure: Change in UPDRS Part II Activities of Daily Living Score
    Description
      Change from baseline in UPDRS Part II score to assess activities of daily living in
      Parkinson's subjects after NCR201 treatment.
    Time frame: Baseline, 3 months, 6 months and 12 months after NCR201 transplantation
  -
    Measure: Change in levodopa equivalent daily dose (LEDD)
    Description
      Change from baseline in levodopa equivalent daily dose (LEDD) to assess reduction of
      anti-parkinson medication requirement.
    Time frame: Baseline, 3 months, 6 months and 12 months after NCR201 transplantation
  -
    Measure: Change in UPDRS Part I Non-motor Experiences of Daily Living Score
    Description
      Change from baseline in UPDRS Part I score to evaluate non-motor symptoms in Parkinson's
      patients receiving NCR201.
    Time frame: Baseline, 3 months, 6 months and 12 months after NCR201 transplantation
  -
    Measure: Incidence of anti-NCR201 alloantibody formation
    Description
      Detection of anti-donor alloantibodies in peripheral blood to assess immune response
      against transplanted NCR201 cells.
    Time frame: Baseline, 1 month, 3 months, 6 months and 12 months after NCR201 transplantation
  -
    Measure: Change in Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire (PDQ-39) quality of life score
    Description
      Change from baseline in PDQ-39 total score to evaluate health-related quality of life
      after NCR201 cell transplantation.
    Time frame: Baseline, 6 months and 12 months after NCR201 transplantation
  -
    Measure: Change in dyskinesia rating scale score
    Description
      Change from baseline in dyskinesia rating scale to assess the severity of
      levodopa-induced dyskinesia.
    Time frame: Baseline, 3 months, 6 months and 12 months after NCR201 transplantation
  -
    Measure: Duration of on-time without troublesome dyskinesia
    Description
      Assessment of daily "on" time without troublesome dyskinesia based on patient diaries
      after NCR201 treatment.
    Time frame: Baseline, 3 months, 6 months and 12 months after NCR201 transplantation

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  Aged between 26 and 70 years (inclusive), male or female.

    -  Diagnosed with primary Parkinson's disease (consistent with Chinese Parkinson's
       Disease Diagnostic Criteria, 2016 version). The subject has previously undergone
       intracranial deep brain stimulation (DBS) implantation surgery, with disease
       duration ≥ 5 years.

    -  Modified Hoehn-Yahr stage 3-4 (inclusive) in the DBS ON and medication OFF state.

    -  MDS-UPDRS Part III score ≥30 in the DBS ON and medication OFF state; a positive
       Madopar challenge test (improvement rate of MDS-UPDRS Part III score ≥30%), and on a
       stable anti-Parkinsonian medication.

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  Atypical or secondary Parkinsonism.

    -  Subjects with persistent active infection, cerebrospinal fluid leak, or unhealed
       wounds in the previous brain surgical area; malfunction of implanted devices,
       electrode displacement, or other conditions judged by the investigator to pose
       significant risks to the subject or interfere with study assessments; subjects who
       are unable to safely complete protocol-specified MRI examinations under the
       conditions specified in the DBS system instructions and study site SOP (including
       but not limited to device/lead combinations that do not meet scanning requirements
       or other MRI contraindications); subjects with any contraindication to surgery or
       anesthesia as assessed by the investigator; or subjects with other prior surgical
       conditions deemed by the investigator to interfere with study participation.

    -  Head CT/MRI demonstrating brain trauma, cerebrovascular malformation, hydrocephalus,
       brain tumor, basal ganglia cerebral infarction lesions or other brain injuries,
       severe cerebral atrophy, or imaging abnormalities in the striatum or other brain
       regions that substantially increase surgical risk.

    -  Subjects with active disseminated intravascular coagulation and obvious bleeding
       tendency within 3 months prior to signing the informed consent form, or subjects who
       cannot discontinue antiplatelet agents or other anticoagulant agents for at least 5
       days prior to surgery.

    -  Subjects with any of the following: positive human immunodeficiency virus (HIV)
       antibody; positive Treponema pallidum antibody; positive hepatitis C virus (HCV)
       antibody with HCV RNA copy number greater than the lower limit of the assay normal
       range; positive hepatitis B virus (HBV) surface antigen with HBV DNA copy number
       greater than the lower limit of the assay normal range; active tuberculosis; or
       other active infections judged by the investigator that may interfere with subject
       participation or study outcomes.

    -  Subjects with dementia or severe cognitive impairment at screening.

    -  Subjects with a significant history of psychiatric disorders at screening, or
       suffering from severe depression.

    -  Subjects who have participated in clinical trials of stem cell therapy or gene
       therapy for Parkinson's disease.

    -  History of severe allergy or hypersensitivity reactions.

    -  Subjects with alcohol addiction, or positive drug abuse screening test.

    -  Lactating women; female subjects with pregnancy or oocyte donation plans from study
       initiation through the follow-up period; male subjects (or their partners) with
       fertility or sperm donation plans from study initiation through the follow-up period
       and refuse to take contraceptive measures.

    -  Subjects with severe dyskinesia in the medication ON state.

    -  Other conditions that, in the investigator's opinion, would render the subject
       unsuitable for this clinical study.
