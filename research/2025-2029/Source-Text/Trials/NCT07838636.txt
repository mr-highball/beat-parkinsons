BRIEF SUMMARY

Parkinson's disease is a common, progressive neurodegenerative disorder. The degeneration
of dopaminergic neurons is associated with characteristic clinical symptoms such as
tremor, rigidity, slowness of movement (bradykinesia) and postural instability.
Subthalamic nucleus deep brain stimulation (DBS-STN) is a recognized and widely used
method of treating advanced stages of Parkinson's disease. It improves clinical symptoms
such as tremor, rigidity and slowness of movement (bradykinesia). This method has limited
application for late motor and non-motor symptoms such as: akinesia, postural
instability, gait, including the phenomenon of "freezing", recurrent falls, balance and
dysarthria. These symptoms reduce the quality of life of patients and make them dependent
on their caregivers. There is a clear need to develop additional forms of therapy that
complement deep brain stimulation in controlling late motor and non-motor symptoms.

Physical activity is a commonly used and recommended form of therapy for patients with
Parkinson's disease and a complement to pharmacological therapy, improving gait, balance
and quality of life. However, there are no clear recommendations including patients after
implantation of DBS-STN stimulators to the group undergoing physiotherapy. To this day,
only one clinical trial has been registered assessing the effect of physiotherapy on
balance and gait in patients with DBS-STN.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Movement Disorder Society-sponsored revision of the Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS), Part III: Motor Examination
    Description
      Change in the full Movement Disorder Society-sponsored revision of the Unified
      Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS), Part III: Motor Examination, score in the
      full ON medication/DBS state from baseline to study completion, comparing the control and
      study groups, with subsequent study time points also reported. Part III assesses the
      severity of motor signs of Parkinson's disease. The total score ranges from 0 to 132,
      with 0 indicating no motor impairment and higher scores indicating greater motor
      impairment (i.e., a worse outcome).
    Time frame: 2 years
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Tinetti Performance-Oriented Mobility Assessment (POMA)
    Description
      Change in the full Tinetti Performance-Oriented Mobility Assessment (POMA), also known as
      the Tinetti Mobility Test, total score in the ON medication/DBS state before and after
      study completion, comparing the control and research groups, with subsequent study time
      points also reported. The POMA assesses balance and gait/mobility. The total score ranges
      from 0 to 28 points (balance: 0-16; gait: 0-12), with higher scores indicating better
      mobility, gait, and balance function (i.e., a better outcome).
    Time frame: 2 years
  -
    Measure: New Freezing of Gait Questionnaire (NFOG-Q)
    Description
      Change in the full New Freezing of Gait Questionnaire (NFOG-Q) total score in the ON
      medication/DBS state before and after study completion, comparing the control and
      research groups, with subsequent study time points also reported. The NFOG-Q is a
      patient-reported measure assessing the severity of freezing of gait and its impact on
      daily life in people with Parkinson's disease. The total score ranges from 0 to 28
      points, with 0 indicating no freezing of gait and higher scores indicating greater
      severity and impact of freezing of gait (i.e., a worse outcome).
    Time frame: 2 years
  -
    Measure: Berg Balance Scale (BBS)
    Description
      Change in the full Berg Balance Scale (BBS) total score in the ON medication/DBS state
      before and after study completion, comparing the control and research groups, with
      subsequent study time points also reported. The BBS is a 14-item performance-based
      measure of functional balance. Each item is scored from 0 to 4, giving a total score
      ranging from 0 to 56 points, with higher scores indicating better balance and functional
      performance (i.e., a better outcome).
    Time frame: 2 years
  -
    Measure: Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA)
    Description
      Change in the full Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) total score in the ON
      medication/DBS state before and after study completion, comparing the control and
      research groups, with subsequent study time points also reported. The MoCA is a cognitive
      screening assessment of multiple cognitive domains, including executive function,
      visuospatial abilities, attention, memory, language, and orientation. The total score
      ranges from 0 to 30 points, with higher scores indicating better cognitive performance
      (i.e., a better outcome).
    Time frame: 2 years

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  Patients diagnosed with idiopathic Parkinson's disease (ICD-10:G20).

    -  Patients qualified for advanced therapies using DBS-STN.

    -  Grade II-IV on the Hoehn & Yahr scale.

    -  Age 50-70.

    -  Consent to participate in the study.

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  Patients diagnosed with atypical parkinsonism.

    -  Patients not eligible for advanced therapies with DBS-STN.

    -  Hoehn & Yahr Grade I and V.

    -  Age <50 and >70

    -  Patients diagnosed with dementia syndrome (MMSE < 24/30).

    -  Diseases of the osteoarticular system and muscles that make it impossible to undergo
       rehabilitation.

    -  Severe kidney, liver and heart failure.

    -  Active malignancy or malignancy diagnosed and treated within 3 years prior to the
       study.
