BRIEF SUMMARY

This study is evaluating MR-guided Focused Ultrasound (MRgFUS) treatment for people with
Parkinson's disease (PD) who have disabling tremor, bradykinesia, and rigidity despite
optimal medical therapy.

The study uses the Exablate Neuro System, which delivers ultrasound energy on a specific
brain target and generates a small lesion (ablation) without making a surgical incision.

This study evaluates three treatment approaches targeting different brain structures
involved in Parkinson's motor symptoms:

  -  Pallidothalamic Tractotomy (PTT): targeting pathways connecting the globus pallidus
     to the thalamus and subthalamic region structures.

  -  Thalamotomy (VIM): targeting the ventral intermediate nucleus of the thalamus, a
     structure involved in pathogenesis of tremor.

  -  Combined PTT+VIM: targeting both structures during a single treatment procedure.

Participants will be randomly assigned to one of four groups: PTT, VIM, PTT+VIM, or
"delayed treatment". Participants in the delayed-treatment group will continue best
medical therapy for 6 months before receiving MRgFUS treatment.

The study aims to determine whether PTT provides greater improvement in motor symptoms
than optimized medical therapy alone, whether adding VIM treatment to PTT improves tremor
control, and whether adding PTT to VIM treatment provides additional improvement in
bradykinesia and rigidity.

The main outcome measures are changes in the MDS-UPDRS Part III motor scores:

  1. Overall motor symptoms: change in the contralateral body-side MDS-UPDRS Part III
     motor score at 6 months after randomization, comparing PTT with delayed treatment.

  2. Tremor: change in the contralateral tremor score at 6, 12 months after treatment,
     comparing PTT+VIM with PTT alone.

  3. Bradykinesia and rigidity: change in the contralateral bradykinesia and rigidity
     score at 6, 12 months after treatment, comparing PTT+VIM with VIM alone.

MDS-UPDRS Part III is a standard clinical scale used to assess Parkinson's motor
symptoms. Assessments are performed in the OFF-medication state, meaning Parkinson's
medications are temporarily withheld to better evaluate the treatment effect.

In addition, changes in motor complications, tremor severity, quality of life, and
medication requirements will be assessed using MDS-UPDRS Parts II-IV, the Clinical Rating
Scale for Tremor (CRST), the Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire (PDQ-39), and levodopa
equivalent daily dose (LEDD).

Treatment-related adverse events, cognitive function, and ablation spot characteristics
will also be evaluated throughout follow-up.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The trial is prospective, single-center, randomized and controlled, with four parallel
groups and an overall allocation ratio of 2:2:2:1. Three active groups receive unilateral
MRgFUS at PTT; VIM; or both targets (PTT+VIM in the same procedure). The fourth group
continues BMT for 6 months before a second randomization (1:1:1) to one of the three
MRgFUS target strategies. There is no sham procedure. The VIM arm is an active
comparator, and the delayed-treatment arm permits evaluation against continued medication
without controlling for placebo or expectancy effects. The protocol does not allow rescue
lesioning of additional targets, such as the posterior subthalamic area, based on
intraoperative response. Baseline and postoperative imaging includes high-resolution 3T
MRI.

A statistician independent of recruitment, surgery and outcome assessment will generate
stratified, variable-block randomization (blocks of 14 and 7) using a centralized
web-based system. Allocation is disclosed to the surgical team immediately before
preoperative planning and no later than 4 weeks before MRgFUS. Patients in the
immediate-treatment arms will not be informed which anatomical target has been treated;
the operator and surgical team cannot be blinded. Participants in the delayed-treatment
arm know they are waiting for surgery. Clinical outcomes in all groups will be evaluated
by an independent blinded neurologist. Part III of the MDS-UPDRS and the tremor rating
scale will be videotaped for blinded central reassessment by two neurologists; rigidity
requires in-person assessment.

Antiparkinsonian medication will remain stable for the first 6 months unless a change is
clinically required. Scheduled visits include baseline, 1-3 days after surgery and 3, 6,
and 12 months following the initial surgery; the delayed group is assessed at 3 and 6
months from randomization, then follows the postoperative schedule after later surgery.
Clinical evaluations include MDS-UPDRS parts II-IV, Clinical Rating Scale for Tremor,
MoCA, HADS, PDQ-39, levodopa equivalent daily dose and adverse event reporting. Motor
outcomes are evaluated in medication-OFF state. Lesion volume, spatial accuracy,
perifocal edema and sonication parameters will also be described.

An independent data and safety monitoring committee is planned. Its members will review
serious adverse events and cumulative safety data at least every 6 months.

STUDY GROUPS AND INTERVENTIONS

Arm groups
  -
    Label: MRgFUS Pallidothalamic Tractotomy (PTT)
    Type: EXPERIMENTAL
    Description
      48 participants undergo unilateral MRI-guided focused ultrasound ablation of the
      pallidothalamic tracts in the Forel field region, contralateral to the more affected body
      side, with 5-7 planned thermal lesions. Optimized antiparkinsonian medication continues.
      Post-procedure follow-up: 1-3 days and 3, 6 and 12 months.
    Intervention names
      - Procedure: MR-guided Focused Ultrasound Pallidothalamic Tractotomy
      - Other: best medical therapy
  -
    Label: MRgFUS Ventral Intermediate Nucleus Thalamotomy (VIM)
    Type: ACTIVE_COMPARATOR
    Description
      48 participants undergo unilateral MRI-guided focused ultrasound VIM thalamotomy using
      the institution's standard procedural method, contralateral to the more affected body
      side. Optimized antiparkinsonian medication continues. Post-procedure follow-up: 1-3 days
      and 3, 6 and 12 months.
    Intervention names
      - Procedure: MR-guided Focused Ultrasound VIM Thalamotomy
      - Other: best medical therapy
  -
    Label: Combined MRgFUS PTT and VIM Treatment
    Type: EXPERIMENTAL
    Description
      48 participants undergo unilateral MRgFUS lesioning of both the pallidothalamic tracts
      and VIM within the same treatment session in a prespecified order. Optimized
      antiparkinsonian medication continues. Post-procedure follow-up: 1-3 days and 3, 6 and 12
      months.
    Intervention names
      - Procedure: Combined MR-guided Focused Ultrasound PTT and VIM Treatment
      - Other: best medical therapy
  -
    Label: Best Medical Therapy With Delayed MRgFUS
    Type: OTHER
    Description
      24 participants continue optimized antiparkinsonian medical therapy for 6 months after
      the initial randomization. They undergo blinded outcome assessment at 3 and 6 months
      without a sham procedure. After the month-6 assessment they undergo second randomization
      in a 1:1:1 ratio to PTT, VIM or combined PTT+VIM and then follow the active-procedure
      postoperative schedule. Early clinically indicated treatment is documented as an
      intercurrent event.
    Intervention names
      - Procedure: MR-guided Focused Ultrasound Pallidothalamic Tractotomy
      - Procedure: MR-guided Focused Ultrasound VIM Thalamotomy
      - Procedure: Combined MR-guided Focused Ultrasound PTT and VIM Treatment
      - Other: best medical therapy

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change From Baseline in Contralateral MDS-UPDRS Part III ULE score in the OFF-medication at 6 Months (PTT vs Delayed Treatment)
    Description
      Change in the summed Movement Disorder Society-Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale
      (MDS-UPDRS) Part III items for the ULE contralateral to the treatment side: limb
      rigidity, bradykinesia and tremor. Score range 0-40; higher scores indicate worse motor
      impairment. Evaluated in the practically defined medication-OFF state by an independent
      blinded neurologist. Primary contrast: PTT vs delayed medical treatment.
    Time frame: Baseline and 6 months after initial randomization
  -
    Measure: Change From Baseline in Contralateral MDS-UPDRS Part III Tremor Sub-score in the OFF-medication at 6, 12 Months (PTT+VIM vs PTT)
    Description
      Change in the sum of the contralateral tremor items from MDS-UPDRS Part III, score range
      0-16; higher scores indicate worse tremor. Evaluated in the practically defined
      medication-OFF state by an independent blinded neurologist. Primary contrast: combined
      PTT+VIM versus PTT alone. Preoperative baseline is the reference assessment.
    Time frame: Baseline, 6 months, and 12 months after initial MRgFUS treatment
  -
    Measure: Change From Baseline in Contralateral MDS-UPDRS Part III Bradykinesia and Rigidity Sub-score in the OFF-medication at 6, 12 Months (PTT+VIM vs VIM)
    Description
      Change in contralateral limb rigidity and bradykinesia items on MDS-UPDRS Part III, score
      range 0-28; higher scores indicate worse motor impairment. Evaluated in the practically
      defined medication-OFF state by an independent blinded neurologist. Primary contrast:
      combined PTT+VIM versus VIM alone.
    Time frame: Baseline, 6 months, and 12 months after initial MRgFUS treatment
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change From Baseline in Contralateral MDS-UPDRS III ULE score in the OFF-medication at 6, 12 Months (PTT vs VIM)
    Description
      Change in the summed MDS-UPDRS Part III items for the ULE contralateral to the treatment
      side: limb rigidity, bradykinesia and tremor. Score range 0-40; higher scores indicate
      worse motor impairment. Evaluated in the practically defined medication-OFF state by an
      independent blinded neurologist. Key secondary prespecified PTT-versus-VIM contrast in
      medication-OFF state.
    Time frame: Baseline, 6, and 12 months after initial MRgFUS treatment
  -
    Measure: Change From Baseline in Contralateral MDS-UPDRS Part III ULE score in the OFF-medication at 6 Months (VIM or PTT+VIM vs Delayed Treatment)
    Description
      Change in the summed MDS-UPDRS Part III items for the ULE contralateral to the treatment
      side: limb rigidity, bradykinesia and tremor. Score range 0-40; higher scores indicate
      worse motor impairment. Evaluated in the practically defined medication-OFF state by an
      independent blinded neurologist. Two key secondary comparisons: VIM vs delayed treatment
      and PTT+VIM vs delayed treatment, medication-OFF state.
    Time frame: Baseline and 6 months after initial randomization
  -
    Measure: Change From Baseline in Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire-39 Summary Index at 6 Months (PTT vs Delayed Treatment)
    Description
      Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire-39 (PDQ-39) Summary Index, scaled 0-100; higher scores
      indicate worse health-related quality of life. Key secondary PTT-versus-delayed-treatment
      contrast.
    Time frame: Baseline and 6 months after initial randomization
  -
    Measure: Change From Baseline in PDQ-39 Summary Index at 6, 12 Months (PTT vs PTT+VIM)
    Description
      PDQ-39 Summary Index, 0-100; higher scores indicate worse health-related quality of life.
      Key secondary comparison of PTT with PTT+VIM.
    Time frame: Baseline, 6, and 12 months after initial MRgFUS treatment
  -
    Measure: Change From Baseline in Total MDS-UPDRS Part III Score in Medication-OFF State
    Description
      Total MDS-UPDRS Part III motor examination score, range 0-132, with higher values
      indicating worse motor impairment. Analyzed over follow-up visits; active groups' visits
      are measured from intervention.
    Time frame: Baseline, 6, and 12 months after MRgFUS treatment
  -
    Measure: Change From Baseline in MDS-UPDRS Part II Score
    Description
      MDS-UPDRS Part II assesses motor experiences of daily living. Range 0-52, with higher
      scores indicating worse symptoms.
    Time frame: Baseline, 6, and 12 months after MRgFUS treatment
  -
    Measure: Change From Baseline in MDS-UPDRS Part IV Score
    Description
      MDS-UPDRS Part IV assesses motor complications including fluctuations and dyskinesias.
      Range 0-24; higher scores indicate more severe motor complications.
    Time frame: Baseline, 6, and 12 months after MRgFUS treatment
  -
    Measure: Change From Baseline in Contralateral Limb CRST Score
    Description
      Sum of CRST items specific to limbs contralateral to the treated hemisphere, according to
      the prespecified CRST item mapping; higher scores indicate worse tremor. The exact item
      list and subscore range must be specified in the final scoring charter.
    Time frame: Baseline, 6, and 12 months after MRgFUS treatment
  -
    Measure: Change From Baseline in Levodopa Equivalent Daily Dose
    Description
      Total levodopa equivalent daily dose (LEDD), expressed in mg/day, calculated from all
      prescribed antiparkinsonian medicines using a prespecified conversion convention.
    Time frame: Baseline, 6, and 12 months after MRgFUS treatment
  -
    Measure: Desirability of Outcome Ranking (DOOR) at 12 Months
    Description
      Participant-level composite benefit-risk ranking (DOOR) based on efficacy and
      adverse-event outcomes. The ordered outcome categories, handling of death and missing
      information, and comparison statistic must be finalized prospectively in the statistical
      analysis plan.
    Time frame: 12 months after MRgFUS treatment
  -
    Measure: Proportion of Participants With Persistent Procedure-Related Adverse Events at 12 Months
    Description
      Number and percentage of treated participants with one or more adverse events judged
      related to MRgFUS that remain present at month 12; this is the key safety endpoint.
    Time frame: From MRgFUS treatment through 12 months after treatment
  -
    Measure: Incidence, Severity and Duration of Treatment-Emergent Adverse Events
    Description
      Number and percentage of participants with adverse events during the procedure, 1-3 days
      afterward and later through month 12; severity, causality and resolution are recorded.
      Events of special interest include gait/balance changes, dysarthria, dysphagia,
      paresthesia, weakness, memory problems and somnolence.
    Time frame: During MRgFUS and up to 12 months after treatment
  -
    Measure: Change From Baseline in Montreal Cognitive Assessment Score
    Description
      Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) total score, 0-30, with higher scores indicating
      better cognitive performance.
    Time frame: Baseline, 6, and 12 months after MRgFUS treatment
  -
    Measure: Change From Baseline in Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale Scores
    Description
      Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) anxiety and depression subscales, each 0-21,
      with higher scores indicating greater symptom severity. Separate anxiety and depression
      results will be reported.
    Time frame: Baseline, 6, and 12 months after MRgFUS treatment
Other outcomes
  -
    Measure: Stereotactic Targeting Accuracy on Postoperative MRI
    Description
      Euclidean distance (mm) between the planned geometric lesion center and the postoperative
      lesion center, with signed displacement along each stereotactic axis.
    Time frame: Preoperative planning and postoperative MRI at 1-3 days after MRgFUS

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    1. Men or women, age 18 years and older

    2. Subject is able and willing to give informed consent and able to attend all study
       visits

    3. Subject with a diagnosis of idiopathic PD established according to the Movement
       Disorder Society (MDS) Clinical Diagnostic Criteria for Parkinson's Disease; Hoehn
       and Yahr stage 2-3 in the practically defined medication-OFF state

    4. Mixed motor phenotype of Parkinson's disease defined by the simultaneous presence of
       clinically significant limb rest tremor, bradykinesia, and rigidity on the more
       affected side in the medication-OFF state:

    5. Disease duration ≥3 years from the onset of motor symptoms.

    6. Subject is Levodopa responsive as defined by at least a 30% reduction in MDS-UPDRS
       motor subscale in the ON vs OFF medication state.

    7. Subject has MDS-UPDRS Part III OFF ULE score of ≥ 15 on each side in the meds OFF
       condition.

    8. Subject is on a stable dose of all PD medications for 30 days prior to screening
       visit PD assessments as determined by medical records

    9. Subject is able to communicate sensations during the Exablate procedure.

   10. Subject's pallidothalamic region can be targeted by the Exablate device.

   11. Presence of contraindications to, or limitations precluding, deep brain stimulation
       (DBS), or patient refusal of DBS after being adequately informed about this
       treatment option.

  Exclusion Criteria:

    1. Subject has other central neurodegenerative disease suspected on neurological
       examination.

    2. Severe postural instability (Hoehn and Yahr stage 4-5 in the medication-OFF state),
       severe dysarthria, or severe dysphagia.

    3. Significant cognitive impairment, defined as a Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA)
       score <24.

    4. Subject with unstable psychiatric disease, uncontrolled depressive symptoms,
       psychosis, delusions, hallucinations, or suicidal ideation.

    5. Subject with standard contraindications for MR imaging such as implanted metallic
       devices.

    6. Subject who has an Overall Skull Density Ratio of less than 0.45 as calculated from
       the screening CT

    7. Subject who is unable to tolerate the required prolonged stationary supine position
       during treatment.

    8. Subject who had prior deep brain stimulation of the basal ganglia or thalamus.

    9. History of intracranial ablative procedures or deep brain stimulation.

   10. Female subject who is pregnant.

   11. Subject exhibiting any behavior consistent with ethanol or substance abuse.

   12. Subject with unstable cardiac status or severe hypertension.

   13. Subject with history of abnormal bleeding, hemorrhage, or coagulopathy.

   14. Subject with cerebrovascular disease.

   15. Subject is receiving anticoagulant or antiplatelet therapy within one week of
       focused ultrasound procedure

   16. Subject with advanced kidney disease or on dialysis.

   17. Subject with a history of seizures within the past year.

   18. Subject with a brain tumor.

   19. Subject with life-threatening systemic disease

   20. Subject who is participating in another clinical investigation with an active
       treatment arm

   21. Subject who is unable to communicate with the investigator and staff
