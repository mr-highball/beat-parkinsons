BRIEF SUMMARY

The goal of this study is to investigate how a one-time exercise session influences
different parameters of brain function in people with neurological diseases compared to
healthy volunteers. The main questions the study aims to answer are:

  -  How do fitness level, exercise performance, and physical exhaustion differ between
     patients with neurological diseases and healthy participants?

  -  How does acute exercise affect brain function and other physiological biomarkers in
     participants with neurological diseases?

Participants will take part in a single, one-day study visit and will be asked to:

  -  Perform a standardized exercise test on a bicycle ergometer

  -  Undergo a brain MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) scan, a blood draw, and autonomic
     measurements before and after the exercise test

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

This study aims to investigate the physiological and neurobiological response to an acute
bout of exhaustive exercise in patients with different neurological diseases (epilepsy,
functional neurological disorders, mild cognitive impairment, dementia, Parkinson's
disease, concussion, and brain tumors). In addition, cardiorespiratory fitness, exercise
performance, as well as objective and subjective physical exhaustion will be
characterized. Outcomes in participants with neurological diseases will be compared to
those of healthy control participants.

All participants will perform a one-time exhaustive cardiopulmonary exercise test (CPET)
on a bicycle ergometer. During exercise, continuous electrocardiography (ECG) and
respiratory gas measurements (spiroergometry) will be recorded to assess maximal oxygen
consumption (VO2max) and other measures of exercise capacity and response. Before and
after the exercise test, participants will undergo magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) to
assess structural and functional brain measures as well as neurochemical and metabolic
markers. Blood-based biomarkers and autonomic nervous system activity will also be
assessed before and after exercise.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Changes in functional brain network economy measures from pre- to post-exercise
    Description
      Resting-state functional connectivity and graph theory measures in whole brain, central
      autonomic network, and default mode network. Assessed before and after an acute bout of
      exercise using functional magnetic resonance imaging.
    Time frame: Before exercise (baseline) and immediately after acute exercise (approximately 10 to 20 minutes) in a single study visit
  -
    Measure: Changes in brain metabolites from pre- to post-exercise
    Description
      Neurochemical and metabolic markers across several brain regions, including N-acetyl
      aspartate (NAA; neuronal marker), myoinositol (mI; glial marker), glutamate (excitatory
      neurotransmitter), creatine (Cr; energy marker), choline (Cho; membrane turnover), and
      lactate. Assessed before and after an acute bout of exercise using magnetic resonance
      spectroscopy.
    Time frame: Before exercise (baseline) and immediately after acute exercise (approximately 10 to 20 minutes) in a single study visit
  -
    Measure: Changes in blood-based biomarkers from pre- to post-exercise
    Description
      Inflammatory and neurotrophic plasma biomarkers assessed before and after an acute bout
      of exercise using ultrasensitive targeted immunoassays.
    Time frame: Before exercise (baseline) and immediately after acute exercise (approximately 10 to 20 minutes) in a single study visit
  -
    Measure: Changes in autonomic nervous system activity from pre- to post-exercise
    Description
      Heart-rate-variability (HRV) and electrodermal activity (EDA) parameters assessed before
      and after an acute bout of exercise using a wearable wrist sensor.
    Time frame: Before exercise (baseline) and immediately after acute exercise (approximately 10 to 20 minutes) in a single study visit
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Cardiorespiratory Fitness
    Description
      Maximal oxygen uptake (relative VO2max) assessed during acute exercise (cardiopulmonary
      exercise testing).
    Time frame: One-time measurement during acute exercise (approximately 10 to 20 minutes).
  -
    Measure: Exercise Performance
    Description
      Maximal workload (Watt per kilogram body weight) achieved during acute exercise
      (cardiopulmonary exercise testing).
    Time frame: One-time measurement during acute exercise (approximately 10 to 20 minutes).
  -
    Measure: Objective exhaustion
    Description
      Achievement of predefined objective exhaustion criteria assessed by spirometry and heart
      rate data during acute exercise (cardiopulmonary exercise testing).
    Time frame: One-time measurement during acute exercise (approximately 10 to 20 minutes).
  -
    Measure: Subjective exhaustion
    Description
      Maximal subjective rating of perceived exertion (RPE) assessed using the Borg scale
      during acute exercise (cardiopulmonary exercise testing). The Borg scale measures the
      subjective level of exertion on a scale from 6 (no exertion) to 20 (maximal exertion).
    Time frame: One-time measurement during acute exercise (approximately 10 to 20 minutes).

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  18 years or older

    -  Clinical diagnosis of one of the following neurological diseases: epilepsy,
       functional neurological disorder, mild cognitive impairment, dementia, Parkinson's
       disease, concussion, brain tumor [for participants with neurological diseases]

    -  No diagnosis of neurological disease [for healthy control participants]

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  Non-english speaking individuals

    -  Exercise contraindications (e.g., physical impairments limiting use of bicycle
       ergometer, acute infections, severe heart disease, uncontrolled hypertension,
       exercise associated seizures)

    -  Current use of medications for cardiac rhythm or rate control

    -  Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) contraindications (e.g., electrical implants,
       ferromagnetic implants or objects, pregnancy)
