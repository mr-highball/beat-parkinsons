BRIEF SUMMARY

Parkinson´s disease and parkinsonian syndromes are associated with progressive motor and
non-motor impairments that may compromise functional independence and quality of life.
Sarcopenia, characterized by reduced muscle strength, muscle mass, and physical
performance, is highly prevalent in these populations and may contribute to an increased
risk of falls, frailty, and disability.

This prospective, parallel-group, three-arm randomized controlled trial aims to compare
the effects of three levels of physiotherapy intervention on sarcopenia-related
parameters and physical function in adults with Parkinson´s disease or parkinsonian
syndromes.

The Education Group will receive standarized nutritional education and general physical
exercise recommendations through structed educational materials, without supervised
exercise training. The Progressive Resistance Training (PRT) Group will receive
standarized nutritional education and participate in supervised progressive resistance
trained combined with cognitive dual-task exercises. The PRT plus Instability Group will
receive the same nutritional education, progressive resistance training, and cognitive
dual-task training, with additional instability and postural control components.
Participants in the two supervised exercise groups will complete two physiotherapy
sessions per week for 12 weeks.

Assessments will be conducted at baseline (T0), immediately after the 12-week
intervention (T1), and four weeks after completion of the intervention (T2). Outcomes
will include clinical and functional status, muscle strength, body composition and other
sarcopenia-related measures, physical performance, nutritional status, neurocognitive
status, number of falls, and Parkinson's disease-related clinical characteristics.

The study will evaluate the effects of supervised progressive resistance training
compared with an educational intervention and will explore whether adding instability and
postural control demands to progressive resistance training provides additional effects
on physical function, sarcopenia-related outcomes, and fall-related functional risk in
people with Parkinson's disease and parkinsonian syndromes.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Parkinson's disease (PD) and parkinsonian syndromes are neurodegenerative conditions
characterized by progressive motor and non-motor impairments that can negatively affect
functional independence and quality of life. In these populations, sarcopenia and
reductions in muscle strength and physical performance may contribute to frailty, falls,
disability, and loss of independence.

Progressive resistance training (PRT) represents a potentially relevant physiotherapy
intervention for addressing muscle weakness and sarcopenia-related impairments in people
with Parkinson's disease. Previous research has shown that resistance training can
improve muscle strength and functional performance when appropriately prescribed and
supervised. However, most available studies have focused primarily on idiopathic
Parkinson's disease and have generally examined resistance training from a functional
perspective rather than within a comprehensive sarcopenia framework integrating muscle
strength, body composition, physical performance, nutritional status, and cognitive
characteristics.

People with Parkinson's disease and parkinsonian syndromes may also experience
difficulties when performing motor and cognitive tasks simultaneously. These dual-task
difficulties can interfere with mobility and activities of daily living. Therefore,
incorporating cognitive tasks into physical training may facilitate the assessment and
training of cognitive-motor interaction within a functional context.

In addition, postural instability and impaired balance are clinically relevant features
in Parkinson's disease and some parkinsonian syndromes. Resistance training performed
with additional instability or postural control demands may provide a different stimulus
from conventional progressive resistance training. Nevertheless, comparative evidence
regarding progressive resistance training with and without instability remains limited.

Nutritional status may also influence sarcopenia and the response to exercise. For this
reason, standardized nutritional education is incorporated into all three study groups,
allowing the supervised exercise components to be compared while maintaining a common
educational approach across interventions.

This study is designed as a prospective, parallel-group, three-arm randomized controlled
trial involving adults with a clinical diagnosis of Parkinson's disease or parkinsonism
confirmed by a neurologist. Participants must be aged 18 years or older, be classified
within Hoehn and Yahr stages 1-3, have stable antiparkinsonian medication for at least
four weeks, be able to ambulate independently with or without an assistive device, and be
able to understand and follow basic safety instructions.

Participants with severe or uncontrolled additional pathology, other neurological
diseases that interfere with assessment, recent fractures, severe dyskinesias or
unpredictable motor fluctuations that compromise safety, or medical conditions
contraindicating supervised physical exercise will be excluded.

Following the baseline assessment, eligible participants will be randomly assigned to one
of three intervention groups:

Education Group: Participants will receive standardized nutritional education and general
physical exercise recommendations according to usual clinical recommendations for people
with Parkinson's disease. The intervention will include an initial face-to-face session
in which structured educational materials will be explained and provided. A follow-up
session will be conducted midway through the 12-week intervention period to answer
questions, reinforce the recommendations included in the educational materials, and
review adherence to the proposed nutritional and exercise guidelines. Participants in
this group will not participate in structured strength training or supervised exercise
sessions as part of the study.

Progressive Resistance Training (PRT) Group: Participants will receive standardized
nutritional education and a supervised progressive resistance training program targeting
the lower limbs, upper limbs, and trunk muscles. Exercises will use dumbbells, resistance
bands, weights, body weight, and other appropriate therapeutic exercise resources.
Training intensity will be individually adjusted and progressively increased toward
approximately 60%-90% of one-repetition maximum (1-RM), according to each participant's
functional status, exercise tolerance, and progression. Training load will be
periodically adjusted by modifying the external resistance or exercise complexity.
Cognitive dual tasks will be simultaneously incorporated into the exercise program to
address cognitive-motor interference and promote functional transfer.

Progressive Resistance Training plus Instability (PRT + Instability) Group: Participants
will receive the same standardized nutritional education, progressive resistance
training, and cognitive dual-task components as the PRT Group. The relative training
intensity and strength-training load will follow the same principles as those used in the
PRT Group. In addition, instability and increased postural control demands will be
incorporated through unstable surfaces and other postural challenges. Exercise intensity
and complexity will be individually adapted according to each participant's functional
and cognitive status, with safety and adherence prioritized throughout the intervention.

Participants allocated to the PRT and PRT + Instability groups will complete two
supervised physiotherapy sessions per week for 12 weeks. Sessions will be supervised by a
physiotherapist and individualized according to the participant's abilities, functional
status, cognitive status, exercise tolerance, and clinical presentation.

Study assessments will be conducted at three time points: baseline before the
intervention (T0), immediately following completion of the 12-week intervention (T1), and
four weeks after completion of the intervention (T2). This design will allow the
evaluation of within-group and between-group changes and the initial persistence of
intervention effects following completion of supervised training.

Clinical and functional assessment will include the Movement Disorder Society-Unified
Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS Parts I-IV), fall recording, the Short
Physical Performance Battery (SPPB), and the Timed Up and Go (TUG). Neurological
diagnosis, relevant clinical characteristics, dopaminergic medication, ON/OFF status,
levodopa equivalent daily dose (LEDD), available neuroimaging, and relevant clinical
history will be recorded at baseline and updated at subsequent assessments when
clinically relevant changes occur.

Sarcopenia-related assessment will follow the conceptual framework of the European
Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People (EWGSOP2). Measures will include SARC-F, calf
circumference, handgrip dynamometry, physical performance assessment, and body
composition measured using bioelectrical impedance analysis.

Nutritional status will be assessed using the Mini Nutritional Assessment-Short Form
(MNA-SF) and the Malnutrition Screening Tool (MST). The EAT-10 questionnaire will also be
used to screen for self-reported swallowing difficulties.

Neurocognitive status will be classified according to DSM-5 criteria as normal cognition,
mild neurocognitive disorder, or major neurocognitive disorder. Neurocognitive status
will be considered as part of the multimodal characterization of participants and as a
potential factor influencing treatment response and tolerance to cognitive-motor
dual-task training. Neurocognitive reassessment will also be performed at
post-intervention and follow-up assessments according to neuropsychological criteria.

The Charlson Comorbidity Index will be recorded as part of the baseline clinical
characterization. When available from participants' medical records or routine clinical
practice, analytical biomarkers including vitamin D, albumin/prealbumin, hemoglobin,
hematocrit, total cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, and LDL cholesterol will also be
recorded. No blood tests will be specifically performed for the purpose of obtaining
these biomarkers for the study.

Available neuroimaging information, including DaTSCAN, PET-DOPA, and brain magnetic
resonance imaging, will be recorded when available in the clinical record. New
neuroimaging or analytical information arising during the study may be incorporated into
subsequent assessments when clinically relevant. These medical examinations are not
performed specifically as part of the study intervention.

The interventions are based on therapeutic exercise and strength-training procedures
commonly used in clinical practice. Potential intervention-related risks are considered
low and may include transient muscle discomfort, fatigue, dizziness, musculoskeletal
pain, or imbalance during exercise. To minimize these risks, supervised sessions will be
individually adapted and will follow principles of progressive loading, safety, and
adequate recovery.

If an adverse event occurs during an intervention session, exercise will be stopped
immediately and the participant will be assessed by the healthcare professional
responsible for the intervention. Appropriate clinical follow-up will be provided
according to the nature and severity of the event, including referral to medical or
healthcare services when required. Adverse events will be recorded and documented,
including their severity, evolution, and possible relationship with the intervention.
Continued participation will be considered individually according to clinical safety
criteria.

The main objective of the trial is to compare the effects of the three intervention
approaches-education, progressive resistance training with cognitive dual tasks, and
progressive resistance training with cognitive dual tasks plus instability-on
sarcopenia-related parameters and physical function in people with Parkinson's disease
and parkinsonian syndromes.

Secondary objectives include describing participants' neurocognitive and nutritional
profiles and their sarcopenia and physical-function characteristics; analyzing changes in
muscle strength, body composition, and functional performance associated with each
intervention; evaluating the effects of incorporating instability on postural control and
fall-related functional risk; and exploring the potential influence of neurocognitive and
nutritional status on response to treatment.

By comparing these three clinically applicable intervention approaches, the study aims to
provide further evidence regarding the role of progressive resistance training within a
multimodal approach to sarcopenia and functional impairment in people with Parkinson's
disease and parkinsonian syndromes, as well as the potential additional contribution of
instability and postural control demands.

Design info
  Allocation: RANDOMIZED
  Intervention model: PARALLEL
  Intervention model description
    Participants will be randomly assigned in parallel to one of three intervention groups
    for 12 weeks: (1) standardized nutritional education and general exercise guidelines; (2)
    progressive resistance training combined with cognitive dual-task training and
    standardized nutritional education; or (3) progressive resistance training combined with

    standardized nutritional education. Participants assigned to Groups 2 and 3 will complete

Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Functional Mobility - Timed Up and Go (TUG)
    Description
      Functional mobility will be assessed using the Timed Up and Go (TUG) test. The time
      required to stand up from a chair, walk 3 meters, turn, walk back, and sit down will be
      recorded in seconds. Lower completion times indicate better functional mobility.
    Time frame: Baseline (T0), immediately after the 12-week intervention (T1), and 4 weeks after intervention completion (T2).
  -
    Measure: Sarcopenia Risk - SARC-F
    Description
      Sarcopenia risk will be assessed using the SARC-F questionnaire, which evaluates
      strength, assistance with walking, rising from a chair, climbing stairs, and falls. The
      total score ranges from 0 to 10 points, with higher scores indicating greater risk of
      sarcopenia.
    Time frame: Baseline (T0), immediately after the 12-week intervention (T1), and 4 weeks after intervention completion (T2).
  -
    Measure: Calf Circumference
    Description
      Calf circumference will be measured as an anthropometric indicator related to muscle mass
      and sarcopenia. Measurements will be recorded in centimeters, with higher values
      indicating greater calf circumference.
    Time frame: Baseline (T0), immediately after the 12-week intervention (T1), and 4 weeks after intervention completion (T2).
  -
    Measure: Parkinson's Disease Severity - Movement Disorder Society-Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS)
    Description
      Parkinson's disease-related motor and non-motor symptoms will be assessed using the
      MDS-UPDRS Parts I-IV. The scale evaluates non-motor experiences of daily living (Part I),
      motor experiences of daily living (Part II), motor examination (Part III), and motor
      complications (Part IV). Higher scores indicate greater disease-related impairment or
      severity.
    Time frame: Baseline (T0), immediately after the 12-week intervention (T1), and 4 weeks after intervention completion (T2).
  -
    Measure: Number of Falls
    Description
      Falls occurring during the study period will be recorded as part of the clinical and
      functional assessment. The number of falls reported by each participant will be
      documented, with a higher number indicating a greater occurrence of falls.
    Time frame: Baseline (T0), throughout the 12-week intervention period, and at the 4-week post-intervention follow-up (T2).
  -
    Measure: Nutritional Status - Mini Nutritional Assessment-Short Form (MNA-SF)
    Description
      Nutritional status will be assessed using the Mini Nutritional Assessment-Short Form
      (MNA-SF). The total score ranges from 0 to 14 points, with higher scores indicating
      better nutritional status and lower scores indicating greater risk of malnutrition.
    Time frame: Baseline (T0), immediately after the 12-week intervention (T1), and 4 weeks after intervention completion (T2).
  -
    Measure: Malnutrition Risk - Malnutrition Screening Tool (MST)
    Description
      Risk of malnutrition will be assessed using the Malnutrition Screening Tool (MST), which
      considers recent unintentional weight loss and reduced food intake related to decreased
      appetite. Higher scores indicate greater risk of malnutrition.
    Time frame: Baseline (T0), immediately after the 12-week intervention (T1), and 4 weeks after intervention completion (T2).
  -
    Measure: Self-Reported Swallowing Difficulties - Eating Assessment Tool-10 (EAT-10)
    Description
      Self-reported swallowing difficulties will be assessed using the Eating Assessment
      Tool-10 (EAT-10), a 10-item questionnaire evaluating symptoms related to swallowing. The
      total score ranges from 0 to 40 points, with higher scores indicating greater
      self-perceived swallowing difficulties.
    Time frame: Baseline (T0), immediately after the 12-week intervention (T1), and 4 weeks after intervention completion (T2).
  -
    Measure: Neurocognitive Status According to DSM-5 Criteria
    Description
      Neurocognitive status will be clinically classified according to DSM-5 criteria into one
      of three categories: normal cognition, mild neurocognitive disorder, or major
      neurocognitive disorder. Neurocognitive classification will be used to characterize
      participants and explore its potential influence on response to the intervention and
      tolerance to cognitive-motor dual-task training.
    Time frame: Baseline (T0), immediately after the 12-week intervention (T1), and 4 weeks after intervention completion (T2).
