BRIEF SUMMARY

Although breakthroughs in the management of Parkinson's disease (PD) have emerged in
recent decades resulting in better control in symptoms, majority of patients with PD are
still living with various disabling motor and non-motor symptoms despite they are already
receiving the best contemporary medical or surgical treatments available to them. There
is obviously a need to develop additional treatment modality to further optimize the
control of symptoms and/or slow the disease progression for patients having PD.

Vagus nerve stimulation (VNS), a type of noninvasive brain stimulation is believed to
modify brain rhythms by modulating the nucleus of the solitary tract and locus coeruleus
and other regions known to play a role in gait. Moreover, VNS may modulate inflammatory
pathways and reduce oxidative stress, making it a potential disease modifying treatment
approach for neurodegenerative diseases linked to neuroinflammation such as PD

Further study is warranted to explore the efficacy of non-invasive VNS for its effects on
gait and other symptoms of PD as well as the anti-inflammatory effects.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The objective of the present study is to investigate the feasibility, safety, and
potential efficacy of 2 different non-invasive VNS stimulation protocols (conventional
and exploratory) for patients with PD. The immediate effects of VNS as well as the
medium-term effects of daily VNS at 2 and 4 weeks poststimulation, and the delayed
effects of non-invasive VNS at 8 weeks of both stimulation protocols will be
investigated. The effects of non-invasive VNS of the 2 different stimulation protocols
will also be compared with sham stimulation. The study will be a randomized single blind
study. The tested subjects, not the assessor, will be kept unaware of which treatment
protocols they are receiving.

The investigator hypothesize the VNS stimulations may provide immediate effects, medium
term effects and delayed effects.

Interventions
  -
    Type: DEVICE
    Name: cVNS
    Description
      The cVNS emits a low-voltage 5.56 KHz sinusoidal wave electrical signal bursts lasting
      0.9 ms via two flat stimulation contact surfaces. (Figure 2) These bursts repeat once
      every 38 millisecond (ms). Each set of stimulation will be applied for 2 minutes.
      Stimulation current can be self-adjusted by the subject. This conventional stimulation
      waveform was approved by the US FDA for the treatment of migraines.
    Arm group labels
      - conventional VNS
  -
    Type: DEVICE
    Name: eVNS
    Description
      The eVNS emits the company-proprietary low-voltage of 3~8 KHz sine-variant
      smooth-transition wave electrical signal bursts lasting near 0.9 ms via two flat
      stimulation contact surfaces. These bursts repeat once in intervals of approximately 40
      ms. A potential benefit of this waveform is that it can produce less unpleasant skin
      sensations at a given current intensity and this may enhance the subject to tolerate
      higher current intensity than that of the conventional stimulation.
    Arm group labels
      - exploratory VNS
  -
    Type: DEVICE
    Name: sVNS
    Description
      The sVNS delivers mechanical motor vibrations to provide a similar perceptible
      stimulation feeling to the skin. No electrical stimulations are delivered. The device
      that delivers the sham stimulation is identical in appearance, weight, and user
      interface. A button to adjust "stimulation current" is also available in the sham device
      so as to allow the subject/caregiver to adjust the "stimulation current"
    Arm group labels
      - sham VNS

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change of gait
    Description
      Timed UP and Go test: Participant sits back in a standard armchair with their back
      against the chair and hands resting on the armrests. Regular footwear and usual walking
      aids (like a cane or walker) are used. On the word "Go", the person stands up, walks a
      distance of 3 meters (10 feet) to a marked line or cone at a normal, safe pace, turns
      around, walks back to the chair, and sits down again. A stopwatch starts the moment the
      word "Go" is spoken and stops the moment the person's buttocks touch the seat. The
      results in second will be interpret to assist mobility and risk of falling. The longer
      the time, the high risk of fall
    Time frame: Baseline, Day 1 stimulation visit, 4 week post-home stimulation visits and 8 week post-home stimulation visits
  -
    Measure: Severity and progression of Parkinson's disease
    Description
      Using Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale to motor and non-motor symptoms. Score
      from 0 to 4. 0 is normal while 4 is severe/marked impairment
    Time frame: Baseline, Day 1 stimulation visit, 4 week post-home stimulation visits and 8 week post-home stimulation visits
  -
    Measure: Quantitative Gait Assessment
    Description: Using an app to track the mobility of participants in a 20 sec walking
    Time frame: Baseline, Day 1 stimulation visit, 4 week post-home stimulation visits and 8 week post-home stimulation visits
  -
    Measure: Cognitive status
    Description
      Using Hong Kong Montreal Cognitive Assessment to evaluate the cognitive status. score
      from 0-30. The lower the score, the more cognitive impaired.
    Time frame: Baseline, Day 1 stimulation visit, 4 week post-home stimulation visits and 8 week post-home stimulation visits
  -
    Measure: Change in mood
    Description
      Using Geriatric depression scale which contain 15 questions. Score from 0-15. The higher
      the score, the more severe the depressed mood
    Time frame: Baseline, Day 1 stimulation visit, 4 week post-home stimulation visits and 8 week post-home stimulation visits

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    1. Idiopathic PD as defined by the UK Parkinson's Disease Society Brain Bank Diagnostic
       Criteria

    2. Age 40 - 75 years old

    3. Presence of gait impairment as defined by any one of the below:

       a UPDRS Part 2 Item 15 (Walking) with a score of 2 b. UPDRS Part 3 Item 26 (leg
       Agility) with score of 2 or 3 c. UPDRS Part 3 Item 29 (Gait) with score of 1 or 2

    4. Availability of a caregiver at home

    5. Cantonese speaker

  Exclusion Criteria:

    1. Patients with atypical parkinsonism (e.g., multiple system atrophy, progressive
       supranuclear palsy, dementia with Lewy Bodies)

    2. PD with dementia

    3. PD patients with DBS in-situ

    4. Severe PD with a Hoehn and Yahr staging of 4 or above

    5. Low blood pressure with systolic of < 110 mmHg or with postural hypotension as
       defined by a decrease in systolic and diastolic blood pressure by 20 mmHg and 10mmHg
       upon standing, respectively)

    6. Cardiac pacemaker in-situ
