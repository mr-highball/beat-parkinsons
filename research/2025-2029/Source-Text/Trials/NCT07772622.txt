BRIEF SUMMARY

The study will include volunteer patients diagnosed with Parkinson's disease who applied
to the Neurology Outpatient Clinic at SANKO University Sani Konukoğlu Application and
Research Hospital.

Patients who meet the inclusion criteria will be evaluated by Dr. Hakan Polat and
Physiotherapist Fatma Yılmaz in the application laboratory of the Department of
Physiotherapy and Rehabilitation, Faculty of Health Sciences, SANKO University.

The assessment form prepared by the researchers will be completed face-to-face with the
patients. First, demographic information and disease-related data (duration since
Parkinson's diagnosis, disease stage, affected side, etc.) will be recorded. The
Mini-BESTest will be used to evaluate balance and gait. The muscle strength of the ankle
plantar flexor and dorsiflexor muscles will be assessed using a manual muscle testing
device, and quality of life will be measured with the Parkinson's Disease
Questionnaire-39 (PDQ-39).

Since fall problems in patients may be related to balance impairments, lower limb muscle
weakness, and reduced quality of life, these parameters will also be evaluated.
Therefore, in this study, while examining the validity and reliability of the modified
paper grip test in individuals with Parkinson's disease, its correlation with these
parameters will also be investigated.

Before administering the tests, participants will receive a practical explanation of how
the tests are performed and will be allowed to rest prior to assessment. The tests will
be administered in the following order: first, the paper grip test by Dr. Hakan Polat,
followed by the Mini-BESTest, assessment of ankle plantar and dorsiflexor muscle
strength, and evaluation of quality of life.

One week after the initial assessment, only the paper grip test will be re-administered
by Physiotherapist Fatma Yılmaz in the same environment. This approach will minimize the
effect of environmental factors on the patients' performance.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Parkinson's disease is one of the most significant neurodegenerative disorders causing
disability worldwide. It is characterized by progressive loss of dopaminergic neurons in
the substantia nigra, leading to motor symptoms such as bradykinesia, rigidity, tremor,
postural instability, and gait disturbances. Symptoms vary depending on disease stage and
the affected neural pathways. Common symptoms observed in individuals with Parkinson's
disease include muscle weakness, reduced upper limb function, gait difficulties, balance
and coordination impairments, and, in some cases, cognitive and communication problems.

Patients with Parkinson's disease are at high risk of falling, particularly due to
balance and postural instability. Consequently, fear of falling can lead to kinesiophobia
(fear of movement), decreased engagement in activities of daily living, and difficulties
in functional adaptation. Balance problems are among the primary motor deficits in
Parkinson's disease and contribute significantly to gait disturbances, postural
instability, and fall risk. Therefore, balance rehabilitation is a key focus in the
management of Parkinson's disease, especially for fall prevention.

A detailed assessment of balance is essential for planning effective rehabilitation
programs. According to the literature, balance evaluation in Parkinson's patients is
generally performed using static and dynamic assessments. Commonly used tools include the
Mini-BESTest, the Functional Reach Test, and the Timed Up and Go Test (TUG). However, a
major contributor to balance problems is lower extremity muscle weakness, particularly in
the ankle. While research shows a significant relationship between lower limb muscle
weakness and increased fall risk in Parkinson's patients, existing assessment tools often
lack direct quantitative measurements, which may reduce their clinical effectiveness.

The Enhanced Paper Grip Test (EPGT) can help address this gap. The EPGT provides a simple
measurement of hallux plantar flexion strength without requiring specialized equipment.
It can be used to assess fall risk in individuals with Parkinson's disease and has been
suggested as a practical screening tool in geriatric populations. The validity and
reliability of the test have also been demonstrated in individuals diagnosed with
multiple sclerosis. The EPGT is easy to administer, requires minimal space, is low-cost,
and quick to perform, making it a practical clinical tool. In this study, the researchers
aimed to examine the validity and reliability of the modified paper grip test in
Parkinson's patients.

Volunteer patients diagnosed with Parkinson's disease who applied to the SANKO University
Sani Konukoğlu Application and Research Hospital Neurology Polyclinic will be included in
the study. Patients who meet the inclusion criteria will be evaluated by Dr. Hakan Polat
and Physiotherapist Fatma Yılmaz in the application laboratory of the Department of
Physiotherapy and Rehabilitation, Faculty of Health Sciences, SANKO University. The role
of Associate Professor Zekiye İpek Kırmacı in the study includes planning the study,
formulating hypotheses, interpreting the results, and preparing the manuscript for
publication.

The assessment form prepared by the researchers will be completed face-to-face with
patients. First, demographic information and disease-related data (such as disease
duration, affected side, and affected limb) will be recorded. Balance and gait will be
assessed using the Mini-BESTest. The muscle strength of the ankle plantar and dorsiflexor
muscles will be measured using a manual muscle testing device, and quality of life will
be evaluated with the Parkinson's Disease Questionnaire-39 (PDQ-39).

Because fall risk in Parkinson's patients may be related to balance impairments, lower
limb muscle weakness, particularly in the ankle, and reduced quality of life, these
parameters will also be examined. Therefore, in this study, while evaluating the validity
and reliability of the modified paper grip test in Parkinson's patients, its correlation
with these parameters will also be investigated.

Before administering the tests, participants will receive a practical explanation of the
procedures and will be allowed to rest prior to assessment. The order of testing will be
as follows: first, the paper grip test performed by Dr. Hakan Polat, followed by the
Mini-BESTest, assessment of ankle muscle strength, and evaluation of quality of life.
