# Dill extract and offspring anxiety-like behavior

Maodaa et al., Journal of Molecular Histology 56, 179, June 2, 2025.
DOI: 10.1007/s10735-025-10457-9
PMID: 40455124
Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10735-025-10457-9
Coverage: Factual summary from the original abstract, figures and Supplementary Table I. The full body and figure captions were access-limited.

The abstract describes oral dill extract to pregnant mice at 50 mg/kg from gestation day 1 to postnatal day 15 and subcutaneous nicotine exposure from gestation day 12 to postnatal day 15. Figure 1 diagrams control, extract alone, nicotine alone, and nicotine plus extract. The preparation's botanical organ, extraction method, cohort size and test age remain unverified.

Figure 4 uses elevated-plus-maze arm entries and arm time. Approximate bar heights for the nicotine-only and combined groups, respectively, were 16 and 22 open-arm entries, 82 and 118 open-arm seconds, 37 and 26 closed-arm entries, and 265 and 205 closed-arm seconds. These estimates come from reading the original graph. The inaccessible caption prevents interpreting pairwise contrast symbols. Supplementary Table I reports omnibus two-way ANOVA and gives no sample sizes or group means. It prints an interaction F=0.4679 with p<.05 for open-arm entries and an extract main effect F=7.375 with p>.05 for closed-arm entries. These values warrant checking against the original analysis. The table gives no degrees of freedom.

The earlier 2021 report used a reported 500 mg/kg dose and assessed peripheral organ outcomes. Overlapping authors, exposure schedules and model components leave cohort reuse open. These experiments do not measure clinical anxiety after dietary dill leaf in Parkinson disease.
