Title: Optimising the caffeine nap for counteracting driver sleepiness in CPAP treated obstructive sleep apnoea patients

Review depth: Original article methods, outcome definitions, study-specific denominators and results.

The study compares commercially canned coffee doses and nap protocols after restricted sleep in adults receiving CPAP for obstructive sleep apnoea. Twenty-one participants completed the dose and nap comparisons; 19 completed the combined caffeine-nap protocol. These were overlapping groups from the same reported research program.

The higher caffeine amount supplied by two cans gave a favorable short-term driver sleepiness result relative to one can. Outcomes include Karolinska sleepiness, EEG signs of drowsiness and driving-simulator measures. There is no separate measured fatigue outcome, habitual brewed-coffee comparison, or Parkinson cohort. Retain this source as coffee and sleepiness context for claim 0121 without assigning a fatigue direction.
