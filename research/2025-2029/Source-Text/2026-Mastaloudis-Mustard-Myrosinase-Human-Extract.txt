Title: Exogenous myrosinase from mustard seed increases bioavailability of sulforaphane from a glucoraphanin-rich broccoli seed extract in a randomized clinical study
Source: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-39389-4
First published online: 2026-02-15
DOI: 10.1038/s41598-026-39389-4
Text coverage: original publisher abstract and Methods.

Sixteen adults received broccoli-seed glucoraphanin extract with and without a myrosinase-containing mustard seed powder. Ascorbic acid was common to both conditions. In the randomized blinded crossover, the urinary-metabolite estimate of sulforaphane bioavailability was 39.8% versus 18.6%. This isolates a difference in the study formulation for a biomarker. The experiment did not administer toasted culinary seed or measure a Parkinson symptom.
