Study design and findings
- Pooled prospective cohort analysis of Nurses' Health Study (n = 86,606, 1980-2023) and Health Professionals Follow-up Study (n = 45,215, 1986-2023; total N = 131,821 free of cancer, PD, or dementia at baseline; median 36.8 years follow-up; 11,033 incident dementia cases).
- Highest vs lowest quartile of caffeinated coffee intake was associated with lower dementia risk (HR 0.82, 95% CI 0.76-0.89), lower subjective cognitive decline (PR 0.85, 95% CI 0.78-0.93), and modestly higher NHS TICS score (+0.11, 95% CI 0.01-0.21; global cognition +0.02, p = 0.06).
- Tea intake showed similar nonlinear inverse associations (most pronounced around 2-3 cups/day caffeinated coffee or 1-2 cups/day tea), whereas decaffeinated coffee showed no protective association.

Relevance and limits for Parkinson's food experiments
- Landmark 2026 cognitive epidemiology context for caffeinated coffee and tea.
- Limits: Excluded baseline Parkinson's disease; observational dementia and cognitive-aging cohort rather than Parkinson executive/attention symptom intervention.
