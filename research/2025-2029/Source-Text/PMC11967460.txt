Title: Myorhythmia: A Quantitative Study of Synchrony and Rhythmicity Between the Head and Upper Limbs
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11967460/
PMID: 40181936
PMCID: PMC11967460
DOI: 10.5334/tohm.986
Publication date: 2025-04-01
Coverage: Selected factual text distilled from the original PMC article XML and included tables; supplementary files are separate.

A movement-recording case study used accelerometers and gyroscopes in a person with low-frequency head and limb oscillations after a multiple sclerosis relapse. The movements had a regular frequency with changing amplitude and intermittent coupling across body parts. The authors distinguish myorhythmia from other movement disorders and did not observe accompanying dystonia in the case.

No Parkinson sage exposure or fall frequency was measured. The movement tracing is relevant to differentiating tremor-like signs by etiology and movement characteristics.
