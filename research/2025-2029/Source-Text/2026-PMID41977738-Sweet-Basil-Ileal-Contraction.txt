The experiment prepared 18 chemically characterized extracts from a single dried food-grade Ocimum basilicum leaf source. Methanol or ethanol extraction and direct exposure of isolated ileal tissue differ from eating dried leaf or ingesting its water extract. Against control solution, several showed concentration-dependent reduction of spontaneous or induced contractions. The strongest reported inhibition in the spontaneous-contraction assay was 46.16% (SD 2.11) for a methanolic preparation at the highest assay concentration, and 30.55% (SD 1.69) for the strongest tested ethanol preparation at that concentration. Figures report six ileal segments per condition.

Reduced isolated contraction is a distinct observation. It may change tone or contractile pattern, but the study did not measure forward transit, constipation, or Parkinson outcomes. It neither replicates nor directly reverses the 2006 whole-animal transit observation. The comparison belongs among mechanism and preparation uncertainties for Round 1 claim 0662.
