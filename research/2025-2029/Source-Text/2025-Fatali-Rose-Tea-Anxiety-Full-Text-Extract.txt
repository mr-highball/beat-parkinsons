Title: A comparative study of the effect of rose essential oil and lemon balm tea on stress reduction among gynecology residents
DOI: 10.22034/jiitm/16.2.5
Source: https://jiitm.ir/article-1-1777-en.html
Original PDF: https://jiitm.ir/article-1-1777-fa.pdf
Reference: Fatali S, Moridi A, Farjami M. Journal of Islamic and Iranian Traditional Medicine 16(2), 87–94, 2025. The publisher lists September 10; the Magiran issue index lists September 1.
Text coverage: Full original Persian paper, especially methods, results and Table 1 on page 89, verified against the English abstract.

Page 89 prints IRCT20220821055767N1. The randomized crossover studied 20 women in gynecology residency, with 10 in each starting sequence. The actual protocols were 14 days per period and a one-week washout. The registry had a target of 40 and specified a two-week washout. The paper describes branded lemon balm tea, 50 mL twice daily, and inhaled rose essence as its active comparator. The report concerns residents outside a Parkinson disease cohort.

The results call the endpoint anxiety. Table 1 reports the same baseline of 17.00 (SD 2.63) in both rows. The post-tea result is 16.85 (SD 2.46) and the post-rose result is 15.80 (SD 3.38). The paper reports p=.453 and p=.853 for within-comparison results and p=.907 for the post-treatment comparison. It uses paired and independent t tests and does not provide a period-adjusted crossover effect or uncertainty interval. The independent Magiran Persian transcription gives a tea SD of 3.46. The original paper verifies 2.46 in its body, table and abstracts.

The Methods name the Beck Depression Inventory while describing 21 anxiety items and anxiety severity cutoffs. The paper reports no separate numerical depression result. The registered depression endpoint thus remains unresolved. The 2024 same-author conference abstract remains a likely companion with inconsistent botanical wording and schedules. The confirmed registry and 2025 paper belong to one study family.
