Title: Caffeine and perceived fatigue during cycling without imposed caffeine restriction
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12291204/
PMID: 40704635
PMCID: PMC12291204
DOI: 10.1080/15502783.2025.2534131
Publication date: 2025-07-24
Date basis: First electronic publication.
Review depth: Authenticated NCBI structured original, Methods, Results and Tables 3–6.

The paper contains two randomized, double-blind crossover studies in physically active young adults who kept their usual diets. Acute isolated caffeine or placebo preceded constant-load cycling. The two samples enrolled 35 and 21 people at different tested doses. A separate 0–10 rating measured perceived fatigue through the exercise, alongside effort, strain and affective measures.

Study 1 caffeine and placebo fatigue means were 3.5 versus 3.3 at the start, 8.3 versus 7.9 before exhaustion, and 9.2 versus 9.1 at exhaustion. Condition p=.303 and time-by-condition p=.685. Study 2 means were 2.7 versus 3.1, 7.6 versus 7.9, and 9.3 versus 9.0. Condition p=.843 and interaction p=.529. Table 6 reports 19 people in the perceptual analysis, while the condition degrees of freedom as printed do not match that note. Neither condition effect was demonstrated and the means vary across times and dose groups.

No abstinence was required, but 8 and 12 participants respectively went at least 12 hours between usual intake and testing. Subset analyses did not establish a caffeine effect or randomly compare withdrawal periods. Daily Parkinson fatigue, brewed coffee and brewed green tea were outside the design. Round 1 claims 0121 and 0948 use this result as a contrary or mixed descriptive comparison with no eligible direction. Record the two dose studies under one publication family while participant overlap is unclear.
