Title: Affron extract and depressive symptoms in adults with low mood
First published: 2025-05-23
PMID: 40414301
DOI: 10.1016/j.tjnut.2025.05.024
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40414301/
Registry: ACTRN12623001358639
Archive scope: Original author abstract authenticated in PubMed and matched to the prospective ANZCTR record. Full publication tables remain an access lead.

The randomized double-blind trial compared standardized extract with placebo for twelve weeks in a low-mood population. The author abstract reports 202 adults. Its primary DASS-21 depression contrast favored extract by 2.92 points (95% CI 0.71 to 5.13 points of greater improvement). At least a seven-point drop occurred in 72.3% of extract participants and 54.3% of placebo participants, p=.010. The aggregate secondary comparisons did not clearly differ. A sleep-disturbance subgroup with more severe symptoms showed a favorable exploratory contrast. That selected result does not establish an overall sleep or insomnia effect. The registry excluded Parkinson disease. The result concerns a measured standardized extract rather than ordinary culinary ground saffron.
