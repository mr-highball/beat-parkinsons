Study design and intervention
- Thirty-two male Swiss mice randomly assigned to four groups (n = 8 per group): Control, Harmaline (10 mg/kg i.p. on days 3, 5, and 7), Quercetin nanoemulsion (Que-NE, 20 mg/kg i.p. daily for 7 days), and Harmaline + Que-NE.
- Tremor severity was rated on a 0-4 observational scale (0 = no tremor, 1 = occasional head/neck tremor, 2 = intermittent full-body tremor, 3 = persistent full-body tremor, 4 = severe tremor preventing standing/walking).
- Additional behavioral tests: open field, elevated plus maze, tail suspension, wire grip, rotarod, and passive avoidance; RT-PCR measured NF-kB, TNF-alpha, IL-1beta, IL-6, NMDA receptor, and Lingo-1 expression.

Verified outcomes
- Tremor severity: Significant treatment-by-day interaction (two-way ANOVA F(6, 83) = 9.058, p < 0.0001); Que-NE significantly reduced harmaline-induced tremor severity versus harmaline alone (p < 0.0001).
- Secondary behavioral tests: Que-NE reduced tail-suspension immobility (p = 0.0003) and improved open-field central/peripheral zone anxiety-like behavior (F(3, 28) = 6.941, p = 0.0012; F(3, 28) = 6.957, p = 0.0012), while showing limited efficacy on rotarod, wire grip, and passive avoidance memory.
- Molecular assays: Downregulated pro-inflammatory mediators and Lingo-1 gene expression (p < 0.0001).

Relevance and limits for Parkinson's food experiments
- Controlled preclinical experiment showing strong tremor reduction with injected quercetin nanoemulsion in harmaline-induced essential tremor mice.
- Limits: Essential tremor (harmaline olivocerebellar model) rather than Parkinsonian resting tremor; intraperitoneal engineered nanoemulsion of purified quercetin rather than dietary intake of raw apples or another whole food; ubiquitous dietary flavonoid cannot isolate a single culinary fruit.
