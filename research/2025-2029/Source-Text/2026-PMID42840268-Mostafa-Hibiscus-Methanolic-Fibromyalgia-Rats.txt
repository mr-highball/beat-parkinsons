The extraction of powdered air-dried (300g) was concerned according to formerly designated research[20]. Dried Hibiscus sabdariffa calyces were extracted using 80% methanol containing 0.1% citric acid by sonication at 60 °C for 45 min (three cycles). The combined extract was filtered, concentrated, lyophilized, and purified following reported procedures using solvent partitioning [21,22] and silica-gel column chromatography[23]. The purified MeOH-Exs was used for all biological experiments. Briefly, for purification and phytochemical characterization, the extract was processed according to the method of [22] using sequential solvent partitioning with petroleum ether, was collected, filtered, and concentrated under reduced pressure at 65 °C until dryness. It should be noted that these procedures were performed for purification and characterization purposes, whereas the pharmacological experiments described in the present study were conducted using the methanolic extract of Hibiscus sabdariffa (MeOH-Exs). [Results] The GC-MS analysis identified a total of 38 compounds in the MeOH-Exs, comprising various types of polyphenols and flavonoids. Flavonoids were the most abundant class, accounting for 57.44% of the total composition. Specifically, the MeOH-Exs contained 28 different flavonoid compounds, including a significant concentration of anthocyanins (10.22%). The major anthocyanins identified were Malvidin-3,5-diglucoside (1.27%), Delphinidin-3-galactoside (1.64%), Cyanidin-3-O-sambubioside (2.14%), Cyanidin-3-rutinoside (2.48%), and Petunidin-3-glucoside, which was the most abundant at 2.69%. In addition, the MeOH-Exs also contained terpenes, which represented 9.45% of the total composition, with linalool and terpinene-4-ol being the major terpene compounds (6.61% and 1.56%, respectively). Other notable compounds identified in the MeOH-Exs included coumarins (10.33%), phenols (17.17%), limonene (5.62%), and luteolin and its derivatives (4.66%). Overall, the GC-MS analysis revealed the rich and diverse phytochemical profile of the MeOH-Exs, with a predominance of polyphenols and flavonoids, particularly anthocyanins, terpenes, and other bioactive compounds. The full GC–MS chromatogram and complete compound list (retention times, peak areas, and molecular ions) are presented in Supplementary File S1. [Discussion] The current study demonstrates that methanolic extract of Hibiscus sabdariffa (MeOH-Exs) exerts noticeable therapeutic effects in the Res-induced model of fibromyalgia (FM), restoring behavioral, biochemical, and neurohistological parameters towards normal levels. These improvements may be attributed to the modulation of the neuroinflammation–oxidative stress–microRNA axis, a central cascade behind FM pathology. The observed reductions in oxidative stress, inflammatory mediators, and apoptotic signaling following MeOH-Exs treatment are consistent with previous reports demonstrating that suppression of oxidative and inflammatory signaling pathways contributes to tissue protection and functional recovery in experimental disease models [40]. reported that attenuation of oxidative stress together with inhibition of NF-κB and MAPK signaling significantly reduced pathological tissue injury and inflammatory responses. Similarly [41], demonstrated that the natural polyphenol resveratrol ameliorated neuroinflammatory alterations through modulation of inflammatory mediators and chemokine-related signaling pathways.
