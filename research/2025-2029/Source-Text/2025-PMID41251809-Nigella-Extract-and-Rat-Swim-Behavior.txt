Title: Nigella seed extract and forced-swim behavior in a reserpine rat experiment

Date basis: Publisher original publication date.
Coverage: Complete open publisher original and visual review of Figure 2.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26

Figure 2 shows lower forced-swim immobility for oral Nigella sativa seed extract than reserpine alone, approximately 2.1 versus 3.6 minutes by graph reading, with different significance letters. Six rats per group. The preparation is an ethanol seed extract rather than toasted seed. No independent general locomotor control is reported, and the study does not estimate Parkinson depression in people.

The paper describes five randomized groups and blinded assessments. Coffee and black seed have the same statistical label in the forced-swim figure, despite different approximate bar heights. Exact group means, adjusted contrasts and confidence intervals are not tabulated. The Results prose and discussion differ in how they rank the two botanical extracts. The Y-maze graph axis gives time while text discusses percentage alternation. Neither difference supplies a Parkinson depression result or independent corroboration of a toasted seed response.
