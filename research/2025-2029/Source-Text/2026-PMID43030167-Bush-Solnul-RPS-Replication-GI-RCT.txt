Primary Abstract:
A prebiotic is a substrate that is selectively utilized by host microbiota to confer a health benefit. Consumption of 3.5 g of Solnul® resistant potato starch (RPS) was previously shown to selectively increase Bifidobacterium and Akkermansia, as well as improve stool form relative to a placebo. The objective of this study was to reproduce these effects in an independent cohort. A 4-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel group trial evaluated the effects of 3.5 g RPS versus a placebo on changes in Bifidobacterium, Akkermansia, stool form, and bowel movement (BM) frequency using 16s rRNA sequencing, the Bristol Stool Form Scale, and personal diaries in healthy adults aged 18-69 years in Guelph, ON. Compared to the placebo (n = 35), the relative abundance of Bifidobacterium increased (p = 0.049, Brunner-Munzel), and the number of normal stools (Type 3 or 4) was higher in the RPS group (n = 35; p = 0.0077, Chi-Square; key secondary endpoints). While differences were not observed for Akkermansia relative to the placebo (p = 0.49; primary endpoint), End-of-Study Akkermansia levels were higher than Run-In levels for RPS (p = 0.046, Wilcoxon signed-rank) but not the placebo (p = 0.14). Participants consuming RPS had higher BM frequency at the End-of-Study compared to the Run-In period, but the effect was not different from the placebo. Comparing End-of-Study and Run-In periods, the RPS group experienced fewer days without a BM relative to the placebo. No changes in the GSRS or serious adverse events were reported in the RPS group. This clinical trial confirmed that 3.5 g of RPS induces a bifidogenic effect and normalized stool form, consistent with the previous study. NCT06949631.

Materials and Methods] The clinical trial aimed to determine the reproducibility of the beneficial gastrointestinal effects previously demonstrated by Bush and colleagues [15] for prebiotic resistant potato starch (RPS) supplementation. Specifically, the present study investigated the gastrointestinal effects of consuming 7 g/day of total starch, which comprised 3.5 g of RPS (Solnul®, MSP Starch Products Inc., Carberry, MB, Canada) and 3.5 g of high-amylopectin corn starch (Amioca TF; Ingredion, Brampton, ON, Canada). Solnul® is a Type 2 RS that is produced via a unique proprietary processing method that preserves starch granule structure, resulting in stable RS contents of ≥60% (AOAC 2002.02). Every 3.5 g dose of Solnul® contains ≥2.1 g of RS on an ‘as-is’ basis. The Solnul® lot used in this study contained 61.4% RS on an ‘as-is’ basis (Lot 008546; AOAC 2002.02). Therefore, each participant in this study consumed 2.1 g of RS per day. In contrast to the treatment, the placebo contained 7 g of high-amylopectin corn starch (Amioca TF). The placebo was previously shown to be fully digestible starch with no RS [22], with no effects on the gut microbiota even when supplemented at high amounts of 50 g/day [9]. The RPS and placebo doses were matched for caloric content (23.9 kcal and 25.1 kcal, respectively), were nearly identical in appearance and taste, and were packaged in identical single-serving sachets using randomization codes generated from Nutrasource SGS (Guelph, ON, Canada; see Section 2.2 for details) by personnel not involved in the collection of study data to ensure blinding of the study. Discussion] Prebiotics are substrates that are selectively utilized by host microorganisms to confer a health benefit [1], and reproducing these effects in clinical trials to provide confirmatory evidence is one of the key criteria stipulated by ISAPP in classifying compounds as prebiotics [2]. A previous study found that daily consumption of 3.5 g of RPS for 4 weeks led to increases in Bifidobacterium and Akkermansia, as well as fewer instances of BSFS Type 7 and Type 1 or 2 stools compared to a placebo in otherwise healthy people [15]. In the present study, which followed a similar protocol comparing 3.5 g of RPS to a fully digestible corn starch placebo over 4 weeks in otherwise healthy people, there were no serious adverse events, and both interventions were well tolerated, as evidenced by the GSRS survey results. RPS supplementation increased Bifidobacterium levels compared to the placebo group, meeting the secondary objective. The prespecified primary endpoint, confirming that RPS supplementation led to greater increases in Akkermansia, did not reach statistical significance between treatment groups.
