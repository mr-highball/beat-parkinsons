CRANMOOD depression model and week twelve estimate
Source: https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/102991/13/CRANMOOD_Clin_Nutr_Final.pdf
PMID: 42134099
DOI: 10.1016/j.clnu.2026.106677
Registration: NCT05260346
Coverage: Version of record, Methods sections 2.1–2.2, Results sections 3.1–3.4 and Table 2, plus official registry verification

The parallel double-blinded trial enrolled final-year university students aged 20–25. Diagnosed mental health disorders were an exclusion. The intervention was 236 mL cranberry juice at breakfast each day for 12 weeks, with 442 mg of measured polyphenols per daily serving. The placebo beverage was designed to match color and taste. The exposure was juice, distinct from dried or cooked cranberry. Total Mood Disturbance was the primary outcome. The separate computerized attention domain showed no clear group difference, while two selected short-term memory measures favored cranberry at week 12. Those memory tasks do not establish a treatment effect on attention. The published intention-to-treat sample and conflicting registry counts below refer to the same randomized family.

The publication describes 82 screened and 72 randomized and included in the intention-to-treat sample, with 34 cranberry-drink and 38 placebo assignments. The older official registry records actual enrollment of 64 and has no posted results. The available records leave that count difference unresolved. Table 2 gives HADS depression overall treatment p = .98, treatment-by-visit p = .70, and a separate adjusted week-12 difference of −0.404 points (95% CI −1.88 to 1.08, p = .59). Table 2 uses baseline as a covariate and defines visits 2, 3 and 4 as weeks 4, 8 and 12 (Methods and Figures 2–3). Cranberry-minus-placebo differences on the separate POMS depression-dejection subscale were +3.34 at week 4 (95% CI 0.526 to 6.16, p = .02), −1.92 at week 8 (95% CI −5.67 to 1.84, p = .31), and −1.01 at week 12 (95% CI −5.18 to 3.16, p = .63). The overall treatment test was p = .91 and the treatment-by-visit interaction was p = .02. Section 3.2 summarizes individual mood scores as inconclusive. Table 2 records a time-specific change and no sustained contrast. The study used a drink in healthy young adults and leaves dried cranberry and Parkinson depression unresolved.
