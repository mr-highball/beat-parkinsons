Title: Striatal DAT binding fully mediates caffeine’s effect on motor progression in Parkinson disease
Source: https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-11054916/v1
DOI: 10.21203/rs.3.rs-11054916/v1
Parent cohort registry: NCT01141023
Publication date: 2026-09-30
Date basis: Research Square version 1.
Checked: 2026-11-24
Review depth: Full preprint body, cohort definitions, and Table 3. Peer review status remains preprint.

The Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative analysis included 261 people with idiopathic Parkinson disease at baseline and 207 with 1–4 years of follow-up. The longitudinal sample contains 96 current coffee consumers, 63 former consumers and 48 never consumers. Current and former consumers had reported caffeinated coffee use at least weekly for at least six months. Coffee, rather than black tea dose, defines the exposure groups.

The longitudinal generalized estimating equation contrasts current and never groups. Table 3 reports the bradykinesia coefficient beta 2.503 (z = 3.078, p = 0.002), rigidity beta 0.863 (z = 1.977, p = 0.048), and rest tremor beta 0.599 (z = 1.152, p = 0.249). The motor subscores are separately computed from MDS-UPDRS items. Mediation estimates and the weekly-cup sensitivity analysis reuse the same underlying cohort.

The reported direction for bradykinesia is adverse. This is an important contrary observation for the plausibility of a simple caffeine mechanism, but questionnaire-defined coffee exposure, observational selection and residual confounding prevent transferring the result directly to brewed black tea. No randomized treatment, matched caffeine dose, specific black tea type, or separated brewed black tea comparison was reported. The tremor analysis was inconclusive. The source is related evidence for claims 0082 and 0904, with scopes kept distinct.
