Title: The Preliminary Effects of Capsaicin Supplementation on Cerebrovascular Function and Cognition in Middle-Aged and Older Adults Who Are Overweight or Obese.

Authors: Thornton T, Kim E, Downs T, Mills D, Bliss E
Journal: Nutrients

PubMed abstract:
Background/Objectives: Cerebrovascular function and cognition decline with age and may be further exacerbated by obesity. Capsaicin may improve cerebrovascular function and cognition, although evidence in humans is lacking. We investigated the effects of 12 weeks of capsaicin supplementation on cerebrovascular function and cognition in middle-aged and older adults who were overweight or obese. Methods: Forty participants were randomly allocated to receive 200 mg/day of Capsifen® or a placebo, with 34 completing the intervention (n = 17 in both). Before and after supplementation, transcranial Doppler ultrasonography was used to measure the cerebrovascular responsiveness (CVR) to physiological (hypercapnia, 5% carbon dioxide) and cognitive stimuli, while cognition was assessed using a comprehensive cognitive battery. The trial was prospectively registered (ACTRN12623000556640). Results: Total CVR to cognitive stimuli increased following Capsifen® supplementation (18 ± 3% to 21 ± 1%) and decreased in the placebo group (21 ± 4% to 19 ± 3%; p = 0.0002). Total cognition increased following Capsifen® supplementation (98 ± 18 to 107 ± 18) compared with the placebo (105 ± 13 to 106 ± 10; p = 0.0085). Capsifen® did not alter CVR to hypercapnia (30 ± 18% to 44 ± 27% vs. 39 ± 23% to 32 ± 16%; p = 0.5981). Working memory, executive function and exercise performance also improved, while waist circumference and waist-to-hip ratio decreased. Conclusions: Twelve weeks of Capsifen® supplementation improved total cognition and CVR to cognitive stimuli but not CVR to hypercapnia. These findings provide preliminary evidence that chronic capsaicin supplementation may improve cognition and neurovascular coupling in humans.

Verified Round 3 factual notes:
Verified PMC13765294 full-text details (ACTRN12623000556640): 12-week double-blind placebo-controlled RCT of 200 mg/day sustained-release encapsulated capsaicinoids (Capsifen) in overweight/obese adults aged 50-80 y without neurological disease (40 randomized, 34 completers: n=17/arm). Key findings and methodological caveats: (1) NIH Toolbox Flanker Inhibitory Control & Attention (p=0.209), Pattern Comparison Processing Speed (p=0.196), Dimensional Change Card Sort executive flexibility (p=0.999), and Trail Making Test Part B minus Part A set-shifting index (p=0.315) were ALL NULL; (2) List Sorting Working Memory (placebo 18.53->16.58 vs Capsifen 15.70->18.76, p<0.0001), Trail Making Part A (placebo 31.65->35.99 vs Capsifen 42.42->29.33 s), 6-minute walk distance (placebo 504->457 m vs Capsifen 451->505 m, p<0.0001), and 60-s sit-to-stand (placebo 28->24 vs Capsifen 23->28 reps) all exhibited pronounced baseline imbalances with mirror regression-to-the-mean without baseline-adjusted ANCOVA; Trail Making Part B did not survive multiple-comparison correction; (3) ALL POMS mood subscales (Tension p=0.319, Depression p=0.798, Fatigue p=0.240, Confusion p=0.513, Vigour p=0.551, Total Mood p=0.863) were NULL; (4) dominant/non-dominant handgrip strength (not manual dexterity) showed small interaction p=0.005/0.014 with opposing baseline drift.
