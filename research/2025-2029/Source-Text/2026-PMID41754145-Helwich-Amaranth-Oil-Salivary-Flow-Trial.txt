Title: Impact of Short-Term Dietary Restriction Combined with Amaranth and Canola Oil Supplementation on Salivary Adipokines in Adults with Obesity.
PMID: 41754145
PMCID: PMC12943514
DOI: 10.3390/nu18040628
First published: 2026
Authors: Helwich M, Kanikowska D, Eliasz W, Kanikowska A, Rutkowski R, Moszak M, et al.
Journal: Nutrients
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41754145/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Three-week inpatient hypocaloric dietary trial (Helwich et al., 2026, Nutrients, PMC12943514) enrolling 115 adults with obesity (BMI >30 kg/m2): 23 supplemented with 20 mL/day Amaranthus cruentus seed oil, 21 with 20 mL/day canola oil, and 71 on hypocaloric diet alone (self-selection into oil arms acknowledged). Unstimulated salivary flow rose within the amaranth oil group (0.33 +/- 0.19 to 0.39 +/- 0.27 mL/min, p=0.0367) vs canola (0.31 to 0.33 mL/min, p=0.3139) and diet control (0.38 to 0.38 mL/min, p=0.6831), with no between-group difference vs canola (p=0.9907) and baseline control already at 0.38 mL/min; salivary pH rose in both oil groups (p=0.0425). Limits: pressed seed oil is not cooked amaranth grain, non-randomized self-selected allocation, null between-group difference, and physiological basal salivation in obese adults does not equate to Parkinson's drooling (sialorrhea driven by impaired swallowing clearance).
