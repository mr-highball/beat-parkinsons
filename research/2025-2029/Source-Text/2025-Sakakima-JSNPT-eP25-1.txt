Title: 緑茶摂取が地域在住高齢パーキンソン病患者の非運動症状に及ぼす影響
English title translation: Green tea intake and nonmotor symptoms in community-dwelling older people with Parkinson's disease
Authors: Harutoshi Sakakima, Toshiro Uchi, Kazuki Nakanishi and colleagues
Source: https://jsnpt23.jp/images/program/program_syouroku.pdf
Meeting: 23rd Annual Meeting of the Japanese Society of Neurological Physical Therapy, Kanazawa, 31 October–1 November 2025
Presentation: 31 October 2025, eP25-1, printed page 372
Text coverage: Original English factual extract prepared from the Japanese conference abstract

The community survey included 100 older people with Parkinson's in Kagoshima Prefecture, mean age 72.7 years (SD 6.8). This abstract describes an extreme-groups analysis of reported daily green tea volumes. The Methods define the groups as greater than 1,000 mL/day (22 people) and 500 mL/day or less (37 people). The Results refer to the high group as 1 L/day or more. The abstract does not clarify where exactly 1 L was placed.

The investigators assessed daily activities with the Barthel Index and instrumental activities with Lawton–Brody. They listed cognition, hallucinations and psychosis, depressed mood, anxious mood, apathy, sleep disturbance and constipation among analyzed Part I nonmotor outcomes. The higher-volume group had a higher Barthel Index (p=.019) and lower proportions with moderate or severe sleep disturbance (p=.047) and constipation (p=.044). The abstract reports logistic associations with sleep and constipation without odds ratios or confidence intervals. It gives no separate fatigue or olfactory outcome and no brewing protocol.

The abstract identifies Kagoshima University ethics approval 220137. Close agreement in investigators, setting, selected nonmotor items and ethics identifier links the report to Sakakima et al. 2026, PMID 42816813. The later paper compares 105 people by drinking frequency and 78 daily drinkers by three quantity groups. Group limits and analysis differ. Public reports provide no participant crosswalk.
