Kaur and Gupta review multiple herbs and constituents in Parkinson disease. The public author abstract does not attribute a measured clinical symptom outcome to whole saffron. The 205-reference Crossref deposit supplies identifiable leads for three distinct reports. Tang (DOI 10.1007/s12035-020-01941-2) administered isolated crocin in a Parkinson mouse model and examined depressive-like behavior. Inoue (DOI 10.1016/j.gene.2021.145811) measured climbing in Parkinson-model flies receiving saffron in feed or isolated crocetin. Soleymani (DOI 10.1016/j.parkreldis.2024.107051) tested isolated crocin in people with Parkinson disease and reported aggregate motor scores.

These citations identify separate exposures, populations and endpoints. They do not establish a ground-saffron Parkinson depression or rigidity result, or a separate clinical insomnia or fatigue comparison inside the review. The publisher body and study-level tables remain open.
