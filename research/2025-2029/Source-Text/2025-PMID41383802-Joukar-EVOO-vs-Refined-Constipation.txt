Title: Comparative efficacy of extra virgin olive oil versus refined olive oil in the treatment of individuals suffering from constipation: A double-blind randomized clinical trial study
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12694839/
PMID: 41383802
PMCID: PMC12694839
DOI: 10.22088/cjim.16.4.674
Registration: IRCT20080901001155N33
Publication date: 2025-10-15
Date basis: Publication date in the official PMC article record (Caspian Journal of Internal Medicine 16(4):674).
Coverage: Paraphrased original methods, results, Tables 1–5 and discussion from official PMC XML.

This single-center, parallel, double-blind phase III randomized clinical trial enrolled 150 adults (75 per arm, mean age 45.88 ± 15.67 years) with chronic functional constipation diagnosed by a gastroenterologist using Rome III criteria and colonoscopy at the Guilan Gastroenterology Clinic in Rasht, Iran (May–November 2021). Participants with neurological anomalies, organic or systemic causes of constipation, opioid use, or laxative use were excluded. Five participants per arm withdrew within days 2–3 due to taste or odor dislike, leaving 140 participants (70 extra-virgin olive oil, 70 refined olive oil) in the per-protocol analysis.

Participants consumed two tablespoons of raw oil daily without mixing it into food for four weeks while maintaining habitual diet and avoiding other vegetable oils and constipation drugs. Jalalat Exir Olive Oil Company supplied the organic cold-pressed extra-virgin olive oil at a reduced price; although Methods describe polyphenol content above 500 mg/kg, the Discussion explicitly notes that no laboratory polyphenol assay was performed during the trial.

In ANCOVA adjusting for baseline scores, Rome III constipation severity decreased from 11.27 ± 2.73 to 3.47 ± 1.73 with extra-virgin olive oil versus 11.37 ± 2.25 to 7.74 ± 2.53 with refined olive oil; the paper reports an adjusted between-group difference of -4.22 (printed as 95% CI 3.63–4.82; F(1,137) = 197.67, p < 0.001, partial eta-squared = 0.591). Bristol Stool Form Scale (BSFS) adjusted mean differences favored extra-virgin over refined oil at week 2 (0.53, 95% CI 0.38–0.68, p < 0.001), week 3 (0.78, 95% CI 0.60–0.97, p < 0.001) and week 4 (1.14, 95% CI 0.94–1.35, p < 0.001; week 4 normal types 3–4 reached 84.3% vs 24.3%). Table 5 contains a printed discrepancy at week 1 (0.70 with 95% CI -0.01 to 0.21, p = 0.056). No complications or adverse events beyond early taste/odor withdrawals were reported. There was no oil-free placebo arm and neurological disease including Parkinson disease was excluded.
