Title: Extra-virgin olive oil and constipation outcomes after cardiac bypass surgery
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40642904/
Publisher: https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jcim-2024-0256/html
PMID: 40642904
DOI: 10.1515/jcim-2024-0256
Publication date: 2025-07-14
Date basis: Electronic publication in the NLM record. Journal issue September 2025.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24
Review depth: Original author abstract at NLM and publisher, with publisher disclosures. The full methods and results tables were unavailable through the public publisher view.

The randomized study assigned 110 adults scheduled for coronary artery bypass surgery to a group that received extra-virgin olive oil with food or to usual care. The report describes a visual pain scale and Bristol stool form assessments across the perioperative period and through 30 days after surgery. The authors report greater reductions in average pain and constipation scores in the olive-oil group (p<.05). The abstract provides neither arm-by-time estimates and intervals nor enough detail to determine how it converted Bristol stool form to a constipation score. Analyzed group sizes were unavailable in the abstract.

The between-group result is favorable in its postoperative population. The comparator is usual care and the tested regimen is dietary oil with food. Generalization to chronic Parkinson constipation is limited by surgery-related bowel changes, different clinical conditions, and incomplete outcome detail. Keep the paper as contextual evidence for Round 1 claim 0072, separate from the Joukar active-oil comparison and other Parkinson whole-diet trials.
