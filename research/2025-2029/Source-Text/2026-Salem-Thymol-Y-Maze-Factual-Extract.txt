Isolated thymol and Y-maze performance in juvenile rats
Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1728908/full
PMID: 41684518
DOI: 10.3389/fphar.2025.1728908
Coverage: Original Methods and Figure 1A

Salem and colleagues first published January 28, 2026. The 50 juvenile male rats were allocated among control, propionic-acid exposure, isolated thymol, probiotic and combined groups. Figure 1A reports six plotted animals per group. Spontaneous alternation was 49.2% lower in the propionic-acid group relative to normal controls, and 35% higher with isolated thymol than in the exposed comparison. The outcome is Y-maze alternation, which reflects spatial working memory and exploration. The study does not directly measure clinical attention, Parkinson symptoms or fresh thyme ingestion.
