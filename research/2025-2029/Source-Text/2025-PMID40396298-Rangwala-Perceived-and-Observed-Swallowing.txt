Title: Association between Perceived Dysphagia Symptoms and Swallowing Physiology in Parkinson's Disease
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12528960/
PMID: 40396298
PMCID: PMC12528960
DOI: 10.1002/mdc3.70129
Publication date: 2025-05-21
Coverage: Paraphrased primary article Methods and Results.

The study assessed 31 people with Parkinson disease and dysphagia. Oral residue and delayed pharyngeal swallow were each observed in 97% of participants, and 45.2% had airway invasion on at least one test swallow. The pharyngeal impairment score was associated with self-reported swallowing symptoms (model R²=.49) and swallowing-related quality of life (model R²=.36). Drooling is discussed in the clinical background. The reported results concern swallowing physiology and patient-rated swallowing, without a standalone raw-fig or drooling severity intervention.
