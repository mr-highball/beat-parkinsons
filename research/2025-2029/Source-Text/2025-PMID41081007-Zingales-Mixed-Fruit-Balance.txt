Title: Mixed whole-fruit intake and balance in adults who usually eat little fruit
PMID: 41081007
PMCID: PMC12508899
DOI: 10.1016/j.cdnut.2025.107544
Authors: Zingales et al.
First published: 2025-09-03
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12508899/
Registration: NCT05063929
Coverage: primary full article, intervention schedule and Table 4. Local factual extract.

The randomized crossover enrolled 40 healthy adults aged 30–70, of whom 38 completed both 8-week periods with an 8-week washout. The 2-cup-per-day phase provided two portions each day from a repeating menu containing banana, apple, orange, grape, strawberry and watermelon. For the final three days the fruits were blended as smoothies. During restriction, participants could consume up to one-half cup from the same list. The contrast varied several fruits at once. Severe neurological disease was among exclusion criteria.

Thirty-seven participants contributed balance testing per the Results section. Table 4 gives mean postural-sway distance for the restricted phase as 27.58 to 26.45 cm and for the higher-fruit phase as 26.04 to 26.01 cm, with time-by-phase p=.457. Variability was substantial and the article reports no significant within- or between-phase balance differences. There was a reported 4.5% within-phase improvement in dominant-hand 9-hole pegboard performance with higher fruit, without a difference relative to restriction. This test measures dexterity.

The report presents no separate raw strawberry arm, clinical Parkinson population or follow-up fall counts. This is a mixed-food balance comparison that supplies a different evidence layer from the strawberry powder trials.
