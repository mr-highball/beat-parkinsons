Title: The Effects of Curcumin on Olfactory Dysfunction and Indoxyl Sulphate in Hemodialysis Patients

This three-arm randomized placebo-controlled trial at Adam Malik Hospital (Medan, Indonesia) enrolled 48 adults aged 16-55 with stage 5 chronic kidney disease on regular hemodialysis and normal ENT exams, randomly allocating 16 participants per arm via concealed color-card draw to placebo control, oral curcumin 500 mg, or oral curcumin 1000 mg for 12 weeks. Objective olfactory function was measured using the Sniffin' Sticks battery (Threshold, Discrimination, Identification, and composite TDI score).

In Table 3 (mean ± SD 12-week delta, one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post-hoc tests), composite TDI olfactory function improved by +3.56 ± 1.92 with 500 mg curcumin (post-hoc p = 0.010 vs control) and +4.00 ± 1.86 with 1000 mg curcumin (post-hoc p = 0.002 vs control) compared with +0.875 ± 5.14 on control (overall ANOVA p = 0.002). Olfactory discrimination delta improved by +2.56 ± 1.41 (500 mg, p = 0.001) and +3.31 ± 1.07 (1000 mg, p = 0.001) versus +0.125 ± 1.25 on control. Between-arm threshold (p = 0.636) and identification (p = 0.618) deltas were inconclusive. Capsule standardization details are limited, blinding of outcome assessors is not fully detailed, and the trial studied formulated curcumin in hemodialysis patients rather than culinary grated turmeric in Parkinson disease.
