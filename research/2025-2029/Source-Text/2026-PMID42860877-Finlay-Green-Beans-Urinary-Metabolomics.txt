Primary OA Full-Text Verification (PMC13655637): Acute human feeding study (n=27) with dose-response follow-up (n=16) consuming cooked green beans (Phaseolus vulgaris L.) and collecting pre-/post-meal urine for 1H-NMR and LC-MS metabolomics. Identified 15 dose-responsive urinary intake biomarkers (including N-phenylacetylglutamine, hydroxyabscisic acid glucuronide, amino-hydroxybenzoic acid) classifying intake with 91.6% accuracy. Confirms human dietary exposure pharmacokinetics without evaluating neurological, gastrointestinal, or urinary symptoms.

[Results] In total, there were 27 participants (14 males and 13 females) enrolled on the discovery study. Of these, 26 individuals completed the green beans study visit. The discovery study participants had an average age of 23.7 ± 6.8 years and average BMI of 23.7 ± 2.8 kg/m2. Sixteen participants consumed green beans in the dose–response study (7 males and 9 females): the participants had an average age of 29.8 ± 9.6 years and average BMI of 23.8 ± 3.2 kg/m2. For the dose–response study, viable samples from all three portion sizes were obtained from 14 participants. Analysis of the 1H‐NMR data from the discovery study identified 1737 spectral regions with a significant difference across time points (FDR‐adjusted <0.05). The time–response over the 6 h of all statistically significant spectral regions were examined and resulted in 745 regions that increased in intensity following green beans consumption. These were analyzed for a response to increased consumption in the dose‐response study. [Discussion] The present study identified several urinary biomarkers displaying a dose–response relationship to green beans intake. The putative biomarkers also increased in urine following green beans intake in an acute setting. The robustness of the biomarkers is confirmed by the fact that the dose–response study was performed when participants consumed their habitual diet. Furthermore, the biomarker panel was capable of classifying individuals as low or high green bean consumers with good accuracy. The current research used a multi‐platform untargeted metabolomics approach to identify a panel of food intake biomarkers for green beans. One of the nine criteria for the validation of food intake biomarkers previously proposed is biological plausibility [23, 47]. Several of the proposed biomarkers in this study have high plausibility, including 3‐hydroxysuberic acid, hydroxyabscisic acid glucuronide, and the amino‐hydroxybenzoic acid isomer. 3‐Hydroxysuberic acid is a medium chain hydroxy acid that is functionally related to suberic acid. Suberic acid is reported to be present in common bean (Phaseolus vulgaris) [48] and the leaves of pea (Pisum savitum) [49]. In addition, the chemical formula and MS/MS spectra for 3‐hydroxysuberic acid in the present study matched with those of a previously reported urinary metabolite found after consumption of green beans [50]. Hydroxyabscisic acid glucuronide is a plausible biomarker for green bean intake as the 13‐hydroxyabscisic acid isomer was reported in cowpea [51]. Hydroxyabscisic acid glucuronide was also identified in urine following ingestion of a bilberry supplement, but this is likely to be the 8‐hydroxyabscisic acid isomer which was previously linked to berries and other fruits [52].
