The animal sedative substudy used adult male Wistar rats. Table 4.2 reports a diazepam-only control and two black-pepper-extract add-on groups. The control values were 194 minutes to regain the righting reflex and 140 minutes to lose it. The two add-on groups report 213 and 260 minutes to regain the reflex, and 64 and 40 minutes to lose it. The table gives no group-level uncertainty. The tested exposure was an ethanolic Piper nigrum seed extract co-administered with diazepam, with no extract-alone sedative comparison shown in the table. This assay measures drug-induced righting-reflex changes rather than sleep quality or clinical insomnia.

Claim 0282 | Ground black pepper and insomnia | Related animal evidence. Earlier and longer drug-induced loss of righting reflex warrants preserving the outcome, with no symptom direction assigned to eating ground black pepper in Parkinson disease.
