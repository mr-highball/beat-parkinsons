Title: Hallucinations and cognitive impairment are associated with blood pressure fluctuations in Parkinson's disease
PMID: 41835016
PMCID: PMC12983681
DOI: 10.1136/bmjno-2025-001497
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12983681/
Publication year: 2026
Coverage: Complete original JATS including clinical definitions and analyses.

The cross-sectional analysis enrolled 121 people with Parkinson disease and also compared ambulatory blood pressure with hospital controls. Forty-six of the Parkinson participants met the hallucinations or psychosis definition. The definition incorporated a combined MDS-UPDRS item threshold or prescription of an antipsychotic for those symptoms. It does not isolate the number, modality or timing of visual hallucination events.

The authors report an association of larger blood pressure fluctuations with the hallucinations or psychosis group after adjustment. The analysis also considered white matter hyperintensities, timing-based blood pressure summaries and neurogenic orthostatic hypotension. No meal composition or dried fig exposure enters the analyses. The association cannot identify which causal direction explains the observed clinical relationship.
