Title: Effect of pumpkin seed extract on premenstrual syndrome pain intensity in adolescent girls
Source: https://ejournal.poltekkes-smg.ac.id/ojs/index.php/jurkeb/article/view/12663
Original: https://ejournal.poltekkes-smg.ac.id/ojs/index.php/jurkeb/article/download/12663/4072
DOI: 10.31983/jkb.v15i1.12663
Publication date: 2025-03-19
Date basis: First journal posting. Jurnal Kebidanan 15(1), pp30–35.
Source depth: Publisher PDF indexed text for the original Methods and Tables 1–2, pp32–33. Direct PDF bytes and page images were unavailable in this review.
Reviewed: 2026-11-27

The 42-participant three-group study reports mean pain decreases of 3.86 (SD 1.46), 1.29 (SD 1.86), and 0.57 (SD 0.94) on a 0–10 scale for high-dose extract, low-dose extract, and starch. The high-dose change comparison was reported p<.001 and low-dose versus starch p=.205. Baseline NRS means were 6.29, 4.93, and 3.86. No baseline-adjusted contrast, concealed allocation, or oil fraction was established. This was a seed extract in adolescents with premenstrual symptoms.

The analysis compares ten days of Cucurbita moschata seed extract with starch. The dose shorthand is ambiguous. The paper does not characterize pressing or establish that the treatment contained an isolated oil. The higher baseline score in the high-dose group raises regression-to-the-mean concerns. The study contains no Parkinson population or effect estimate for cold-pressed pumpkin seed oil. See the 2026 same-family report, DOI 10.52365/jm.v12i1.1657.
