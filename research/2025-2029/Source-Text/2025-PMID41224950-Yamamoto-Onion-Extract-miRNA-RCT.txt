[Results] Sixty healthy Japanese participants aged 30–65 years were initially evaluated for eligibility. Twenty participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either placebo tablets (PT) or onion extract tablets (OET); 19 (PT: n = 9; OET: n = 10) completed the study. One participaint in the PT group was excluded from the analysis because of absence due to moving. No adverse or unexpected effects were observed in both groups. The clinical trial protocol and flow diagram of the study are shown in Fig. 1. All 19 test participants met the inclusion criteria and none were excluded. The characteristics of the 19 participants are shown in Table 1, and no statistically significant differences were observed between the two groups. For each participants group, plasma samples before and after 2 weeks of PT or OET intake were subjected to next-generation sequencing (NGS) for the comprehensive analysis of miRNAs (Fig. 1). The miRNAs detected in more than half of the participants in the PT and OET groups were included in the analysis. In the PT group, 173 types of miRNAs were detected before intake, 162 types were detected after intake, and 154 types were common before and after intake (Fig. 2a). In the OET group, 162 types of miRNAs were detected before intake, 186 miRNAs were detected after intake, and 156 miRNAs were common before and after intake (Fig. 2b). Additionally, 143 miRNAs were common to both the PT and OET groups before and after intake. These 143 miRNAs were analyzed in subsequent studies (Fig. 2c, Supplementary Table 1). [Discussion] Although miRNAs are involved in various biological phenomena and health promotion, the effects of food and supplements on miRNA expression in vivo remain poorly understood. Onions have been reported to have various physiological effects, such as anti-inflammatory18, antioxidant19, and immunomodulatory20. The onion extract tablet (OET) used in this study is known to alleviate male menopausal symptoms and relieve stress; however, its effect on miRNA expression in vivo is unknown. In contrast, this study is the first to identify three types of miRNAs whose expression varied with the consumption of OET; among them, miR-106b-5p was the most capable of discriminating between the placebo tablet (PT) and OET intake groups. These findings indicate that OET can alter plasma miRNA levels in vivo. Further analysis is needed to determine how this ability is related to the bioregulatory function of these tablets. In this study, the DESeq method, which is the most common method for analyzing changes in miRNA expression, was used. Nine miRNAs whose expression varied only in the OET group did not differ significantly between the PT and OET groups at any time point after intake. Even if miRNAs that changed significantly in the OET group but not in the PT group were selected, baseline changes may have made it difficult to capture the original changes caused by the tablet. Dietary studies have focused on the changes before and after intake. A study demonstrating that the Mediterranean diet is effective for weight loss via miRNAs21 showed changes in miRNA expression before and after the intake of food components. [Conclusions] Herein, we investigated the effects of a 2-week consumption of OET on plasma miRNA expression in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study. Although the small sample size limited the statistical power and generalizability of the results, miR-106b-5p, miR-339-3p, and miR-181b-5p levels were significantly higher in the OET group than in the PT group. ROC analysis of these miRNAs showed that miR-106b-5p was the most discriminative miRNA between the two groups. Furthermore, we obtained information on the biological pathways associated with the target genes of these miRNAs; however, functional validation was not conducted in this study. Therefore, we cannot conclude whether the physiological effects of OET are regulated through these putative pathways. Further research is necessary to determine whether the proposed pathways are involved. This is the first study to show that onion extracts modulate miRNA expression in humans. Our findings also suggest that these miRNAs are potential biomarkers for assessing the intake of onion components.
