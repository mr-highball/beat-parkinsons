Title: Challenges of Pain in Parkinson’s Disease: Results from the OCEAN Study
Source: https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70390
Open publisher version: https://research.unipd.it/handle/11577/3616660
PMID: 42259665
DOI: 10.1002/mds.70390
First published online: 2026-06-08
Citation: Chaudhuri et al., Movement Disorders, 2026.
Text coverage: project extraction of study design and reported statistical results.

OCEAN was a 24-week randomized, double-blind trial of opicapone 50 mg daily versus placebo as an adjunct to levodopa in people with Parkinson’s disease and wearing-off-associated pain. The prespecified primary endpoint was the change from baseline in domain 3 of the King's Parkinson's Disease Pain Scale (KPPS), which assesses fluctuation-related pain. The matching 2022 protocol is PMID 35279112, DOI 10.1186/s12883-022-02602-8. EudraCT 2020-001175-32 and ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04986982 describe the same protocol. The registry first posted results on 2025-03-28. The local record is research/2020-2024/Source-Text/Trials/NCT04986982.txt.

Of 144 participants enrolled, 64 opicapone and 63 placebo participants entered the safety set, and 59 and 63 entered the full analysis set. At week 24, the primary domain-3 score changed by −8.8 points (SD 6.9) and −9.3 points (SD 6.2), respectively. The adjusted difference for opicapone minus placebo was +0.30 points (SE 1.14, 95% CI −1.96 to +2.55, p = 0.7940). The corresponding registry changes were −8.8 (SD 6.85) and −9.3 (SD 6.24).

Changes in the total KPPS were −18.1 (SD 19.5) and −16.1 (SD 16.4), with an adjusted difference of −0.91 (95% CI −6.6 to +4.8, p = 0.7538). Total MDS-NMS changes were −28.4 (SD 49.3) and −27.1 (SD 55.1), p = 0.9391. These between-group results did not establish superiority on the primary pain endpoint. The exposures were a medication and placebo, so the study supplies clinical context for fluctuation-related pain and no comparison of dried dates, sage, or other culinary exposures.
