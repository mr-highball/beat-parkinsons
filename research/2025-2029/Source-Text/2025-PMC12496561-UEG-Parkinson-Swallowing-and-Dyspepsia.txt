November 22 access follow-up. The publisher and PMC identify the complete poster section as S803–S1476. The publicly hosted ten-page candidate has an oral-presentations PDF title and some visible sections. It does not authenticate the separate complete poster component. It does not cover that published extent. The 39 remaining mapped pairs require verification against the complete original poster section or individual matched members.

PP0011, printed p. 808. Sasegbon and colleagues screened adult hospital admissions in a February 2022 to February 2023 period. They excluded known pre-existing dysphagia. New dysphagia was recorded in 189 of 4,475 in the infection group and 54 of 865 in the sepsis group. Parkinson disease was associated with higher odds of recorded dysphagia in an adjusted model (OR 3.88, p<.001). The same author group, window, denominators and result recur in the 2026 article, PMID 41779180, including its ascertainment and regression tables. Treat these as one cohort. This measures dysphagia occurrence in a hospital context without an assigned food, ingestion dose or ingredient-specific symptom rating.

PP0021, printed pp. 814–815. Jandee and colleagues obtained gastrointestinal questionnaires in 76 people with Parkinson disease. Table 1 gives an adjusted odds ratio of 2.96 (95% CI 1.01 to 8.71, p=.043) for a Gastroparesis Cardinal Symptom Index of at least 9 among participants with at least six years of disease. Eight underwent scintigraphy and one showed definite delayed gastric emptying. The investigators report no correlation between questionnaire score and physiologic testing in those eight. The 82.9% symptom figure cannot be read as confirmed gastroparesis in the whole group. The questionnaire set includes PAC-SYM, with no ingredient-stratified constipation estimate and no assigned food exposure. The independently referenced PASS-GI trial, NCT02775591, tests DA-9701 in a separate cohort.

PP0024, printed p. 816. Phang and colleagues summarize previous functional dyspepsia studies and supply no new participant comparisons. Their narrative gives a favorable p-value under cinnamon oil while their table says both trial arms improved. The original Zobeiri report compared a cinnamon-bark macerate in sesame oil with sesame oil alone. Its upper abdominal pain item does not show a clear incremental benefit (p=.69). The ginger–artichoke result refers to Giacosa et al., PMID 25954317, and the ginger–perilla result to the 2020 Di Pierro single-group series. These involve different extracts and endpoints. The primary sources control effect interpretation and should not be counted again as new studies through this poster.
