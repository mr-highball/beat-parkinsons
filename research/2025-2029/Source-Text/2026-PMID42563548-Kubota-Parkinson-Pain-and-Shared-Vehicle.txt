Title: Cannabis-Based Oil for Pain and Other Non-Motor Symptoms in Parkinson's Disease: A Randomized Controlled Trial
PMID: 42563548
DOI: 10.1002/mds.70449
Source: https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.70449
Registration: RBR-3tvjjkx
First published: 2026-08-07
Reviewed: 2026-11-22 UTC
Coverage: Factual digest of the original journal Methods, Results and Figure 1.

The investigators randomized 101 people with Parkinson-related chronic pain to a CBD/THC formulation or placebo. The modified intention-to-treat analysis included 99 participants, and 87 completed the nine-week study. The primary comparison used the Parkinson Disease Pain Classification System score at the final visit. The between-arm comparison was inconclusive (p = .757). Figure 1 reports a day-63 effect size of 0.09 with a 95% interval from -0.44 to 0.61.

Both arms used the same medium-chain triglyceride base and the same excipient. The study compares the assigned cannabinoid formulation with its placebo. It provides no independently estimated effect of the shared oil base. The study population and pain measure are relevant to assessing Parkinson pain, with no dried-date intervention or ingredient-specific evidence for claim 0502.
