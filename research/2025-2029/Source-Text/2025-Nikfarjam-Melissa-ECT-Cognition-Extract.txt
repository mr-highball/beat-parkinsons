Title: Evaluation of the Effect of Melissa officinalis L. on Cognitive Impairments in Major Depressive Disorder Patients Treated with Electroshock Therapy
DOI: 10.34172/jsums.931
Source: https://j.skums.ac.ir/PDF/jskums-27-94.pdf
First published: 2025-09-08.
Text coverage: Official journal PDF, original Methods, Table 4 and ethics section.

Printed page 99 explicitly identifies IRCT20180613040083N1. The study reports 70 participants, 35 per arm, and tests one month of dried Melissa leaf powder capsules versus wheat starch. Both groups receive electroconvulsive therapy and usual depression-related care.

Table 4, p.98, reports MMSE cognition. Active and comparator mean scores were 24.46 and 24.86 at baseline, 24.21 and 24.10 after intervention, and 24.66 and 25.71 at two-month follow-up. The reported group-by-time interaction was F=.865, p=.356. The article reports cognitive outcomes in a depressed population. It provides no separate measure of depression symptom response and no Parkinson or brewed-tea comparison.
