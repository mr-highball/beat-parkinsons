Primary Abstract:
BACKGROUND: Cerebral autosomal dominant arteriopathy with subcortical infarcts and leukoencephalopathy (CADASIL) is the most common monogenic small vessel disease, causing recurrent stroke and progressive cognitive decline, with no disease-modifying treatment available. Tocotrienols, members of the vitamin E family, have demonstrated neuroprotective effects in preclinical studies. We evaluated the efficacy and safety of a tocotrienol-rich vitamin E complex (HOV12020) in patients with CADASIL. METHODS: In this randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 51 patients (median age (range): 58 years (43-73 y), male (n = 24, 47%)) with genetically confirmed CADASIL were assigned (1:1) to receive HOV12020 or placebo for 24 months. The primary endpoint was treatment failure, defined as any stroke occurrence, disability progression, or cognitive decline, and was analysed within a Bayesian framework to estimate the probability of treatment benefit. The secondary endpoints included the time to the first failure event, changes in clinical and cognitive scores, and safety. Exploratory MRI endpoints were analysed using quantile regression with Wilcoxon rank-sum sensitivity tests. EudraCT 2019-002867-23; ClinicalTrials.govNCT04658823. FINDINGS: Patients were enrolled in the trial between 15/01/2021 and 11/02/2022. Treatment failure occurred in 68.0% of patients in the HOV12020 group and 61.5% in the placebo group, both exceeding the 40% placebo event rate that was initially expected. Bayesian analysis indicated a low posterior probability of benefit, meeting the predefined futility criteria. No significant differences were observed in the secondary endpoints or exploratory MRI measurements. HOV12020 was well tolerated, with an excellent safety profile and no excess of adverse events. INTERPRETATION: HOV12020 did not reduce clinical progression in CADASIL over 24 months. Bayesian analysis confirmed futility, suggesting that tocotrienol-rich vitamin E is unlikely to confer benefits in the advanced stages of the disease. This rigorously designed trial illustrated the potential of Bayesian randomised studies in a rare, slowly progressive cerebral small vessel disease as CADASIL and provides key information for the development of future preventive strategies in cerebral small vessel disease. [Outcomes] The primary efficacy outcome was the occurrence of one failure event within 24 months after the baseline. A failure event was considered when at least one of the following manifestations was detected during the study period: 1) Stroke events defined as acute clinical neurological deficits consistent with stroke, centrally adjudicated by an independent blinded expert committee, with neuroimaging (MRI or CT) required in all suspected cases; imaging lesions without new neurological symptoms were not classified as stroke events; 2) increase in disability corresponding to an increase in the Modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score of at least 1 point to result in a score of 2 or greater (a threshold widely used in clinical trials to reflect clinically meaningful disability)18,19 and judged, based on clinical assessment, to be attributable to CADASIL-related disease progression, and 3) cognitive decline corresponding to an increase of at least 4 points of the Vascular Dementia Assessment Scale Cognitive subscale (VADAS-Cog) score20,21 (a threshold pragmatically derived from ADAS-Cog–based trials).22 Adjudicated strokes were categorized by a blinded, independent Central Adjudication Committee (CEC) according to a predefined charter. [Results] A total of 55 patients were initially enrolled (Fig. 1) at the National Referral Centre for rare cerebrovascular diseases CERVCO (https://cervco.fr/) in Paris, France, between 15/01/2021 and 11/02/2022, follow-up of the last patient ended on the 26/02/2024. All participants had a genetically confirmed diagnosis of CADASIL. The initiation of the trial was delayed by the COVID-19 pandemic; although the study had been largely prepared beforehand, it could only be initiated after the end of the pandemic period, and once recruitment started, study conduct and follow-up were not materially affected. Four patients, who entered the screening period of up to 28 days, failed screening because predefined inclusion or exclusion criteria were not met and 51 patients were subsequently randomised (25 and 26 patients in the HOV12020 and placebo groups, respectively). A total of 46 patients completed the study, and 5 patients withdrew before study completion. A similar proportion of patients withdrew from the study in the HOV12020 group (3/25 patients) and the placebo group (2/26 patients). The demographics and baseline characteristics of the patients are summarised for the FAS in Table 1. Approximately half of the patients were male (28/55 patients), with a mean age of 58 years (range: 45–73 years) and mean BMI of 28 kg/m2 [SD: 5 kg/m2]. Baseline characteristics, including demographic data, clinical history, and prior medications did not differ between the HOV12020 and placebo groups (Table 1). The use of concomitant medications at baseline was limited and comparable across treatment arms: hypolipidemic agents (statins) were taken by 2 patients (10%) in the HOV12020 group versus 4 (20%) in the placebo group; antithrombotic/antiplatelet therapy by 2 (10%) versus 3 (15%); and antihypertensive therapy by 2 (10%) versus 1 (5%), respectively. These medical history details are summarised for the FAS in Appendix—Table A. Treatment compliance was 98%, with no difference between the HOV12020 and placebo groups.
[Change in patient reported outcomes] A very slight increase in the mean total DAD score for the HOV12020 group was observed compared to a small decrease for the placebo group of −2.2 at Month 24 (Tables 3 and 4). No significant changes in the median score or median percentage were detected. A slight reduction of mean SF36 scores was observed in each group.
[Safety] A total of 172 treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAE) were reported for 46 patients (90.2%) during the study (Table 5), with TEAEs reported for a similar proportion of patients in the HOV12020 and placebo groups. Any TEAE reported during the study was considered to be related to the study treatment. The most common AE was cognitive disorder (18 patients [35.3%]), with a slightly higher proportion of patients reporting this type of event in the HOV12020 group than in the placebo group. No haemorrhagic or bleeding-related safety signal was observed during the trial. Four fatal TAEs were reported in three patients (5.9%). Of the four fatal AEs, three were associated with severe clinical complications attributable to CADASIL (dysarthria, dysphagia, and severe motor dysfunction), and one was related to lung adenocarcinoma diagnosed after entry into the clinical trial. Twenty-seven severe TEAEs were also reported in eight patients (15.7%). With the exception of cognitive disorder, dysarthria, and motor dysfunction, no other severe TEAE was reported in more than one patient. Overall, there were 32 other serious TAEs reported for 22 patients (43.1%), of which the majority were mild or moderate in severity, most commonly reported as ischaemic stroke or cerebrovascular accident which was expected for a CADASIL patient population. Three TEAEs led to withdrawal in two patients (3.9%); they were moderate in severity and also expected for the study population (dysphagia, hemiplegia, and ischaemic stroke). Finally, there was no notable difference between patients in the HOV12020 and placebo groups in terms of the occurrence of severe TEAEs, TEAEs leading to death, serious TEAEs, and TEAEs leading to withdrawal. Overall, no notable trends were detected in the haematology data, clinical chemistry parameters, or coagulation data. There was no notable difference between the HOV12020 and placebo groups. No notable differences were detected between the HOV12020 and placebo groups in terms of vital signs, physical examinations, ECG data, and concomitant medications. [Discussion] In this randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in patients with CADASIL, treatment with a tocotrienol-rich vitamin E complex did not reduce the occurrence of the primary clinical endpoint over 24 months, and the posterior probability of experiencing a failure event over this period did not differ between the two groups. The absence of treatment effect is unlikely to be attributable to insufficient statistical power, as the observed incidence of primary endpoint events was substantially higher than anticipated. Specifically, failure events occurred in 68% of patients assigned to active treatment and in 62% of those receiving placebo, compared with the 40% event rate assumed in the Bayesian design. This unexpectedly high event rate most plausibly reflects selection bias inherent to rare-disease trials, in which individuals with recent or clinically meaningful disease progression are more likely to be referred to, or volunteer for therapeutic studies conducted in expert centres—often requiring long-distance travel—than patients represented in historical cohorts. In addition, differences in event ascertainment are likely to have contributed to the elevated number of detected events. In the present trial, all cerebrovascular events were prospectively and systematically adjudicated using combined clinical and imaging criteria, capturing not only strokes with persistent neurological deficits but also transient neurological symptoms associated with acute ischaemic lesions on MRI that may have been classified differently or incompletely captured in historical datasets. Importantly, however, these factors would be expected to affect both treatment groups similarly and therefore do not account for the lack of separation between the groups. Consistent results in both the full analysis set and the per-protocol population further support this interpretation and strengthen confidence in the robustness of the overall findings. In this context, the composite endpoint combines outcomes that may differ in their clinical relevance.
