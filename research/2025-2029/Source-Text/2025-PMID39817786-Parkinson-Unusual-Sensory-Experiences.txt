Title: Paranoia and unusual sensory experiences in Parkinson's disease
PMID: 39817786
DOI: 10.1080/13607863.2025.2450258
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39817786/
Publisher full article: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/13607863.2025.2450258
First publication date: 2025-01-16 (electronic publication in NLM record).
Coverage: Complete publisher article, including questionnaire descriptions and item-level tables.

A cross-sectional questionnaire battery recruited people with Parkinson disease through Parkinson's UK, social media and local groups. The analysis included 369 people who completed over 90% of the questionnaire, including 343 online and 26 postal responses. The cohort mean time since diagnosis was five years.

The Parkinson-specific psychosis and hallucination questionnaire measured frequency and distress over the preceding month. At least one unusual sensory experience was reported by 182 participants (49%) and 83 (23%) reported some distress. The authors distinguish corner-of-vision experiences, misperceptions and sensed presences from other visual or nonvisual items. The trigger questionnaire includes setting, mood, boredom and nighttime, with an option to specify another trigger. The article does not analyze fig exposure or report visual episode frequency by any culinary preparation. The pooled sensory percentage should not be used as the rate of formed visual hallucinations.
