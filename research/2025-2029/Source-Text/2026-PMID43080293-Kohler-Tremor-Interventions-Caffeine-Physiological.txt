Verified PubMed abstract: BACKGROUND: Machine learning in tremor time-series analysis has so far been applied to differentiate tremor disorders. Respective analytical approaches can similarly be geared to explore therapeutic interventions, which conventionally are assessed by their induced amplitude changes. This may overlook subtle signal alterations. Feature-based machine learning offers the potential to explore much more granular tremor signal changes induced by therapeutic interventions. OBJECTIVE: To investigate multidimensional time-series signal changes associated with tremor-modulating interventions, and to identify shared and distinct intervention-specific patterns across tremor conditions. METHODS: Sixty-six participants (Essential Tremor (ET): propranolol n = 13, deep brain stimulation (DBS) n = 10; Parkinson's Disease (PD): levodopa n = 23; enhanced Physiological Tremor (ePT): caffeine n = 20) were assessed in therapeutic OFF and ON states using high-resolution tri-axial accelerometry. In addition to established tremor characteristics, advanced time-series features were extracted from raw recordings. Machine Learning (ML) approaches, including ensemble classification and Principal Component Analysis (PCA), were applied to detect intervention-related changes. RESULTS: Significant clinical and signal-based intervention effects were observed across cohorts with the strongest tremor suppression following DBS in ET and expected tremor enhancement following caffeine in ePT. While amplitude-related features decreased consistently after therapeutic interventions, dopaminergic treatment in PD was additionally associated with changes in frequency-related characteristics. Caffeine-induced tremor modulation formed a distinct multidimensional signal pattern, whereas therapeutic interventions showed partially overlapping effects. No single feature consistently characterized successful tremor modulation across all interventions. CONCLUSION: Feature-based ML reveals intervention-specific tremor patterns beyond conventional measures, supporting data-driven approaches for objective tremor characterization.

[Materials and methods] A total of 66 participants ≥18 years of age were examined at the Department of Neurology, University Hospital Wuerzburg, in therapeutic OFF and ON states under the following interventions: ET treated with propranolol (ET med n = 13), ET treated with DBS following insufficient response to oral medication (ET DBS n = 10), PD treated with levodopa (PD n = 23), and ePT following caffeine intake (ePT n = 20). Clinical and demographic data were available for all participants. Diagnoses were established on the basis of at least two independent clinical evaluations (1, 2). The study protocol was approved by the local ethics committee (IRB University of Würzburg, Nr. 217/21) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to inclusion according to local ethical regulations. All participants underwent prospective tremor recordings using wearable high-resolution tri-axial accelerometers (Biometrics Ltd., ACL500; 2,000 Hz sampling frequency) attached to the dorsum of the middle finger of both hands. Tremor was assessed in four standardized hand positions capturing resting, postural, and intention tremor. Patients with the clinical diagnosis of PD and ET and individual clinically optimized treatment regimens were examined in clinically defined OFF and ON states following a standardized withdrawal period of tremor-modifying medication. Levodopa was withheld for 5 h, propranolol overnight, and primidone, benzodiazepines, and dopamine agonists for 24 h before OFF recordings. Medication was administered according to a standardized dosing regimen corresponding to 1.5 times the individual daily morning dose of the respective tremor-modulating medication. [Discussion] This study investigated the effects of several frequently used therapeutic interventions on tremor accelerometry signal using established tremor characteristics and ML–based feature analysis. The findings provide first insights into intervention-related changes in tremor signals across different tremor entities and highlight both shared and distinct patterns of tremor modulation. Machine learning approaches in tremor research so far concentrated on diagnostic stratification (19). Feature-based machine learning is a relatively novel and powerful analytical concept to explore time-series signal changes beyond simple standard tremor metrics, which has been shown to allow the identification of therapeutic mechanisms of non-invasive brain stimulation for tremor (21). The current study successfully applied this method in the comparative analysis of different standard tremor interventions across the most frequent tremor diagnoses.
