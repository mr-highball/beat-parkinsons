Study design and findings
- Combined network pharmacology, molecular docking, and in vivo MPTP-induced Parkinson's disease mouse validation testing emulsion-solvent-evaporation curcumin nanoparticles.
- Behavioral tests (Figure 7) evaluated open-field total distance traveled, total movement time, center time, spontaneous locomotor activity counts, and Morris water maze alongside Akt signaling, hippocampal histopathology, and 16S rRNA gut microbiota profiling.
- Curcumin nanoparticles improved open-field locomotor performance and spontaneous activity counts in MPTP mice, reduced oxidative stress and neuronal apoptosis via Akt signaling, and remodeled gut microbiota composition.

Relevance and limits for Parkinson's food experiments
- Additional 2026 preclinical curcumin-delivery PD motor dataset relevant as indirect context for ground turmeric bradykinesia (0507) and pain/neuroprotection records.
- Limits: Engineered curcumin nanoparticles rather than culinary ground turmeric; MPTP mouse model without human Parkinson clinical bradykinesia measurement.
