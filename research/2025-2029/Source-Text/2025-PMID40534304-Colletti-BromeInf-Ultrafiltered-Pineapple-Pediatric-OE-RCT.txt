Primary Abstract:
A freeze-dried pineapple extract-Brome-Inf, from pineapple by-products of industrial rings production, was enriched in bioactive polypeptides and bromelain by membrane ultrafiltration. Orchiepididymitis (OE), a painful pediatric pathology, was treated with this extract. A pilot, double-blind, randomized study involving 60 pediatric OE patients compared the effects of Brome-Inf to a placebo over a 15-day period, in conjunction with antibiotic treatment. In the present study, the primary objective was to assess the disparity in NSAIDs usage and pain intensity between the Brome-Inf group and the placebo group, utilizing the Wong-Becker, Numerical Rating Scale (NRS), and Visual Analogue Scale (VAS) pain rating scales. Results revealed a significant reduction in VAS and NRS within the Brome-Inf group from Days 1 to 15 compared with placebo. A rapid regression of edema was observed in the active group after 1 week and at the end of the study. We can conclude that oral supplementation with Brome-Inf emerges as a promising co-management option for OE for its action on pain and edema, potentially enhancing the quality of life for affected children. Importantly, due to its well-balanced fruity flavor, the palatability of Brome-Inf was appreciated, ensuring optimal patient compliance and treatment safety.

Verified Round 6 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Double-blind RCT (Mol Nutr Food Res 2025; PMC12490183) in n=60 pediatric acute orchiepididymitis patients adding cold-ultrafiltered (4 C, 5000 Da, tap-water cooled coil to prevent heat inactivation of bromelain, liquid-N2 freeze-dried) Brome-Inf extract to antibiotics, lowering acute scrotal VAS/NRS pain vs placebo. Non-culinary cold-concentrated extract in acute pediatric infection; directly underscores thermal destruction of bromelain during cooking/grilling.

[Materials and Methods] In this work we scaled up the laboratory procedure previously reported [20]. Thoroughly washed and sized pineapples (Ananas comosus L.) at a uniform stage of ripeness were processed into fruit slices (rings) using the GINACA—TFGK‐5, a processing machine from Tropical Food Machinery srl (Busseto, Italy). This automatic cylinder‐forming machine produces cored cylinders from calibrated pineapples and is equipped with a continuous system for loading, transporting and centering the fruit as well as a processing group for peeling, cutting, and coring. The excess pineapple core and outer pulp are re‐used for juice production, while the peels are discarded. The cored cylinders and pulp were immediately cooled to 4°C, mechanically blended using a pilot blender (Waring Commercial, Stamford, CT, USA) and centrifuged at 5000 g at 4°C (Beckman Instruments, Palo Alto, CA, USA) to effectively separate insoluble particles from the juice. A dry matter analysis was performed on 50 g of juice, which was dried to a constant weight overnight at 105°C in a laboratory drying oven according to AOAC official method 922.10. The resulting residue was quantified and expressed as a percentage of the starting material, expressed in grams of solids per 100 g of fresh pineapple. The centrifuged and filtered juice was concentrated by ultrafiltration (5000 Da, at 5 bar) in a semi‐industrial unit HydroAir Research Srl (Lodi—Italy). [Discussion] To the best of our knowledge, this is the first clinical trial that investigated the analgesic effects of oral pineapple extract in children with OE. In this context, the reduction of VAS, NRS, and Wong‐Becker (Table 3) was more significant in the active group compared with placebo after 7 and 15 days of treatment. Only 11 people of the active group needed the assumption of ibuprofen compared with 27 of placebo in T = 1 and 3 versus 19 in T = 2 (Table 2). Moreover, the average intake of grams of ibuprofen taken per person was significantly higher in placebo at T = 1, suggesting that the nutraceutical supplementation may be useful to reduce the need of NSAIDs. The analgesic effect of bromelain reported in this clinical trial is in agreement with the results of other studies, which showed the utility of this supplement in different conditions associated with pain. Zatuchni et al. [30] showed an improvement of rate of edema, inflammation, and pain in individuals affected by perineal pain.
