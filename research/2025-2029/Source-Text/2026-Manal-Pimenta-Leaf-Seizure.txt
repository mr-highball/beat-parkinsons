Oral Pimenta dioica leaf extract in mouse seizure models
Primary source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13381042/
PMID: 42165191
DOI: 10.1002/cbdv.202502906
Coverage: factual extract from primary plant authentication, preparation and animal outcome sections. The text below records source facts and scope.

Manal and colleagues published this study on May 21, 2026. They authenticated Pimenta dioica leaves under voucher FPH-UG06, dried them and prepared a hydroalcoholic extract. Swiss albino mice received oral extract in maximal electric-shock and pentylenetetrazole seizure experiments. The high extract level shortened tonic flexion in the electric-shock model. A separate acute oral toxicity test recorded no general toxicity signs during the first 24 hours and no deaths during the 14-day follow-up. Those observations cover gross short-term signs and induced seizure outcomes under the studied conditions. Ground berry consumption, a Parkinson population and the five frozen outcomes were outside the study.
