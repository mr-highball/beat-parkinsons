Title: The effect of saffron supplementation on indices of oxidative stress, inflammation, mental health, and quality of life in patients with Parkinson's disease: a randomized, triple-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial

Coverage: Selected primary methods, author-abstract outcomes, and linked protocol and registry facts.

First published December 24, 2025. In the same Isfahan cohort of 92 randomized adults, adjusted AMT total difference was +0.78 (95% CI 0.16 to 1.40, p = .01) and PDQ-39 cognitive quality-of-life domain difference was −8.07 (95% CI −14.39 to −1.75, p = .01). Intervention used 100 mg daily of milled Sargol stigma powder in a tablet. These are a brief global cognitive screen and a separate patient-rated domain. No executive-function subscore is available. This note verifies separate outcomes from the same trial.

The intention-to-treat trial abstract reports adjusted 12-week DASS depression difference −4.58 points (95% CI −8.34 to −0.81, p=.01) and fatigue difference −9.20 points (95% CI −13.76 to −4.65, p<.001), both favoring the whole-powder tablet over placebo. The linked protocol identifies the 16-item Parkinson Fatigue Scale and verifies the Sargol dose. Trial planning used a biomarker for sample size, and these were secondary symptoms. The original public registry included the depression domain. The first archived outcome revision naming the standalone fatigue scale follows receipt of the outcomes manuscript. The recovered September 2024 submission omits the separate scale, and the August 2025 revised submission includes it. The exact study completion date remains unavailable. The fixed tablet, supplier grade and follow-up duration leave effects at food quantities unresolved.
