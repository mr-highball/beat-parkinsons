Title: Neural Correlates of Cognitive Alterations and Minor and Structured Hallucinations in Parkinson's Disease
Source: https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.06.26357376v1.full
DOI: 10.64898/2026.07.06.26357376
Publication date: 2026-07-10
Source depth: complete original medRxiv JATS body, version 1 preprint; local research digest

Investigators analyzed 53 people with diagnosed Parkinson disease from a larger multicenter project. A structured interview identified 18 with recurrent minor hallucinations, 16 with recurrent structured hallucinations with or without minor symptoms, and 19 with none. The structured group included multiple sensory modalities. The comparison examined neuropsychological testing, resting-state connectivity and experimental robot-induced sensed presence. One multivariate pattern distinguished combined symptomatic groups from the group without hallucinations (FDR-adjusted p = 0.04, 20.6% of model covariance). The largest contribution of significant links was between subcortical and visual regions. The group labels and provoked presence endpoint cannot provide separately counted spontaneous visual hallucinations after food exposure. The source cites the ocular study for background and does not report using its participants.
