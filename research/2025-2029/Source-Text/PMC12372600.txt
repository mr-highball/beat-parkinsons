Title: A 69-Year-Old Male with Subacute Gait Disturbance—Blastocystis hominis Causing Extensive Colitis with Thiamine Deficiency
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12372600/
PMID: 40019110
PMCID: PMC12372600
DOI: 10.1002/mdc3.70023
Publication date: 2025-02-28
Coverage: Selected factual text distilled from the original PMC article HTML and embedded figures or tables; supplementary files are separate.

An adult with enterocolitis had subacute cerebellar gait dysfunction, tremor and visual perceptual disturbances. The examination supported cerebellar dysfunction. Colitis was evaluated by colonoscopy and clinical laboratory testing. Neurologic improvement followed vitamin replacement, and the authors reported clinical and intestinal recovery at follow-up after additional treatment.

Serum thiamine was not measured directly. The paper discusses possible infection-related cerebellar dysfunction. It does not identify idiopathic Parkinson disease, a dried-fig or date exposure, or an ingredient-specific symptom comparison. The authors described a sequence of care in one case.
