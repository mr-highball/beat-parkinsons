Primary Abstract:
BACKGROUND: Pistachios contain key nutrients and bioactive compounds that may be particularly relevant for cognition health. Although short-term pistachio intervention trials have demonstrated improvements in cardiometabolic risk factors implicated in cognitive decline, the potential cognitive effects of pistachio consumption remain to be adequately elucidated. METHODS AND DESIGN: This remote-based, 6-month randomized controlled trial will test the effects of a pistachio-enriched diet (1.25 oz./d), compared to usual diet, on cognitive function in older US adults (target n = 80). Key eligibility criteria include: active participation in the COcoa Supplement and Multivitamin Outcomes Study (COSMOS), age 65-80 years, current nut intake of ≤1 serving/week, and being free of dementia or recent cardiovascular disease, cancer, or changes in relevant medications. The primary outcome is Mobile Toolbox fluid cognition composite scores, derived by averaging standardized test scores across five domains (associative memory, episodic memory, working memory, processing speed, executive function). Secondary outcomes include individual cognitive domain scores, cardiometabolic risk factors (body weight, waist circumference, blood pressure, fasting glucose, lipids, interleukin-6, insulin-like growth factor 1), and life satisfaction. DISCUSSION: Results from this trial will contribute to the growing literature on the cardiometabolic effects of pistachio consumption and may offer insight into its potential role in cognitive function, life satisfaction, and the mechanisms by which it could influence these interrelated domains of health in older adults. Findings will be interpreted in the context of a modest sample size and other pragmatic study design considerations. TRIAL REGISTRATION: This trial is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT06992544).

[METHODS] This is a pragmatic remote-based, parallel-arm, open-label RCT to test the effects of a pistachio-enriched diet, compared to usual diet, on 6-month changes in cognitive function, cardiometabolic risk factors, and life satisfaction in older US adults (target n = 80). The parallel-arm design and trial duration ensure we can reliably and adequately evaluate any changes in cognitive function beyond the practice effects by trial participants when taking neurocognitive tests, typically undermining cross-over designs with wash-out periods. Assessments will be conducted at baseline, 3 months (subjective adherence and adverse events only), and 6 months via the MyCap application (for cognitive assessments), REDCap questionnaires (for life satisfaction, adherence, adverse events, dietary, and other phenotypic assessments), and at local Quest Diagnostics sites (for biometric measurements and biospecimen collection, which will be returned to our biorepository). After all baseline assessments are completed, participants will be randomized to either the pistachio-enriched diet or usual diet group. The participant flow chart is shown in Figure 1. The trial was approved by the Mass General Brigham (MGB) Institutional Review Board (IRB #2024P002479, approved 20 January 2025), and informed consent will be obtained for all participants prior to enrollment. Funding for this study was provided by the American Pistachio Growers, who had no role in study design, data collection, analysis, or interpretation. We will recruit participants from the COcoa Supplement and Multivitamin Outcomes Study (COSMOS; ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT02422745), a completed randomized, double-blinded, placebo-controlled, two-by-two factorial trial with ongoing observational follow-up. [DISCUSSION] To our knowledge, this will be the first RCT to evaluate the effects of a pistachio-enriched diet on cognitive function, cardiometabolic risk factors, and life satisfaction in older adults. This study leverages recruitment from a large trial cohort with detailed baseline data and prior engagement in remote-based research and extends prior nut intervention research by jointly assessing changes across cognitive, cardiometabolic, and psychological domains. Findings may help clarify how pistachio consumption impacts these interrelated domains of health and inform the design of larger, longer-term trials aimed at supporting healthy aging. The trial has several limitations that warrant consideration. First, our modest sample size limits statistical power to detect small intervention effects, though it is sufficient to evaluate moderate effects. Second, the chosen pistachio dose represents a moderate, pragmatic intake aimed at enhancing adherence and minimizing excess caloric intake in an older adult population. While higher nut doses have been examined in prior studies, this approach prioritizes feasibility over maximal exposure. Moreover, this trial does not include a run-in period as participants cannot be blinded to the intervention, which could influence adherence to the pistachio intervention and introduce open-label bias. Third, despite the MTB cognitive battery demonstrating strong reliability and validity,68–72 its use in interventional research remains limited; thus, it is unclear how sensitive test scores will be in response to the pistachio intervention. The present study will provide early evidence regarding its responsiveness to dietary modification in older adults.
