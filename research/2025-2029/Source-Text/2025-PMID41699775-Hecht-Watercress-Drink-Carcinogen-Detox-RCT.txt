Primary Abstract:
Human exposure to toxicants and carcinogens occurs via polluted air, the diet, occupational settings, endogenous processes, and tobacco products. The glutathione-S-transferase detoxification pathway resulting in the excretion of mercapturic acids is one of the most important human systems for processing and excreting toxicants and carcinogens. Maximizing effectiveness of this pathway through dietary modifications can potentially decrease the impact of such exposures. We conducted a two-site clinical trial with a crossover, single blind (to participants) design, randomized to order of product receipt comparing levels of selected urinary mercapturic acids when participants consumed a drink made from freeze-dried watercress (3× daily, 2 weeks) naturally containing 2-phenethyl isothiocyanate (PEITC) vs. a placebo drink containing maltodextrin (3× daily, 2 weeks) with a 4-week washout between the treatment periods. Two hundred forty participants (157 F) were recruited and 188 (125 F, 152 non-smokers) were compliant and completed the study. Among compliant subjects, urinary mercapturic acid detoxification products of acrolein, acrylonitrile, benzene, crotonaldehyde, methacrolein, and methyl vinyl ketone increased significantly (P ≤ 0.002), with the largest increases being observed for the mercapturic acids of acrolein (65.6%) and benzene (37.3%), both P < 0.001. Consumption of a drink, three times daily, prepared from freeze-dried watercress containing PEITC (total of approximately 40 mg/day) significantly increased the detoxification of 6 of 7 environmental toxicants and carcinogens monitored. The results indicate that watercress consumption can ameliorate exposure to environmental toxicants and carcinogens. These results are particularly timely and relevant to exposures to atmospheric pyrolysis products resulting from wildfires. Clinical trial registration number: NCT03978117.

Verified OA full-text (Carcinogenesis 2025; PMC13221842; NCT03978117): two-site single-blind randomized crossover trial in 240 completing adults (188 compliant on urinary PEITC-NAC; 152 non-smokers, 36 smokers; median age 38 y) drinking freeze-dried watercress powder in water + flavor mix (4 g x 3/day releasing ~40 mg/day PEITC) vs maltodextrin placebo for 2 weeks each with 4-week washout. Significantly increased urinary mercapturic acid detoxification of 6/7 environmental toxicants including acrolein (+65.6%, p<0.001) and benzene (+37.3%, p<0.001; Table 2). Pure xenobiotic detoxification biomarker trial with a freeze-dried drink; zero neurological, cognitive, mood, sleep, GI, or Parkinson symptom outcomes.

[Methods] We conducted a cross-over (randomized to order of product receipt), single blind (to participants), two site clinical trial comparing levels of selected urinary mercapturic acids when participants consumed a watercress drink preparation vs. a maltodextrin drink preparation (placebo). See Figure 1. The watercress drink for this study and confirmation that study participants were exposed to the anticipated amounts of PEITC upon consumption of the watercress drink have been described in detail previously [21]. Watercress was donated by B&W Quality Growers, the largest producer of watercress in the U.S. It was harvested in 2020 in Tennessee and shipped to Van Drunen Farms (Momence, IL) for freeze-drying as described previously [21]. A one chamber load of 1040 lbs of fresh watercress yielded 95.9 lbs of freeze-dried product [21]. This freeze-dried watercress yielded PEITC when added to H2O at room temperature [21]. Each participant received 2 canisters of freeze-dried watercress, a pre-measured scoop, and packets of “Crystal Light®” sweetened drink mix. They mixed the freeze-dried watercress (4 g) with H2O, using the method and schedule described below, releasing about 13 - 15 mg of PEITC each time [21]. They added the Crystal Light packet (containing 4 g of flavor material) to the drink containing PEITC, and drank the mixture. Three of these watercress drinks per day provided the target dose of approximately 40 mg PEITC per day. Approval processes for human consumption of the freeze-dried watercress have been described previously [21]. A maltodextrin drink served as the placebo, chosen because it blends readily with H2O and has little flavor. [Results] A total of 240 participants completed the study (157 females and 83 males). The time course for participant enrollment was as follows: a) first telephone screen was February 22, 2021; b) first consent was March 11, 2021; c) first randomization was March 16, 2021; d) last randomization was October 24, 2023; e) last participant visit was December 19, 2023. A consort diagram is shown in Figure 2. PEITC-NAC was quantified in participants’ urine to assess compliance with the study protocol. In our previous clinical trial of PEITC, a daily dose of 40 mg was administered orally in olive oil (4 x 10 mg/dose), and 34% of this 40 mg dose, on a molar basis, was excreted in the urine as PEITC-NAC in 24 h [17]. In the current trial, we quantified first morning urinary PEITC-NAC levels on days 4, 7, and 14 of the 2-week treatment period as an estimate of compliance with consumption of 3 watercress drinks per day, which would provide about 40 mg of PEITC/day. We excluded from the final data analysis 52 participants (22%) who, based on these measurements, excreted ≤ 8% of the expected daily amount of PEITC-NAC from a 40 mg daily dose of PEITC [e.g., indicating that they consumed less than about one quarter (8%/34%) of their watercress drinks]. [Discussion] This is the first randomized clinical trial to evaluate a watercress drink for its potential cancer prevention activity resulting from increased carcinogen detoxification. The results clearly demonstrate that consumption of a drink made from freeze-dried watercress significantly enhances the detoxification of common environmental toxicants and carcinogens by increasing their excretion in urine as non-toxic mercapturic acids. There was remarkable consistency in the results with amounts of 6 of the 7 quantified urinary mercapturic acids being significantly increased. The greatest increases in detoxification were observed for acrolein (65.6%) and benzene (37.3%) – well established environmental carcinogens to which all humans are exposed. Acrolein is considered “probably carcinogenic to humans” and benzene as “carcinogenic to humans” by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) [4, 48]. Significant increases in detoxification of the carcinogens acrylonitrile and crotonaldehyde, as well as the toxicants methacrolein and methyl vinyl ketone, were also observed.
