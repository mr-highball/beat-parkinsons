Koeda et al., March 24, 2026. Hinoki Cypress Aroma Facilitates Swallowing via Premotor and Hypothalamic Down-modulation: A Randomized Within-subject Crossover fMRI Study.
Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00455-026-10947-2
Access and coverage: Publisher full article, Methods, Figure 1, Tables 1 and 2. PMC Cloud article license was outside the archive scope.
Reviewed: 2026-11-14 local date (America/New_York).

Twenty healthy adults inhaled Boso Komeabura rice oil and hinoki cypress essential oil separately. During exposure, mean odor intensity was 0.4 (SEM 0.1) for rice oil and 2.9 (SEM 0.2) for hinoki on a 0–4 scale, P<.001. Repetitive saliva swallow count change was +0.30 (SEM 0.30) under rice oil and +1.35 (SEM 0.30) under hinoki, paired P=.011. The participant's rating of a presented odor does not measure whether olfactory impairment changed. The trial tests short odor exposure with no food or Parkinson participants. The study methods and Figure 1 give different amounts applied to the mask cotton. Manufacturer information identifies the named oil family as refined edible rice bran oil but cannot verify the lot.
