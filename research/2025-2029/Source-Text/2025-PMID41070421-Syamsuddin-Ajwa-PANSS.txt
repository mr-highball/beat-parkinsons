Title: Effect of Ajwa dates adjuvant therapy on improvement of clinical symptoms and serum interleukin-1β levels in schizophrenia patients
Source: https://www.rivistadipsichiatria.it/archivio/4583/articoli/45902/
DOI: 10.1708/4583.45902
PMID: 41070421
Publication period: September–October 2025
Coverage: Verified publisher original including Methods, participant flow description and Tables 2–3.
Reviewed: 2026-11-22 UTC

The study compared added whole Ajwa dates with usual risperidone alone. It took place at Dadi Makassar Regional Mental Hospital from June to August 2024. The report identifies 73 eligible participants and 13 withdrawals or exclusions during observation. The analysis includes 60 men, 30 per group. Matching food placebo and measured fruit maturity or drying were not reported.

Table 3 gives mean eight-week decreases in aggregate PANSS negative symptoms of 5.87 points (SD 1.91) with added dates and 3.53 (SD 1.31) with control. Table 2 endpoint means are 11.83 and 13.97. The tables give p values rounded to .000 for these contrasts, without an interval for the between-arm change. The separate negative symptom item N4 for passive or apathetic social withdrawal is not presented. The reported change establishes an observed group contrast on a composite schizophrenia measure. Whether an apathy-specific result contributed to that contrast remains open.

Cohort lineage: Firdaus’s thesis has the same intervention, sample and IL-1β outcome as the paper. Its full text has stated repository restrictions through October 29, 2027. Rismayanti’s quality-of-life trial reports a different study period, May 2023 through March 2024, with 44 participants. The different documented study periods and results support treating the published samples as distinct for this review.
