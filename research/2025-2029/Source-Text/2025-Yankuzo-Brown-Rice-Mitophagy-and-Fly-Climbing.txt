Figure 2 reports better short-distance climbing after the brown-rice feed than with iron alone. The reported threshold is p < .05 and the caption gives three replicates of 20 flies. Methods describe 6 cm in six seconds, while the figure axis describes 8 cm. The report uses 1% brown rice in experimental feed and does not establish a clinical postural-instability outcome.

The original describes five groups and a ten-day iron exposure followed by five days of the assigned diets. The assay measures negative geotaxis. It differs from a human balance or pull test.
The authors, setting, exposure schedule, arms, and endpoints overlap closely with PMID 39588846. The related report can clarify the program, but no participant-level linkage or proof of independent replication is available. Treat this article as an associated report within the existing family. The exact 2024-published article body remains unavailable.

November 26 figure and methods follow-through

Reading Figure 2 directly gives approximate mean successful climbers per 20 flies of 18.1 in controls, 13.0 in iron only, 17.9 in iron plus brown rice, 14.5 in iron plus white rice, and 18.4 in iron plus levodopa. These are graphical readings, not an underlying numerical dataset. The brown-rice-plus-iron group is marked against iron only. No brown-versus-white pairwise marker is provided.

The methods describe Damboto grain ground and sifted, with no cooking step identified. The Figure 1 caption names eight groups, while the described design and Figure 2 use five. Table 1 lists iron in rice feeds, which leaves the timing of continuing exposure incompletely resolved. A results sentence names brown rice plus levodopa, while the plot shows iron plus levodopa. The figure estimates cannot be transferred to PMID 39588846 or counted as an independent replication of that report.
