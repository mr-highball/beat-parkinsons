Title: Dietary interventions for menopausal symptoms: a systematic review.
PMID: 42790873
DOI: 10.1016/j.ajcnut.2026.101539
First published: 2026
Authors: O'Neill HM, English CJ, Polley M, Bettiol C, Esper E, Baumann O
Journal: The American journal of clinical nutrition
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42790873/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 4 review).

Key round-4 appraisal facts: 2026 systematic review of dietary interventions for menopausal symptoms noting inconsistent clinical evidence for flaxseed across vasomotor and sleep/mood domains.

Verified PubMed abstract: BACKGROUND: Menopausal transition significantly affects females's health and quality of life, largely driven by declining estrogen. Although pharmacological therapies remain the first-line treatment, interest in dietary interventions as accessible, nonpharmacological strategies is growing. OBJECTIVES: This study aims to systematically evaluate the effectiveness of dietary interventions on vasomotor, psychological, and cognitive symptoms in menopausal females. METHODS: This systematic review included 15 randomized controlled trials (n = 1952 participants) conducted according to Cochrane methodology and Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines [International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews: CRD42024610754]. Databases searched included CINAHL, Cochrane, Embase, PubMed, and PsycINFO. Eligible studies included females aged 40-65 y experiencing menopause-related symptoms (or reporting relevant outcomes) and evaluated whole food-based interventions, structured dietary patterns, or energy restriction, excluding supplement-only trials. Risk of bias was assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 tool, and certainty of evidence was assessed using Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation. Random-effects meta-analyses were conducted where appropriate. RESULTS: Twelve trials included postmenopausal females, 2 included peri- and postmenopausal females, 1 included all menopausal stages, and 1 did not specify status. Soy-based interventions may reduce hot flash frequency and severity [standardized mean difference (SMD) = -0.79; 95% confidence interval (CI): -1.34, -0.23; I2 = 69%; n = 206, 4 trials (5 comparisons)], but not overall vasomotor symptoms (SMD = -0.43; 95% CI: -1.12, 0.26; I2 = 86%; n = 259, 3 trials) or psychological outcomes (SMD = -0.21; 95% CI: -0.470, 0.05; I2 = 0%; n = 236, 3 trials); overall certainty was very low. Other dietary patterns [e.g., Dietary Approach to Stop Hypertension (DASH), lactoovovegetarian, Mediterranean-DASH Intervention for Neurodegenerative Delay] suggested modest benefits for mood and cognition in narrative synthesis. Evidence for flaxseed is inconsistent, and evidence for intermittent fasting for cognition is limited. CONCLUSIONS: Soy-based dietary interventions may modestly improve vasomotor and psychological symptoms; however, the certainty of evidence is very low. Dietary modification represents a feasible, low-risk approach, but larger, high-quality trials with standardized protocols and longer follow-up are required. This trial was registered at PROSPERO: The Impact of Dietary Interventions on Menopausal Symptoms: A Systematic Review as CRD42024610754.
