Seventy-three of the 101 Parkinson participants returned for follow-up, with 28 lost between assessments (27.6% attrition). The original baseline enrollment shared the hospital, February–July 2023 collection period, protocol reference 2284 and Parkinson sample count with the 2025 reports PMID 39812680 and PMID 40257190. These details support likely cohort reuse and a returning subset. An individual crosswalk was unavailable. Treat this largely as one cohort family with distinct comparisons.

Table 4 reports mean MDS-UPDRS item 2.2 saliva and drooling score of 1.29 at baseline and 1.44 at follow-up (p=.239). The clinician-rated posture item increased from 1.27 to 1.48 (p<.001). The swallowing score changed from 10.92 to 11.45 (p=.835). The methods required a significant change in the oral outcome before performing a progression regression, and Table 3 lists dysarthria only in the model. The paired results do not estimate a within-person posture-to-drooling association. Water, yogurt and biscuit were swallowing test materials, with no dietary intervention or fig comparison.

The article abstract and main Results differ in the reported dysarthria p value and switch the motor and cognitive predictor coefficient assignments. No predictor estimate for dysarthria is transferred to saliva. The verified S1 and S2 values concern within-group follow-up by speech-language pathology or surgery status and belong to the same cohort.
