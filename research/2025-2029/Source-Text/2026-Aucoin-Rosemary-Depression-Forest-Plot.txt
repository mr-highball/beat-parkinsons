The Aucoin group's earlier conference report screened 1,564 unique records and included 14 studies with 865 participants. Nine used inhalation and five used oral rosemary. Five studies were rated high risk of bias and six raised some concerns. The later journal abstract also reports 14 studies and 865 participants, with a different search yield of 1,858 records. Treat the poster and journal record as companion reports in one review family.

Figure 2 pools five studies for depression across 126 experimental and 125 comparison participants. The earlier random-effects estimate was a standardized mean difference of −0.29 (95% CI −1.01 to 0.42, I²=85%). Five comparisons are shown: Diego 1998, 0.20 (−0.42 to 0.82); Burnett 2004, 0.33 (−0.23 to 0.89); Nematolahi 2018, −0.30 (−0.78 to 0.18); Araki 2020, 0.00 (−0.60 to 0.60); and Azizi 2022, −1.74 (−2.39 to −1.09). Lower scores favor rosemary in the plotted orientation. In particular, the Nematolahi endpoint comparison from the poster spans zero despite the author abstract's more favorable wording. The strong Azizi endpoint contrast uses week-eight scores without incorporating the baseline imbalance documented in the original trial. The favorable oral subgroup estimate in Figure 1 is for anxiety.

The poster recovers an earlier depression analysis and the five study labels. It supplies neither the final journal forest plot nor a fresh-rosemary Parkinson subgroup. The final review's complete study table and study-level analysis choices remain open.
