Triple-blind active-controlled RCT (n=110 randomized, 49 analyzed) comparing topical Traditional Persian Medicine 4-herb cream (myrtle, chamomile, wild leek Allium ampeloprasum, Russian olive) vs lidocaine/hydrocortisone cream in grade I-II hemorrhoids: NO treatment-group effect (p = 0.50) or treatment-by-time interaction (p = 0.77). Paired with Mosavat 2015 (PMID:25608984, n=20 topical A. ampeloprasum cream: NULL on pain) and Mehdi 2021 (PMID:33825411, n=52 multi-herb topical Hemoheal cream). All non-oral topical anorectal preparations—strictly excluded from oral braised/roasted leek pain attribution.

[MATERIALS AND METHODS] Medicinal plants including the dried parts of chamomile, Russian olive, and myrtle and fresh leaves of wild leek were collected from a botanical market and identified by the Department of Agriculture at the Medicinal Plants Research Center of Barij, Kashan, Iran. Their quality was evaluated using tests for foreign materials, total ash content, acid-insoluble ash content, and quantitative assays. Wild leek juice was extracted by grinding and filtering washed leaves. Hydroalcoholic extracts of Russian olive and chamomile were prepared in a percolator using 70% ethanol, concentrated using a rotary evaporator (Heidolph, Germany), and stored under cool, dry conditions. Myrtle essential oil was hydrodistilled for 4 h in a Clevenger apparatus. For the oily phase of HC, olive oil and camel hump fat were sourced from reputable commercial stores. The physicochemical properties of the obtained extracts and essential oils were analyzed. The control medication was a CC dispensed in tubes similar to those of the HC. Every 30 g of CC contained 5 g of lidocaine, 0.275 g of hydrocortisone acetate, 3.5 g of aluminum subacetate, and 18 g of zinc oxide. The form, color, smell, consistency, and packaging of the CC were modified to reflect those of the HC for blinding. The oil and aqueous phases of the HC were heated separately, after which the aqueous phase was gradually added to the oil phase to form an emulsion. Chamomile and Russian olive extracts, fresh wild leek juice, and myrtle essential oil were incorporated via continuous stirring until the HC was formed (batch no. 9510156). Methanol, ethanol, Folin–Ciocalteu reagent, and sodium carbonate were purchased from Merck (Darmstadt, Germany). [DISCUSSION] This study evaluated the standard local treatment for hemorrhoids with a multicomponent HC formulation developed based on traditional texts and previous experimental and clinical evidence. Specifically, this randomized, triple-blind, active-controlled trial revealed that hemorrhoid-related symptom scores changed over time among participants in the per-protocol/complete-case analysis. However, the main multivariate repeated-measures analysis revealed no significant treatment-group effect or treatment-by-time interaction. Therefore, the findings do not demonstrate a statistically significant superiority of the HC over the CC. This distinction is crucial for interpreting the trial results. A statistically significant improvement within a single treatment group without a corresponding between-group difference or treatment-by-time interaction cannot be used as evidence that one intervention is superior to the other. Accordingly, the observed within-group reductions in symptoms should be considered exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory evidence of comparative efficacy.
