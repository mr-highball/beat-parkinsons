Title: Safety and stress-reducing effects of rosemary extract: an open-label trial and randomized double-blind crossover study
Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jcbn/77/3/77_25-157/_article
PMCID: PMC12646844
DOI: 10.3164/jcbn.25-157
Published online: 2025-09-10
Accessed: 2026-11-19
Text coverage: Distilled methods and Table 5 from the J-STAGE published paper.

Kuwata and colleagues studied an oral rosemary extract in a four-week open-label safety study in 22 healthy adults and a separate placebo-controlled crossover with 40 participants. The article reports no clinically relevant adverse events in the safety trial and reports stress measures. This is an extract in healthy adults, with no fresh culinary leaf intervention or Parkinson visual-hallucination frequency measure; the reported safety outcomes do not measure hallucination frequency.

Study II (UMIN000054368) analyzed 38 of 40 enrolled adults. Participants took capsules with extract from the residue left after rosemary essential-oil distillation. BDI-II was used for baseline selection and characterization. Table 5 gives estimated week-four trait anxiety scores of 46.0 on extract and 48.2 on placebo (p=.014); estimated state anxiety scores were 42.9 and 44.5 (p=.153). The latter comparison is the pair reproduced for Kuwata under anxiety in the Aucoin poster. No follow-up depression comparison was reported.
