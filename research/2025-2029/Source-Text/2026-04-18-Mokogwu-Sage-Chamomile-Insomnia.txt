Title: Dried common sage tea and primary insomnia
First published: 2026-04-18
Source: https://currentsci.com/images/articlesFile/1778339879.pdf
DOI: 10.58985/jpam.2026.v04i01.42
Text type: original-source evidence summary from complete Methods, Table 2 and Discussion

Mokogwu and colleagues compared dried common-sage leaf tea with active chamomile tea for 14 days in 30 adults with primary insomnia. Methods state ages 20–60 and a baseline Insomnia Severity Index eligibility score of 18–21. Table 2 gives mean baseline scores of 13.36 for sage and 16.14 for chamomile. Day-14 means were 6.04 and 6.36 (between-group P=.78). Day-21 means were 6.36 (SD 3.76) and 6.12 (SD 2.18), P=.83. Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index means at day 21 were 5.98 (3.71) and 4.42 (3.34), P=.41. Abstract and Methods differ on how they describe the timing of follow-up. The study has no inactive control or measured Parkinson insomnia. The Discussion reports no adverse effects in the sage group without a systematic event table. No registration ID or public supplemental dataset has been verified.

The article refers to a 2025 Thai pepper-leaf and chamomile comparison under incorrect DOI 10.26538/tjnpr/v9i5.1. The correct DOI is 10.26538/tjnpr/v9i5.14. The studies report the same pilot sample size and follow-up instruments and share several design details. They report different investigators, settings, ethics approvals and group means. The Thai record TCTR20241101001 identifies the pepper-leaf study. It supplies no registration or shared-cohort evidence for the sage paper.
