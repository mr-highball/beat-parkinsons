Title: The relationship between carrot juice consumption and constipation in pregnancy
Source: https://jurnal.unismuhpalu.ac.id/index.php/IJHESS/article/view/10930
DOI: 10.56338/ijhess.v8i2.10930
Original locator: https://jurnal.unismuhpalu.ac.id/index.php/IJHESS/article/download/10930/7315
Publication date: 2026-04-30
Reviewed: 2026-11-25
Review depth: full_text

The original report gives a 15-person single-group pre/post comparison. Twelve participants were classified as constipated before carrot juice, compared with three afterwards. No cases of diarrhea were recorded. The authors report p=0.001 from a t-test. The paper provides aggregate outcome counts without paired transitions or data allowing reanalysis. It does not specify the actual preparation, pulp retention, dose, duration or concurrent care in the intervention methods. The absence of a concurrent comparator leaves natural changes and other care unresolved. The preliminary result favors easing within this cohort. Juicing and steaming differ, and neither dose equivalence nor transfer from pregnancy-specific changes to Parkinson constipation is established. The cited 2020 Wulandari result was not linked to a verifiable original.
