Text coverage: Distilled original journal methods, Table 1, Table 2, Figure 2 and results, with conflicting and transfer-limited evidence.

In the randomized investigator-blinded parallel comparison, 40 Japanese women aged 20–49 with no more than four bowel movements per week were assigned a 72% dark chocolate or white chocolate for 14 days. Thirty-one completed and contributed to the efficacy analysis (16 dark, 15 white), with four and five participants respectively removed for poor health or protocol violations. The reported study occurred in June–July 2015.

Each arm consumed 25 g per day. The dark chocolate supplied 2.7 g of cocoa protein per serving. The white comparator contained no cocoa proteins and 2.1 g of milk protein. Energy and macronutrients were similar with several ingredient differences. This comparison establishes an observation for the tested bars and cannot isolate the effect of cocoa protein. Researchers measured diary-reported bowel movement frequency and stool amounts estimated visually. Figure 2 reports a favorable post-treatment between-arm difference in frequency for dark chocolate. Within-person stool amount rose in the dark arm from 396.2 ± 75.5 to 694.5 ± 137.5 g/week, p=0.001. White chocolate's within-arm stool-amount change had p=0.481. The test assessed neither clinical Parkinson constipation severity nor transit time.

The paper reports no conflicts of interest. Eight of 10 authors list affiliations with the manufacturer, which produced both bars. The trial registry was disclosed on March 27, 2023, after the reported trial dates. The open registry and original article are one study family. The short follow-up, self-recorded outcomes, exclusions, and absence of diagnosed Parkinson participants constrain transfer.

Contrast with Fox et al. 2019, PMID 30912735. That five-day crossover tested 100 g/day of dark chocolate against white in 16 healthy adults and reported firmer stools and a possible slowing of colonic transit. The trials differ in population, amount, duration and endpoint. Both should be considered when reviewing Round 3 claim 0918. The comparator records different aspects of bowel function, so the contrary signs do not by themselves determine the Parkinson verdict.
