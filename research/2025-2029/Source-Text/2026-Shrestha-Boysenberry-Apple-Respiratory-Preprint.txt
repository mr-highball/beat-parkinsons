7       Abstract

8       Background/Objective:
9       Seasonal         upper      respiratory        tract    infections       (URTIs)        are    common         in   children       and
10      contribute to absenteeism and reduced quality of life. Over-the-counter treatments
11      show limited efficacy and may cause adverse effects, thus warranting the need for
12      natural alternatives. This study evaluated the efficacy of a whole-fruit supplement
13      derived       from     boysenberry           and    apple      (BerriQi)       in reducing         symptom         severity       and
14      duration in school-aged children with URTIs using the Wisconsin Upper Respiratory
15      Symptoms Survey for Kids (WURSS-K).

16      Methods:
17      In this double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled study, 84 children aged 5–13
18      years      with    URTI-associated              school absence             were      assigned        to   receive      BerriQi      or
19      placebo (two chewable tablets daily) for 14 days. Outcomes included the severity and
20      duration of global illness, composite symptom and function scores.

21      Results:
22      Both groups showed progressive reductions in global illness severity, symptom, and
23      function score over 14 days.  However, the BerriQi group consistently reported lower
24      scores across all outcomes. While global illness severity did not differ significantly
25      between         groups,       total    symptom          severity       was      significantly       reduced         with    BerriQi
26      (P=0.04), and functional scores showed a trend toward improvement (P=0.05). The
27      BerriQi group experienced fewer sick days compared with placebo (8 vs. 11 days) and
28      demonstrated a higher likelihood of functional recovery (HR = 1.79, 95% CI: 1.09–
29      2.91; P=0.02).

38      1            Introduction

39      The common cold, a viral upper respiratory tract infection (URTI), is the most common
40      acute illness in childhood and a major cause of absenteeism from school and work
41      (Pappas, 2017). Children aged 5-9 years’ experience largest episodes of URTI (Sirota
42      et al., 2025), which contribute substantially to healthcare utilisation, parental work loss
43      and school absenteeism (Schot et al., 2019), particularly during colder months when
44      respiratory infections are more prevalent (Tang et al., 2025). In New Zealand, national
45      education data indicate higher school absenteeism during terms two and three (April–
46      September),           coinciding         with    peak      circulation        of   seasonal         respiratory        pathogens
47      (Ministry of Education, 2022). These non-influenza viral respiratory tract infections
48      incur a high cost in both direct healthcare expenditures and indirect costs associated
49      with absenteeism from school and work; in the USA, this annual cost exceeds 40 billion
50      dollars (Fendrick et al, 2003), rising to $170 billion when all respiratory diseases are
51      included (Nurmagambetov, 2022).

52      The treatment for URTIs mainly focuses on symptom relief that involves over-the-
53      counter       medications           such      as    antipyretics,        decongestants             and     nonsteroidal          anti-
54      inflammatory drugs, yet there is limited evidence that these therapies reduce illness
55      duration. Moreover, several of these medications have not demonstrated clear benefit
56      over placebo in paediatric populations and may be associated with adverse effects
57      (Cotton et al 2011). Dietary supplements, especially vitamin C, have been suggested
58      as a means of preventing the common cold, however, findings across studies have

59      been inconsistent. A randomised controlled study with low and high dose vitamin C
60      over a 5-year period in Japan found no reduction in the duration or severity of the
61      common cold, although the study suggested a reduction in the frequency of common
62      cold episodes (Sasazuki et al., 2006). Similarly, different parts of elderberry have been
63      used in traditional practice for respiratory illness, but its use does not reduce the
64      incidence of colds. A recent systematic review suggested that elderberries may reduce
65      the duration and severity of colds, although the certainty of  the evidence was low
66      (Wieland et al., 2021). With respect to safety, the European Medicines Agency has
67      advised that only the flowers and fully ripened berries are suitable for consumption,
68      and that adequate heating of the fruit or juice is recommended prior to use (Gro et al.,
69      2014). To the best of our knowledge, few evidence-based, child-appropriate, non-
70      pharmaceutical interventions offering consistent and reliable symptomatic relief are
71      currently available for paediatric populations. There is therefore an unmet, growing
72      clinical need for safe, natural therapeutic options that can reduce both the severity and
73      duration of URTI symptoms.

74      Epidemiological             evidence        supports        a   positive      correlation        between         high     fruit   and
75      vegetable consumption and improved pulmonary function, as well as a reduced risk of
76      chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and asthma (Kaluza et al., 2018; Park
77      and Park, 2025) These benefits are largely attributed to the high concentrations of
78      vitamins,        minerals,        and      phenolic        compounds             in   fruits     and     vegetables,           which
79      collectively confer antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties (Emran et al., 2024).

                   ®
80      BerriQi       is a whole-fruit formulation derived from New Zealand Boysenberry and apple
81      (naturally rich in anthocyanins, quercetin and procyanidins) that has demonstrated
82      efficacy       in   preclinical        models        of   allergic      airway       inflammation,           reducing        mucus
83      production, immune cell infiltration, and airway tissue damage, thereby improving lung
84      function (Shaw et al., 2020). Despite these mechanistic and in vivo data, BerriQi has
85      yet to be evaluated in the context of acute viral URTIs. Given its established preclinical
86      effects on airway inflammation, it is plausible that BerriQi may attenuate the severity
87      and     duration       of   respiratory        symptoms          in   paediatric       URTIs       and     thereby       influence
88      functional outcomes such as school attendance. Therefore, the present study was
89      designed to assess whether BerriQi reduces the perceived severity and duration of
90      URTI       symptoms          in   school       aged      children,       using      the    validated       Wisconsin          Upper
91      Respiratory Symptoms Survey for Kids (WURSS-K).

92         2            Materials and Methods

93         2.1          Study Designs and Procedures

94         This was a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled parallel trial evaluating the
95         effect     of    BerriQi®,         a   boysenberry            and      apple      blend,       on     post-respiratory-infection
96         recovery among primary-school children in New Zealand. The trial was registered with
97         the    Australia        New       Zealand         Clinical      Trials      Registry        (registration         number          ACTRN:
98         12624000398505).

99         2.1.1        Recruitment

100        Participants were recruited New Zealand-wide through social media advertisements
                      st                              th
101        from 1        August 2024 to 5                 August 2025. Interested families (caregivers) of potential
102        participants were informed about the study and provided written consent. Families and
103        caregivers were encouraged to include their children in the decision to participate in
104        the study and the children also completed assent forms. Only children for whom both
105        consent and assent forms had been completed, and who subsequently developed a
106        URTI leading to school absence, were randomised to the intervention and enrolled in
107        the study.

108        2.1.2        Participants

109        Eligible participants were children aged 5–13 years who reported URTI symptoms,
110        whose caregivers provided informed consent, and who provided informed assent.
111        Exclusion         criteria      were      allergy       to   berries       or    fruits    and      any     condition         judged        by
112        investigators to interfere with participation.

122       2.3          Interventions

123       The interventions comprised either BerriQi® or placebo chewables. BerriQi is a freeze-
124       dried powder derived from Boysenberries and apples. For placebo, the Boysenberry
125       component           was      heat-treated          to    remove         heat-sensitive          actives,       but     the     apple
126       component remained the same. Each chewable was composed of 500 mg of BerriQi,
127       417 mg xylitol, and 33 mg magnesium stearate. The placebo chewable had the same
128       composition except the Boysenberry component was heated prior to the freeze-drying
129       process.  Participants took two chewable tablets (active or placebo) daily for 14 days.
130       The     active      treatment        delivered        6.4    mg     of   actives       (anthocyanins,           quercetin        and
131       procyanidins)           per    day      (3.2    mg      per    tablet),      compared          with     0.5    mg      of   actives
132       (anthocyanins, quercetin and procyanidins) per day in the placebo (i.e., 0.25 mg per
133       tablet). The active and placebo chewables were matched for shape, colour, and size.

134       2.4          Outcome Measure

135       Wisconsin Upper Respiratory Symptoms Survey for Kids (WURSS-K) is a validated
136       and reliable illness-specific quality of life instrument focusing on both illness-specific
137       symptoms and their impact on quality of life (Schmit et al., 2021). It is a shorter and
138       simpler version of the WURSS questionnaires specifically designed for children aged
139       4-10 years to evaluate the impact of upper respiratory infections on children. It includes
140       15 items covering global illness severity (item 1), severity of symptoms (items 2-7; i.e.,
141       runny nose, stuffy nose, sneezing, sore throat, cough and feeling tired), functional
142       impacts        (items      8-14;     i.e.    thinking,      sleeping,        breathing,        talking,      walking/climbing
143       stairs/exercise, going to school, and playing with friends), and evaluation of change
144       over time (item 15) (Supplementary Figure 1). Happy- and sad-face ordinal scale
145       representations            were      included       to   help     survey       completion         by    children.       However,
146       parental help may have been required, especially for children below 8 years of age.

148       2.4.1        Primary outcome:

149       The primary aim of the study was to investigate whether BerriQi improved the severity
150       of daily global illness severity score (WURSS-K item 1: “How sick do you feel today?”;
151       1 = not sick, 4 = very bad) over 14 days compared to placebo.

152       2.4.2        Secondary outcomes:

153       The     study      measured          whether       BerriQi      improved         symptom          severity      and     functional
154       symptoms across 14 days compared to placebo.  Symptom sum (items 2-7 i.e., runny
155       nose,      stuffy    nose,      sneezing,        sore     throat,      cough,      and     feeling      tired;    range      0–18).
156       Function sum (items 8-14; i.e., thinking, sleeping, breathing, talking, walking/climbing
157       stairs/exercise, going to school, and playing with friends; range 0–21).

158       Additionally, the study compared total sick days and symptom resolution using both
159       the    global     illness     severity      score      and     the   symptom          sum      score.     Time to        symptom
160       resolution was calculated based on the following criteria:

161            •     First occurrence of two consecutive days with global illness score = 1 (“not
162                  sick”)
163            •     First occurrence of two consecutive days with symptom sum = 6 (corresponding
164                  to “not sick” for all the symptoms measured)

165       2.5          Sample size calculations

166       Based on Barrett et al. (2005) using the WURSS, an effect size of 0.709 was expected.
167       A total of 52 participants (26 per group) provided 80 % power at α = 0.05 (two-sided).
168       Allowing for 20% attrition, the target sample size was 62 participants.

169       2.5.1        Analysis populations

170       Intention-to-treat (ITT) included all randomised participants, analysed by assigned
171       group regardless of product receipt or diary completion.
172       Per-protocol (PP) included participants with ≥ 80 % product-consumption days and ≥
173       10 of 14 daily WURSS-K entries.

174       2.6          Statistical Analysis

175       2.6.1        Primary analysis:

176       Daily global illness scores were analysed using a linear mixed-effects model with
177       fixed effects for treatment group (BerriQi vs placebo), day (1–14), and their
178       interaction, plus a random intercept for participant. Baseline global illness score (day
179       0) was included as a covariate.
180       The treatment effect was expressed as the adjusted mean difference in global score
181       with 95% confidence interval (CI). Day-by-day post-hoc contrasts were performed
182       with Tukey-HSD, and p-values were adjusted using the false discovery rate (FDR)
183       method for multiplicity. All analyses followed the intention-to-treat principle with two-
184       sided α = 0.05. Continuous data were summarised as mean ± SEM (or median with
185       confidence interval).

186       2.6.2        Secondary analyses:

187       Symptom-sum and function-sum scores were modelled identically to the primary
188       outcome.
189       Days absent from school were compared using negative binomial regression,
190       expressed as incidence-rate ratios (IRR [95% CI]).
191       Time-to-resolution was analysed by Kaplan–Meier curves, log-rank test, and Cox
192       proportional-hazards model, reported as hazard ratios (HR [95 % CI]).

193       Sensitivity analyses:

194       Per-protocol (PP) analysis repeated the primary model among compliant participants.

195       Cluster handling:
196       Participants from the same family (siblings) were to be treated as clusters by adding
197       a random intercept for family ID when ≥ 2 families had multiple children enrolled.

198       Analyses         were     performed          in   R   (version       4.4.1)      using     the    packages          dplyr,     lme4,
199       lmerTest, emmeans, survival, MASS, and gtsummary.

313       4            Discussion

314       This     randomised,           double-blind,          placebo-controlled              trial   provides        the    first   clinical
315       evidence         that    a   whole-fruit        Boysenberry            and     apple      blend      (BerriQi)       significantly
316       alleviates URTI symptoms in children. The global illness score declined steadily over
317       the study period, reflecting progressive symptom improvement, however, this did not
318       reach statistical significance. Nevetheless, BerriQi significantly reduced the number of
319       sick days, shortening the duration and severity of common cold symptoms, thereby
320       supporting children's return to normal daily function. As URTI remains the leading
321       cause of school absenteeism globally, these findings suggest that daily BerriQi intake
322       may help reduce this burden.

323       BerriQi has previously demonstrated in vivo efficacy in reducing mucus production and
324       airway tissue damage due to inflammation, which results in improved lung capacity
325       and     function      (Shaw       et   al.,   2021).      The     resolution        of   inflammation          is mediated          by
326       alveolar macrophages, which change their action depending on the pathogen and
327       phase of infection (Byrne et al., 2015). During respiratory infection, macrophages
328       adopt a pro-inflammatory state (M1) in which they recruit additional immune cells,
329       produce reactive oxygen species, and release pro-inflammatory cytokines to fight the
330       pathogen (Forman & Torres, 2002; Martinez et al., 2008). Once the pathogen is
331       cleared,       recovery       relies     on    macrophages             switching       to   a   tissue-repair         state     (M2),
332       producing anti-inflammatory cytokines and depositing collagen at the sites of mucosal
333       damage. After tissue repair, macrophages enter a clean-up state (M2C), which breaks

       medRxiv preprint doi: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.26.26358747; this version posted August 3, 2026. The copyright holder for this
       preprint (which was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted medRxiv a license to display the preprint in
                                                                        perpetuity.
                                             It is made available under a CC-BY 4.0 International license .

334       downcollagen, allowing normal airway function to be restored (Russell et al., 2002;
335       Lim et al., 2000). When this macrophage switching sequence is delayed or incomplete,
336       the    pro-inflammatory              M1     and     collagen-intensive              M2     states      persist      longer      than
337       necessary, maintaining the conditions that produce ongoing mucus, congestion, and
338       fatigue even after the infection itself has resolved, resulting in lingering symptoms after
339       infection. BerriQi has been shown to actively trigger this macrophage switching from
340       M1     into    the    M2     and      further     into    the    M2C       recovery        states,      with     corresponding
341       reductions in immune cell infiltration, excess collagen, and mucus production in airway
342       tissue (Shaw et al., 2016, 2021).This activity is attributed to the unique polyphenol
343       profile      of    BerriQi,       particularly         the     complementary               activities       of    procyanidins,
344       anthocyanins and quercetin. Anthocyanins mainly come from the boysenberries, but
345       quercetin and procyanidins are derived from both apples and boysenberries. The
346       unique ratio of Boysenberries and apples in BerriQi provides a synergistic effect which
347       is not demonstrated by Boysenberries or apples alone (Shaw & Hurst, 2019).

348       The preclinical rationale described by Shaw & Hurst directly guided the placebo design
349       of the present study. Rather than an inert placebo, children in the placebo group
350       received a heat-treated Boysenberry and apple — a matrix that preserves the full
351       complement           of   heat-stable         nutrients,       vitamins,       and     minerals        naturally       present       in
352       Boysenberry while selectively degrading the heat-sensitive fraction of the polyphenols.
353       The total dose of actives (anthocyanins, quercetin, and procyanidins) delivered in the
354       BerriQi formulation was 6.4 mg per day (two chewable tablets), compared with 0.5 mg
355       per day (two chewable tablets) in the placebo. This design was chosen deliberately;
356       given      that    participants        were      acutely      unwell       children,      it was       considered         ethically
357       appropriate to provide a placebo that retained the general nutritional benefits of whole
358       fruit rather than an entirely inert placebo. Had a fully inert placebo been used, the
359       magnitude of the observed treatment effect would likely have been greater. The clinical
360       outcomes reported here therefore represent a conservative estimate of BerriQi's true
361       benefit in paediatric URTI.

362       While the intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis served as the primary outcome measure, the
363       per-protocol          (PP)      analysis,        restricted       to    compliant          participants,         yielded       more
364       pronounced           treatment         effects,      highlighting        the    importance          of    consistent        BerriQi
365       consumption in achieving observable clinical benefits. Notably, the overall pattern of
366       results      remained         consistent        across       both      analytical       approaches.           All   parameters,

       medRxiv preprint doi: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.26.26358747; this version posted August 3, 2026. The copyright holder for this
       preprint (which was not certified by peer review) is the author/funder, who has granted medRxiv a license to display the preprint in

367       including mean global illness severity scores, symptom scores, and function scores,
368       declined steadily over time in each group. However, a statistically significant between-
369       group difference emerged at day 5, with the BerriQi group demonstrating markedly
370       lower values compared to placebo. This finding suggests that early intervention may
371       be critical in attenuating symptom burden and potentially reducing total sick days.
372       Nevertheless, these results should be interpreted with caution, as false discovery rate
373       (FDR)-adjusted comparisons did not reach statistical significance. Therefore, further
374       studies are warranted in larger cohorts to substantiate these findings.

375       The     benefits       of   BerriQi      in   reducing        respiratory        symptoms          have      been      previously
376       demonstrated             following       exposures           to   environmental            stressors         such      as     ozone
377       (Lomiwes et al., 2025). While Lomiwes et al reported that a BerriQi intervention for 5
378       days improved respiratory symptoms such as throat irritation after ozone exposure,
379       this is the first study to explore its effect on URTI symptoms in children. This study
380       provides evidence for the benefits of BerriQi in reducing URTI-related symptoms in
381       children, but a few limitations warrant consideration. The study design presented
382       logistical challenges, particularly regarding timely investigational product (IP) delivery,
383       as participants were recruited from across New Zealand and were required to receive
384       the IP within 24–48 hours of symptom onset. Consequently, three participants did not
385       receive       the     product        within      the    stipulated         timeframe.         Quantification           of    school
386       absenteeism was complicated by scheduled holidays and weekends, and sick days
387       were therefore estimated using negative binomial regression based on two criteria: the
388       global illness score, reflecting overall wellbeing, and the composite symptom score,
389       capturing UTRI-specific symptoms. Symptom resolution was conservatively defined
390       as two consecutive symptom-free days, minimising misclassification and enhancing
391       analytical rigour. Furthermore, compliance was suboptimal in a subset of participants
392       due to poor acceptability of the chewable formulation's taste and texture, leading to
393       reduced        adherence           or   voluntary        withdrawal.         Additionally,         inconsistent         caregiver
394       reporting        resulted       in   missing        symptom          data.     Despite        these      limitations,        overall
395       compliance exceeded 80%, providing sufficient statistical power and supporting the
396       robustness of the study findings. Nevertheless, a larger sample size, as well as studies
397       in adults, would have further strengthened the interpretability and generalisability of
398       the findings. Future studies in larger cohorts and across a broader range of age groups
399       are therefore warranted.

400       5            Conclusions

401       The findings from the present study suggest that consistent intake of BerriQi may
402       contribute to faster and more complete recovery from mild respiratory illness.  BerriQi
403       may be considered a safe and natural paediatric supplement that may help alleviate
404       symptoms and support faster recovery from respiratory illness.
