Title: Evaluation of Adverse Effects and Tolerability of Dietary Ginger Supplementation in Patients With Functional Dyspepsia
PMID: 40474983
PMCID: PMC12137165
DOI: 10.1016/j.curtheres.2025.100792
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12137165/
Registration: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06313814
Published: 2025-04-14, Current Therapeutic Research 102, 100792.
Archive scope: Factual summary of original methods, study flow and adverse-effect and tolerability tables.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

The Debrecen investigators used an eight-week open-label group with 50 enrolled and 47 completing. Table 2 reports bloating in 7 of 47, heartburn in 6, diarrhea in 5 and headache in 1. The investigators report no serious events and say no participants discontinued due to adverse effects. Without a comparator, these are event counts among completers and do not isolate a ginger-attributable rate. The article alternates between standardized gingerols and a root powder with stated volatile-oil content. An authenticated lot analysis is unavailable.

The thesis records this registration and repeats the 47-person Table 2 counts and tolerability breakdown as its adverse-effect subset. The 51-person symptom/quality-of-life reporting has different denominators and endpoints. Treat safety results as overlapping with the thesis, without assuming all 47 individuals can be matched to the 51 symptom completers. A diarrhea observation supplies no constipation-severity estimate or Parkinson-specific result.
