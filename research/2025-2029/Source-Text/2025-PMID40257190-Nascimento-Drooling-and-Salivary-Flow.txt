Title: Self-perception of drooling impact in people with Parkinson's disease: a case-control and cross-sectional study
PMID: 40257190
PMCID: PMC12371465
DOI: 10.1002/mdc3.70077
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12371465/
First published: 2025-04-21 online
Coverage: Original article and clinical Tables 2–5.

Nascimento and colleagues compared 101 people with Parkinson disease and 101 age- and sex-matched controls. Mean MDS-UPDRS item 2.2 was 1.33 versus 0.12. In the same Table 3, mean unstimulated saliva flow was 0.14 versus 0.32 mL/min (reported P=.001), and stimulated flow was 1.01 versus 1.18 mL/min (reported P=.011). Perceived impact, oral accumulation and salivary output are different measurements. The Parkinson group had more drooling impact despite lower mean salivary flow. The printed Parkinson unstimulated median of 0.5 mL/min is internally inconsistent with its mean of 0.14 and stated nonnegative range for the group, so the median remains unresolved.

The study is cross-sectional and did not assign fig or measure fig-specific intake. It helps distinguish chronic drooling severity and saliva production, without estimating an effect of raw fig.
