The survey enrolled 422 students with primary dysmenorrhea in November and December 2024. Table 4 gives 54 ginger tea responses out of 342 reported nonpharmacological answers, with multiple selections allowed. Other listed practices include chewing ginger alone or with salt. Text elsewhere gives a different count and percentage for ginger tea. No preparation or processing was established.

Table 8 reports the row labeled “Drinking tea with a giggle” as B = 0.42 (95% CI −0.37 to 1.21, p = 0.295). The two rows labeled “Chewing giggles only” and “Chewing giggles with ashes” have B = 0.29 (−1.66 to 2.24) and B = 0.48 (−0.63 to 1.60), respectively. The labels do not provide a verified link to the Table 4 ginger responses. The aggregate nonpharmacological pain change cannot identify a ginger effect. The cross-sectional survey does not establish a fresh-ginger or Parkinson pain result.
