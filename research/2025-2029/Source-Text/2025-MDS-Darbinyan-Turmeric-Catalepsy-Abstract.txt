Title: Tumeric and curcumin improve motor function and reduce catalepsy in a rotenone-induced Parkinson's disease model
Source: https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/tumeric-and-curcumin-improve-motor-function-and-reduce-catalepsy-in-a-rotenone-induced-parkinsons-disease-model/
Publication date: 2025
Date basis: 2025 International Congress, Movement Disorders 40 supplement 1
Checked: 2026-11-22
Review depth: abstract

Original study scope
The investigator report describes four rat groups with sunflower oil, rotenone, rotenone plus isolated curcumin, or rotenone plus Curcuma longa extract. The named extract is a distinct turmeric intervention. The report describes shorter bar-test retraction times in each active arm relative to rotenone alone. Bar-test latency measures release from an imposed posture. It is an indirect model of motor immobility and can reflect rigidity and the ability to initiate movement. The abstract gives no arm-specific estimates, analyzed animal count, extraction profile, accompanying passive-tone result, or explicit administration route for the extract.

Continuing evidence gaps
Obtain the poster, underlying arm data and study protocol, including timing, route and animal recruitment. The shared authors and extract amount raise possible overlap with PMID 40694716, whose full journal article has three groups and gives a null rearing contrast. The September 2025 injection study and its 2026 companion reports use nine weeks. Common investigators do not establish either reuse or independence. Keep these reports in an uncertain lineage when counting directional support. Neither report tests the frozen grated turmeric exposure in diagnosed Parkinson disease.
