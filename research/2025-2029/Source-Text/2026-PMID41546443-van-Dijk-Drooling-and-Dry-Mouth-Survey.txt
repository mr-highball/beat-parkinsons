Title: Parkinson's Disease and Salivary Issues: A Survey

Publication date: 2026-01-16
Date basis: electronic publication in original JATS
Source depth: factual extract of original methods, Table 2 and Table 3

Van Dijk and colleagues surveyed 111 people with Parkinson disease online. The study reports 64.9% with subjective dry mouth, 27.0% with drooling, and 18.0% with both. Table 3 models the drooling complaint score separately from the dry-mouth score.

The unadjusted drooling coefficients for reported chewing-gum hours (−0.63, 95% CI −1.70 to 0.45), sour candy use (−0.71, 95% CI −3.92 to 2.50) and coffee cups per day (−0.61, 95% CI −1.53 to 0.32) all have intervals spanning zero. The separate adjusted dry-mouth model gives a positive gum association (1.27, 95% CI 0.52 to 2.02). The authors discuss whether people with dry mouth might choose to chew gum more often.

This cross-sectional self-report study provides distinct symptom associations. The survey assigned no gum, candy, coffee or raw fig exposure and provides no raw-fig estimate. It does not objectively measure saliva flow.
