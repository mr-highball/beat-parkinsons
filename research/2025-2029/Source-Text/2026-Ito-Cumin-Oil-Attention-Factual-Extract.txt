Title: Cumin essential oil and measured attention in older adults
Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2026.1784027/full
Format: Factual extract from the cited record.

Ito and colleagues, April 20, 2026. UMIN000050328. This randomized double-blind pilot allocated 43 cognitively healthy older Japanese adults. Thirty-eight were analyzed, 19 in each arm. The intervention was steam-distilled cumin essential oil with 25 mg cuminaldehyde per capsule, versus an MCT placebo, for 12 weeks.
Complex attention, lower is better: 26.5 ± 30.8 to 18.5 ± 16.5 on cumin versus 16.5 ± 12.7 to 22.2 ± 30.9 on placebo. Group-by-time p = .062. Simple attention: 29.0 ± 25.6 to 36.9 ± 9.3 versus 37.0 ± 5.7 to 30.9 ± 24.8, p = .057. Psychomotor speed and reaction time were distinct outcomes, with interactions p = .012 and .022. Attention contrasts were inconclusive. Broad domain testing, baseline imbalances, attrition and company-affiliated authors are material limitations. Registry disclosure followed the trial's intervention. No Parkinson cohort or toasted whole-spice comparison was tested.
