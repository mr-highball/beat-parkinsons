Herbal terpenoids activate autophagy and mitophagy through modulation of bioenergetics and protect from metabolic stress, sarcopenia and epigenetic aging
Gabriele Civiletto and colleagues. Nature Aging 5, 2003–2021. Published September 24, 2025.
PMID 40993327. PMCID PMC12532568. DOI 10.1038/s43587-025-00957-4.
Original full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12532568/

Coverage: selected original Methods, Results, Figure 7 and Extended Data Figure 7.

Eight-month-old male SAMP8 mice received isolated thymol at 20 mg/kg by gavage or corn-oil vehicle five days per week for 12 weeks. The groups started with 15 mice each. Treadmill and rotarod outcomes included 11 controls and 13 treated mice. Grip testing included 12 controls and 13 treated mice. Average treadmill distance roughly doubled from baseline in treated animals and stayed near baseline in controls. Vehicle grip strength decreased 36%, while the treated-group average stayed near baseline. Rotarod latency to fall improved in the treated group as reported. The figure captions specify one-tailed testing for several measures. Mice that died were excluded from relevant analyses.

Figure 7 outcomes address motor performance in an accelerated-aging model. The group did not assess step or stride length, culinary oregano, Parkinson disease or human symptom scores. The 2021 Civiletto conference member DOI 10.1093/geroni/igab046.1297 describes overlapping investigators, thymol and zebrafish/human-cell/high-fat mouse work. This later publication adds the aging mouse motor scope. The overlap does not establish an independent replication.
