Primary Abstract:
Carica papaya (C. papaya) has long been used in traditional medicine, and experimental studies have reported neuroprotective effects across several models; however, the available evidence is distributed across different plant parts, preparations, and experimental systems. This review synthesizes existing evidence from in vitro and in vivo studies assessing the neuroprotective, antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and neuromodulatory effects of C. papaya extracts derived from seeds, leaves, pulp, fruit, and fermented preparations. A comprehensive literature search of PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar, and ScienceDirect identified relevant studies across multiple experimental models through 22 September 2026. Findings show that in Alzheimer's disease (AD) models, aluminum-induced neurotoxicity, D-galactose-induced brain aging, 6-hydroxydopamine (6-OHDA)-mediated oxidative damage, and chemically induced neurotoxicity (cyclophosphamide, acrylamide, and fluoride), C. papaya preparations were associated with improvements in selected cognitive, behavioral, biochemical, and oxidative-stress-related outcomes across the included experimental models. Toxicity studies generally reported no mortality within the tested dose ranges; however, behavioral, biochemical, and mild histopathological alterations were observed in some studies, particularly at higher doses, indicating that safety may vary according to extract type, dose, and duration of exposure. Overall, the available preclinical evidence suggests that C. papaya preparations may affect multiple pathways relevant to oxidative-stress-associated neurodegeneration. However, further mechanistic studies, bioactive compound isolation, pharmacokinetic assessments, and well-designed clinical trials are essential to support its future therapeutic development.

CRITICAL CLINICAL CONFIRMATION: Explicitly concludes that NO eligible clinical trial has evaluated Carica papaya for any diagnosed neurodegenerative disorder (including Parkinson's disease or Alzheimer's disease); all neuroprotection data remain predominantly preclinical/oxidative-stress models.

[Results and Discussion] C. papaya contains chemically diverse constituents distributed unevenly among the fruit, peel, leaves, seeds, latex, and roots. The major phytochemical and nutritional constituents reported in the literature include phenolic acids, flavonoids, carotenoids, glucosinolates, cyanogenic glycosides, alkaloids, tannins, saponins, terpenoids, vitamins, minerals, and proteolytic enzymes (Khatun et al. 2026; Cao et al. 2024). Another study found that papaya fruit contains phenolic acids and flavonoids, including caffeic acid, protocatechuic acid derivatives, gallic acid derivatives, quercetin, rutin, kaempferol, myricetin, and isorhamnetin. In liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry (LC–MS) analyses, several of these compounds were detected in the peel, whereas the flesh contained mainly caffeic acid and conjugated protocatechuic and gallic acid derivatives (Rivera‐Pastrana et al. 2010). However, the pulp contains carotenoids, predominantly lycopene, followed by β‐cryptoxanthin and β‐carotene, which contribute to its antioxidant potential (Cao et al. 2024). A separate study reported that papaya leaves contain a complex mixture of phenylpropanoids and flavonoids. Metabolomic analysis identified caffeic, cinnamic, chlorogenic, quinic, coumaric, vanillic, and protocatechuic acids, together with naringenin, hesperidin, rutin, and kaempferol (Gogna et al. 2015). [Conclusion] The evidence compiled in this review suggests potential neuroprotective effects of selected C. papaya preparations in preclinical experimental models involving oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, neurotransmitter imbalance, and Aβ aggregation. Studies of extracts prepared from different plant parts reported improvements in selected cognitive, behavioral, biochemical, and neuronal outcomes, although the findings varied by preparation and experimental model. However, available evidence is predominantly preclinical. In neuronal‐cell and animal studies, C. papaya preparations were associated with changes in measured outcomes including oxidative‐stress markers, antioxidant‐enzyme activities, cholinesterase and neurotransmitter‐related measures, Aβ‐associated cellular toxicity or aggregation, neuronal viability or histology, cerebral infarct volume, and selected behavioral tests. The findings varied by preparation and model, and several studies used small experimental groups.
