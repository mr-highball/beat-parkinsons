Spinach ethanol extract and memory in a toxin-exposed mouse model
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42921944/
PMID: 42921944
DOI: 10.1177/1096620X261500417
Coverage: factual extract from primary abstract

Chan Kyu Park and colleagues. Journal of Medicinal Food, October 22, 2026, ahead of print.
Male ICR mice received spinach ethanolic extract at 400–1200 mg/kg/day or separate linoleic acid at 10–40 mg/kg/day for three weeks before trimethyltin challenge. The article abstract reports improvements in Y-maze spontaneous alternation and passive avoidance latency after the extract and separately administered linoleic acid. The latter also affected brain acetylcholinesterase activity and lipid peroxidation. Those observations concern experimental memory responses after a toxicant. The abstract does not identify a Parkinson model, a cooked spinach exposure, a separate attention assay, or per-arm numerical comparisons and oral route. Full intervention methods and figures remain unverified here.
