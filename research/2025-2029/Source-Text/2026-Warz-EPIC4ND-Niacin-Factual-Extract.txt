Factual extract of Research Square preprint, version 1
Warz SI, Dierkes J and colleagues. Association of dietary niacin and tryptophan intake with the risk of Parkinson disease in the EPIC4ND cohort. Posted May 11, 2026. DOI 10.21203/rs.3.rs-9436550/v1.
The primary observational study included 130,622 EPIC4ND participants, among whom 494 met one of the study definition levels for Parkinson disease. The study used baseline diet questionnaires or interviews to estimate niacin and tryptophan and used Cox models to analyze subsequent diagnosis.
The multivariable cohort models showed no statistically significant associations for dose-decrease and quartile exposures. In men, the lowest versus highest quartile of niacin had HR 1.52 (95% CI 1.04–2.24). This subgroup estimate lacked an orderly quartile gradient, and the association weakened under alternate censoring windows. The study has no single-food or symptom change effect.
Population and case ascertainment differ from the Norwegian cohort proposal in EPO6_127. The preprint is a separate source, and this does not resolve whether the Norwegian study completed.
