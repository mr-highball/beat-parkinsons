Title: The Effectiveness of Hesperidin, Diosmin and Proanthocyanidins Nutritional Supplementation on Cognitive and Motor Functions in Older Adults: A Pilot Randomized Control Study
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40694049/
PMID: 40694049
DOI: 10.1080/27697061.2025.2526600
Registration: NCT06352099
Publication date: 2025-07-23
Date basis: First online publication year (2025) in DOI 10.1080/27697061.2025.2526600 and PubMed abstract record (Journal of the American Nutrition Association, 2026 issue; links to existing 2024 registry record research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2024-NCT06352099-Altemor-Registry-Extract.txt).
Coverage: Paraphrased official PubMed abstract and linked ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06352099 registration; full publisher text is paywalled (OA:N).

This randomized, crossover-controlled pilot study (Giovannini et al., NCT06352099) randomized 36 older adults into three arms: G-ALT12 (1 stick/day of Altemor for 8 weeks, 4-week washout, then 2 sticks/day for 8 weeks), G-ALT21 (reverse dose sequence), and G-CON (routine care without supplementation). Altemor is a multi-ingredient dietary supplement combining micronized diosmin, hesperidin, and proanthocyanidins/plant extracts rather than whole raw orange or isolated hesperidin alone.

Participants were evaluated at baseline and weeks 8, 12, and 20 for cognitive and motor performance (including Trail Making Test primary outcome and Hunova Silver Index fall-risk screen), mood, fatigue, and quality of life, plus attention at weeks 1 and 13. The PubMed abstract reports statistically significant improvements in both experimental arms versus routine-care control for cognitive function (particularly attention) and fall risk, with no statistically significant difference between 1 stick/day and 2 sticks/day. Exact numeric estimates and confidence intervals remain unrecovered behind the publisher paywall; the multi-ingredient formulation, open routine-care control, small pilot sample (n = 36), and non-Parkinson geriatric population prevent attributing the reported fall-risk change to raw orange consumption in Parkinson disease.
