Title: Safr'Inside extract and secondary sleep, depression and fatigue outcomes in perimenopause
Source: https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.10.12.26365297v1.full.pdf
DOI: 10.64898/2026.10.12.26365297
Registration: NCT06227858
First posted: 2026-10-22
Coverage: Original preprint v1, factual extract of Methods, Tables 3–4 and disclosures. Peer review has not been established.

Sultana and colleagues randomised 104 perimenopausal women to standardized saffron extract or placebo for 12 weeks. It analyzed 53 extract and 51 placebo participants in the intention-to-treat group. The primary endpoint was total menopausal symptoms on the Greene Climacteric Scale. Separate sleep and mood instruments were secondary.

Table 3 reports a PSQI total interaction of p=.2, with mean 12-week changes of −4.6 and −2.2 in the extract and placebo arms. The sleep latency interaction was p=.7. Subjective sleep quality and daytime dysfunction favored extract in the intention-to-treat analysis. Their interactions were p=.04 and p=.004, respectively, and neither interaction remained significant in the per-protocol analysis. These outcomes give no separate insomnia severity result.

In Table 4 the POMS fatigue subscale favored extract (interaction p=.0003, week-12 changes −7.6 versus −1.7). The depression subscale interaction was p=.02 in intention-to-treat (changes −8.4 versus −1.2) and inconclusive per protocol. The study has multiple secondary endpoints and sponsor-affiliated authors. It examined standardized extract in perimenopause, with no Parkinson cohort or ground-stigma arm. The current ClinicalTrials.gov record has no results module. The preprint and registry describe the same trial family.
