Factual extract from the original Methods, Results and Table 2, checked 2026-11-14 (US Eastern).

Thirty patients aged 20–60 with primary insomnia were randomized equally to tea using 2 g dried Piper nigrum leaves or 2 g chamomile steeped in 120 mL water for five minutes, once per night for fourteen days. Mean ISI scores (SD) at baseline, day 7, day 14 and day 21 were 13.60 (2.72), 6.73 (3.92), 5.33 (3.56) and 6.07 (3.59) in the leaf-tea group and 15.13 (3.46), 7.40 (1.88), 5.60 (2.38), and 6.33 (3.67) in the chamomile group. The corresponding between-group p values were .19, .56, .81 and .84. PSQI at day 14 was 4.93 (3.55) and 4.13 (1.81), respectively, p=.44. The trial used an active comparator and reported one episode of nausea and dizziness in the leaf group. This exposure used leaves. The article did not report a subgroup of people with Parkinson or a dream-enactment outcome.
