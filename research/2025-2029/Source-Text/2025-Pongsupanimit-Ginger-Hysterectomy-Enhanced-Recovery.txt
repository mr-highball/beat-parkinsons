Title: The Impact of Ginger on Preventing Postoperative Ileus after Hysterectomy Under the Enhanced Recovery after Surgery Protocol: A randomized controlled trial
DOI: 10.14456/tjog.2025.6
Source: https://he02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/tjog/article/view/270744
Original PDF: https://he02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/tjog/article/download/270744/185465
Registration: https://www.thaiclinicaltrials.org/show/TCTR20230228001
Published: 2025-02-13, Thai Journal of Obstetrics and Gynaecology 33, 53–63.
Archive scope: Factual summary of original Methods, Table 2 and Discussion.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

The Siriraj study randomized 80 patients per arm after hysterectomy to enhanced recovery alone or the same program plus ginger capsules, with blinded outcome assessor and no placebo capsule. It started oral intake and 1 g ginger at three to four hours, then added 1 g after meals for nine doses overall. Table 2 gives ileus in 23/80 in each arm and first flatus at 17.1 versus 17.3 hours, ginger versus control, p=.869. The primary combined ileus interval, oral feeding endpoints and reported postoperative pain scores were inconclusive. Defecation time was not available for all participants. The result covers a different surgical, feeding and comparator context from the smaller double-blind trial. It does not quantify chronic constipation severity or Parkinson pain.
