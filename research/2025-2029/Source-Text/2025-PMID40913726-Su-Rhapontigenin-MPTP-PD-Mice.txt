Preclinical experiment in 60 male C57BL/6J mice with chronic 5-week MPTP parkinsonism treated by gavage with isolated stilbenoid rhapontigenin (50 or 100 mg/kg, enriched in Vitis vinifera). Attenuated open-field hypokinesia, dopaminergic neuron loss, and cGAS-STING-NF-kB neuroinflammation via PINK1/DRP1 microglial mitophagy. Isolated compound in mice (remote from whole raw/frozen grape consumption in humans).

[Results] We established an MPTP-induced chronic PD mouse model to evaluate the neuroprotective effects of Rhap on DAergic neurons [35]. The chronic PD model was constructed by intraperitoneally injecting MPTP for 5 weeks, during which a Rhap intervention was administered by gavage (Fig. 1A). We conducted behavioral tests within one week postmodeling to evaluate the therapeutic effects of Rhap on PD mice. We found that the 50 and 100 mg/kg Rhap doses attenuated the MPTP-induced decreases in total distance traveled and the mean velocity of movement in the open field test (Fig. 1B-D), the decreased holding time in the grasping test, and the latency to fall in the rotarod test (Fig. 1E, F). Moreover, the pole-climbing time in the pole-climbing test was increased in MPTP-induced PD mice treated with 50 or 100 mg/kg Rhap (Fig. 1G). The immunohistochemical results indicated that the 50 and 100 mg/kg Rhap doses increased the number of TH-positive cells in the substantia nigra region of the MPTP-induced PD mice, as well as the density of TH-positive fibers in the striatum (Fig. 1H-J). Quantitative analysis showed comparable numbers of TH-positive neurons in the SN and similar densities of TH-positive fibers in the striatum among all groups, including Rhap-treated (50 or 100 mg/kg) and Ctrl animals (Supplementary Fig S1). Furthermore, Western blotting revealed that different doses of Rhap significantly increased the TH protein expression level in the SN and striatum of PD mice (Fig. 1K-N). These results suggested that Rhap at appropriate doses exhibits no neurotoxicity and effectively ameliorates both motor deficits and DAergic neuron damage in MPTP-induced PD mice. [Discussion] In this study, we identified Rhap as a potential therapeutic agent for PD. The compound alleviated neuroinflammation by suppressing the activation of the cGAS-STING-NF-κB signaling pathway in microglia, as observed in both in vivo and in vitro models. This anti-inflammatory effect ultimately results in significant improvements in motor deficits and pathological hallmarks characteristic of PD. Mechanistically, Rhap bound to PINK1 and potentiated its molecular interaction with DRP1, thereby maintaining the homeostatic balance between mitochondrial fission and mitophagy in microglia. This regulatory mechanism improved mitochondrial quality control, which consequently mitigated the aberrant cytosolic leakage of mtDNA. This restoration of mitochondrial homeostasis effectively alleviated the excessive activation of the cGAS-STING-NF-κB pathway caused by aberrant mtDNA leakage, ultimately mitigating PD-associated neuroinflammation and pathological progression (Fig. 9).
