Title: Effect of Topical Hemp (Cannabis sativa L.) Seed Oil on Knee Osteoarthritis: A Randomized Double-Blind Controlled Trial.
PMID: 39256070
DOI: 10.1016/j.pmn.2024.08.001
First published: 2025
Authors: Abbasifard M, Moosavi Z, Azimi M, Kamiab Z, Bazmandegan G, Madahian A
Journal: Pain management nursing : official journal of the American Society of Pain Management Nurses
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39256070/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 4 review).

Randomized, double-blind, 3-arm active- and placebo-controlled clinical trial (n=90 knee osteoarthritis patients randomized via blocked randomization to daily topical Cannabis sativa L. seed oil, topical diclofenac gel, or topical placebo for 8 weeks; assessed at baseline, 4, and 8 weeks). VAS pain scores and WOMAC parameters improved significantly with topical hemp seed oil compared with placebo (p<0.05) and did not differ significantly from topical diclofenac gel; heel-to-thigh flexion range did not differ between groups. Critical limitations: TOPICAL skin application rather than oral culinary cold-pressed oil (exposure_fit: remote), knee OA rather than PD pain (population_fit: partial, outcome_fit: partial), and abstract-only access lacking arm-level VAS/WOMAC means and cold-pressing specifications.

Primary PubMed Abstract:
Knee osteoarthritis (OA) is one of the most common and debilitating diseases, especially in the elderly. Hemp seed oil is a plant product that has been used as a food or drug since ancient times because of its anti-inflammatory and analgesic properties. A double-blind, active, placebo-controlled trial was done to assess the efficacy of hemp seed oil on knee OA. Ninety patients were randomly allocated to three groups; hemp seed oil, diclofenac gel, and placebo via a blocked randomization method, and were asked to apply the topical treatment daily for 2 months. The study participant underwent assessments before, and four and 8 weeks after the intervention. Evaluation included measurements of the heel-to-thigh distance, utilization of Western Ontario and McMaster Universities Osteoarthritis Index (WOMAC), and application of visual analog scale (VAS). Data analysis was performed using SPSS.24 and the significance level was considered as p < .05. All parameters, except heel-to-thigh distance, improved significantly in the hemp seed oil group compared to placebo group. Improvements in VAS and WOMAC parameters were not different comparing the hemp seed oil and diclofenac gel groups. Heel-to-thigh distance decreased significantly within all groups during the study. There were no significant differences in improvements in heel-to-thigh distance comparing the three groups. Hemp seed oil led to greater improvements in VAS pain score and WOMAC parameters, but not knee flexion range, compared to placebo. There were no differences in measured outcomes comparing hemp seed oil and diclofenac gel.
