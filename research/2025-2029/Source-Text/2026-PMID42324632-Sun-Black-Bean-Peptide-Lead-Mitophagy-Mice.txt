Title: Black Bean Peptide Alleviates Lead-Induced Hippocampal Neurotoxicity in Mice by Regulating PINK1/Parkin-Mediated Mitophagy.
PMID: 42324632
DOI: 10.1111/1750-3841.71233
First published: 2026
Authors: Sun J, Li Q, Li T, Li X, Huang X, Cheng Y, et al.
Journal: Journal of food science
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42324632/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Preclinical toxicology study (Sun et al., 2026, J Food Sci) testing oral black bean peptide (BSP, 200 mg/kg/day) vs resveratrol in lead-exposed mice. BSP improved hippocampal antioxidant enzymes (T-AOC, SOD, CAT), activated PINK1/Parkin mitophagy markers (PINK1, Parkin, LC3B-II up; p62 down), and lowered Bax/Caspase-3 apoptosis markers (p<0.05). Limits: isolated peptide hydrolysate in a heavy-metal lead-toxicity mouse model with purely biochemical/molecular endpoints and no behavioral cognitive, motor, or Parkinson's symptom assessments.
