Primary Abstract:
INTRODUCTION: A bezoar is an indigestible conglomeration trapped in the gastrointestinal tract. Persimmon phytobezoars, known as diospyrobezoars, are formed after the frequent consumption of persimmons and are a distinct type of phytobezoar characterized by their hard consistency. CASE PRESENTATION: A 74-year-old female presented to our hospital with persistent abdominal pain and vomiting, and an intestinal strangulating obstruction was suspected. An emergency operation was performed, and a dietary small bowel obstruction was diagnosed. A small intestinal resection, including removal of a foreign body resembling a sponge-like mass, was performed. During detailed questioning, we asked her about any history of persimmon ingestion, but she denied it. The symptoms recurred on POD 8, and another dietary small bowel obstruction was suspected after a CT examination. Conservative treatment, including Coca-Cola dissolution therapy, was performed, but the symptoms were not alleviated, and she developed a fever on POD 24. On POD 30-the 22nd day after the 2nd onset-she suddenly remembered that she had eaten 16 dried persimmons between 24 and 19 days before the onset of symptoms. A 2nd operation was performed the following day. Because feces were found in the abdominal cavity and the perforated site could not be found laparoscopically, we converted to laparotomy. At the location of the obstruction in the ileum, a foreign body and a perforation were seen, and a partial ileectomy was performed. The foreign body also resembled a sponge-like mass, and a component analysis showed that it consisted of 98% tannin. A diospyrobezoar was finally diagnosed. CONCLUSIONS: Because multiple diospyrobezoars could exist in the gastrointestinal tract, we should check for additional bezoars throughout the entire tract. Much more detailed questionnaires, including both dried and ripe persimmons, are needed. During conservative therapy, the occurrence of severe complications should be recognized.

[DISCUSSION] In this case, we learned many lessons. We were unable to make an accurate preoperative diagnosis and did not discover the patient’s history of dried persimmon ingestion. We performed a laparoscopic-assisted surgery, a procedure less commonly used in these cases. We encountered the recurrence of bezoars-induced small bowel obstructions, and the patient was resistant to Coca-Cola dissolution treatment. During conservative treatment, we discovered an intestinal perforation at the site where the bezoar was impacted. Among the aforementioned lessons, we identified the following 3 important clinical issues. First, bezoars other than the one responsible for the intestinal obstruction might exist. Phytobezoars are usually visualized by CT as an ovoid or round mass occupying the gastrointestinal tract with air bubbles retained inside and a mottled appearance.2) Kawano et al.4) described a “bubbly mass and impaction” at the distal site of a small bowel dilatation, which is a characteristic sign of small bowel obstruction due to diet. Altintoprak et al.5) reported that among 39 patients with bezoars that were surgically removed, 41.0% (16 patients) had multiple bezoar locations in the gastrointestinal tract, and the percentage of multiple bezoars located in the stomach and ileum, as in this case, was 12.8% (5 patients). Tsukioka et al.6) reported intestinal obstructions due to 2 diospyrobezoars with reoperation and cited another case report in Japan. Hoover et al.7) also emphasized this important detail in a report of a patient who required a 2nd operation because of an overlooked bezoar, despite preoperative CT examination. [CONCLUSIONS] We experienced a case of bezoars-induced intestinal obstruction caused by dried persimmons that required reoperation. We learned 3 lessons: since persimmon bezoars may be present in more than 1 location, a search from the stomach to the small intestine is necessary in addition to the site of the obstruction. A detailed interview, including gathering information about dried persimmons, is necessary, and during conservative therapy, attention should be paid to the possible occurrence of severe complications.
