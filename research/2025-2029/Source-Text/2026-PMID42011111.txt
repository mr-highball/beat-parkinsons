Title: Sleep after multicomponent aromatherapy in older residential-care adults
First published: 2026-04-20
PMID: 42011111
PMCID: PMC13199057
DOI: 10.1093/gerona/glag105
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13199057/
Text type: original-source evidence summary

Ede Sarıkaya and colleagues randomized 60 adults to 30 days of a combined two-oil morning and five-oil evening inhalation program or to no intervention. Participants knew their assignment. The original describes clary sage (musk sage) as one of the nighttime oils. The paper does not identify the effect of a single oil or study common sage eaten as a food. Table 4 PSQI means (SD) were 13.97 (1.38) versus 13.93 (1.44) at baseline and 7.73 (1.86) versus 13.83 (1.26) at day 30, P<.001. Table 2 day-30 latency means were 38.87 (6.13) versus 63.83 (9.82) minutes. The day-30 between-group latency effect size in Table 2 is −3.050, while the abstract reports −1.12. The difference in reported standardized estimates remains unresolved. NCT06208800 identifies the same study. The cohort and inhalation intervention do not answer Parkinson insomnia after consuming dried common sage.
