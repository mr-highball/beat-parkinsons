Title: Beneficial Effects of Caraway Oil in Aluminium Chloride-Induced Neurotoxicity
Authors: Sandip T Auti and Yogesh A Kulkarni
Date: 2025-06-26
Identifiers: PMID 40718314; PMCID PMC12296912; DOI 10.7759/cureus.86783
Source: https://assets.cureus.com/uploads/original_article/pdf/376280/20250626-48345-bmf1pq.pdf
Coverage: Original factual extract of Materials, gas chromatography characterization and Figure 5.

Commercial caraway oil was sourced from iFRAGRANCE INDIA. The authors identify carvone (reported at about 55%) and limonene as substantial constituents. The Wistar rats receiving aluminum chloride and either 100 or 200 mg/kg oral caraway oil daily had higher five-minute actophotometer counts than the aluminum chloride group at the 21-day and 42-day tests, with p < .001 reported. The study used an aluminum-associated cognitive-neurotoxicity model. The oil lot was chemically characterized, but did not represent ground culinary caraway. Individual tremor or dystonia signs were not measured.
