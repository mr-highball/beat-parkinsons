Title: Aniseed, Caraway, and Cumin: Appetite-Enhancing Effects and Identification of Active Compounds
Authors: Kakuyou Ogawa and colleagues
Date: 2026, volume 49 issue 4
Identifiers: PMID 41951376; DOI 10.1248/bpb.b26-00069
Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/bpb/49/4/49_b26-00069/_html/-char/en
Coverage: Original factual extract of essential oil characterization, animal Methods and Figure 2B.

The caraway lot was Carum carvi seed from the Netherlands. The study hydrodistilled the material and verified that the extraction solvent was absent from the final test solution. GC analysis gave 82.82% carvone by peak area and identified that carvone as the d-isomer. Fasted male ddY mice inhaled a caraway essential oil preparation. The feeding data show increased food intake at two nonadjacent tested doses, with p < .01 versus carrier and eight mice per group in Figure 2. The route was inhalation. There was no toasted fruit administration, Parkinson population, or apathy scale.
