Title: Neurobehavioral and metabolic effects of polyphenol-rich olive oil in male rats during the adolescent-to-young adult transition: a comparative study
Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11055-025-01891-z
DOI: 10.1007/s11055-025-01891-z
First public: 2025-10-09
Coverage: Factual methods and Figure 2 results from the complete original article in the Balikesir University repository.

Ilgin and colleagues randomized 24 adolescent male Sprague Dawley rats to distilled water or one of two cold-pressed Memecik olive oil preparations for eight weeks by daily oral gavage. Both oils were pressed at or below 27 °C. The lab measured 775 mg/kg total polyphenols in the higher preparation and 77 mg/kg in the lower. The comparison had eight animals assigned per arm and six to eight per arm in the behavioral figures.

Figure 2 compares both oils with water. Open-field center time did not differ clearly between groups (p>.05). Both oil arms spent less time along the periphery and less time in closed arms of the elevated plus maze than controls (p<.01 for each pair). Open-arm time was longer with the higher-phenol oil than with water (p<.05); the lower-phenol comparison did not clearly separate from water. The report identifies no clear difference between the two oil preparations on these anxiety-like comparisons. Forced-swim immobility did not differ clearly. The oil-versus-water contrast differs in energy intake as well as oil exposure, and young rats without a Parkinson model do not establish a clinical anxiety effect.
