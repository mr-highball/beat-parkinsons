Marinaccio and colleagues test ethanol–water extract of immature whole green walnut fruit by local paw injection in male mice. This is not oral food or pressed kernel oil. Groups of six show reduced induced thermal hypersensitivity and formalin-associated licking relative to vehicle; reported figure comparisons are p<.01 for thermal response and p<.05 or p<.001 for formalin phases.

The separate edema experiment has a small, statistically inconclusive reduction. Keep that outcome alongside the favorable nociception findings. Enzyme and computational binding arguments are mechanisms proposed for investigation, not demonstrated human efficacy or plasma stability.

Plant part, extraction, local route, species and induced endpoint all limit transfer to cold-pressed walnut oil and Parkinson pain. This is a separate preclinical lead, not another whole-oil trial or human replication of the 2018 kernel-oil findings.
