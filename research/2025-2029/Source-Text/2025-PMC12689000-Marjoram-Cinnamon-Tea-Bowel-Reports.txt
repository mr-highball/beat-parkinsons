Title: Safety and effectiveness of marjoram and cinnamon herbal tea supplementation in managing polycystic ovary syndrome: a pilot randomized, double-blind, controlled trial
Source: https://doi.org/10.1097/MS9.0000000000004181
DOI: 10.1097/MS9.0000000000004181
Publication date: 2025-10-22
Reviewed: 2026-11-26
Text coverage: Published methods, participant flow, tables and narrative bowel observations.
PMID: 41377384
PMCID: PMC12689000
Original full text: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12689000/

The randomized pilot assigned 60 participants with polycystic ovary syndrome to a combined marjoram and cinnamon tea or placebo. The Methods describe powdered dried leaf material for the mixture. Thirty-six completed the study (21 versus 15). The authors describe improved constipation or bowel symptoms in some tea users and mild colicky abdominal pain in the intervention group. The article gives no constipation-event denominators or baseline-adjusted between-arm result, and timing in the prose is unclear. Unequal completion, qualitative symptoms and the mixed preparation prevent attributing either observation to cinnamon. The trial did not enroll a defined Parkinson cohort.
