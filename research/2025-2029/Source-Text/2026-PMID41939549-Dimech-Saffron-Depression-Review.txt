Dimech searched selectively through May 2025 and organized adult depression studies by comparator and preparation. The synthesis did not apply a formal risk-of-bias tool. Most summarized results concern low mood or mild-to-moderate depression, with short courses of stigma-derived products, isolated crocin or, separately, petal extracts. Trials and previous syntheses can overlap.

The review explicitly separates stigma and petal products and acknowledges that active-drug comparisons were small and generally lacked a formal noninferiority design. It traces favorable adjunctive findings to specific extracts, including the standardized Affron product. It also cites an earlier source for Persian traditional use of saffron for depressive symptoms. That citation preserves the reported use and supplies no new patient outcome.

This review contains no new randomized comparison and gives no direct result for ground culinary saffron and Parkinson depression. Its search cutoff precedes the December 2025 Isfahan whole-powder publication. Compare the earlier originals by plant part, extraction and separately reported endpoints.
