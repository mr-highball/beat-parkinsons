Figure 1 shows five mice per group. Compared with model controls,
the isolated 6-shogaol group retained balance on the rotarod longer
and completed both pole tasks faster. Approximate readings from the
bars are 103 to 167 seconds for rotarod retention and 8.3 to 4.4
seconds for pole descent. The two immobility panels also favor the
compound group. The caption identifies tail suspension and forced
swim tasks. The second immobility panel is marked F in the graphic
while the caption calls it E. These readings describe chart heights
rather than raw data or independently calculated effect estimates.
Figure 2 separately shows striatal dopamine, norepinephrine and
serotonin comparisons.

Voluntary movement tasks concern motor performance. Immobility
tasks are model proxies interpreted by the authors as
depression-like behavior. Rigidity and levodopa-induced dyskinesia
are separate outcomes. The inspected comparisons include neither
culinary ground or grated ginger nor a relevant human endpoint.
The same study is citation 88 in the later culinary-spice review.
