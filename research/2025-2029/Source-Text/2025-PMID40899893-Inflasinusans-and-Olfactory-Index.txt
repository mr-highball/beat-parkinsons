Title: The role of Inflasinusans in the treatment of acute rhinosinusitis
Source: https://www.mediasphera.ru/issues/vestnik-otorinolaringologii/2025/4/1004246682025041064
PMID: 40899893
DOI: 10.17116/otorino20259004164
Publication date: 2025-09-03
Date basis: Original Russian journal publication date, volume 90 issue 4, pages 64–71.
Coverage: Russian publisher full original and figures 6–9 captioned results.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26

In 70 adults randomized 35 per group, the thyme-containing combined product plus usual rhinosinusitis care showed an olfactory threshold score of 6.3 versus 6.1 (reported p ≤ .01) and an olfactory index of 0.5 versus 0.3 (p ≤ .05). At three weeks, olfactory disturbance was reported in zero adjunct participants and three controls. The comparison concerns the combined product. The study excludes neurodegenerative disease and does not isolate fresh thyme.

The authors report random allocation. The publication does not describe matched placebo or isolation of the constituent. The adjunct includes thyme and other botanical extracts, vitamin C, zinc and dihydroquercetin. The publisher reports industry support. Endpoints and contrasts in this source concern acute rhinosinusitis rather than a Parkinson-specific olfactory estimate.
