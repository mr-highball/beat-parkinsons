Coverage: Factual extract of publicly available methods, attention and adverse-event tables, and official registry fields. The article states CC BY-NC-SA 1.0 and falls outside the project's full-text archive license set.

Participants aged 6–14 with ADHD received methylphenidate and were randomized to commercially manufactured fig-and-almond syrup, a separate almond syrup, or placebo syrup for 12 weeks. The paper names Tinlose as the fig-containing product. The official registry identifies NIAK and Iranian FDA registration 8953162594580741 for that arm. Its separate almond comparator was made from sweet almonds and raisins by a university pharmacy. The investigators say they used a checklist based on CTCAE v4.03 every two weeks. Published Table 3 reports drooling in 17/40, 15/40 and 5/40, respectively, with an omnibus p-value of .008. The publication contains no reported pairwise comparison or drooling grade distribution. The product specification, upstream fruit preparation and thesis remain unresolved. This was a pediatric adverse-event comparison.

The 12-week pediatric ADHD study assigned 40 participants per syrup arm, all receiving methylphenidate. Table 2 reports mean parent-rated cognitive problems/inattention at baseline and week 12 of 11.57 ± 3.52 and 6.20 ± 2.28 with sweet-almond syrup, and 8.05 ± 5.22 and 8.10 ± 3.56 with placebo syrup. The table shows a B versus C p=.003 at baseline and p=.011 at week 12 for Tukey comparisons. The Methods also mention ANCOVA, but the table does not clearly identify a baseline-adjusted B versus C effect. The syrup preparation uses sweet almonds and raisins rather than documented oral cold-pressed almond oil. No participants with Parkinson disease were identified.
