Title: Nature and severity of the effect of herbal medicinal products based on Melissa officinalis L. on animal behaviour when administrating different drug dosages
Source: https://mppt.hu/magazin/pdf/vol27issue1/v27i1p06.pdf
PMID: 40173426
Publication: Neuropsychopharmacologia Hungarica 27(1), March 2025, pages 6–13.
Coverage: Factual extract of original Methods and Table 2.

Thirty male Wistar rats were assigned to two amounts of a combined botanical extract or a control. The ingredients were lemon balm, oregano, sage, hawthorn and licorice. Table 2 reports mean open-arm entry counts of 6.25 versus 2.80 in the higher-amount and control groups (p < .05). Mean durations were 98.70 versus 54.00 seconds (reported p > .05). The extract also increased locomotion, a competing explanation for more entries.

There was no oregano-only group, and the model had no Parkinson pathology. The report describes animal exploration rather than clinical anxiety. Table 1 and its narrative have ambiguous row alignment in extracted text, so those time values were not used here.
