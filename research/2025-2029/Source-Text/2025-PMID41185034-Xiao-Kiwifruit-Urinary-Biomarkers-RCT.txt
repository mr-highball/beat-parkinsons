Primary Abstract:
BACKGROUND: Kiwifruit is widely recognized for its nutritional value and health benefits, yet reliable and objective methods for assessing kiwifruit intake in populations remain limited. OBJECTIVE: This study aimed to identify urinary biomarkers of kiwifruit intake and develop an optimal biomarker panel for differentiating consumers within days. METHODS: A randomized, controlled, crossover dietary intervention was conducted among 17 healthy volunteers. The intervention included four phases: run-in, single-exposure, repeat-exposure, and follow-up. Urine samples at multiple time-point and fruit samples were prepared and analyzed using untargeted metabolomics via dual-column ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (UHPLC-MS). Candidate biomarkers were identified through a systematic statistical strategy on kinetic profiles within 24 h, and annotated for potential fruit-derived origin through spectral matching. Machine learning algorithms were employed to establish an optimal biomarker panel for assessing kiwifruit intake under habitual diet conditions. RESULTS: Twenty-three urinary metabolites showed significantly elevated kinetic profiles, among which 15 were matched to compounds detected in the original fruit or in vitro digestion samples. These metabolites mainly included polyphenol-related metabolites and plant-derived amino acid derivatives. The excretion of many metabolites turned to be delayed compared to those typically observed for other fruits. For example, 2-isopropylmalic acid usually peaked in urine or blood within 6 h of consuming other fruits, but in our study urinary level at 24 h was much higher compared to 6 h. Most of the selected candidates are not specific to kiwifruit based on existing literature, such as hippuric acid. In this regard, an XGBoost algorithm-based model using 7 metabolites achieved substantial discriminative performance (accuracy = 0.88) in predicting kiwifruit intake within two days. CONCLUSIONS: This study identified potential biomarkers of kiwifruit and developed a prediction model that may differentiate consumers. Further validation is necessary to confirm the reliability and generalizability of our findings. TRIAL REGISTRATION: Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, ChiCTR2100048279. Registered on July 5, 2021.

[Method] This analysis is part of a food biomarker discovery study that aimed to identify and/or validate biomarkers of nine commonly consumed or local fruits among eastern China population. A comprehensive description of the study design and procedures is provided in the Supplementary Method. Overall, the study followed a controlled, open-label, crossover study design (Supplementary Fig. 1). Participants were recruited through posters, social media, and referrals. The inclusion criteria are healthy adults aged 18–25 with a BMI of 18.5–30.0 kg/m2. Exclusion criteria included any diagnosed health condition (chronic or infectious diseases), pregnancy or lactation, smoking or drinking, recent history of surgery or participation in other research, regular use of medications/nutritional supplements, or any allergies/intolerances to the test foods. A general questionnaire (covering demographics, medication use, and supplement use) and a food frequency questionnaire for food intake with 12 months were completed after recruitment. At the baseline and upon completion of the study, participants have height, weight and blood pressure measured. The study was approved by the ethics committee of Shanghai Institute of Nutrition and Health, Chinese Academy of Sciences (No. ER-SINH-252103) and the ethic committee of Shanghai Fourth People's Hospital (No. 2021075–001), and registered on the Chinese Clinical Trials Registry (ChiCTR2100048279). All participants provided their written informed consent. The green kiwifruit used in this study was sourced from Shaanxi, China, and provided by the Shanghai Fourth People's Hospital. A total of 30,065 features were obtained from urine samples including 5,629 features in Hilic(+) mode, 5,031 features in Hilic(-) mode, 8710 features in T3(+) mode, and 10,695 features in T3(-) mode. Cholic acid and its derivatives were widely identified across multiple modes, which may also come from the bile salts added during the intestinal digestion [21]. [Discussion] This intervention study examined urinary excretion of metabolites after a standardized kiwifruit dose and identified 23 candidate biomarkers as potential BFIs of kiwifruits with elevated postprandial levels at 24 h. Fifteen of them were matched to compounds detected in original kiwifruit or its in vitro digested samples. Although these metabolites may not be specific to kiwifruit, a XGBoost algorithm-based model using 7 metabolites achieved substantial discriminative performance (accuracy = 0.88) in predicting kiwifruit intake within two days. Our strategy prioritized fruit-originated metabolites that remained significantly elevated in next morning urine, which enables the capture of metabolites with detection windows exceeding a day and the prediction of food intake from at least 1–2 days prior to sampling. This strategy offers a practical solution, balancing feasibility with reliability in dietary biomarker research compared to the collection of 24-h urine samples.
