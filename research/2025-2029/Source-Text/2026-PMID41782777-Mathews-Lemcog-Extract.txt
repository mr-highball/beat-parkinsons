Title: Acute effects of Zensera lemon balm extract on mood and cognitive performance during cognitive overload
PMID: 41782777
PMCID: PMC12954045
DOI: 10.1177/20451253261415706
Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20451253261415706
Published: 2026-02-28.
Text coverage: Full original, Figure 2 and all four original supplementary files. Registration NCT06183372.

The report studies a single capsule of 300 mg hydroalcoholic Melissa leaf and stem extract versus maltodextrin placebo. Figure 2 shows 123 enrolled and 106 allocated and analyzed, 52 assigned to extract and 54 to placebo. Seventeen withdrew after familiarization and before allocation. Supplementary Table S2 excludes diagnosed neurological conditions and recent psychiatric diagnosis or treatment, including for depression or anxiety. Earlier anxiety or depression history could be eligible. Baseline perceived stress differed between arms (p=.037) and was included in the analysis as a covariate.

Supplementary Table S5 estimates overall calmness of 5.96 to 6.23 under extract versus 6.14 to 6.32 under placebo. The treatment-by-session comparison is nonsignificant. Fatigue rose from 3.61 to 5.62 with extract and from 3.83 to 4.87 with placebo. The reported interaction is p=.051 and the post hoc between-arm comparisons are nonsignificant. The transient visually rated calmness interaction is p=.056, with nonsignificant post hoc comparisons. The Table 4 treatment-by-time anxiety interaction is p=.86. These are distinct short-term scales.

The hardest executive switching trials favored extract at five hours in a pairwise comparison (d=.69, 95% CI .08–1.29, p=.026), while their treatment main effect was p=.08. For the hardest attention-network trials, the interaction is p=.014 and the five-hour between-arm comparison is p=.053. Overall switching scores differed at baseline. The trial included multiple exploratory measurements and found no separate depression score. Its formulation and participants differ from brewed lemon balm and Parkinson disease.
