Original source scope
This cross-sectional NHANES analysis includes 11,612 adults. Higher aMED scores correlate with lower odds of hard stool-classified constipation, including OR 0.82 (95% CI 0.75–0.89) per point in the fully adjusted model.

Evidence boundary or continuing gap
The fat component is the dietary monounsaturated-to-saturated fat ratio, which can reflect multiple foods. The analysis does not assign or distinguish extra-virgin oil, or estimate symptom response in Parkinson disease.

A diet-wide monounsaturated-to-saturated fat ratio at or above the median was associated with lower odds of stool-defined chronic constipation than a ratio below the median (OR 0.78, 95% CI 0.65 to 0.93, p=.009). The separate food rows report OR 0.57 (0.46 to 0.71, p<.001) for vegetables and 0.73 (0.55 to 0.95, p=.029) for whole grains. The appendix and article distinguish the fat ratio from the composite Mediterranean score. Fatty acids can come from multiple foods. The table names no olive oil grade or clinical Parkinson symptom comparison.
