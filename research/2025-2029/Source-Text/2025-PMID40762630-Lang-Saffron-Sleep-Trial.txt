Title: A standardised saffron extract improves subjective and objective sleep quality in healthy older adults with sleep complaints

A four-week randomized placebo-controlled pilot at the University of East Anglia assigned 52 adults aged 55–85 with sleep complaints to a gummy containing standardized Safr'Inside saffron extract or a matched placebo. The CONSORT figure places four losses before randomization and shows 26 people per arm in the analysis. The source uses both a baseline sleep-quality threshold and an insomnia severity threshold for eligibility, with current neurological or psychiatric conditions among the exclusions.

Table 2 reports mean Insomnia Severity Index scores of 11.2 at baseline and 11.4 at follow-up for the saffron group and 8.7 and 11.1 for the placebo group. The time-by-treatment interaction was inconclusive (p = 0.3). The global Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index moved from 9.4 to 7.4 with saffron and from 8.1 to 8.8 with placebo (interaction p = 0.02). Objective sleep onset latency favored the extract (interaction p = 0.03). The study discusses subjective sleep efficiency favorably in the narrative, but final Table 2 reports the corresponding interaction as 0.4. That discrepancy remains unresolved.

PHQ-9 change was listed as a secondary outcome in the registry. Table 1 uses PHQ-9 to characterize the participants at baseline, and the article provides no randomized follow-up depression contrast. Separate outcome and population limits apply to Round 1 claims 0218 and 0063. The journal article explicitly identifies NCT05315986, which still displays an estimated enrollment of 48, status Unknown and no posted registry results. These are two records of the same trial.
