Title: Advanced Nutraceutical Approach for Mood/Calmness, Sleep, and Skin Health in Moderately Stressed Adults
DOI: 10.15226/jnhfs.2025.001193
Source: https://symbiosisonlinepublishing.com/nutritionalhealth-foodscience/nutritionalhealth-foodscience193.pdf
First published: 2025-06-12
Text coverage: Original Methods, Figures 3 and 5, and disclosure, distilled.

Sixty adults with moderate perceived stress were analyzed, 30 per arm. The study excluded histories of psychiatric disorders. A capsule blend of lemon balm extract, magnolia bark extract and L-theanine was compared with a placebo over 30 days. The reported daily amounts were 600 mg lemon balm extract, 100 mg magnolia extract providing 40 mg honokiol, and 200 mg L-theanine. Ingredient characterization cites a 45% aqueous ethanol extraction example. The administered batch's individual constituent quantities are unavailable.

Figure 5 reports baseline and day-30 mean Hamilton depression scores of 17.3 and 1.2 in the combination group and 18.0 and 20.8 in placebo, with reported p<.01. The figure caption names a signed-rank test despite parallel randomization and no description of arm pairing. The prose calls the depression score change an anxiety result. The placebo mean exceeds the range stated in the retrospective registry. No group interval, post-treatment perceived-stress trajectory, or separately reported Profile of Mood States estimate was found.

Major's 2024 conference abstract plausibly describes the same study. It reports single masking, different magnolia wording and a smaller placebo depression score increase. The journal report describes double masking and declares no prior overlapping publication. The 2024 and 2025 versions should be treated as a probable common family with unresolved discrepancies. The combination comparison has no brewed leaf or Parkinson cohort.
