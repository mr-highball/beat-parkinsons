The feasibility report was first published February 4, 2026. Both eight-week phases emphasized olive oil alongside other Mediterranean foods. The article body gives no olive-oil grade, dose or separate participant-level intake estimates. The trial assigned the order of full dietary patterns. The Table 3 safety record includes one mild intermittent insomnia event judged possibly related to the ketogenic diet and one unrelated post-DBS serious insomnia and psychosis event during the MCT-supplemented diet. The investigators asked about adverse events at each check-in and visit. The paper reports 41 participants completed at least one phase and 33 both. Table 4 lists 39 and 41 post-visit observations. Event incidence by completed phase cannot be calculated reliably from those denominators without reconciling the difference.

Related dataset released 2026-10-26: https://doi.org/10.5683/SP4/EKV3YZ
A local count of the publicly released Borealis V1 file `KIM_clinical.tab` uses nonmissing post-visit anxiety ratings and the dictionary categories 1–3 improved, 4 unchanged and 5–7 worse. The ketogenic phase gives 10 improved, 20 unchanged and 4 worse (34 ratings). The MCT-supplemented phase gives 9 improved, 22 unchanged and 6 worse (37 ratings). These are unadjusted responses during overlapping crossover phases, without an olive-only comparator. The public file gives ordinal anxiety change ratings. Post-phase Parkinson Anxiety Scale estimates remain unverified. The later bowel report from the same cohort gives an imprecise bowel-frequency comparison between the full diets (OR 1.03, 95% CI 0.82–1.28).
