The secondary analysis describes 155 adults with chronic pain and depression in a randomized comparison of group medical visits with a standard primary-care visit. Cinnamon intake reportedly rose at nine weeks alongside fruit, vegetable and whole-grain intake. The abstract groups these secondary food findings as p<.05 and supplies no separate cinnamon amount, preparation, change estimate or symptom comparison by food intake.

The parent study likely is NCT02262377, based on the shared investigator, sample, intervention, comparison, timepoints and demographics. The abstract gives no registry identifier. The 2019 clinical report describes inconclusive between-arm comparisons for pain and PHQ-9 at both follow-ups. Both studies address the program. Neither assigns ground cinnamon as a clinical intervention or reports a Parkinson-specific comparison.
