# Food and diet studies from the 2026 Movement Disorders congress

The journal supplement gives October 1, 2026 as its first publication date. The numerical identifiers below identify individual pages in the society archive. [Journal supplement](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/toc/15318257/2026/41/S1)

## Diet diary and executive function in CogTrAiL-RBD

Ophey et al.'s August 5, 2026 primary report and the MDS 35920 abstract describe the same randomized 82-person isolated REM sleep behavior disorder trial, with 40 assigned to cognitive training plus lifestyle education and 42 to the waitlist. The primary report confirmed that the intervention group filled out MEDAS diaries. Baseline MEDAS totals averaged 5.23 ± 2.52 for controls and 6.08 ± 2.18 for intervention participants, with three and one missing scores, respectively. For executive function the combined program yielded d = 0.500 (95% CI 0.221–0.779) at six weeks and d = 0.397 (0.115–0.679) at six months. Secondary cognitive and motor measures, including MDS-UPDRS III, showed no significant between-group change. The congress figure plots baseline cognitive and imaging predictors. The paper and supplementary tables give no food-item scores or ingredient-by-outcome estimates, and the investigators restrict participant data. These results concern an isolated RBD cohort and a program combining cognitive training and education. [Primary results, Tables 1 and 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13440496/) · [Local licensed article text](Source-Text/PMC13440496.txt) · [Original supplement](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC13440496.1/ALZ-22-e71707-s001.pdf) · [MDS archive record 35920 and figure](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/digital-cognitive-training-and-promoting-a-healthy-lifestyle-as-a-booster-for-cognitive-reserve-in-prodromal-parkinsons-disease/) · [Protocol and named food items](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-MDS-Diet-Followup.md#diet-monitoring-in-the-cogtrail-rbd-protocol).

## Diet and premotor REM sleep behavior disorder in Seoul

Jo et al. (poster 1706, archive 37248) compared 87 people with Parkinson’s, 15 with idiopathic REM sleep behavior disorder (iRBD), and 45 controls. The Parkinson’s group was split by premotor RBD (44 with, 43 without). Within the subgroup with premotor RBD, greater reported lipid intake, particularly from ultra-processed foods, was associated with lower microbial alpha diversity. The outcome for that intake comparison was microbiome composition. RBD status defined the subgroups, and the abstract gives no estimate for a food’s association with dream enactment frequency or RBD severity. [Primary congress abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/altered-diet-microbiome-associations-in-parkinsons-disease-and-prodromal-rem-sleep-behavior-disorder/) · [Poster schedule, p. 154](https://www.mdscongress.org/Abstract-Schedule.pdf#page=154)

The related Lee et al. paper, published April 30, 2025, compared 57 Parkinson’s participants with premotor RBD, 47 without it and 85 controls. Its dietary supplement includes 52, 45 and 78 participants from those groups, respectively. Median total fiber was 36.3, 31.1 and 34.0 g (p = .822 across the three groups). Mean fat intake was 67.1, 69.8 and 67.7 g (p = .910). The Methods explicitly identify the preceding 2019–2020 Asan study and report adding 19 Parkinson’s participants without premotor RBD in 2024. Authors and phenotype overlap with the 2026 abstract, but the exact participant overlap with that abstract is unverified. Supplement Table S1 supplies aggregate nutrient comparisons without an individual food or a change in RBD symptoms. [Primary article, PMID 40307949](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40168-025-02095-w) · [Supplementary Table S1](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC12042535.1/40168_2025_2095_MOESM1_ESM.docx) · [Earlier Asan report](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-MDS-Diet-Followup.md#diet-questionnaire-in-the-earlier-asan-microbiome-cohort)

## Probiotic capsule trial with measured REM sleep behavior symptoms

Du et al.’s July 27, 2025 paper reports an open-label randomized comparison of 50 people with idiopathic Parkinson’s, 25 per group. The intervention combined Bacillus licheniformis with BIFICO capsules while both groups continued usual treatment. At 12 weeks, the total UPDRS changed by −4.8 ± 7.5 versus +1.8 ± 13.1 (p = .009). RBD questionnaire change was −7.5 ± 6.5 in 21 probiotic participants versus 0 ± 5.8 in 17 controls with RBD scores (p = .015). The clinical estimate concerns a multi-strain capsule and a composite RBD scale, with no separate dream enactment frequency result. The 2026 Suhendro review (poster 805, archive 35600) includes this trial and an earlier constipation report from the same lead author and center. Their participant overlap remains unverified. The review also includes a fecal microbiota transplant among its six studies. The two other useful product comparisons cited in that table are Enterolactis Duo and Symprove. ## Tofu intake and Parkinson’s diagnosis

Guo and Li (poster 1110, archive 36500) used BioBank Japan genetic instruments for tofu intake and Taiwan Precision Medicine Initiative genetic summary data for Parkinson’s diagnosis. The primary inverse-variance weighted estimate was OR 0.86 (95% CI 0.65 to 1.15, p = .32). The data provide no preparation information, participant exposure to baked tofu, or drooling measure for statement 0693. ## Community design of the Engage in Healthy Eating program

Shah et al. (poster 1599, archive 37242) used surveys in English, Spanish and simplified Chinese, and an English-language focus group, to design a Parkinson’s nutrition program. Participants identified barriers involving food preparation, access, dysphagia, and how Mediterranean dietary guidance fits their food traditions. The abstract describes developing a nutrition manual, cookbook, and coaching pilot. It does not report the number of participants, a completed diet intervention, or measured changes in symptoms. Columbia’s similarly named Engage-PD program has overlapping investigators and offers physical activity coaching. No matching public registry, methods supplement, cookbook, or outcome report for this nutrition pilot was located by November 12, 2026. [Primary congress abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/engage-in-healthy-eating-a-community-guided-approach-to-culturally-sensitive-nutrition-support-for-people-with-parkinsons-disease-pd/) · [Poster schedule, p. 37](https://www.mdscongress.org/Abstract-Schedule.pdf#page=37) · [Columbia physical activity program](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/neurorehab/research/intervention-studies-/studies-on-parkinsons-disease-pd/engage-parkinsons-disease-engage-pd/)

## Vancouver probiotic powder trials and symptom outcomes

Leung and Appel-Cresswell’s poster 1037 (archive 37440) names the Bjorn Moller Research Repository, the Vancouver Parkinson’s Research Initiative, and an ongoing probiotic trial. The abstract describes anticipated associations between depression, cognition and gastrointestinal symptoms. It supplies no observed coefficients, sample sizes, or randomized comparison by exposure. The trial description matches UBC’s ProD registration NCT05568498, with the same team and both IDS-C and PD-CRS endpoints. The record was first posted October 5, 2022, lists January 20, 2025 as the actual start, and remains recruiting with no posted results as of November 12, 2026. It compares 12 weeks of oral Ecologic BARRIER 849 powder with placebo. The observational cohorts’ overlap with ProD is unreported. [Primary congress abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/evaluating-the-relationship-between-depression-and-other-non-motor-symptoms-in-parkinsons-disease/) · [Poster schedule, p. 64](https://www.mdscongress.org/Abstract-Schedule.pdf#page=64) · [UBC ProD and Vancouver Parkinson’s Research Initiative](https://www.parkinsons.ubc.ca/our-studies) · [Registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05568498) · [Local registry record](../2020-2024/Source-Text/Trials/NCT05568498.txt)

The Bjorn Moller repository’s 2025 primary abstract describes diet and symptom questionnaires and MoCA and MDS-UPDRS assessments at visits about 18 months apart. It reports 128 recruits overall and 59 first visits as of March 11, 2025. Table 1 describes an earlier 47-person baseline subset with 14 Parkinson’s diagnoses. The 2025 table contains no dietary intake values or food-by-symptom comparison. The 2026 poster does not identify which repository participants were selected for its analyses. [2025 primary congress report](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/the-bjorn-moller-research-repository-a-resource-for-movement-disorders-research/) · [Original Table 1](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/0936_1402_001780_1.png)

Lam et al.’s April 25, 2026 paper reports the separate UBC anxiety trial NCT03968133. The paper dates screening to December 2020–February 2023 and the last participant follow-up to July 2023. Sixty-one participants with Parkinson’s and clinically significant anxiety were randomized to 12 weeks of nine-strain freeze-dried Ecologic Barrier powder (30) or its maize-starch and maltodextrin carrier (31). In the intention-to-treat analysis, the adjusted probiotic-minus-placebo difference was +1.0 points for the primary Parkinson Anxiety Scale (95% CI −2.9 to 4.9, p = .60), +0.4 on the Fatigue Severity Scale (−0.4 to 1.1, p = .35), +0.5 on Rome III constipation severity (−1.9 to 2.8, p = .68) and −2.6 on MDS-UPDRS Part III (−7.8 to 2.5, p = .31). The secondary total MoCA difference was +1.1 points (0.04 to 2.1, p = .043), with no multiple-comparison adjustment. Table 4 gives untested descriptive attention, delayed recall and other MoCA domain scores. The paper does not report a causal estimate for individual dietary foods, including yogurt and kefir. [Primary article, Tables 2 and 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13125604/) · [Local CC BY article](Source-Text/PMC13125604.txt) · [Anxiety trial registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03968133)

The trial collected a 165-item food-frequency questionnaire at baseline. Supplementary Table 7 reports only group aggregates, including mean caffeine of 143.6 versus 179.5 mg (p = .29) and fiber of 26.3 versus 24.2 g (p = .33). Diet was not reassessed after treatment. The published data give no beverage-specific association with anxiety, fatigue, or cognition. Supplementary Table 8 reports a similar per-protocol fatigue difference (+0.3 points, 95% CI −0.3 to 0.9, p = .27). [Original supplementary tables](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC13125604.1/41531_2026_1364_MOESM1_ESM.pdf) · [Local CC BY text of Tables 7–9](Source-Text/PMC13125604-Supplement.txt)

## Modified low-dairy ketogenic diet and freezing of gait

Worster et al. published this patient-initiated, single-group feasibility study on July 23, 2025. Twelve people with Parkinson’s at Hoehn and Yahr stages 2–3 completed 12 weeks of a ketogenic pattern with minimal animal dairy, plant foods for fiber, and weekly dietary coaching. Average recorded intake was 70% fat, 18% protein, and 5% net carbohydrate. The paper describes weekly menus and food diaries, and publishes aggregate macronutrients. It gives no participant-level food quantities, recipe supplement, or ingredient-specific symptom contrast. [Primary report, Table 2 and Figure 3](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1601446/full) · [Local CC BY article](Source-Text/PMC12325006.txt)

Mean UPDRS Part III changed from 29.50 to 23.33, with 50% improving and 25% worsening by the paper’s clinically important difference criterion. The mean freezing-of-gait score changed from 13.83 to 13.00, with 25% improving and 50% worsening by that criterion. The mean mini-BEST changed from 22.58 to 23.42, with 25% improving and 17% worsening. Seven reported unchanged constipation, four worse constipation, and one improved constipation. The PDQ-39 cognition domain changed from 2.92 to 1.92, with 42% improving by the same criterion. These within-person observations have no concurrent control. [Primary results](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1601446/full)

## Gastric motility and motor function in Suzhou

Two 2026 congress abstracts by Jin, Cheng, Mao and Liu use the same counts of 56 people with early Parkinson’s and 26 controls, with electrogastrography and gastric emptying ultrasound. Their authors, setting, measurements, and counts indicate likely cohort overlap. Jin’s imaging abstract reports greater dysrhythmia and slower emptying in the gastric-motility-disorder subgroup. ## Serum biomarkers and phenoconversion in Beijing

Zang et al.’s April 15, 2026 paper and Zhang et al.’s October MDS poster 975 (archive 36133) both report 148 participants with polysomnography-confirmed isolated RBD, 142 matched controls and 138 followed participants. The article reports 34 conversions after a mean 3.67 years. The congress abstract reports 35 after a mean 3.79 years and repeats the paper’s dementia-first hazard ratios. The shared investigators, sample counts and estimates indicate overlapping reports. The analyses give dementia-first HRs of 0.992 (95% CI 0.985–0.998) per unit of serum uric acid, 1.070 (1.028–1.113) for homocysteine and 1.477 (1.081–2.017) for fasting blood glucose. The poster’s Table 3 gives parkinsonism-first HRs of 0.996, 1.023 and 1.072, respectively, with each CI including 1. The report gives 22 enrollees at that point and no comparative symptoms. ## Beverage fluid intake and a timed water swallow in Aberdeen

Sleeman et al.’s October 9, 2025 case-control study used a 24-hour dietary recall in 19 people with recently diagnosed Parkinson’s and 11 household controls. The abstract reports median fluid from drinks of 1,124 versus 1,799 mL (p = .005). The Results section labels the same numbers means and prints p < .001. Both comparisons are by diagnosis. The study measured a 150 mL water challenge by blinded video review. Drinking speed was 6.0 versus 7.5 mL/second (p = .23), while Bristol stool score was 3.2 versus 4.6 (p = .01). The report gives no intake estimates by beverage or within-Parkinson beverage association with stool consistency, swallowing, or orthostatic symptoms. ## Nicotine synthesis and the Iranian diet trial

The August 17, 2026 Rissardo et al. preprint and the MDS 2026 abstract (archive 35565) report the same six trials, 389 participants, and pooled motor, daily living, quality-of-life and levodopa-equivalent dose estimates. Their author lists and adverse-event estimates differ. The Iranian trial by Lorvand Amiri et al. is the only dietary intervention in Table 1. The other studies used nicotine patches, nicotine gum or nicotine capsules. The congress motor forest plot gives SMD −2.04 (95% CI −2.80 to −1.28) for the Iranian whole-diet trial, which it lists once. The preprint Table 1 reports a 20 μg/day dietary nicotine target with and without protein redistribution. It gives no item-specific symptom estimate. The Iranian primary paper and registry are already cataloged. [Preprint, Table 1 and §3.4](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202608.1088) · [Congress abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/clinical-impact-of-nicotine-in-parkinsons-disease-evidence-from-randomized-controlled-studies/) · [Congress motor forest plot](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/0988_1474_000301_1.jpeg) · [Iranian diet trial](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Iran-Trial-Results.md#nicotine-rich-dietary-pattern-and-protein-timing)

Between-study heterogeneity fell from 85% to 28.7% when the Iranian study was omitted. The pooled estimates mix routes of administration. They provide context for the trial with no independent test of its dietary intervention. ## Okara tofu and saliva secretion during chewing

First electronically published March 30, 2026. The original abstract compared soybean residue-enriched tofu containing 15% or 50% okara with comparator tofu during chewing and reported higher salivary secretion with the enriched formulations. Number of participants, detailed contrasts and a baked preparation were unverified in the abstract. The study measured acute oral processing and secretion. It did not measure saliva escaping the mouth or Parkinson drooling. [Original author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41931940/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2026-Zhang-Okara-Tofu-Saliva-Abstract-Extract.txt)
