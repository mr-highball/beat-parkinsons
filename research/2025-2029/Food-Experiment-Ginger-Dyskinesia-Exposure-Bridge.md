# Ground ginger and levodopa-induced dyskinesia evidence

## Measured animal signal

The 2024 WO patent and 2025 Korean disclosure present the same inventor-reported levodopa-induced dyskinesia mouse comparison. The Korean original gives repeated axial, limb and orolingual abnormal involuntary movement (AIM) scoring. Its table 3 reports means and standard errors of 144.00 ± 15.56 with dried rhizome extract and 276.75 ± 26.00 with the levodopa comparator. Both isolated 6-shogaol arms also had lower reported means. Figure 3 identifies benserazide alongside levodopa. These are multiple arms of one experiment. [2024 disclosure](../2020-2024/Source-Text/2024-Kyung-Hee-Ginger-Patent-Abnormal-Movement-and-Fecal-Water.txt) · [2025 counterpart](Source-Text/2025-KR20250059906A-Dried-Ginger-Extract-Lineage-Extract.txt)

The extract group had lower mean AIM scores at all eight nonzero observations. The reported AUC difference was −132.75, or −47.97% of the comparator mean. Integrating the printed 20-minute series produces values about four times the reported AUC across all nonzero arms. That shared scale issue leaves the direction intact, while the source's AUC units, animal counts, allocation, route, masking and exact pairwise inference remain unresolved. The negative zingerone comparison involved a different ginger constituent and observed post-levodopa hindlimb stiffening under a separate design. ## Preparation and conflicting scope

Shao and colleagues measured 6-shogaol in three commercial ground dried ginger rhizome powders, with mean concentrations from 1.157 to 1.495 mg/g of product. This establishes that the same compound occurs in the frozen food preparation. Neither paper measured the active composition of the administered patent extract against those retail powders or measured an equivalent absorbed exposure. The bridge supports only the weakest evidence tier. [Ground ginger analysis](../2010-2014/Source-Text/2010-PMID21090746-Shao-Commercial-Ground-Ginger-Shogaol.txt)

The 2026 6-shogaol and levodopa article reports a separate mouse pole task and bead-expulsion results. It gives no AIM outcome. Purified dehydrozingerone improved fly climbing in one Parkinson model and showed pain-related and mood-related outcomes in separate non-Parkinson animal studies. These do not independently corroborate a dyskinesia effect. ## Clinical checks

The published September 2026 review includes six multi-ingredient trials in people with levodopa-induced dyskinesia. The itemized formulas and symptom-based additions contain no culinary ginger or Zingiber officinale. Their reported effects do not transfer to the ginger claim. Targeted searches and registry checks on November 25 found no posted dyskinesia result for the three registered ginger exposures. NCT07882108 is recruiting and lists composite MDS-UPDRS Parts I–IV as another outcome. IRCT20251028067794N1 reports recruitment complete and gives total MDS-UPDRS as its primary outcome. NCT05001217 is a completed multi-ingredient trial with no posted summary results. [Formula review](Source-Text/2026-DOI10.22246-jikm.2026.47.4.761-Herbal-Dyskinesia-Formula-Review.txt) · [Ginger-PD](Source-Text/Trials/NCT07882108.txt) · [Standardized powder trial](Source-Text/Trials/IRCT20251028067794N1.txt) · [Multi-herb trial](../2020-2024/Source-Text/Trials/NCT05001217.txt)

Claim 0271 asks if ground ginger worsens dyskinesia. The one preclinical AIM family and the measured ground-preparation bridge support a minimal easing lean, **Maybe False** against the frozen wording, score anchor **50.005**. The group estimate is an animal result and the score is a rubric tier. A human ground-ginger comparison with a separate dyskinesia scale and a preparation-matched replication are still missing.
