# Brown rice, postural instability and dream enactment

## Optional brown rice in registered Parkinson meal boxes

Brain Bites PD lists brown rice among example ingredients in ACTRN12625000607471p, first posted June 12, 2025. The program plans home preparation of supplied ingredients for eight meals over two weeks in 20 people with Parkinson's and their care partners. Feasibility is primary, with cooking and eating interviews and PDQ-39 secondary. Quality-of-life mobility and examples of motor barriers do not supply a specific postural instability or dream-enactment outcome. The registry provides neither confirmation of actual brown-rice preparation or consumption nor participant results. This is a methods-only scope match. [Official registration](https://www.anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=389684) · [Prior local registration context](Food-Experiment-Registry-Followup.md)

## Retort brown rice and post-meal sleepiness

The official UMIN record UMIN000056929 was first publicly posted February 4, 2025 and gives the same date for its posted results. It describes a randomized open crossover of retort-packaged processed brown rice and white rice in 15 young adult female students. The Stanford Sleepiness Scale was primary. The bilingual registry summary reports less post-lunch drowsiness with the brown-rice product and no association with the presumed glycemic-index explanation. There are no arm-level scale values, uncertainty estimates, nocturnal sleep measures, or reports about dream enactment. The result is currently a qualitative registration summary, while the separate journal-publication field remains blank. [Official bilingual registry and results](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000063159) · [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/UMIN000056929.txt)

## Brown rice protein hydrolysate and wearable REM estimates

Mohan and colleagues first published July 28, 2026, before the December issue date. Their 250 mg PeptiSleep-versus-microcrystalline-cellulose subgroup randomized 30 adults with mild to moderate sleep impairment and retained 26 in a per-protocol analysis. The orally consumed product was an enzymatic hydrolysate of brown-rice protein isolate. Both groups had wearable-estimated REM fractions in the 21–23% range, with a largest between-group difference in change of 11.63 minutes across time windows. The REM result was an inconclusive trend. Secondary comparisons had no multiplicity adjustment. Wearable sleep staging from a ring, REM-related muscle activity and observed dream enactment are distinct measurement targets. No Parkinson cohort or steamed grain exposure is reported. [Original Methods and Figure 5E](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13463274/) · [Local original factual extract](Source-Text/PMID42591960-rice-peptide-rem-extract.txt)

## Fermented rice germ EEG in a sedative rat model

2025-08-12. Ye and colleagues orally gave GABA-rich fermented rice-germ material to pentobarbital-treated rats and assessed EEG-defined sleep states. Figure 3 reports reduced wake time and more NREM sleep under the extract conditions. The highlighted REM increase compares pentobarbital-treated controls with untreated rats. The report gives no extract-related REM-atonia or observed dream-enactment estimate. It used germ extract and a sedative animal model. [Original Methods and Figure 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12342256/) · [Local source text](Source-Text/PMC12342256.txt)

## Separate fermented rice germ trial of perceived stress

2026. An eight-week randomized trial in 80 adults with perceived stress reported a Stress Response Inventory difference of −5.39 points versus placebo (95% CI −10.25 to −0.54, p = .035). The accessible primary abstract lists stress, quality of life and biochemical outcomes and does not give any REM or dream-enactment result. The original body was unavailable through the publisher route tested. It is independent of the rat study and uses the same branded germ extract. [Primary structured abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jff.2026.107183)
