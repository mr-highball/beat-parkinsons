# Curry leaf motor tests and cilantro source follow-up

## Curry leaf extract in rotenone-exposed mice

Anjum, Raza and Faheem published this mouse study online May 10, 2025. The publisher preview includes Figure 2. In the leaf extract plus rotenone group, plotted beam traversal was roughly 7 seconds versus 108 with rotenone alone. Pole descent was about 10 versus 13 seconds, and open-field distance about 15 versus 8 meters. These are visual estimates from the graph. The extract arm shows the authors’ comparison markers on those panels and no corresponding marker on tail-suspension immobility. Open-field distance remained below the control group. Figure 1 shows that extract was given before toxin exposure during the study. The complete methods and exact estimates remain restricted. The tested leaf extract and timed mouse tasks leave clinical freezing, tremor, rigidity and bradykinesia after cooked or ground leaf unresolved. ## Cilantro leaf extract in two mouse models

Lee and colleagues’ paper first appeared online September 17, 2025, and appeared in the January 2026 issue. The investigators report lower motor impairment in both mouse models. In the MPTP model, the publisher preview reports grip strength of 2.31 ± 0.05 N with MPTP alone, 2.65 ± 0.08 N with 30 mg/kg oral coriander leaf extract, and 2.75 ± 0.06 N with 100 mg/kg. It also names rotarod and pole tests. The preview truncates the next result before identifying the endpoint for two reported fold changes. The individual rotarod and pole estimates and numerical motor results for the *Proteus mirabilis* arm remain unverified. Grip strength does not measure walking speed. The study does not establish a walking outcome after eating dried culinary cilantro. [Primary report and date](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40972938/) · [Publisher methods and results preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0955286325002785).

Myung Sook Oh supervised the Lee study and was an inventor on the [2016 Kyung Hee patent](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Umbellifer-Herbs.md#coriander-aerial-part-extract-in-a-korean-patent), which reports a single-dose, six-day aerial-part extract protocol and matches Gunhyuk Park’s 2014 dissertation. The Lee MPTP arm used two leaf-extract doses over 15 days, added grip testing, and was accompanied by a second, gut-first model. The Lee study and Park dissertation describe distinct experiments from the same research program. The separate Koppula study used coriander fruit. [Kyung Hee article record](https://khu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/coriander-coriandrum-sativum-l-leaves-improves-brain-and-gut-path/) · [Earlier dissertation](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Umbellifer-Herbs.md#coriander-aerial-part-extract-and-motor-performance-in-a-korean-dissertation) · [2021 fruit extract](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Chives-Cilantro-Curry-Leaf.md#coriander-fruit-extract-in-mptp-exposed-mice).
