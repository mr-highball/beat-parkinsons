# Chive anxiety evidence in 2025–2029

## Open-arm entries in the Simran supplement

The publisher supplement to the 21 July 2026 Simran report identifies open-arm entries as an anxiety-like behavior measure. It separates those entries in Supplementary Table S3 from the line-crossing measure of locomotion in Table S2. For example, hippocampal serotonin correlated with open-arm entries at Spearman r=.8739, p<.0001. Table S3 gives neurochemical correlations rather than group means or effects for each ferulic acid dose or the fluoxetine comparator. The public main abstract reports reduced behavioral deficits after oral purified ferulic acid in a mouse maternal-separation model. The full article remains behind a subscription preview.

The supplement resolves the exact anxiety-related behavioral measure. The isolated compound, model and preparation leave effects of eating dried chives on anxiety in people with Parkinson's open for claim 0789.

## Oral ferulic acid and anxiety behaviors with locomotor control

Çetiner and colleagues published online on 12 October 2025. Their female BALB/c mouse experiment compared oral ferulic acid with vehicle and included intraperitoneal diazepam as a positive control. The abstract reports across-test anxiolytic-like behavior without a locomotor change at 100 mg/kg and partial effects in some elevated-plus-maze and open-field parameters at 0.1 and 10 mg/kg. It also reports that yohimbine and WAY-100635 opposed the high-dose response while flumazenil did not. The full numerical arm results are still needed. A 2017 thesis indexed under the same advisor and with highly similar intervention and assay details warrants cohort deduplication before being counted as independent evidence. 

This compound study presents a favorable anxiety-related signal with a separate locomotor check. The exposures were purified ferulic acid and a laboratory mouse model.
