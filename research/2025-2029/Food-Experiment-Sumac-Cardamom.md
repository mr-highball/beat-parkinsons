# Food experiment evidence for sumac and cardamom

## 2025 Sumac aqueous extract and constipation scores in dyspepsia

Saravani and colleagues randomized 104 adults with functional dyspepsia into four arms receiving aqueous *Rhus coriaria* extract, eating-habit changes, both or omeprazole. Figure 1 shows that 25, 11, 24 and 25 participants were analyzed in the combination, habit-only, extract-only and omeprazole arms, respectively. Table 6 marks greater improvement in constipation subscores with combination and extract-only treatment versus omeprazole only in adjusted model 6 at week eight (printed p = .000 and .002, respectively). The image reports p-values and directions without effect magnitudes or confidence intervals. The authors reported no adverse events. This gives an opposite-direction clinical lead for 0210 using aqueous extract in dyspepsia. ## 2025 Cardamom allergy with abdominal pain and presyncope

Pinhal and colleagues reported a 32-year-old man who developed hives after a cardamom seed in tea and later experienced generalized itching, abdominal pain and presyncope after butter chicken made with cardamom. Their allergy work-up found reactivity to cardamom. This presents a food-associated allergy lead for 0363 and 0453 in a distinct case. Orthostatic measurements and a Parkinson diagnosis were outside the report. [Primary conference abstract](https://eaaci2025.process.y-congress.com/ScientificProcess/Data/67/587/Abstracts/545af15bd4cbd1cac2a05b07b2322e47.html)

## 2026 Cardamonin and cineole in Parkinson-related models

Bhor and colleagues tested isolated cardamonin in reserpine- and haloperidol-induced mouse models and reported changes in motor behavior, oxidative markers and tissue dopamine. A separate 2026 cardamonin paper focused on NF-κB signaling. A third team tested β-cyclodextrin-complexed 1,8-cineole in a reserpine model and reported changes in motor measures and tyrosine hydroxylase immunoreactivity. These search hits involve defined compounds or a delivery complex. None establishes exposure to ground *Elettaria cardamomum*. Their model outcomes do not establish a clinical effect for 0602, 0706, 0829 or 0884. [Bhor primary report, PMID 42975774](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42975774/) · [Cardamonin signaling study, PMID 41803928](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13085287/) · [Cineole complex primary report, PMID 42879042](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42879042/)

## 2026 Black cardamom extract and attention tasks

An acute randomized report and a 14-day randomized report assessed MA2-24, an aqueous extract of black cardamom (*Amomum subulatum*), alone and with caffeine in healthy working adults. Both cite CTRI/2024/09/074472 and use the Shifting Attention Test. Treat the papers as one registration family. The 14-day report gives a placebo-adjusted difference of 7.46 correct responses at one hour on day 14 for extract alone (p < .001). At eight hours, the significant contrast was limited to the extract-caffeine group. A patent covers black and green cardamom formulations, while the published attention experiments identify black cardamom. These are task-based attention findings in another species and preparation for 0574. Neither paper tests ground green cardamom in Parkinson’s. [Acute primary report](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2026.1786880/full) · [14-day primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13662912/) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC13662912.txt) · [Patent publication](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2026093920A1/en)

## 2026 Sumac and anise pediatric appetite trial registration

IRCT20260913071038N1 registered a placebo-controlled trial on September 17, 2026. Its intervention combines oral sumac and anise extract syrup for eight weeks. The summary says each 5 mL contains 250 mg of each extract and the detailed intervention field says 500 mg each. The registry lists pediatric appetite and eating behavior, excludes organic constipation, and showed recruiting status with no results on November 13, 2026. [Official registration](https://irct.ir/trial/93284)

## Ground cardamom challenge in an allergy case

Pinhal and colleagues describe one 32-year-old person with reactions after eating a cardamom seed and a cardamom-containing meal. A supervised cardamom tea challenge was negative. Ground cardamom provocation elicited generalized urticaria and abdominal discomfort. The report gives no swallowing difficulty during that provocation. The previously reported mortadella challenge involved a different patient with acute dysphagia under a mixed food. Both are allergy cases, and neither measures the severity of Parkinson swallowing difficulty.
