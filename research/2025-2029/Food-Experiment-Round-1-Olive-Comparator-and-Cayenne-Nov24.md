# Round 1 olive oil comparator and cayenne source audit

The November 24, 2026 batch resolved 76 exact claim–source checks across 60 originals, resulting in 64 contextual exclusions and 12 related scopes. Seventeen related assessments were added. The earlier ulcerative-colitis oil comparison was refreshed in place after verifying its original figure caption.

## Extra-virgin olive oil

Figure 2 of the ulcerative-colitis crossover reports lower constipation scores after extra-virgin olive oil than after canola oil in a paired comparison among 32 people who completed both periods (p<.001). The verified caption supplies a second active-oil comparison in humans, alongside the prior extra-virgin versus refined olive oil trial in functional constipation. The effect size, crossover period and sequence effects, and safety could not be verified from the available primary report sections. The cohorts were outside Parkinson disease. Claim 0072 retains its existing minimal easing tier with both comparisons represented. [Original oil comparison](../2020-2024/Source-Text/2020-PMID31901082-Morvaridi-EVOO-vs-Canola-UC-Constipation.txt) · [Earlier provenance of the same crossover](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2019-Morvaridi-Ulcerative-Colitis-Oil-Trial-Dataset.txt)

The COLONIC trial proposes dietary fat substitution with extra-virgin olive oil in adults with and without ulcerative colitis. It registers disease activity and microbiota as primary outcomes and includes HADS anxiety and an IBD fatigue scale. The Cochrane table estimated 50 participants. The official registry later lists a target of 56 and carries no results. The registered measurements do not establish a cold-pressed olive oil anxiety effect. [Official registry record](../2015-2019/Source-Text/Trials/2019-ACTRN12619000150145-COLONIC-Extra-Virgin-Olive-Oil.txt) · [Cochrane review](../2020-2024/Source-Text/2020-PMID32297974-IBD-Fatigue-Review-and-Extra-Virgin-Oil-Protocol.txt)

## Cayenne and clove

The cardiac review links concentrated cayenne slimming products to case reports of acute coronary events. The January 2012 case documents clinically measured findings after five days of product use and suspected vasospasm. A different April 2012 case is verified to the original author abstract. The 2010 series describes two arrhythmias, distinct from the later ischemic outcomes. The separate case records capture possible harms without establishing attribution to a specific ingredient or a risk rate. They supply no comparison for ground cayenne and Parkinson pain. The same cardiac review identifies eugenol in clove oil and addresses cardiac effects. [Cardiac review](../2020-2024/Source-Text/2020-PMID32586044-Capsaicin-Eugenol-and-Cardiac-Outcome-Boundaries.txt) · [2010 arrhythmia series](../2010-2014/Source-Text/2010-TJEM-294-Cayenne-Pill-Arrhythmia-Cases.txt) · [January 2012 clinical case](../2010-2014/Source-Text/2012-PMID22264348-Cayenne-Pill-Vasospasm-and-Chest-Pain.txt) · [April 2012 clinical case](../2010-2014/Source-Text/2012-PMID22527825-Cayenne-Pill-and-Myocardial-Infarction.txt)

## Tomato, sesame oil and coffee

The prospective tomato questionnaire study reports pooled highest-versus-lowest incidence hazard ratios of 0.91 (95% CI 0.67–1.25) for tomatoes and 0.86 (0.61–1.22) for processed tomatoes. One male cohort had lower rates in intermediate processed-tomato categories. Highest-frequency estimates were inconclusive and the outcome was incident diagnosis. The rat sesame study used injected oil and evaluated hippocampal biomarkers. The caffeine and migraine review describes acute wakefulness or perceived fatigue separately from headaches after withdrawal. These endpoint and exposure differences limit reuse for the respective culinary Parkinson claims.
