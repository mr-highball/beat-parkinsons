## Caffeine and distinct fatigue measures

The 12-person older-adult crossover reported increased vigor after isolated caffeine. Its fatigue subscale and exertion results were inconclusive, even though vigor differed. The separate 19-person report gives functional task results and readiness to invest effort. It does not supply the fatigue endpoint needed to establish a replication. Whether participants overlap is still open. An earlier older-adult crossover found lower exertion and better endurance after a longer period of pretest caffeine abstinence, while a report with shorter abstinence had inconclusive results. [2013 fatigue subscale](../2010-2014/Source-Text/2013-PMID24303144-Caffeine-and-Older-Adult-Fatigue.txt) · [2014 functional outcomes](../2010-2014/Source-Text/2014-PMID25470803-Caffeine-and-Functional-Effort.txt) · [Prior endurance family](../2005-2009/Source-Text/2005-PMID16081625-Norager-Caffeine-Endurance-and-Sway.txt)

The same concentration of isolated caffeine shortened mouse soleus endurance by 17.6% during maximal activation and extended it by 19.2% during submaximal activation. An earlier study found an inconclusive lower-concentration recovery result. It found opposite net-power effects in two muscle types at a much higher concentration. The age-stratified study measures maximal power, with fatigue resistance discussed as an extrapolation. These comparisons qualify a general fatigue claim, and the existing small brewed-coffee easing lean still rests on the closer beverage data. ## Botanical preparations and documented use

The 2016 screen distinguishes turmeric rhizome, common sage leaf, Indonesian cinnamon and clove extracts. Its endpoints are biochemical or cellular. A separate tumor study used both curcumin and a branded turmeric preparation, with outcomes of cell viability and death. The Marquesan interviews document turmeric in hydration and perfume, and name culinary ginger separately from shampoo ginger. The reports of those practices supply plant identity and preparation context with neither measured Parkinson pain outcomes nor ingested-spice comparisons. ## Cinnamon odor and clinical screening

The 2017 iRBD study names cinnamon as one of its odor identification items, reports aggregate olfaction, and records constipation and sleep variables separately. A previously indexed Chinese diagnostic study provides individual cinnamon recognition. The rates were 25/54 in healthy controls, 16/54 in people with iRBD and 17/54 in people with Parkinson disease (three-group p=.154). This comparison helps interpret recognition of a familiar or unfamiliar smell, with the effects of eating cinnamon on olfaction, constipation or dream enactment still open.
