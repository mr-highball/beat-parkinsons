# Potato Off Time

## Broad diet answer and daily OFF time in the N-DOSE protocol

The N-DOSE protocol includes MDS-UPDRS IV item 4.3 at printed page 103, where typical daily OFF hours contribute to motor complication scoring. Its lifestyle questionnaire at printed page 129 offers a broad Western diet option with examples including meat and roasted potatoes or vegetables. The fields do not assign or quantify potato intake. The retrieved version gives no estimate relating the diet answer to OFF hours. The registered intervention concerns nicotinamide riboside dose. [Original source](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/66/NCT05589766/Prot_000.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2025-N-DOSE-Diet-and-Daily-Off-Time-Protocol-Extract.txt)

## Fried or roasted potatoes in a Parkinson case-control questionnaire

The food-frequency questionnaire asked 680 Parkinson cases and 612 controls about a combined fried or roasted potato category. The paper evaluates dietary patterns and case status. Its clinical descriptions do not give daily OFF duration by that food. The survey combines frying and roasting and does not report an equivalent isolated preparation comparison. It is the same study family used in other protocol notes for pooled fish and individual carrot terms. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13347557/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2025-Vitucci-Pooled-Potato-Questionnaire-Extract.txt)

## N-DOSE analysis plan and motor complication endpoint scope

The June 16, 2025 statistical analysis plan was uploaded July 2, 2025 and uses the June 5 protocol. Sections 4.4 and 5.3 plan MDS-UPDRS analyses for the total and individual parts, including part IV. The protocol measures usual daily OFF hours as a part IV item and asks a broad, separate diet question. The statistical plan does not specify a separate potato intake or potato-to-OFF analysis. The named trial completed April 22, 2025 with 81 enrollees and the registry still has no posted results. The analysis plan, protocol and registration describe one trial.
