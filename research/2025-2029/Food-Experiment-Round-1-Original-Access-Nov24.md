## Coconut oil, MCTs and whole-diet comparisons

Tidman and colleagues' 32-week nonrandomized Parkinson crossover had 16 completers. Compared with the preceding habitual-diet phase, the habitual-diet-plus-C8/C10-MCT phase had mean fatigue 1.1 points lower and mean Epworth sleepiness 0.5 points lower. These are descriptive contrasts, and overall phase effects were inconclusive for both outcomes. The ketogenic-plus-MCT phase had higher mean fatigue than the initial diet phase. Participants selected MCT supplements, and the final article does not document a virgin or refined coconut oil intervention. Some participants who volunteered for interviews reported less fatigue or better energy, with varied results and no separate oil comparison. [Final article, result table and protocol discrepancy](Source-Text/2026-DOI10.4102-jmh.v9i1.154-Tidman-MCT-Crossover.txt) · [Participant accounts](Cultural-and-Experiential/Personal-Reports/2026-Tidman-MCT-Phase-Interview-Experiences.txt)

The 2018 Phillips randomized diet comparison reports a 0.50-point descriptive fatigue advantage and a 0.45-point daytime sleepiness advantage for the ketogenic pattern. Its recipe includes virgin coconut oil within a set of changed foods. The independent virgin-oil chemotherapy trial shows raw fatigue differences that change sign over cycles, with a baseline imbalance and no detected between-group difference at any cycle. The 0229 fatigue, 0513 daytime sleepiness and 0598 refined-oil executive-function scorecards stay open at anchor 50.
