# Pumpkin seed preparations and peanut oil outcome reports

## Pumpkin seed extract and motor tests in rotenone exposed mice

Two spring 2025 papers by Saranya and the same coauthors have the same title, group means and experimental design. They are one study for evidence counting. Both used an ethanolic seed extract, whereas the 2009 Ahmed paper used commercial oil and measured tissue outcomes. On day 28, the reported catalepsy bar times were 8.1 and 4.6 seconds in the extract groups versus 31.4 for the rotenone control. At day 14, the lower-dose group was slower on the bar than the toxin control (18.3 versus 14.9 seconds). The day-28 narrow-beam traversal times were 8.0 and 4.85 seconds versus 29.25 for the toxin control. Significance markers differ between the reports, and the caption for some tests refers to the active comparator rather than the toxin group. These results describe timed movement and induced catalepsy in five mice per arm. They do not measure clinical stride length, muscle tone or effects of toasted seed oil. A purported antecedent cited in both versions by DOI 10.1016/j.xphs.2019.12.004 actually concerns polymer drug delivery. [Quest primary report, Tables 5–8](https://www.questjournals.org/jrbm/papers/vol13-issue3/13032026.pdf) · [Parallel primary report](https://doi.org/10.35629/4494-1002876881) · [Earlier oil experiment](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Pumpkin-Peanut-Oil.md#oral-pumpkin-seed-oil-in-a-rotenone-rat-model) · [Miscited DOI](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31816297/)

## Cold pressed pumpkin seed oil in a colchicine model

Hegab and colleagues’ February 2025 article identifies the experimental oil as commercial cold-pressed pumpkin seed oil. The rats received an oil comparison with and without a colchicine challenge. The publisher preview identifies open-field and elevated-plus-maze outcomes and summarizes favorable changes under oil. A separate attention result and the precise challenge route remain unverified. The model concerns colchicine-related neurotoxicity rather than Parkinson’s. Full behavioral tables remain unavailable through the public publisher preview. [Primary publisher preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0040816625000722) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39965508/)

## Pumpkin seed oil added to tadalafil for chronic pelvic pain

Khalil and colleagues randomized 50 men with chronic pelvic pain syndrome to tadalafil with or without a pumpkin seed oil capsule for 12 weeks. Overall clinical improvement occurred in 11 of 25 receiving the combination and 12 of 25 receiving tadalafil alone. The primary abstract reports a more favorable pain-domain result with the combination, without a numerical domain estimate. The publisher-deposited PDF and landing page could not be retrieved. The report neither verifies oil processing nor addresses Parkinson’s pain. [Primary DOI](https://doi.org/10.21608/aimj.2025.446526) · [Original abstract indexed by OpenAlex](https://openalex.org/works/W4414047556)

## Intranasal peanut oil and post COVID smell outcomes

Yeap and colleagues’ January 2026 APOLLO report compared vitamin A nasal drops with matched peanut-oil nasal drops in post-COVID olfactory loss. Table 3 includes 15 oil-comparator completers with mean Sniffin’ Sticks total score change of +3.88 points and 30 vitamin A completers with +2.85. The adjusted between-arm contrast was 0.40 points (95% CI −3.27 to 4.08, p = .825). Six of 15 and 12 of 30 improved by more than 5.5 points. There was no untreated arm. This is an intranasal comparator in postviral loss, with no oral oil exposure or Parkinson’s olfactory outcome. [Local primary article, Table 3](Source-Text/PMC13016870.txt#L386) · [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1093/chemse/bjag001)

## Oral peanut oil comparator and physical function in older adults

The 2026 ENHANce paper analyzed 58 community-dwelling older adults with sarcopenia. Four arms received peanut-oil softgels as the omega-3 comparator, three with different exercise or protein interventions. The standard-care control arm showed no clear changes in the published physical-function outcomes. The paper reports gait speed and handgrip strength, which do not measure freezing episodes or dexterity, and supplies no fall-frequency outcome. Parkinson’s was among the screening exclusions. The trial cannot isolate a peanut-oil effect because no untreated but otherwise matched arm was included. [Primary paper, Methods and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12887399/) · [Registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03649698)

## Registered pumpkin oil diet and measured blood pressure in hyperlipidemia

NCT07123844 first posted August 14, 2025 after the recorded 2023 completion date. The record lists blood pressure among secondary outcomes and gives an actual enrollment of 159. The October 2026 primary paper reports that 107 adults were randomized and 89 were analyzed, including 33 in the oil group. The abstract says blood pressure was measured but does not give an arm-specific estimate. No orthostatic symptoms or Parkinson’s cohort were reported. The reported and registry cohort accounting and full blood-pressure table remain open. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07123844) · [Primary paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42955537/)

## Pumpkin oil nanoemulsion and arthritis model endpoints

The October 2026 CFA rat study included a pumpkin seed oil nanoemulsion arm alongside quercetin alone and a combined formulation. The study monitored pain behavior to determine rescue analgesia. It reports that no rat reached the rescue threshold and does not give a separate nociception comparison. The reported changes include joint pathology and biochemical markers. The oil arm used a nanoemulsion and does not establish an effect on pain after culinary oil. [Local full text, Section 2 and results](Source-Text/PMC13702007.txt) · [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedicines14102253)

## Cold-pressed label for registered pumpkin seed oil bladder pilot

NCT06944392 first posted April 25, 2025, following the recorded actual start of April 11. The one-arm Northwestern pilot enrolled 40 adults with overactive bladder, excludes neurogenic bladder, and pairs the LURN-SI with a planned Pelvic Pain and Urgency/Frequency questionnaire. It specifies one NOW Cucurbita pepo seed-oil softgel each day for 12 weeks and identifies the 1,000 mg preparation and 100-count bottle. The corresponding manufacturer product is described as cold pressed. The protocol lists actual completion September 1, 2026, with no results in the November 16 UTC retrieval. The design does not control changes against an oil-free arm and the pelvic symptom composite is broader than Parkinson pain severity. ## Peanut butter and mobility tests in older adults at fall risk

Published in February 2026. One hundred twenty older adults at fall risk were randomized to 43 g/day peanut butter or usual care for six months. The adjusted five-times sit-to-stand estimate was −1.23 s (95% CI −2.09 to −0.37), while gait speed and most other function endpoints showed no clear contrast. SARC-F includes a history-of-falls item. The publication provides no verified prospective fall-frequency comparison. Whole peanut butter, measured physical function and the non-Parkinson cohort leave refined oil and Parkinson falls unresolved. Registration ACTRN12622001291774. ## Pumpkin oil fatty-acid supplement and clinical table access

The October 2026 supplemental material for the Karli trial contains pumpkin product sample preparation and fatty-acid GC-MS laboratory reports. It contains no blood-pressure table, orthostatic symptom assessment or oil processing grade. The registered blood-pressure procedure is seated after five minutes of rest. The accessible primary abstract records that blood pressure was measured. It does not provide arm-specific values. The main clinical table and comparison with the registered participant count remain open. ## Pumpkin seed oil and weekly pressure in hypertensive rats

Adeniran and Oladapo published on March 27, 2025. The week-8 Results paragraph gives systolic pressure of 134.20 ± 1.53 mmHg in the hypertensive pumpkin-oil group and 149.40 ± 0.40 in the hypertensive control (p = .0022). The 162.17 ± 2.30 control value in the abstract is the week-5 control in the Results. It is not the concurrent week-8 comparator. The paper describes commercial oral oil and leaves its pressing grade unverified. The Methods describe the normotensive oil group inconsistently and specify different delivery details for the hypertension group. Tail-cuff pressure in animals with induced hypertension supplies vascular context and no standing, lightheadedness or Parkinson outcome.
