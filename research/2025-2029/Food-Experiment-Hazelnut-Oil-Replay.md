# Cold-pressed hazelnut oil, gastric injury and oral symptom measures

## Oral cold-pressed oil and gastric lesion area in rats

Akalin and colleagues assigned 35 male rats to five arms in a gastric injury experiment. The two oil groups received oil pressed cold from *Corylus avellana* grown in the Black Sea region. Their oral pretreatment lasted seven days before the ethanol challenge. The median ulcer area in Table 1 was 19.00 mm² with ethanol alone, 15.00 mm² with the lower oil amount and 4.00 mm² with the higher oil amount. The overall comparison had p < .001, and the paper reports Tukey p < .001 for each oil group against ethanol alone. Histology included tissue inflammation, edema, epithelial damage and bleeding. The authors assessed tissue damage and did not measure pain behavior, nociception or a clinical symptom scale. ## Hazelnuts in a Parkinson diet-adherence questionnaire

NCT07187739 records a Turkish Parkinson study of Mediterranean diet counseling after deep brain stimulation. It recommends olive oil as the main fat and includes hazelnuts in the diet-adherence questionnaire. It does not assign a hazelnut-oil product. The secondary motor outcome names chewing, swallowing and eating but cites items 2.2 and 2.4. On the original MDS-UPDRS, item 2.2 is saliva and drooling, 2.3 is chewing and swallowing, and 2.4 covers eating tasks. The registry discrepancy leaves the exact items to be verified in study results. Results were unposted at the November 2026 check. The original scale gives 2.2 saliva and drooling, 2.3 chewing and swallowing, and 2.4 eating tasks, scored 0–4. ## Hazelnut oil in a sheep tongue wetting experiment

Yapar and colleagues reported a companion formulation study with 4% hazelnut oil, 2% cinnamon leaf oil and 0.5% carboxymethyl cellulose solution. The original abstract reports greater liquid retention and lower adhesiveness in an ex vivo sheep tongue model. Literature searches reached the primary abstract and the separate 2024 formulation paper. The comparison concerns wetting by a compound gargle. It did not measure saliva secretion, drooling, swallow physiology, hypophonia or dietary oil effects. The original abstract does not specify roasting or pressing.
