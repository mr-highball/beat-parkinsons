# Avocado oil preparation and Parkinson symptom outcomes

## Avocado oil as an oral-formulation screening ingredient

The May 20, 2025 perioceutic study listed avocado, olive, blackseed, and coconut oils in the materials section. All nine reported formulations used olive oil. The paper gives no separate avocado-oil testing result. It used simulated saliva in benchtop drug release testing. There was no in vivo drooling or salivary flow evaluation. The primary table is useful for distinguishing a materials-list oil from the oil used in outcomes. [Primary article, methods and formulation table](https://www.mdpi.com/2310-2861/11/5/373) · [Licensed plain text](Source-Text/PMC12111423.txt)

A separate July 8, 2025 trial in 40 people with gingivitis compared a sesame-based multi-ingredient tooth oil with distilled water. Rinses were spat out. Between-group comparisons showed no significant difference in subjective reduced saliva or mouth dryness at eight weeks. The intervention involved local contact with a different oil product and did not measure drooling. [Primary trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12294441/) · [Licensed plain text](Source-Text/PMC12294441.txt)

## Somnolence in a mixed-oil fibromyalgia placebo group

Kurlyandchik et al. published results May 16, 2026 for 24 adults with fibromyalgia, 12 per arm. The oral placebo combined MCT, walnut and avocado oils in undisclosed proportions. Both study formulations used MCT carrier. Table 2 lists five somnolence reports in the cannabinoid arm and three in the placebo arm. Report counts can include more than one event per patient. One placebo participant withdrew after somnolence, cognitive symptoms, urticaria and pruritus. The trial measured sleep quality with the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index, a broad sleep composite. No oil-specific effect, cold-pressed designation or Parkinson daytime sleepiness measure is reported. The later plasma analysis uses the same registration and does not add an independent clinical sample. [Primary result and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13179683/) · [Licensed plain text](Source-Text/PMC13179683.txt) · [Same-cohort plasma report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42464432/)

## Avocado oil and abnormal turning in a rat lesion model

The July 24, 2025 González-Guevara report and its 2022 poster describe a 20-day dietary avocado oil comparison in rats with a unilateral quinolinic-acid lesion used as a Huntington model. The poster reports approximately 89% fewer ipsilateral turns. Body weight, food intake and biochemical parameters measured in that poster did not clearly differ. Week-specific dystonic contractions, sensorimotor clinical examination and oil processing were not available in the public reports. Turning direction and dystonia are separate endpoints. This passage extends the existing archive's relevance trace to claim 0780 and links the prior publication family. [2025 primary report](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11064-025-04496-7) · [2022 conference poster text](https://smb.org.mx/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/2022_11_24-Memorias_SBM.pdf#page=802) · [Earlier local source notes](Food-Experiment-Almond-Avocado-Oils.md#avocado-oil-diet-and-rotational-behavior)

## Avocado oil in a simulated oral digestion model

Lin and Li's 2026 avocado oil versus olive oil study simulated the oral stage with laboratory saliva before simulated gastric and intestinal stages. The oils came from the same brand and pressing process; no cold-pressed status was reported. Avocado oil's reported free fatty acid release was 69.92 ± 1.78% after the simulated digestion procedure. This measures oil breakdown and gives no information about production, retention or swallowing of saliva in people. [Primary report and oral-phase methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13476764/)

## Anxiety ratings in the avocado and walnut oil blend arm

Table 4 reports mean HADS anxiety of 9.40 at baseline and 9.60 at week 12 in the mixed-oil placebo group. The pre-post estimate was +0.20 points (95% CI −2.45 to 2.85, p=.868). The between-group estimate was −1.56 points for the cannabinoid comparison (95% CI −4.43 to 1.32). The measure is separate from screening GAD-7 and somnolence adverse events. The placebo included avocado, walnut and MCT oil. There were no isolated oil groups, verified oil processing grades or Parkinson anxiety outcomes. This estimate is relevant to the exposure trail for both oil ingredients, with uncertainty about any single ingredient's contribution. ## Whole avocado and nighttime sleep duration in HAT

2025-02-19. Damani and colleagues’ ancillary analysis of NCT03528031 found a 3.20-point avocado-versus-habitual-diet change in the nighttime-duration-based Life’s Essential 8 sleep component (95% CI 0.38 to 6.02). The complete-data sensitivity estimate was 2.93 points (95% CI −0.10 to 5.98). The Pittsburgh questionnaire was collected, but the reported LE8 component was calculated from self-reported hours, without an Epworth or a daytime item result. A separate microbiome report from the same cohort gave an inconclusive global sleep-quality comparison in its supplement. Whole fruit and nocturnal sleep duration cannot estimate daytime sleepiness after avocado oil. [Original ancillary study and Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1161/JAHA.124.039130) · [Local source text](Source-Text/2025-Damani-HAT-Sleep-Factual-Extract.txt)

## Retrospective Brazilian avocado oil trial registration

2026-08-03. ReBEC RBR-255fy6v links an adolescent oral avocado oil trial to the 2024 preliminary report through ethics identifier 61242122.3.0000.0062. The registry states a target of 45, lists eligibility of 15–18 years, and describes different capsule counts and a lower control oil amount than the publication. It says there were no serious adverse events and that mild gastrointestinal symptoms were monitored, without event counts or reported sleepiness. Its results-posting field precedes the listed registration date. Processing grade and the link between the registered and analyzed cohorts remain unresolved. ## Parkinson salivary flow and drooling in an oral case-control study

The September 19, 2026 case–control study examined 20 people with Parkinson disease and 20 controls. Its primary abstract reports lower unstimulated whole saliva flow and more frequent drooling and dry mouth in the Parkinson group. The finding helps distinguish salivary flow from clinical drooling when evaluating the earlier oil-rinsing reports. The study does not examine edible oil exposure. ## Avocado and mango report omits registered cognitive outcomes

Published online February 16, 2026. Preiss and colleagues report vascular and dietary results from the registered trial of avocado plus mango in prediabetes. The Methods say cognitive testing took place after breakfast at the prestudy visit and week 7. The paper excludes cognition and ocular data and says those will be reported separately. Supplement Tables S1–S10 have no cognition results. No separate executive estimates were located through the checked public routes. The assigned exposure is a whole-fruit dietary package. ## Registered whole avocado and cognitive performance in prediabetes

First posted August 7, 2026. Maastricht investigators plan a 34-participant crossover in prediabetes, one whole avocado daily for 12 weeks versus no avocado. Cerebral blood flow is primary. Secondary cognitive endpoints include CANTAB tests and flanker performance. The August 27 version showed not yet recruiting with no posted results as checked November 16, 2026.
