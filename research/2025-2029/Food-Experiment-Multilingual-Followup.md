# Food experiment evidence traced through Spanish-language records

## Dietary vitamins and motor severity in northeastern Mexico

Trejo-Ayala and colleagues presented WPC abstract P26.06 in 2026, reporting a 24-hour recall, MDS-UPDRS and MoCA in seven people with Parkinson’s assessed in October 2024 at Hospital Universitario Dr. José E. González in Monterrey. The abstract reports that all seven fell below the intake reference for water and vitamin E and six fell below it for vitamin D. One participant with higher caloric and vitamin D intake had MoCA 26 and total MDS-UPDRS 38. Table 1 gives group means and standard deviations for nutrients and clinical scales. The abstract provides no comparison isolating the effects of vitamin D, energy intake or a specific food. [World Parkinson Congress P26.06, published May 22, 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13360760/) · [Table 1](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC13360760.1/10.1177_1877718X261451755-img71.jpg)

The same seven-person cohort appears in the MDS 2026 abstract, whose page metadata gives September 1, 2026. Figure 1 reports Pearson correlations with MDS-UPDRS Part III of −.670 for vitamin B12 and −.739 for vitamin D, and with total MDS-UPDRS of −.559 and −.649, respectively. No vitamin intake correlation was statistically significant. The figure covers B6, B9, B12 and D. It gives no vitamin E estimate or separate rigidity score, even though the abstract has both as keywords. The reports share participants and provide no estimates for individual foods or isolated symptoms. [MDS 2026 conference abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/correlation-between-daily-vitamin-intake-and-clinical-severity-of-parkinsons-in-northeastern-mexico-an-exploratory-study/) · [Figure 1 correlation heatmap](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/0988_1474_002718_1.png)

## 2026 nutritional status and Parkinson symptoms in Buenos Aires

Charra Castellani and colleagues assessed 37 people with Parkinson disease in a cross-sectional study. The Mini Nutritional Assessment identified 37.8% at risk of malnutrition, and the abstract reports associations with dysphagia, depression, and dyskinesia without giving effect estimates for those outcomes. UPDRS Part II correlated with risk of malnutrition (Spearman ρ = .338, p = .041). The study measured nutrition status and symptoms without quantifying intake of specific foods. [MDS abstract, posted September 1, 2026](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/nutritional-status-assessment-in-patients-with-parkinsons-disease/).

## Nutritional status during Parkinson treatments in Barakaldo

Ayo Mentxakatorre and colleagues’ 2025 MDS abstract reports 55 patients treated with intestinal levodopa/carbidopa gel (25) or deep brain stimulation (30). It reports associations between nutritional status and UPDRS Part II (ρ = .504, p < .001), III (ρ = .346, p = .025), and IV (ρ = .348, p = .026). The abstract does not identify the coding behind the correlations. The team’s September 2026 abstract reports a 12-month follow-up of 63 patients treated with oral levodopa (33) or deep brain stimulation (30). Vitamin B12 was lower in the stimulation group at baseline and at 12 months (p = .044 and .024), and the authors list measured B12 and other nutritional markers as associated with motor or peripheral outcomes without giving the corresponding estimates. The oral levodopa and stimulation group sizes match the team’s 2024 pilot report. The degree of participant overlap remains unverified, and these studies provide no comparison of food intake or changes in food exposure. [2025 MDS report](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/nutrition-motor-and-non-motor-symptoms-impact-on-lifes-quality-in-parkinsons-disease-in-second-line-therapies/) · [2026 MDS report](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/nutritional-status-as-a-modifiable-determinant-of-clinical-outcomes-in-parkinsons-disease-a-12-month-longitudinal-study-in-patients-treated-with-dbs-and-l-dopa/) · [Earlier Spanish proceedings](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Multilingual-Followup.md#nutritional-assessments-with-levodopa-and-stimulation-in-barakaldo).

## 2026 diet quality and constipation in Egypt

Abed compared 130 people with Parkinson disease and 100 controls recruited in two Egyptian hospitals. Chronic constipation was reported in 48 of 130 patients and 12 of 100 controls. Within the Parkinson group, people with chronic constipation had Healthy Eating Index scores reported as 5.1 points lower than people without it. The abstract gives β = −0.51 per 10 points, 95% CI −1.02 to −0.01. Across patients and controls, mean fiber was 18 ± 4 versus 25 ± 5 g/day and added sugars were 42 ± 15 versus 28 ± 12 g/day. Those nutrient values compare disease status. They are not stratified by constipation. The author also reports greater sugar intake at higher dopamine agonist doses, without an estimate. The tables provide no individual food comparisons. [MDS abstract, posted September 1, 2026](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/diet-quality-and-nutrient-intake-in-parkinsons-disease-a-multicenter-study-in-egypt/) · [Table 1](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/0988_1474_000060_2.png) · [Table 2](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/0988_1474_000060_3.png).

## Kiwis and prunes in a Parkinson constipation care program

Hey and colleagues’ 2026 MDS abstract retrospectively describes 110 people with Parkinson disease and moderate to severe gastrointestinal symptoms treated at the University of Florida PRISM clinic. Table 1 records hydration, kiwis and prunes for all 110, a Mediterranean diet for 108, oatmeal for 18 and coffee for 12. The table gives no amounts or preparation details. Patients could use rescue laxatives and most received additional treatment, including secretagogues, prokinetics or pelvic floor physical therapy. Mean constipation scores were 7.22 at baseline, 5.72 at six months and 2.98 at one year (p < .001). Nausea was 4.78, 3.91 and 2.03 (p < .001), abdominal pain 3.27, 2.27 and 1.78 (p = .033), and diarrhea 3.36, 2.84 and 1.97 (p = .041). Table 3 presents outcomes by treatment escalation. Its subgroup counts exceed the overall sample and Table 1 contains a sex count mismatch within the escalation column. No group isolates the effect of any individual food. The experiment’s kiwi, oats, plum and coffee statements ask about outcomes outside the gastrointestinal symptom measures. [MDS abstract, posted September 1, 2026](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/a-pragmatic-phenotype-guided-algorithm-for-constipation-in-parkinsons-disease/) · [Table 1 exposures](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/0988_1474_003057_2.png) · [Table 2 outcomes](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/0988_1474_003057_3.png) · [Table 3](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/0988_1474_003057_4.png).

An April 17, 2025 paper from the same clinic describes the earlier PRISM program in 44 patients with refractory gastrointestinal symptoms. Fifteen returned for follow-up after an average of 200 days. In that subset, the mean GGSI constipation score fell from 2.908 to 0.799 (p < .001). The paper describes personalized management by several clinical specialties and gives no named food exposure. The later abstract cites this paper. The number of shared participants is unknown. [2025 original report, Table 4, DOI 10.1177/1877718X251335047](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/1877718X251335047).

The official schedule lists the 2026 study as poster 1569. The 2025 report covers August 2021 to March 2024 and records a decrease in the same 15 patients’ GGSI dysphagia score from 2.971 to 0.915 (p = .049). The published questionnaire asked about saliva swallowing, choking on food or drink, and a sensation of food or liquid sticking. It does not record named foods. The authors report median hard stools per week decreasing from 2 to 0.5, skipped bowel movements from 2 to 1, and incomplete bowel movements from 2 to 1.5. These are changes during the multidisciplinary program. The article’s license is CC BY-NC. A separate randomized trial tested a gold kiwifruit-derived powder for constipation. [Poster schedule, p. 129](https://www.mdscongress.org/Abstract-Schedule.pdf#page=129) · [2025 article, Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13347461/) · [Original GGSI supplement](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC13347461.1/sj-pdf-1-pkn-10.1177_1877718X251335047.pdf) · [Gold kiwifruit-derived powder trial](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-MDS-Diet-Followup.md#gold-kiwifruit-powder-and-constipation)

## 2025 nutritional status and motor severity in Piura

César Andrés Cruz Baca defended the Piura thesis on January 8, 2025. The repository lists January 29 availability. The methods describe a prospective cross-sectional study of 178 people with Parkinson’s seen in 2024. The study compared Mini Nutritional Assessment categories with Hoehn and Yahr stages at assessment. In Table 8, the displayed stage counts total 121. Summing the three most advanced categories gives 7 of 44 participants with normal nutritional status, 25 of 58 at risk of malnutrition and 16 of 19 with malnutrition. The reason for the discrepancy between 121 and 178 is unverified. The adjusted model’s printed row labels also conflict with the accompanying prose about the reference category. The thesis describes cross-sectional associations. Its data do not measure progression over time or the effect of changing any food. ## 2025 Cuenca review of fermented-food references

Buele Llanos and Enríquez Liger’s University of Cuenca thesis is dated September 2, 2025. Table 2 and Annex A summarize fermented foods across microbiome, metabolic and neurodegeneration studies. Its kefir leads include Alzheimer’s disease models and general gut-health sources. Its recommendations call for clinical trials in the disease populations. The review’s relevant tables give no plain kefir Parkinson’s result for falls, hand dexterity or fatigue in `0526`, `0811` or `0950`.
