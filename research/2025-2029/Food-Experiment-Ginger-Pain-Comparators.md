# Ginger Pain Comparators

## Registered ginger extract trial in sciatica

The trial schedules an eight-week ginger-root extract comparison with placebo in 80 adults with chronic sciatica. Registered primary measures include Brief Pain Inventory and the Short-Form McGill Pain Questionnaire. The official record shows actual start July 1, 2025 and recruiting status last verified December 2025. No results were posted at retrieval. The 2026 protocol explicitly links the same accession. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06817018) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/NCT06817018-Ginger-Sciatica-Registry.txt)

## Registered ginger granules and menstrual pain

The registry lists 100 planned participants, enrollment from May 13, 2025 and double-blind parallel arms. Standardized ginger extract granules or matched placebo were assigned twice daily for 90 days, with VAS and WaLIDD pain measures. The linked publication's exact pain table remains subject to its internal reporting discrepancy. ## Joint pain ginger extract registration and enrollment chronology

The registration identifies a blinded randomized comparison of standardized ginger extract and placebo for 58 days. Its primary outcomes include pressure-evoked muscle pain, function and inflammatory measures. It lists first enrollment April 6, 2023, final enrollment August 8, 2024 and completion December 16, 2024. Public registration therefore followed enrollment and study completion. The conference and journal accounts identify the same study. [Original source](https://www.isrctn.com/pdf/74292348) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/ISRCTN74292348-Ginger-Joint-Pain-Registration.txt)

## Ginger extract and joint pain during exercise testing

Broeckel and colleagues report 30 completers, 15 per arm, on supercritical carbon-dioxide-extracted and fermented ginger or placebo. The multivariate WOMAC pain, stiffness and function group-by-time test was inconclusive (p=.690), and no between-group univariate difference was clear. Selected items and within-arm tests show favorable changes. The registration was retrospective, and the conference abstract is another report of the same group. The population and extracted preparation limit transfer to grated ginger or Parkinson pain. [Original source](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/17/14/2365) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/PMID40732990-Ginger-Joint-Pain.txt)

## Abdominal pain in the existing GGE03 dyspepsia trial

Baek and colleagues randomized 40 participants per arm to steamed ginger extract GGE03 or placebo for functional dyspepsia. GSRS abdominal pain changed by −5.0 (SD 4.4) with extract versus −1.2 (3.2) with placebo (p<.001). SF-36 bodily pain changed +30.8 (36.9) versus +10.2 (27.1), p=.006, where higher scores indicate better function. The article identifies CTRI/2020/04/024448 and April 3, 2020 registration. This is the same trial as the catalog's constipation report. The intervention uses processed extract, with no Parkinson cohort. [Original source](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2025/fo/d5fo01172h) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/PMID40878144-GGE03-Abdominal-Pain-Table-2.txt)

## Published sciatica ginger extract pain protocol

The planned blinded comparison assigns supercritical carbon-dioxide ginger-root extract or placebo to 80 adults with chronic sciatica for eight weeks. It schedules pain-associated outcomes at baseline and study end. The official registry names the Brief Pain Inventory and Short-Form McGill Pain Questionnaire. Outcomes remain pending. The preparation and population differ from grated ginger and Parkinson pain. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13489092/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/PMID41831719-Ginger-Sciatica-Protocol.txt)

## Ginger granules menstrual pain and inconsistency in baseline table

The randomized study reports a standardized ginger-extract granule group of 49 and placebo of 47. Table 2 shows baseline VAS means 5.36 (SD 0.56) and 5.30 (0.58) but prints a between-group t=5.15 and p<.001, inconsistent with the displayed summary and the prose. The text describes cycle-three changes without giving the cycle-specific arm VAS values in the table. The official registry verifies design and timing but gives no corrected results. The exact pain contrast remains unresolved. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13590112/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/PMID42765050-Ginger-Granules-Menstrual-Pain.txt)

## Ginger and fennel menstrual pain and crossover inconsistency

The article reports 384 participants, all-person crossover in its Methods, and three independent groups of 128 in the reported analysis. The registry gives a 378-person planned crossover. The ginger-labeled arm uses a Neotea Thirikadum product identified with Zingiber officinale, and the article mentions hydroalcoholic extracts. Exact product contents and the analysis partition remain unresolved. Table 2 reports post-treatment fennel VAS 4.44 while the text and abstract give 4.61. A precise independently attributable ginger treatment estimate is unavailable.
