# Basil and parsley evidence

## Dried sweet basil and postpartum sleep quality

Abdi and colleagues first published January 20, 2025. The trial randomized 78 postpartum participants to dried sweet basil leaf and aerial-part powder or starch capsules. The placebo containers were scented with basil essential oil. Table 4 gives postpartum sleep quality scores of 17.63 ± 3.44 and 16.15 ± 3.20, with adjusted p = .400. One participant reported nausea. The published data do not isolate daytime sleepiness, clinical insomnia or Parkinson fatigue. The trial identifier is IRCT20220926056046N2. [Local primary text](Source-Text/PMC11795059.txt) · [Official registration](https://irct.behdasht.gov.ir/trial/68333)

## Sweet basil extract syrup and depression scores

Talaei and colleagues published a sweet basil leaf extract syrup trial on November 14, 2025. Participants with major depressive disorder and anxiety received sertraline in both groups. The paper reports a larger BDI score decrease after basil (p < .001) and describes mild gastrointestinal discomfort and headache in the treatment group. It did not separately score apathy, daytime sleepiness or fatigue. The article describes the syrup dose using inconsistent units in different paragraphs. The clinical population and concentrated extract differ from the frozen culinary claims. [Local primary text](Source-Text/PMC12617443.txt) · [Published article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12617443/)

## Sweet basil seed extract and mouse memory tasks

Palanivelu and colleagues reported spatial, working and recognition memory changes in a 2025 haloperidol-induced amnesia model. The preparation was a hydroalcoholic sweet basil seed extract. The authors report improved performance on Morris water maze, Y-maze, passive avoidance and novel object recognition measures. This is a seed-extract cognitive experiment in mice. It did not test dried culinary leaves or Parkinson executive function. [Author institution record](https://scholarprofiles.sunwayuniversity.edu.my/en/publications/basil-seeds-unlock-memory-hydroalcoholic-extract-of-ocimum-basili/) · [Primary DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.prmcm.2025.100719)

## Dried sweet basil tea and reported nighttime urination

Arutunyan’s June 2026 self-posted case series describes seven people who reported fewer nighttime voids after drinking tea made from dried culinary basil leaves. One participant with benign prostatic hyperplasia described changing from four or five to zero or one nighttime trips. The report is uncontrolled and includes the author’s self-experiment. It gives no blinded assessment or verified Parkinson cohort, and nighttime voids differ from urinary urgency episodes. [Author-posted report](https://www.academia.edu/168382437/Mitigating_Nocturia_and_Improving_Diastolic_Blood_Pressure_Homeostasis_via_Basil_Tea_A_Seven_Subject_Case_Series)

## Oral sweet basil oil and rotarod falls

Sá Filho and colleagues published September 30, 2026. They tested oral essential oil distilled from sweet basil leaves that were dried before extraction. In Figure 4, the highest tested oil group had a shorter first-fall latency and more falls on a rotating rod than vehicle (p < .05). The intermediate group showed no clear motor deficit. There were ten Swiss mice per group, and the experiment did not use a Parkinson model. Falls in a one-minute rotarod test measure coordination and balance under laboratory conditions. The intervention was concentrated oil and the experiment did not measure everyday clinical falls or postural instability.
