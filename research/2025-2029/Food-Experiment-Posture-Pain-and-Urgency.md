# Posture, pain, urinary urgency and related outcomes

## Danshen root urgency results versus saw palmetto

Kim et al. measured individual urgency in a different 25-completer pilot. The 12-week changes were −0.8 ± 0.6 versus −0.4 ± 0.9 (p = .257). Frequency and the storage composite favored the root extract. The report identifies retroactive Korean registration KCT0011824 dated April 9, 2026. The root preparation and urologic population do not inform ground common sage and Parkinson urgency directly. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13258752/) · [Local source text](Source-Text/PMC13258752.txt)

## Korean registration for the danshen and saw palmetto pilot

The study was registered retroactively on April 9, 2026. The registry includes IPSS and IIEF for a 12-week comparison of danshen extract and saw palmetto. Its initial results and registration are dated the same day, preceding the journal article. [Official CRIS detail](https://cris.nih.go.kr/cris/search/detailSearch.do/33164) · [Local text](Source-Text/Trials/KCT0011824.txt)

## Korean registry results on urinary frequency and storage symptoms

The official results summary lists 25 participants and reports favorable total IPSS, frequency, quality of life and composite storage symptoms. Storage combines frequency, urgency and nocturia. The summary gives no separate urgency contrast. The journal publication has the individual urgency comparison (p = .257). The registry links result and protocol attachments as well as the summary. [Official CRIS detail](https://cris.nih.go.kr/cris/search/detailSearch.do/33164) · [Local text](Source-Text/Trials/KCT0011824-results.txt)
