# Parkinson nutrition and swallowing in the 2025 JSPEN proceedings

The 40th meeting took place on February 14–15, 2025. J-STAGE released the supplement in January 2026. These reports are grouped with their presentation year. [Publisher issue](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/browse/ejspen/7/Supplement1/_contents/-char/ja)

## Carbonated water washout for a gastric bezoar

Kano and Kawamura reported a woman in her eighties with about 20 years of Parkinson’s, severe swallowing difficulty and nasogastric feeding. After about a month on a viscosity-changing liquid formula, pre-gastrostomy endoscopy found a white gastric bezoar. A week of lavage with commercial strongly carbonated water produced slight shrinkage on CT. After digestive enzymes were given, CT a week later and endoscopy the following week showed no bezoar. The authors mention cola and persimmon in other reports. This case used carbonated water for lavage and measured bezoar resolution. [JSPEN 2025 O-35-06, printed p578](https://web.archive.org/web/20260120180231id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/7/Supplement1/7_335/_pdf/-char/ja#page=244)

## MCT oil during pressure injury care

Kudoku and colleagues reported an 87-year-old bedridden woman with Parkinson’s, low appetite and a stage IV pressure injury. Her weight rose from 26.9 to 28.9 kg during the first month of nutritional support with enteral feeding, collagen peptides, zinc and intravenous nutrition. The team later added carbohydrate and MCT oil while changing the protein and total energy prescription. Wound healing was progressing during concurrent negative-pressure therapy. The abstract supplies no source oil, isolated MCT comparison or neurological symptom result. [JSPEN 2025 O-33-08, printed p565](https://web.archive.org/web/20260120180231id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/7/Supplement1/7_335/_pdf/-char/ja#page=231)

## Esophageal tube access and swallowing care

Tsuchida and Nakajima described 23 patients receiving percutaneous transesophageal feeding access (PTEG) when gastrostomy was unsuitable. Two had Parkinson’s. Initial technical success was 96%, all procedures ultimately succeeded and one patient removed the tube. The abstract gives no outcomes by diagnosis or food. A separate report by Okita and colleagues described a 90-year-old woman with Parkinson’s whose long-term PTEG leaked and refluxed before admission for aspiration pneumonia. Endoscopy later supported a transition to a texture-modified oral diet, and the persistent tract required closure after tube removal. Formula, oral ingredients and a food-specific swallowing comparison were unreported. [JSPEN 2025 R-07-02, printed p309](https://web.archive.org/web/20260120204845id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/7/Supplement1/7_266/_pdf/-char/ja#page=44) · [O-34-06, printed p571](https://web.archive.org/web/20260120180231id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/7/Supplement1/7_335/_pdf/-char/ja#page=237)

## Dysphagia during withdrawal of a suspected medication

Kubo and colleagues reported a 67-year-old man with schizophrenia, aspiration pneumonia and parkinsonism attributed to brexpiprazole. The suspected drug was stopped on day 27. Changes to texture, utensils and mealtime posture and nutritional supplementation began later alongside swallowing support. Functional Oral Intake Scale rose from 2 on day 21 to 7 on day 93. The report does not name an individual food or isolate the contribution of the different measures. [JSPEN 2025 R-07-04, printed p311](https://web.archive.org/web/20260120204845id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/7/Supplement1/7_266/_pdf/-char/ja#page=46)

## Progressive supranuclear palsy compared with an earlier Parkinson gastrostomy series

Ishida and colleagues compared 27 new progressive supranuclear palsy cases with their earlier 31-person Parkinson gastrostomy series. Median survival after gastrostomy was 50 months (95% CI 36–77) in the new cohort and 32 months in the earlier Parkinson report. The abstract reports no significant survival difference. The Parkinson cases are reused. No specific food or feeding comparison was reported. [JSPEN 2025 O-16-03, printed p442](https://web.archive.org/web/20260120180231id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/7/Supplement1/7_335/_pdf/-char/ja#page=108) · [Earlier analyses](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-NHO-Later.md#2023-and-2024-oral-feeding-after-aspiration-procedures)

## Blended and soft rice porridge during postoperative care

Watanabe and colleagues describe an 84-year-old man with Parkinson’s undergoing treatment for obstructing sigmoid cancer. While he also received tube feeding, oral blended rice porridge was advanced to soft rice porridge. White cell count and C-reactive protein rose mildly and improved after the team returned to blended rice porridge. The report gives no fever or oxygen saturation drop during the contrast. The authors suspected microaspiration without instrumental confirmation. The case does not report a choking event. [JSPEN 2025 P-59-01, printed p1109](https://web.archive.org/web/20260120180416id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/7/Supplement1/7_642/_pdf/-char/ja#page=468)

## Medication assistance jelly and oral feeding

Koshiba and colleagues describe an 87-year-old woman with Parkinson’s, prior stroke and aspiration pneumonia. Alertness improved after intravenous levodopa, direct swallowing training resumed and the endoscopic swallowing score went from 8 to 5. Medication assistance jelly facilitated taking tablets. The report supplies no isolated jelly result. Separately, Okuyama and colleagues recorded use of Ensure H and advancement to medium rice porridge during nutrition and swallowing care, with no isolated food result. [JSPEN 2025 P-63-01](https://web.archive.org/web/20260120180416id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/7/Supplement1/7_642/_pdf/-char/ja#page=496) · [P-50-06](https://web.archive.org/web/20260120180416id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/7/Supplement1/7_642/_pdf/-char/ja#page=406)
