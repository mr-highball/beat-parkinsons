## Diet and bowel findings trace to three different questions

The Thai proposal's score describes adherence to a whole dietary pattern. Table 1 and the supplement describe substitutions and preparation choices. The proposed effects on swallowing, choking and bowel symptoms are expectations. The cited functional-dyspepsia trial allocated 75 participants to curcumin or placebo with famotidine in both arms, found no statistically significant between-group difference in symptom severity, and did not include basil. [Thai viewpoint](../2020-2024/Source-Text/2023-PMID37334427-Thai-Mediterranean-Parkinson-Viewpoint-Food-Scopes.txt) · [Curcumin trial](../2020-2024/Source-Text/2021-PMID33861441-Curcumin-Functional-Dyspepsia.txt)

The 2002 Indo-Mediterranean trial in the viewpoint describes a pattern with cardiovascular outcomes. The Lancet published an expression of concern in 2005. Neither establishes a symptom-specific result for any of its ingredients. The December 2024 Parkinson diet review points to reference 9 for the Mischley observations. Reference 9 is a different article. Reference 8 is the 2017 cross-sectional study of 1,053 participants whose total PRO-PD score cannot resolve the mapped individual symptoms. ## Swallowing physiology and a traditional formulation

The TRP review cites a pooled reanalysis that includes the original Rofes piperine subgroup. The acute instrumented comparison uses fortified nectar and reuses the trial's piperine participants. It does not add another family or a cracked-pepper meal comparison. [TRP review](Source-Text/2025-PMID41118703-TRP-Sensory-Channels-Review-Food-Scopes.txt) · [Pooled study](../2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC9506471.txt)

The Siddha review describes a three-spice formulation with cardamom, clove and Indian bay leaf. Its sesame-oil reference concerns external use in a multicomponent stroke case. The primary treatment section names two medicinal formulations different from the review The separate rat depression study reports improvement in forced-swim performance and locomotion with cardamom oil. The locomotor change can affect the forced-swim readout. Neither study measures the effect of a frozen culinary comparison on the requested Parkinson symptoms.
