# Ginger preparations and measured anxiety outside Parkinson disease

## Oral ginger powder suspension and open-arm behavior in mice

Alzalitini, El-Kakalli and Issadiq reported a water-controlled elevated plus-maze experiment in male albino mice. Table 1 gives mean open-arm time of 56.7 ± 6.5 seconds with water, 101 ± 5.2 with diluted oral ginger suspension, 164 ± 5.7 with concentrated ginger and 179.7 ± 2.8 with diazepam. The table identifies these values as mean ± SEM and marks both ginger comparisons against water p < .001. Open-arm entries were 6.6 ± 0.4, 10 ± 0.4, 9.5 ± 1.3 and 5.6 ± 0.1 respectively. The reported comparisons address water controls and leave the difference between ginger groups and the contribution of general locomotion unverified. The Methods also differ on three versus four groups and diazepam dose. The results describe 12 mice per group. ## Ginger extract and anxiety in a blinded craniotomy comparison

Kaewborisutsakul and colleagues randomized 48 adults undergoing craniotomy to a preoperative carbohydrate drink with standardized ginger extract or a matched drink using artificial flavor. Figure 2 reports no clear anxiety visual analogue scale difference at baseline or postoperative days one and two. The design directly tests addition of extract in this perioperative setting. The study cites TCTR20220124002. The public registry page returned a shell, leaving the preregistered outcome fields unverified. [Local source text](Source-Text/PMC12532379.txt) · [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12532379/)

The original supplemental Table 1 gives VAS means of 2.00 versus 1.21 on day one (p = .420) and 1.62 versus 0.71 on day two (p = .266). The contrasts were measured per visit and no adjusted anxiety change was reported. ## Oral ginger extract and behavior after prenatal valproate exposure

Choi and colleagues first published February 3, 2026. The authors administered oral ginger extract for four weeks starting at age six weeks to mice exposed prenatally to valproate. The abstract reports improvement in anxiety-like behavior, social and memory tests. It does not give the anxiety assay estimates, exact effect sizes or extract preparation details. ## Oral gingerol extract and open-field behavior after spinal nerve ligation

After four weeks in a female rat nerve-ligation model, the higher of two gingerol-extract doses increased open-field center entries relative to vehicle (Figure 2, p < .05, n = 5–8 per group). The paper's abstract describes both doses broadly. The Results specifically identify the higher-dose comparison for center entries. This behavioral measure supplies indirect context for anxiety.
