# Strawberry leaf extract in a Parkinson-related cell model

## Concurrent leaf extract and rotenone in cultured cells

Ueda and colleagues added garden-strawberry leaf extract concurrently with rotenone to human SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cultures. The presentation reports higher viability and lower reactive oxygen species than with rotenone exposure alone, with Nrf2 nuclear translocation and related expression changes. It appeared as EPO8_118 in the Paris 2025 meeting collection published July 23, 2026. The endpoints are cellular and there were no participants, falls measurements or strawberry fruit doses. [Original Cambridge proceedings, EPO8_118](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-nutritional-science/article/special-collection-abstracts-from-iunsicn-paris-2025/C51F3C05B11BD8180F05A3C37B4292A0)

## Published mouse and cell experiments

The later original article by Ueda and colleagues includes a 28-mouse randomized experiment alongside SH-SY5Y cell work. In the rotenone mouse model, the high-dose garden-strawberry leaf extract diet prolonged rotarod latency versus the rotenone comparison on day 28 and shortened the time to turn on a pole. Wire-hang time showed a trend. The rotarod outcome measures time until a test fall and has no spontaneous or recurrent fall count. Figure 10C is labeled total pole time in the caption, while the results prose calls it distance slid. The direction judgment here uses the explicitly identified panels 10A and 10B.

The author and model overlap connects the cell poster and paper. They are retained as one research family with a separate, newly reported animal experiment. The extract is prepared from leaves, and the paper has no raw-fruit arm or clinical participants.
