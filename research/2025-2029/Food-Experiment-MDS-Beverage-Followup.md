# Coffee, tea and caffeine in the 2026 Movement Disorders congress

The October 2026 Movement Disorders supplement gives October 1 as its first publication date. The society archive reports the individual studies under their titles. The society archive record identifiers and the assigned presentation numbers from the official congress schedule are both recorded below. [Journal supplement](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/toc/15318257/2026/41/S1)

## Coffee and executive testing in the Chonnam cohort

Lee et al.’s paper was published online April 15, 2026. It enrolled 149 people with early Parkinson’s disease at Chonnam National University Hospital between January 2022 and December 2024. The October congress abstract by Choi and Lee reports the same cohort, including 115 coffee consumers and 48 smokers, with matching cognitive results. Coffee intake came from interview and was grouped as none, up to one cup daily, or more than one cup daily. After adjustment, coffee consumers performed better on Go–No–Go, with no significant difference on the other cognitive tests in the binary comparison. In post hoc analyses, the highest intake group performed better than non-consumers on Go–No–Go and better than the up-to-one-cup group on Calculation. The paper preview also reports no significant difference on motor examination scores. The accessible reports provide no group estimates, confidence intervals, brewing method, caffeine content, or separate fatigue result. Earlier Chonnam work involved recruitment in 2011–2016, before this cohort. ## Reported caffeine and nonmotor symptoms in Sousse

Rekik et al. compared 42 caffeine consumers with 20 non-consumers in a Sahloul Hospital clinic database. Total NMSS was lower among consumers (p = .001), with higher MMSE and Frontal Assessment Battery performance (p = .004 and .013). The abstract reports no significant difference in depression, REM sleep behavior disorder, or dyskinesia prevalence. Consumers had shorter latency to the levodopa ON state (p = .008) and greater response on an acute levodopa test (p = .012). More than two cups per day was associated with tremor-dominant subtype (p = .015). Motor subtype describes the relative symptom pattern. The report gives no rest-tremor severity result, individual fatigue estimate, or specific beverage or preparation. [Poster 1896](https://www.mdscongress.org/Abstract-Schedule.pdf#page=20) · [Primary congress archive record 37226](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/impact-of-caffeine-consumption-on-clinical-phenotype-and-non-motor-symptoms-in-parkinsons-disease/)

## Coffee tea and dyskinesia in the STAT-PD cohort

Clarke et al. analyzed 74 STAT-PD participants with recalled caffeine and tobacco exposure data. Table 2A compares the prevalence of reported coffee and tea use by the presence of levodopa-induced dyskinesia. Ever coffee use was 39/50 among participants with dyskinesia and 18/23 among those without it (p = .99). Ever tea use was 33/50 and 13/23 (p = .44). The comparisons for current use also gave no clear association with dyskinesia presence (coffee p = .21, tea p = .47). Mean dyskinesia severity AUC was 17.02 (SD 14.0) among current users of any caffeine source and 10.3 (SD 8.8) among non-current users (p = .03). Lifetime caffeine exposure showed little correlation with severity (r = .03, p = .86). The severity comparison combines coffee, tea and soda, and day-of-assessment caffeine intake was untracked. The table provides no separate coffee or tea severity estimate, brewing method, or black versus green tea contrast. [Primary journal supplement](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.70477) · [Poster 536](https://www.mdscongress.org/Abstract-Schedule.pdf#page=121) · [Congress archive record 36652](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/caffeine-and-tobacco-exposure-and-the-presence-of-levodopa-induced-dyskinesia-lid-in-the-stat-pd-trial/) · [Original Table 2 image](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/0988_1474_001874_2.png)

## Prediagnostic caffeine and later outcomes in two US cohorts

Zhang et al. included 689 Nurses’ Health Study participants and 755 Health Professionals Follow-up Study participants diagnosed with Parkinson’s. The analysis used self-reported total caffeine intake averaged from questionnaires up to four years before diagnosis. The abstract reports no clear caffeine association and supplies no caffeine-specific coefficient, confidence interval, or beverage-level estimate. The endpoints were mortality and subjective cognitive decline. The reported lower risk of subjective cognitive decline with multivitamin use (RR 0.65, 95% CI 0.52–0.82) applies to the multivitamin exposure. [Poster 1326](https://www.mdscongress.org/Abstract-Schedule.pdf#page=74) · [Primary congress archive record 37279](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/lifestyle-factors-as-predictors-of-progression-in-parkinsons-disease/)

## Coffee habits and sleep questionnaire in a Romanian case-control study

Murasan et al. compared 131 people with Parkinson’s with 131 age- and sex-matched controls. Fewer Parkinson’s participants reported regular coffee intake (p = .025). More reported no regular sleep routine (p = .041), and sleep medication use was 35.87% versus 9.92% (p < .001). The abstract compares diagnostic groups for each outcome. It gives no within-Parkinson’s coffee-by-sleep or coffee-by-fatigue estimate and does not state the coffee preparation. [Poster 1636](https://www.mdscongress.org/Abstract-Schedule.pdf#page=116) · [Primary congress archive record 36753](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/sleep-habits-and-lifestyle-factors-in-people-with-parkinsons-disease-compared-with-healthy-controls/)

Related primary reports from Brașov use the same 131 Parkinson’s and 131 control counts and share investigators. The January 31, 2026 article reports recruitment during 2019–2023 and the same ethics identifier as Diaconu et al.’s April 19, 2024 fatigue paper. The 2026 congress abstract gives no recruitment dates or ethics number. Cohort overlap is likely, with participant-level overlap unverified. The articles quantify insomnia, sleep quality, fatigue and other symptoms, but contain no coffee or alcohol exposure values. The 2024 MDS abstract additionally names the Hong Kong RBD questionnaire and Parkinson’s Fatigue Scale. It gives no beverage-by-symptom estimate. ## Coffee and fatigue as separate survival predictors in China

Song et al.’s January 8, 2026 article and MDS archive 35440 report the same 3,148 people with Parkinson’s from 19 Chinese Parkinson’s Disease Registry sites, with 562 deaths. Figure 2A gives adjusted hazard ratios for death of 0.99 for coffee versus no coffee (95% CI 0.58–1.69, p = .963), 0.96 for tea versus no tea (0.75–1.22, p = .718) and 1.18 for fatigue versus no fatigue (0.99–1.42, p = .067). The beverage and fatigue rows are separate predictors of death. Supplementary Table 1 lists separate adjustments for lifestyle habits and fatigue. Supplementary Table 2 gives coffee history for 70 participants (2.22%), tea history for 425 (13.50%) and fatigue for 1,276 (40.53%), with no joint counts or coffee preparation. Supplementary Figure 4 excludes 146 people whose survival status was unknown and gives death HRs of 0.99 for coffee (p = .968) and 1.18 for fatigue (p = .069). These sources supply no coffee-by-fatigue estimate for [0121](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0121.txt). The publisher preview gives the abstract, figures, supplement and an investigator-request route for additional data. [Original journal article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00415-026-13617-3) · [Journal supplement](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00415-026-13617-3/MediaObjects/415_2026_13617_MOESM1_ESM.docx) · [Original MDS figure](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/0988_1474_000087_3.jpg) · [MDS abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/survival-and-related-factors-in-patients-with-parkinsons-disease-a-multicenter-prospective-cohort-study-in-china/)

The registry protocol specifies coffee and tea habits and a Parkinson Fatigue Scale assessment. The 2022 registry design report describes baseline characteristics of the same 3,148-person group. The 2023 fatigue paper follows 2,100 people from the same research network, with recruitment dates spanning those of the mortality cohort and no published coffee-by-fatigue result. Exact participant overlap with the fatigue paper is unverified. A separate 218-person northern Chinese cohort also reports beverage intake and fatigue as separate predictors of mortality.
