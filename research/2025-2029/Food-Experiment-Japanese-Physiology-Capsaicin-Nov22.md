# Japanese physiology originals and cayenne swallowing

The November 22 checkpoint reviewed all 24 Round 1 claim–source pairs mapped to the recovered 2019 Japanese physiology proceedings. The 314-page PDF includes 1,468 identified presentation boundaries, with some legacy non-ASCII glyphs unverified in machine text. Material comparisons were checked against their printed columns. [Original source text](../2015-2019/Source-Text/2019-PMC10716970-Physiology-Original-Access.txt) · [Exact review and page inventory](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/followup/japanese-physiology-originals-nov22/README.md)

- **Contrasting capsaicin reflex findings.** In one non-Parkinson rat experiment, brief local capsaicin triggered reflex swallowing. In the other, prolonged laryngeal exposure was followed by fewer mechanically induced reflex swallows. The earlier Yoshihara proceedings and the 2020 journal report are one research line. The original journal pages G412–G419 remain under access review. [2020 journal abstract and original access note](../2020-2024/Source-Text/2020-PMID32755305-Yoshihara-Sustained-Capsaicin-Swallowing.txt)
- **Human timing and attribution.** The healthy-volunteer 2021 red-pepper sauce comparison favors some cued swallow measures after brief exposure. Its continuous exposure comparison differs. The 2026 crossover gives both conditions the same oral capsaicinoids. The evidence does not establish the effect of ground cayenne on Parkinson swallowing difficulty. Claim 0724 records four additional source families at Unknown under rubric v1.
