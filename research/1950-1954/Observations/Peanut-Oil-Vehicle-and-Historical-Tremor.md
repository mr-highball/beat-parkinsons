# Peanut oil vehicle and historical tremor

The 1946 report describes an injectable preparation containing peanut oil and wax, with variable rigidity responses and a clinical view that tremor did not respond sufficiently to justify that treatment. Its 200 patients span multiple diagnoses. The 1949 Parkinsonian series distinguishes lower amplitude from unchanged tremor occurrence and frequency. That same series informs the 1951 surgical review, so the review's clinical summary adds context to an existing comparison. Both older studies describe coformulated treatment with an injected neuromuscular blocker. Later exposure designs show why the word oil alone is insufficient. A 1997 oral oil challenge distinguishes crude and refined grades for allergy outcomes. A 2023 primate experiment used an oil carrier and scored a drug's effect on levodopa-induced dyskinesia. The evidence leaves the separate cold-pressed oil comparison for Parkinson resting tremor open.
