Publication: D. W. Rowntree, S. Nevin and A. Wilson, Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery and Psychiatry 13(1):47–62, February 1950.
DOI: 10.1136/jnnp.13.1.47. PMID 15405311, PMC498598.
Original issue scan: https://archive.org/details/sim_journal-of-neurology-neurosurgery-and-psychiatry_1950_13
Coverage: Factual extract of relevant methods on printed page 47 and psychiatric outcomes on printed page 62. Original page images were checked.

The investigators used an intramuscular drug preparation containing peanut oil as the vehicle. One patient with recurrent mania developed a new manic episode with auditory and visual hallucinations two days after stopping the active drug. The paper describes previous psychiatric illness and other symptoms. The study does not give a clinical effect attributable to the vehicle, an oral exposure, a pressing grade, or a Parkinson cohort.
