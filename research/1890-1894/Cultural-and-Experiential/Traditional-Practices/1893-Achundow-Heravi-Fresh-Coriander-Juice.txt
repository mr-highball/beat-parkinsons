Community/context: Persian materia-medica tradition as transmitted in a nineteenth-century German edition.
Exposure and reported symptom: Juice of fresh coriander. The passage does not identify leaves, seeds or another fresh plant part. Confusion and marked sleepiness are attributed to an earlier author.
Reported direction: Historical warning about adverse mental or alertness effects.
Efficacy evidence: Historical attributed warning. No verified source comparison for apathy or facial expressiveness in Parkinson disease.
Evidence and limits: The 1893 printed translation reports a warning about fresh coriander juice and attributes confusion and pathological sleepiness to Galen. The juiced plant part is unspecified. The account gives neither a patient-level comparison nor a measured Parkinson symptom effect. Sleepiness does not establish apathy or facial-expression impairment.
Authenticity: Digitized title page and source passage checked against the scan. The text's Galen attribution remains unverified independently.
Related research: The 1993 Alam encyclopedia entry relays this Heravi lineage. It is not a second independent account.
Access and follow-up: Verify the cited Persian edition and whether earlier versions specify the plant part. The separately discussed dried preparation has an uncertain alternative botanical name.

The printed source calls the plant coriander in the fresh-juice passage. It discusses dried coriander in a separate context with uncertain nomenclature.
