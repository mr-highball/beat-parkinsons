# Clinical findings and diagnosis, 1870-1874

### 1872 — Charcot’s published lectures distinguish slowness, rigidity, and tremor

Charcot’s Salpêtrière teaching, edited by Désiré-Magloire Bourneville, describes rigidity, a characteristic bent posture and hand configuration, and slowness that can persist independently of tremor or rigidity. It also differentiates shaking palsy from the tremor of disseminated sclerosis. These lectures expand earlier teaching through additional bedside descriptions.

[Charcot, Leçons sur les maladies du système nerveux, first series, lectures V–VII, scan](https://archive.org/details/leonssurlesma00char)

### 1873 — Handfield Jones presents clinically varied cases

C. Handfield Jones presented patients labeled as having paralysis agitans, with marked variation in age, symptoms, and persistence. He discussed the overlap with other neurologic presentations and noted inconsistent postmortem observations from other authors. The series predates modern diagnostic criteria and likely mixes several causes.

[Handfield Jones, British Medical Journal, part 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2293425/)
