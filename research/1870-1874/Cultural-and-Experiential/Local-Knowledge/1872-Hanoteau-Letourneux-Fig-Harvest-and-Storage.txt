Title: Fresh fig harvest and dried fig storage in the 1872 Kabylie first edition
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Historical ethnographic account
Source: https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_8jgbAAAAYAAJ/page/n447/mode/1up
Publication date: 1872
Reviewed: 2026-11-19
Date basis: Imprimerie nationale first-volume title page, printed MDCCCLXXII. The library scan title page is leaf 5.
Location inspected: Volume I, Arboriculture, printed pages 439–440, scan leaves 447–448.
Page images: https://archive.org/download/bub_gb_8jgbAAAAYAAJ/page/n447.jpg and https://archive.org/download/bub_gb_8jgbAAAAYAAJ/page/n448.jpg
Community and context: A colonial-era account by Adolphe Hanoteau and Aristide Letourneux describes seasonal diets, cultivars and preparation in Kabylie. The behavioral description is attributed to these writers and reflects their viewpoint.
Exposure: Fresh common figs during harvest. Elsewhere on the page, sun-dried figs are eaten during the rest of the year, sometimes dipped in oil. The authors describe separate drying by variety, multiple storage practices, and a pressed dried-fig cake called thiferecht.
Reported direction: The authors liken excitement during heavy fresh fig intake to intoxication. In a separate passage, the reported purpose of oil with dried figs is easier digestion. The account gives no direction for visual symptoms from either preparation.
Authenticity: The original title date and quoted-on-page context were checked on the scanned print images. The 2003 Bouchène edition retains the fresh and dried distinction and is another edition of the same source.
Evidence and limits: A historical description of behavior and preparations. It gives no clinical visual event, episode count, individual comparison or Parkinson diagnosis.
Efficacy evidence: Cultural and preparation context for future research. It does not estimate an effect on a Parkinson symptom.
Related research: Compare the 1867 Duhousset and 1875 Vilbort descriptions and the 2003 edition. Do not count literary parallels or the reprint as independent clinical evidence.
