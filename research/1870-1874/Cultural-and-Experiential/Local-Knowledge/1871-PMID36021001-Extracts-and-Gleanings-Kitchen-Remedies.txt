Title: Culinary ingredients in reprinted 1871 remedy accounts
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8893456/
PMID: 36021001
PMCID: PMC8893456
Source kind: Local knowledge
Publication date: 1871-12
Date basis: December issue of the original published miscellany
Source locator: Extracts and Gleanings, volume 1, issue 12, pages 653–692, including Remedy for Removing Biliary Calculi.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25
Reported direction: The biliary remedy contributor interpreted objects passed afterward as calculi. The remaining entries record intended uses or supportive foods.
Authenticity: Original printed publication preserved as a structured retrospective transcription.
Efficacy evidence: Reprinted anecdotes and remedy descriptions. The substances were not independently verified and there is no Parkinson comparison.

The gallbladder anecdote uses olive oil and another remedy, with attribution based on the appearance of the material reported afterward. The same issue records cinnamon oil in a combination remedy for gonorrhea, aniseed essence in a cholera mixture and barley water among fluids given during a childhood illness. These records identify intended historical uses and their original contexts. They do not supply outcomes for anxiety, rigidity or pain in Parkinson disease.

A separate 2005 clinical and laboratory report examined material passed after a different olive-oil flush and found it compositionally distinct from the patient's confirmed stones. That later case offers a plausible alternative explanation for appearance-based claims. It cannot identify the material in the historical report.
