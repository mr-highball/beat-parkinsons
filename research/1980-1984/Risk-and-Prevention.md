# Risk and Prevention for 1980–1984

### 1980 — Smoking history in matched cases and controls

Marttila and Rinne interviewed 443 people with Parkinson’s disease and 443 age- and sex-matched controls. Cases were less likely to report having smoked and were more likely to have quit. The authors explicitly considered selective mortality and differences in behavior before diagnosis. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7468155/)

### 1982 — Smoking and inverse association in a second case-control study

Godwin-Austen and colleagues reported an inverse smoking association in a separate case-control study. The investigators emphasized that established harms of tobacco outweighed any hypothetical reduction in Parkinson’s risk. Retrospective exposure measurement and premorbid behavior remain relevant to interpreting the association. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7119826/)
