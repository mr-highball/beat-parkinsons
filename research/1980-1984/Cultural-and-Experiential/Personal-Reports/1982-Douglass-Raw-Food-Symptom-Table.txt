Title: Food categories and sensory complaints in a 1982 raw-food commentary
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Published self-experiment commentary
Source: https://theancestralnutritionfoundation.org/pdfs/RS012.pdf
Publication date: 1982-07
Reviewed: 2026-11-19
Date basis: Date printed on the original journal pages.
Location inspected: Journal of IAPM, pp. 53–57, Methods on p. 55 and Table II on p. 56.
Community and context: Douglass and colleagues report two author-subjects following extensive raw-food diets and describe their observations. Table II groups numerous foods.
Exposure: The very sweet dried-fruit row groups dried figs with dates, raisins, pears and other examples. A fresh-fruit row also groups figs with other foods.
Reported direction: The grouped dried-fruit row attributes unspecified cognitive complaints, alongside other symptoms. Hallucinations are listed in separate food categories, including the melon and nut rows. The table footnote defines cognitive dysfunction using memory, attention, judgment and organization complaints.
Evidence and limits: The authors do not provide item-specific participant counts, blinded comparisons, Parkinson status or timed visual episodes after figs. Grouped rows cannot be assigned to a specific ingredient.
Authenticity: Original commentary and table inspected. The reported associations lack independent verification and clinical diagnostic detail.
Efficacy evidence: The figures and symptom groups describe author reports. They do not connect dried figs to a timed hallucination report.
Related research: The 2014 experimental study tests a fruit extract in a scopolamine maze model rather than participant-reported effects from eating figs.
Open trail: Look for food-specific original observation logs or independent accounts with identified exposures, timing and symptoms.
