# Bay leaf and potato in the 1980 BMJ index

A printed index link can be a useful route to a primary observation. The bay-leaf entry leads to Johns’s already-cataloged report on intact leaves after meals. It contributes no additional independent cases and does not establish what ground leaf does. The potato entry leads to a separate question about sprouts, with neither roasted potato nor sleep outcomes.

Historical dispensing records in the earlier archive show a parallel boundary. A record of supplied food names can document practice or enable source discovery while leaving the patient outcome and clinical effect unmeasured.
