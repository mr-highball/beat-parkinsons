Title: The on-off phenomenon in Parkinson's disease. Relation to levodopa absorption and transport
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6694694/
PMID: 6694694
DOI: 10.1056/NEJM198402233100802
Citation: Nutt JG, Woodward WR, Hammerstad JP, Carter JH, Anderson JL. New England Journal of Medicine. 1984 Feb 23. 310(8):483–488.
Coverage: Author abstract from PubMed. Local factual summary.
Reviewed: 2026-11-22 UTC

This study investigated nine people with Parkinson disease and fluctuating motor response. Under oral levodopa, meals lowered the reported peak plasma concentration by 29% and delayed absorption by 34 minutes. Stable intravenous delivery yielded a stable clinical state for at least 12 hours in the six participants assessed that way.

During constant intravenous delivery, a high-protein meal and separate oral phenylalanine, leucine or isoleucine challenges reversed the clinical benefit without lowering circulating levodopa. Separate glycine and lysine challenges did not have that effect. The experiments distinguish an effect of meals during oral dosing from a motor-response effect under stable infusion. The latter supports competition past the intestinal step without directly measuring transport into the brain.

These were whole-meal and isolated amino-acid tests in selected people with fluctuations. The abstract gives no isolated ingredient preparation, separate rest-tremor measure or ingredient-specific hours of daily OFF. Later Carter/Nutt research shares authors. The reports alone do not establish participant overlap or independence.
