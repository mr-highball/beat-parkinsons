Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Small-intestinal charcoal transit in rats depended on the compound and administration route. Table VI shows 30.9% traversed after intravenous 6-shogaol at 3.5 mg/kg versus 71.8% for its control, and 77.4% after oral 6-shogaol at 35 mg/kg versus 63.1% for its oral control. The abstract agrees with the table. The discussion's sentence on oral inhibition conflicts with the table. Intravenous 6-gingerol at 3.5 mg/kg also reduced transit in the table, while the oral 6-gingerol rows show no marked difference from their own control.

Both constituents suppressed in situ gastric contractions, with stronger suppression reported for 6-shogaol. The separate isolated-ileum experiment recorded increased spontaneous movement and tone after direct application. In Table V, selected amounts also reduced experimentally elicited mouse writhing. These comparisons measured isolated constituents and acute physiology. They provide no chronic symptom, human stool, or clinical Parkinson estimate.
