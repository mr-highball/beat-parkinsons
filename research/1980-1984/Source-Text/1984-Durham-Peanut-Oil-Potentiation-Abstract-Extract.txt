Title: The function of motor nerves innervating slow tonic skeletal muscle in hens with delayed neuropathy induced by tri-o-tolyl phosphate
Authors: H. D. Durham and D. J. Ecobichon
Journal: Canadian Journal of Physiology and Pharmacology. 1984 Oct;62(10):1268–1273.
PMID: 6509374
DOI: 10.1139/y84-212

Study and contrast
Adult White Leghorn hens received a single oral dose of peanut oil vehicle or the organophosphate used to model delayed peripheral neuropathy. Researchers measured responses in the plantaris muscle following sciatic nerve stimulation under anesthesia. The abstract does not specify the oil grade or identify an oil-free comparison.

Results
Sciatic nerve conduction velocity was unchanged by the organophosphate. Average posttetanic potentiation in treated birds was lower at the one-, two-, and four-week assessments. These between-group comparisons were inconclusive in the presence of large individual variation. In a separate two-hour periodic stimulation measure, potentiation stayed roughly stable or increased slightly among oil-vehicle birds and declined gradually in the toxicant group with substantial variability. The decline did not correlate with ataxia severity.

Scope
The study measured stimulated peripheral muscle responses in a hen neuropathy model. It does not quantify patient-experienced fatigue, Parkinson symptoms, or an effect attributable to dietary peanut oil.
