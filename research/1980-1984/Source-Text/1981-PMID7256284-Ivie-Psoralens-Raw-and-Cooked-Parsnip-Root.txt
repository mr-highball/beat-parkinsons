Title: Natural toxicants in human foods: psoralens in raw and cooked parsnip root.
PMID: 7256284
DOI: 10.1126/science.7256284
First published: 1981
Authors: Ivie GW, Holt DL, Ivey MC
Journal: Science (New York, N.Y.)
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7256284/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 5 review).

Primary Abstract:
Parsnip root contains three photoactive, mutagenic, and photocarcinogenic psoralens in a total concentration of about 40 parts per million. These chemicals are not destroyed by normal cooking procedures (boiling or microwave); thus humans are exposed to appreciable levels of psoralens through the consumption of parsnip and possibly other psoralen-containing foodstuffs. The toxicologic consequences to man of such exposure may be speculated on the basis of medicinal and laboratory studies, but epidemiologic data are not available.

Verified Round 5 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Landmark analytical food-toxicology study (Science 1981) quantifying three linear photoactive psoralens (xanthotoxin/8-MOP, bergapten/5-MOP, psoralen) in raw and cooked (boiled or microwaved) Pastinaca sativa roots at ~40 ppm total (~4 mg/100 g). Demonstrates that ordinary boiling/microwaving does not destroy root psoralens—meaning dietary exposure is orders of magnitude below pharmacological i.p. 25 mg/kg xanthotoxin doses while remaining relevant to skin phototoxicity and CYP1A2 metabolism.
