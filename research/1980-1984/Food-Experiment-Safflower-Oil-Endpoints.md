# Safflower oil and neurologic outcomes in 1980–1984

## Melancon Friedreich ataxia crossover methods

The May 1982 trial report describes two six-month periods in which 22 people with Friedreich ataxia and healthy controls crossed between lecithin and oral capsules found after the trial to contain safflower oil with nearly 90% linoleic acid. The commercial oil's processing grade is unspecified. Assessments included stationary balance, gait, speech and strength. Allocation grouped siblings and took residence and age into account. Both treatment periods supplied an oil-rich preparation, so the comparison cannot isolate safflower relative to the background diet.

[Melancon et al., methods, PMID 7049340](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0317167100043869)

## Melancon Friedreich ataxia crossover clinical results

The report analyzed 22 patients and ten healthy controls. Table XXVIII reports gait change of −29.3 cm with safflower oil and −36.2 cm with lecithin, stability −0.1% and −0.8%, respectively, and overall timed functional change −1.2% and −1.0%. The paper describes small improvements in the timed speech task during safflower periods. The authors reported no statistically significant oil-versus-lecithin difference in mean change for a specific feature, and observed practice-related gains. The speech result is a useful direction to follow for 0043, with a related disease, endpoint and a preparation of uncertain grade. Stationary equilibrium was largely unchanged. The active comparator and progressive disease make the gait trajectory unsuitable for attributing harm to safflower itself. The tables give no fall frequency, Parkinsonian bradykinesia, rigidity or swallowing test.

[Melancon et al., clinical results, PMID 7049341](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0317167100043870)

## Melancon Friedreich ataxia crossover biochemical results

The third paper used the same participants to measure serum lipoamide dehydrogenase activity and kinetics. The authors proposed linoleic acid as a candidate explanation for similar findings during both supplements. This paper adds a candidate mechanism and no independent speech, gait or balance result.

[Melancon et al., biochemical results, PMID 6896672](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0317167100043882)
