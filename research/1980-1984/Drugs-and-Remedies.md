# Drugs and Remedies for 1980–1984

### 1980 — Six-year levodopa follow-up

Shaw and colleagues followed 178 people who started levodopa in 1969–1972. Initial disability improvement diminished over time, although 47 remained better than before treatment at six years. The cohort documented wearing off, several patterns of dyskinesia, and psychiatric effects that could lead to discontinuation. The study had no randomized untreated comparison. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7465763/)

### 1981 — Bromocriptine before levodopa

Lees and Stern followed 50 previously untreated people who received bromocriptine and compared them with a historical levodopa group. Twenty-eight had sustained benefit at one year and five beyond five years. Dyskinesia and oscillations were less common with bromocriptine. Dose-limiting effects and waning control restricted use, and the sequential strategy failed to extend the duration of effective control in this comparison. [Original publication](https://jnnp.bmj.com/content/44/11/1020)

### 1982 — Infused levodopa during severe fluctuations

Quinn and colleagues described more stable motor benefit with intravenous levodopa in people with severe, irregular responses to oral medication. This brief clinical report tested a pharmacokinetic explanation for fluctuations in selected patients and offered limited information on generalizability. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6124807/)

### 1982 — Confusion and anticholinergics in hospitalized patients

De Smet and colleagues reviewed 75 hospitalized people with parkinsonism. Among those with dementia, confusion was observed more frequently during anticholinergic treatment. This nonrandomized clinical comparison raised a tolerability concern, with possible effects from illness severity and treatment selection. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7161613/)

### 1983 — Pergolide in Parkinson’s disease and PSP

Jankovic and colleagues used blinded placebo substitution after treatment adjustment in 22 people with Parkinson’s disease and three with progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP). The Parkinson’s group had better disability scores and longer on time during pergolide treatment. PSP responses were small, and light-headedness and reversible mental changes were reported. The small sample and initial treatment optimization limit the comparison. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6339985/)

### 1984 — Early controlled-release carbidopa and levodopa

Hutton and colleagues compared an early controlled-release preparation with standard carbidopa–levodopa in a blinded crossover study of 20 people. The standard formulation performed better on most measures. The authors saw improvement at higher controlled-release exposure, so the result applies specifically to the formulation tested. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6375861/)

### 1984 — Twelve-year levodopa follow-up

Curtis and colleagues revisited the earlier London treatment cohort. The observed-to-expected death ratio was higher at 12 years than at six. The authors inferred a waning survival advantage. Expected population mortality and cohort follow-up cannot isolate the treatment’s causal effect on survival. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6146763/)
