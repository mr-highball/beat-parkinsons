# Food and Nutrition for 1980–1984

### 1981 — Orange juice as a levodopa vehicle

Evans and colleagues compared gastric emptying and oral levodopa pharmacokinetics in six older patients with parkinsonism, five older controls, and six younger controls. Both older groups showed slower gastric emptying and greater levodopa exposure than the younger controls. The FDA review records that all groups received levodopa dissolved in orange juice. The study did not vary the juice or count falls, so it leaves [0259, raw orange and fall frequency](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0259.txt) unresolved. [Primary abstract, PMID 7202139](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7202139/) · [FDA review, pp. 81–82](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2003/21-485_STALEVO_BioPharmr_P2.pdf)

### 1982 — Boiled rice obstruction with severe esophageal achalasia

Yoshimura, Shoji, and Matsui reported the autopsy of a 73-year-old woman with Parkinson’s disease, hyperphagia, dysphagia, and severe esophageal achalasia. The abstract identifies boiled rice filling the esophagus and extending into the airways. The authors reported degeneration in the esophageal neural plexuses and vagal branches, along with cricopharyngeal atrophy. This case identifies the obstructing food and underlying pathology. It provides no comparative estimate of swallowing difficulty by food or preparation. The publisher makes the bilingual abstract available and restricts the full report. [Primary report, August 1, 1982](https://webview.isho.jp/journal/detail/pdf/10.11477/mf.1406204978)

### 1983 — Ascorbic acid for levodopa on–off fluctuations

Reilly and colleagues tested ascorbic acid in a blinded crossover study of six people with on–off fluctuations. Functional performance improved modestly, while the overall pattern of fluctuations, parkinsonism and dyskinesia severity, and self-ratings showed no meaningful change. The trial tested supplementation as adjunctive treatment. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6344591/)

### 1984 — Meals, amino acids, and levodopa response

Nutt and colleagues studied nine people with fluctuating motor response. Meals delayed absorption and lowered peak levels of oral levodopa. High-protein meals and selected large neutral amino acids also reduced motor benefit during intravenous levodopa while measured plasma levodopa remained stable. Other tested amino acids had no such effect. This small study addressed medication response around meals. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6694694/)
