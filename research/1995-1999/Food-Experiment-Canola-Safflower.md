# Canola and safflower oil evidence

## Canola and high-oleic safflower oil in defined formula diets

Sunvold and colleagues’ December 1995 study assigned 18 healthy men to three defined formula diets. One combined canola oil, high-oleic safflower oil and medium-chain triglycerides with a lower amount and different composition of fiber. It produced slightly firmer stools and the greatest fecal output per unit fiber intake. Multiple oils and fiber properties varied. The study did not report an isolated safflower-oil or Parkinson constipation estimate. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7491889/)

## Rapeseed and safflower oils in mouse behavior tests

The March 1996 study compared six dietary oils or mixtures over eight months after weaning. Relative to soybean oil, mice in the rapeseed group exhibited more ambulation and rearing, faster water-maze acquisition and slower habituation. Wheel activity was greater on safflower oil than on perilla oil, with their blend intermediate. These experiments concern behavior in mice without Parkinson model induction. The preparation grades and clinical outcomes are unverified. [Primary paper, Figures 1–4](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/bpb1993/19/3/19_3_400/_pdf)
