# Food and Nutrition for 1995–1999

### 1995 — Lunch and orthostatic blood pressure in older adults

Loew and colleagues compared 10 older people with Parkinson’s disease and 10 age-matched controls. After a meal, supine systolic blood pressure fell by 27 mm Hg in the Parkinson group and 8 mm Hg in controls. On a separate day, standing and head-up tilt tests found a smaller orthostatic blood pressure fall after lunch. The public abstract gives no food-specific contrast or lightheadedness rating. [Primary abstract, November 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8984127/) · [Author institution record](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:28141)

### 1996 — Rice cake and alcohol in an unresolved syncope case

Hattori and colleagues' neurological case report (PMID 8951900) describes rice cake and alcohol before sudden chest discomfort and loss of consciousness in one person with parkinsonism. The preceding assessment recorded no orthostatic hypotension, and there is no event pressure measurement or rice-only contrast. Autopsy favored striatonigral degeneration, and the cause of death remained undetermined. The publisher dates the issue November 1, 1996, while the English abstract dates the event to January 1997. The first issue date governs this entry and the chronology discrepancy remains open. [Publisher metadata and first-page preview](https://webview.isho.jp/journal/detail/pdf/10.11477/mf.1406901033) · [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8951900/)

### 1997 — Post-meal presyncope and aggregate motor scores

Chaudhuri and colleagues compared 20 people with idiopathic Parkinson’s disease and 16 age-matched controls after a standard liquid meal. The abstract reports post-meal supine hypotension, postural intolerance with presyncopal symptoms, and worsening on a combined tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, posture and gait score in the Parkinson group. The meal preparation and endpoint differ from steamed brown rice and orthostatic lightheadedness severity. [Primary abstract, November 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9399210/) · [Author institution record](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/postprandial-hypotension-and-parkinsonian-state-in-parkinsons-dis/)

### 1996 — Chicken extract and laboratory-induced mental fatigue

Nagai and colleagues tested commercial essence of chicken and a matched gelatin drink in healthy male students. After seven days on each drink, the Profile of Mood States fatigue score rose less after an arithmetic task with extract (paired t(15) = 2.576, p < .05), with no clear fatigue difference after the short-term memory task. The preparation was a concentrated drink and the outcome was short-term task-induced fatigue. This is indirect evidence for poached chicken and Parkinson’s fatigue (`0348`). [Primary report, Figure 5](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ahs/15/6/15_6_281/_pdf) · [Later crossover trial](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#chicken-essence-and-task-induced-fatigue)

### 1997 — Psyllium and constipation

Ashraf and colleagues initially evaluated 12 people with Parkinson’s disease and reported constipation. Prospective stool diaries confirmed constipation in only a subset. In the small subsequent comparison, psyllium increased stool frequency and weight while colonic transit and anorectal measures showed no clear change. The study tested isolated seed husk and contributes context for other fiber-containing foods. [Primary report, PMID 9399219](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9399219/) · [Earlier primary conference report](https://doi.org/10.1016/0016-5085%2895%2926569-4)

### 1995 — Ayurvedic Mucuna-derived HP-200

The HP-200 Study Group followed 60 people for 12 weeks in an open study of a levodopa-containing Mucuna pruriens preparation. Motor and disability scores improved from baseline. Reported adverse effects were mainly gastrointestinal. The study lacked a blinded comparator and used a specific preparation. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9395621/)

### 1996 — German case-control study of specific foods and beverages

Hellenbrand and colleagues compared food recall from 342 cases and 342 controls. Coffee and potatoes were inversely associated, and sweets, snacks, and ever eating raw meat were positively associated. Total fruit and vegetable intake showed no significant difference. The authors discussed disease-related dietary changes and recall bias. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8797456/)

### 1996 — Nutrient estimates in the same German sample

In a companion analysis of the same participants, higher estimated carbohydrates tracked case status and higher estimated niacin tracked lower odds. Fat, protein, and alpha-tocopherol were null after energy adjustment. Niacin intake could reflect coffee or alcoholic beverage consumption, and the analyses share the original cohort’s recall limitations. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8797457/)

### 1996 — Prospectively recorded diet and vitamin E in Honolulu

Morens and colleagues compared 84 incident cases and 336 controls using diet information recorded before diagnosis. Total dietary vitamin E had no significant association with Parkinson’s disease. Legumes showed a suggestive signal, with uncertainty about chance and correlated foods. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8628465/)

### 1997 — Rotterdam dietary antioxidant study

De Rijk and colleagues used cross-sectional screening and a food-frequency questionnaire. Higher dietary vitamin E was associated with lower prevalence. Other estimated antioxidants had less clear results. The cross-sectional design leaves diet changes after symptom onset and residual confounding possible. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9193212/)

### 1997 — Diet during a Parkinson’s spa regimen

The May 1997 issue of the Spanish medical hydrology society bulletin contains three short communications by María Angeles Ceballos Hernansanz on page 95. The diet note proposes a low-protein diet with most protein eaten at night during supervised spa care, citing competition between levodopa and large neutral amino acids at intestinal absorption. The adjacent communications discuss mineral waters for gastrointestinal symptoms, physical spa treatments, and possible outcome scales. The page gives no sample size, individual food, protein quantity, measured symptom score, or control group. The society’s three scan links render the same printed page, and the publisher provides no eligible reuse license for the scan. [Society issue contents, volume 12, number 2](https://hidromed.org/hm/index.php/revista-cientifica/volumen12n2-1997) · [Publisher scan, page 95](https://hidromed.org/hm/images/pdf/BSEHM%201997_12%282%2995_Ceballos-Hernansanz-MA-Dieta.pdf)

The same author’s 1992 and 1995 reports describe physical treatment and rehabilitation without a measured dietary outcome. [1992 narrative](../1990-1994/Movement-and-Rehabilitation.md#1992--hydrology-and-hydrotherapy-proposals-in-parkinsons) · [1995 communication](Movement-and-Rehabilitation.md#1995--spa-treatment-and-movement-proposals)

### 1999 — Tomato intake and Parkinson disease odds in Sweden

Fall et al. compared 113 Parkinson cases with 263 controls using a structured diet and occupation questionnaire in southeastern Sweden. The January 1999 abstract lists tomato intake among several foods or beverages associated with lower odds of diagnosis. It provides no tomato-specific effect estimate or preparation detail. The endpoint was case status, leaving cooked tomato and Parkinson symptom severity unresolved. [Primary report, PMID 9918341](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9918341/) · [Later nightshade case-control study](../2010-2014/Food-and-Nutrition.md#2013--edible-nightshades-and-parkinson-disease-odds).

### 1999 — Nicotine measurements in nightshade foods

Siegmund, Leitner, and Pfannhauser quantified nicotine in tomatoes, potatoes, eggplants, peppers, and some processed products. Fresh edible fruits had roughly 2–7 μg/kg. Tomato concentrations declined with ripening. The results underpin the two later US incidence studies' estimated exposures. They are food chemistry measurements without human symptom data and do not recover the ingredients used in the 2021 Iranian diet trial. [Primary report, PMID 10552617](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10552617/) · [Graz University of Technology publication record](https://tugraz.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/determination-of-nicotine-content-of-various-edible-in-edible-nig/) · [US prospective cohort](../2020-2024/Food-and-Nutrition.md#2020--dietary-nicotine-and-new-parkinson-diagnoses).
