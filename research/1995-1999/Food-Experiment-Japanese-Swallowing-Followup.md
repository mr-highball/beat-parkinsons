# Japanese primary swallowing evidence for the food thought experiment

## Post-meal choking and food records at Toneyama

In October 1999 Nozaki and colleagues compared 105 people with Parkinson’s disease and 47 family controls using swallowing questionnaires and a five-day food history. The author abstract reports choking after food intake in 22% and 6%, respectively, and at least one queried dysphagia symptom in 31% and 7% (p < .005 for the symptom composite). Within Parkinson’s, the group reporting dysphagia had lower reported carbohydrate intake, 186 ± 49 g versus 215 ± 52 g (p < .05), and lower mean BMI. These cross-sectional results leave the direction of the intake association unresolved. The abstract reports aggregate nutrient intake and gives no specific food or preparation for the choking reports. [Primary abstract, PMID 10655760](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10655760/)

The author’s Japanese publication list in a ministry report confirms the title and original issue, volume 39(10), pp. 1010–1014. CiNii records print holdings covering that issue, including Shinshu University’s medical library and Showa Medical University’s Nagatsuta collection. The society’s electronic backfile checked begins in 2008. Exact Japanese and English title searches yielded the abstract and subsequent references, with original methods and food tables still open.
