# Food experiment choking evidence

## Mashed potato in a Wisconsin Parkinson manometry study

Easterling and colleagues’ 1998 abstract G3083 studied 12 people with Parkinson disease, 10 age-matched controls, 10 people with multiple sclerosis and 9 people with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis. The participants ate ordinary diets and had no dysphagia. The protocol lists water at three temperatures and mashed potato at 22°C. The test boluses were 5 and 10 mL. The pressure table explicitly pools water swallows. Mean contraction amplitude in Parkinson participants was lower at all four sites (102, 131, 149 and 191 mmHg versus 116, 140, 170 and 202 in controls). Mean duration was shorter at the first site and longer at the other three. The abstract supplies no separate potato result, cooking method or choking outcome. [Primary proceedings page A747](https://www.gastrojournal.org/article/S0016508598830576/pdf#page=2) · [Abstract DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0016-5085%2898%2983059-X)

The Wisconsin team also reported on pharyngeal peristalsis in mild Parkinson disease at the October 16–18, 1997 Dysphagia Research Society meeting in Toronto. ## Autopsy of a person with long-standing young-onset parkinsonism

Miyasaka and colleagues’ May 1996 clinicopathological report describes a 78-year-old with parkinsonism beginning at age 23. The autopsy found aspirated food obstructing the trachea, with suffocation considered the likely cause of death. The authors found no Lewy bodies and discuss competing clinical diagnoses and an unusual nigral finding. The public abstract gives no ingredient or preparation. The publisher offers the full Japanese report through paid access. [Primary abstract, PMID 8672309](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8672309/) · [Japanese publisher record and date](https://webview.isho.jp/journal/detail/pdf/10.11477/mf.1406900953)

## Dry barium-coated toast in an Australian medication challenge

Hunter and colleagues’ November 1, 1997 report used videofluoroscopy in 15 people with idiopathic Parkinson’s and dysphagia before and after oral levodopa or subcutaneous apomorphine on separate days. The solid was dry toast coated in barium. The other test boluses had thin-liquid and semisolid consistencies described as equivalent to milk and jelly. After levodopa, mean oral-phase duration for toast rose from 18.24 to 24.01 seconds and mean initial-swallow duration rose from 19.99 to 27.40 seconds (both p < 0.05). The authors caution that few of their many comparisons were significant. Their definition of aspiration used persistent material in the laryngeal vestibule without specifying passage below the vocal folds. The experiment tested medication response and did not include potato. [Primary report, Methods and Tables 1–2](https://jnnp.bmj.com/content/jnnp/63/5/579.full.pdf)
