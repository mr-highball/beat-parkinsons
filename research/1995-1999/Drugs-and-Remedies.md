# Drugs and Remedies for 1995–1999

### 1995 — Botulinum toxin for painful off-period foot dystonia

Pacchetti and colleagues treated 30 people with painful foot dystonia during off periods. Pain improved in the series, including temporary complete relief in 21 people. Without a blinded control, the size and duration of treatment-specific benefit were uncertain. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7651452/)

### 1995 — UK trial reports a mortality signal with levodopa plus selegiline

The Parkinson’s Disease Research Group of the United Kingdom reported higher mortality with combination treatment than with levodopa alone in an open randomized comparison of 520 early cases. The study found no clinical benefit of combination treatment on the reported disability measures. The mortality finding required independent examination. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8555803/)

### 1996 — Open study of intravenous glutathione

Sechi and colleagues tested reduced glutathione in an uncontrolled group with untreated early Parkinson’s disease. Disability scores improved from baseline during follow-up. The small, open design left symptomatic benefit and effects on progression unconfirmed. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8938817/)

### 1997 — Ropinirole monotherapy in early Parkinson’s disease

Adler and colleagues randomized 241 people with early Parkinson’s disease in a six-month placebo-controlled trial. Motor scores improved with ropinirole, and most reported adverse effects were dopaminergic. Follow-up addressed short-term efficacy and tolerability. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9270567/)

### 1997 — Pramipexole monotherapy in early Parkinson’s disease

Shannon and colleagues randomized 335 people not receiving levodopa to pramipexole or placebo. Disability and motor scores favored pramipexole. Somnolence, hallucinations, nausea, and several other adverse events were more frequent on active treatment. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9305331/)

### 1997 — Entacapone and time with motor symptom relief

The Parkinson Study Group randomized 205 levodopa-treated people with motor fluctuations to adjunct entacapone or placebo. Home diaries showed more waking on time with entacapone over the 24-week study. The diary endpoint reflected time with symptomatic benefit. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9392574/)

### 1997 — Tolcapone in patients without motor fluctuations

Waters and colleagues studied adjunct tolcapone in a placebo-controlled trial of 298 levodopa-treated patients. Some motor and daily-activity measures improved, and diarrhea was a common nondopaminergic adverse effect. Rare severe liver injury was identified after marketing. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9305320/)

### 1998 — Tolcapone boxed warning after reports of severe liver injury

The FDA’s historical tolcapone label recorded fatal acute liver failure reports and narrowed the appropriate treatment population. The postmarketing signal shows why tolerability in a few hundred trial participants gave limited information on rare harms. [Original source](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/1998/20697lbl-s4.pdf)

### 1998 — DATATOP follow-up mortality findings

In the DATATOP cohort, the Parkinson Study Group found no association between mortality and the original selegiline, tocopherol, or combination assignments. This analysis addressed a different protocol and population from the UK combination-treatment comparison. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9506548/)

### 1999 — Five-year comparison of immediate- and controlled-release levodopa

Koller and colleagues randomized 618 levodopa-naive patients. Both formulations maintained similar overall control at five years, with no significant between-group difference in the incidence of motor fluctuations or dyskinesias. The trial tested prevention of complications in people starting levodopa. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10496260/)

### 1999 — Clozapine for treatment-associated psychosis

The Parkinson Study Group randomized patients with drug-induced psychosis to clozapine or placebo. Psychosis scores improved with clozapine without worse motor scores during the trial. One participant discontinued clozapine because of leukopenia. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10072410/)
