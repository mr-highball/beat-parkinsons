Title: Long-term dietary strawberry, spinach, or vitamin E supplementation retards the onset of age-related neuronal signal-transduction and cognitive behavioral deficits
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6792999/
PMCID: PMC6792999
PMID: 9742171
DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.18-19-08047.1998
Published: 1998-10-01
Accessed: 2026-11-19
Text coverage: Curated primary Experimental Design, Results and Figure 5

Joseph and colleagues fed aging Fischer 344 rats a control diet or a
diet supplemented with aqueous strawberry extract, spinach extract or
vitamin E for eight months. The strawberry diet contained 9.5 g/kg of
dried extract. This prevention cohort is separate from the later
short-duration intervention in older rats reported in 1999.

Striatal tissue from the strawberry group had greater oxotremorine-
enhanced dopamine release than control (p<.03). The strawberry-control
contrast for the reported striatal oxidative protection assay did not
reach significance (p>.05). In the water maze, the authors report
shorter reference-trial latency for spinach and shorter distance for
spinach and vitamin E. They do not report a strawberry-control
improvement in these comparisons. Swim speed showed no overall diet
effect and the working-memory trial showed no group difference.

No spontaneous fall count or Parkinson disease model is reported. The
tissue and maze outcomes do not isolate an effect of eating raw
strawberries on falls.
