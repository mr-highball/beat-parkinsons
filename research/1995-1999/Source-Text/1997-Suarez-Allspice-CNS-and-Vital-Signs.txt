Fresh allspice leaf extracts and rat neurobehavioral screening
Source: https://tropicalstudies.org/rbt/attachments/volumes/vol44-3A/06_Suarez_Pimenta_dioica.pdf
PMID: 9404514
Coverage: Original full Spanish paper including Table 1 and Figure 1

Suárez Urhan, Ulate Montero and Cicció used fresh Pimenta dioica leaves, voucher USJ 508060, to make aqueous and ethanolic extracts. Injections reduced rat motor and reflex responses in the acute Hippocratic screen. The authors called this central nervous system depression and noted possible peritoneal irritation. The separate 14-day oral experiment had four rats per strain and condition and measured systolic pressure, pulse and body weight. Figure 1 plots pressure, and its higher dose appears to be 240 mg/kg while Methods give 280 mg/kg. The study assessed neither depression severity nor attention after eating ground allspice fruit. PubMed lists March 1997 for the combined 1996–1997 issue.
