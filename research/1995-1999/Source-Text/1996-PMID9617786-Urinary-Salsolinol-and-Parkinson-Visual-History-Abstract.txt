Title: Salsolinol, catecholamine metabolites, and visual hallucinations in L-dopa treated patients with Parkinson's disease
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9617786/
PMID: 9617786
DOI: 10.1007/BF01276418
Publication date: 1996-04, publisher issue date.
Publisher: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01276418
Text coverage: Paraphrased original abstract and verified publisher reference list. Main methods and result tables remain open.

The study measures urinary salsolinol in people with Parkinson disease and controls. In the levodopa-treated Parkinson groups, the mean urinary value in the group with a visual hallucination history was reported as almost three times that in the group without hallucinations. Mean daily levodopa doses were described as nearly identical. The cerebrospinal fluid comparison separately concerns a ratio of dopamine metabolites and other clinical markers.

The abstract does not provide absolute urinary values, meal data, a repeated episode count, or a randomized exposure. Similar average medication dose does not resolve diet, other exposures, or temporal changes. The publication explicitly references the 1989 dried-banana and urinary enantiomer study (PMID 2754429). This connection motivates an exposure-ascertainment check. It does not establish whether any food caused the visual symptoms.
