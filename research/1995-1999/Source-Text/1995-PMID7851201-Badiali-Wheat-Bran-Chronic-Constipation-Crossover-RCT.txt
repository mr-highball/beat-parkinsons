Title: Effect of wheat bran in treatment of chronic nonorganic constipation. A double-blind controlled trial.
PMID: 7851201
DOI: 10.1007/BF02065421
First published: 1995
Authors: Badiali D, Corazziari E, Habib FI, Tomei E, Bausano G, Magrini P
Journal: Digestive diseases and sciences
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7851201/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 7 review).

Primary Abstract:
After a two-week basal period, 24 patients were randomly allocated to receive, with a crossover double-blind design, for two consecutive four-week periods, bran (20 g/24 hr) or placebo. The daily intake of water and dietary fibers was standardized. Symptomatology, oroanal transit time, bowel frequency, and stool weight were assessed in basal conditions and at week 4 and 8 of the treatment. Oroanal transit time decreased and bowel frequency and stool weight increased significantly during both bran and placebo administration in comparison with basal period. Bran treatment was more effective than placebo in improving bowel frequency and oroanal transit. During bran treatment oroanal transit time became normal only in patients with slow colonic transit and not in those with slow rectal transit. Neither the occurrence nor the severity of the most frequent accompanying symptoms of chronic constipation differed significantly between placebo and bran treatments.

Verified Round 7 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal: Double-blind randomized crossover RCT in 24 adults with chronic nonorganic constipation receiving 20 g/day isolated wheat bran powder vs placebo for two 4-week periods (the sole wheat bran RCT eligible for Van Der Schoot 2022 / BDA 2025). Wheat bran improved bowel frequency and oroanal transit vs placebo only in slow colonic transit (not slow rectal/outlet transit), did NOT differ from placebo in occurrence or severity of accompanying constipation symptoms, and caused 1 withdrawal for unbearable abdominal pain (per PMC9535527). Isolated raw wheat bran powder (exposure_fit=remote), non-PD cohort.
