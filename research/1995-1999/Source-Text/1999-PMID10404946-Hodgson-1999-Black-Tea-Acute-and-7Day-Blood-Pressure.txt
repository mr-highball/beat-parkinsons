Title: Effects on blood pressure of drinking green and black tea.
PMID: 10404946
DOI: 10.1097/00004872-199917040-00002
First published: 1999
Authors: Hodgson JM, Puddey IB, Burke V, Beilin LJ, Jordan N
Journal: Journal of hypertension
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10404946/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Two controlled crossover trials in men: (1) acute Latin-square study (n=20 normotensive men) found brewed black tea (4-cup equivalent) raised 30-min BP more than caffeine-matched water (+10.7 mmHg SBP [95% CI 4.0 to 17.4], +5.1 mmHg DBP [1.8 to 8.4]), returning to nonsignificance by 60 min; (2) 7-day 3-period crossover (n=13 men with high-normal/mildly elevated SBP) consuming 5 cups/d found no significant 24-h ambulatory BP difference for black tea vs caffeine (+0.7 mmHg SBP [-2.6 to 4.0], -0.7 mmHg DBP [-2.9 to 1.5]). Limits: non-PD men; systemic BP proxy without orthostatic lightheadedness symptoms.
