Title: Human psychopharmacology of hoasca, a plant hallucinogen used in ritual context in Brazil

The 1993 research program evaluated 15 long-term members of a Brazilian religious group and 15 matched people with no past exposure. Both groups completed psychiatric, personality and neuropsychological assessments. Qualitative accounts of altered perception and life history were obtained in the exposed group. Reported differences in prior or later functioning are observational and are affected by group selection, ceremonial context and differences in the instruments administered.

The research was part of the same program as the 1999 pharmacokinetic report, and the member-group size is the same. Until precise subject membership is established, do not sum these reports as independent cohorts. There is no diagnosed Parkinson sample, dried fig exposure or clinical visual episode frequency outcome.
