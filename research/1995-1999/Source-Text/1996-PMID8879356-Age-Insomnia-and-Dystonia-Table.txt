The authors compared age at adverse events in 165 charts. Methods and Table I give 77 patients aged 41–64 and 88 aged at least 65. The main comparison concerns recorded complications and medication use. There is no dietary assignment or botanical sage exposure. Sage in the byline is an investigator.

The printed insomnia row reads 22/38 (57.9%) and 59/72 (81.9%). The abstract reports 57.9% and 18.1%, and the main narrative says insomnia was more common in the younger group. The difference is visible on the original page and is not attributable to the PDF text extraction. A complement of 59/72 is 13/72, or 18.1%, but the source does not establish that a complement is the correct interpretation. Dystonia is printed as 51/62 (82.3%) and 50/102 (49.0%), with an older-group denominator exceeding the 88 stated in the methods. Table III also prints 41–46 where the methods and other tables give 41–64. Keep the printed inconsistencies visible and leave the affected estimates unresolved unless primary data or a correction becomes available.
