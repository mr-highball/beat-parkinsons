Title: Use of apomorphine in parkinsonian patients with neuropsychiatric complications to oral treatment
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18591063/
PMID: 18591063
DOI: 10.1016/S1353-8020(97)00009-6
Publication date: 1997-04
Source depth: complete original article text and tables on the public manuscript display; locally distilled

An uncontrolled report describes 12 people with idiopathic Parkinson disease and neuropsychiatric complications on oral treatment. Apomorphine was delivered by subcutaneous injection or infusion, with changes to oral medication. Table 1 lists eight with visual hallucinations at baseline. The later entries for seven do not list a continuing visual hallucination, while one records reduced but persistent hallucinations and effects of medication changes. One participant with baseline complex hallucinations discontinued because of falls. The report includes general confusion, nightmares, delusions and other symptoms as separate outcomes. It does not establish a standardized visual episode rate, an effect isolated from the other medicines, or a dried fig exposure.
