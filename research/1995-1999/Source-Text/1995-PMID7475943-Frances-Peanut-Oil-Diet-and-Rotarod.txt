Title: Effects of dietary alpha-linolenic acid deficiency on neuromuscular and cognitive functions in mice
PMID: 7475943
DOI: 10.1016/0024-3205(95)02180-Q
Publication year: 1995
Citation: Francès, Monier and Bourre. Life Sciences 57(21), 1935–1947.
Source: https://www.bourre.fr/pdf/publications_scientifiques/210.pdf
Text coverage: Full primary paper, diet methods and Tables II–IV.

The study compares offspring maintained on a low-alpha-linolenic acid peanut-oil diet with offspring receiving a peanut-plus-rapeseed-oil diet. Both diets contain peanut oil. The low rod result favored longer retention in the deficient group at 669.2 ± 134.3 seconds versus 74.6 ± 21.4 seconds in controls, mean ± SEM, p<.0002. The high rod result was 1,294.3 ± 162.6 versus 1,187.5 ± 169.8 seconds with no significant contrast. Traction was 100% in both groups. The authors distinguish the physical coordination assay from motivated behavior on the low apparatus and describe weaker learning elsewhere in the deficient group. This bundled dietary contrast in mice without a Parkinson model does not measure resting tremor.
