Title: Effects of a sour bolus on oropharyngeal swallowing measures in patients with neurogenic dysphagia.
PMID: 7674647
DOI: 10.1044/jshr.3803.556
First published: 1995
Authors: Logemann JA, Pauloski BR, Colangelo L, Lazarus C, Fujiu M, Kahrilas PJ
Journal: Journal of speech and hearing research
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7674647/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 5 review).

Primary Abstract:
This study examines the effects of a sour bolus (50% lemon juice, 50% barium liquid) on pharyngeal swallow measures in two groups of patients with neurogenic dysphagia. Group 1 consisted of 19 patients who had suffered at least one stroke. Group 2 consisted of 8 patients with dysphagia related to other neurogenic etiologies. All patients were selected because they exhibited delays in the onset of the oral swallow and delays in triggering the pharyngeal swallow on boluses of 1 ml and 3 ml liquid barium during videofluoroscopy. Results showed significant improvement in oral onset of the swallow in both groups of patients and a significant reduction in pharyngeal swallow delay in Group 1 patients and in frequency of aspiration in Group 2 patients with the sour as compared to the non-sour boluses. Other selected swallow measures in both subject groups also improved with the sour bolus. Volume effects were present but not as consistently as in prior studies. Implications for swallow therapy are discussed.

Verified Round 5 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Videofluoroscopic crossover study (J Speech Hear Res 1995;38(3):556-563) in 27 patients with neurogenic dysphagia and delayed swallow onset (Group 1: 19 stroke; Group 2: 8 other neurogenic etiologies) comparing a 50% lemon juice / 50% liquid barium sour bolus vs non-sour barium (1 mL and 3 mL). Sour lemon bolus significantly improved oral swallow onset in BOTH groups, shortened pharyngeal swallow delay in Group 1 (stroke), and reduced aspiration frequency in Group 2 (non-stroke neurogenic). Direct human instrumental corroboration of lemon juice sour bolus on oropharyngeal swallowing, outside a dedicated PD cohort.
