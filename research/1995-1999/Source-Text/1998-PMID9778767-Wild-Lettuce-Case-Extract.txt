Title: The case of the salad shooters: intravenous injection of wild lettuce extract
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9778767/
PMID: 9778767
First public article date: 1998-10
Accessed: 2026-11-17
Text coverage: Curated extract of authors' full original pages 290–291

Mullins and Horowitz describe three acute illnesses after
intravenous exposure to an extract labeled wild lettuce.
Species identity was not confirmed, all three had a concurrent
analgesic exposure, and one also injected a valerian product.
Reported signs include fever, pain, back spasms and leukocytosis.
The authors discuss alternative explanations involving
contamination and uncertain botanical identity.

The article's reference to hand and foot tremor belongs to
a separate orally administered valerian overdose cited in the
discussion. It is not a finding in the three wild-lettuce
cases. No case involves raw commercial romaine or a measured
rest-tremor outcome in Parkinson disease.
