Title: Dietary fish oil affects monoaminergic neurotransmission and behavior in rats
PMID: 9868201
DOI: 10.1093/jn/128.12.2512
Publication date: 1998-12
Citation: Chalon and colleagues. Journal of Nutrition 128(12), 2512–2519.
Source: https://jn.nutrition.org/article/S0022-3166%2823%2902251-4/fulltext
Text coverage: Publisher article, dietary composition, behavior results and discussion.

The reference diet's oils were 60.5% African peanut oil and 39.5% rapeseed oil. The other diet used equal parts salmon and palm oil. Rats receiving the fish-oil blend had lower measured general locomotion. The authors could not distinguish slower movement from faster habituation to the apparatus. Cortical and striatal dopamine-related findings differed by region. This is a bundled lipid-substitution experiment with no separate peanut-oil treatment or resting-tremor assessment.
