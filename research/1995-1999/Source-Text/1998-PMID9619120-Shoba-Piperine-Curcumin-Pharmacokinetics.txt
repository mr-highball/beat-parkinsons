Title: Influence of piperine on the pharmacokinetics of curcumin in animals and human volunteers
Source: https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/pdf/10.1055/s-2006-957450.pdf
PMID: 9619120
DOI: 10.1055/s-2006-957450
Publication date: 1998-05
Date basis: Planta Medica journal issue, volume 64, pages 353–356.
Checked: 2026-11-24
Review depth: Original publisher PDF, human methods, results and Table 2.

This randomized two-period crossover enrolled ten healthy men, with two nonmedical withdrawals and eight participants included in the reported calculations. The human methods compare 2 g isolated curcumin with the same amount plus 20 mg isolated piperine in capsules. The Table 2 header has per-kilogram notation that conflicts with the human methods and abstract. Use the original human methods for the administered amounts.

Reported mean curcumin concentration-time area was 0.004 versus 0.08 microgram-hours per milliliter. The ratio is about 20-fold, or 2000% of the curcumin-alone result, and the unenhanced concentrations were often below detection. The article also reports a separate animal experiment.

This source measures pharmacokinetics. It tests neither cracked black pepper nor culinary turmeric powder and reports no clinical pain or Parkinson outcomes. The comparison supplies constituent context for claims 0312 and 0812, with no transferable symptom direction. It is the primary reference behind the piperine statement in the 2008 Alzheimer overview, PMID 19966973.
