Title: Solid-state fermentation of agricultural wastes into food through pleurotus cultivation
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7626144/
PMID: 7626144
DOI: 10.1007/BF02788041
Publication date: 1995-01
Citation: Jwanny EW, Rashad MM, Abdu HM. Applied Biochemistry and Biotechnology 50(1), 71–78.
Coverage: Original author abstract. The complete methods and composition tables remain open.

The researchers compared date-industry waste and mango-industry waste as substrates for cultivating oyster mushrooms, Pleurotus ostreatus NRRL-0366. A separate set of comparisons combined date waste with rice straw or rice straw and corncobs. The abstract reports the best yield percentages as 11.96% for the date mixture and 10.18% for the mango mixture, each at a 1:1 residue-to-straw ratio. The measurements concern mushroom yield, dry weight, nutrients and elements. Date and mango waste were growth materials. The study did not report human consumption or a symptom outcome.

The publisher's issue and PubMed date the article to January 1995. Some later references use 1994. The 2002 polysaccharide study and the 1996 rat diet study used separate analytical or exposure designs.
