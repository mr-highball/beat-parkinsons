Title: The effects of black tea and other beverages on aspects of cognition and psychomotor performance.
PMID: 9784078
DOI: 10.1007/s002130050709
First published: 1998
Authors: Hindmarch I, Quinlan PT, Moore KL, Parkin C
Journal: Psychopharmacology
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9784078/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Five-way randomized crossover trial in n=19 healthy volunteers ingesting 400 mL brewed black tea, coffee, caffeinated water (100 mg caffeine), decaffeinated tea, or plain water three times daily (09:00, 14:00, 19:00). Brewed black tea acutely improved critical flicker fusion (CFF) within 10 min vs water and, together with caffeinated drinks, reduced subjective sedation (LARS) and prevented diurnal declines in alertness and cognitive processing across the day; short-term memory did not differ. Limits: healthy non-PD adults; acute 1-day test.
