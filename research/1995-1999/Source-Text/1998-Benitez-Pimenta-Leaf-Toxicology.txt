Benítez López A, Tillán Capó J, Cabrera González Y. Actividad analgésica y antipirética de un extracto fluido de Pimenta dioica L. y evaluación de su toxicidad aguda oral. Rev Cubana Farm. 1998;32(3):198–203. Original article identifier SciELO S0034-75151998000300009. https://www.imbiomed.com.mx/articulo.php?id=20401

Indexed original Spanish text available at https://www.studocu.com/pe/document/universidad-maria-auxiliadora/farmacologia/actividad-analgesica-y-antipiretica-de-u/119096151 . It names herbarium specimen 4609, leaves harvested 1995, 70% alcohol extraction solvent, final 50.19% alcohol and 16.63% solids. The oral acute toxicity experiment involved 60 male Swiss mice and a 50.19% alcohol vehicle. The Results narrative reports sedation, rapid breathing, motor incoordination and deaths in the acute oral toxicity experiment; it does not specify the dose, number or extract-versus-vehicle distribution for each behavioral sign. The authors consider much of toxicity potentially attributable to alcohol and report differences in LD50 between extract and vehicle. The indexed transcription truncates numerical tables; do not reconstruct per-group signs or dose-response. Leaf extract is distinct from ground dried fruit, and an acute mouse toxicity screen does not test handwriting or Parkinson disease.
