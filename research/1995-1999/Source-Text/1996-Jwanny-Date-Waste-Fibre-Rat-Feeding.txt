Title: Biological evaluation of date-waste dietary fibre and Endomycopsis fibuligera protein with rats
Source: https://research.asu.edu.eg/handle/123456789/191417
DOI: 10.1016/0960-8524(96)00024-7
Publication year: 1996
Citation: Jwanny EW, Rashad MM, Moharib SA, El-Beih NM. Bioresource Technology 56(2–3), 201–205.
Publisher: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0960852496000247
Coverage: Original author abstract in the Ain Shams University repository. Publisher full-text access and outcome tables remain open.

The eight-week rat comparison used a high-carbohydrate control diet and two diets containing 100 g/kg of fibre from date processing waste. The two fibre groups received untreated material or material previously used to grow Endomycopsis fibuligera. The investigators measured intake, growth, feed and protein efficiencies, apparent digestibility and tissue biochemistry. The abstract describes initially favorable nutrition indices in all three groups, followed by lower indices with continued feeding in the two fibre groups. It also reports lower serum glucose and liver and muscle glycogen in those groups.

The abstract provides no group size, stool frequency, pain response or neurological endpoint. The processed fibre comparison does not establish an effect of whole dried dates. This diet experiment studied a separate fungal species and exposure from the authors' oyster mushroom cultivation program.
