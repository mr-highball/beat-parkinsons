Title: Effects of Panel Experience on Olfactory Memory Performance: Influence of Stimuli Familiarity and Labeling Ability of Subjects
Source: https://doi.org/10.1093/chemse/21.6.699
Open primary article copy: https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=88c418b62354ffd41d7f1e67cd7e9ce35e472b54&repid=rep1&type=pdf
PMID: 8985598
Publication: December 1996. Chemical Senses 21, 699–709.
Text coverage: Original factual extraction from Methods, Table 1, and Results.

Fifty people took part, including 17 with extensive descriptive panel experience and 33 without it. Sixteen items were encoded, then presented among 32 items in a recognition and written description session one week later. Tarragon was supplied as essential oil. The article reports greater tarragon familiarity and consistent labeling among the inexperienced panel, an exception to the trained panel's usual label consistency advantage. It also describes occasional tarragon-anethole confusions. Reference to d-prime detectability concerns recognition after prior presentation, rather than a psychophysical concentration detection threshold.
