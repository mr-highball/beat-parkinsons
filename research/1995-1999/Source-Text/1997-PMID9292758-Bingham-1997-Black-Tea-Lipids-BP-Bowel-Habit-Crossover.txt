Title: Effect of black tea drinking on blood lipids, blood pressure and aspects of bowel habit.
PMID: 9292758
DOI: 10.1079/bjn19970117
First published: 1997
Authors: Bingham SA, Vorster H, Jerling JC, Magee E, Mulligan A, Runswick SA, et al.
Journal: The British journal of nutrition
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9292758/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Randomized 4-week crossover trial in n=65 healthy adults (31 men, 34 women) comparing 6 mugs/day of brewed black tea vs caffeine-, milk-, and sugar-matched water placebo, with adherence verified via urinary PABA recovery. Blood lipids and blood pressure did not differ significantly, whereas stool consistency was significantly softened during the black tea period vs placebo (p=0.04), persisting after excluding 15 non-compliers; other bowel-habit measures were unchanged. Limits: healthy non-PD adults without chronic constipation; stool consistency rather than validated PD constipation scale.
