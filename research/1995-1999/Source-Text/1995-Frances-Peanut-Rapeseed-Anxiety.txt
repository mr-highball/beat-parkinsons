Peanut and rapeseed oil diets in developmental mouse anxiety assays
Source: https://www.bourre.fr/pdf/publications_scientifiques/210.pdf
PMID: 7475943
DOI: 10.1016/0024-3205(95)02180-q
Coverage: complete original journal PDF in author archive; Table I, anxiety assays and Table III

Francès and colleagues reported two purified mouse diets in 1995. Both contained 60 g/kg of oil. The alpha-linolenic-acid-deficient diet used 60 g/kg peanut oil. The comparison used 23.6 g/kg peanut and 36.4 g/kg rapeseed oil. The diets differed in alpha-linolenic acid, and exposure began before mating and continued in offspring. The three anxiety-like tests gave no significant dietary contrast. The lower rotarod showed longer retention in deficient animals, while elevated rotarod, traction and swimming did not separate the diets. The authors discussed learning, motivation or sensory demands as possible explanations. Grade and human clinical symptoms were unmeasured.
