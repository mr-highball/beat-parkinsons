Title: Strawberry, spinach and blueberry extract in aging rat motor and cognitive tests
PMID: 10479711
PMCID: PMC6782471
DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.19-18-08114.1999
Authors: Joseph et al.
First published: 1999-09-15
Source: https://www.jneurosci.org/content/jneuro/19/18/8114.full.pdf
Coverage: primary results and discussion, including Figure 3 motor comparisons. Local factual extract.

Aged Fischer 344 rats received diets with dried aqueous strawberry, spinach or blueberry extract, or a control diet, for eight weeks. The authors reported some cognitive and striatal signaling differences in supplemented groups, including changes in the strawberry group. The published motor findings attribute significant reversal on rod walking and accelerod to the blueberry group. Strawberry did not reproduce the blueberry motor findings. The combined opening summary can sound as though every extract improved all tests. The separate-group motor results resolve that ambiguity.

These motor-performance measurements concern aging rats and an extract. Neither raw strawberry eating in Parkinson disease nor spontaneous falls in people was studied. NCT03162913 cites this paper as background, so it does not stand in for the registered human gait comparison.
