# Leafy vegetable and grain evidence, 1995–1999

## Supine and orthostatic blood pressure after a Parkinson meal

Loew and colleagues compared ten older people with Parkinson’s and ten age-matched controls. After lunch, supine systolic pressure fell by 27 mmHg in the Parkinson group and 8 mmHg in controls. Active standing and passive tilt were tested separately before and after lunch. The orthostatic pressure drop was smaller after the meal. The directions therefore depended on the measurement condition. The accessible author abstract does not identify barley or give a lightheadedness score. The comparison informs the measurement question in claim 0515. [Primary author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8984127/)

## Spinach extract and balance tasks in aged rats

Joseph and colleagues assigned aged Fischer rats to chow with dried aqueous spinach, blueberry, or strawberry extract, or to control chow. In Figure 3, the blueberry group had longer latency to fall in the stationary rod task than the spinach and control groups. In the rotarod, blueberry also outperformed spinach. The paper does not report a spinach advantage over control on either balance measure. Its broad abstract wording groups the supplements, while the pairwise balance tests separate them. The spinach exposure was a dried extract and the model was aging. [Primary report, Fig. 3 and Results](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/jneuro/19/18/8114.full.pdf)
