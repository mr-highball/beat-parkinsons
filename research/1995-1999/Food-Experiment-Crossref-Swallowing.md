# Swallowing sources from Crossref citations, 1995–1999

## Food texture avoidance in the Michigan dry-mouth study

Loesche and colleagues’ 1995 cross-sectional study included 529 adults in four geriatric settings. Table 2 compares respondents with and without morning dry mouth. Avoidance of crunchy foods was reported by 17.7% and 9.4%, respectively (odds ratio 2.1, 95% CI 1.2–3.5). Sticky-food avoidance was 8.0% and 2.9% (odds ratio 2.9, 95% CI 1.2–6.8). Carrot and peanut butter were examples used to explain the categories. The same table compares difficulty starting a swallow by dry-mouth status. It does not link the individual food examples to measured swallow outcomes or identify a Parkinson subgroup. [Primary full report, Table 2](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/2027.42/111175/1/j.1532-5415.1995.tb05815.x.pdf#page=4)

## Oral and pharyngoesophageal imaging in the Leopold and Kagel reports

Leopold and Kagel's January 1996 report grouped 72 people with idiopathic Parkinson disease into 38 mild or moderate and 34 advanced cases. The primary abstract reports dysphagia symptoms in 82% and impaired mastication or oral lingual preparation in 48 of 71 imaged participants. Their January 1997 report describes 71 participants (38 mild or moderate and 33 advanced), of whom two had normal pharyngoesophageal bolus transport. Aspiration and pharyngeal residue were among the reported abnormalities. A February 1997 report from the same investigators also describes laryngeal videofluoroscopy in 71. These reports may share participants. The available abstracts and publication records do not establish participant-level linkage, identify the test food preparations or provide by-food aspiration counts. The Springer PDF links for the first two reports lead to subscription pages. [1996 primary report abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00385794) · [1997 primary report abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8997827/) · [February 1997 companion record](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.48.2.373)

## Choking in a psychiatric ward series with two Parkinson cases

Fioritti and colleagues' June 1997 report records 31 choking episodes in 18 psychiatric patients. Its episode definition required sudden airway obstruction during food or drink intake, with expulsion of the obstructing material needed for recovery. One patient died during the episode and another died after probable aspiration pneumonia. The report identifies Parkinson disease as a comorbidity in two patients. It does not connect an individual food, fatal outcome or food preparation to either Parkinson case. The series provides no comparator for baked potato or other specific foods. [Primary report, pp. 516–517](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=787d43fdbc2b157ffa31c4d1447375889beb2070&repid=rep1&type=pdf) · [Publisher record](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/070674379704200509)
