# Oregano-like odor in Parkinson’s smell testing

## Pizza odor in the University of Pennsylvania Smell Identification Test

Hawkes, Shephard and Daniel compared smell identification in 96 people with Parkinson’s and 96 controls, with 70 matched participants in each arm for individual odor comparisons. A pizza-labeled odor, described in their discussion as oregano-like, gave 76% sensitivity and 90% specificity by itself in this sample. The pizza and wintergreen pair gave 90% sensitivity and 86% specificity. The materials were standardized diagnostic odors. The experiment did not test change after consuming oregano. [May 1997 primary paper, Figures 4–5](https://jnnp.bmj.com/content/jnnp/62/5/436.full.pdf)

## Cinnamon and other odor cues in the 1997 UPSIT comparison

Hawkes, Shephard and Daniel published this Parkinson and control comparison in May 1997. Figure 4 labels cinnamon, clove, onion and grape among the diagnostic UPSIT stimuli. The text reports that 71 of 96 Parkinson participants scored below age-matched identification norms. A matched subset of 70 Parkinson participants and 70 controls contributed to individual odor comparisons. The odors were presented for identification. Eating these ingredients and post-exposure rigidity were outside the comparison. This is the same study as the pizza and wintergreen entry above.
