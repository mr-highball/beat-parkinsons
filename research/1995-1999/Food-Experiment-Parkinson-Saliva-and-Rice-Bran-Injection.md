# Parkinson saliva measurements and a suspected rice-bran oil injection

## Unstimulated flow in Parkinson disease

Bagheri H, Damase-Michel C, Lapeyre-Mestre M, et al. A study of salivary secretion in Parkinson's disease. Clin Neuropharmacol. 1999;22(4):213–215. PMID 10442250. The original abstract compares 83 patients (8 untreated and 75 receiving levodopa and a dopamine agonist) with 65 age- and sex-matched controls. It reports reduced unstimulated secretion in Parkinson disease and discusses swallowing difficulty as a possible explanation for reported hypersialorrhea. It did not test a food or a short-term eating stimulus. This is endpoint context when assessing mustard and pomegranate claims. [Primary author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10442250/)

## Suspected oil identity after cosmetic injection

Kiyokawa and colleagues, Internal Medicine. 1995;34(5):380–383. DOI `10.2169/internalmedicine.34.380`. A woman developed acute lung injury and fat embolism following cosmetic injection. Clinicians described her level of consciousness as apathetic during acute illness. The suspect oil's fatty acid profile resembled rice bran oil, with product identity and processing unconfirmed. An oral culinary exposure, a clinical apathy assessment and a Parkinson cohort were absent. The case cannot establish direction for claim 0407. [Original case report](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/internalmedicine1992/34/5/34_5_380/_pdf)
