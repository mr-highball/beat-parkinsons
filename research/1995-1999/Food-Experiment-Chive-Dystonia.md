# Chives, related Allium species and dystonia

## 1997 and 1999 — Reported garlic use in familial paroxysmal dystonic choreoathetosis

The 1997 Japanese-language report by Matsuo and colleagues describes a family with at least 17 affected individuals across six generations. Its abstract reports perceived benefit with garlic or clonazepam. The 1999 report examines 26 members of the same pedigree and distinguishes participant and family history from experimentally observed results. Affected family members said garlic softened their involuntary attacks. One woman whose episodes consisted of shoulder or arm stiffness without dystonic movements reported fewer or less severe episodes with garlic. The authors conducted a caffeine challenge with electromyography in a different patient and describe no controlled challenge with garlic, botanical identification, part, preparation, dose or quantitative garlic response. The neurologic condition was familial paroxysmal dystonic choreoathetosis, described as nonkinesigenic dyskinesia. This is a single family’s garlic history outside Parkinson disease. A separate December 1999 genetic follow-up tested a candidate gene and provides no independent dietary observation.
