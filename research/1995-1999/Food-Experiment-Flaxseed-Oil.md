# Flaxseed oil evidence

## First rat reports of flax oil and odor discrimination

At an NIH workshop on April 7, 1999, Salem and colleagues reported diets with differing ALA supplied by flax oil and adequate linoleic acid from safflower oil. The deficient rats learned an odor discrimination more slowly and made more errors. Their overall motor activity was similar to that of the adequate group. The workshop also reported a longer time to find the platform in the water maze among deficient rats. [NIH workshop abstract](https://ods.od.nih.gov/pubs/conferences/w6w3_abstracts.html)

The 1999 Lipids article reports that omega-3-deficient second-generation male rats made more errors in the seven-problem, two-odor task and took longer to reach the water-maze platform. Their olfactory-bulb DHA was 82% lower than that of the omega-3-adequate group. The overlapping author group, diet, generation and DHA result support treating the odor finding as one study family with the [2001 detailed olfactory report](../2000-2004/Food-Experiment-Flaxseed-Oil.md#flaxseed-oil-in-a-rat-odor-discrimination-diet). The experiment compared developmental fatty-acid adequacy in rats. Oil pressing method and clinical smell outcomes in Parkinson’s were unreported. [Original 1999 paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10419165/)
