# Corn oil comparison during analgesic withdrawal

## Rheumatoid arthritis and diclofenac withdrawal

Kremer and colleagues compared fish oil with corn oil while both groups initially took diclofenac. From the end of active diclofenac to the first visit on its placebo, Table 2 shows a within-corn-oil increase of 0.58 ± 0.19 in patient-rated pain (p = .007) and 0.47 ± 0.23 in physician-rated pain (p = .06), both 0–4 scales, mean change ± SEM. The 0.37 ± 0.14 physician-rated change (p = .02) is in the fish-oil column. The already indexed corn-oil annotation had assigned that fish-oil value to corn oil. The authors found no statistically clear difference in clinical changes between oils in this interval. For Parkinson pain claim `0278`, the drug withdrawal, disease setting and unidentified oil refining leave the oil's independent direction unresolved. ## Fatigue onset during diclofenac withdrawal

Table 2 gives a within-arm change in time to fatigue of −0.76 ± 0.56 hours in the corn-oil arm (n = 20, p = .19) and −0.19 ± 0.40 hours in the fish-oil arm (n = 19, p = .64) at the first visit after stopping active diclofenac. Table 3 compares baseline with the end of the eight-week withdrawal period. Its values are −0.63 ± 0.46 hours for corn oil (n = 14, p = .20) and +0.23 ± 0.67 hours for fish oil (n = 15, p = .74). Estimates are mean changes ± SEM. The study times onset in rheumatoid arthritis. Claim `0694` concerns fatigue severity in Parkinson’s disease following refined oil.
