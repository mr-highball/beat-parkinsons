# Allspice and cumin evidence

## Cumin allergy after a cooked mixed meal

Boxer and colleagues reported nausea, dyspnea, itching and vomiting within minutes of a falafel containing ground cumin and several other ingredients in a 68-year-old woman. Their report describes skin and laboratory evidence of cumin sensitization. The public article excerpt names ground cumin in a fried mixed meal and does not report Parkinson's disease, a separate toasted-seed challenge, or quantified swallowing difficulty. [Primary report, PMID 9155844](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9155844/) · [Publisher case excerpt](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0091674997700393)

## Occupational skin reactions to allspice

Kanerva and colleagues identified five cases of spice-related contact dermatitis among approximately 1,000 patients referred for occupational skin disease. Allspice was among the implicated spices. The affected areas were hands or fingers. This case series concerns skin contact and patch-test outcomes. [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0536.1996.tb02334.x)

## Injected allspice extracts and blood pressure in rats

Suárez and colleagues observed a fall in mean arterial pressure after intravenous aqueous or ethanol extracts of *Pimenta dioica* in anesthetized rats. The aqueous extract gave a larger fall than the ethanolic extract at the same reported dose. The paper concerns concentrated extract and rat hemodynamics. No gait, falls, or oral culinary outcome was measured. [Primary abstract, PMID 9173181](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9173181/)
