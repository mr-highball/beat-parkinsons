# Movement and Rehabilitation for 1995–1999

### 1995 — Spa treatment and movement proposals

Ceballos Hernansanz’s communication describes continuing medication alongside massage, baths, mud packs, and exercises for balance, breathing, and walking. The report gives no observed patient outcomes or dietary comparison. [Publisher scan, pages 53–54](https://hidromed.org/hm/images/pdf/BSEHM%201995_10%281%2953-54_Ceballos%20Hernansanz-MA.pdf) · [Earlier narrative](../1990-1994/Movement-and-Rehabilitation.md#1992--hydrology-and-hydrotherapy-proposals-in-parkinsons)

### 1996 — Rhythmic auditory cueing for gait

Thaut and colleagues studied a short home walking program paced by rhythmic sound. Walking speed and other gait measures improved more in the cueing group than in the comparison groups. The small groups and three-week program limited assessment of long-term function. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8684391/)

### 1996 — Intensive voice and respiratory therapy comparison

Ramig and colleagues compared intensive treatment emphasizing voice with respiratory effort treatment in 35 people. Voice-focused therapy improved vocal intensity, with some gains still observable at 12 months. The trial primarily measured voice performance. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8960734/)

### 1998 — Exercise for axial mobility and functional reach

Schenkman and colleagues randomized 51 people with early- to mid-stage disease. The ten-week intervention improved functional axial rotation and reach, while time to sit up from lying did not change significantly. The results were specific to the tests and duration studied. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9777901/)
