# Lentil and kidney bean evidence

## Canned and dry red kidney bean laboratory comparison

Venter and colleagues compared laboratory hemagglutination activity for dry bean cultivars, imported dry red kidney beans, and imported canned red kidney beans. The abstract reports that uncooked and incompletely cooked red kidney beans did not exceed half the other varieties tested. The abstract omits a canned-specific assay estimate, and the publisher version was unavailable during this pass. No Parkinson clinical measures were used. [Primary abstract, PMID 7777998](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7777998/)

## Immediate allergy case and tests of canned kidney bean extracts

Zacharisen and Kurup reported a woman who developed acute pharyngeal itching, lip burning, wheezing, rash and other symptoms after a homemade chili containing kidney and pinto beans. Clinical challenge was withheld because of the severity of the reaction. Commercial kidney bean extract and a crude extract from canned kidney beans showed IgE reactivity in laboratory or skin tests. The patient's reaction involved a mixed dish and the report did not specify a canned source for that meal. Mouth or throat allergy symptoms and clinical Parkinson drooling are different outcomes. [Primary case report](https://www.jacionline.org/article/S0091674998703653/pdf)
