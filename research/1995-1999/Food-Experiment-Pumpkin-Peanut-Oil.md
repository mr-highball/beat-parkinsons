# Peanut oil in an oral allergy challenge

## Crude and refined peanut oil challenges

Hourihane and colleagues enrolled 60 participants with confirmed peanut allergy in a blinded crossover. Six reacted during the crude-oil challenge and none reacted during the refined-oil challenge. Table 2 records three cases of oral itch and one each of throat itch, wheeze and lip swelling. The report does not identify crude oil as the frozen cold-pressed preparation. It measures acute allergy without evaluating drooling, speech intelligibility or voice volume in Parkinson’s. ## Functional fatigue and the Pharmaton capsule formulation

Le Gal and colleagues randomized 232 adults with functional fatigue to Pharmaton capsules or placebo. They measured five patient-selected complaints at baseline, day 21 and day 42. The original abstract reports a group difference favoring the supplement on the fatigue score at day 42. A September 2020 Irish regulator label for Pharmaton capsules names refined arachis oil as an excipient. The label does not establish the excipients or placebo contents of the 1996 French trial, and no oil-specific fatigue effect can be inferred. The distinct 2003 Wesnes night-shift study used film-coated caplets. Product branding alone cannot establish oil exposure in either trial.
