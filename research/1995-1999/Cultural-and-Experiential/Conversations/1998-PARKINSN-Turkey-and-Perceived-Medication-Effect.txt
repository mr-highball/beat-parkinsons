Title: Turkey leftovers and perceived medication response in a Parkinson mailing-list conversation
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Public conversation, firsthand recollection
Publication date: 1998-11-19
Date basis: Message header in the indexed university mailing-list archive. The recalled holiday exposure occurred in the preceding year.
Source: https://listserv.utoronto.ca/scripts/wa.exe?A2=1805A&D=0&H=A&L=PARKINSN&P=31712683
Coverage: Full message body surfaced by the search index, with header and a quoted preceding question. Direct retrieval timed out.

A participant reporting frequent use of prescribed carbidopa/levodopa recalled feeling several doses had become less effective while repeatedly eating small amounts of leftover Thanksgiving turkey through the day. They attributed the experience retrospectively to the food and separately described difficult afternoons after protein-containing lunches. The estimate about drug absorption was a personal inference. No drug level, objective score, cooking method or tremor observation was supplied. The message quotes a second participant's question about food timing, which is a separate question and supplies no additional turkey or symptom observation.

The record preserves a firsthand adverse experience under a specific medication and meal context. Repeated eating over one holiday supplies one account. The self-reported diagnosis, unspecified food preparation, medication timing and other foods limit attribution and leave the frozen resting-tremor endpoint open.
