# Tree nut oil processing and allergy

## Gourmet oils and residual allergen binding

Teuber, Brown and Haapanen (1997) examined commercial hazelnut, walnut, macadamia, almond and pistachio oils. Serum IgE binding and residual protein tended to be greater for products processed less or at lower temperatures. The authors tested extracted material in a laboratory and did not give the oils to patients. This provides context for exposure differences in 0481 and 0935. It leaves both the clinical swallowing effect and the processing of any specific culinary product untested. A similarly titled earlier meeting abstract describes the same research team and testing and is counted with the journal article. [Journal report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9111495/) · [Earlier meeting abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0091-6749%2896%2980441-6)

## Macadamia meal case and skin oil test

Sutherland and colleagues (1999) described anaphylaxis after ingestion of a cake containing macadamia meal and assessed IgE reactivity to the nut. The article is indexed under macadamia nut anaphylaxis. A later primary protein study recounts that the patient reacted to direct skin contact with a cut roasted kernel, while an oil skin application caused no reaction in that patient or one control. That comparison used a different route from the meal exposure and did not test swallowing after ingesting cold-pressed macadamia oil. [Original case](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0091-6749%2899%2970304-0) · [Later primary protein paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/09540105.2010.508815)
