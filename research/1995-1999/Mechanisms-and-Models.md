# Mechanisms and Models for 1995–1999

### 1997 — SNCA mutation in families with Parkinson’s disease

Polymeropoulos and colleagues identified a missense change in the alpha-synuclein gene in an Italian kindred and three Greek families with dominant Parkinson’s disease. The family studies established a molecular cause in a subset of inherited disease. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9197268/)

### 1997 — Alpha-synuclein staining in Lewy bodies

Spillantini and colleagues found alpha-synuclein in Lewy bodies in Parkinson’s disease and dementia with Lewy bodies. The finding connected a protein implicated by family genetics to characteristic inclusions in human brain tissue. [Original source](https://www.nature.com/articles/42166)

### 1998 — Alpha-synuclein in isolated Lewy-body filaments

Spillantini and colleagues examined filaments from brains with Parkinson’s disease or dementia with Lewy bodies and found strong alpha-synuclein labeling. The tissue study identified a major filament component without resolving the roles of inclusions in neuron injury. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9600990/)

### 1998 — Parkin mutations and recessive juvenile parkinsonism

Kitada and colleagues identified deletions in the gene encoding parkin in families with autosomal recessive juvenile parkinsonism. The study established a second inherited mechanism, with a different inheritance pattern and phenotype from the SNCA families. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9560156/)
