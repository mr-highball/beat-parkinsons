Title: Fresh figs and harvest excitement in an 1875 Kabylie travel book
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Historical travel narrative
Source: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/15434/pg15434-images.html
Publication date: 1875
Reviewed: 2026-11-19
Date basis: Transcribed title page of J. Vilbort, En Kabylie, Charpentier et Cie. The digital transcription was first released in 2005.
Location inspected: Original French passage beginning Pendant ces jours d'abondance and the transcribed title page.
Community and context: The travel narrator describes a seasonal harvest in Kabylie and compares the celebration to a wine harvest. The account reflects a specific outsider's literary framing.
Exposure: Fresh white or black figs, eaten during the harvest. The preparation of dried figs is not the stated exposure.
Reported direction: A literary description of intoxication-like excitement tied to the harvest.
Evidence and limits: No named firsthand eater, clinical description of a visual hallucination, Parkinson diagnosis or episode count. The metaphor alone cannot identify a clinical or physiological state.
Authenticity: Verifiable historical text. The practical significance and independence of this account from the earlier Duhousset account are uncertain.
Efficacy evidence: Historical context for the reported experience with fresh figs. The text does not measure a symptom or a treatment effect.
Related research: Duhousset 1867, Le Tour du monde, p. 304 documents similar wording with less specific preparation.
Open trail: Compare original print versions and identify community-authored corroboration with explicit preparations and experiences.
