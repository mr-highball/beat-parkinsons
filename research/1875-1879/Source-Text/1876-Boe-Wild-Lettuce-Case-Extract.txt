Title: Sur un cas d’empoisonnement par la laitue vireuse
Source: https://archive.org/download/BIUSante_90014x1876x90/BIUSante_90014x1876x90.pdf#page=372
First public article date: April 1876 as cited in a contemporaneous report, exact date unresolved
Accessed: 2026-11-17
Text coverage: Curated extract of original printed pages 368–369

A. Boe describes three people who shared a salad containing
foraged leaves he identified in the field as Lactuca virosa.
The report describes visual disturbances and delirium with
hallucinations and jumping in a child. It gives no record of
tremor, Parkinson disease or standardized motor testing. One
person who declined the lettuce was unaffected in this account.
The field identification predates modern laboratory confirmation.
The exposure was not identified as commercial romaine lettuce.

Richardson's July 1876 digest on printed pages 233–234
(https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5144800/) and Bouchardat's
1877 Annuaire, pp. 23–25, repeat the same family case.
