# Food and nutrition, 1865-1869

### 1868 — Tea, coffee, and spirits reported to worsen shaking in one case

Trousseau reproduced Oppolzer’s history of a 72-year-old with progressive tremor and difficulty standing or walking. The history states that tea, coffee, and spirits increased the shaking. The report is an uncontrolled observation in one historical case, with uncertain diagnosis and no measurement of dose or exposure. It concerns an immediate symptom and gives no estimate of long-term risk.

[Trousseau, Lectures on Clinical Medicine, vol. I, p. 446, scan](https://archive.org/details/b2490773x_0001/page/446/mode/2up)

### 1868 — Sanders records a nutritious diet as part of a general regimen

Sanders’s textbook chapter listed a nutritious diet, rest or moderate activity, and other supportive measures among the approaches in use. It provides no comparison, individual food effects, or measured outcomes attributable to diet.

[Sanders, A System of Medicine, vol. II, pp. 184–204, scan](https://archive.org/details/b20415126_002/page/184/mode/2up)
