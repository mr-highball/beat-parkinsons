# Clinical findings and diagnosis, 1865-1869

### 1868 — Ordenstein compares shaking palsy with disseminated sclerosis

Léopold Ordenstein’s thesis drew on Charcot’s Salpêtrière patients. It contrasts the tremor patterns and progression of shaking palsy and disseminated sclerosis, and documents clinical and autopsy evidence in one sclerosis case. The thesis records a diagnostic distinction built from selected cases.

[Ordenstein, Sur la paralysie agitante et la sclérose en plaques généralisée, scan](https://archive.org/details/BIUSante_151566x02)

### 1868 — Trousseau documents slowing on repetitive hand movements

In an English translation of a Hôtel-Dieu lecture, Armand Trousseau described a patient whose repeated hand opening and closing started quickly, slowed within about 15 seconds, and stopped. He also contrasted persistent muscle contraction with conspicuous shaking in other cases. These are clinical observations with no standardized measurement.

[Trousseau, Lectures on Clinical Medicine, vol. I, pp. 440–449, scan](https://archive.org/details/b2490773x_0001/page/440/mode/2up)

### 1869 — Haynes reports unilateral paralysis agitans

S. L. Haynes presented a case predominantly affecting the right side, with speech difficulty and right-sided limb signs. Historical diagnoses were based on the bedside phenotype, so alternative causes remain possible.

[Haynes, Edinburgh Medical Journal, scan](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5332779/)
