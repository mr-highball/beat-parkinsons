Title: Fig harvesting and a reported intoxication comparison in Kabylie
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Historical travel observation with an attributed local saying
Source: https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Le_Tour_du_monde_-_16.djvu/304
Publication date: 1867
Reviewed: 2026-11-19
Date basis: Duhousset, Excursions dans la grande Kabylie, Le Tour du monde, volume 16, second installment, printed p. 304.
Location inspected: Original French page transcription and its volume and installment metadata.
Community and context: A French officer's travel account discusses harvesting and preserving figs near Achouba. The account uses a colonial outsider's perspective.
Exposure: Eating large amounts of figs during the harvest. The author also describes drying the fruit separately. The passage gives no individual intake record, specific cultivar or confirmation that the reported effect followed dried fruit.
Reported direction: The author suggests greater excitement and invokes a saying comparing someone full of figs to a drunk person. The suggested sugar fermentation is the author's interpretation, without a measurement of fermentation or alcohol.
Evidence and limits: A seasonal impression and metaphor, with no identified first-person episode, comparator, medical diagnosis, hallucination description or frequency.
Authenticity: The French publication and location are verifiable. The underlying experience, the speaker of the saying and the interpretation are not independently verified.
Efficacy evidence: Literary and observational context. The report supplies no individual visual outcome or before-and-after comparison.
Related research: The 1875 Vilbort travel account specifically mentions fresh figs. Its overlapping motif cannot be treated as an independent clinical observation.
Open trail: Check the original Hanoteau and Letourneux passage attributed in Condit's book and seek community-authored material before drawing conclusions about local experience.
