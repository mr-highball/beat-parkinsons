# Mechanisms and models

### 1935 — Brainstem pathology after encephalitis

Julius Hallervorden’s postencephalitic series examined brain tissue in 21 cases. Greenfield and Bosanquet’s later primary pathology study reports that Hallervorden consistently found neurofibrillary changes in the substantia nigra, with less consistent changes elsewhere. Group differences in age and early staining methods complicate comparison with idiopathic cases. [Original study](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01840815) · [1953 pathology comparison](https://jnnp.bmj.com/content/jnnp/16/4/213.full.pdf)

### 1936 — Cerebellar tremor and the cerebral cortex in monkeys

Aring and Fulton reported that experimental removal of motor and premotor cortical areas could abolish cerebellar tremor, while other lesions could accentuate it. This supported a circuit-based rationale for subsequent human tremor experiments. Induced cerebellar tremor and Parkinson’s resting tremor represent different phenomena. [Source](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/archneurpsyc/article-abstract/646741)

### 1937 — An anatomical map of the substantia nigra

Rolf Hassler used serially sectioned material to propose anatomical subdivisions of the substantia nigra, explaining that finer localization was needed for comparing paralysis agitans and postencephalitic parkinsonism. This paper establishes the anatomical method. The German archive scan is the basis for the publication date. [Source](https://www.thehumanbrain.info/brain/db_literature/HasslerSubstantianigra.pdf)
