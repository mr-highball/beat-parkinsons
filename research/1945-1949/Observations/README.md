# Observations for 1945–1949

## Major observations

Synthetic trihexyphenidyl entered larger preliminary clinical series. Human stereotactic instrumentation made precise deep-brain targeting possible, and primate work linked the subthalamic region to hyperkinetic movement. [Remedies](../Drugs-and-Remedies.md) · [Procedures](../Procedures-and-Devices.md) · [Models](../Mechanisms-and-Models.md)

## Minor observations

Electrostriograms failed to show a consistent relationship with parkinsonian disability. Concurrent atropine in several patients in an early diphenhydramine series complicates the reported improvement. [Models](../Mechanisms-and-Models.md) · [Remedies](../Drugs-and-Remedies.md)

## Comparison with 1940–1944

[Previous observations](../../1940-1944/Observations/README.md)

The earlier focus on variation in plant preparations broadened to named synthetic drugs with defined ingredients and larger clinical series. Both periods still relied heavily on uncontrolled observations for outcomes. Deep targeting and recording added approaches distinct from the earlier cortical and tract work.
