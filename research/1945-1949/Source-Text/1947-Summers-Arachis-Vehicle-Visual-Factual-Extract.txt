Publication: G. D. Summers, Case of Camphor Poisoning, British Medical Journal, December 20, 1947, 2(4537), p. 1009.
DOI: 10.1136/bmj.2.4537.1009-c. PMID 18935501, PMC2057513.
Original: https://www.bmj.com/content/bmj/2/4537/1009.1.full.pdf
Coverage: Factual summary of symptom, observed route and the historical vehicle statement. Article wording is paraphrased.

A child was observed to be delirious and appeared to see things after topical exposure to a camphor preparation. The reporting physician attributed the presentation to camphor toxicity. The method of preparation and exact batch were not independently verified. The author cited arachis oil as the contemporary formulation vehicle. A later guideline interprets the experience as visual hallucination and cites the same case. No oral peanut oil exposure, pressing grade, Parkinson diagnosis, or symptom frequency estimate was documented.
