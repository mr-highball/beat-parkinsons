Title: A clinical and electromyographic study of the effects of d-tubocurarine and belladonna alkaloids in Parkinson's disease
PMID: 18130078
Publication date: 1949-05
Citation: H. Waldo Bird and Harold L. Meyers. Bulletin of the Menninger Clinic 13(3):100–105.
Source: https://archive.org/details/sim_bulletin-of-the-menninger-clinic_1949-05_13_3/page/n29/mode/1up
Index: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18130078/
Text coverage: All six printed pages compared with the digitized issue's OCR and scan images. This extract summarizes the relevant results.

The report describes 11 patients diagnosed with a Parkinsonian syndrome, eight with tremor. Four clinical groups received different sequences of an intramuscular d-tubocurarine formulation and oral belladonna alkaloids. The injected medication used peanut oil and myricin, a wax, as vehicle. The article gives the manufacturer and no oil pressing grade. Tables and methods give no oil-alone, alternate vehicle or oral peanut-oil contrast. Five comparison subjects supplied normal electromyograms.

Clinical observations on page 103 distinguish a reduction in tremor amplitude under both types of medication from unchanged tremor rate and persistence of tremor. The electromyography section on page 104 reports frequencies of 4–7.5 Hz in the eight tremulous patients and describes unchanged tremor incidence under either drug. Tremor bursts present during relaxation persisted through 7% of subsequent active motions in non-curarized observations and 14% in curarized observations. These denominators are motions, and raw counts are unspecified. The page 102 table counts voluntary elbow flexion-extension as a rigidity measure. It supplies no numerical size or uncertainty for the amplitude change. The authors concluded on page 105 that the formulation and belladonna had little overall effect on tremor. Two combined-medication observations included weakness, visual symptoms, vertigo and a decrease in systolic blood pressure.

The direct historical evidence concerns the injected drug formulation. The lower amplitude and null incidence and frequency findings all belong to this one study. The 1951 Ebin review (PMID 14879233) summarizes the same family. The report supplies no dietary inference.
