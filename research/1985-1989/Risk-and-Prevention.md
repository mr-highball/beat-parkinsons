# Risk and Prevention for 1985–1989

### 1986 — Childhood rural residence and well water

Rajput and colleagues described rural childhood residence and well-water use in a Saskatchewan early-onset Parkinson’s series. The comparison used expected provincial exposure patterns. Rural residence, water source, and other exposures were difficult to disentangle. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3779530/)

### 1987 — Pesticide use and geographic patterns in Quebec

Barbeau and colleagues compared Parkinson’s prevalence across nine rural Quebec regions. Regions with higher prevalence included areas of intensive agriculture and high pesticide use. The regional comparison did not measure individual exposures. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3815163/)

### 1987 — Saskatchewan water chemistry and agricultural exposures

Rajput and colleagues revisited the Saskatchewan rural association and reported no correlation with the measured water metals or the agricultural chemicals assessed. The authors retained well water as a possible vehicle for an unidentified exposure. The report’s measures could leave an agent or exposure window undetected. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3676917/)

### 1989 — Occupational manganese-associated parkinsonism

Wang and colleagues examined workers after a cluster of parkinsonism at a ferromanganese smelter. Cases clustered in the most heavily exposed workers, and the authors reported no new cases after ventilation repair. This investigation addressed occupational secondary parkinsonism. It could not quantify risk in the general population. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2611159/)
