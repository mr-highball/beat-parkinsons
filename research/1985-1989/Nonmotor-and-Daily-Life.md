# Nonmotor and Daily Life for 1985–1989

### 1986 — Pain that changes with the levodopa cycle

Quinn and colleagues described severe pain that could vary in parallel with levodopa-related motor fluctuations. The clinical observations drew attention to medication state during pain assessment. They did not estimate how common this pain pattern was. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2872480/)

### 1986 — First description of chronic REM sleep behavior disorder

Schenck and colleagues documented dream enactment and abnormal REM sleep muscle activity in five adults. This sleep series provides historical context for later studies linking REM sleep behavior disorder to parkinsonian disorders. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3505730/)

### 1988 — Dementia in hospital and outpatient records

Mayeux and colleagues reviewed clinical records for people with idiopathic Parkinson’s disease. Dementia was more frequent in older patients and more common than expected from general population data. Retrospective record ascertainment and the single clinical center constrain prevalence interpretation. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3341950/)

### 1988 — Smell impairment and patient awareness

Doty and colleagues found marked odor-identification and detection impairment in 81 patients compared with matched controls. Most participants with Parkinson’s disease were unaware of the deficit. Test scores did not clearly track disease stage or duration, and similar deficits occurred in the Alzheimer’s comparison data. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3399075/)

### 1989 — Cognition and levodopa-resistant disability

Pillon and colleagues compared neuropsychological scores and motor response to levodopa in 120 people. Cognitive deficits tracked residual motor disability in the optimally treated state. The association supported the hypothesis of nondopaminergic contributions without identifying a particular pathway. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2703838/)
