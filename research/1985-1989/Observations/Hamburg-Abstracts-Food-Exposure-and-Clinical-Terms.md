# Beverage reports and chicken tissue at the Hamburg neurology congress

The 1985 program contains a stroke case-control abstract that lists tea and coffee, without reporting a beverage-specific estimate. A distinct familial movement abstract mentions coffee in its differential discussion. A later Japanese pedigree directly reports drinks as attack triggers within a familial episodic diagnosis. The studies preserve context for the apparent exposure links without conflating fatigue as an outcome with exercise, stress or fatigue as a reported attack precipitant. [1985 conference abstracts](../Source-Text/1985-PMID4067611-Hamburg-Coffee-and-Chicken-Contexts.txt) · [1999 pedigree](../../1995-1999/Source-Text/1999-PMID10369313-Matsuo-Drink-Associated-Movement-Attacks.txt) · [2007 phenotype cohort](../../2005-2009/Source-Text/2007-PMID17515540-Bruno-Paroxysmal-Dyskinesia-Triggers.txt)

An independent member 05.20.07 reports meal-associated blood-pressure drops in untreated Parkinson disease. A 1977 case records meal-associated symptoms and a separate measured glucose-challenge response. The 1987 follow-up from the Pavia team adds a comparison with healthy controls and leaves possible cohort overlap open. [1977 case](../../1975-1979/Source-Text/1977-PMID589129-Seyer-Hansen-Meal-Blood-Pressure.txt) · [1987 study](../Source-Text/1987-PMID3822130-Micieli-Meal-and-Orthostatic-Pressure.txt)

A separate 1995 comparison reported a larger drop in supine systolic pressure after meals in ten older Parkinson patients than in age-matched controls. Lunch did not amplify the orthostatic decrease under the study's standing and tilt tests. [Supine and standing measurements](../../1995-1999/Source-Text/1995-PMID8984127-Loew-Meal-and-Standing-Blood-Pressure.txt)

The chicken contexts concern muscle specimens and experimental avian disease. The traceable 1984, 1985 and 1988 enzyme papers have overlapping source teams and tissue comparisons. Treat the chronology as a research program whose sampling independence still needs verification.
