# Protein, motor response and dyskinesia

The previous bucket's Nutt experiments separated oral-meal effects on plasma levels from a motor-response change during stable intravenous levodopa delivery. The 1985–1989 controlled comparisons add two kinds of clinical context. Protein distribution within an equal daily total changed time ON in a five-person study, and low daytime protein in the Pincus and Barry series coincided with more dyskinesia even as mobility improved. [1984 oral and infusion report](../../1980-1984/Source-Text/1984-PMID6694694-Meals-and-Levodopa-On-Off.txt) · [1989 time ON study](../Source-Text/1989-PMID2648188-Protein-Amount-and-Distribution.txt) · [1987 printed figures](../Source-Text/1987-Pincus-Barry-Daytime-Protein-and-Motor-Fluctuation.txt)

The later Barichella experiments record both outcomes in one family. All six participants in the 2007 comparison came from the prior 18-person crossover. They recorded less OFF time and more dyskinetic ON time in the same assigned condition. Record each outcome separately and retain the medication context and ingredient attribution gaps.
