# Mechanisms and Models for 1985–1989

### 1988 — Incidental Lewy bodies in older autopsy samples

Gibb and Lees found incidental Lewy bodies in brains from people who had died with conditions other than Parkinson’s disease, increasingly often with age. They proposed that some represented a presymptomatic stage and discussed a historical hypothesis about inclusion composition. Postmortem sampling could not establish whether a given individual would develop clinical disease. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2841426/)

### 1989 — Mitochondrial complex I hypothesis in human tissue

Schapira and colleagues reported a mitochondrial complex I deficiency in Parkinson’s disease. A larger report from the same team followed in 1990. The brief Lancet paper established a human-tissue lead for studying energy metabolism and oxidative stress. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2566813/)

### 1989 — Region-specific iron changes in postmortem brain

Dexter and colleagues compared several metals in brains from Parkinson’s cases and age-matched controls. Total iron was higher in the substantia nigra and lower in the globus pallidus. The tissue study motivated research on oxidative injury. It did not measure dietary or occupational iron exposure. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2723638/)
