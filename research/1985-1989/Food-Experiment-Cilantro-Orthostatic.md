# Cilantro, blood pressure and orthostatic symptoms

## 1986 — Boiled dried coriander seed control in a human diuresis study

Udupihille and Jiffry compared a boiled extract of dried *Coriandrum sativum* seeds with water, saline and *Aerva lanata* preparations in male medical students aged 18–25. Participants drank 200 mL of the assigned liquid, and urine was collected every 15 minutes for two hours. The authors reported no significant diuresis after 200 mL of 100 g/L coriander seed extract. They reported very mild diuresis with a peak of 1.5 mL/min at 45 minutes at the reported 800 g/L concentration, which was substantially higher than the concentrations listed in Methods. Figure 1 labels its plotted coriander concentration 50 g/L, while the Results describe a 100 g/L comparison. The report does not describe Parkinson diagnosis, blood pressure measurements or standing symptoms. ## 1986 — Coriander in a preliminary anesthetized-rat blood pressure report

Medhin and colleagues' title identifies an anesthetized-rat comparison of *Lupinus termis* and *Coriandrum sativum*. The indexed record has no abstract and does not identify the coriander plant part, preparation or numerical effect. Later papers cite it as a hypotensive result. Original full text remains an access lead. [Primary bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3705966/)
