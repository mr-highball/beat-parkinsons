# Lemongrass preparation and symptom evidence

## Brewed leaf tea and sleep reports in Brazilian volunteers

July 1986, PMID 2429120, DOI 10.1016/0378-8741(86)90074-7. This is Part III of the lemongrass pharmacology series.

Fifty healthy volunteers drank two preparations of Cymbopogon citratus dried leaf tea and a placebo on separate nights. The researchers issued 4 g sachets for 200 mL tea, and one tea condition was identified in advance while the other active tea and placebo were coded. Their Table 3 includes dream recall, nightmares, sleep onset, sleep interruptions and morning experience. They reported no differences across the three conditions, including among seven participants with previous sleep difficulties. Dream recall was a questionnaire response without bed-partner, motor or polysomnographic observation. Separate uncontrolled safety sets documented self-reported somnolence (5/11) and polyuria (4/11) after one dose, and one report of somnolence with dizziness among eight volunteers after daily use. These adverse symptom sets do not measure validated daytime sleepiness, sudden urinary urgency, falls or Parkinson outcomes. ## Morning sleepiness and acute somnolence in the 1986 paper

Table 3, question 9 records 34, 37 and 33 of 50 as answering no to feeling sleepy after the labeled tea, placebo and coded active tea. The implied sleepy proportions were 16/50, 13/50 and 17/50. Both tea conditions were numerically higher than placebo. The authors found no significant paired comparison, and individual transition counts were unreported. The acute safety experiment used a morning brew and recorded 5/11 somnolence reports, with no concurrent placebo. The Results describe symptoms one hour after intake, while Methods place questionnaire completion later that day. A separate two-week morning-use group recorded one person with somnolence and dizziness. All groups were healthy. The single morning item and symptom reports leave sustained clinical severity and Parkinson transfer uncertain. ## Oral leaf tea and measured sleep in laboratory animals

July 1986, PMID 3762195, DOI 10.1016/0378-8741(86)90072-3. This is Part I of the series. The original report spans pages 37–64. The linked rendering has a Part III page title, but the article body begins at printed page 37 and identifies Part I and its laboratory-animal experiments.

The investigators examined leaf tea from three Brazilian regions in two sleep tests. Table 6 and the Results describe similar pentobarbital-induced sleeping time across the tested oral and intraperitoneal tea preparations in mice. The positive comparison was for isolated citral administered intraperitoneally. The rat experiment used an overnight EEG recording after oral leaf tea and reported unchanged slow-wave and REM sleep parameters. The dose labels differ between the rendered Methods and Results, and the displayed table does not settle the dose. The report concerns laboratory sleep measures. It provides no observed dream enactment, REM sleep without atonia, or Parkinson cohort.
