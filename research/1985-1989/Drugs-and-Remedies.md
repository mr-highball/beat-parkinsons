# Drugs and Remedies for 1985–1989

### 1985 — Chronic levodopa with adjunct bromocriptine

Hoehn compared experience from before and after levodopa entered practice and described adjunct bromocriptine for treatment complications. A subset had smoother response and reduced off-period pain or dystonia. The historical comparison was vulnerable to changes in case mix and care. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3984685/)

### 1988 — Apomorphine infusion or injections for on–off fluctuations

Stibe and colleagues treated 19 people with severe fluctuations using subcutaneous apomorphine. Off periods shortened in groups receiving infusion or intermittent injections, and infusion recipients used less levodopa on average. The uncontrolled series included people selected for severe treatment complications. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2893200/)

### 1989 — Early bromocriptine versus levodopa in Sydney

The Sydney Multicentre Study followed 129 newly diagnosed people in a double-blind comparison. The three-year report found better motor control with levodopa–carbidopa and more dyskinesia than with bromocriptine. Results capture the specific treatment regimens and follow-up studied. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2647907/)

### 1989 — Selegiline and time to additional therapy

Tetrud and Langston reported a double-blind trial in early disease in which selegiline delayed the point at which levodopa became necessary. The threshold reflects clinical disability and could shift because of symptomatic benefit. The study proposed a progression effect for further testing. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2502843/)

### 1989 — DATATOP interim selegiline result

The Parkinson Study Group’s multicenter trial enrolled 800 people with early, untreated Parkinson’s disease and tested selegiline and vitamin E. The interim comparison found delayed disability prompting levodopa in the selegiline groups. The group continued to assess vitamin E and the meaning of the clinical endpoint. [Original publication](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM198911163212004)
