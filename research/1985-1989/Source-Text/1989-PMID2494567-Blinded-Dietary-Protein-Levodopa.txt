Title: The effect of dietary protein on the efficacy of L-dopa: a double-blind study
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2494567/
PMID: 2494567
DOI: 10.1212/wnl.39.4.549
Citation: Tsui JK, Ross S, Poulin K, Douglas J, Postnikoff D, Calne S, Woodward W, Calne DB. Neurology. 1989 Apr. 39(4):549–552.
Coverage: Author abstract from PubMed. Local factual summary.
Reviewed: 2026-11-22 UTC

Ten participants with Parkinson disease received a higher- and lower-protein diet in randomized order for one week each. The diets were matched in appearance and taste. Participants remained on the same medication regimens. A blinded physician used the modified Columbia scale, rigidity measurements, movement velocity and pegboard testing. Participants kept hourly fluctuation records.

The authors reported better overall performance during the lower-protein week. In three participants, higher peaks of plasma levodopa coincided with poorer performance and more OFF hours during the higher-protein week. The available abstract does not provide group means or uncertainty for individual rigidity or movement measures, separate rest-tremor data or a comparison of named foods.
