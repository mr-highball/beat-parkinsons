Title: Dietary method for reducing fluctuations in Parkinson's disease
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2590325/
PMCID: PMC2590325
PMID: 3577210
Citation: Pincus JH, Barry KM. Yale Journal of Biology and Medicine. 1987 Mar–Apr. 60(2):133–137.
Coverage: Full original printed pages 133–137, including Table 1 and Figures 1–3. Local factual summary.
Reviewed: 2026-11-22 UTC

The series comprised 15 people with Parkinson disease, eight with motor fluctuations and seven considered nonresponsive to carbidopa/levodopa. Seven of eight fluctuators and six of seven participants in the nonresponse subgroup had an immediate clinical response during the lower-protein daytime regimen. In the latter subgroup the mean Northwestern Disability score was 33 on the regular diet and 16 on the low-protein diet. The investigators adjusted treatment to address chorea in six fluctuators and two nonresponders. The overall clinical series therefore includes co-intervention and clinical follow-up.

Some participants had an inpatient fixed-order comparison of isocaloric diets at 7 versus 160 g daytime protein with medication timing and dose held constant on both days. The paper does not state how many participants underwent that comparison. The clinical report describes predominantly more chorea on low protein and more immobility on high protein. Figures 1–3 show one fluctuating participant, with higher dyskinesia scores on low protein and worse disability, higher circulating large neutral amino acids and higher circulating levodopa on high protein. Those three figures are one case, not three independent results.

The equal-calorie diets and stable medication applied to the inpatient challenge. The paper does not isolate a culinary ingredient, report group hours of OFF time for any ingredient, or give a separate measure of rest tremor. Potential participant overlap with the same authors' 1987 and 1988 reports remains unresolved.
