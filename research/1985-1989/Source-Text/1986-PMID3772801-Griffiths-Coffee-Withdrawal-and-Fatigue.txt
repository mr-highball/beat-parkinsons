Review depth: primary abstract and investigator reanalysis of the same data

The original abstract reports nine volunteers with heavy coffee intake across residential experiments. In a withdrawal comparison, investigators replaced caffeinated coffee with decaf under masking. Sleepiness and inactivity increased and alertness fell during withdrawal. Some participants without current dependence avoided caffeine and reported adverse symptoms. The abstract separates subjective, staff and performance observations but does not give a numerical fatigue score.

The same lead investigator's 1988 review gives a seven-person withdrawal subsample and republishes data collected in the 1986 experiment. Its Table 2, Figure 1 and discussion use the Profile of Mood States and report higher Fatigue and lower Vigor during decaf substitution, with the withdrawal syndrome peaking early and subsequently receding. The nine people in the primary abstract and seven plotted are different analysis denominators in one study family. The 1988 figure compares withdrawal days with the preceding caffeinated period.

Claim 0121. This is evidence that short-term caffeine withdrawal can alter perceived fatigue in heavy coffee users. Both conditions used coffee, the original full methods are still needed for brew and measurement details, and the population did not comprise Parkinson patients. Keep the result as context for acute comparisons after abstention.
