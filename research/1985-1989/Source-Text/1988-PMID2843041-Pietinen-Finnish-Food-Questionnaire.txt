Title: Reproducibility and validity of dietary assessment instruments II. A qualitative food frequency questionnaire
PMID: 2843041
DOI: 10.1093/oxfordjournals.aje.a115014
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2843041/
Publisher: https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.aje.a115014
Publication: 1988-09
Coverage: Original structured bibliographic record and author abstract. Full questionnaire and article body remain open.

Pietinen and colleagues assessed a 44-item questionnaire in 297 Finnish men aged 55–69. Repeat questionnaires in 107 men gave intraclass correlations of 0.52 to 0.85 across selected nutrient estimates. The validation group of 190 compared questionnaire estimates with 12 two-day records. The reported correlations ranged from 0.33 to 0.68 for the selected nutrients. This was a nutrient instrument study without a Parkinson or drooling outcome.

Aho and colleagues cite this work as a basis for their 2019 instrument, which they describe as 163 items. The different item counts establish that the cited paper cannot supply the later instrument's exact food list. Whether the 2019 survey separately recorded raw fig remains open.
