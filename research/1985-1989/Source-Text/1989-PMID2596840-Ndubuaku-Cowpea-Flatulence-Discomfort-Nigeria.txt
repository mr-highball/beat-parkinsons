Title: Flatulence and other discomforts associated with consumption of cowpea (Vigna unguiculata).
PMID: 2596840
DOI: 10.1016/0195-6663(89)90010-x
First published: 1989
Authors: Ndubuaku VO, Uwaegbute AC, Nnanyelugo DO
Journal: Appetite
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2596840/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Two-part Nigerian study (Ndubuaku, Uwaegbute & Nnanyelugo, 1989, Appetite) combining a questionnaire of 448 families (28% never experienced flatulence) and a non-placebo-controlled crossover feeding 40 adults pre-selected for serious cowpea abdominal discomfort eight cowpea cooking methods across three dinners each. Mild abdominal discomfort occurred in 72.5% after atmospheric-cooked undehulled cowpeas, 42.5% after atmospheric dehulled cowpeas, and 12.5% after pressure-cooked dehulled cowpeas. Indigestion, diarrhea, flatulence, constipation, and sleepiness were listed among complaints, but per-symptom incidence rates, severity scores, and non-cowpea comparators were not reported. Limits: pre-selected intolerant sample without non-cowpea control or quantified constipation/sleepiness outcomes.
