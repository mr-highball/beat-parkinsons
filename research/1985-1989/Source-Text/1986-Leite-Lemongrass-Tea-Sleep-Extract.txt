Title: Pharmacology of lemongrass (Cymbopogon citratus Stapf) III. Assessment of eventual toxic, hypnotic and anxiolytic effects on humans
Publication: Leite and colleagues, July 1986, Journal of Ethnopharmacology 17(1):75–83
Source: https://eurekamag.com/research/001/653/001653609.pdf
Coverage: factual extract of the accessible primary source

Fifty healthy volunteers drank two preparations of Cymbopogon citratus dried leaf tea and a placebo on separate nights. The researchers issued 4 g sachets for 200 mL tea, and one tea condition was identified in advance while the other active tea and placebo were coded. Their Table 3 includes dream recall, nightmares, sleep onset, sleep interruptions and morning experience. They reported no differences across the three conditions, including among seven participants with previous sleep difficulties. Dream recall was a questionnaire response without bed-partner, motor or polysomnographic observation. Separate uncontrolled safety sets documented self-reported somnolence (5/11) and polyuria (4/11) after one dose, and one report of somnolence with dizziness among eight volunteers after daily use. These adverse symptom sets do not measure validated daytime sleepiness, sudden urinary urgency, falls or Parkinson outcomes.
