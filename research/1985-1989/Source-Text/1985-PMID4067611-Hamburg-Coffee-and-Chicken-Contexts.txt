Text coverage: Selected original conference members verified directly against their printed pages

Abstract 06.21.06, page 93. Dalal and colleagues report a prospective hospital-based stroke case-control comparison including 268 patients younger than 45 with recent stroke, 262 older stroke patients and 305 non-stroke controls. Tea and coffee are grouped among candidate factors. The printed abstract gives no separate coffee estimate, preparation grade or fatigue outcome. The matching authors published later papers on younger stroke cohorts. Overlap with this case-control analysis remains unresolved.

Abstract 15.21.07, page 235. Vizioli, Di Cerbo and Vigliotti describe five members in two generations with movement-triggered choreoathetosis. Coffee and alcohol are mentioned in the differential description of the other paroxysmal dystonic presentation. The abstract does not report coffee-triggered attacks among the five members or a result for Parkinson disease.

Abstract 07.09.01, page 97. Isonishi and colleagues study chick breast and leg muscle myosin across development. They report laboratory ATPase comparisons involving muscle samples. This is a materials comparison, with no dietary chicken exposure.

Abstract 11.30.11, page 166. Airaksinen and Partanen describe a human muscle-biopsy taurine comparison and cite a prior 25-fold difference between white and red chicken muscle fibres. The chicken comparison is a cited tissue measure and is separate from the human biopsy data. See PMID 4059221 for the cited chapter identity.

Abstract 17.07.04, page 257. Mizuno and Ohta describe enzyme markers in pectoral muscle of dystrophic and control chickens. Related original articles report overlapping investigators, muscle preparations and age ranges. Cohort independence is unresolved. The reports concern avian disease and tissue biochemistry rather than poached chicken intake or Parkinson fatigue.

Abstract 05.20.07, page 70. Micieli and colleagues describe autonomic measurements in untreated people with Parkinson disease and compare features with two autonomic-failure presentations. They report drops in measured blood pressure after meals. The abstract does not assign a named food or report falls as events. The investigators' 1987 report PMID 3822130 supplies cohort numbers. The conference and later cohort linkage remains unverified.

Abstract 05.09.04, page 64. Finkelstein, Bental and Gilad describe rat hippocampal cholinergic measurements after stress and after intraperitoneal corticosterone in peanut oil. The oil is a drug carrier. The original poster does not report a separately quantified oil effect. The later paper PMID 2998557 identifies vehicle controls in a related experiment, with a different hormone dose.

The NLM bibliographic title uses XIIIth while the publisher displays XIIth. Identifier, location, dates, volume and 306-page scope agree.
