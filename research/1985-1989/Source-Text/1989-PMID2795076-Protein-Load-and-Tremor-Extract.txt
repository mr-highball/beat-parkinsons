
Title: The effects of oral protein on the absorption of intraduodenal levodopa and motor performance
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1031741/
Publisher PDF: https://jnnp.bmj.com/content/jnnp/52/9/1063.full.pdf
First published: 1989-09
PMID: 2795076
PMCID: PMC1031741
DOI: 10.1136/jnnp.52.9.1063
Text coverage: original methods and results, with visual inspection of PMC page scans for pages 1064–1065.

Four people with idiopathic Parkinson disease and levodopa-related motor fluctuations received steady intestinal levodopa infusion. Three received a revised protein challenge following a smaller challenge and different benserazide schedule in the first participant. Study staff measured walking and tapping at half-hourly visits and also rated tremor and dyskinesia. The scale definitions describe severity without naming a rest, postural or action condition.

On the original page images, the separate tremor strip in Figure 2 shows tremor reappearing after the protein drink in participant 2. Figure 3 also shows a later interval of tremor for participant 4 after the drink. Figure 1 shows no analogous return around the lower challenges in participant 1, with tremor after the infusion stops. Participant 3 is not among the three figures. The figure strips have no separate rest-tremor label or numeric axis. The before-and-after table reports biochemical, tapping and walking values, with no numeric tremor comparison. The source identifies a medication and dietary protein interaction context, without a baked turkey comparison.
