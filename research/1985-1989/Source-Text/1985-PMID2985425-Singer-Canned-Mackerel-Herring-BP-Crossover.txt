Title: Blood pressure lowering effect of eicosapentaenoic acid-rich diet in normotensive, hypertensive and hyperlipemic subjects.
PMID: 2985425
DOI: 10.1007/BF01966147
First published: 1985
Authors: Singer P, Wirth M, Gödicke W, Heine H
Journal: Experientia
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2985425/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 5 review).

Primary Abstract:
A mackerel diet or a herring diet in which two cans of fish fillet were consumed daily over 2 weeks within a prescribed regimen, in a crossover design, were given to 15 normotensive volunteers, 14 patients with mild essential hypertension and eight patients with type IV and V hyperlipoproteinemia (HLP). In normotensives a markedly lower systolic and diastolic blood pressure at the end of the period on the mackerel diet could be observed, whereas in hypertensive and hyperlipemic subjects only systolic blood pressure was significantly decreased. After the herring diet, which served as control, changes in blood pressure were of a minor degree.

Verified Round 5 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Unblinded 2-wk dietary crossover studies (Experientia 1985; expanding Singer 1983 PMID:6316995) providing 2 cans/day commercial mackerel fillet in tomato pulp vs 2 cans/day herring in 15 normotensives, 14 mild essential hypertensives, and 8 hyperlipemics. Canned mackerel lowered resting SBP (and DBP in normotensives) more than herring. Unblinded small studies, conflicted by von Houwelingen 1987 (n=84 null), and did not assess postural BP drop or orthostatic lightheadedness.
