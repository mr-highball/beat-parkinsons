Title: Effect of a moderate fish intake on blood pressure, bleeding time, hematology, and clinical chemistry in healthy males.
PMID: 3630961
DOI: 10.1093/ajcn/46.3.424
First published: 1987
Authors: von Houwelingen R, Nordøy A, van der Beek E, Houtsmuller U, de Metz M, Hornstra G
Journal: The American journal of clinical nutrition
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3630961/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 5 review).

Primary Abstract:
This paper describes the outline and first results of an international study to investigate the effect of a reasonable amount of dietary fish on some aspects of cardiovascular risk. In Maastricht and Zeist, The Netherlands, and Tromsø, Norway, healthy male volunteers were given a dietary supplement consisting of 100 g/d of mackerel or meat for a 6-wk period. Compliance was monitored on the basis of the urinary excretion of lithium, which was added to the supplements. Average compliance was approximately 80% and this decreased slightly in time. Systolic blood pressure decreased in both groups to a comparable degree; consequently no specific effect of the fish supplement was observed. The fish supplement significantly prolonged bleeding times. Hematology was hardly affected but platelet counts decreased significantly. No indications were obtained for adverse effects of the fish supplement.

Verified Round 5 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
International multicenter controlled trial (Am J Clin Nutr 1987; Maastricht, Zeist, Tromso) randomizing n=84 healthy men to 100-135 g/d canned mackerel paste vs meat paste for 6 wk (~80% compliance via lithium marker). Systolic blood pressure decreased comparably in both arms with NO specific BP-lowering effect of mackerel; bleeding time increased and platelet counts fell slightly. Direct multicenter counterweight to Singer 1983-1990 BP reports; orthostatic BP delta and lightheadedness were not assessed.
