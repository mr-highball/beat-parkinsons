Title: Influence of food intake on the enantiomeric composition of urinary salsolinol in man
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2754429/
PMID: 2754429
DOI: 10.1007/BF01247112
Publication year: 1989
Text coverage: Paraphrased original NLM abstract. Complete main text remains open.

The investigators measured the two forms of salsolinol in urine before and after six healthy participants ate dried banana. The S form appeared after eating in both collection intervals for all six, while it appeared in three before eating and only during the later interval. The paper reports that the combined urinary recovery over 24 hours represented 0.75% of the amount supplied under the study conditions. The authors also note possible alcohol intake as a contributor to the baseline results.

This comparison concerns dried banana and a urinary biomarker. The sample lacks a Parkinson clinical cohort and the investigators did not report visual episode frequency. It shows that the measured biomarker can follow a particular food exposure, without establishing the food's clinical effect.
