# Food and Nutrition for 1985–1989

### 1987 — Reported coffee and acute oral caffeine in tremor

Koller, Cone and Herbster reported that 6% of participants with Parkinson’s disease perceived worse tremor after coffee. The proportions were 8% in essential tremor and 2% in controls. In a separate formal test, a single oral dose of 325 mg caffeine did not increase parkinsonian tremor at one, two or three hours in the PubMed abstract. The public abstract does not identify the administered formulation, a coffee preparation, Parkinson subgroup denominators, or an effect estimate for the formal test. The publisher landing page repeats the three-hour time point, so the timing here follows the PubMed record. The full report requires access. No fatigue outcome is reported in the abstract. [Primary abstract, PMID 3796831](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3796831/) · [Publisher record, January 1987](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.37.1.169)

### 1987 — Supine and orthostatic blood pressure around meals

Micieli and colleagues monitored 13 untreated people with Parkinson’s disease and 11 age-matched controls. Seven patients had a post-meal supine systolic drop greater than the controls and exceeding 20% of their pre-meal values. Eight patients had orthostatic hypotension on morning tilt testing and in the post-meal phase. The abstract reports blood pressure, without a specified food or a rating of orthostatic lightheadedness. [Primary abstract, March 1987](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3822130/)

### 1987 — Amino acids, daytime protein, and fluctuation trade-offs

Pincus and Barry followed seven people with severe levodopa fluctuations across diets while levodopa doses were held steady. Higher protein tracked worse mobility despite higher plasma levodopa, while lower daytime amino acid levels tracked greater mobility and substantial dyskinesia. This physiologic study involved a small, selected group. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3632370/)

### 1987 — Corn oil in an enteral feeding formula

Kato and colleagues evaluated a multicomponent enteral formula in 30 oral-surgery and related inpatients. The formulation contained corn oil as a source of essential fatty acids. One patient with a parkinsonian syndrome had painful stomatitis that prevented oral intake. Seven of 30 patients developed diarrhea during enteral feeding. The paper gives no isolated oil contrast or Parkinson-specific estimates for constipation or pain, and the patient’s underlying syndrome was not specified as idiopathic Parkinson’s disease. [Primary report, pp. 93 and 97](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsotp1982/6/2/6_2_92/_pdf)

### 1988 — Evening protein redistribution in selected off periods

Pincus and Barry observed improved responsiveness to levodopa in 14 of 16 people with previously drug-resistant off periods after shifting protein to the evening. Ten continued the diet during follow-up. The series addressed treatment timing in a selected subgroup and lacked a parallel comparison. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3126411/)

### 1988 — Recalled early-adult diet in affected and unaffected siblings

Golbe and colleagues compared 81 people with Parkinson’s disease and their unaffected same-sex siblings. Nuts, salad oil or dressing, and plums were inversely associated with case status. None of the items was positively associated. This small retrospective comparison tested multiple foods, and vitamin E was a hypothesis about the pattern. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3196195/)

### 1989 — Ferrous sulfate and levodopa absorption in healthy participants

Campbell and Hasinoff studied eight people without Parkinson’s disease in a randomized crossover comparison. Taking ferrous sulfate with levodopa reduced peak levodopa levels and total exposure. The investigators proposed chelation as a mechanism. Clinical effects in people with Parkinson’s disease were still uncertain. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2920496/)

### 1989 — Blinded dietary protein crossover and motor performance

Tsui and colleagues randomized ten people with Parkinson’s to one week each of higher- and lower-protein diets with matching appearance and taste. Medications stayed stable. A blinded physician measured rigidity, movement velocity, and pegboard performance, and participants recorded hourly motor fluctuations. The authors reported better overall motor performance and fewer off hours with lower protein. Blood levodopa peaks were higher in three participants during the higher-protein period despite poorer clinical performance. The published abstract does not identify an effect from an individual food. [Original publication, PMID 2494567](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2494567/)

### 1989 — Clermont-Ferrand thesis on diet and levodopa motor fluctuations

The French doctoral thesis catalog identifies Christine Bellonnet’s 1989 medical thesis on dietary influence on motor fluctuations in levodopa-treated Parkinson patients. Michel Tournilhac supervised the work at Clermont-Ferrand 1. The national thesis number is 1989CLF13044 and the Sudoc record is 043189318. The national export gives January 1, and the original bibliographic record verifies only the year. The record has no public abstract or online thesis. The holdings service lists copies at the Clermont-Ferrand health library and the Centre Technique du Livre de l’Enseignement Supérieur. Diet, sample, timing and outcomes require the original text.
