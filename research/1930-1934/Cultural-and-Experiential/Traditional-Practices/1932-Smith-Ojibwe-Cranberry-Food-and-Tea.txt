Reported direction: Tea use for mild nausea was reported. The food passage does not specify a symptom effect.

Smith records small cranberry (Vaccinium oxycoccos) as a wild food of the Lac du Flambeau and Pillager Ojibwe and describes its use in a sauce. The separate medicinal entry gives cranberry tea as a preparation used when someone feels mildly nauseated. It does not unambiguously specify which part of the plant was used or report a measured response. A nearby sentence about drying berries refers to blueberries and should not be attributed to cranberry.

The ethnographic record documents named communities and practices as described by an outside collector. It contains no Parkinson diagnosis, dried-cranberry depression outcome or efficacy test. The distinct modern NATIFS fact sheet directly documents drying cranberries for storage.

Authenticity: Original published ethnobotany, verified against the digitized printed pages.
Efficacy evidence: Attributed use without a comparative outcome.
Related research: Cross-reference the NATIFS cranberry preservation source, CRANMOOD (PMID 42134099), and the cranberry traditional urinary-use review.
Access and follow-up: Ask which plant part was prepared only if a documented source establishes it.
