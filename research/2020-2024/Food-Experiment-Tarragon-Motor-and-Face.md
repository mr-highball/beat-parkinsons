# Tarragon motor and facial outcome follow-up

## Posted blood pressure values for the tarragon capsule trial

Official results first posted October 8, 2020. At week 12 the registry gives mean systolic values of 113 ± 11 mmHg for extract and 125 ± 8 mmHg for placebo. The reported p values are explicitly respective within-arm baseline-to-endpoint comparisons (p=.017 and .082), without a baseline-adjusted between-arm estimate. The posture was not described in the outcome field, and orthostatic lightheadedness was unmeasured. The registry also lists constipation among nonserious adverse events in 1 of 12 extract participants and 2 of 12 placebo participants. These data update the same study family as the 2016 report.

[Original 2020 record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02330341?tab=results) · [Local text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT02330341-results.txt)

## Registered Russian tarragon bar palatability and absorption study

The registry first posted November 23, 2021. The protocol tested a bar with a soy-protein Russian tarragon complex for palatability and assayed a tarragon-associated compound in plasma. The registered intervention concerns a formulated bar, and the protocol does not collect clinical smell, facial expression or walking outcomes.

[Original 2021 record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05130593) · [Local text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT05130593.txt)

## Fresh and dried French tarragon volatile profiles

Hazarika and colleagues compared freshly harvested leaves of two French tarragon cultivars with multiple drying and freezing methods. Freezing and shade drying retained more of the measured volatile material, and lyophilization shifted the relative compound profile, including a lower estragole proportion. The study distinguishes the analytical exposures hidden by a generic dried-leaf label. Its methods include instrumental taste measurements. It does not study facial expressiveness or speech.

[Original 2022 record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lwt.2022.114006) · [Local text](Source-Text/2022-Hazarika-Fresh-and-Dried-Tarragon-Extract.txt)

## Oral estragole and gross coordination in intact mice

The primary study compared oral isolated estragole or an estragole inclusion complex with controls. The authors reported similar rotarod retention and fall counts, and no detected overall change in the selected open-field measures in these healthy mice. The exposure, species and design differ from the earlier injected whole tarragon oil experiments. The test provides no human freezing frequency or clinical dystonia assessment.

[Original 2022 record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9654024/) · [Local text](Source-Text/PMC9654024.txt)

## Palatability and plasma results for the Russian tarragon bar

Registry results first posted November 18, 2023. Ten participants in the palatability phase gave a product-smell rating of 59.5 ± 19.2 and taste rating of 27.8 ± 24.4 on 0–100 scales (mean ± SD). Ten in the exposure phase had their plasma DMC-2 measured, with a registered 30-minute mean of 0.094 ± 0.026 µg/mL versus 0.05 ± 0.00 at baseline. These outcomes measure one product's acceptability and intake exposure. They do not grade facial expression, vocal intelligibility or Parkinson motor impairment.

[Original 2023 record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05130593?tab=results) · [Local text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT05130593-results.txt)
