# Kiwifruit, blackberry and raspberry studies

## Kiwi and raspberry in a Parkinson diet and symptom cohort

Hegelmaier and colleagues (February 6, 2020) list kiwi and raspberry in Table 3 among ingredients used in the two-week ovo-lacto-vegetarian diet. UPDRS III covered gait and postural stability, among other motor items. The paper and supplement report composite motor scores, including lower pooled scores at one year and a separate 14-day composite comparison. Food amounts, raw or frozen status, and symptom item estimates are unavailable. All patients received the diet, and some received bowel cleansing. The paper names orthostatic dysregulation as a possible enema side effect, without reporting an observed raspberry effect. [Local primary text, Methods and Table 3](Source-Text/PMC7072275.txt#L123) · [Primary report and supplement listing](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4409/9/2/376)

Fox and colleagues’ 2022 study linked the MIND questionnaire’s generic berry item to lower total PRO-PD symptom burden (coefficient −119, 95% CI −184 to −54.4, Holm-adjusted p = .03). Individual anxiety and other symptom analyses used total diet scores. The generic berry question and aggregate score do not identify individual fruits, preparations or symptom-specific estimates. The same study’s raw clinical data are available only under the stated data access process. [Local primary text](Source-Text/PMC9739738.txt#L117) · [Local Table S4](Source-Text/PMC9739738-Supplement.txt#L42)

## Hardy kiwi juice and movement after MPTP

Kitamura and colleagues first published April 2, 2021. Mice received Actinidia arguta (hardy kiwi or sarunashi) juice before exposure to MPTP. The primary abstract reports alleviated motor impairment and preserved tyrosine hydroxylase measures. The publisher preview names pole, beam-walk and catalepsy tests 22 days after exposure. A same-lab 2022 conference report additionally names rotarod. It compares impaired MPTP plus water mice with negative controls and reports no clear motor impairment for MPTP plus juice versus negative controls. The conference comparison gives no direct estimates between the two MPTP arms or confirmation that its cohort is independent of the paper. Hardy kiwi juice and animal motor tests cannot isolate effects of raw common kiwifruit on human freezing or postural instability. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33819745/) · [Publisher preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0006291X21005143) · [2022 primary conference report](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpssuppl/95/0/95_1-P-065/_pdf)

## Green kiwi juice and reduced spontaneous activity in mice

Satish and colleagues (July 15, 2022) extracted juice from fresh Actinidia deliciosa flesh and administered it orally to Swiss mice without a Parkinson model. With six mice per group, at 60 minutes the two juice groups averaged 159.8 and 140.5 movement counts compared with 204.5 for saline (p < .01 and p < .001). Pentobarbital sleep lasted longer after the juice. Table 3 marks post-treatment rotarod fall times of 34.17 and 34.33 seconds versus 35.50 for saline as p < .05, although the discussion claims absence of muscle relaxation. These endpoints and preparation differ from human gait freezing and postural instability after eating raw kiwi. [Primary full report, Tables 1–3](https://globalresearchonline.net/ijpsrr/v75-1/09.pdf)

## Fresh and dried kiwi and waking alertness

Kanon and colleagues’ 24-person crossover used two fresh Hayward kiwifruits, freeze-dried powder equivalent to two fruits including skin, or water with a standardized evening meal in healthy men. Fresh fruit yielded waking alertness scores of 53.1 versus 44.2 for control (post hoc p = .082). Freeze-dried fruit yielded 57.1 (p = .018). The 2022 abstract, 2023 article and 2024 dissertation report this same crossover. Waking alertness was self-rated and the trial did not measure attention task performance. The dissertation also reports separate pig studies of biochemical and gut outcomes. [Local primary article, Figure 3](Source-Text/PMC10043399.txt) · [ANZCTR registration](https://anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=380481) · [2022 abstract](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-9992/9/1/30) · [2024 dissertation](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/items/fde72c35-0032-46b2-8b80-49b187ef94d0)

## Separate dried kiwi cognitive assessment registration

ACTRN12621001685808, first posted December 9, 2021, planned a separate three-period crossover in men with poor sleep. Freeze-dried green kiwi powder was to be compared with sugar and flavor controls, with morning computerized COMPASS testing after an evening dose. The public status remains not yet recruiting and the record provides no results. [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/ACTRN12621001685808.txt) · [Official registry record](https://anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=382485)

## Frozen raspberries and seated blood pressure

Franck and colleagues first published December 17, 2020. They allocated 59 adults at risk for metabolic syndrome to 280 g/day of frozen raspberries or their usual diet. Twenty-four participants per arm contributed seated blood-pressure data. The abstract summarizes blood pressure as unchanged. Table 4 gives a systolic time-by-treatment interaction of p = .03 and a treatment main effect of p = .37. Raspberry and control systolic means were 112.8 and 110.8 mmHg at baseline, and 110.9 and 113.1 at eight weeks. The diastolic interaction had p = .34. No raspberry-related adverse events were reported. Measurements were taken seated after rest, without an orthostatic assessment. Later metabolomic publications use the same participants. [Local primary article, Methods and Table 4](Source-Text/PMC7767072.txt#L248) · [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7767072/)

## Red and yellow raspberry in a mouse dementia model

Alonso Torrens and colleagues first published November 30, 2022. They compared freeze-dried red or yellow Rubus idaeus with control feed for 24 weeks in wild-type and APP/PS-1 mice. Supplementary Figure S9 provides open-field behavior. Grooming and defecation, used as anxiety-related measures, showed no clear diet effects. Neither object recognition nor the reported spatial memory analysis demonstrated a clear benefit. The model also did not show a clear genotype difference in object recognition. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/09637486.2022.2141209) · [Institutional copy](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/193948/)

## Blackberry intake and general sleep difficulty

The December 15, 2023 NHANES analysis included 29,217 adults and linked diet recalls to whether respondents had ever told a clinician they had trouble sleeping. The fully adjusted odds ratio for blackberry consumers was 0.63 (95% CI 0.40–0.97, p = .036). Food codes included blackberry ingredients in recipes. USDA data distinguish raw and frozen blackberry codes, but the report pooled forms and did not measure dream enactment or identify a Parkinson group. Public recall data can refine the food form, while the analyzed sleep field does not identify the frozen claim’s outcome. [Local primary text, Methods and Table 5](Source-Text/PMC10745662.txt#L89) · [Primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10745662/) · [FDA/USDA preparation code examples](https://www.fda.gov/media/124721/download)

## Raspberry oral challenge and respiratory reaction

Akamatsu and colleagues first published August 1, 2023. A patient developed cough, wheeze, throat discomfort and tachycardia after an open challenge with raspberry, despite negative prick-to-prick testing. Blood pressure remained normal during the reported episode. The paper’s review of earlier original cases includes anaphylactic shock after raspberry ingestion, documented separately in 2014. These are allergic reactions, without a Parkinson orthostatic or anxiety endpoint. [Primary case and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1002/cia2.12317) · [Earlier case](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Kiwi-and-Caneberries.md#oral-allergy-cases-after-kiwifruit-and-raspberry)

## Parkinson Mediterranean trial diet recalls and anxiety fields

Rusch and colleagues’ 2024 trial reports the use of ASA24 food recalls and exploratory Hamilton anxiety ratings. It lists Table S5 for anxiety and depression scores by arm and visit. The main report does not name raspberry, blackberry or kiwi as exposures. The article says the authors and University of Florida will make raw data available on request. A food-specific contrast would require checking actual recall and symptom fields, and the randomization applied to the whole diet. [Local primary text, Methods and data statement](Source-Text/PMC11396875.txt#L93) · [Primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11396875/)

## Massey thesis and the separate kiwi attention protocol

2024. Alexander Putra Kanon’s Massey University thesis describes the fresh and freeze-dried kiwi comparison in Chapter 6 under ACTRN12621000046808. It represents the same cohort as the 2023 article and reports subjective waking alertness. The searchable thesis gave no COMPASS result or reference to the separately registered ACTRN12621001685808. Its official entry was first posted and last updated on December 9, 2021, and still listed anticipated dates and no results on review. The lack of a public update leaves the study’s progress uncertain. [Institutional thesis](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/items/fde72c35-0032-46b2-8b80-49b187ef94d0)

## Stomatitis and salivation after eating kiwi

Zang and colleagues reported a 22-year-old who described painful stomatitis, increased saliva, fatigue and dizziness after eating kiwi. A clinician inferred allergic contact stomatitis. Kiwi avoidance and multiple treatments began together. ## Raspberry consumers in a cardiac blood pressure survey

Naqvi and colleagues report 73 survey responses among 100 cardiac patients. Table 3 lists five raspberry consumers. Two were prehypertensive and three hypertensive, and the comparison reports p = .710. The survey gives no isolated preparation, orthostatic assessment or treatment comparison. The same report includes separate laboratory fruit extracts that do not describe what the survey patients consumed. ## Kiwi extract mouse memory report with inconsistent methods

Kaur and colleagues’ 2024 article describes dried kiwi extract with a scopolamine model, water-maze and other behavioral measures. Methods describe a six-day intervention and animal ethics approval. Figure 1 gives a different 21-day schedule and dose labels, while the final ethics section denies animal or human experiments. The original leaves group-level data and procedures unresolved. The published account supplies no patient objective attention measure or Parkinson comparison. ## Cultivar meal and brain bioamines in the Kanon thesis

Massey made the 2024 thesis available on November 20, 2024. Chapter 5 describes 48 growing male pigs and a final meal with soda bread and green Hayward or gold Hort16A kiwifruit. Each cultivar-by-time group contains six pigs, with blood and brain measurements at 20 minutes, one, two and five hours. Tissue samples are terminal, so comparisons across times are between animals. Table 5.1 documents differences in fiber, vitamin C and polyphenols as well as actinidin. Chapter 3 reports an 18-animal antioxidant analysis of material from the same parent experiment. Chapter 5 matches the 2026 article's sample, cultivars, time points and sampled brain regions. Neither analysis measured cognitive attention or modeled Parkinson disease.
