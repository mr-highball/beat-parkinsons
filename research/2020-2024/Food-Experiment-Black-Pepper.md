# Black pepper aroma and piperine in swallowing research

## Black pepper odor and carbonated water in healthy adults

Won and colleagues compared four counterbalanced water-swallow conditions in 20 healthy adults. Mean muscle activity did not differ between conditions. Peak activity was higher in the carbonated conditions, with no detected immediate effect of black pepper oil aroma. The main text is inconsistent in task numbering. Exposure names in the abstract and results identify the conditions. No Parkinson participants were studied. [2020 primary report](https://oak.go.kr/central/journallist/journaldetail.do?article_seq=22908)

## Black pepper patches and mixed swallowing assessments

Sameshima reported six patch recipients in a leprosy sanatorium. Table 4a gives complete pre/post swallowing scores for four recipients. Scores changed from 167 to 167, 159 to 151, 102 to 107, and 137 to 135. The remaining two lack a complete comparison. The person with multiple system atrophy appears in the separate rotigotine tables, with no post-treatment swallowing score. The patch report supplies no Parkinson subgroup or isolated oral exposure. [2021 foundation report, PDF p5](https://www.shf.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/4b6535a9b013d9a7fc0f53d99b4fd84c.pdf#page=5)

## Whole pepper aroma and increased drooling during combined care

Ichihara and Owa presented two women in their eighties who smelled whole peppercorns held in gauze before meals. The report gives no Parkinson diagnosis. Capsaicin oral film was added later. In one participant, repeated saliva swallow counts were 2, 3, 4 and 3 over four assessments. Mealtime coughing varied. The team stopped the combined intervention for this participant after drooling increased. The abstract does not identify which exposure contributed. [2022 proceedings, PDF p70](https://teamcare-society.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/%E3%83%81%E3%83%BC%E3%83%A0%E3%82%B1%E3%82%A2%E5%AD%A6%E4%BC%9A2022-%E5%86%8A%E5%AD%90%E3%83%87%E3%83%BC%E3%82%BF-0909%E6%9C%80%E6%96%B0.pdf#page=70)

## Registered piperine and aroma studies

NCT05735626, posted February 21, 2023, planned to test piperine with stimulation in poststroke dysphagia and excludes neurodegenerative disorders. NCT05958173, posted July 24, 2023, compares isolated piperine, capsaicin and placebo in older adults. It names no Parkinson subgroup. Both registry records have unknown recruitment status and no posted results in the review snapshot. [Stroke trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05735626) · [Older-adult trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05958173)

UMIN000054347 was first disclosed May 15, 2024. The registry records 30 healthy young adults and compares aromas including black pepper, ginger and lavender against water. Outcomes include salivary volume and repeated saliva swallowing. Dysphagia is an exclusion. The registry indicates results were published and supplies no results values or citation. [UMIN record](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000061933)

Kumamoto Health Science University identifies the same Japanese title under ethics approval 23015 and applicant Megumi Miyamoto. The Ministry of Health mirror confirms 50 planned participants, distinct from the 30 enrolled in UMIN. It describes 60-second odor exposures and gives no outcomes. [University ethics record, p. 1](https://www.kumamoto-hsu.ac.jp/hsu-info/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/R05rinri-1.pdf#page=1) · [Ministry registry mirror](https://rctportal.mhlw.go.jp/detail/um?trial_id=UMIN000054347)

The 2025 and 2026 Japanese swallowing-society abstract books, 2024–2026 national speech-language meeting title lists and university output reports did not identify the result. The 2024 society abstract body and Kyushu regional proceedings remain access gaps. A 2021 abstract by the same investigator used a food-preference questionnaire in 40 healthy older adults under ethics approval 18048 and gave no pepper or aroma exposure.
