# Pea protein breakfasts and cognitive outcome trails

## Pea-protein breakfast outcome report and open task results

The 2021 Griffith and Leidy conference abstract describes 37 participants and breakfast doses and comparators matching registered NCT03595436 methods. The original abstract provides appetite and food intake outcomes. It contains no executive-function result. Treat the publication as a likely companion pending full study linkage and preserve the cognitive-reporting trail. [original conference abstract and registry comparison](https://doi.org/10.1093/cdn/nzab038_021) · [Local text](Source-Text/2021-Griffith-Pea-Protein-Breakfast-Abstract-Extract.txt)

## Separate breakfast protein isolate crossover

Braden and colleagues first published April 6, 2023, PMID 37030593, for NCT03154606. Thirty-two healthy adults received pea, soy, or whey isolates or micellar casein in 250 kcal drinks. Reported four-hour appetite and peptide YY favored pea versus soy, and food intake comparisons were inconclusive. The publication concerns intake and satiety. Its linked registration and assigned doses distinguish it from NCT03595436, whose cognitive outcomes remain unavailable. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37030593/) · [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03154606)

## Pea protein comparator in the phospholipid cognition trial

NCT06107075 first posted October 30, 2023. The completed Northumbria study (66CH1) reports 119 actual enrollees aged 25–49. The study kept an older target of 220 in its narrative. Twelve weeks of phospholipid-rich whey protein were compared with a macronutrient-matched control supplying approximately 40 g of pea protein per day. The protocol makes memory accuracy primary and gives secondary executive accuracy, interference and working-memory measurements at baseline, six and twelve weeks. The June 2026 registry lists no results, documents or publications. A distinct registration from the same group, NCT06109922 (66CH2), first posted October 31, 2023, uses the same pea-protein comparator and emphasizes stress reactivity. It reports 150 actual enrollees and no posted results. Participant overlap is unresolved. Both studies lack a cooked whole-pea arm or Parkinson cohort. [Official cognition registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06107075) · [Official stress registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06109922)
