# Rosemary tea, extract hydration and clinical outcomes

## Blood marker changes after dried rosemary tea

Achour and colleagues’ first online report describes a 22-volunteer pre/post pilot with tea made from dried rosemary. The author abstract gives higher plasma BDNF and TNF-alpha after the intake period, with no depression scale or concurrent control described. A blood marker cannot estimate depressive symptom severity. ## Rosemary beverage and event-related attention

Riby and colleagues’ 2023 study assigned 48 healthy people to a rosemary-extract and hydrolat drink or water and studied visual-task performance and brain responses. The primary ERP report does not publish a depression outcome. Its participant summary, cognitive task and intervention match the same program’s later eye-blink and mood report very closely. Those linked reports do not establish separate cohorts. [2023 original article](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0286113) · [Later outcome report and comparison](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Rosemary-Preparation-and-Mood.md) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2023-Riby-Rosemary-Drink-and-ERP.txt)

## Botanical-blend trial registration and mood outcomes

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT05597189 on October 27, 2022, after the listed September 26 study start. The current record gives 135 enrolled and three intervention groups, with pain as primary and Beck depression among its secondary outcomes. It does not specify a depression cut point or report registry results. The later paper identifies the compound as standardized extracts of rosemary, ashwagandha and sesame. Its full-sample BDI-II comparison (123 analyzed) was inconclusive between groups (p = .149). A secondary baseline-restricted subgroup gave a between-group p = .016. The subgroup and the three-ingredient exposure preclude assigning a fresh rosemary effect. Table 8 gives an inconclusive PROMIS depression between-group p = .798. Its scores increase over time in all groups, while Methods describes higher scores as worse and Results calls the increases improvement. Direction on that measure requires a scoring clarification. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05597189) · [Original 2024 paper, Tables 8–10](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2024.1403108/full) · [Factual registration record](Source-Text/NCT05597189-Botanical-Blend-Trial.txt) · [Factual results record](Source-Text/2024-Perez-Pinero-Botanical-Blend-and-Depression.txt)

The authenticated supplemental tables give psychological quality of life and inflammatory biomarkers. WHOQOL-BREF psychological-domain scores gave a reported between-group p = .036, for a different outcome from depression. The two supplements give no PROMIS scoring clarification or evidence of BDI-II subgroup prespecification.
