# Seafood and Parkinson’s symptom evidence

## Purified DHA and rat stride length

Chitre and colleagues first published the full article on January 27, 2020 after a 2019 conference report. Rats given intraperitoneal purified DHA had longer stride than untreated 6-OHDA-lesioned rats (p < .001, groups n = 8 and 7). The study’s exposure, route and population differ from grilled trout in people with Parkinson’s. [Full primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7110909/) · [2019 first public report](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Trout-Sardine-Shrimp.md#early-report-of-purified-dha-and-rat-stride)

## Canned sardine feeding in older adults

Díaz-Rizzolo and colleagues randomized 152 older adults with prediabetes to standard dietary guidance with or without 200 g of sardines weekly, supplied canned in olive oil. The 2021 paper reports lower diabetes risk scores and insulin resistance in the sardine arm. It did not measure fatigue severity. The flowchart’s pooled reference to participants tiring of the intervention describes withdrawals. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33932804/) · [Publisher full text](https://www.clinicalnutritionjournal.com/article/S0261561421001539/pdf) · [Registration](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Trout-Sardine-Shrimp.md#canned-sardine-prevention-trial-registration)

## Marine omega-3 capsules and incident depression

The 2021 VITAL-DEP trial involved 18,353 older adults without clinically relevant depressive symptoms at baseline. Marine omega-3 capsules increased the risk of incident depression or clinically relevant symptoms relative to placebo (HR 1.13, 95% CI 1.01–1.26). The mean change on the PHQ-8 differed by 0.03 points (−0.01 to 0.07). Two smaller Parkinson’s trials with different populations and instruments reported favorable clinician-rated depression outcomes with supplements. [VITAL-DEP primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8693224/) · [2008 Parkinson’s trial](../2005-2009/Food-and-Nutrition.md#2008--fish-oil-for-depression-in-a-pilot-trial) · [2014 Parkinson’s trial](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md)

## Sardine oil and postpartum mood

The 2023 double-blind trial assigned 250 healthy Japanese first-time pregnant women to perilla oil or sardine oil. The separate historical control came from an earlier observational cohort. At one month postpartum, EPDS scores of at least 9 occurred in 22.3% with sardine oil, 12.0% with perilla oil, and 21.6% of the historical controls. The randomized comparison was inconclusive (p = .059). EPDS and K6 covered depression and general distress. The report gives no standalone anxiety outcome for baked sardines. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37892462/) · [Local CC BY article text](Source-Text/PMC10610328.txt)

## Fatigue in a canned sardine botulism case

In a Brazilian Amazon case series, a four-year-old boy had fatigue, weakness and gait impairment after eating canned sardines at school. The authors used the exposure history, clinical findings and electrophysiology to support the foodborne botulism diagnosis. The paper does not report laboratory confirmation for this child or a fatigue severity score. This is an adverse illness report involving suspected contamination and no Parkinson cohort. [Primary case series](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36580960/) · [Local CC BY article text](Source-Text/PMC9800156.txt)

## Dysarthria in a preserved sardine outbreak

Courtot-Melciolle and colleagues described eight intensive-care patients in the September 2023 Bordeaux botulism outbreak. The clinical table identifies dysarthria in four cases. Investigators confirmed the presence of botulinum neurotoxin in the home-preserved sardines and found laboratory evidence of botulism in several patients. The exposure was contaminated preserved fish. The patients did not undergo formal speech-intelligibility testing, and the report gives no grilled-sardine or Parkinson-specific estimate. [Primary outbreak investigation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37824251/) · [Local CC BY article text](Source-Text/PMC10571493.txt)

## Seafood intake and frailty components

The 2023 Korean Frailty and Aging Cohort analysis used 953 community-dwelling adults at baseline and 623 at four-year follow-up. Total seafood and fish intake were inversely associated with exhaustion, low grip strength and slow gait at follow-up. The shellfish category had no clear association with frailty. The adjusted analyses did not estimate changes in stride length, manual dexterity, or fall frequency for a specific species or preparation. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37706211/) · [Local CC BY article text](Source-Text/PMC10497173.txt)

## Whole trout and emotion study registration

NCT06721468 first posted on December 6, 2024. The project includes sensory testing of three strains of trout and interventions combining whole-trout exposure with education or child-centered messages in children and families. Registered outcomes include a change in emotion score at 12 weeks. The November 13, 2026 API snapshot reported recruiting and no posted results. The population is outside Parkinson’s and has no specified poached-trout depression comparison. [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06721468) · [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT06721468.txt)
