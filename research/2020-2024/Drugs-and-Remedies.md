# Drugs and Remedies

### 2020 — Opicapone formulation and meal timing in regulatory studies

The 2020 PMDA dossier describes a separate Japanese 12-person crossover, ONO-2370-03. The standard meal supplied at most 700 kcal, with at most 20% from fat. Compared with fasting, the fed-to-fasted ratio was 0.53 for peak concentration (90% CI 0.45–0.62) and 0.57 for total exposure (0.51–0.64). The study used healthy volunteers and reports no culinary ingredient comparison. The regulatory review also records that Parkinson efficacy study 301 did not specify meal separation, while study 302 did. Meal timing was not randomized between those trials. [Japanese applicant’s methods, pp. 99–100](https://www.pmda.go.jp/drugs/2020/P20200619001/180188000_30200AMX00487_K104_1.pdf) · [PMDA review, sections 6.1.1 and 6.R.2](https://www.pmda.go.jp/files/000243472.pdf) · [Japanese trial record](https://jrct.mhlw.go.jp/latest-detail/jRCT2080223528)

### 2020 — Isradipine in early Parkinson’s

A 36-month randomized trial found no slowing of measured clinical progression versus placebo at the tested dose. The study tested a specific calcium-channel blocker regimen. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32227247/)

### 2021 — Raising urate with inosine in SURE-PD3

The randomized phase 3 trial raised serum urate but found no slower clinical progression and stopped for futility. Kidney stones occurred more often with inosine. This directly tested the intervention motivated by earlier observational urate findings. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34519802/)

### 2022 — Cinpanemab and prasinezumab antibodies

Separate randomized phase 2 trials in early Parkinson’s found no significant primary clinical benefit from either anti-α-synuclein antibody versus placebo. Endpoint definitions and agents differ, and exploratory findings generated follow-up questions for prasinezumab. [Primary source](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2203395) [Related primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35921451/)

### 2024 — Lixisenatide phase 2 motor signal and adverse effects

A randomized trial of 156 people with early Parkinson’s reported less worsening in on-medication motor scores at 12 months with lixisenatide. Nausea and vomiting were frequent. Further testing is needed to determine durability and whether the effect reflects disease modification. [Primary source](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2312323)
