# Oral sesame oil and mood outcome comparisons

## Refined sesame and sunflower oil anxiety report

In the 2023 mood and fatigue report, Atefi and colleagues randomized 60 women with fatty liver disease to sesame or sunflower oil as part of calorie restriction for 12 weeks. Fifty-three completed. The original abstract concludes that anxiety decreased with sesame compared with sunflower. The anxiety p value of .011 appears in a separate results sentence. The accessible abstract does not identify which statistical contrast generated this p value or provide arm values, an adjusted difference, or its confidence interval. The matched 2022 reports identify both oils as refined and odorless. The registered metabolic trial and later paper share the same intervention, investigators, award, and participant flow, with 60 randomized and 53 analyzed. The mood article's inaccessible body leaves the registration identifier for that specific report unverified. The available 2020 registration does not list an anxiety endpoint. The original report provides a weak directional comparison for edible sesame oil in a separate clinical population. ## Depressive symptoms in the oral oil comparison

The same Atefi et al. abstract concludes that depressive symptoms decreased with sesame compared with sunflower oil. It gives p = .013 in the description of change after sesame and leaves the specific within- or between-group contrast unidentified. The arm changes, confidence interval and instrument remain inaccessible. Both arms received calorie-restriction guidance, both oils were refined in the matching trial reports, and women with fatty liver disease were studied. The Parkinson cannabinoid trial used sesame oil on both sides, precluding an isolated comparison. ## Sesame oil aroma in mouse stress experiments

The 2020 original stress experiment reports anxiety-related behavioral outcomes after inhalation of sesame oil aroma. The 2022 follow-up varied inhalation timing and examined volatile components, reporting a transient increase in open-arm exploration. These animal experiments concern aroma, timing and maze behaviors. They supply no dietary oil contrast. The earlier acute injection experiment found fewer open-arm entries and less open-arm time.
