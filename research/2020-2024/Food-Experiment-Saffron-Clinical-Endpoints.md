# Saffron registration endpoints and a flavored-salt study

## Tehran registry and the accessible motor total

The official [IRCT20090117001556N145 record](https://irct.ir/trial/65247) describes an eight-week, double-blind trial in PD with mild-to-moderate depression: 15 mg saffron capsules twice daily plus stable usual PD therapy versus placebo. Its primary registration names Hamilton Depression Rating Scale and MDS-UPDRS Part III at weeks 0, 4 and 8; the secondary-outcomes section is empty. The registry does not identify a Sargol grade or confirm a culinary ground-stigma preparation. It is already cataloged at `research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Registry-Followup.md#iranian-registration-of-the-tehran-saffron-capsule-trial`.

The related 2024 Lalezari publication, DOI 10.1016/j.hermed.2024.100968, reports 52 participants and a composite Part III interaction p=.424. It also lists other depression scales and Hoehn–Yahr as secondary measures in the abstract, despite the empty registry secondary section. No individual 3.1 speech or other item results are accessible in the abstract. Part III speech evaluates volume as well as other characteristics, so even an item score would not isolate voice volume; the total result is weaker still. The registration belongs to the same trial as the article.

## Isfahan registry and the planned MDS-UPDRS items

The official [IRCT20121216011763N61 record](https://irct.ir/trial/75378) describes 92 PD participants, 100 mg saffron daily versus 100 mg lactose for 12 weeks, and prespecified broad motor/nonmotor symptoms with MDS-UPDRS. It is already cataloged in `research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Registry-Followup.md#iranian-registration-of-the-saffron-powder-trial`. The later [2025 protocol](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2024-093167) specifies first-class Sargol powder from Mojtahedi, 100 mg tablet, and planned HPLC crocin checks before and after tablet preparation. Sargol is dried red stigma grade; powder in a fixed tablet is close to, but not identical to, unconstrained culinary use. Planned testing does not establish actual batch composition.

The protocol names all four MDS-UPDRS parts. The corresponding results paper (first online 24 December 2025, DOI 10.1039/D5FO01924A) reports broad outcomes in its abstract, including PDQ-39 mobility and daily-activity domains, but no accessible item-specific speech, drooling, smell or fall counts. A mobility-domain change is not a fall-frequency result. The full original table and public individual data were not retrieved. The registry and protocol share an identifier. The published regimen and sample size align, and the available report is treated as the same probable study family pending confirmation from the full manuscript.

## Orange and saffron flavored salt in a hyposmia taste study

Published 23 November 2022, [PMID 36501005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36501005/), DOI 10.3390/nu14234976; licensed source text is included in this period. The study compared salty taste judgments in 57 people with hyposmia and 91 controls using several salt solutions. One commercial preparation combined sea salt with orange and saffron extracts; other salts included different botanical ingredients. It evaluated salt taste intensity, pleasantness and familiarity, not the change in olfactory impairment following ingestion. Participants were grouped by hyposmia and COVID status, not a PD trial. The saffron effect cannot be isolated from orange and salt, and the exposure is not ground saffron. This is context for 0730, with no support or opposition to its direction.

## Original Isfahan registration and outcome list

The March 5, 2024 public IRCT revision, 296350, lists a target of 86 participants. It names DASS-21, the MDS-UPDRS, Parkinson sleep and cognitive measures, and quality of life. Its outcome list omits the standalone Parkinson Fatigue Scale. The broad registered MDS-UPDRS also includes a fatigue item. Expected recruitment dates were June 21, 2024 to April 21, 2025. Actual recruitment dates were unfilled in the checked history. ## Registered affron perimenopause trial

The ANZCTR record lists prospective registration on March 12, 2020, and first enrollment on April 3, 2020. It specifies standardized affron saffron extract, 14 mg twice daily for 12 weeks, and explicitly excludes Parkinson’s and other neurological diseases. The main outcome is the total Greene Climacteric Scale at baseline and weeks 4, 8 and 12. The psychological subscale is secondary. The registry says allocation was unconcealed, while the paper describes sequence concealment. The final registered enrollment is 86, and the publication listed in the registry is the same trial. ## Saffron extract and perimenopausal mood

Lopresti and Smith randomized 86 perimenopausal women to 14 mg affron twice daily or matched placebo for 12 weeks. The standardized stigma extract contained more than 3.5% Lepticrosalides by the reported specification. The analysis included 82 participants. The registered total Greene Climacteric Scale group-by-time comparison gave p=.078, and the separate baseline-to-week-12 percentage-change comparison gave p=.027. The psychological subscale group-by-time p value was .032. A post-hoc depression subscore comparison found reductions of 32% and 9%, p=.028. The extract preparation, population and scale differ from the frozen Parkinson claim. ## Computational saffron target and docking preprint

Gong and colleagues used network pharmacology and molecular docking to explore possible saffron constituent and Parkinson-related target relationships. The full Methods, Results, Discussion and Conclusion were accessible in the Research Square version 1 page. Targets and docking models are computational predictions. The work includes no participant intervention, culinary preparation comparison, or measured depressive symptom outcome.
