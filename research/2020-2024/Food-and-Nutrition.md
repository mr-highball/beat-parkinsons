# Food and Nutrition

### 2020 — Coffee and tea history in the Bamako case-control study

Koné and colleagues compared 43 Parkinson cases and 86 age- and sex-matched controls. Table II gives crude case-status odds ratios of 0.33 for coffee (95% CI 0.15–0.76) and 0.38 for tea (95% CI 0.18–0.84). The adjusted results table uses those exposures as covariates and does not give adjusted beverage estimates. Clinical variables included sleep problems and motor signs, without a symptom severity comparison by coffee or tea intake. The publisher posted the article July 24, 2020. The institutional thesis has matching recruitment and results. [Primary article and date](https://www.revues.ml/index.php/msp/article/view/1658) · [Full paper, Tables II–IV](https://www.revues.ml/index.php/msp/article/download/1658/1172) · [University thesis](https://www.bibliosante.ml/handle/123456789/6176)

### 2024 — Repository record for the Brazilian filtered-coffee crossover

Sachett’s university repository record was accessioned September 10, 2024. Its public abstract describes an acute crossover among people with Parkinson’s or stroke and gives qualitative findings for mobility, cognition and balance. The PDF requires repository login. The 2025 journal version provides the accessible methods and outcome table. ### 2022 — Constipation, swallowing and nutrition in the Recife clinic

Arruda’s dissertation was issued February 17 and made available in the repository August 12, 2022. Among 55 older participants with Parkinson’s, 42 met the study’s Rome III constipation classification and 16 screened positive for dysphagia using EAT-10. The dietary questionnaire includes fruit and juice entries. The results present grouped dietary quality and water intake by nutritional status and do not pair individual foods with either symptom. [Full dissertation and questionnaire](https://repositorio.ufpe.br/handle/123456789/45662) · [Repository accession and availability](https://repositorio.ufpe.br/handle/123456789/45662?mode=full)

### 2024 — Dietary protein and four-meter gait speed in Fortaleza

Monteiro’s dissertation reports a cross-sectional study of 117 participants with mild to moderate Parkinson’s. The university lists 2023 as the issue year and gives December 17, 2024 as an access date in its citation. Higher total protein intake had a small positive correlation with four-meter gait speed (Spearman ρ = 0.19, p = 0.042). The table comparison by low or normal protein intake gave an inconclusive result (p = 0.152). The study measured total protein, with no individual food estimate. The later journal article reports the same sample and gait association. [Institutional record](https://repositorio.ufc.br/handle/riufc/79154?mode=simple) · [Original dissertation, Table 5](https://repositorio.ufc.br/bitstream/riufc/79154/1/2024_dis_pamonteiro.pdf) · [2025 publication](../2025-2029/Food-and-Nutrition.md#2025--journal-report-of-the-fortaleza-protein-and-gait-study)

### 2024 — Dietary recalls and timing of levodopa in a Brazilian clinic sample

Wermann and Fassina’s October 28 report analyzed seven of 21 eligible participants recruited from a university extension program. It recorded meal and levodopa timing and summarized energy, macronutrients, sugar, fiber and selected minerals and vitamins from three dietary recalls. The paper reports normal nutritional status by the Mini Nutritional Assessment and meal timing consistent with the study’s levodopa guidance. It gives no pharmacokinetic, OFF-time, or single-food symptom comparison. [Publisher publication date](https://www.univates.br/revistas/index.php/destaques/article/view/3857) · [Primary report, Methods and Results](https://univates.br/revistas/index.php/destaques/article/download/3857/2480/13688)

### 2020 — Probiotics for constipation

A four-week randomized placebo-controlled trial reported more spontaneous bowel movements and improved constipation-related outcomes with the study’s probiotic formulation. The findings apply to constipation and short-term use in the trial population. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33046607/)

### 2021 — Added sugar intake and nonmotor symptoms

A case-control dietary study found higher reported added sugar intake among participants with Parkinson’s than controls. Higher sugar intake correlated with several nonmotor measures, including worse constipation and quality of life. Symptoms and treatment could change eating behavior and the study cannot establish the direction of effect. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34026805/)

### 2022 — Nicotinamide riboside target engagement

The phase 1 NADPARK randomized trial enrolled 30 newly diagnosed, untreated participants for 30 days. It reported variable increases in brain NAD and metabolic changes. The short study assessed tolerability and target engagement and supplied a rationale for larger clinical testing. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35235774/)

### 2024 — Longer follow-up revisits flavonoids and incidence

An extended analysis of the US health professional cohorts found no statistically clear association between higher baseline total flavonoid intake and lower incidence in either sex. The cohorts overlap the 2012 analysis and follow-up and exposure definitions matter. [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11187684/)

### 2024 — Mediterranean diet added to standard constipation care

The MEDI-PD study (NCT04683900) assessed 46 people for eligibility and randomized 45. The reported eight-week analysis included 19 people with Parkinson's who received Mediterranean diet plus standard care and 17 who received standard care alone. Nine withdrew before receiving their assigned intervention. Median constipation score changes were −0.5 (IQR −1.0 to 0) and −0.8 (IQR −1.0 to 0.2), respectively, with an inconclusive between-group comparison (p=.60). The diet advice included olive oil for cooking and dressing. The protocol supplied no separate oil supplement, and the study estimates a dietary-program comparison. [Original article, Figures 1 and 3](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/16/17/2946) · [Original protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8460525/) · [Registry and supplement audit](Food-Experiment-MEDI-PD-Constipation.md)

### 2024 — Dairy in the EPIC4ND cohort

A European prospective cohort examined milk, other dairy, calcium and vitamin D across 183,225 people. The adjusted analysis found no compelling evidence for an association between dairy intake or vitamin D intake and Parkinson’s incidence. Associations from food-frequency data remain sensitive to exposure definitions and adjustment. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39625618/)

### 2022 — Red quinoa extract in a fly-model dissertation

Barrientos’s dissertation, available in the university repository on October 10, 2022, includes the manuscript subsequently published by the group in 2025. Its four-arm experiment assessed an ethanol extract of red quinoa seeds during simultaneous exposure to rotenone. The combined group performed better than the rotenone-only group in climbing, open-field crossings, and a six-hour memory test. The thesis reports interaction p values of 0.0006 for climbing time and 0.0038 for crossings. It supplies a movement proxy in flies for the investigation of [0215](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0215.txt), with no walking or rigidity measurement in people and no boiled or cooked quinoa exposure for [0405](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0405.txt). [Repository metadata](https://repositorio.unipampa.edu.br/items/45d441e9-6412-4ebc-834f-ecc383e22045/full) · [Dissertation, manuscript sections 2 and 3](https://repositorio.unipampa.edu.br/bitstreams/18d873d5-9447-4419-aaaf-b4a716b24eec/download) · [2025 journal publication](../2025-2029/Food-and-Nutrition.md#2025--journal-report-of-the-red-quinoa-fly-study)

### 2024 — Nutrition follow-up protocol in Córdoba

Visentini’s project is dated March 2023. The Universidad Nacional de Córdoba repository documents accession and public availability in June 2024. It proposes nutritional screening, dysphagia and constipation history, dietary recall, caregiver education, and monitoring indicators for a movement-disorders service. Its annexes include forms and patient material. It supplies a proposed assessment and follow-up process without observed changes in symptom severity by food. The 2025 journal abstract describes the same protocol. [University repository metadata and report](https://rdu.unc.edu.ar/handle/11086/552279) · [Later journal abstract](../2025-2029/Food-and-Nutrition.md#2025--journal-abstract-of-the-córdoba-nutrition-protocol)

### 2024 — Swallowing endoscopy with four food consistencies

Araújo and colleagues’ August 5 report compared 22 people with Parkinson’s and dysphagia with 32 people with dysphagia and no Parkinson’s diagnosis. The fixed sequence used juice-flavored test liquids with an instant corn-starch thickener for the thickened textures and a cracker as the regular solid. Table 3 gives thin-liquid aspiration in 5 of 22 versus 0 of 32 (p = 0.008) and cracker aspiration in 2 of 22 versus 0 of 32 (p = 0.161). These are between-diagnosis comparisons under the test protocol. The study does not report a comparison for baked potato. [Primary report, procedures and Table 3](https://www.scielo.br/j/codas/a/R9zmYbKWydMTpXKKfwpBCBr/?lang=en) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC11340878.txt#L125)

### 2022 — Diet quality and sleep in the Cork cohort

Dunk and colleagues recruited 45 community-dwelling people with Parkinson’s and included 33 in the analysis after incomplete questionnaires and an outlier exclusion. Table 6 reports inconclusive correlations between the seven-component Healthy Diet Indicator and the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (r = −0.260, p = .18), Parkinson’s Disease Sleep Scale 2 (ρ = −0.229, p = .22), sleep latency (ρ = −0.063, p = .75) and daytime dysfunction (ρ = −0.184, p = .35). The Results prose reverses the order of the two sleep-scale correlations relative to Table 6. The questionnaire collected individual foods, but the reported sleep analyses use an overall diet score. The final paper first appeared on September 2, 2022. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36056229/) · [Local full text, Table 6](Source-Text/PMC10250501.txt#L280)

### 2020 — Dietary nicotine and new Parkinson diagnoses

Ma et al. followed 31,615 women and 19,523 men who had never smoked, with 601 new Parkinson diagnoses over 26 years. The group estimated dietary nicotine from peppers, fresh ripe tomatoes, tomato juice and sauce, potatoes, and tea. The pooled hazard ratio comparing the highest with lowest nicotine-intake quintile was 0.70 (95% CI 0.51–0.94). For peppers five or more times weekly versus three or fewer times monthly, the hazard ratio was 0.49 (0.25–0.94) in women and 1.04 (0.57–1.90) in men. Table 3 gives pooled highest-versus-lowest estimates of 0.86 (0.61–1.22) for processed tomatoes and 0.88 (0.64–1.22) for potatoes. The food-frequency questionnaire categories and the incidence endpoint leave the experiment's food-preparation and symptom combinations open. The group reported the same cohort in a June 2020 conference abstract before the July journal release. These exposures do not identify the foods in the later Iranian symptom trial. [First conference report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7258060/) · [Full journal report, Tables 2–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7528555/) · [Iranian symptom trial](Food-Experiment-Iran-Trial-Results.md#nicotine-rich-dietary-pattern-and-protein-timing).
