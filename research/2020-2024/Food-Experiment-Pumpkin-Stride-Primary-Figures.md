# Pumpkin seed extract and measured stride in animal models

## Saleem pumpkin seed extract and measured stride length

Saleem and colleagues, Metabolic Brain Disease 36:1231–1251, 2021, DOI 10.1007/s11011-021-00707-6, PMID 33759084. Springer lists first online publication March 24, 2021. Its original Figure 4 labels stride length in centimeters and uses separate baseline control, disease control, standard comparator and three methanolic Cucurbita pepo seed extract arms. Approximate figure-derived means are 9.1 cm in the baseline control, 5.7 cm in the disease control, 8.4 cm with the standard comparator and 6.4, 7.3 and 8.0 cm in the ascending extract arms. This is a measured stride endpoint in haloperidol-exposed rats. Numeric means are approximate readings from the graph. Comparator footnotes, animal counts for that figure and gait protocol are not visible in the public figure or abstract. Figures 1 and 2 label several phenolic peaks including ferulic acid, but separate sample identities and active compound attribution require the article methods. The primary test material was a methanolic seed extract rather than roasted seed oil, and no Parkinson cohort was enrolled. [Primary source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11011-021-00707-6)

## Isolated p-coumaric acid and mouse stride length

Pathan and colleagues, Journal of Pharmacology and Pharmacotherapeutics 13(4):364–374, first online February 22, 2023, issue dated December 2022. DOI 10.1177/0976500X221150837. Its original Figure 4 reports longer stride at day 21 for the two higher oral isolated compound groups versus untreated haloperidol exposure (p < .05 and p < .01), with six animals per group. The compound occurs in roasted pumpkin seed oil at concentrations measured in micrograms per gram. The animal experiment used purified compound at much higher exposures and does not establish that ordinary oil has the same effect. This study measures stride and adds a mechanistic candidate without resolving preparation, effective exposure or human transfer. [Primary source](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0976500X221150837)

## Different motor outcomes with Cucurbita maxima extract

Wahid, Alqahtani and Khan, Behavioural Neurology, March 22, 2023. DOI 10.1155/2023/7509937. In mice receiving ethanol extract of Cucurbita maxima seed, Table 4 shows longer rotating-rod retention in higher-dose groups than control. At day 21, Table 5 shows faster stationary-rod arrival at the lower two extract doses and slower arrival at the highest dose (64.1 versus 26.3 seconds in the control). This shows discordance across tasks, doses and test days. Timed stationary-rod arrival is a learning and coordination measure. There is no stride-length, culinary oil or Parkinson cohort comparison. [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10060065/)
