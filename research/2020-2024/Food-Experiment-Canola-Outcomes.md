# Canola and rapeseed oil outcome follow-up

## High oleic canola and ordinary rapeseed oils in an anxiety behavior assay

Sakai, Kuroe, Inamori and Tamagawa reported this study in volume 24 of the Kagoshima Immaculate Heart University nursing and nutrition bulletin in 2020, pages 21–26. Four-week-old female Sprague Dawley rats were randomized to ten groups across five oils with or without a yogurt preparation and followed for 13 weeks. Table 1 distinguishes commercial high-oleic canola from ordinary rapeseed. The authors report greater anxiety-like avoidance for rapeseed groups than sunflower groups in their comparison and describe a more pronounced response in the high-oleic canola group than in the high-oleic sunflower group. The Japanese abstract reports no clear yogurt effect for high-oleic canola. The animals had no Parkinson model, the assays were behavioral proxies and the report did not establish cold pressing. ## Canola comparator and measured walking balance in older adults

Mutoh et al. published the primary MCT SMILE trial April 5, 2022. The trial analyzed 63 healthy older adults, including 31 in the control group. Control jelly sticks used canola oil and provided up to 18 g per day in a calorically matched formula. At three months, the control Lissajous index was 34.7 ± 15.6 at baseline and 31.3 ± 18.9 after intervention (within-arm p=.410). The MCT group's corresponding values were 33.9 ± 19.9 and 23.1 ± 14.4 (within-arm p=.011, between-arm p=.009). Walking speed and step length between-arm p values were .695 and .785. The study used gait displacement as a walking-balance proxy. It did not use a Parkinson pull test, count freezing events, identify oil processing or include a no-oil arm. ## Canola comparator and timed up and go with exercise

The July 2023 trial by Kojima et al. paired an exercise program with four oil formulas in generally healthy middle-aged and older adults. The control package contained 3 g of commercial Nisshin canola oil. Of 120 enrolled participants, 119 completed the trial. Table 6 shows the canola group decreasing from 6.7 ± 0.3 to 6.1 ± 0.1 seconds over 12 weeks, a reported change of −0.6 ± 0.2 seconds. All groups improved from baseline and between-group differences were statistically inconclusive. Exercise and repeated testing were common to all groups. The publication did not identify pressing, isolate canola against no oil or record Parkinson postural instability or freeze counts. ## Optional canola and psychological outcomes in CHILEMED

NCT05454904 first posted July 12, 2022. CHILEMED compares low-fat counseling, Mediterranean counseling and Mediterranean counseling with psychological support among adults with metabolic syndrome. Mediterranean guidance offers canola cooking or dressing oil or avocado as alternatives. No fixed oil dose or pressing method is specified. The published protocol includes a Depression Anxiety Stress Scale among secondary measures. An oil-specific anxiety estimate requires a separate exposure comparison. ## Hydrogenated rapeseed excipient and planned anxiety scores

NCT06425094 first posted May 22, 2024. The detailed intervention record lists hydrogenated rapeseed oil in the active microalgae formulation and leaves it out of the maltodextrin placebo description. The secondary GAD-7 measurements were scheduled at baseline, two weeks and four weeks. Formulation ingredients vary together. Hydrogenation and use as an excipient limit application to culinary cold-pressed oil. ## Canola comparators and freezing of gait in separate 2024 abstracts

The 2024 Gerontological Society of America proceedings contain separate ketone-ester and freezing-of-gait studies. Printed abstract IDs 3646 and 3705 describe safety and exploratory function from the same pilot of 30 community-dwelling older adults, 23 completing, with canola oil in the comparison drink. The exploratory report found no clear between-group changes in variables covering function, activity and quality of life. Printed abstract 3780 describes sleep, anxiety and walking-triggered freezing in 13 people with Parkinson disease and has no oil intervention. Neither canola abstract reports a Parkinson freezing comparison. The full exploratory pilot publication followed in 2025.
