# Saffron preparations and fatigue outside Parkinson’s

## Simple saffron syrup in multiple sclerosis

Ashtiani et al. reported a July 27, 2020 uncontrolled before-and-after study in 30 people with multiple sclerosis, PMID 32716906. Participants took 7.5 mL of syrup containing 0.5% saffron water extract every eight hours over 60 days. Table 2 in the author-uploaded full article gives means (SD) of 71.10 (7.76) at baseline, 48.40 (9.95) at 30 days and 35.30 (15.34) at 60 days. The authors report p < .001 for comparisons with baseline. Their Methods describe nine Fatigue Severity Scale items scored 1–7, whose untransformed sum has a maximum of 63. The article does not describe a conversion that would reconcile the reported baseline mean with that range. The numbers document a reported within-group decline with uncertain units, without a control group to estimate a treatment effect. The syrup and multiple sclerosis population provide indirect context for `0485`. The article cites registry ID `IRCT2017061226667N6`. [Publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1515/jbcpp-2020-0063) · [Author-uploaded full report, Methods and Table 2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343260009_The_effect_of_novel_simple_saffron_syrup_on_fatigue_reduction_in_patients_with_multiple_sclerosis)

## Saffron honey and rose-water beverage in breast cancer

Mirzaei et al. randomized 75 patients to Jollab containing saffron, honey, and rose water or placebo, published June 29, 2022. The abstract reports lower fatigue on the Cancer Fatigue Scale physical and cognitive subscales compared with placebo (p < .05) and no clear change on the affective subscale. Fatigue Severity Scale scores improved within the Jollab group (p < .001) and changed less clearly in the placebo group (p = .096). The abstract gives no between-group FSS estimate or interval. The beverage was a blend and the population had cancer-related fatigue. [Primary report abstract](https://doi.org/10.1159/000525775)

## Jollab Monzej blend and fatigue in multiple sclerosis

In a spring 2023 double-blind trial, 56 people with multiple sclerosis were randomized to a one-month course of Jollab Monzej syrup or placebo. The blend included saffron stigma, rose water, sugar, lemon balm, lavender, and Iranian borage. The intention-to-treat analysis reported 8.80 points greater improvement in Fatigue Severity Scale score with the blend (95% CI 2.90 to 14.70) and a 5.21-point greater decrease in Beck Depression Inventory score (95% CI 2.25 to 8.17). The intervention does not isolate saffron. The formulation and trial ID `IRCT20190623043984N1` distinguish it from the March 2025 Jollab trial. ## Acute saffron stigma extract and sprint fatigability

Ouerghi et al. reported a September 3, 2024 randomized crossover study in 22 physically active young men. The Methods describe three capsules of commercial standardized saffron stigma extract totaling 300 mg, given two hours before repeated sprints, and lactose placebo on the other day. Table 1 shows total sprint fatigue index 5.97% (SD 2.05) with saffron and 5.51% (SD 1.95) with placebo (p = .425). The index measures slowing across repeated sprints. The participants, outcome and acute extract exposure give indirect context for `0485`. The article is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. [Primary report, Methods and Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11373116/)
