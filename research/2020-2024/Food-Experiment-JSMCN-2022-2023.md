# 2022 and 2023 Japanese Society of Metabolism and Clinical Nutrition abstracts

Meetings 24 and 25 shared one supplement for the joint January 28–30, 2022 meeting. The society announced the program and abstracts on January 24. Meeting 26 ran January 13–15, 2023. Its public supplement is labeled 2023. Both conference program pages state that selected invited abstracts were omitted. The accessible PDFs provide the general abstracts cited below. ## Enteral drug therapy and nutrition symposium

The January 29, 2022 symposium program lists Yasunori Yamamoto of Ehime University on enteral drug therapy and nutrition management for dysphagia in Parkinson's. The public supplement gives the title and affiliation. It supplies no treatment group, named food or results for this invited presentation. Searches for the exact title, author and institutional research records did not recover an abstract body. ## Eucalyptus hot-water extract and alpha-synuclein

Misaki Ono and colleagues studied an *Eucalyptus gunnii* hot-water extract in a cell-free alpha-synuclein assay. They reported lower thioflavin T fluorescence in the presence of the extract. Rat PC12 cells exposed to fibrils formed with or without the extract yielded reported activity readings of 8.7 ± 5.3% and 15.3 ± 5.6%, respectively. The abstract calls the assay LDH in its methods and labels the result LDL. This report concerns an aqueous extract and cell-level outcomes, without evidence on eucalyptus oil consumption or Parkinson symptoms. [2022 O-133, printed S39](https://www.eiyou.or.jp/gakujutsu/25th/pdf/jsmcn_Vol24-25.Supplement_vo.pdf#page=121)

## Discharge counseling and texture-modified foods

Wakako Arakane and colleagues reviewed five patients receiving discharge nutrition counseling for dysphagia. Three had Parkinson's, one had myasthenia gravis and one had epilepsy. Counseling included cooking methods, texture adjustment, thickeners, commercial care-food selection and hydration jelly. Initially, three patients used enteral nutrition and two ate a texture-modified diet orally. At discharge, all five had some oral intake, three in Japanese dysphagia diet code 2013-2 and two in code 2013-3. One still used enteral nutrition. Outcomes were pooled, and the authors did not identify which foods each Parkinson participant ate or measure ingredient-specific swallowing effects. The adjacent complete lateral positioning report was a separate study. [2022 O-399, printed S105](https://www.eiyou.or.jp/gakujutsu/25th/pdf/jsmcn_Vol24-25.Supplement_vo.pdf#page=187)

## Gastrostomy complications in two Parkinson cases

In O-298, an 85-year-old woman with Parkinson's and post-stroke dysphagia had increased sputum after unspecified gastrostomy feeds. Contrast imaging and CT subsequently identified a gastrocolocutaneous fistula. In O-388, an 88-year-old woman with Parkinson's had tube leakage and skin problems that improved after a device adjustment. The reports concern different patients and complications, with no named formula or ingredient-level symptom comparison. [2022 O-298, printed S80](https://www.eiyou.or.jp/gakujutsu/25th/pdf/jsmcn_Vol24-25.Supplement_vo.pdf#page=162) · [2022 O-388, printed S102](https://www.eiyou.or.jp/gakujutsu/25th/pdf/jsmcn_Vol24-25.Supplement_vo.pdf#page=184)

## Mushroom hot-water extracts and alpha-synuclein

Ono and colleagues compared hot-water extracts of shiitake, button mushroom, maitake, enoki, shimeji, king oyster mushroom and nameko. Maitake showed the largest fluorescence reduction, with thioflavin T signal of 5% of untreated control when added before fibrils formed and 50% when added to preformed fibrils. The authors interpreted these measurements as lower fibril formation and greater breakdown. None of the extracts changed SH-SY5Y cell proliferation or injury in the assays reported. No patients or clinical symptoms were studied. The 2024 abstract from the same group separately compared maitake hot-water and ethanol extracts. [2023 O-118, printed S34](https://www.eiyou.or.jp/gakujutsu/26th/pdf/jsmcn_Vol26.Supplement_vo.pdf#page=108) · [2024 maitake extract experiment](Food-Experiment-JSMCN-2024.md#maitake-extracts-in-a-cell-free-alpha-synuclein-assay)

## Home dental nutrition and separate rice jelly study

Toru Saito and colleagues reviewed 117 people who received nutrition counseling alongside home dental care, including 13 with Parkinson's. Forty-one were malnourished by the Mini Nutritional Assessment–Short Form, and 36 received advice on chewing or swallowing. The reported figures combine diagnoses. The abstract provides no ingredient-level outcomes for the Parkinson participants.

The adjacent O-267 from Kazuhiko Nishimura is a separate cooking and tasting exercise in nine older community volunteers. Eight rated high-amylose rice flour jelly easy to swallow and one rated it somewhat difficult. In the separate at-home O-270 exercise on the next PDF page, all ten participants reported it easy to swallow. Neither report identifies a Parkinson cohort or measures aspiration. [2023 O-267 and O-268, printed S71](https://www.eiyou.or.jp/gakujutsu/26th/pdf/jsmcn_Vol26.Supplement_vo.pdf#page=145) · [2023 O-270, printed S72](https://www.eiyou.or.jp/gakujutsu/26th/pdf/jsmcn_Vol26.Supplement_vo.pdf#page=146)
