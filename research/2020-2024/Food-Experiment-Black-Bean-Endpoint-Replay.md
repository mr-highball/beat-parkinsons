# Black bean preparation and symptom evidence

## Pooled beans and whole diet sleep findings in the MVP cohort

[Fox et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9739738/), first published 6 December 2022, PMID 36501214, DOI 10.3390/nu14235185, analyzed 1,205 self-reported Parkinsonism participants (1,145 idiopathic PD) in a cross-sectional survey. Higher MIND and Mediterranean diet scores were associated with lower reported insomnia and daytime sleepiness; Table 2 gives, per point of MIND score, −2.14 for insomnia and −1.91 for daytime sleepiness, after adjustment and multiplicity correction. These are diet-pattern associations, not black-bean effects or changes after treatment. Self-selection, diet measurement and reverse causation limit interpretation.

The diet instruments group beans or legumes, not boiled or canned black common beans. The already archived `research/2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC9739738-Supplement.txt` transcribes Supplementary Table S4: MIND bean item compared with total symptom burden gives −111 (printed interval −195 to −27.8, adjusted p=.07); Mediterranean legume item −43.6 (−98.2 to 11.0, adjusted p=.6). The printed p value is multiplicity-adjusted and the displayed interval is nominal. These are whole-symptom outcomes, not sleepiness, insomnia or postural instability for beans, and do not identify bean type or preparation. The scores do not establish either frozen direction. Prior dated catalog: `research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Lentil-Kidney-Bean.md`.

## Levodopa chemistry in common bean samples

[Tesoro et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37401097/), DOI 10.1002/jms.4952, published July 2023, used LC–ESI/MS/MS to measure L-dopa in uncooked *Phaseolus vulgaris* fresh/dried seeds and pods, including a commercial dried black-bean comparator. Across tested matrices reported values ranged 0.020 ± 0.005 to 2.34 ± 0.05 µg/g dry weight; that range is not an estimate for a cooked serving. This is analytical composition, not exposure-response, clinical PD, sleep, or balance. Existing detailed catalog `research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Green-Pinto-Tempeh-Garlic.md` flags an older 0.25% common-bean claim whose citations did not support the value; a separate 2018 UV assay gave higher values in different dried seed accessions. Do not combine divergent assays or extrapolate to boiled or canned beans.

## Cooked black beans in a mixed pulse skin trial

This skin-microbiome study first posted August 5, 2024. The ten-week intervention alternates a cooked tri-bean blend containing kidney, pinto and black beans with cooked black lentils. The comparator consumes cooked white rice. The study does not isolate black beans and its population and outcomes do not address the frozen Parkinson sleep and postural endpoints. No summary results were posted in the retrieved registry snapshot. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06538415)

## Vascular outcomes in the published boiled black bean crossover

The Clark, Taylor and Zahradka paper was first published online August 2, 2020, before the January 2021 journal issue. Eight healthy adults each consumed meals of black, navy, red kidney or pinto beans or white rice. The abstract's black-bean contrasts include lower pulse wave velocity versus pinto at six hours and lower augmentation pressure versus rice and pinto. The original [2019 dissertation](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Bean-Sleep-Followup.md#boiled-black-common-beans-in-the-clark-dissertation) describes soaking and boiling the bean portions. The journal abstract and registered outcomes concern vascular and metabolic responses rather than sleepiness or Parkinson disease. The report, dissertation and registry concern one study.
