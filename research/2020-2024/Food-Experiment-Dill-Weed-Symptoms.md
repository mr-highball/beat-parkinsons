# Dill weed symptom evidence

## Dill fruit in a Moroccan ethnobotanical survey

Survey entries describe dill fruit, voucher 2041/IS/N°38, used orally for gastric complaints. The right-hand table fields are literature synthesis. The dill row credits Ouahdi 2010 for toxicity and convulsions associated with prolonged use. A narrative passage lists hallucinations across several plants without assigning that outcome to the dill row. No identified original report in this search verifies a visual hallucination event or rate after consuming fresh dill foliage. [Najem full article](https://ethnobotanyjournal.org/index.php/era/article/download/1731/1017/12025).

## Fatigue row in the Phenthymol trial

The paper describes oral thyme, ajwain, unspecified Anethum graveolens powder and honey alongside usual treatment versus a starch and sugar syrup placebo alongside usual treatment. Table 1 lists the fatigue counts 16/30 placebo and 10/30 formula with P=.118, despite abstract/narrative language of significant improvement. No validated fatigue severity scale or dill component comparison is presented. The paper also prints an incongruous January 2016 onset date for the COVID study and its abstract and body describe the group structure differently. [Mazinani full article in institutional issue PDF](https://ibdigital.uib.es/greenstone/sites/localsite/collect/medicinaBalear/import/volums/2022/AJHS_Medicina_Balear_2022v37n1.pdf).

## Frozen dill product contamination notice

The Class 1 manufacturing notice identifies individual lots of commercially frozen dill sold in Ontario for possible Listeria contamination, documented as a product hazard. It supplies no patient histories, participant exposure outcomes, or hallucination data. [Canadian Food Inspection Agency notice](https://recalls-rappels.canada.ca/en/alert-recall/supherb-farms-brand-iqf-dill-recalled-due-listeria-monocytogenes).

## Dill-containing soup and weakness during severe COVID

A 78-year-old man with severe COVID received multiple dietary and drug interventions. The soup directions list chopped Anethum graveolens and allow either fresh or dried. The table records soup intake but does not identify the herb form actually consumed. Combined weakness and lethargy was 9/10 before the advice, 5/10 on March 13 and about 8/10 on March 15, with fatigue again listed March 20. There was no isolated dill intervention or comparison. Dryness or stickiness of the throat appeared in the report and salivation or drooling was not scored. [Nabimeybodi full case report](https://publish.kne-publishing.com/index.php/APTJ/article/download/12488/11839/).

## Dill in test meals for older adults

One hundred community-dwelling volunteers participated in the meat or vegetarian comparison. Dill and other spices were actually served in the seasoned chicken salad and in the seasoned chickpea and rice salad. Appendix A lists dill without specifying fresh or dried. Study outcomes were meal liking, flavor and protein consumption. Parkinson disease, hand dexterity and the other five frozen symptom outcomes were not reported as endpoints. [Nutrients full article and Appendix A](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/15/5/1172/pdf?version=1677654776).

## Dill seed extract in neuroblastoma cells

Sharma and colleagues exposed SH-SY5Y human neuroblastoma cells to hydrogen peroxide and assayed hexane and ethyl acetate extracts of dill seeds and selected constituents. The assays reported differences in oxidative stress markers, mitochondrial potential and other cellular or biochemical endpoints. The introduction mentions Parkinson disease among neurodegenerative diseases. The experiments did not identify a Parkinson population, a Parkinson-specific symptom, or culinary dill leaf exposure. [Sharma full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11241255/).
