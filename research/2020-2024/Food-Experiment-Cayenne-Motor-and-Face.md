# Cayenne pepper motor and facial outcome follow-up

## Fall reports in the knee capsaicin injection study

The official results first posted April 8, 2022. Over the one-year adverse-event observation period, 4 of 189 assigned the capsaicin injection and 2 of 136 assigned the placebo injection were affected by a reported fall, with the same counts of events. The official pain-with-walking primary contrast at week 12 was −0.33 on a 0–10 scale (95% CI −0.84 to 0.17, p=.1904). The falls are adverse-event reports in knee osteoarthritis, and the endpoint did not measure intervention effects on Parkinson falls. Companion investigator results for repeated injections (NCT03660943, first posted 2018-09-07, results 2022-07-21) do not enumerate a fall row above the registry reporting threshold.

## Oral red chili extract and treadmill time to exhaustion

Section 3.5 reports early abdominal pain in four participants in the higher-dose group. The closing discussion gives a conflicting account, and no pain severity estimate is reported.

Roopashree and colleagues tested placebo versus two doses of capsaicinoid-enriched red chili extract in sustained-release beadlets with fenugreek mucilage. The treadmill held each participant's selected speed while the grade rose. The paper reports increased time to exhaustion relative to placebo for the active groups. Treadmill endurance under increasing grade leaves ordinary walking speed and Parkinson walking slowness unmeasured. The ground-spice form was not used.

## Recurrent fainting reported after chili ingestion

Villagrán Ruíz de la Peña and colleagues report a 50-year-old woman who briefly fainted while seated after eating mole poblano and did not fall to the floor. The nearest ambulatory reading was 95/60 mmHg with pulse 62, versus 100/58 and 78 at the previous reading. The half-hour cuff schedule may miss the nadir, and the measured diastolic readings differ from the prose’s stated decrease. The case reports no episodes during six months of avoiding chili and three subsequent syncopal episodes after resumption. The journal assigns the issue to 2019 and lists public posting November 29, 2021. The food preparation does not confirm ground cayenne, and the case gives no Parkinson fall frequency. ## Blood pressure and heart rate after chili in a syncope case

Huang and colleagues reported a 60-year-old woman with repeated loss of consciousness. An oral chili challenge produced dizziness and altered awareness within about five minutes. Figure 1 reports pressure falling from 125/83 to 56/37 mmHg and heart rate from 73 to 37 beats per minute. A standalone tilt test was negative. The discussion calls the trigger cayenne, while the clinical description does not verify the formulation or dose. The study recorded no incidental falls or longitudinal fall frequency and did not enroll people with Parkinson’s.
