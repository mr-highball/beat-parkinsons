# Cold-pressed walnut oil in experimental Parkinson models

Avallone and colleagues published September 4, 2024 a rotenone-exposed fruit-fly experiment. The oil was cold-pressed, unrefined 2022-harvest *Juglans regia* with declared 9.1% saturated, 16.5% monounsaturated and 69.9% polyunsaturated fat. Four groups received standard diet, walnut-oil diet, rotenone diet, or both; 10 μL oil was added to 10 mL food. Co-exposed flies climbed better than rotenone-only flies after three and seven days. Oil plus rotenone remained below untreated controls at day seven. Climbing is not a clinical dystonia or dyskinesia measure. [Primary article and methods](Source-Text/PMC11397333.txt#L105) · [Published article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11397333/)

Filaferro and colleagues published December 4, 2024 a separate study in human HMC3 microglial and SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cell cultures using cold-pressed unrefined *J. regia* oil from the same named 2022 producer and declared lipid profile. The culture model is not a clinical cohort. In HMC3 cells, after 5 μM rotenone, co-treatment with 62.5 and 125 μg/mL oil increased measured viability by 12% and 17% relative to rotenone alone; oil alone at 250 and 500 μg/mL reduced viability. Results varied with dose and time. Viability and reactive oxygen species are not dystonia, dyskinesia or dream-enactment outcomes. The cell and fly papers represent distinct experiments, not independent human evidence. [Primary article, sections 2.3–2.4 and 4.2](Source-Text/PMC11643845.txt#L134) · [Published article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11643845/)
