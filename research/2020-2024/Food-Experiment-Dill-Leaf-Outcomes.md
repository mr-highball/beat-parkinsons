# Dill leaf preparation and clinical outcomes

## Dried leaf capsules and esophageal motility symptoms

Haidari and colleagues randomized 48 adults with type 2 diabetes and analyzed 42, 21 per arm. They administered capsules containing botanically identified dried dill leaves or starch for eight weeks. Table 5 groups esophageal motility complaints into absent, occasional and persistent symptoms. The numbers with any complaint were 3 of 21 initially and 5 of 21 at follow-up with dill, versus 5 of 21 at both visits with placebo. Persistent symptoms were reported in two participants per arm at both visits. The table gives within-arm p=.317 with dill and p=1.00 with placebo and supplies no between-arm comparison. A separate colonic symptom category showed a within-arm reduction. No Parkinson diagnosis, fresh leaf contrast or dedicated swallowing severity estimate appears in the report. ## Thai dill extract beverage and registered mood and sleep outcomes

The official TCTR20210407002 export records April 7, 2021 registration and planned enrollment of 60 working-age adults. It lists a daily placebo beverage and two extract beverages at 1,000 or 1,500 mg for six weeks. The primary measure is the Thai DASS-21. Secondary measures include the Thai Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index, a mood questionnaire, physical fitness without a specified motor task, working memory and event-related potentials. The record describes an extract beverage without a culinary leaf preparation. It marks recruitment completed. The public results fields are empty. No fatigue-specific outcome or defined hand-motor task appears in the export. ## Dill extract formulation and perinatal nicotine organ outcomes

The 2021 Methods describe oral methanolic dill extract at 500 mg/kg and a nicotine schedule that disagrees with the abstract on the last exposure day. The offspring outcomes were hepatic and renal. The later neonatal maze article cites this report but lists a lower extract dose. Cohort reuse remains unverified.
