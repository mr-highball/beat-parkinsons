# Cherry, cranberry and watermelon primary studies, 2020–2024

## Cranberry juice in two Poznań rotenone experiments

Kurpik and colleagues’ March 2021 article studied five groups under animal approval 63/2015. It measured brain, liver and kidney markers and did not report a behavioral outcome. A July 2021 conference report under approval 58/2016 describes tissue endpoints from a four-group study. Witucki and colleagues’ May 2022 paper uses the same approval, treatment regimen, investigators and markers as the conference report, and adds a postural stepping test. These are two experiments and three reports. In the later experiment, stepping distance after postural displacement was 11% less with juice plus rotenone than with rotenone alone (p < .05). The juice-only group had 15% fewer counted midbrain cells than the untreated control in a histology analysis of three animals per group. The data concern concentrated juice and experimental motor or tissue outcomes. They give no clinical bradykinesia or facial-expression comparison after cooked cranberry. [2021 primary paper](Source-Text/PMC8063919.txt) · [Conference report](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-9992/8/1/5) · [2022 article, Figures 4–5](Source-Text/PMC9144186.txt#L115)

## Cranberry powder and measured attention

Flanagan and colleagues’ May 2022 COMBAT report used freeze-dried cranberry powder in healthy older adults for 12 weeks. The group-by-time contrast on the Addenbrooke attention subscore was inconclusive (p = .467). Delayed Rey complex-figure recall improved in the powder group relative to placebo (interaction p = .028). Neither a cooked cranberry preparation nor people with Parkinson disease were included. [Primary paper, Table 2](Source-Text/PMC9160193.txt#L202) · [2018 registration](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Cherry-Cranberry-Watermelon.md#registered-cranberry-powder-cognitive-feasibility-study)

## Tart cherry concentrate and mental fatigue

Kimble and colleagues’ February 2022 companion report to NCT04021342 found lower mental fatigue ratings during a cognitive battery after 12 weeks of tart cherry concentrate versus placebo (difference −9.5 points, 95% CI −16.5 to −2.5, treatment p = .009, 46 with fatigue data). Table 5 reports an inconclusive treatment-by-task repetition interaction (p = .257). The result is a treatment main effect from a battery with multiple outcomes. The trial was powered for systolic pressure and examined middle-aged adults outside Parkinson disease. The 2021 cardiovascular report describes the same cohort. A diluted concentrate and session fatigue differ from dried whole fruit and Parkinson fatigue severity. [Fatigue and cognition report, Table 5](Source-Text/PMC9723490.txt) · [Primary cardiovascular report, Table 3](Source-Text/PMC8145763.txt) · [Registration](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Cherry-Cranberry-Watermelon.md#registered-tart-cherry-concentrate-cognition-study)

## Sweet cherry extract and movement in a fly model

Filaferro and colleagues’ January 2022 paper gave a sweet cherry anthocyanin-rich extract with fly food. Spontaneous movements and induced climbing were better under the extract–toxin combination than under rotenone alone. Their 24-hour activity and negative geotaxis assays measure fly movement. The material was a concentrated extract and the study did not assess dystonia or clinical fatigue after eating dried cherry. [Primary article, Figure 4](Source-Text/PMC8868341.txt)

## Registered cranberry drink and student mental health

NCT05260346 was first posted March 2, 2022. It lists 64 actual participants and mood as the primary outcome, with mental well-being and cognitive outcomes among the secondary measures. The later trial paper describes a 72-person sample. The authors explicitly identify the registration, but the enrollment difference remains. ## Registered watermelon dose and ambulatory pressure study

NCT05892328 was first posted June 7, 2023. It lists 39 adults with elevated blood pressure and a four-week comparison of watermelon doses and control. The 2025 article identifies the registry and reports 24-hour, daytime and nighttime pressure. The record supplies no posted registry results, orthostatic challenge or symptom scores. [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT05892328.txt) · [2025 results](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Cherry-Cranberry-Watermelon.md#watermelon-beverage-and-ambulatory-blood-pressure)

## Watermelon juice cognition and vascular outcomes in postmenopausal women

The vascular and cognitive articles by Ellis, Crowe-White and colleagues describe companion analyses of NCT03626168. The 2018 registry reports 21 participants and has posted vascular outcomes. The 2021 articles analyze 16 postmenopausal women in a crossover with pasteurized 100% watermelon juice. The vascular report found no clear group difference on ambulatory pressure or other vascular outcomes. The cognitive report found no clear cognitive benefit and did not assess clinical apathy. The study gives no standing-pressure or lightheadedness comparison. [Official 2018 registration and posted results](../2015-2019/Source-Text/Trials/NCT03626168.txt) · [Vascular article](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34344546/) · [Cognition article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9930684/)

## Cranberry beverage crossover with cognition and mood measures

2021-08-17. NCT05008549 registered a randomized crossover comparing cranberry reconstituted from concentrate, matched placebo, and separate combination arms in healthy adults. The 25-person record lists completed status and no posted results as of November 15, 2026. It planned COMPASS speed and accuracy composites and Profile of Mood States total mood disturbance. It does not establish a separate attention or depression estimate and did not assign dried cranberries or cooked whole fruit. The design and sponsorship differ from CRANMOOD. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05008549) · [Local source text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT05008549.txt)

## Watermelon reported in the Belem Parkinson cohort

Fernandez and colleagues surveyed 40 people with Parkinson disease. Table 3 shows 15 weekly watermelon consumers, nine monthly, 16 rarely or never and zero daily. Preparation was unspecified. The survey has no comparison of bradykinesia by watermelon intake. The same cohort is used in the orange exposure review. [Licensed local article, Table 3](Source-Text/2021-Fernandez-Belem-Parkinson-Food-Frequency.txt#L240) · [Original article](https://doi.org/10.1590/0004-282X-ANP-2020-0053)

## Commercial watermelon juice and resting pressure in a pilot crossover

Hussein and colleagues randomized 10 healthy adults in a crossover with commercial watermelon juice and water. At two hours the reported difference in systolic pressure change was −13.1 mmHg (95% CI −24.09 to −2.11, p=.02). The treatment-by-time test gave p=.91. The two estimates describe different comparisons. The report did not test raw watermelon, pressure upon standing or orthostatic lightheadedness. A distinct 21-person watermelon juice crossover, linked to NCT03626168, did not find a clear longer-term ambulatory pressure difference.
