# Parkinson’s food, swallowing and nutrition in Japanese hospital proceedings

## Nutrition support referrals and energy intake

The 2020 nutrition support service reviewed 13 of 15 referred patients with Parkinson. Lower body mass, swallowing difficulty and tube feeding were common. The four who gained weight were receiving an estimated 45.9 ± 9.3 kcal/kg at the end of follow-up. The analysis reports care and energy estimates without food names or a comparison group. [2020 NHO P-196, page 849](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000144330.pdf#page=849)

## Body composition in Parkinson

The 2020 DXA comparison included 62 patients with Parkinson and 79 disease controls selected for low skeletal muscle index. Mean BMI was 18.8 versus 20.6. The authors also report differences in fat and muscle measures. Dietary intake was outside the reported comparison. [2020 NHO P-200, page 853](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000144330.pdf#page=853)

## Sweet potatoes in a recreational food program

The 2020 Shinsei tea-room report lists roasted sweet potatoes among planned seasonal foods. The program provided foods adapted for participants with swallowing difficulties. Two events drew 30–40 of 56 residents, and a tube-fed participant tasted soft-serve. No diagnosis-specific outcome or response to a particular potato preparation was reported. [2020 NHO P-216, page 869](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000144330.pdf#page=869)

## Gelled feeds and hydrolyzed guar gum in wound care

Oike and colleagues described a patient with Parkinson, diabetes, recurrent aspiration pneumonia and eight pressure injuries. The team changed tube feeds and positioning while treating the wounds. A formula higher in hydrolyzed guar gum replaced the preceding formula after frequent stools on day 107. The authors reported fewer stools after the switch, with no counts or constipation outcome. Two pressure injuries remained and the sacral wound improved from D4 to D3 during combined care. Product identities and the dose of guar gum were unreported. [2020 NHO P-231, page 884](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000144330.pdf#page=884)

## Porridge and thickened tea during swallowing care

Otsubo and Mori described a stage III Parkinson case with oral residue, cough and esophageal transit difficulty. Care included thickened cold tea of unspecified type and mashed rice porridge, a limited period of self-feeding followed by assistance, and cold liquid food. The team also adjusted medication and positioning, used suction and oral care, and reduced distractions during meals. At two months the person sometimes completed a meal. The abstract gives no isolated effect of tea or porridge and no post-treatment instrumental aspiration result. [2020 NHO P-304, page 957](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000144330.pdf#page=957)

## Barium jelly and bolus formation

The 2020 hospital series reviewed 136 swallowing studies that used barium jelly. Parkinson accounted for about 18% of tests and the authors reported relatively poor bolus formation in that group. All comparisons concern performance with a diagnostic bolus and the report gives no ingredient contrast. [2020 NHO P-891, page 1544](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000144330.pdf#page=1544)

## Mizu yokan after individualized swallowing training

Morimoto described an 84-year-old with stage V Parkinson and recurrent pneumonia who had stopped eating orally. Training included cough, breathing and tongue exercises, oral care and speech therapy. At about three months the patient began direct practice with a preferred mizu yōkan, a soft bean-paste jelly, and recovered some supplementary oral intake. The abstract gives no ingredient list or comparison with other foods. [2020 NHO P-896, page 1549](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000144330.pdf#page=1549)

## Esophageal dilation on static CT

Kawamura and colleagues compared static esophageal diameter in 39 patients with Parkinson and 12 with multiple system atrophy. Upper thoracic diameter reached at least 10 mm in 69.2% and 58.3%, respectively. The cervical proportions were 20.5% and 66.7%. The 2021 report added an ALS comparison and appears to reuse the Parkinson and MSA groups. The imaging used no test bolus. [2020 NHO P-914, page 1567](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000144330.pdf#page=1567) · [2021 NHO P-1010, page 1465](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000164406.pdf#page=1465)

## Chewing and swallowing screen in 44 patients

The team assessed 44 patients with Parkinson with tongue-pressure testing, a glucose-sensor chewing measure and EAT-10. The authors reported low tongue pressure in 54%, swallowing impairment in 52% and reduced chewing performance in 20%. The abstract names the sensor method and gives no test food or habitual food exposure. [2021 NHO O-192, page 366](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000164406.pdf#page=366)

## Yogurt and soft rice porridge during rehabilitation

Watanabe and Iwaki reported a person with stage III Parkinson, coughing on water before a period of serious cardiac and renal illness. Initial swallowing care included thickened water in a side-lying position. The person began taking a few mouthfuls of yogurt while seated on day 116 and finished a container by day 150. By day 220 they were taking half a paste-texture meal, and by day 246 they were eating three soft rice-porridge meals. The Food Intake LEVEL Scale rose from 2 to 8, and weight from 51.1 to 56.2 kg during recovery and therapy. Yogurt preparation and handwriting were unreported. [2021 NHO P-0708, page 1163](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000164406.pdf#page=1163)

## Speech and swallowing care in three patients

A Parkinson care pathway assessed communication and swallowing in three patients. The mean Mann Assessment of Swallowing Ability score changed from 194.3 to 196. The authors reported little swallowing change and gave no food names. [2021 NHO P-0709, page 1164](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000164406.pdf#page=1164)

## Yogurt question during nutrition counseling

The Tottori hospital service reported 29 individual nutrition counseling encounters and seven group sessions. One participant asked which yogurt to choose. The report records the question without an intervention, yogurt comparison or follow-up symptom outcome. [2022 NHO O56-2, page 603](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000186584.pdf#page=603)

## Jelly after gastrostomy

Nakagawa and colleagues followed a patient with Parkinson and prior stroke after gastrostomy. With multidisciplinary swallowing care the patient began with thickened water, tried jelly at about one month and was taking jelly for pleasure three days a week by two months. The abstract identifies the texture and reports no jelly ingredients or concurrent comparison. [2022 NHO O60-1, page 626](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000186584.pdf#page=626)

## Glucose monitoring during tube feeding

The study monitored ten bedbound patients on tube feeds, six with Parkinson or parkinsonism. All ten had glucose nadirs at or below 70 mg/dL. Outcomes were pooled and the report gives no ingredient-level exposure or Parkinson-specific estimate. [2022 NHO P2-2-990, page 1626](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000186584.pdf#page=1626)

## Fixed contrast jelly in the Utano cohort

The 2022 report assessed motor severity and swallowing mechanics in 184 patients with Parkinson using a single 3 mL jelly bolus. Six of eight oral findings and all four timing measures had nominal associations with UPDRS III. None of eight pharyngeal findings did. The cohort size, 2005–2015 accrual dates, hospital, prior-pneumonia exclusion and bolus match Tomita and colleagues' 2018 aspiration-pneumonia prediction paper. Treat the reports as one cohort. The 2018 team observed 25 subsequent aspiration-pneumonia cases over six months. Their jelly-based scale had 92% sensitivity and 82% specificity at the selected cutoff in the same cohort used to build it. The contrast jelly was a common test stimulus and the study did not compare foods. [2022 NHO P2-2-1102, page 1739](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000186584.pdf#page=1739) · [2018 local full text](../2015-2019/Source-Text/PMC5991364.txt) · [2018 Dryad record](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.g0v07)

## Energy intake and odor identification in the Utano survey

Park and colleagues reported a food-frequency questionnaire, body composition and odor identification in 93 people with Parkinson and 57 controls. Reported energy intake was higher in Parkinson in men (2,267 vs 2,046 kcal/day, p = .028) and women (1,952 vs 1,742 kcal/day, p = .020). The Parkinson group had lower BMI, body fat and odor identification. Odor identification showed no detected association with energy intake or BMI. The 2021 report identifies June 2015–June 2016 recruitment and reports no adjusted association with low BMI. The same authors, sample sizes and numerical results identify one survey across the two abstracts. Neither abstract gives the questionnaire item list or food-level results. [2021 neurology society Pj-21-9, S318](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/clinicalneurol/61/Supplement/61_61_supplement_S269/_pdf/-char/ja#page=50) · [2022 NHO P2-2-1105, page 1742](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000186584.pdf#page=1742)

## Mealtime fatigue after a change in feeding setup

Irie and colleagues changed seating and arm support and practiced using the other hand for feeding in a stage IV Parkinson case. Mealtime fell from over 60 minutes to about 30 minutes and the patient reported less fatigue. The report names the changes to feeding setup and gives no food names. [2022 NHO P2-2-1261, page 1897](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000186584.pdf#page=1897)

## Lunch and levodopa variability during continuous intestinal infusion

Miyaue and colleagues compared afternoons with and without lunch in ten patients with Parkinson receiving levodopa-carbidopa intestinal gel. Between noon and 6 p.m. the coefficient of variation for plasma levodopa was 16.73% without lunch and 9.22% with lunch (p = .004). Mean concentration (p = .49) and the area under the concentration curve (p = .27) showed no detected differences. The 2019 Ehime registration specifies a lunch containing 20–25 g of protein. The published abstract gives no menu items or quantitative estimate for daily OFF time. The 2021 Hosokawa conference abstract reported a smaller sample of six from the same team using the same meal comparison. It found larger drug concentration variation without lunch and reported no obvious clinical symptom change between the two study days. These reports are treated as one study family. [2021 primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.parkreldis.2021.09.001) · [Authors' indexed abstract](https://researchers.office.ehime-u.ac.jp/html/100000121_ja.html) · [2021 neurology society Pj-09-4, S288](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/clinicalneurol/61/Supplement/61_61_supplement_S269/_pdf/-char/ja#page=20) · [2019 registration](../2015-2019/Source-Text/Trials/UMIN000037387.txt)

## Afternoon motor worsening and levodopa metabolites at Utano

Kohsaka and colleagues evaluated motor scores and plasma levodopa and 3-O-methyldopa hourly from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. in patients using intestinal levodopa gel. Their June 2023 poster included 17 patients, ten with afternoon motor worsening. Their later 2023 NHO report included 18 patients, 11 with worsening. The author list, center, sampling schedule and grouping suggest overlapping reports. In the later report the metabolite-to-levodopa ratio trajectory differed between groups (p = .004), with no clear decline in levodopa itself to account for evening worsening. Two of three people with measurements after adding a COMT inhibitor had lower evening motor scores. Neither abstract describes a meal intervention or named ingredient. This Utano series is distinct from the 2021 Ehime lunch study. [2023 neurology society Pj-085-5, S315](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/clinicalneurol/63/Supplement/63_63_supplement_S237/_pdf/-char/ja#page=79) · [2023 NHO P1-107-4, page 430](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000204087.pdf#page=430)
