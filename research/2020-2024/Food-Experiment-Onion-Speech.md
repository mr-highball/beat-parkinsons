# Onion exposure and speech intelligibility

## Sofrito in a Mediterranean diet pilot in diagnosed Parkinson disease

In the five-week pilot published on 23 December 2021, eight people with Parkinson disease completed the Mediterranean diet intervention. Researchers encouraged cooking with sofrito at least twice per week and used the Mediterranean Diet Adherence Screener as a diary. The PREDIMED questionnaire can include onion, leek or garlic simmered with tomato and olive oil. The protocol therefore allows more than one Allium species and does not establish the amount of cooked bulb consumed. Baseline MDS-UPDRS scores were reported by part. The clinical outcomes during treatment concerned constipation, other gastrointestinal complaints and fecal measures. The before-after reflux scores were 1.31 and 1.06 (p=.50), without a speech-specific outcome. The later eight-week randomized trial reported baseline MDS-UPDRS in the in-person subset and focused on constipation and related endpoints. ## Sofrito exposure fields and the speech question in the MVP study

Fox and colleagues reported a cross-sectional analysis of 1,205 people with parkinsonism. Table S1 gives a self-rated Speech item in PRO-PD. Table S2 asks how often participants ate dishes seasoned with sofrito, and the Methods explicitly accept tomato sauce in a positive response. Table S4 estimated +45.0 PRO-PD total points for the sofrito item (reported interval −11.6 to +102, p=.5). Published results do not link this item to the speech rating. Even with a speech response, the adapted screener crossing this ingredient would require actual recipe and preparation information. [Primary MVP report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9739738/) · [Dated nutrition evidence note](Food-Experiment-Clinical-Nutrition-Followup.md#non-oil-items-in-the-2022-mvp-diet-analysis) · [Local supplement](Source-Text/2022-Fox-MIND-MEDI-Supplement.txt)

## Onion intake and hoarseness within a Kashmir reflux series

Abbas, Masoodi and Mir described 130 patients treated with lifestyle changes, antacids and proton pump inhibitors, with follow-up at four weeks. Table 2 gives onion a mean food-frequency score of 1.74. Table 5 reports mean hoarseness ratings from 0.92 to 0.32 for the multi-component intervention. There was no onion-specific hoarseness estimate, culinary preparation or Parkinson subgroup. The text labels overall dietary-score associations with vocal-fold and diffuse laryngeal edema significant while printing p=.45 and .49. Those printed values do not support significance at the conventional .05 level. ## Onion and garlic grouped in a Croatian reflux cohort

Prpić and colleagues analyzed 100 patients with laryngopharyngeal reflux and 65 controls in a nonrandomized study. The FFQ pooled onion and garlic without cooking or portion detail. The grouped intake score was 3 (IQR 2–4) in patients and 2 (1–3) in controls at baseline. Among patients, the grouped median fell from 3 to 2 at 30 days and remained 2 at 60 days during treatment with proton pump inhibitors and diet and lifestyle changes. The study reports outcomes based on reflux-related quality of life, including its voice domain, with no separate onion-specific voice or intelligibility result. [Prpić primary article, Tables 4 and 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10775783/) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC10775783-Prpic-Diet-Laryngeal-Reflux.txt)

## Onion and garlic extracts and respiratory symptom counts in older adults

The published results of NCT04647071 report 65 older residents in the Methods and describe randomization to a capsule with concentrated onion extract plus garlic powder or placebo. The study followed cough and throat pain separately, among other respiratory or general symptoms. The between-group throat-pain estimate in Table 3 was inconclusive (p=.599). The extract mix, onset of acute infectious symptoms, and absence of a voice or speech measure limit transfer to any effect of cooked onion on Parkinson speech. [Primary results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10221607/) · [Trial registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04647071) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC10221607-Garlic-Onion-Respiratory-Symptoms.txt)

## Heated water onion peel extract and self rated hoarseness

Cho and colleagues randomized 80 adults with a recent history of upper respiratory symptoms and elevated perceived stress. Participants took 1,000 mg/day of onion peel extract or placebo for eight weeks. The extract came from dried outer peel using hot water, concentration and freeze-drying. Table 3 reports hoarseness means of 1.35 to 0.73 in the extract arm and 0.24 to 0.70 in the placebo arm, with a reported adjusted comparison of changes at p=.038. Baseline values differed. The total WURSS-21 symptom comparison was p=.164 and the full cohort's primary natural killer cell comparison was inconclusive. The trial measured a self-rated upper respiratory symptom, without listener testing, cooked onion bulb or a Parkinson population. [Cho primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10861340/) · [Trial registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05666752) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2024-Cho-Onion-Peel-Hoarseness-Extract.txt)


## Registered onion capsule mass and extract amount

The registration specifies four capsules per day and labels their total 1,200 mg/day in both groups. The published Methods give 249.9 mg onion peel extract powder, 49.2 mg cellulose and 0.9 mg silica per active capsule. Those components total 300 mg per capsule. Four capsules contain 999.6 mg of extract powder, which explains the reported 1,000 mg/day active extract. The instrument assessed upper respiratory symptoms, including self-rated hoarseness, without a speech intelligibility measurement. The registered preparation was heated-water peel extract.
