# Raspberry source integrity and human mood outcomes

## Raspberry leaf use in a pregnancy survey

Gantner et al. published a cross-sectional survey on October 8, 2021. Table 3 compares self-reported raspberry-leaf tea use in participants with or without broadly defined mental disorders or symptoms. The proportions were 76/172 (44.2%) and 74/157 (47.1%), respectively (p = .552). The report gives no frozen-fruit exposure or controlled anxiety response for raspberry leaf. ## Retracted red raspberry extract report

Chen et al. published an extract experiment in a chronic unpredictable mild stress rat model on July 2, 2022. The paper described locomotion and exploration tests, including open-field and elevated plus-maze measures, within a study centered on depression-like behavior. Its formal retraction excludes it from efficacy assessment. The exposure was raspberry extract and the population was a stress model. ## Publisher retraction of the raspberry extract report

The journal published its notice on June 21, 2023. It identifies the 2022 DOI and says the investigation detected signs of potential publication-process manipulation that prevent the journal from vouching for the report. The notice lists possible indicators and leaves author awareness or involvement unassessed. The article and notice constitute one study family. ## Unripe black raspberry extract in a male climacteric trial

Jung et al. published a 12-week randomized parallel pilot on July 26, 2023. Thirty men were randomized equally between unripe black raspberry extract and placebo. The between-group comparison for the total Aging Male’s Symptoms score was p = .967 in Table 4. The publication reports the total composite and distinct urinary outcomes. It provides no separately reported anxiety estimate for the culinary fruit or a Parkinson group.
