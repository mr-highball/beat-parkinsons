# Rosemary, saffron and odor trials, 2020-2024

## Cinnamon among five odors in postinfectious smell training

Choi and colleagues compared three months of odor training with observation in 104 patients with postinfectious olfactory dysfunction. The 40 training participants used rose, lemon, cinnamon, orange and peach odorants; 64 were observed. The threshold-discrimination-identification total went from 17.5 to 22.1 and from 15.6 to 18.3, respectively. The reported adjusted repeated-measures group comparison gave p=.018. The Results print identical statistics for identification and discrimination while describing different significance for them. This contrasts a multi-odor program with observation, with no separate cinnamon exposure or Parkinson food outcome. ## Recorded multiodor cognitive program in dementia

The first public registry entry followed the stated January 31, 2021 study completion. The 30-participant pilot registered cognitive games that used rosemary essential oil among 15 odors, a board-game group and a control. It included the Geriatric Depression Scale. The official API has no posted registry results. The matching 2022 paper reports one comparison across these groups, with rosemary used as a smell. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT05168098) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2021-lin-reg-Clinical-Source-Extract.txt)

## Dementia pilot using multiple odors including rosemary

Lin and colleagues analyzed 28 of 30 randomized dementia participants. Median depression scores changed from 3 to 0 in the odor program, 2 to 0 in the board-game group and 1 to 0 in the control group. The group sizes were 9, 10 and 9 and the reported posttest comparison was inconclusive. Rosemary was one essential oil among several scents used. The trial gives no separate rosemary estimate and studied neither dietary leaves nor Parkinson depressive symptoms. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9531625/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2022-lin-Clinical-Source-Extract.txt)

## Rosemary in a mixed essential-oil massage trial

Mehrabian and colleagues compared an essential-oil massage blend with control in nursing-home residents, analyzing 19 per arm. The intervention contained lavender, chamomile and rosemary in a jojoba carrier. The mean HADS depression rating for the intervention group reached 4.15 at week seven and the repeated-measures depression caption reports F(3,36)=17.23, p<.001. Groups differed at baseline and the text and baseline table print different depression comparators. The design combines oils and massage, without a daily oral rosemary or Parkinson exposure. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8887855/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2022-mehrabian-Clinical-Source-Extract.txt)

## Inhaled rosemary oil and depression symptom scores

Alvarado-García and colleagues assigned existing classes to inhale rosemary essential oil or remain on a waiting list, analyzing 37 and 36 participants. Zung depression scores went from 49.65 to 45.86 with the oil and from 49.39 to 49.81 in the comparison group. Posttest p=.020 was reported for the group contrast. It was not a comparison of changes. The rosemary leaves were sourced fresh, then dried and hydrodistilled. Course allocation, an unblinded waiting-list comparison, and the inhaled product limit transfer to dietary leaves and Parkinson disease.
