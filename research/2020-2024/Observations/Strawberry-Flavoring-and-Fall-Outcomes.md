# Strawberry flavoring and fall outcomes

The earlier [1998 rat prevention study](../../1995-1999/Source-Text/1998-PMID9742171-Joseph-Strawberry-Prevention-Maze-Study.txt) assigned dried strawberry extract. It found a tissue dopamine response, while the reported water-maze improvements belonged to the spinach and vitamin E groups. The [2018 older-women cohort](../../2015-2019/Source-Text/2018-PMID30153877-Sim-Fruit-Injurious-Falls.txt) linked food-frequency records to fall admissions. It grouped strawberries with six other fruits and estimated a hazard ratio of 1.00 (95% CI 0.98–1.03) for the group.

The [2024 Parkinson trial](../Source-Text/2024-PMID38487964-Sesame-Carrier-Rest-Tremor-Extract.txt) closes a population gap. It counts people with at least one fall in a trial that used strawberry extract. Both arms received the extract, so the fall count comparison applies to the cannabinoid assignment. The [2020 rehabilitation trial](../Source-Text/2020-PMID32961041-Sarcopenia-Rehabilitation-and-Shared-Strawberry-Flavor.txt) similarly used strawberry and vanilla in both arms and reported the effect of the assigned nutrition formula on gait. A separate [2026 protocol](../../2025-2029/Source-Text/2026-PMID41835341-TROFFi-Fisetin-and-Falls-Protocol.txt) proposes fall recall after isolated fisetin in a cancer-survivor population and has no results yet.

The [First Foods account](../Cultural-and-Experiential/Local-Knowledge/2024-NY-First-Foods-Wild-Strawberry-Context.txt) preserves a reported use and food-access context. The [habitual-diet study](../Source-Text/2024-PMID38779015-Strawberry-Seasonality-in-Habitual-Diets.txt) provides documented human intake and seasonality. These sources contain no symptom comparison. A study with a separate raw strawberry comparison and recurring falls in Parkinson disease would address the main remaining evidence gap.
