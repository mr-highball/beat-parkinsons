# Shiitake texture and swallowing outcomes

The earlier sources establish distinct adverse events. In 1992, endoscopy identified a lower-esophageal obstruction. The 2009 clinical letter described transient symptoms and suspected esophageal inflammation. In 2019, the obstructing material was identified as shiitake in a person with multiple sclerosis. The case settings leave mushroom preparation and population frequency unresolved. [1992 series](../../1990-1994/Source-Text/1992-Komaki-Shiitake-Esophageal-Impaction.txt) · [2009 case](../../2005-2009/Source-Text/2009-PMID19783350-Shiitake-Esophageal-Allergy-Case.txt) · [2019 meeting member](../../2015-2019/Source-Text/2019-Terada-Shiitake-Airway-Foreign-Body.txt)

The 2023 food experiment gives a preparation comparison. Longer oven cooking reduced measured hardness and chewiness while increasing the magnitude of the adhesion reading. These properties can differ within the same preparation. The 2024 Parkinson cohort established that bolus volume and viscosity alter instrumental measures in the same clinical condition, but its manometry protocol did not test solid food. A study linking food-specific texture and chewing to observed clinical swallowing outcomes would address a bridge left open by these separate source families.
