# Tarragon uses and pain outcome boundaries

A 2021 scholarly review attributes toothache and skin-related uses to earlier publications. The citations do not establish how often tarragon was used or how participants responded. [Attributed-use record](../Cultural-and-Experiential/Traditional-Practices/2021-PMC8076785-Tarragon-Attributed-Toothache-Uses.txt)

The prior 2010–2014 bucket contains both a critical review of customary topical use and an animal study using injected essential oil. Their preparation, route and pain outcome differ from one another and from the frozen fresh-leaf claim. The 2026 primary knee study measures WOMAC pain after topical application, with the clearest difference immediately after treatment. [Earlier traditional review](../../2010-2014/Cultural-and-Experiential/Traditional-Practices/2011-PMID21942448-Tarragon-Eurasian-Persian-French-Traditions-Toxicology.txt) · [2013 rodent study](../../2010-2014/Source-Text/2013-PMID24074293-Tarragon-Essential-Oil-and-Nociception.txt) · [Knee pain trial audit](../../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Tarragon-Savory-Oregano.md)

The collection contains multiple plausible pain contexts for this ingredient. The distinct customary, injected, and topical exposures cannot establish a response after oral fresh tarragon in Parkinson disease.
