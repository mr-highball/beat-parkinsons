# Dried fig magnesium and visual symptom timing

The USDA analysis identifies magnesium in dried uncooked figs. A 2023 hospital series describes delirium with visual hallucinations, severe low serum magnesium and cerebellar edema in case 2. Symptoms improved overall after intravenous magnesium was started, following other treatment changes. The report does not describe the separate time course or number of hallucinations. Low magnesium recurred with gait symptoms a month later, without reported cognitive symptoms. [2019 USDA composition](../../2015-2019/Source-Text/2019-FDC174665-Dried-Fig-Pantothenic-Acid.txt) · [2023 case series, case 2](../Source-Text/PMC10272920.txt).

The 2011 case presentation establishes that an hours-long visual episode had resolved before magnesium replacement, even though the abstract pairs later overall improvement with supplementation. In 2024, visual hallucinations appeared after the initial serum magnesium correction and persisted until additional treatment. These timelines limit attributing the visual symptoms to magnesium alone. The original reports concern acute electrolyte disturbance and clinical replacement. They supply no ordinary dried fig or Parkinson visual episode-frequency comparison. [2011 case](../../2010-2014/Source-Text/PMC3233929.txt) · [2024 case](../Source-Text/PMC11392027.txt).

A separate NHANES analysis compares reported magnesium intake and a depletion score against Parkinson disease status. Higher depletion score was associated with higher odds of recorded disease, whereas the main analysis did not establish a clear dietary magnesium association. This corroborates the need to distinguish intake, systemic magnesium markers and symptoms. The earlier visual source observations make a parallel distinction between vitamin B5 in the food and its concentration in tissue. [Publisher abstract and release date](../Source-Text/2024-PMID39465480-Dietary-Magnesium-and-Parkinson-Prevalence.txt) · [Earlier constituent and symptom observations](Dried-Fig-Constituent-and-Visual-Episode-Measures.md).

The early Parkinson study reports different perceptual experiences and combines frequency and duration in one scale. The other questionnaire identifies visual modality separately from the frequency of the most severe reported psychotic experience. The phantom-boarder study distinguishes sensed presence from complex visual events. The cholinergic imaging paper defines a yes/no visual hallucination measure and finds no clear association within its small dementia with Lewy bodies group. The commentary reuses that study. A plasma study also reuses a previously collected biomarker and draws from the longitudinal programme that included cardiac MIBG measurements. The exact patient overlap is unavailable. [Early Parkinson scale comparison](../Source-Text/PMC10657660.txt) · [Sensorimotor study](../Source-Text/PMC10105112.txt) · [Cholinergic PET paper](../Source-Text/2023-PMID37279796-Cholinergic-PET-and-Hallucination-Presence.txt) · [Plasma study](../Source-Text/PMC10755229.txt).

The EU register identifies NCT02702076 as the same study as EudraCT 2016-000102-11. Entry requires visual hallucinations more than three times per week, with onset after Parkinson diagnosis. The four-week primary endpoint is a global severity judgment. The U.S. record separately names the Dutch Visual Hallucinations Questionnaire among its secondary measures. Neither registry provides posted outcomes for this study. TOLEDO is a separate motor-fluctuation study with a different registration and treatment objective. Its adverse-event reports are not measurements of improvement among patients with refractory visual hallucinations.
