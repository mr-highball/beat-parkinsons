# Dried fig extracts and visual biomarker comparisons

Three different measurements can enter a single narrative about Parkinson visual symptoms. The 2024 paper predicts a change in a metabolite using transcriptome-based modeling. The 1996 study recorded an association between urinary salsolinol and established visual hallucination status among levodopa-treated Parkinson participants. The 1989 feeding study recorded urinary enantiomers after dried banana in healthy participants. These studies have different exposures and outcomes. [2024 modeling paper](../Source-Text/PMC10770157.txt) · [Parkinson biomarker abstract](../../1995-1999/Source-Text/1996-PMID9617786-Urinary-Salsolinol-and-Parkinson-Visual-History-Abstract.txt) · [Dried banana feeding abstract](../../1985-1989/Source-Text/1989-PMID2754429-Dried-Banana-and-Urinary-Salsolinol-Abstract.txt).

The banana tissue assay compares peel and pulp by ripeness. Its measured concentration varies with both. The later enzyme paper studies banana pulp oxidation outside the body. Neither reports concentrations in intact dried common figs. These establish a path for measuring exposure and checking biomarker confounding. [Original banana tissue comparison](../../1975-1979/Source-Text/1976-PMID1245664-Salsolinol-in-Banana-Pulp-and-Peel.txt) · [Banana enzyme assay](../../2000-2004/Source-Text/2000-PMID11087516-Banana-Pulp-Oxidation-and-Salsolinol-Abstract.txt).

The full 2008 fig extract paper authenticates ripe dried material and distinguishes isolated rabbit gut responses from an ex vivo human platelet assay. The platelet concentration differs between the abstract and body. The prior 2016 controlled trial tested a supplied food paste and measured bowel outcomes. This set leaves the clinical dried fig and Parkinson visual episode outcome open.
