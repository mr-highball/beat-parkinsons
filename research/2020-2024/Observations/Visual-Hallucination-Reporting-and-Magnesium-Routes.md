# Visual symptom reporting and treatment routes

This source group distinguishes daytime visual hallucinations, visual illusions, sensed presence, visuospatial test performance and future dementia. In the preceding catalog work, prospective cohorts documented visual symptom onset and sleep studies described separate dream phenomena. The current sources add different observers and measures. [2015–2019 clinical context](../../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Recovered-Parkinson-Clinical-Context.md).

A case series reports daytime visual hallucinations and dream enactment ceasing within two days of starting an oral seven-ingredient Kampo extract in one person with Parkinson disease. The six-month statement concerns parasomnia recurrence. There was no comparator, fig ingredient or separately measured response to cinnamon or ginger. [Clinical original](../Source-Text/PMC9152866.txt) · [Cinnamon and ginger preparation context](../Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#cinnamon-bark-and-ginger-formula-in-a-parkinson-dream-enactment-case).

A 328-person Parkinson cohort distinguishes caregiver, patient and clinician instruments. Its adjusted prediction analysis tracks later dementia. The ocular baseline comparison and telephone follow-up give different visual symptom states within the same cohort. A nested 54-person comparison reports more frequent and longer hallucination events than illusion events using the Psycho-Sensory Hallucinations Scale. A separate Lewy body imaging study defines sensed presence without seeing or hearing a person. These sources do not establish the number of visual hallucinations following dried fig consumption. [Cohort instruments](../Source-Text/PMC8015884.txt) · [Ocular baseline](../Source-Text/2020-PMID32447550-Parkinson-Illusions-and-Retina.txt) · [Follow-up](../Source-Text/2022-PMID35296961-Parkinson-Visual-Phenotype-Followup.txt) · [Nested frequency measurement](../Source-Text/2021-PMID34238577-Parkinson-Visual-Phenomenology.txt) · [Sensed presence](../Source-Text/PMC8403753.txt).

The 1960–1964 records supply an earlier route comparison. In surgical cases, ongoing gastrointestinal losses and the ability to absorb orally administered salts changed the observed response. Orange juice carried the oral salt in one case. Neither case records a separately identified visual hallucination or Parkinson diagnosis. The 1960 review describes continuing delirium after magnesium normalization in the original case family, whose body remains open.
