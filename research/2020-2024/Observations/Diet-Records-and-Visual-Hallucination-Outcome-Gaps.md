# Diet records and visual hallucination outcome gaps

An earlier multiethnic study measured nonmotor domains including perception and hallucinations. Its recorded food groups include fresh fruit. The exploratory nonmotor analysis compares dietary patterns, supplements and selected herbs or drinks, with no item-specific result for dried fig. The combined perception domain and its multiple-comparison result do not establish exposure-linked visual episode frequency. [Original questionnaire and analysis](../../2015-2019/Source-Text/PMC6312592.txt).

DUPARC collected a three-day dietary diary in its Dutch microbiome cohort and used nutrient variables as potential confounders. The paired Finnish analysis used a simpler dietary questionnaire. Sequence data are public under PRJEB55464 and sample metadata are by request. The original output does not publish itemized meal diaries or a hallucination analysis. An independent food-history study analyzes diet quality, fiber and added sugar in relation to the microbiome. [DUPARC protocol](../Source-Text/PMC7293131.txt) · [Reported microbiome results](../Source-Text/PMC9551094.txt) · [Sequence accession](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/ena/browser/view/PRJEB55464) · [Independent diet-history study](../Source-Text/2024-PMID38649365-Diet-History-and-Parkinson-Microbiome.txt).

Genetic analyses repeatedly report associations for generic dried fruit and Parkinson diagnosis. UK Biobank field 1319 defines counts of a broad food category. Its examples establish the unit of a piece and supply no fig-specific outcome. The analyses have overlapping data sources and make different adjustments. Keep the clinical diagnosis endpoint separate from symptoms among diagnosed participants.
