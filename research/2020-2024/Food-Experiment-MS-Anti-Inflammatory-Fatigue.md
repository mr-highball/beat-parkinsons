# Anti-inflammatory diet, oil and tea in a multiple-sclerosis fatigue trial

## Mixed-diet fatigue effects and endpoint extraction

Mousavi-Shirazi-Fard and colleagues first published the trial on April 16, 2020. In the accessible author abstract, the personalized anti-inflammatory diet favored the diet arm over healthy-diet recommendations on the Modified Fatigue Impact Scale at 12 weeks (p = .001). The abstract describes 100 participants. NICE's independent study extraction records 104 randomized and 100 analyzed, with 50 analyzed per arm. It lists canola and olive oil for cooking or salad dressing and allows green or white tea within the overall dietary prescription. The extraction lists spices generally and does not name cinnamon or verify individual food consumption or preparation. [Publisher author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32249637/) · [NICE intervention and outcome tables, printed pp. 524–528](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585277/bin/niceng220er3-app-et1.pdf#page=524)

NICE reports mean MFIS totals of 47.96 to 47.22 for the diet arm and 47.84 to 47.92 for controls. Its unadjusted endpoint difference is −0.70 points (95% CI −5.34 to 3.94). This is a different analysis from the favorable p value reported in the article's abstract. The original outcome table and model specification remain unverified. Neither source isolates refined canola oil or brewed green tea, and the population has multiple sclerosis. The trial registration lists the planned fatigue outcome and the two diet groups. [NICE endpoint comparison, printed pp. 1144–1145](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585277/bin/niceng220er3-app-et1.pdf#page=1144) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2020-Mousavi-Shirazi-Fard-MS-Diet-Fatigue.txt) · [2018 registration](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-MS-Anti-Inflammatory-Registration.md)

## Fatigue subscales and thesis access

NICE Appendix D, pp. 523–529, gives the following baseline and week-12 values in the diet and advice groups. The note describes 50 analyzed participants per arm despite headings identifying 52 randomized per arm. The extraction is secondary. The original publisher confirms a thesis-derived study funded under project 96-16241. Its abstract gives p=.001 for fatigue, and the exact original model, tables and thesis were not recovered. No isolated canola intake, refining grade or Parkinson outcome is reported.

| MFIS subscale | Mixed diet at baseline and week 12 | Comparison at baseline and week 12 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical | 22.42 (6.39) to 22.18 (6.37) | 22.90 (4.19) to 22.98 (4.21) |
| Cognitive | 22.58 (7.88) to 22.24 (7.80) | 22.68 (8.18) to 22.72 (8.20) |
| Psychosocial | 2.96 (1.89) to 2.66 (1.81) | 2.24 (1.39) to 2.28 (1.40) |

Values in parentheses are standard deviations. NICE rated the total MFIS evidence at high risk of bias. Its endpoint contrast was inconclusive, with a mean difference of −0.70 (95% CI −5.34 to 3.94).
