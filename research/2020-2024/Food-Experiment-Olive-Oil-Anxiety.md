# Olive oil preparations and anxiety outcomes

## Aged rats and open-field behavior

Kimizoglu and colleagues followed 34 older female rats in high-polyphenol EVOO, low-polyphenol olive oil and distilled-water groups. Both assigned oil groups received oral gavage. The 2022 author abstract reports no statistically clear open-field anxiety-like behavioral difference after six weeks. It provides no group-level estimate or uncertainty interval. Certain neurogenesis markers improved with the higher-polyphenol oil. ## Injected olive phenolics and exploratory activity

The Kokras experiment found more open-field center entries after two doses of injected olive-oil phenolics in female mice. Lab staff also recorded increased vertical activity at both doses and increased horizontal movement at the lower dose. Increased activity gives an alternative explanation for the center visits. An independent oleacein experiment observed less immobility in tail suspension after oral isolated oleacein in an inflammatory mouse model. Tail suspension was a depression-like outcome and the study did not report a separate anxiety assay. ## Shared lycopene-enriched oil and anxiety reporting

The Carrasco pilot gave the same proprietary lycopene-enriched extra-virgin oil to ten men with prostate enlargement and ten described as healthy. Anxiety was an objective. The abstract reports selected sleep, prostate and antioxidant outcomes and no anxiety estimate. The full report remains open. The two groups offer no way to isolate olive oil from lycopene, and neither group enrolled people for Parkinson disease. ## Original DieTBra commentary trail

The official DieTBra record identifies three active groups and HADS change as a secondary outcome. The original results abstract reports within-arm changes. The methodologist's letter describes a post-treatment anxiety comparison favoring the diet-only arm. The authors published their reply in November 2020. Its argument and group estimates remain unread. The trial, its registration and both letters belong to one clinical evidence family.
