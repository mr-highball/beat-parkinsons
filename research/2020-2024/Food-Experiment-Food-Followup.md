# Food experiment follow-up evidence

## Asparagus extract preparation and fatigue in shift workers

Sakai and colleagues published a single-hospital before-and-after study of a food containing asparagus extract in March 2021. Thirty-three of 34 shift-working nurses completed the study, and 29 contributed the fatigue comparison. Table 2 reports that the visual analogue fatigue score fell from 5.83 to 4.31 (paired p = .003), which the results interpret as improvement. The stated scale anchors in the methods put the least tired response at the high end. The paper does not explain whether scores were reversed for analysis, so the direction remains ambiguous. There was no control group or Parkinson population. [Primary report, pp. 18 and 20–21](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/juoeh/43/1/43_15/_pdf/-char/en) · [Local 2018 registration note](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#asparagus-extract-in-shift-workers-trial-registration)

## Asparagus extract and sleep trial registration

UMIN000052913 was first publicly disclosed on December 1, 2023. The record describes a blinded crossover of standardized asparagus stem extract and placebo in healthy male day workers with poor sleep. Sleep EEG was the primary outcome and the OSA-MA sleep questionnaire was secondary. The registered planned start was May 6, 2023, before public disclosure. The later report names the registration and presents the sleep and awakening outcomes. [Primary registration](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000060374) · [Registry history](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_his_list.cgi?recptno=R000060374) · [Local 2025 report note](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#asparagus-stem-extract-and-awakening-symptoms)

## Boiled potato as a videofluoroscopic test bolus

Kim and colleagues reviewed swallow studies in 52 hospitalized people with dysphagia and without neurological disease. Boiled potato coated with barium served as the standardized solid test bolus. The analysis examined factors associated with airway invasion across the swallow evaluation. The study did not estimate change after baked potato and did not enroll a Parkinson cohort. [Local primary full text](Source-Text/PMC7647595.txt#L95) · [Primary report, PMID 33157940](https://doi.org/10.1097/MD.0000000000022977)

## Matcha and task-related fatigue in young adults

Baba, Kaneko, and Takihara randomized 42 adults aged 25–34 to matcha or placebo. Participants in the matcha group consumed 2 g daily for two weeks before a laboratory psychological stress task. The study reported better performance on selected attention and emotion-perception measures, with no clear difference in subjective fatigue. The matcha preparation and healthy young population differ from brewed green tea and fatigue in Parkinson’s in `0948`. [Primary report, PMID 33744591](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33744591/) · [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nutres.2020.12.024)

## Orange flavor in a Parkinson's constipation trial

Ibrahim and colleagues randomized 55 people with Parkinson’s and functional constipation. Forty-eight completed eight weeks of Hexbio or placebo. The active product included multiple probiotic strains and fructo-oligosaccharide, while both sachets had orange flavoring. The mean between-group difference at eight weeks was 1.37 bowel movements per week (95% CI 0.68–2.07), with a −36.22-hour difference in whole-gut transit time (95% CI −68.90 to −3.54). The other eight constipation questionnaire items did not clearly differ. The study tested the probiotic formulation in Parkinson’s. The shared flavor gives no raw-orange result for `0878`. The trial was registered retrospectively, with first posting in June 2020. A separate seven-day trial of Hexbio in older people with multimorbidity reported increased stool frequency and a taste-matched placebo. [Primary Parkinson’s report, Methods and Table 4](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0244680) · [Registration NCT04451096](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04451096) · [Earlier functional-constipation study](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#orange-flavored-probiotic-for-functional-constipation) · [Separate older-adult trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12876730/) · [Earlier registration notes](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#orange-juice-pomace-and-orange-flavored-probiotic-registrations)

## Japanese orange odor and Parkinson's symptom profiles

Iijima and colleagues examined 404 people with Parkinson’s. Japanese orange was an odor in the smell identification test. Table 4 compares mean MDS-UPDRS constipation item scores of 1.30 without versus 1.70 with probable REM sleep behavior disorder (p = .002). The comparison concerns sleep behavior status. Gait was evaluated clinically, and falls are discussed with reference to earlier research. There is no analysis of falls or constipation by eating orange (`0259`, `0878`). ## Boiled mashed potato in a swallowing sensory test

Eighteen healthy older adults rated immediate pharyngeal swallowing difficulty and sensation of residue after tasting nine commercial semisolid foods in blinded order. Table 1 reports means of 4.83 (SD 1.95) and 3.78 (SD 1.80), respectively, for boiled mashed potato on 9-point scales. Adhesiveness across the tested foods was correlated with perceived difficulty (r = .882, p = .002). The study used boiled mashed potato and measured immediate swallowing sensation. A separate case report documented endoscopic removal of a whole steamed potato after acute swallowing difficulty in a 77-year-old man. No Parkinson’s diagnosis was reported. Both reports differ in population and preparation from the frozen baked-potato and Parkinson’s swallowing hypothesis (`0290`). [Primary sensory study, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7684931/) · [Primary case report](https://www.jcdr.net/articles/PDF/18560/66437_CE%5BRa1%5D_F%28SS%29_QC%28KK_RDW_SS%29_PF1%28VD_KM%29_PFA_NC%28VM%29.pdf) · [Later texture-modified dish study](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#fork-mashable-potato-and-swallowing-in-older-inpatients)

## Diet-pattern scores, sofrito and poultry in Parkinson's

This cross-sectional analysis from the MVP study included 1,205 people reporting Parkinson’s or other forms of parkinsonism. Table 2 provides separate self-reported symptom estimates for overall Mediterranean and MIND adherence scores. Supplementary Table S4 links individual diet items to total PRO-PD. Sofrito, which included tomato sauce, was associated with +45.0 points (95% CI −11.6 to 102, reported p = .5). Preference for chicken, turkey or rabbit over red meat was associated with −27.7 points (95% CI −85.3 to 29.9, p = .3). The separate nonfried poultry item had an estimate of +60.1 (95% CI −5.33 to 125, p = .4). All three estimates were inconclusive. The study gives no estimate of cooked tomato and rigidity (`0544`), poached chicken and fatigue (`0348`), or an individual turkey–symptom contrast (`0755`). [Primary report, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9739738/) · [NLM Supplementary Table S4, page 3 of the PDF in the ZIP](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC9739738.1/nutrients-14-05185-s001.zip) · [2017 predecessor cohort analysis](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md)

## CheckPD questionnaire validation and coffee history

The 2024 Thai CheckPD methods paper compared self-reported coffee history by sex in a 100-person Parkinson’s questionnaire-reliability cohort (Table 3, p = .532). The methods and baseline table supply no change in fatigue or any other symptom attributable to individual foods. ## Diet interviews and oral microbiota conference reports

Ferguson and colleagues’ 2021 Gerontological Society of America abstract reports interviews with 11 older adults with Parkinson’s and their care partners. Participants described altering food groups and protein timing in response to symptoms and medication use. A 2022 paper reports an analysis of food-related activities with the same interview design and sample size. Treat the two reports as likely overlapping. The qualitative findings supply no preparation-specific symptom estimate. [2021 primary abstract, pp. 988–989](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8682295/) · [2022 primary report, PMID 36062816](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36062816/)

Fleury and colleagues’ Swiss Neurological Society abstract P38 reports food-frequency data for 20 people with Parkinson’s and 20 controls. The Parkinson’s group reported more of several combined food categories, including cauliflower/broccoli, and the abstract reports correlations between those categories and chronic constipation measures. It gives no direction or estimate for an individual food. Boiled potatoes and sunflower seed oil also appeared in the diet and were associated with salivary microbiota composition. The journal article from the same group used the same sample sizes and clinical stage and reports oral microbiota and inflammatory measurements. These likely overlapping reports supply no isolated result for raw or steamed broccoli (`0147`, `1000`) or baked or roasted potato (`0020`, `0290`). [Primary dietary abstract P38](https://www.mdpi.com/2514-183X/5/3/22) · [Likely overlapping primary cohort report, PMID 33646178](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33646178/)

## Date fruit extract and paraquat-related motor impairment in rats

Edobor and colleagues assigned 25 rats to five groups and assessed beam walking, oxidative stress biomarkers, and cortical and nigral histology after paraquat exposure. The report describes better beam-walking performance with aqueous fruit pulp extract and changes in some biochemical and histological measures. The study used an extract given during toxin exposure. Dried fruit, human Parkinson’s, pain, and apathy remain outside the experiment. ## Date palm sap and labor pain

Rahmani and colleagues analyzed 60 women assigned to date palm sap or a sugar-and-water control. ## Date syrup, honey-saffron syrup, and labor pain

In a three-arm study at Besat Hospital in Sanandaj, Sohrabi and colleagues reported pain ratings for 180 women in labor. Table 2 of the April 2022 Persian paper gives mean pain scores at the end of stage two of 7.25 with date syrup, 7.67 with honey-saffron syrup and 8.47 with placebo. Table 3 reports p = .015 for the two-syrup contrast at that stage, with inconclusive comparisons at the end of active labor (p = .928) and stage three (p = .652). Both syrup groups had lower scores than placebo at the three post-intervention measurements. The 0–10 pain scale measures acute labor pain. The companion report covers duration and analyzes 180 participants after 189 were allocated. The full reports identify the same IRCT and ethics IDs, establishing that they concern one trial. [Primary pain paper, Tables 2–3, PDF p. 7](https://ijogi.mums.ac.ir/article_20328_b4127a4ff9ad328f6991bcceb2b5a8eb.pdf#page=7) · [Companion labor-progress report, PMID 36275332](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9580570/)

The companion article identifies IRCT20190924044873N1. The January 12, 2020 registry entry describes an unblinded comparison with a target of 180 participants and a primary outcome covering labor-stage duration. The companion article reports assessor masking. The registered outcome list gives no separate pain measure. [Primary registration](https://irct.ir/trial/42486) · [Companion paper, Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9580570/#sec2-3)

Shalaby and colleagues compared no chocolate with seven chocolate formulations in a 48-participant dysmenorrhea study. All formulations contained Ajwa dates, dark chocolate, and other dried fruits, and six added herbs or oils. The study supplies no date-specific pain contrast for `0502`. ## Whole Ajwa dates and abdominal pain in children with autism

The 2023 Metwally and colleagues preprint analyzed 131 children who completed a 12-week study of three whole dates per day, five per day, or no dates. Table 3 gives rounded post-intervention abdominal-pain scores of 0.2 in all three groups. Table 4 reports improvement in 15 of 47, 15 of 42, and 13 of 42 children, respectively (three-group p = 0.883). These estimates concern abdominal pain in children and leave Parkinson’s pain severity in `0502` unresolved. A peer-reviewed biomarker paper confirms Ajwa dates, identical group sizes, and the same registration. A behavioral preprint from that trial reports changes in Childhood Autism Rating Scale activity level, which the authors describe in terms of hyperactivity and sleep. It supplies no apathy measure for `0758`. The registry describes nonrandomized allocation, while the papers describe random assignment. The allocation discrepancy and completer analysis limit inference. [Primary pain preprint, Tables 3–4, DOI 10.21203/rs.3.rs-2511698/v1](https://www.academia.edu/117200458/Impact_of_Palm_dates_fruit_intake_in_the_alleviation_of_gastrointestinal_manifestations_of_autistic_children_A_randomized_clinical_trial) · [Biomarker companion paper](https://ejchem.journals.ekb.eg/article_390300_ae1b51605467d4f5389c3e7103810e59.pdf) · [Behavioral companion preprint, DOI 10.21203/rs.3.rs-2909141/v1](https://www.academia.edu/111734735/The_alleviative_remedy_effect_of_Dates_fruit_consumption_on_severity_symptoms_of_Autism_spectrum_disorder_A_randomized_controlled_three_month_trial) · [Trial registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04261595) · [Indexed registry text](https://ichgcp.net/clinical-trials-registry/NCT04261595)

## Ajwa date extract and sickness behavior in rats

Shivanandappa and colleagues compared a methanol-water extract of dried Ajwa fruit with controls in a 30-rat lipopolysaccharide sickness-behavior study. Extract-treated rats showed more locomotion and less immobility on the tail-suspension and forced-swim tests than the lipopolysaccharide controls. ## Potato salad and apple puree in a measured lunch

Fagerberg and colleagues observed a standardized lunch in 21 people with advanced Parkinson’s disease, 20 with early Parkinson’s disease, and 23 controls. Table 4 reports mean potato-salad consumption of 232 g, 291 g, and 255 g, respectively. The authors found no significant group difference in apple purée consumption. Dysphagia, upper-extremity tremor, other eating problems, and fewer spoonfuls helped explain lower energy intake in the advanced group. This analysis evaluates how existing symptoms relate to food intake. It supplies no symptom change from eating baked potato (`0290`) or raw apple (`0833`). [Primary paper, PMID 32708668, Tables 3–4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7400863/)

Kyritsis and colleagues analyzed sensor recordings from the same clinical meal protocol. The clinical sensor dataset included 21 people with Parkinson’s and seven controls, with 28 meal sessions. Each received cold potato salad and apple purée as part of a standardized lunch. The study measured the time it took to move a utensil from the plate to the mouth. Treat the overlapping clinical meal analyses as related reports. The movement measure supplies no ingredient-specific estimate for `0290` or `0833`. [Companion primary report, PMID 33452324](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-80394-y) · [Local text](Source-Text/PMC7810687.txt)

## Osteoporosis education, calcium estimates, and falls

Şahin and colleagues randomized 54 people with Parkinson’s disease to an osteoporosis brochure with or without a verbal session covering diet, exercise, and fall prevention. Cooked broccoli and kale appeared in the questionnaire’s calcium calculation alongside dairy and supplements. The groups had no clear difference in total estimated calcium at week 12 (p = 0.42). The verbal education group had fewer reported falls in the preceding four weeks by rank comparison (p = 0.02), although both medians were zero and within-group changes were unclear. The investigators excluded one extreme baseline fall count from the comparison. The randomized contrast was the education session, with no separate intake or effect estimates for roasted kale (`0495`) or steamed broccoli (`1000`). [Primary report, PMID 39679117, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11639503/) · [Trial record](Source-Text/Trials/NCT04536610.txt)

## Acute toxicity case after exceptionally high coffee intake

Ohta and colleagues reported a woman with Parkinson’s disease and depression who sought care with drowsiness, vomiting, tremor, and autonomic signs after exceptionally high coffee consumption. She had used coffee because she already felt excessively sleepy. The treating team suspected serotonin syndrome in the setting of paroxetine and trazodone use. After she missed levodopa/carbidopa, new findings led the team to suspect neuroleptic malignant syndrome. The case documents a possible adverse interaction in an exceptional exposure setting and supplies no fatigue severity comparison for `0121`. [Primary case, PMID 35345760](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8942071/)

## Purple grape juice alongside aquatic exercise

Two Brazilian reports share registration RBR-2c4xxb and describe four weeks of aquatic exercise with or without whole purple grape juice (*Vitis labrusca*). Treat the papers as reports from one registered intervention when counting studies. The 2020 report analyzed 19 participants and found no statistically significant added effect of juice on the six-minute walk, Timed Up and Go, Berg Balance Scale, or measured blood markers. The 2021 report found no significant cognitive change on MoCA and reported a decrease in protein oxidation within the juice group. That exploratory biomarker signal is distinct from a clinical symptom effect and from an estimated between-group difference. [2020 functional report, PMID 32798568](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32798568/) · [2021 full text, PMID 33644467](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7887390/)

Frozen claims `0055`, `0712`, `0860`, and `0881` name raw or frozen grapes and respectively ask about daytime sleepiness, hand dexterity, anxiety, and rigidity. The intervention was juice. The functional tests, global cognition measure, and blood assays supply indirect context for these claims. The small trial gives limited precision even for its measured outcomes.

## High-flavonoid cocoa beverage and fatigue

Coe and colleagues randomized 30 participants in Italy to a high- or low-flavonoid cocoa beverage for six days. Both groups received cocoa in a rice beverage. The estimated effect size for fatigability on the six-minute walk was 0.11 with a 95% confidence interval from −0.11 to 0.26. The pilot established feasibility and left the effect uncertain. This is the published study linked to the earlier trial registration. [Primary abstract, PMID 35331536](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35331536/) · [Local trial record](../2015-2019/Source-Text/Trials/NCT03288155.txt)

Frozen claims `0100`, `0660`, and `0703` name plain dark chocolate and respectively ask about anxiety, low voice volume, and speech intelligibility. This study varied flavonoid content in a beverage and used fatigue and fatigability outcomes.

## Strain-defined fermented milk in Parkinson’s disease with constipation

Yang and colleagues randomized 128 participants at a Shanghai movement disorders clinic to *Lacticaseibacillus paracasei* strain Shirota fermented milk or matched acidified milk for 12 weeks. Adjusted between-group differences favored the fermented milk for bowel movements per week (+1.26, 95% CI 0.72 to 1.79), constipation severity (Wexner score −2.78, 95% CI −3.51 to −2.05), and aggregate nonmotor symptoms (NMSS −6.50, 95% CI −9.28 to −3.73). Secondary estimates also favored the intervention for anxiety (HAMA −1.44, 95% CI −2.20 to −0.67) and depression (HAMD-17 −1.23, 95% CI −1.80 to −0.65). The report found no significant treatment effect for the motor examination score. The constipation population, strain, and formulation define what the trial tested. [Primary paper, PMID 37470081 and ChiCTR1800016795](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2023/fo/d3fo00728f)

Frozen claims `0526`, `0811`, and `0950` ask about falls, hand dexterity, and fatigue after consuming plain kefir. `0832` asks about handwriting after strained yogurt. `0558` and `0745` ask about facial expressiveness and speech intelligibility after pasteurized cow’s milk. The active product, comparator, and reported outcomes differ from these claims. Aggregate scales provide indirect context for individual symptoms.

In the 2023 Yang trial, Table 2 presents MDS-UPDRS III ON means (SD) of 33.82 (12.38) to 34.28 (12.21) for 65 people receiving strain-defined fermented milk and 33.37 (14.40) to 34.51 (14.65) for 63 receiving acidified milk. The table reports within-arm p=.404 and p=.002, respectively. It gives no adjusted between-arm motor contrast or individual hand dexterity result, and the product is not identified as plain kefir. [Original Table 2](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlepdf/2023/fo/d3fo00728f)

## Banana and levodopa interaction evidence

Hotozuka and colleagues published a single-patient letter on banana and levodopa in Parkinson’s disease. The lead author’s September 2022 Japanese conference abstract describes suspected reduction of levodopa’s effect in a patient who regularly ate bananas for constipation and was subsequently advised to stop. The author reinterviewed the patient and reviewed prescribing and symptom changes in the chart. The abstract describes drafting the English letter from this same case. An August 2022 *Reactions Weekly* case heading describes worsened dyskinesia during levodopa/carbidopa wearing off and cites the Hotozuka letter as its sole reference. This is a qualitative adverse-event signal for `0999` from the same patient. The accessible records give no dyskinesia rating, comparative outcome, medication schedule, or verified banana preparation. No apathy or anxiety outcome is reported for `0196` or `0309`. ## MiniMeal register recheck and depression measure

On November 15, 2026 the official NCT06353984 record showed active, not recruiting and no posted results. The last registered update was February 18, 2026. It lists the nine-week Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale outcome. The report published later in 2026 says data collection was complete, with 114 randomized and 106 completing all visits. The study assigns complete food programs, with mango or passion fruit among separate drink options and no independent mango comparison. ## Whole Navel orange and pomace products in acute glycemia comparisons

Two randomized crossover trials reported by Guzman and colleagues in 2021 tested 227 g of whole Navel orange, orange juice with 5 g of pomace fiber, and juice without added fiber. The whole-fruit serving also provided 5 g of fiber in the reported nutrient table. Both trials studied two-hour glucose and insulin, with no bowel outcome. [Original report](https://jn.nutrition.org/article/S0022-3166%2822%2900191-2/fulltext) · [First trial registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04369716) · [Second trial registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03685201)

## Retracted date seed animal report

Ismail and colleagues’ January 9, 2023 report tested a date-seed extract in a rat model of Alzheimer-like injury. The journal subsequently withdrew the paper after finding unreliable underlying data. The original article remains available for provenance. Its outcomes are excluded from the experiment.
