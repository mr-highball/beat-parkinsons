# Basil and parsley evidence

## Basil and parsley in a Parkinson diet menu

Hegelmaier and colleagues published February 6, 2020. Appendix A names basil and parsley in the seasoning list for an inpatient vegetarian dietary intervention. Sixteen people with Parkinson’s received the diet and ten also received bowel cleansing. Figure 4 reports an improved composite UPDRS III score in both groups. UPDRS III includes examination of speech, facial expression, rigidity, finger movement, gait and postural stability. Figure S2 provides individual changes in total scores. The source does not report which preparation or amount of either herb each person received or separate results for individual motor items. Table 4 reports levodopa dose, which does not supply daily off time or dyskinesia outcomes. [Primary text with Appendix A](Source-Text/PMC7072275.txt) · [Published article and supplements](https://doi.org/10.3390/cells9020376)

## Hairy basil seed and bowel symptoms

Saengov’s university study bears academic year 2021 and was also reported in conference proceedings. Thirty adults with constipation used soaked hairy basil seeds for seven days in a single-group design. The report describes softer stool and increased defecation frequency (p < .05). Participants reported abdominal distension, abdominal pain or thirst. Hairy basil seed is distinct from dried culinary sweet basil leaf and the participants were not recruited for Parkinson’s. [University study](https://libdoc.dpu.ac.th/thesis/Pornnapa.Sae.pdf) · [Proceedings report](https://www.smartwellnessthailand.com/upload/content/files/proceeding_smart_4.pdf?t=2025071811575959595959)

## Parsley flavonoids and exertional fatigue in mice

Wang and colleagues first published in January 2022. They isolated parsley flavonoid glycosides and reported that flavonoid-rich preparations improved fatigue-related physiological measures and gastrocnemius morphology in an excessive-fatigue mouse model. The authors also report changes to oxidative stress markers and gut bacteria. The experiment used concentrated preparations and did not test fresh parsley or clinical fatigue. [Primary paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1756464622000330)

## Sweet basil extract and insomnia in menopause

Karimi and colleagues first published their insomnia trial on February 7, 2023. Sixty menopausal women received a capsule containing sweet basil leaf extract or placebo. At one month, reported insomnia scores were 5.6 ± 0.5 versus 11.0 ± 0.5 (p < .001), and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index scores were 3.7 ± 0.3 versus 9.1 ± 0.3 (p = .015). The 2021 depression and 2024 menopause symptom papers report the same Iranian trial family, IRCT20200104046001N1. The 2024 paper reports psychological and somato-vegetative domains, which include fatigue items, without separate fatigue estimates. Its urogenital composite, which includes urinary problems, did not differ clearly at one month (p = .114). No urinary urgency count or daytime sleepiness severity score was available in the public results. The capsule contained an extract with a filler. [2023 primary insomnia abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36750371/) · [Local 2024 companion text](Source-Text/PMC11253358.txt) · [2021 depression report](https://www.magiran.com/paper/2285515/effect-of-oral-capsule-of-ocimum-basilicum-leaf-extract-on-depression-in-menopausal-women-a-randomized-clinical-trial?lang=en)

## Basil odor identification in a Parkinson cohort

Tambasco and colleagues first published March 19, 2024. Basil odor was misidentified by 74.3% of patients, the highest proportion among the 33 items in the Italian Olfactory Identification Test. The test assessed odor recognition without eating basil. The study did not measure treatment-related change in olfaction. The related 2013 and 2016 Perugia abstracts mention earlier basil failures without a standalone estimate, and study-level overlap remains unresolved. [Primary clinical report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38499888/) · [2016 MDS abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/italian-olfactory-identification-test-ioit-scores-correlates-with-clinical-features-in-parkinsons-disease/) · [2012 instrument validation](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Basil-Parsley.md#basil-aroma-in-the-italian-olfactory-identification-test)

## Parsley root juice and reported muscle cramps

Ghitea and colleagues first published January 27, 2024. The study reports a decrease in reported cramps after seven days of cold-pressed parsley root juice. The cramp comparison used before-and-after reports without a contemporaneous control. The paper gives 334 and 206 reports with percentages 37.02% and 21.95%, which do not share the stated denominator of 902. Its reported chi-square statistic and p-value also lack enough information for reconciliation. This is a cramp-frequency measure in a general cohort with no clinical rigidity, dystonia or pain rating. [Local primary text, Sections 2.2 and 4.8](Source-Text/PMC10856782.txt) · [Published article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10856782/)

## Fresh sweet basil leaves and behavior in aging mice

Pires and Moraes first published their journal report September 28, 2024. Mature and aged female mice received standard chow or chow mixed with a fresh sweet basil leaf homogenate, repelleted and oven-dried. The aged supplemented group scored higher in novel object recognition. Its water-maze comparison reached nominal significance only in the subset of animals that reached the platform. The all-animal aged comparison was inconclusive. The journal also reports that supplementation prevented an age-associated decrease in anxiety-like behavior in the elevated plus maze. The experiment measured memory tasks in natural aging and supplies no culinary dried-leaf or Parkinson executive-function estimate. [Primary report](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/02601060241281765) · [Local workbook results](#sweet-basil-aging-study-workbook-results)

## Sweet basil dietary preparation in the 2022 dissertation

Pires’s dissertation is dated July 18, 2022. It describes aqueous homogenate prepared from fresh sweet basil leaves, mixed into crushed chow, repelleted and oven-dried before feeding. The dissertation, 2023 dataset and 2024 journal paper describe the same project. The English and Portuguese repository abstracts differ on which age group benefited most. The age-stratified recognition and water-maze data below give the most specific public comparisons. [University dissertation, DOI 10.14393/ufu.di.2023.6007](https://repositorio.ufu.br/handle/123456789/37615) · [2024 paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/02601060241281765)

## Sweet basil aging study workbook results

The dataset was released September 26, 2023. In the processed workbook, aged control and supplemented mice had median novel-object discrimination indices of −0.0965 (n = 5) and 0.3865 (n = 7), respectively (Conover p = .0058). The mature comparison gave p = .1505. In the water maze, aged control and supplemented mice had median distances of 32.38 and 36.64 cm using all animals (n = 11 each, pairwise p = .482, omnibus p = .074). When limited to the animals that reached the target area, the medians were 30.12 cm (n = 6) and 22.66 cm (n = 4), respectively (pairwise p = .0369, omnibus p = .17). That restricted comparison does not establish better performance across the full aged group. [Local data and provenance text](Source-Text/2023-Pires-Basil-Aging-Data.txt) · [CC BY dataset](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/53tf32z76p/1)

## Purple basil extract and isolated mouse bladder

Arslan and Dondas published December 30, 2024. They applied a hydroethanolic extract made from dried purple basil leaves to isolated mouse bladder strips. Figure 2 reports lower electrically stimulated contractions with the extract. It also altered contractions when combined with other experimental agents. The preparation was an extract applied directly to isolated tissue. There was no oral exposure, urination count, urinary urgency rating or Parkinson model. [Local primary text](Source-Text/2024-Arslan-Purple-Basil-Bladder.txt) · [Publisher](https://rrpharmacology.ru/index.php/journal/article/view/507)

## Sweet basil and toxin induced movement impairment

Behera and colleagues’ 2024 paper reports longer rotarod times and shorter haloperidol catalepsy after methanolic leaf extract. It names *Ocimum basilicum* and sweet basil but also calls the material tulsi, and gives two animals in its methods despite multiple groups. The study’s identity and group sizes require clarification. A later rotenone experiment measured rotarod and stair climbing following injected extract. Neither experiment establishes the direction of fresh basil ingestion on clinical rigidity, balance or stride. [2024 primary paper](https://bepls.com/beplsjune2024/17.pdf) · [Existing methods review](Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#sweet-basil-methanolic-extract-and-motor-tasks-in-rats) · [Later rotenone report](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#sweet-basil-extract-in-a-rotenone-model)

## Parsley in a mushroom meal protocol with finger tapping

First posted October 26, 2022. The acute older-adult crossover registers mushroom meals and cognitive tasks including simple and complex finger tapping. The recipe descriptions list parsley as a background ingredient. There is no separately assigned parsley arm, confirmed fresh preparation comparison or Parkinson subgroup. The retrieved official record has no posted results.
