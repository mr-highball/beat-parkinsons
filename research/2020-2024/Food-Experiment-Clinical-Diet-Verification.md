# Original clinical diet and component outcomes

## 2023 turmeric and black pepper in a supplemented Mediterranean diet

Sala-Climent and colleagues first published July 13, 2023. Forty-five patients with rheumatic chronic pain received multicomponent dietary advice and a commercial turmeric and black pepper powder. Thirty reported improved pain. The decrease in Oviedo insomnia score appeared in 27 of the 30 with improved pain and in 5 of the 15 with worsened pain. These are associations between changes in one cohort. Figure 3 marks the turmeric-score association with pain at p < .10. The link for the article's supplement leads to a different kidney-disease sarcopenia study, leaving the intended supplementary data unverified. The original report has no component-isolating comparison or Parkinson subgroup. ## Generalized anxiety diet program registration

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT05573672 on October 10, 2022, after its listed August 12 start. The trial compares individualized dietary counseling plus omega-3 with a waitlist. The 2023 original protocol describes the broader program. An anxiety score belongs to the complete intervention. The public protocol does not give a separately assigned or analyzed toasted cumin ingredient. [Selected registry fields](Source-Text/Trials/NCT05573672.txt) · [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05573672) · [Published protocol](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40814-023-01414-y)

## Primary generalized anxiety outcomes after a diet program

The original EASe-GAD trial report was first published online September 24, 2024. Fifty adult women with generalized anxiety disorder were randomized to counseling plus omega-3 or waitlist. The open-label feasibility pilot reported a baseline-adjusted week-12 Beck Anxiety Inventory contrast of −13.72 points (95% CI −18.54 to −8.90), a secondary outcome. The article and statistical protocol supplied no individual toasted-cumin consumption data or a cumin-only comparison. The result applies to the whole program.
