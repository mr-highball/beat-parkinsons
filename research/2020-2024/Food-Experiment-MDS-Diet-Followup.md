# Food and diet studies traced from the Movement Disorders congress

## Earlier MENASA Parkinson database development abstract

Bajwa and colleagues described the Consortium for Global Diversity in Parkinson’s Disease and its planned patient database in 2021 MDS abstract 633. The abstract describes questionnaire development and a plan for 30 sites. It provides no individual tea intake records or symptom association. A 2025 protocol describes 20 participating centers and supplies the original questionnaires. Counts refer to different design statements, with no basis to infer completed enrollment from them. [2021 original congress abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/development-of-the-consortium-for-global-diversity-in-parkinsons-disease-cdg-pd-patient-database/) · [Dated 2025 protocol and supplement](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-MENASA-Questionnaire.md)

## Diet monitoring in the CogTrAiL-RBD protocol

The German trial registry first posted DRKS00024898 on March 11, 2022. Ophey et al. published the protocol on June 28, 2024 for people with isolated REM sleep behavior disorder. The early-intervention group would receive a five-week cognitive training course and a lifestyle booklet describing Mediterranean eating, with weekly and monthly digital diaries. The delayed-start group would complete the diaries after its waitlist phase. The planned instrument was the Mediterranean Diet Adherence Screener (MEDAS). The cited German validation has separate olive oil cooking and daily-use items, plus a single dish item for tomatoes, garlic, onion or leeks sautéed in olive oil. The protocol did not assign a cooking oil or sauce, and the instrument validation did not study Parkinson disease. [Original registry](https://drks.de/search/en/trial/DRKS00024898) · [Primary protocol and diary methods](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-024-08265-9) · [Local licensed protocol text](Source-Text/2024-Ophey-CogTrAiL-RBD-Protocol.txt) · [German MEDAS validation, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12885-017-3337-y) · [2026 parallel-group results](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-MDS-Diet-Followup.md#diet-diary-and-executive-function-in-cogtrail-rbd).

## Gold kiwifruit powder and constipation

Australian New Zealand Clinical Trials Registry record ACTRN12620000991910 planned a double-blind crossover of 2.4 g/day of Livaux, a gold kiwifruit-derived prebiotic powder, and matching placebo in people with Parkinson’s and functional constipation. The trial synopsis describes about 50 participants. The official enrollment fields show a target of 20, actual accrual of 14 and early stopping. The abstracts report that 12 were randomized. [Exact registry text](Source-Text/Trials/ACTRN12620000991910.txt) · [Dated conference outcome text](Source-Text/2024-Livaux-Parkinson-Constipation-Conference-Results.txt) The May 2023 conference program lists the presentation, and the earliest verified written results are the February 1, 2024 BMJ Neurology Open abstract. The 2024 MDS abstract reports the same 12-person comparison. Each period lasted six weeks, with at least four weeks of washout. [Australian public registry index](https://www.australianclinicaltrials.gov.au/anzctr-search-results?condition_category=Neurological&condition_code=all&ethics_approval=all&page=92&recruitment_status=all&search_text=) · [May 2023 meeting program](https://s3-ap-southeast-2.amazonaws.com/ap-southeast-2.accounts.ivvy.com/account22895/events/177181/files/1237782347642a490cb1d1f.pdf) · [BMJ Neurology Open abstract 23](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjno-2023-ANZAN.24)

Complete bowel movements averaged 2.2 per week in both periods (difference 0.0, 95% CI −0.7 to 0.8, p = .90). Spontaneous bowel movements, one secondary endpoint, averaged 3.8 versus 3.3 per week (difference 0.5, 95% CI 0.0 to 1.0, p = .047). The authors report no clear differences for the other secondary endpoints, including PAC-SYM, and no significant adverse events. The study supplies no raw-kiwi estimate for freezing of gait (0152), postural instability (0711), or attention impairment (0819). The 2026 PRISM abstract includes kiwis alongside several other measures and does not isolate them. [MDS 2024 abstract 753](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/therapeutic-restoration-of-faecalibacterium-prausnitzii-in-parkinsons-disease-using-targeted-fructoligosaccharides-a-double-blind-placebo-controlled-cross-over-trial/) · [2026 PRISM report](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Multilingual-Followup.md#kiwis-and-prunes-in-a-parkinson-constipation-care-program)

## Diet questionnaire in the earlier Asan microbiome cohort

Jo et al.’s July 7, 2022 study enrolled 91 patients with Parkinson’s and 85 spouses at Asan Medical Center in 2019–2020. It collected a semi-quantitative diet questionnaire, constipation history, and measures of dysphagia, olfaction and motor severity. Its primary comparisons address microbiome outcomes and clinical covariates. The paper gives no individual food estimate for these symptoms. Lee et al. explicitly drew on this cohort for their 2025 premotor REM sleep behavior disorder analysis. [Primary study and supplement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9262988/) · [Local CC BY article text](Source-Text/PMC9262988.txt) · [2025 and 2026 reports](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-MDS-Diet-Followup.md#diet-and-premotor-rem-sleep-behavior-disorder-in-seoul)

## Four-strain capsules and constipation in Beijing

Du et al.’s September 5, 2022 randomized trial analyzed 46 patients with Parkinson’s and Rome III constipation. After 12 weeks, complete bowel movements increased by 1.09 ± 1.24 per week in the capsule group and 0.04 ± 0.64 in controls (p < .001). The proportion meeting the paper’s constipation improvement criterion was 52.2% versus 8.7% (p = .001). The 2026 review’s extraction table prints 76 as the study count and describes Bacillus licheniformis plus BIFICO capsules. The clinical article reports 46 in the randomized comparison and separately mentions healthy controls for microbiome comparison. The measured constipation result concerns a capsule formulation. [Primary trial abstract, PMID 36087572](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36087572/) · [Congress extraction table](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/0988_1474_000356_2.png)

## Enterolactis Duo in Parkinson’s with constipation

Andreozzi et al.’s October 4, 2024 open-label study gave Enterolactis Duo to 30 patients with stable Parkinson’s and Rome IV functional constipation or constipation-predominant irritable bowel syndrome. The sachets combined Lacticaseibacillus paracasei DG, inulin, fructose and B vitamins. The 12-week pre–post comparison found lower MDS-UPDRS Part I scores (15.13 ± 7.98 to 10.37 ± 6.05, p < .001) and lower autonomic symptom scores (SCOPA-AUT 17.0 to 13.5, p = .001). PAC-SYM and complete bowel movements also improved (both p < .001). The reported anxiety subscale changed from 7.30 to 6.03 (p = .048). MoCA and total motor examination did not show clear changes. There was no control group or test of kefir, yogurt, or an isolated ingredient. [Primary report, Tables 2–4, and article license](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11452401/) · [Registration NCT04293159](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04293159)

## Coix seed and other foods in a gastric ultrasound test meal

Zou et al.’s February 12, 2024 study in Chengdu gave the same 500 mL semi-liquid test meal to 38 people with idiopathic Parkinson’s and 34 matched controls. The meal consisted of 50 g of ultrasound contrast preparation boiled in water, with coix seed, yam, soybeans, tangerine peel and rice. Gastric emptying was slower in Parkinson’s at 60, 90 and 120 minutes (each p < .05). Antral contraction frequency was 2.53 versus 3.32 (p < .001). In the Parkinson’s group, worse motor scores correlated with slower emptying. Table 3 reports no significant association between gastric emptying and constipation (OR 0.959, 95% CI 0.817–1.125, p = .603) or other measured gastrointestinal symptoms. These are comparisons by diagnosis, symptoms, or gastric physiology after a common meal, with no difference in ingredient exposure. [Primary article, methods, Tables 1–3 and Figure 3](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2024.1294260/full) · [Local CC BY article](Source-Text/PMC10895041.txt) · [Separate Suzhou cohort](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-MDS-Diet-Followup.md#gastric-motility-and-motor-function-in-suzhou)

## Fatigue and sleep in the Brasov cohort

Diaconu et al.’s April 19, 2024 case-control study includes 131 people with Parkinson’s and 131 age- and sex-matched controls. It reports fatigue in 38.16% versus 26.71% on the Chalder scale and in 46.54% of the Parkinson’s participants on the Parkinson’s Fatigue Scale. The study reports sleep disturbances and other symptoms by fatigue status and gives no beverage exposure estimate. It shares the ethics identifier and sample counts of the group’s January 2026 full report, making cohort overlap likely. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11048391/) · [Local CC BY article](Source-Text/PMC11048391.txt) · [Related beverage abstract and cohort comparison](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-MDS-Beverage-Followup.md#coffee-habits-and-sleep-questionnaire-in-a-romanian-case-control-study)

## Diet quality and nonmotor symptoms in central California

Kwon et al.’s August 19, 2023 report used a 134-item food questionnaire in 98 people with Parkinson’s and 83 household or community controls. Within Parkinson’s, participants with constipation requiring laxatives reported a 4.84-point lower Healthy Eating Index score. The abstract gives β = −0.48 per 10-point HEI change (95% CI −0.97 to −0.00). Figure 1 gives diet-pattern estimates for other symptoms. The main text reports higher total sugar intake in participants with swallowing difficulties. The 2026 Egyptian abstract reports similar results and different recruitment and sample counts. The California paper and supplement give results for the California sample. [2023 primary paper and Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11121503/) · [Local CC BY manuscript](Source-Text/PMC11121503.txt) · [Egyptian case-control report](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Multilingual-Followup.md#2026-diet-quality-and-constipation-in-egypt)

Supplementary Table S7 compares diet measures by symptom status within Parkinson’s, adjusting for age and sex. The reported coefficients include total sugars and daytime sleepiness (β 2.68, 95% CI 0.11 to 5.24), trans fats and daytime sleepiness (1.06, 0.14 to 1.98), total sugars and restless sleep (3.53, 0.95 to 6.10), and fiber and cognitive impairment (−1.01, −1.94 to −0.09). Dietary pattern scores and nutrient categories do not identify a specific food preparation. The study relates food intake to symptoms measured in the same observational study. It supplies no change in symptoms after a dietary intervention. [Original supplement, Table S7, p. 12](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC11121503.1/NIHMS1990725-supplement-Supplemental_Material.pdf#page=12) · [Local CC BY supplement text](Source-Text/PMC11121503-Supplement.txt) · [Italian food-frequency study cited for swallowing](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-MDS-Diet-Followup.md#food-intake-by-swallowing-status-in-the-italian-dietary-cohort)

## Fatigue in the Chinese multicenter database

Zhou et al.’s March 10, 2023 article assessed 2,100 people with Parkinson’s recruited between January 2017 and August 2022 from the PD-MDCNC network. It used the 16-item Parkinson Fatigue Scale at baseline and two years, with a mean score of at least 3.3 defining fatigue. At baseline 772 people (36.8%) met that threshold. Tables 1–3 cover clinical and medication correlates, with no coffee or tea intake variable or estimate. The 2026 Song et al. mortality analysis uses a larger group from the same network and presents coffee and fatigue as separate predictors of death. The recruitment windows overlap and the papers share investigators, while the exact participant overlap is unverified.
