# Verified stride endpoint in an animal mint study

## Peppermint extract and patterned stride in a rotenone model

The 2024 original assessed actual stride length in mice. Mean was 4.27 ± 0.15 cm after rotenone and 6.05 ± 0.21 cm after ethanolic peppermint leaf extract with rotenone (p < .001). The untreated control was 6.79 ± 0.08 cm. The plant is peppermint, *Mentha piperita*, a hybrid with spearmint ancestry, and the exposure was ethanolic extract. The human spearmint tea trial assessed six-minute walking distance in knee osteoarthritis, while the Parkinson chewing study used unidentified mint flavor and gave no stride estimate. ## Peppermint extract and tail-suspension immobility

Anjum and colleagues first published online April 16, 2024. In the same male mouse experiment, Figure 2D reports mean tail-suspension immobility of 251.48 ± 6.80 seconds with rotenone and 183.67 ± 11.91 with oral ethanolic peppermint leaf extract plus rotenone (mean ± SEM, nine mice per arm, p < .001). The authors treated immobility as a depression-like behavioral measure, and the same animals also improved on several locomotor measures. Movement recovery limits interpretation of the tail-suspension result as a mood effect. The species was peppermint (*Mentha × piperita*) and the intervention was an ethanol extract.
