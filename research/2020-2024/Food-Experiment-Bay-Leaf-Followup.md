# Bay leaf symptom evidence

## Memory and exploratory activity after bay leaf incense in rats

Brinza and colleagues’ February 2021 study allocated 25 rats to five groups for testing *Laurus nobilis* incense, scopolamine, and control treatments. In the scopolamine comparison, incense exposure increased spontaneous alternation and reduced working- and reference-memory errors. Figure 3A also reports a difference in Y-maze arm-entry count between scopolamine alone and scopolamine plus incense. These tests measure memory and exploratory activity. They provide additional context for attention and walking hypotheses without testing attention impairment or walking speed as a Parkinson outcome. [Local CC BY full text and Fig. 3](Source-Text/PMC7914738.txt) · [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.3390/antiox10020259)

## Severe liver injury after daily bay leaf tea

Kara and colleagues published a Turkish case in December 2021. A 51-year-old woman reported daily *Laurus nobilis* tea for about one month. The authors report jaundice, abdominal pain, progressive liver failure and death during follow-up. Their attribution was based on a single exposure history and clinical findings. A separate January 2026 case report cites this earlier case as an analogue and describes its own fatigue symptom. The Turkish case provides additional safety context for oral tea exposure. [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.33706/jemcr.972191) · [Primary full case](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/1883602)

## Constipation and salivation in a Japanese laurel extract study

Tokiwa’s WO2023026502A1 application was published March 2, 2023. The example describes a randomized, placebo-controlled study in healthy Japanese adults using orally dissolved tablets of *Laurus nobilis* leaf ethanol extract. Table 11 of the U.S. patent equivalent gives median constipation questionnaire scores of 2.0 at baseline and 1.0 after four weeks in the extract arm (P = .021) and 2.0 and 1.5 in the placebo arm (P = .086). The prose claims a comparative effect. The table gives pre-post P values for each group and supplies no between-group test or effect estimate. The single-dose measurements show a significant pre-post increase in resting salivary weight in the extract group and an inconclusive change for the placebo group. A significant comparative result for oral moisture is a separate outcome. The text gives inconsistent daily totals if the stated tablet mass and tablet count are multiplied, so actual daily extract exposure remains to be verified. The study used a standardized extract and did not measure stool frequency, objective constipation, drooling severity or swallowing function in Parkinson’s. Its results inform claims 0477 and 0299. [U.S. equivalent, Table 11](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20230330002A1/en)

A November 2021 Japanese oral-environment trial by Kuniyoshi and colleagues used branded LAURESH leaf extract and a randomized placebo-controlled design. The sponsor, preparation, time period and oral endpoints point to potential overlap. The [2020 registration trail](#registry-trail-for-the-japanese-laurel-extract-trial) strongly links these records to one trial. The original journal tables and first disclosure of the constipation item remain unresolved. Later national patent versions are members of the same family. [Earliest verified patent disclosure of the constipation result, sections 2, 9 and 13](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2023026502A1/en) · [Parallel Japanese disclosure with the same tables](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2023031621A/en) · [2021 Japanese journal metadata](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=202102227018503584)

## Registry trail for the Japanese laurel extract trial

UMIN000041594 was first posted on August 28, 2020. It registered a four-week blinded randomized trial in healthy Japanese adults, with a single three-tablet exposure at baseline and three tablets dissolved orally each day thereafter. ORTHOMEDICO sponsored the trial, Tokiwa Phytochemical funded it and Horiuchi Dental Clinic was a collaborating site. It planned 40 participants and records 44 enrolled. Registered outcomes include periodontal bacterial counts, oral moisture, saliva volume and questionnaires. The registry supplies no botanical name or questionnaire item list. It marks the trial completed and carries no posted results or journal link. [Local UMIN record summary](Source-Text/Trials/UMIN000041594.txt) · [Official registration](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000047482) · [Official version history](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_his_list.cgi?recptno=R000047482)

Kuniyoshi and colleagues published a branded LAURESH trial on November 20, 2021. The sponsor’s March 31, 2022 food notification G1435 describes a four-week healthy-adult study with 44 participants and 108 mg per day. It reports the same between-arm periodontal bacteria result (P=.028) and gingival index result (P=.038) as patent Tables 1 and 3. The notification specifies that the latter result was for women. Enrollment, research organizations, exposure schedule, outcome pattern and numerical gum results give strong evidence for linkage. The open report trail leaves the full 2021 results table, the date of first disclosure of the constipation item and the patent's per-tablet mass arithmetic unresolved.
