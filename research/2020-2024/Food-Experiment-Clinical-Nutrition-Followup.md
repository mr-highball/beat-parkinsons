# Food questionnaire items and composite symptom burden

## Non-oil items in the 2022 MVP diet analysis

Fox and colleagues published a cross-sectional analysis of 1,205 MVP participants on December 6, 2022. Of these, 1,145 self-reported idiopathic Parkinson’s. Supplementary Table S4 gives food questionnaire items against total PRO-PD symptom burden, adjusting for age, gender, income and years since diagnosis. The Mediterranean item for at least three weekly servings of nuts, including peanuts, had an estimate of −102 points (reported interval −161 to −43.6, p = .005). The corresponding fish-or-shellfish item had an estimate of −31.0 (−88.9 to 27.0, p = .9), and sofrito at least twice weekly had +45.0 (−11.6 to 102, p = .5). Less than one sweet or carbonated drink per day had −135 (−203 to −68.2, p < .001). The drink item combines sweetness and carbonation and does not identify plain carbonated water. [Primary report, Methods and Table 1](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/14/23/5185) · [Verified Supplementary Table S4 text](Source-Text/PMC9739738-Supplement.txt)

The MIND questionnaire berry item had an estimate of −119 total points (−184 to −54.4, p = .03), and nonfried fish had −93.6 (−164 to −23.6, corrected p = .08). The butter or margarine item had +273 (printed interval 370 to 177, p < .001). The paper applies a Holm–Bonferroni adjustment to the item tests. The estimates describe composite symptoms and pooled food categories, with no specific berry, nut, fish or preparation estimate. Article Table 2 reports individual symptoms against total diet scores. For fatigue, Section 3.3 gives p = .04 for MEDI and p < .001 for MIND, while Table 2 prints p = .4 for both.
