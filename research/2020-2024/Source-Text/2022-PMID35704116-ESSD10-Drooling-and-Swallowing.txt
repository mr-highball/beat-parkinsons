The Labeit abstract on S25 compares 50 Parkinson, 50 post-stroke dysphagia and 50 healthy participants using endoscopic records of oropharyngeal freezing. The matching journal study, PMID 32417630, describes the same group sizes and authors. It reports signs of freezing in 17 Parkinson participants, two post-stroke participants and none in the healthy group. This is a swallowing-initiation outcome.

The Battel and Walshe poster on S37 reports a Parkinson swallowing rehabilitation study with 12 recruits and two pre-intervention withdrawals. It reports reduced pharyngeal residue from saliva and solids. The same authors' journal report matches the enrollment and follow-up design. Its separate patient-rated drooling measure was inconclusive. No raw fig was used.

The render of S22 contains bolus swallowing traces and plots for test consistency, volume and multiple-swallow patterns. Those figure panels supply physiological context.

On S23, the Park, Lee and Yoo Eposter Session E abstract tests nine semisolid items, including mango pudding and soft tofu, in a texture and participant swallowing assessment. Across foods, adhesiveness correlated with perceived swallowing difficulty and residue. The poster supplies no separate effect on mango-related fatigue or depressive symptoms or a tofu-specific Parkinson drooling measure. Separate Parkinson swallowing abstracts address other comparisons. The date terms are calendar references. S22 shows swallowing patterns by consistency and volume, without a date fruit exposure.
