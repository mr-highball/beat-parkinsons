Gao et al., August 14, 2024. PMID 39141644; PMCID PMC11324131; DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0307263.
Twelve recreational cyclists completed a randomized crossover of tart cherry juice and a high-glycemic sports drink. Neither the low-frequency muscle fatigue measure nor perceived exertion showed a clear beverage difference. Cycling time-trial performance was 17 minutes in both conditions (p=.27). Low-frequency fatigue increased after exercise and recovered by 24 hours across conditions. The test measured evoked force after cycling, with no Parkinson cohort or dried whole fruit intervention.
