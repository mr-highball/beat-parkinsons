Boiled spinach and pastry in an Italian food-contamination investigation
Source: https://air.unimi.it/retrieve/f98dce64-0ab5-42be-be13-0a2ac622ba56/1-s2.0-S0956713524000513-main.pdf
DOI: 10.1016/j.foodcont.2024.110334
Coverage: factual extract from case methods and Table 2

Elisabetta Caprai and colleagues. Food Control 160:110334. First online January 23, 2024.
Four reports of anticholinergic poisoning associated with spinach or spinach-based food involved thirteen suspected cases in three Italian regions during October 2022. Two members of one family supplied leftover boiled spinach, and a second family with two hospitalizations supplied spinach-stuffed puff pastry and raw spinach residue. In Table 2, chemical measurements detected atropine and scopolamine in meal leftovers. Botanical examination did not identify the contaminant species. The authors suspected Datura and explicitly described the limitations of that identification. A case with suspected poisoning after boiled spinach in a third region supplied a pouch residue only. The investigation identifies a contamination exposure affecting cooked spinach preparations, without a clean spinach comparator, a Parkinson cohort, or a frequency estimate for visual hallucinations.
