Title: Dried fruit consumption and cardiometabolic health: a randomised crossover trial
Source: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114520002007
Source alias: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32513313/
PMID: 32513313
DOI: 10.1017/S0007114520002007
Trial registration: NCT03020758
Publication date: 2020-06-09
Date basis: Cambridge online publication. November 2020 print issue, British Journal of Nutrition 124(9), 912–921.
Text coverage: Complete publisher paper, including participant flow, interventions, primary and secondary outcomes. Research summary.

Sullivan and colleagues studied 55 adults in a randomized crossover comparing four weeks of equal parts dried plums, Black Mission figs, dates and raisins with a carbohydrate- and energy-matched snack. There was no clear between-condition difference in LDL cholesterol, the primary outcome. Relative to the control, fasting glucose was 0.08 mmol/L higher after the dried-fruit condition (95% confidence interval 0.005 to 0.16). The authors report no clear difference in aggregate vascular measures, insulin or C-reactive protein. Analysis by time of day gave a small increase in awake diastolic pressure with the mix. That secondary signal applies to the combined exposure.

The source does not isolate a fig effect. The cohort was selected for cardiometabolic risk and the trial did not measure Parkinson disease or visual hallucination frequency. The registration represents the same study and does not post separate results.
