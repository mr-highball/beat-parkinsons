Title: Extra-Virgin Olive Oil Improves Depression Symptoms Without Affecting Salivary Cortisol and Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor in Patients With Major Depression: A Double-Blind Randomized Controlled Trial.
PMID: 34358723
DOI: 10.1016/j.jand.2021.07.016
First published: 2022
Authors: Foshati S, Ghanizadeh A, Akhlaghi M
Journal: Journal of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34358723/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 5 review).

Verified Round 5 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Double-blind randomized controlled trial (J Acad Nutr Diet 2022;122(2):284-297.e1; first online Aug 2021) in 73 patients with major depressive disorder (MDD) randomized to 25 mL/day extra-virgin olive oil (EVOO) or sunflower oil for 52 days alongside usual care. Intention-to-treat ANCOVA found significantly greater reduction in HAMD-7 (p = 0.001) and BDI-II (p = 0.021) depression scores with EVOO versus sunflower oil, driven primarily by patients with severe depression (p = 0.017 and p = 0.008; null in mild-to-moderate depression), with no significant between-group differences in salivary cortisol or serum BDNF. Companion cardiometabolic report from the same trial (Foshati 2021, Int J Clin Pract, PMID:33884713, n=62) found lower waist circumference (p < 0.001) without between-group changes in hs-CRP or MDA. Critical appraisal: active sunflower-oil comparator rather than oil-free control; published letter to the editor (Flynn 2022, J Acad Nutr Diet 122(7):1239, PMID:35276417) questioned methodological and comparator details; non-Parkinson MDD cohort.
