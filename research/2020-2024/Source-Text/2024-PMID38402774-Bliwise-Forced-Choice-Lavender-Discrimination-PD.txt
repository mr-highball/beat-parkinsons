Title: Forced-choice lavender discrimination in Parkinson's disease.
PMID: 38402774
DOI: 10.1016/j.parkreldis.2024.106035
First published: 2024
Authors: Bliwise DL, Trotti LM
Journal: Parkinsonism & related disorders
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38402774/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Clinical letter (Bliwise & Trotti, 2024, Parkinsonism Relat Disord, DOI:10.1016/j.parkreldis.2024.106035) evaluating forced-choice olfactory discrimination of lavender vs rose odor in patients with Parkinson's disease against UPSIT scores and disease severity. Roughly one-third of PD patients could discriminate lavender scent above chance independently of UPSIT scores or disease duration, suggesting potential nasal trigeminal afferent perception. Purely a diagnostic sensory perception test using inhaled odorants — NOT an oral culinary lavender intervention and does not measure symptom modification.
