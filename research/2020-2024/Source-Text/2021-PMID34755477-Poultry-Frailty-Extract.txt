Title: Red meat consumption and risk of frailty in older women

Reviewed: 2026-11-19

The Nurses' Health Study analysis included 85,871 women aged at least 60. A composite frailty outcome required three of five self-reported criteria and incorporated low energy or fatigue. The individual component analysis examined red meat. A separate model estimated the rate of composite frailty if other protein foods replaced red meat. Its poultry substitution estimate did not reach the study's significance threshold. The paper separately describes favorable modeled substitutions for some fish, nuts or other foods.

The poultry model comes from observational questionnaires and neither isolates poached chicken nor gives a poultry-specific fatigue result in Parkinson disease. The observed red-meat component association cannot be transferred to the poultry substitution as a fatigue comparison.
