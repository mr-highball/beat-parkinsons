Title: The impact of almonds and almond processing on gastrointestinal physiology, luminal microbiology, and gastrointestinal symptoms: a randomized controlled trial and mastication study.
PMID: 36130222
PMCID: PMC9761756
DOI: 10.1093/ajcn/nqac265
Registry ID: NCT03581812
First published: 2022
Authors: Creedon, Dimidi, Hung, Rossi, Probert
Journal: The American journal of clinical nutrition
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36130222/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

4-week parallel 3-arm RCT (NCT03581812) in 87 healthy adults (79 mITT / 81 diary ITT) assigned to whole natural raw Nonpareil almonds with skins (56 g/d, n=30 analyzed), ground almonds (56 g/d, n=26), or isocaloric control muffins (n=25). Primary outcome fecal bifidobacteria did not differ (whole 8.7% +/- 7.7%, ground 7.8% +/- 6.9%, control 13.0% +/- 10.2%, q = 0.613). Pooled almonds raised fecal butyrate (24.1 +/- 15.0 vs 18.2 +/- 9.1 umol/g, P = 0.046). In Table 5, change in stool frequency differed across arms (Kruskal-Wallis P = 0.017), with whole raw almonds increasing stool frequency (+1.5 [IQR -1.5 to 4.5] BMs/wk; endpoint 9.2 +/- 3.7 vs baseline 7.9 +/- 3.5) significantly more than control muffins (-1.0 [IQR -4.0 to 2.0] BMs/wk; P = 0.034 vs control; ground -0.5 BMs/wk, P = 0.061 vs whole; ANCOVA endpoint P = 0.070). No between-group differences in Bristol Stool Form Scale (P = 0.508), % normal stools (P = 0.617), wireless-motility-capsule whole-gut transit time, or GSRS gut symptoms. Almond Board of California funded; non-PD healthy adults.
