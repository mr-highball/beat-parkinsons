Title: Visual hallucinations and illusions in Parkinson's disease: the role of ocular pathology
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32447550/
PMID: 32447550
DOI: 10.1007/s00415-020-09925-x
Publication date: 2020-05-23
Source depth: original author abstract and journal metadata; local research digest; complete article remains open

Baseline comparison linked to NCT01114321. Investigators compared 26 people with Parkinson disease and visual illusions, 28 with visual hallucinations, and 28 with neither. Optical coherence tomography found thinner parafoveal retina in the hallucination group relative to the unaffected visual comparison (p = 0.005) and the illusion group (p = 0.009). The illusion and unaffected groups had an inconclusive difference (p = 0.85). The authors also examined ophthalmologic complaints, cognition and brain imaging.

The comparison is cross-sectional and uses distinct visual symptom groups. The abstract supplies neither a dried fig exposure nor a repeated visual episode count after ingestion. The 2022 telephone study follows this same baseline sample. Publisher: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00415-020-09925-x
