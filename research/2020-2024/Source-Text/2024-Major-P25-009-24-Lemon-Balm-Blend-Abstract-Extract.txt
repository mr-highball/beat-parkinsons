Title: Assessment of Stress-Alleviating Efficacy of a Dietary Supplement
Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cdnut.2024.103186
DOI: 10.1016/j.cdnut.2024.103186
Reference: Major et al., Current Developments in Nutrition, abstract P25-009-24, July 2024.
Text coverage: Original conference abstract, distilled.

The report describes 60 moderately stressed adults assigned to a multi-ingredient supplement or placebo for 30 days. It calls the study single blind and reports an approximately 93% decline in mean Hamilton depression score in the active arm and an approximately 2% increase with placebo. The abstract describes lemon balm extract, magnolia and L-theanine, with a 40 mg magnolia extract statement and 40% honokiol wording.

The 2025 Hester article shares authors, sponsor, enrollment and arm sizes, timing and a closely matching active-arm result. Its reported masking, ingredient wording and placebo change differ. The journal statement on prior publication also differs from the apparent overlap. Preserve one probable study family and the remaining uncertainty about the reports.
