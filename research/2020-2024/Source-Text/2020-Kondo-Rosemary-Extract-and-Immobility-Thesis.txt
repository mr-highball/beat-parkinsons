The thesis describes earlier work in the laboratory involving an ethanol extract prepared from dried Tunisian Rosmarinus officinalis leaves. The described preparation contains approximately 5.1% rosmarinic acid by dried extract. Figure 1-14 marks shorter tail-suspension immobility in both oral extract groups compared with water (p<.01). These are mouse activity observations. The dissertation cites Sasaki et al. and related lab work but does not establish that the animal groups or tested extract are the same as in the 2012-online article. This is not evidence of independent replication or clinical depression.
