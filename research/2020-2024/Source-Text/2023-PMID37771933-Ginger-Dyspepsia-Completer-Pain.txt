Title: The Effect of Ginger Supplementation on the Improvement of Dyspeptic Symptoms in Patients With Functional Dyspepsia
PMID: 37771933
PMCID: PMC10525921
DOI: 10.7759/cureus.46061
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37771933/

Published: 2023-09-27, Cureus 15(9), e46061.
Archive scope: Factual summary of original methods, Figure 1 and Tables 1–3, with later publication and registry comparison.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

The Debrecen report is a four-week, single-group before-and-after study. Figure 1 shows 124 screened, 62 started the supplement, 11 discontinued and 51 entered the analysis (82.3% of starters). The study assigns two 540 mg branded ginger capsules per day. The same methods section calls the product an extract and also describes root powder with a minimum volatile-oil content. The brand's public SW535 product information lists whole ginger root as the source, without the paper's volatile-oil specification. The tested lot is unverified. These descriptions do not establish equivalence to fresh grated ginger.

Among the 51 completers, Table 2 reports epigastric pain in 34 at baseline and 19 at four weeks. Table 3 gives means 0.67 and 0.37 and a reported change interval of 0.16–0.42. The means coincide with the proportions in Table 2 to rounding, so this result should be described as symptom reporting. The original pain-intensity distributions and paired transitions are absent from the tables. The paper discusses visual analog assessment but does not supply a separately identifiable pain-intensity estimate. No control arm was available. Reasons for the eleven discontinuations were not itemized in this report. The study gives no Parkinson pain or graded constipation comparison.

The eight-week quality-of-life paper matches the screening, enrollment, attrition, sex distribution and age mean and SD. The thesis calls this article preliminary and repeats the flow and baseline descriptors. The later registry NCT06313814 shares ethics reference DE RKEB/IKEB 5622-2020 and intervention, but was posted after this article. The shared recruitment profile suggests one study family while exact individual linkage is unreported. Retain four-week symptom reporting and eight-week follow-ups as separate endpoints.

The original reports cited for acute motility differ from some narrative descriptions. Citation 46 gives 24 healthy volunteers rather than 12. Citation 19 is an isocordoin melanoma cell paper, unrelated to the stated 24-person dyspepsia trial. Citation 18 used 11 functional-dyspepsia patients, with an unclear difference in antral contraction frequency (p=.06) and a clearer difference in the emptying estimate. The cited 14-person Haniadka trial could not be verified by title or journal-year-volume. A verified 16-person Phillips crossover supplies a separate null gastric-emptying comparison.
