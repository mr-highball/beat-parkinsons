Title: Cerebellar Syndrome Induced by Hypomagnesemia: A Treatable Cause of Ataxia Not to be Missed. Report of Three Cases and a Review of the Literature
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10272920/
PMID: 37332648
PMCID: PMC10272920
DOI: 10.1002/mdc3.13739
Publication date: 2023-04-17
Date basis: electronic publication date in original JATS
Source depth: complete official PMC HTML publication; local research digest

Case 2 had a low serum magnesium concentration, cerebellar edema and delirium with visual hallucinations. Overall symptoms improved after intravenous magnesium, withdrawal of antiviral medication and prior thiamine treatment. Later low magnesium recurred with ataxia and no reported cognitive symptoms. Dried figs contain measured magnesium, but the case has no Parkinson diagnosis, food exposure or separately timed episode frequency.

Case 2 reports an initial serum magnesium measurement of 0.3 mmol/L. Visual hallucinations are listed among delirium signs before treatment. The case describes improvement in general symptoms within 24 hours of magnesium replacement but supplies no separate hallucination trajectory. It conveys no dosage or dietary equivalence between dried fruit and medical replacement.
