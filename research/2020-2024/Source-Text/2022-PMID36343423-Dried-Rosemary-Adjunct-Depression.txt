Title: Rosemary as an adjunctive treatment in patients with major depressive disorder: A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36343423/
PMID: 36343423
DOI: 10.1016/j.ctcp.2022.101685
Published online: 2022-11-02
Accessed: 2026-11-19
Text coverage: Distilled original paper methods and Tables 2–3, verified against the indexed full-paper copy and PubMed.
Publisher: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1744388122001530

Azizi and colleagues randomized 59 adults starting an SSRI for major depressive disorder. The intervention was capsules of dried, milled rosemary leaves, taken twice daily for eight weeks. Ethanol extraction served the plant chemistry assay. The powder contained 21.13 ± 0.56 mg/g rosmarinic acid. The analysis used 51 completers, with 26 in the rosemary group and 25 in the placebo group.

Depression was measured with BDI-II. Rosemary group means at baseline, week four and week eight were 33.46, 20.50 and 14.19, versus 42.20, 34.48 and 29.52 for placebo. The paper reports a group-by-time p=.005. The baseline depression imbalance favored the rosemary group (p=.003), and the report does not identify a baseline-covariate-adjusted estimate. The week-eight separation includes the baseline difference. Heartburn was reported by 11 of 26 rosemary participants and none of 25 on placebo.

The comparison concerns an adjunct to SSRI initiation and dried leaves outside Parkinson disease. It does not measure fresh culinary rosemary or depression severity in Parkinson disease. The published trial is the same family reused by the 2026 Aucoin synthesis and its conference presentation.
