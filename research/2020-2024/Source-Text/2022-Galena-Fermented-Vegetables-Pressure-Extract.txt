Title: The effects of fermented vegetable consumption on the composition of the intestinal microbiota and levels of inflammatory markers in women: A pilot and feasibility study
PMID: 36201455
PMCID: PMC9536613
DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0275275
First public publication: 2022-10-06
Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0275275
Format: Factual extraction from the original Methods, Table 3 and protocol supplement.
Galena and colleagues randomized 34 adult women and report 31 completing six weeks. The groups received fermented cabbage and/or cucumber, pickled cabbage and/or cucumber, or no supplied food. Cabbage cultivar and participant-level component intake were not reported. The registry labels the pickled arm nonfermented.
Table 3 systolic medians in mmHg at baseline and week six were 118 and 121 fermented, 110.5 and 107 pickled, and 114 and 104 usual diet. Within-arm p values were .858, .866 and .037, respectively. The cross-sectional between-group p at week six was .093. This is distinct from testing differences in change. The twice-measured clinical pressure outcome was not a standing test or an orthostatic symptom score.
The journal labels the fermented and pickled groups as n=11 and n=10, respectively. The ClinicalTrials.gov posted systolic results list 10 and 11. The publication describes 2019 recruitment and completion, while the registry reports activity from 2018 to 2021. The 2024 correction (DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0306219) concerns the third author's name. The discrepancies in the outcome denominators and dates remain open.
Registration: NCT03407794.
