Title: Effect of chewing gum containing Xylitol and blackberry powder on oral bacteria: A randomized controlled crossover trial
Authors: Craig S Miller, Robert J Danaher, Sree Kirakodu, Charles R Carlson, Russell J Mumper
Primary original abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36037565/

Identifiers: PMID 36037565, DOI 10.1016/j.archoralbio.2022.105523, NCT05133557
Date: 2022-08-17 electronic publication
Coverage: factual extract of original abstract, prepared 2026-11-14

Fifty volunteers crossed over from xylitol gum to the gum containing xylitol and freeze-dried blackberry powder or the reverse, with one week between sessions. The abstract specifies 700 mg xylitol with or without 50 mg powder. Participants chewed assigned gum four times for 20 minutes within eight hours. Investigators assayed nine saliva bacterial taxa using qPCR and evaluated bacteria recovered from enamel chips. The berry arm showed reductions in total bacterial load and the abundance of six taxa after chewing (p<0.05), while four taxa declined with xylitol alone. These statements preserve the abstract's results and provide no estimated contrast between arms for any voice-related variable. The trial measured oral microbiota in volunteers.
