Title: Amelioration of motor and non-motor deficits and increased striatal APoE levels highlight the beneficial role of pistachio supplementation in rotenone-induced rat model of PD.
PMID: 32529399
DOI: 10.1007/s11011-020-00584-5
First published: 2020
Authors: Haider S, Madiha S, Batool Z
Journal: Metabolic brain disease
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32529399/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 6 review).

Primary Abstract:
Pistachio contains polyphenolic compounds including flavonoids and anthocyanins which have antioxidant and antiinflammatory activity. Present study was aimed to evaluate the protective effects of pistachio on neurobehavioral and neurochemical changes in rats with Parkinson's disease (PD). Animal model of PD was induced by the injection of rotenone (1.5 mg/kg/day, s.c.) for 8 days. Pistachio (800 mg/kg/day, p.o.) was given for two weeks in both pre- and post-treatment. At the end of treatment brain was dissected out and striatum was isolated for biochemical and neurochemical analysis. Memory was assessed by Morris water maze (MWM) and novel object recognition (NOR) test while open field test (OFT), Kondziela inverted screen test (KIST), pole test (PT), beam walking test (BWT), inclined plane test (IPT) and footprint (FP) test were used to observe motor behavior. Rotenone administration significantly (p < 0.01) impaired the memory but pistachio in both pre- and post-treatment groups significantly (p < 0.01) improved memory performance. Rotenone-induced motor deficits were significantly attenuated in both pre- and post-pistachio treatment. Increased oxidative stress and decreased DA and 5-HT levels induced by rotenone were also significantly attenuated by pistachio supplementation. Furthermore, raised apolipoprotein E (APoE) levels in rotenone injected rats were also normalized following treatment with pistachio. Present findings show that pistachio possesses neuroprotective effects and improves memory and motor deficits via increasing DA levels and improving oxidative status in brain.

Verified Round 6 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Controlled Parkinson's disease animal study (Metab Brain Dis 2020;35:1189-1200; DOI:10.1007/s11011-020-00584-5) evaluating oral whole pistachio supplementation (800 mg/kg/day p.o. for 2 weeks in both pre-treatment and post-treatment arms) in a rotenone-induced rat model of PD (rotenone 1.5 mg/kg/day s.c. x 8 days). Motor behavior was evaluated using Open Field Test (OFT), Pole Test (PT; bradykinesia), Beam Walking Test (BWT; balance/gait), Inclined Plane Test (IPT; postural stability), Kondziela Inverted Screen Test (KIST; grip/strength), and Footprint (FP) test (stride length); memory was tested by Morris Water Maze (MWM) and Novel Object Recognition (NOR). Results: pistachio significantly attenuated rotenone-induced motor deficits and memory impairments (p<0.01) in both pre- and post-treatment groups, attenuated striatal oxidative stress and dopamine (DA)/serotonin (5-HT) depletion, and normalized striatal ApoE. Limitations: non-human rat toxin model, raw/unspecified roasting grade vs frozen roasted pistachio, paywalled full text precludes inspecting per-arm n and individual test-specific effect sizes/p-values beyond the abstract statement.
