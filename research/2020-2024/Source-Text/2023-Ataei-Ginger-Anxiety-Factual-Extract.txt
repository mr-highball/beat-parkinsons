Title: Investigating the Effect of Ginger as an Adjunctive Treatment along with SSRIs to Reduce Anxiety in Patients with GAD: A Clinical Trial Study
Source: https://www.benthamscience.com/article/135835
DOI: 10.2174/0126660822258340231025171755
Publication: Ataei S, Jahangard L, Anaraki S, Dastan D, Khazaei S. Current Psychiatry Research and Reviews 21(2), 177–185 (issue 2025). Publisher first publication November 2, 2023.
Registration: https://en.irct.ir/trial/53072
Coverage: Publisher abstract, supported formulation identification from the official protocol. The original tables were not accessible.

The double-blind randomized trial assigned 80 adults with generalized anxiety disorder to a ginger capsule or starch adjunct. Both arms received an SSRI. The abstract describes a statistically significant post-intervention between-group difference in anxiety and describes an increase in the active arm's scores at follow-up. Its conclusion claims reduction. With no original arm-level scores available, the direction and magnitude are unresolved. The official protocol identifies the capsule as ginger rhizome powder and identifies Hamilton anxiety measurement.

The ScienceDirect-hosted issue abstract repeats the same discrepancy. The registered DOI matches both publisher copies and Crossref. A different DOI printed in a public recipe article's citation returned no Crossref record on November 20, 2026. The publisher and Crossref records examined display no linked correction.
