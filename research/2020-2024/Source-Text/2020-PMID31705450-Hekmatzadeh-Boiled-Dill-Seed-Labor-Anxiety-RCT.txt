Title: Effects of Boiled Dill Seed on Anxiety During Labor: A Randomized Clinical Trial.
PMID: 31705450
DOI: 10.1007/s11655-019-3222-4
First published: 2020
Authors: Hekmatzadeh SF, Bazarganipour F, Allan H, Aramesh S, Mohammadi J
Journal: Chinese journal of integrative medicine
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31705450/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Randomized clinical trial (Hekmatzadeh et al., 2020, Chin J Integr Med, IRCT201607177065N2) enrolling 100 pregnant women in active labor (50 boiled dill seed, 50 IV oxytocin routine care). Intervention participants drank a single oral filtered infusion of 10 g Anethum graveolens seeds boiled in 100 mL water for 10 minutes. Six hours postpartum, fewer women in the boiled dill seed arm had severe (0% vs 8.0%) or almost-severe (0% vs 14.0%) STAI trait anxiety (p=0.050) and almost-severe STAI state anxiety (0% vs 14.0%, p=0.041), alongside shorter first-stage labor (p=0.01) and no maternal/fetal complications. Limits: open-label comparison vs IV oxytocin rather than placebo, boiled water infusion vs toasted dry seed, acute obstetric labor setting rather than chronic Parkinson's anxiety, categorical rather than continuous STAI difference reporting.
