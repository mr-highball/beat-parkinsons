Title: Hordenine improves Parkinsonian-like motor deficits in mice and nematodes by activating dopamine D2 receptor-mediated signaling.
PMID: 36883794
DOI: 10.1002/ptr.7790
First published: 2023
Authors: Li H, Gao M, Chen Z, Zhou Z, Li W, Zhang X
Journal: Phytotherapy research : PTR
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36883794/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Preclinical pharmacology study testing isolated hordenine (a phenolic alkaloid found in germinated barley/malt and beer) as a selective dopamine D2 receptor (DRD2, not DRD1) agonist in MPTP and 6-OHDA PD mouse models and C. elegans. Isolated hordenine improved locomotor dysfunction, gait/stride length (Figure 5), and postural imbalance in PD mice and reduced alpha-synuclein accumulation via DRD2 signaling in C. elegans. Critical transferability limit: hordenine accumulates during barley germination/malting (rootlets/beer) and is negligible in ungerminated cooked/boiled culinary barley grain.
