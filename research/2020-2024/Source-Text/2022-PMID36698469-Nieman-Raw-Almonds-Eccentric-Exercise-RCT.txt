Title: Almond intake alters the acute plasma dihydroxy-octadecenoic acid (DiHOME) response to eccentric exercise.
PMID: 36698469
PMCID: PMC9868138
DOI: 10.3389/fnut.2022.1042719
Registry ID: NCT04958018
First published: 2022
Authors: Nieman, Omar, Kay, Kasote, Sakaguchi
Journal: Frontiers in nutrition
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36698469/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

4-week parallel RCT (NCT04958018) in 64 non-obese untrained adults (ages 30-65 y) randomized to raw almonds (57 g/d, n=33) or calorie-matched cereal bars (93 g/d, n=31) prior to a 90-min eccentric exercise bout and 4-day recovery. In Table 4 (2 group x 7 timepoint interaction), almond intake was associated with lower POMS tension (p = 0.033; AL 1.52 -> 1.06 -> 1.15 -> 0.52 -> 0.45 -> 0.52 -> 0.24 vs CB 1.55 -> 2.97 -> 0.87 -> 1.35 -> 1.58...) and lower POMS fatigue across all five post-exercise recovery timepoints (p = 0.051; AL 7.18, 3.79, 3.42, 3.15, 1.79 vs CB 8.55, 5.87, 4.90, 3.48, 3.68). Leg-back strength interaction favored almonds (p = 0.029) and serum CK was lower at 0 h and 1 d post-exercise (p = 0.034, 0.013). No group differences for DOMS muscle soreness (p = 0.187), POMS total mood disturbance (p = 0.130), shuttle run time (p = 0.857), or six plasma cytokines. Limits: acute exercise-stress model, raw almonds, Almond Board funded.
