Title: Dietary supplement of tomato can accelerate urinary aMT6s level and improve sleep quality in obese postmenopausal women.
PMID: 30792141
DOI: 10.1016/j.clnu.2019.02.009
First published: 2020
Authors: Yang TH, Chen YC, Ou TH, Chien YW
Journal: Clinical nutrition (Edinburgh, Scotland)
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30792141/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 7 review).

Primary Abstract:
The aim of this study was to investigate the effect of the ingestion of tomato before bed on obese postmenopausal women's urinary 6-sulphatoxymelatonin (aMT6s) level and sleep quality. We quantified melatonin concentrations in beefsteak tomato, black tomato, and two commercial tomato juices and found that beefsteak tomato contained the highest level of melatonin. In this 8-week open-label, randomized controlled dietary intervention trial, 36 subjects completed the entire trial. The tomato group ate 250 g of beefsteak tomatoes 2 h before sleep for 8 weeks. Blood and urine samples were collected at the baseline and in the 8th week and were analyzed. The Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) in the tomato group significantly decreased with time (p for trend = 0.0297). After 8 weeks of the beefsteak intervention, all components of the PSQI in tomato group had significantly improved, and their aMT6s level was 10-fold significantly higher than that of the control group. Therefore, supplementation with beefsteak tomato before sleep can increase circulating melatonin and improve sleep quality in obese postmenopausal women.

Verified Round 7 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
8-week open-label randomized controlled dietary trial (Clin Nutr 2020; n=36 completers, obese postmenopausal women; appraised in Nisar 2026 scoping review at JBI 69%). Assay showed whole beefsteak tomato contained higher endogenous melatonin than black tomato or commercial tomato juices. Consuming 250 g whole beefsteak tomato 2 h before bedtime for 8 weeks significantly improved global Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI, p for trend = 0.0297) and all PSQI components (sleep quality, latency, efficiency), with 10-fold higher urinary 6-sulfatoxymelatonin (aMT6s) vs control. Limitations: open-label without placebo blinding, small n=36, non-PD obese postmenopausal cohort, raw fresh beefsteak tomato rather than roasted tomato (roasting may alter phytomelatonin), and acidic bedtime tomato may trigger reflux in susceptible PD patients.
