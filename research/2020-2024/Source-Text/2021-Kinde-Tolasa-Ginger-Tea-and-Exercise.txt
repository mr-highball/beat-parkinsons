Kinde and Tolasa. Comparative Study on Ginger Supplement and Aerobic Exercise on Primary Dysmenorrhea: the Case of Debre Markos University Students, Amhara Regional State, Ethiopia. Research Square preprint posted May 19, 2021.

Forty students aged 19–22 received ginger tea or aerobic exercise, 20 per group. The method describes random allocation but also says the groups corresponded to two different academic departments. The beverage contained 2 g ginger, 2 g tea, water and brown sugar. The ginger source and processing were unspecified. The study reported 12 weeks of treatment and no placebo beverage.

Table 5 gives mean VAS pain of 7.20 (SD 1.105) before and 5.60 (SD 1.465) after ginger tea. The exercise values are 7.40 (SD 1.188) and 7.40 (SD 1.539), conflicting with the abstract’s exercise change of 0.900. There is no interpretable between-arm change analysis or uncertainty interval. The preprint provides a within-arm observation with design and reporting limits. It does not study Parkinson pain or independently isolate grated ginger.
