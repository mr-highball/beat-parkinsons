Title: Association between consumption of fruits and vegetables in midlife and depressive symptoms in late life: the Singapore Chinese Health Study.
PMID: 38810515
PMCID: PMC12275727
DOI: 10.1016/j.jnha.2024.100275
First published: 2024
Authors: Li H, Sheng LT, Jin A, Pan A, Koh WP
Journal: The journal of nutrition, health & aging
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38810515/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Prospective population-based cohort study (PMC12275727; Singapore Chinese Health Study, n=13,738 adults, baseline age 45-74 y, mean follow-up 19.6 y; 3,180 cases with GDS-15 >= 5 at mean age 72.5 y). Total fruit intake was inversely associated with late-life depressive symptoms (extreme quartile OR 0.71, 95% CI 0.63-0.81, P-trend <0.01; vegetables null OR 0.95). In individual-fruit analyses (Figure 2), per-serving midlife intake of orange, tangerine, banana, papaya, and watermelon were each significantly inversely associated with late-life depressive symptoms. Limits: observational FFQ cohort with potential residual confounding, unspecified preparation (typically raw), non-PD population.
