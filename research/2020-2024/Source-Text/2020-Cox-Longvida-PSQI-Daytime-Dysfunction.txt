Lipidated curcumin and daytime dysfunction or divided attention

Cox and colleagues randomized 80 healthy adults aged 50–80 to 12 weeks of Longvida or placebo. The article reports no clear PSQI treatment differences in the ANOVAs. A separate 12-week chi-square analysis of change direction on the PSQI daytime-dysfunction component favored curcumin (n=76, p=.018). The author supplement, Table S12, reports 12-week worsening in 2.6% with curcumin and 23.7% with placebo. Improvement was 2.6% and 5.3%, respectively, and the largest separation was the placebo group deterioration. Four weeks gave p=.566. The component covers daytime function and does not give a separate sleepiness item or Epworth estimate. A divided-attention recognition-deficit result was marginal before adjustment (p=.050) and after additional adjustment (p=.060). Arrow Flanker reaction times showed no direct treatment effect. Formulation, population and outcome scope limit transfer to grated turmeric in Parkinson disease.
