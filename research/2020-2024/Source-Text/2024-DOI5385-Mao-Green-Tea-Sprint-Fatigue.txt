Title: Mao and Thanaphonganan, green-tea extract and sprint-test performance
Source: https://ecohumanism.co.uk/joe/ecohumanism/article/view/5385
Original PDF: https://ecohumanism.co.uk/joe/ecohumanism/article/download/5385/5130
DOI: 10.62754/joe.v3i8.5385
Publication date: 2024-12-17
Reviewed: 2026-11-25
Evidence depth: Complete original PDF, participant selection and intervention, and Tables 4 and 6.

Thirty healthy college students entered three groups. The groups combined athletic training with placebo, one session with a green-tea extract capsule, or four weeks of training with green-tea extract. Each group is reported to contain three male and seven female students. The abstract describes group formation from baseline sprint scores, while the methods also describe random allocation. The groups and their analysis should be read at the depth of those descriptions.

Table 4 gives female fatigue-index means of 2.28 before and 3.11 after the longer extract/training phase (reported p < .001). Male means are 3.48 and 4.98 (p=.058). The single-session extract arm's within-group fatigue-index comparisons are inconclusive. The index belongs to a repeated sprint test. It provides a different endpoint from an independently reported Parkinson fatigue severity scale, and the intervention has a capsule exposure rather than brewed tea. The 2026 review describes better fatigue resistance. The increase in the sprint index and the design differences limit any inference about the direction of clinical fatigue.
