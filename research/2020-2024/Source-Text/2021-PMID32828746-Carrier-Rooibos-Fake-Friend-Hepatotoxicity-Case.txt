Title: Rooibos, a fake friend.
PMID: 32828746
DOI: 10.1016/j.clinre.2020.06.020
First published: 2021
Authors: Carrier P, Debette-Gratien M, Jacques J, Grau M, Loustaud-Ratti V
Journal: Clinics and research in hepatology and gastroenterology
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32828746/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 6 review).

Primary Abstract:
Rooibos is consumed worldwide and its use considered safe. It contains scavengers of free radicals and is so is deemed to be a liver protector. Nevertheless, hepatic toxicity exists even if rare. It is probably underdiagnosed and clinicians should think about it in case of acute hepatitis. We report a case of liver injury attributed to Rooibos.

Verified Round 6 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Published clinical case report (Clin Res Hepatol Gastroenterol 2021; doi:10.1016/j.clinre.2020.06.020) documenting an additional clinical case of acute hepatitis attributed to rooibos consumption, cautioning clinicians to ask about herbal tea use in unexplained acute liver injury. Systemic hepatic safety record; no PD symptom outcomes.
