Title: Triheptanoin Did Not Show Benefit versus Placebo for the Treatment of Paroxysmal Movement Disorders in Glut1 Deficiency Syndrome: Results of a Randomized Phase 3 Study
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38725190/
PMID: 38725190
DOI: 10.1002/mds.29822
First public article date: 2024-05-09
Accessed: 2026-11-19
Text coverage: Distilled original PDF at the Catalan public health repository, Methods, Results, Table 1 and safety context.

NCT02960217 randomized 44 people with Glut1 deficiency. Forty-three received treatment during the trial. Two crossover periods compared triheptanoin with oral safflower oil. The mean disabling paroxysmal movement count per four weeks was 14.3 (IQR 4.7–38.3) on triheptanoin and 11.8 (3.2–28.7) on safflower. The Hodges–Lehmann difference was 1.46 events (95% CI −1.12 to 4.36). This relative result concerns the combined episodic movement endpoint. The paper records baseline dystonia in 28 of 43 and tremor in 16 of 43. These are population characteristics. The reported group comparisons give no separate follow-up dystonia, rest tremor or swallowing effect. The safflower grade and cold-press status are unreported.

This original report and the posted results share the NCT02960217 study family. The earlier randomized Glut1 seizure study used a separate safflower comparator cohort.
