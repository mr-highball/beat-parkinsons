Title: Effects of Continuous Intake of Rosemary Extracts on Mental Health in Working Generation Healthy Japanese Men: Post-Hoc Testing of a Randomized Controlled Trial
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7699484/
PMID: 33233510
PMCID: PMC7699484
DOI: 10.3390/nu12113551
Citation: Araki R, Sasaki K, Onda H, et al. Nutrients. 2020 Nov 20. 12:3551. UMIN000036762.
Coverage: PMC original full main text, Methods, Table 1, Figure 2 and Discussion. Distilled factual summary.
Reviewed: 2026-11-22 UTC

The same parent randomized trial enrolled 42 healthy working men reporting fatigue. This analysis selected 21 participants with above-median total mood disturbance at baseline (extract 8, placebo 13). The product was a spray-dried hot-water rosemary extract with carriers. Table 1 reports unadjusted median Depression–Dejection T-scores of 54.0 versus 50.0 at baseline and 46.5 versus 46.0 after four weeks, with extract first. The baseline-adjusted Quade test gives p = .058 and the paper gives no adjusted depression difference or confidence interval. The authors describe a favorable adjusted tendency, while the raw endpoint median is slightly higher on the extract and Figure 2 identifies significant between-arm differences for other outcomes. Treat the depression result as inconclusive and keep both accounts visible. This reanalysis is part of the same UMIN000036762 cohort and did not test fresh leaves or include people with Parkinson disease.
