Title: Low-grade dried figs in Morocco: sanitary quality and possibilities of valorization
Source: https://www.agrimaroc.org/index.php/Actes_IAVH2/article/view/851
Original publisher PDF consulted: https://www.agrimaroc.org/index.php/Actes_IAVH2/article/download/851/982
Publication date: 2020-06-16
Date basis: Publisher date and original June 2020 issue, Revue Marocaine des Sciences Agronomiques et Vétérinaires 8(2), pages 258–261.
Text coverage: Complete original article, including Table 1 and Figure 1 on page 260.

Salih studied ten low-grade dried fig samples identified as originating around Skoura and obtained from the wholesale trade. The study cultured 44 isolates, including 22 Aspergillus and eight Penicillium. These 30 isolates account for the reported 68%. The paper identifies Aspergillus flavus in three samples. The percentage describes isolates rather than the share of figs or servings contaminated.

The individual toxin concentrations described elsewhere in the article come from cited surveys. The Skoura study reports cultured fungi and fruit composition without measuring toxin concentrations or consumer intake. Its jam trial concerns product processing and supplies no paired measurement of toxin reduction or clinical outcomes.
