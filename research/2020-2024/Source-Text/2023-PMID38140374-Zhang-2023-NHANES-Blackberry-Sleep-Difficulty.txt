Title: Berry Consumption and Sleep in the Adult US General Population: Results from the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey 2005-2018.
PMID: 38140374
PMCID: PMC10745662
DOI: 10.3390/nu15245115
First published: 2023
Authors: Zhang L, Muscat JE, Kris-Etherton PM, Chinchilli VM, Fernandez-Mendoza J, Al-Shaar L et al.
Journal: Nutrients
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38140374/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Nationally representative US NHANES 2005-2018 cross-sectional study (N=29,217 adults aged >=20 y; two non-consecutive 24-h dietary recalls). Approximately 27% reported sleep difficulty and 22% consumed berries. In population-weighted multivariable logistic regression adjusted for sociodemographics, lifestyle, PHQ-9 depression, HEI-2015 diet quality, caffeine, alcohol, and melatonin use, blackberry consumers specifically had significantly lower odds of self-reported sleep difficulty / insomnia symptoms (adjusted OR = 0.63, 95% CI 0.40-0.97, p = 0.036) — uniquely among individual berry types. Limits: cross-sectional observational design, general non-PD adult population, binary self-reported trouble sleeping, preparation (raw vs frozen/processed) unspecified, and low blackberry prevalence with residual confounding possible.
