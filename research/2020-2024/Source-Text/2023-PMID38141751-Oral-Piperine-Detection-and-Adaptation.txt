Title: Oral detection and adaptation to piperine in edible films
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38141751/
PMID: 38141751
DOI: 10.1016/j.physbeh.2023.114448
Publication date: 2023-12-21
Date basis: NLM electronic publication date, ahead of the March 2024 issue.
Review basis: Original author abstract. Full numerical subgroup tables and methods were not inspected.
Source type: Human oral sensory experiment.

The investigators delivered isolated piperine using controlled edible film preparations. The average detection threshold was about 35 nanomoles. Perceived intensity varied, and repeated exposure at the higher studied amount reduced sensitivity temporarily. The paper directly addresses oral sensory detection and water solubility but does not measure swallowing physiology.

This source supplies a formulation and sensory bridge question for claim 0124. It does not measure piperine release or sensory responses during chewing of cracked pepper, link individual detection thresholds to pharyngeal safety, or establish a clinically relevant symptom contrast. Detection and the dose, matrix and timing at which a swallowing response might occur remain distinct.
