Gorgian P, Askari G, Heidari-Beni M, Tarrahi MJ, Bahreini N. Effects of Curcuma, Black Pepper, and Ginger on the Pain and Performance of Patients with Chronic Knee Osteoarthritis. J Health Syst Res. 2020;15(4):246–254, Winter 2020.
DOI 10.48305/hsr.2020.15.4.100. Original PDF: https://hsr.mui.ac.ir/article-1-1095-fa.pdf
Coverage: original Persian Methods, Results, Tables 2 and 3, and English author abstract.

Sixty adults with grade 2–3 chronic knee osteoarthritis were randomized equally to Mixodin or naproxen for four weeks. Mixodin contains turmeric, ginger and black-pepper extracts. Table 2 VAS values for weeks one to four were 5.90 (SD 1.37), 5.23 (0.93), 4.93 (0.94), and 4.53 (0.68) with Mixodin versus 6.03 (1.24), 5.47 (1.16), 5.03 (0.92), and 4.37 (0.89) with naproxen. The reported between-group p was .747 and the time p .001. The function table reports a group p=.762 and time p=.001. No noninferiority margin or independent black-pepper effect was established. The group result is inconclusive. This is outside Parkinson disease.
