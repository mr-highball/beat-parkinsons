Text coverage: Original Methods, Figure 1 and Table 2, read with the published erratum PMID 33876922.

The flow diagram and erratum report 107 randomized and 99 analyzed. Placebo, cinnamon and dried-fig completer counts were 32, 34 and 33. The comparisons used empty capsules, cinnamon powder in warm water, or whole dried figs with warm water, on the first three days of each of three intervention cycles. Participants knew the assigned preparation. Cinnamon species was unspecified and the paper's energy matching calculation does not reconcile.

Table 2 reports intervention-cycle VAS pain means as placebo, cinnamon and dried fig, respectively. Cycle 1: 6.3, 5.2 and 3.8. Cycle 2: 6.1, 5.7 and 3.4. Cycle 3: 6.3, 5.4 and 3.2. Published placebo versus cinnamon post-hoc p values are .002, .002 and .001. Placebo versus dried fig is .003, .004 and .001. Cinnamon versus dried fig is .002, .002 and .003. These are unblinded self-reports from one school and the implausible printed pretreatment dispersion should not be used to reconstruct a change-from-baseline estimate. The published erratum corrects participant counts, not Table 2 entries. Whole dried fig data address menstrual pain rather than raw fig and rest tremor. Cinnamon data provide oral powder pain context outside Parkinson disease.
