Title: Effects of odors on sleep quality in 139 healthy participants
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9562345/
PMID: 36229501
PMCID: PMC9562345
DOI: 10.1038/s41598-022-21371-5
Publication date: 2022-10-13
Date basis: Publication date in official Europe PMC and NCBI PMC XML records (Scientific Reports).
Coverage: Paraphrased original methods and results from official NCBI PMC JATS XML.

This controlled human sleep study enrolled 139 healthy adult participants exposed overnight via nasal clips to l-Laurinal, orange essential-oil odor, a complex perfume, or no odor across 14 consecutive nights (phase one), followed by 14 odor-free nights (phase two). Sleep diaries, physiological recordings, and olfactory/cognitive questionnaires were collected before phase one, between phases, and after phase two.

Repeated-measures ANOVA showed no consistent main effect of orange odor condition versus no-odor control on subjective or objective sleep quality, whereas individual hedonic pleasantness ratings of the odor positively correlated with perceived sleep quality. All three occurrences of the stem "fall" in the full text refer exclusively to "how fast did you fall asleep" / "falling asleep" on the sleep diary. The study did not administer oral raw orange, did not enroll people with Parkinson disease, and did not measure physical falls or postural balance.
