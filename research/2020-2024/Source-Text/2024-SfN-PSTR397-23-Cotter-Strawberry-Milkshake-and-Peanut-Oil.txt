Title: Establishing salient rewards for operant touchscreen tasks in aged female mice
Source: https://www.sfn.org/-/media/SfN/Documents/NEW-SfN/Meetings/Neuroscience-2024/Abstracts-and-Sessions/Abstract-PDFs/SFN24_Abstracts-PDF-Posters_WED_AM.pdf
Poster: PSTR397.23/D3
Meeting presentation date: 2024-10-09
Accessed: 2026-11-19
Text coverage: Curated original conference poster abstract

Cotter and colleagues describe the pretreatment touchscreen data for
40 aged female mice given either strawberry milkshake (13 animals)
or peanut oil (27 animals) as the reward. The groups did not undergo
food restriction. On the first paired-associate learning day, 8 of
13 in the milkshake group and 0 of 27 in the oil group met the stated
task criterion. Mean trials per 30-minute session across five days
were 22.46 and 11.14 respectively (reported p<.001). The mean
autoshaping trial count per 60 minutes was 38.53 and 23.58
respectively (reported p<.0001).

The foods differ in formulation and reported reward amounts. The
abstract does not establish random assignment of rewards or measure
spontaneous falls or resting tremor. The most direct interpretation
concerns task engagement with the two rewards in this setting. The
milkshake cohort is likely related to the 13-animal group discussed
in the ASN proceeding. Individual overlap and the exact reconciliation
of the earlier and later peanut-oil groups are unverified.
