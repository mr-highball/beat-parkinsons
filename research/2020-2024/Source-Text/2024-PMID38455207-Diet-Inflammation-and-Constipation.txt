Title: Dietary inflammatory score and constipation in NHANES 2005–2010

DOI: 10.1002/fsn3.3914
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10916608/
Published: 2024-01-17
Archive scope: Evidence-focused summary of the inspected original article body.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

The study analyzed 12,308 adults, including 986 who reported fewer than three bowel movements per week. Table 2 reports an adjusted odds ratio of 1.20 per unit of the Dietary Inflammatory Index (95% CI 1.13–1.28). The exposure was a pooled score calculated from a 24-hour recall. The model gives no separate estimate for ginger, turmeric or dried dates. The endpoint is low stool frequency, with no measured pain comparison. Parkinson disease appears as a possible cause of constipation in the background. These population-level associations do not identify an effect of an individual food.
