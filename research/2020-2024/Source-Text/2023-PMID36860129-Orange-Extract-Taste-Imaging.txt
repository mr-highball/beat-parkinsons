Title: Brain activity associated with taste stimulation: A mechanism for neuroplastic change?

Format: Original-source scope digest.

In an fMRI study of 21 healthy adults, the investigators compared five taste stimuli. The orange stimulus contained orange extract in a sucrose and citric acid solution. The sweet-sour comparator shared the sugar and acid composition. Several swallowing-relevant brain regions showed different blood oxygen level-dependent responses for orange and sweet-sour. The direction depended on the region and comparison.

The earlier Dysphagia Research Society report appears to describe the same experiment based on the identical investigators, participant count, stimuli and imaging results. Count this as one evidence family. The study measures brain activity in a healthy group. Clinical swallowing outcomes, constipation and falls from eating raw orange remain unassessed in this experiment.
