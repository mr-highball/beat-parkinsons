Title: Common and distinct roles of amygdala subregional functional connectivity in non-motor symptoms of Parkinson's disease
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9938150/
PMID: 36806219
PMCID: PMC9938150
DOI: 10.1038/s41531-023-00469-1
Publication date: 2023-02-17
Date basis: electronic publication in original JATS
Source depth: factual extract of original body, Table 1 and two original supplements

The analysis retained 115 Parkinson participants and 78 controls across two cohorts. Table 1 reports MDS-UPDRS item 2.2, saliva and drooling, with a median of 0 and IQR of 0–2 for the Parkinson group, and a median and IQR of 0 for controls. The group comparison gave p<0.001. Swallowing uses item 2.3. The paper also relates autonomic symptoms to amygdala subregion connectivity.

Both supplementary PDFs match the publisher checksums recorded in JATS. The first includes separate drooling and swallowing headings in the correlation matrices. This is a cross-sectional imaging and symptom association study, with no raw-fig exposure or change after a dietary intervention.
