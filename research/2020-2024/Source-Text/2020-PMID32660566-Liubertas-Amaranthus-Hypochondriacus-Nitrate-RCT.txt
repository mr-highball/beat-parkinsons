Title: The influence of amaranth (Amaranthus hypochondriacus) dietary nitrates on the aerobic capacity of physically active young persons.
PMID: 32660566
PMCID: PMC7359009
DOI: 10.1186/s12970-020-00366-5
First published: 2020
Authors: Liubertas T, Kairaitis R, Stasiule L, Capkauskiene S, Stasiulis A, Viskelis P, et al.
Journal: Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32660566/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial (Liubertas et al., 2020, J Int Soc Sports Nutr, PMC7359009) in 13 physically active young Lithuanian male athletes (n=7 active, n=6 placebo) receiving an oat bar with 4 g Amaranthus hypochondriacus concentrate standardized to 9-11% dietary nitrate (~400 mg NO3-, >10% potassium, amaranthine, sugar- and oxalate-free; leaf/aerial-type nitrate extract rather than boiled grain) vs placebo for 6 days. Only within-group Wilcoxon increases were observed in the active arm for peak cycling power (4.44 +/- 0.50 to 4.55 +/- 0.43 W/kg, p<0.05), VO2max (37.7 +/- 2.7 to 41.2 +/- 5.4 mL/kg/min, p<0.05), and first ventilatory threshold (178.6 +/- 30.3 to 188.6 +/- 35.2 W, p<0.05). Limits: very small athlete sample (n=13), no statistically significant between-group interaction reported, nitrate concentrate differs fundamentally from boiled amaranth grain, and ergometer aerobic capacity does not measure clinical Parkinson's fatigue.
