Title: Dietary Caffeine and Brain Dopaminergic Function in Parkinson Disease
Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ana.26957
PMID: 38767012
DOI: 10.1002/ana.26957
Publication date: 2024-05-20
Coverage: Original publisher text, caffeine assessment methods and Tables 1 and 3. The local beverage fatigue note separately summarizes nonmotor endpoints from the same cohort.

The early-Parkinson cross-sectional comparison grouped 57 unmedicated participants reporting zero to three cups of coffee daily and 58 reporting more than three. Table 1 reports mean MDS-UPDRS motor scores of 34.5 (SD 15.0) and 36.5 (SD 15.1), respectively (p=.50). The paper reports one missing motor score.
Forty-four participants contributed to the longitudinal follow-up, including 29 initially unmedicated and 15 initially medicated. In Table 3, the association between diary-based total caffeine and annual change in MDS-UPDRS motor total was r=.18 (p=.35) in the initially unmedicated group and r=−.14 (p=.62) in the initially medicated group.
The observational estimates concern coffee or total caffeine intake and an aggregate motor score. The study gives no separate bradykinesia result or comparison of brewed black tea.
