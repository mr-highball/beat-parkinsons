Title: Effects of a 4-week pecan-enriched diet on cognitive function in healthy older adults.
DOI: 10.3233/NHA-220181
First published: 2022
Authors: Cogan B, Pearson RC, Paton CM, Jenkins NT, Cooper JA
Journal: Nutrition and Healthy Aging
Source: https://doi.org/10.3233/NHA-220181
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 6 review).

Primary Abstract:
BACKGROUND: Pecans are rich in nutrients known to benefit cognition. OBJECTIVE: To examine the effect of daily pecan consumption on cognitive function in older adults. METHODS: In this randomized controlled trial, 42 adults (50-75 y) either consumed 68 g of pecans/day for 4 weeks (pecan; n = 21) or avoided all nuts (control; n = 21). At pre- (V1) and post-intervention (V2) visits, cognitive function was assessed using a fluid composite score and four subtests from the NIH Toolbox-Cognitive Battery (NIHTB-CB) (Flanker Test, Dimensional Change Card Sort Test [DCCS], Picture Sequence Memory Test [PSMT], NIHTB Auditory Verbal Learning Test [RAVLT]) at fasting, 30- and 210-min after a high-saturated fat meal void of pecans. RESULTS: From V1 to V2, fasting and postprandial cognitive performance did not differ between groups. There were improvements in both groups for fasting fluid composite score (p < 0.001) and performance on RAVLT, PSMT, DCCS, and Flanker tests from V1 to V2 (p < 0.001 for all), with no differences between groups. Additionally, postprandial performance on RAVLT, PSMT, and Flanker tests improved at V2 (p < 0.01 for each), with no differences between groups.

Verified Round 6 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
4-week parallel RCT (Nutr Healthy Aging 2022;7:159-171; NCT04385537; OpenAlex metadata and Sandhu 2025 PMC12694537 Table 5 verified; not indexed in PubMed) in n=42 healthy older adults aged 50-75 y consuming 68 g/d raw pecan halves vs nut-free control. Directly assessed attention/inhibitory control (Flanker), executive function/cognitive flexibility (DCCS), and episodic/verbal memory (PSMT, RAVLT). Strictly NULL between groups for all fasting and postprandial cognitive endpoints; both arms improved equally (p<0.001) from repeat-testing practice effects. Critical opposing/null RCT tempering acute Guadagni 2025 attention findings and refuting any chronic executive-function worsening or benefit.
