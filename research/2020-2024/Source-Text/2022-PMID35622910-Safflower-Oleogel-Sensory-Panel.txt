Kirtane and colleagues used 11 oils for preliminary taste screening and compounded 45 oleogels from five oils and three waxes at different concentrations. Safflower oil with 1% carnauba wax was characterized as a comparatively smooth, thickened-beverage-like formulation with slight chalkiness. The trained human panel used a sip-and-spit method and supplied consensus taste and texture ratings. Six to 12 panelists attended each session. There was no measured clinical swallow, aspiration or dysphagia response to this safflower formulation. The proposed pediatric use and the animal drug-delivery tests are different assessments.

The result is about this safflower-and-wax matrix. The preparation differs from plain cold-pressed oil and the panel supplies no clinical transfer to Parkinson disease.
