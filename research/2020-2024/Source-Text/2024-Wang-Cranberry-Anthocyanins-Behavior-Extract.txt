Cranberry anthocyanin extract and mouse immobility
First published: 2024-06-14
DOI: 10.1002/fsn3.4280
PMID: 39554328
PMCID: PMC11561776
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11561776/
Coverage: Original Methods 2.5, Results 3.2 and Figure 2

The authors compared anthocyanin-rich ethanol extracts from several berries. In the acute mouse assays, cranberry extract at both tested strengths had no statistically significant difference from vehicle in tail-suspension and forced-swimming immobility, with eight mice per group in Figure 2. Other berry extracts had comparisons differing from vehicle. The preparation was concentrated cranberry extract. The animal behavioral proxies do not identify effects on human clinical depression.
