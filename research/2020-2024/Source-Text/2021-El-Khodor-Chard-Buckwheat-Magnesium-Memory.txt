Swiss chard and buckwheat juice blend in dietary magnesium reduction
Source: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/1028415X.2021.1995119
PMID: 34730480
DOI: 10.1080/1028415X.2021.1995119
Coverage: factual extract from primary methods and results

Bassem F. El-Khodor, Karma James, Qing Chang, Wei Zhang, Yvette R. Loiselle, Chinmayee Panda and Taleen Hanania. Nutritional Neuroscience 25(12):2638–2649, first published November 3, 2021, issue 2022.
Healthy male Sprague Dawley rats had 0.23% or 0.20% magnesium chow. The intervention was a proprietary blend made from juice of Swiss chard leaves and whole aerial buckwheat plant, dried then dissolved in water. Dose was 20 mg/kg elemental magnesium per gavage twice per day. Separate cohorts had a 14-day CSF/red cell experiment and a 45-day behavioral experiment, with approximately 14–15 rats per group. Comparators included water vehicle in low and normal magnesium conditions and selected magnesium salts. The blended extract and threonate raised CSF magnesium relative to vehicle groups. The published Results give a recognition-index group effect (p = 0.0266), an extract versus low-magnesium vehicle contrast (p = 0.0358), and no treatment-by-time interaction (p = 0.237). Figure 7 caption marks a comparison against normal-diet control instead. The two descriptions of comparator are preserved separately. Spontaneous alternation did not yield a significant treatment effect (p = 0.6988) or interaction (p = 0.0853). Low-magnesium diet produced an anxiety-like elevated plus-maze phenotype which was not reversed by the blend. Recognition was an object memory outcome. Aerial-plant juice blend cannot isolate buckwheat groats or chard, and the study contains no Parkinson or human attention measurement.
