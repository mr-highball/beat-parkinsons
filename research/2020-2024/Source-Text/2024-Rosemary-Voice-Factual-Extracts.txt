# Rosemary and voice outcomes: staged 2024 evidence

Safavi and colleagues published 30 September 2024 a randomized trial in 116 surgical patients: 31 gargled 30 drops of Rosmarinus officinalis extract in 30 mL water, 31 gargled ketamine, and 54 gargled water before intubation. Voice hoarseness was measured with the auditory GRBAS scale, not acoustic loudness. Table 2 reports an overall hoarseness P = .15; the adjacent narrative says P = .001 and significant, whereas the abstract and conclusion report no significant effect on hoarseness. This conflict prevents a confident numerical interpretation. The non-Parkinson population, short local extract exposure, and outcome differ from eating dried leaves and low voice volume. [Original report, Methods and Table 2](https://doi.org/10.5812/jkums-139553) · [Full article](https://brieflands.com/journals/jkums/articles/139553)

Dehghan and colleagues published online 6 November 2024 (2025 print) a randomized trial of 90 patients undergoing kidney stone surgery: gauze with rosemary extract, lavender extract, or water was placed in the throat after intubation. The author abstract reports lower hoarseness scores in both extract groups than control at 2 and 6 hours, but not 24 hours. Full tables and exact measurement method were unavailable on the accessible publisher/abstract route. The topical extract, perioperative condition, and hoarseness outcome are not ingestion of dried rosemary or measured low voice volume in Parkinson disease. The apparent short-term finding and Safavi's inconsistent reporting cannot establish or refute the frozen claim. [PubMed abstract, PMID 39503638](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39503638/) · [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jopan.2024.07.024)
