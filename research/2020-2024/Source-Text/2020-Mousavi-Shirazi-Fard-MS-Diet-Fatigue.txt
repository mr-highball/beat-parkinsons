Mousavi-Shirazi-Fard Z, Mazloom Z, Izadi S, Fararouei M. The effects of modified anti-inflammatory diet on fatigue, quality of life, and inflammatory biomarkers in relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis patients: a randomized clinical trial. Int J Neurosci. 2021;131(7):657–665. First online April 16, 2020. PMID 32249637. DOI 10.1080/00207454.2020.1750398. Coverage: original author abstract and independent NICE study extraction, not a full original article. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32249637/

Original abstract: 12-week personalized anti-inflammatory diet comparison with healthy-diet recommendations in relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis, 100 participants described. The authors report MFIS improvement versus controls p=.001. Public full-text publisher request returned HTTP 403 and the repository entry exposes an abstract. Registry IRCT20171217037916N2 publicly lists 120 planned; these counts are different stages of one study.

NICE NG220 fatigue review, Appendix D printed pp. 523–529 and forest plot Figure 323, pp. 1144–1145, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585277/bin/niceng220er3-app-et1.pdf. Same study, 104 randomized, 100 analyzed per protocol. The intervention description lists canola and olive oil for cooking or dressing, and permits white or green tea. Spices are listed without identifying cinnamon. Food consumption, oil refining and tea brewing are unverified. Diet total MFIS mean (SD) 47.96 (12.63) at baseline and 47.22 (12.54) at 12 weeks; controls 47.84 (11.18) and 47.92 (11.11). The review's endpoint mean difference for 50 analyzed per arm was -0.70 points (95% confidence interval -5.34 to 3.94). That unadjusted estimate and the abstract p value are distinct analyses; full article method reconciliation remains open. This trial tests a multi-food prescription, without ingredient-specific effect or Parkinson subgroup.
