Poster P169 compares fish-oil capsules with sunflower-oil capsules when both groups undertake resistance training for 14 weeks. Participants were sarcopenic older women. The poster reports no clear group or time difference for measured sitting time and steps. Its text and these behavioral outcomes belong to the poster family, not the symposium body. The 2022 Alves article has a closely matching trial design and investigator group and reports different physical performance endpoints. The oil grade, separate fatigue and muscle rigidity effects and the Parkinson executive endpoint are all unresolved.

Supplementary P233 and unrelated-term review
P233 presents 23 older adults allocated to fish omega-3 or corn oil with a shared resistance-training intervention. Isometric strength changed in different directions between groups. The full report gives a small difference from the conference value for the corn comparator. Leg press improved over time across arms. These outcomes, without a separate refined-oil or Parkinson comparison, do not measure rigidity, stride, pain, fatigue, constipation, depression or apathy. The original printed poster source and longer oral-key HTML reproduce the same member. research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2020-PMID33284965-Corn-Oil-and-Resistance-Training.txt holds the final results. The SAGE, Melissa and date occurrences refer to a study acronym, researcher names and chronology or cognitive orientation.
