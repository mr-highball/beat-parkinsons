Title: Acute and chronic cognitive effects of a mixed sage extract
First published: 2021-01-14
PMID: 33466627
PMCID: PMC7828691
DOI: 10.3390/nu13010218
Source: https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/13/1/218
Text type: original-source evidence summary

Wightman and colleagues compared an encapsulated mixture of common-sage leaf extract and Spanish-sage essential oil with placebo in healthy adults. Results section 3.1 reports 34 possibly related adverse events in the active group and ten in the placebo group across categories. The muscular or bodily pain and injury category contains nine pooled events. The paper does not give counts by assigned arm for that category or report a clinical pain severity comparison. The mixture and population differ from eating fresh common sage in Parkinson disease.

Methods section 2.3 and Results section 3.4 describe separate Bond-Lader and State Trait Anxiety Inventory assessments. The article identified no significant treatment difference in mood. A contentment treatment-by-repetition result on day 29 had p=0.08, with no significant post hoc comparison. The cohort did not have diagnosed Parkinson disease and clinical apathy was not measured. NCT03695003 is a probable registration for the same comparison based on the product, actual enrollment of 94, age eligibility, assignment and outcome timing. The paper and the official record lack a direct identifier cross-link, so they do not count as independent studies.
