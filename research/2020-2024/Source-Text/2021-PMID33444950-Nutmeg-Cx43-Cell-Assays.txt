Title: The effect of nutmeg essential oil constituents on Novikoff hepatoma cell viability and communication through Cx43 gap junctions
PMID: 33444950
DOI: 10.1016/j.biopha.2021.111229
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33444950/
Publisher: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0753332221000147
Published: 2021, Biomedicine & Pharmacotherapy 135, 111229.
Archive scope: Factual record of the original author abstract and public publisher highlights. Full methods and results tables remain open.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Mickus and colleagues characterized nutmeg essential oil and performed docking on connexin 43. They tested the oil and sabinene, alpha-pinene and alpha-copaene in the Novikoff rat hepatoma cell system. The original abstract reports short-term, reversible inhibition of gap-junction conductance and later reduction in Cx43 protein and functional channel number. Reduced viability, growth and colony formation were reported separately. The authors state those effects could arise independently of gap-junction intercellular communication.

The July FEBS poster P-03.2-20 has the same authors, oil, three constituents, model and reported outcomes. Count the paper and poster as one assay family unless new experimental series are documented. The applied essential oil and isolated constituents differ from eaten ground or grated nutmeg. Neither source gives a constipation, fatigue or depression measurement in participants with Parkinson disease. The PDF dose–response figures and method-level vehicle are still open.
