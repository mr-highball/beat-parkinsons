Published: 2021-12-11, Foods 10(12), 3082.
Archive scope: Factual record from original article sections 2.1–2.2 and Tables 1–2, with subsequent sample provenance in DOI 10.3390/foods12010223.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Moiseenko and colleagues purchased three packages per product for three kefirs, two ryazhenkas, three amasis and one mahewu. Table 1 provides the label composition, including three kefir products based on milk and starter cultures. The study compares packaged beverages using chemical analyses. Table 2 contains pH, antioxidant capacity and an in vitro inhibition assay for angiotensin-converting enzyme. The tested dairy beverages had higher antioxidant capacity than the corn-based mahewu. Mahewu had greater inhibition of the enzyme in the assay. This is a laboratory activity measure without measured blood pressure, falls or fatigue in participants.

The 2023 isolate study explicitly cites this purchase and characterization when identifying the mahewu source for MA2 and MA3. The commercial beverage measurements differ from the 2023 isolate assays. The latter also uses previously described kefir grain strains. The 2021 beverage paper supplies ordinary retail exposure context and no Parkinson symptom comparison.
