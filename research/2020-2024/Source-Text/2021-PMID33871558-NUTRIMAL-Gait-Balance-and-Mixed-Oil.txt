Title: Does supplementation with leucine-enriched protein alone and in combination with fish-oil-derived n-3 PUFA affect muscle mass, strength, physical performance, and muscle protein synthesis in well-nourished older adults?
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8168361/
PMID: 33871558
PMCID: PMC8168361
DOI: 10.1093/ajcn/nqaa449
Publication date: 2021-04-19
Registry identifier: NCT03429491
Text coverage: Distilled full original article, including supplement composition, Table 4, and adverse events.

In 108 older adults at risk of sarcopenia, the 24-week blinded trial compared control, leucine-enriched protein, and leucine-enriched protein with fish oil. Both the control and protein-only drinks used a mixture of high-oleic sunflower and corn oil. The control included maltodextrin to match energy. The recipe does not isolate corn oil or name its refining grade.

Table 4 reports mean changes in gait speed of −0.04 m/s (SD 0.08) for control, 0.00 (0.10) for protein, and +0.02 (0.11) for protein plus fish oil. The adjusted differences at follow-up relative to control were +0.03 (95% CI −0.02 to 0.08) and +0.04 m/s (−0.01 to 0.09), respectively. The single-leg standing contrasts were +1.5 s (−6.5 to 9.5) and +4.4 s (−3.5 to 12.3). Timed Up-and-Go differences were +0.3 s (−0.2 to 0.7) and +0.2 s (−0.3 to 0.6). These contrasts do not resolve a directional effect on these functional outcomes. Gait speed does not identify stride length. Fall frequency was not a reported outcome.

Participants reported adverse events throughout the trial. The authors describe one incident of type 2 diabetes in the control group as possibly related to the study nutrition. They also report gastrointestinal symptoms in four control, nine protein, and five protein-plus-fish-oil participants. These events cannot be attributed to a single ingredient in any drink.
