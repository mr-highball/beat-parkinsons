Symptom reporting when an item is named or volunteered
Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12904-024-01405-7
PMID: 38493155
PMCID: PMC10943902
DOI: 10.1186/s12904-024-01405-7
Coverage: Primary full body and questionnaire comparisons

Published March 16, 2024. This separate cross-sectional component of the Oral Health in Advanced Cancer project compared versions of the Edmonton Symptom Assessment System. It used two different palliative cancer cohorts, 184 patients in 2013 and 156 in 2017, and evaluated symptom reporting without assigning sage rinse.

When the form named dry mouth, constipation and sleep problems, 83%, 73% and 71% of patients reported them. In the cohort with an optional single other-problem entry, 3%, 15% and less than 1%, respectively, volunteered these problems. Different calendar cohorts and form structures limit causal interpretation. The results help explain why omission and sparse reporting do not establish symptom absence or the direction of a food effect.
