Title: Biochemical and Genomic Characterization of Two New Strains of Lacticaseibacillus paracasei Isolated from the Traditional Corn-Based Beverage of South Africa, Mahewu, and Their Comparison with Strains Isolated from Kefir Grains
PMID: 36613437

DOI: 10.3390/foods12010223
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9818903/
Published: 2023-01-03, Foods 12(1), 223.
Archive scope: Factual record from the original article methods and sections 3.2–3.3. Figure 3 explicitly identifies reused data.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Moiseenko and colleagues compared two new mahewu-derived isolates, MA2 and MA3, with two previously reported kefir-grain isolates, KF1 and ABK. Figure 3 reuses the kefir isolates' enzyme-activity results from Begunova et al. (2021), DOI 10.1134/S0003683821040037. Ten of nineteen tested activities did not differentiate the four isolates in that comparison, and seven activities distinguished the two origins. Figure 5 reports different acidification and fermentation trajectories. Figure 6 describes laboratory antioxidant and ACE-inhibitory activity. Neither figure records any intervention in people.

Sampling in the methods traces the mahewu beverage to the purchase and characterization published as DOI 10.3390/foods10123082. The earlier kefir grain isolates, kefir-derived enzyme data in Figure 3, commercial beverage chemistry and new isolate comparisons each have different provenance. Shared data should be counted once. The July 2021 FEBS poster gives no isolate identifiers. The paper does not establish a plain kefir effect on falls, fatigue or other Parkinson symptoms.
