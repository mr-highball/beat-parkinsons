Title: Neuroprotective Effect of Phloretin in Rotenone-Induced Mice Model of Parkinson's Disease: Modulating mTOR-NRF2-p62 Mediated Autophagy-Oxidative Stress Crosstalk.
PMID: 36463449
PMCID: PMC10473071
DOI: 10.3233/JAD-220793
First published: 2023
Authors: Shirgadwar SM, Kumar R, Preeti K, Khatri DK, Singh SB
Journal: Journal of Alzheimer's disease : JAD
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36463449/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Controlled rotenone-induced Parkinson disease mouse study (N=50 C57BL/6 mice randomly divided into 5 groups, n=10/group: normal control, CMC-Na vehicle, rotenone 1 mg/kg i.p., rotenone + oral phloretin 50 mg/kg, rotenone + oral phloretin 100 mg/kg for 21 days). Phloretin (a signature apple dihydrochalcone) significantly reversed rotenone-induced deficits in accelerating rotarod stay time/balance (p<0.01 at both 50 and 100 mg/kg vs rotenone p<0.001), open-field total locomotor distance (p<0.01 at 50 mg/kg; p<0.001 at 100 mg/kg), and open-field center-zone entries / anxiety-like behavior (p<0.01 at 50 mg/kg; p<0.001 at 100 mg/kg), while preserving dopaminergic neurons via mTOR-Nrf2-p62 autophagy regulation. Limits: isolated apple dihydrochalcone at pharmacologic rodent doses in a toxin PD model rather than culinary whole raw or baked apple in humans.
