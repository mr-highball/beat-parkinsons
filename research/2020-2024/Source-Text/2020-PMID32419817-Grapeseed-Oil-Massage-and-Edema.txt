Title: Comparing the effect of foot massage with grape seed oil and sweet almond oil on physiological leg edema in primigravidae
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7201824/
PMID: 32419817
PMCID: PMC7201824
DOI: 10.1155/2020/6835814
Publication date: 2020-01-13
Date basis: PubMed electronic date and published article metadata.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26
Review depth: full_text

Ninety first-pregnancy participants were allocated in three groups of 30. Two groups received foot massage using grapeseed oil or sweet almond oil, and the third received usual care. Foot and ankle edema measurements were the study outcomes. The change comparison between the oil groups was reported at p=.865, which does not establish equivalence. The oil was applied topically and the refining grade was not reported. Anxiety and depression were not measured. The registration is IRCT2015072723370N1. The author's distinct presurgical massage study must not be used as an outcome from this cohort.

Claim 0169 | Related evidence: Topical grapeseed-oil massage in pregnancy was studied against almond-oil massage and usual care for edema. The study measured no depression outcome and does not establish the oral or refined oil exposure.

Claim 0467 | Related evidence: The randomized study measured edema after topical massage in pregnancy. No anxiety outcome or oral refined grapeseed oil exposure was reported.
