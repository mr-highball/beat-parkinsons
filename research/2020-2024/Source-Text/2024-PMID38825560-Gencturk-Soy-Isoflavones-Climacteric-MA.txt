Title: The effect of soy isoflavones given to women in the climacteric period on menopausal symptoms and quality of life: Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials.
PMID: 38825560
DOI: 10.1016/j.explore.2024.05.010
First published: 2024
Authors: Gençtürk N, Bilgiç FŞ, Kaban HU
Journal: Explore (New York, N.Y.)
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38825560/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 4 review).

Cochrane-guided systematic review and meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD420234479700) of 5 RCTs (n=425 climacteric women) evaluating isolated soy isoflavone supplements. Found NULL effects on overall menopausal symptoms (SMD -0.49, 95% CI -1.13 to 0.16, p=0.14), physical QoL (MD -1.10, p=0.49), and mental QoL component (MD +0.81, 95% CI -6.73 to 8.35, p=0.83), while depression scores were lower (SMD -0.41, 95% CI -0.73 to -0.09, p=0.01). Authors explicitly noted high risk of conflict of interest in included trials; exposure is isolated isoflavone supplements in menopausal women rather than cooked edamame in PD.
