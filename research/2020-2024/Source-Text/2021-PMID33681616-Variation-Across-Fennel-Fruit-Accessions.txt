Title: Gas Chromatography-Mass Spectrometry-Based Classification of 12 Fennel (Foeniculum vulgare Miller) Varieties Based on Their Aroma Profiles and Estragole Levels as Analyzed Using Chemometric Tools
PMID: 33681616
PMCID: PMC7931402
DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.0c06188
Publication date: 2021-02-12
Citation: Afifi and colleagues. ACS Omega 6(8), 5775–5785.
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7931402/
Coverage: Europe PMC full article XML, author abstract, and Tables 1–3.

The study compared 12 cultivated and wild fennel-fruit accessions from different origins. Several cultivated accessions had 95.9–98.4% relative headspace (E)-anethole, while some wild accessions had roughly 72% relative estragole. Table 3 gives 89.86 ± 1.05 mg/g estragole for wild accession P-02 from Minia, mean ± SD over three measurements. Percentages of measured volatiles and estimates per unit fruit are distinct quantities. This unroasted comparison establishes substantial variation in the starting material. The overlap of one author with the 2018 roasting paper does not establish overlap of samples. It offers chemical context for food or oil comparisons, with no measured symptom.
