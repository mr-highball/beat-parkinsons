Prathamesh S. More, V. V. Paithankar, J. V. Vyas and A. M. Wankhade. Antipsychotic Activity of Apium Graveolens Extract on Alcohol-Induced Psychosis. International Journal of Creative Research Thoughts 12(5), m915–m920. May 2024. Identifier IJCRT24A5475.

The article describes methanol extract of dried celery seeds in an alcohol-exposure mouse model, with six animals per group. Measured outcomes include locomotion, stereotyped behaviors and social interaction. Neither dried celery leaf nor a measure of visual hallucination frequency was included.

The treatment-protocol table gives 100 and 200 mg/kg for the two extract groups. The Results tables label them 200 and 300 mg/kg. The locomotion values for those two groups are 412.8 and 389.0 versus 527.7 in the alcohol control, in the stated activity units. The dose attribution is unresolved, and the behavioral endpoints cannot be interpreted as visual hallucination events. These limitations prevent transfer to a clinical Parkinson symptom.
