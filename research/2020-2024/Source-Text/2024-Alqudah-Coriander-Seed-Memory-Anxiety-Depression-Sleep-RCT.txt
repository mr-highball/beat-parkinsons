Title: Effects of Coriandrum sativum seeds on memory, anxiety, depression, and sleep quality in students: a randomized controlled study
DOI: 10.1108/nfs-08-2023-0193
First published: 2024
Authors: Alqudah A, Qnais E, Sabi SH, Bseiso Y, Gammoh O, Wedyan M
Journal: Nutrition & Food Science (2024)
Source: https://doi.org/10.1108/nfs-08-2023-0193
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Peer-reviewed randomized controlled trial published in Nutrition & Food Science (2024; DOI 10.1108/nfs-08-2023-0193; linked to ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05999071 already archived locally).
Design & Participants: 86 university students (mean age 20.05 +/- 1.6 yr, age range 18-25 yr, free of coriander allergy or psychotropic medication) computer-randomized to 500 mg dried powdered Coriandrum sativum seeds (twice daily for 30 days per NCT05999071 protocol) or placebo.
Outcomes & Results: Compared with control, the coriander seed group showed statistically significant improvements in self-reported memory (PRMQ: p = 0.006), anxiety (Hospital Anxiety Scale: p = 0.04), depression (Hospital Depression Scale: p = 0.002), and sleep quality (PSQI: p = 0.03).
Methodological caveats: Retrospective trial registration on NCT05999071, young healthy student cohort (not PD), dried powdered seed capsules (requires thermal-retention bridge for toasted seed claims), and abstract reports p-values without arm-level effect sizes or confidence intervals (reliability_fit: remote).
