Jackson and colleagues counted losses of contact by all four feet as falls on suspended rods. They also measured foot slips, traversal time and a composite that combined these measures. They compared age groups, a distractor originating from female mouse cages, an anti-inflammatory drug, scopolamine, alpha-flupentixol and paired antagonists. Ordinary reward pellets were available at the finish. No sage plant preparation or other assigned food appears in the methods.

Table 4 displays mean falls of 0.45 under vehicle and 0.18 under carprofen in the older group, and 0.08 and 0.25 in the younger group. Fall counts were too low for the paper's planned statistical comparisons. The authors tested foot slips separately, reporting fewer after carprofen in older mice. Figure 4 gives more slips after scopolamine on the widest rod. Table 5 reports average falls on that rod of 0 under vehicle, 0.27 with 1 mg/kg scopolamine and 0.18 with 3 mg/kg. Those are provoked apparatus falls under a drug manipulation.

The paper shortened older-mouse sessions to reduce fatigue as a potential nuisance variable. It did not establish a fatigue severity endpoint. SAGE is the publisher. Neither the publishing imprint nor discussion of fatigue defines an herbal treatment. These methods help distinguish individual falls from foot slips and total motor task scores.
