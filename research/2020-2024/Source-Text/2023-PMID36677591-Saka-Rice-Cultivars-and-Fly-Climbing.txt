The seven-day test used normal wild-type flies and prepared the study diet from equal parts of the basal cornmeal medium and one of fifteen matched brown and white rice cultivars. Basal cornmeal and a high-fat diet were separate controls. The selected assay used 30 flies per diet and recorded how many crossed 6 cm within ten seconds, repeated three times. The primary methods do not establish conventional cooking of the brown rice.

Supplementary Table S1 reports mean (SD) male and female climbing counts of 8.33 (0.55) and 9.33 (0.29) for brown Danboto, 7.33 (0.48) and 5.33 (0.52) for its white counterpart, and 9.67 (0.08) and 9.33 (0.28) for the normal cornmeal control. Descriptively, all fifteen brown varieties exceed their matched white varieties in both sexes. None of the male brown means exceeds the normal control. The table legend interprets letter markers for male–female comparisons, so it cannot establish a tested contrast for every brown–white pair.

These results compare timed climbing and differ with the reference diet. The animals did not have induced Parkinson-like motor impairment. There is no isolated postural recovery or instability endpoint. The study helps interpret why a better brown-versus-white mobility contrast cannot be treated as a general improvement against normal feed.
