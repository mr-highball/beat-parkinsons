Title: Mushroom intake and depression: A population-based study using data from the US National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), 2005-2016.
PMID: 34333177
DOI: 10.1016/j.jad.2021.07.080
First published: 2021
Authors: Ba DM, Gao X, Al-Shaar L, Muscat JE, Chinchilli VM, Beelman RB
Journal: Journal of affective disorders
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34333177/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Cross-sectional NHANES (2005-2016) analysis of n=24,699 U.S. adults (5.2% mushroom consumers). Middle tertile (4.9 g/d) had lower PHQ-9 depression odds (adjusted OR 0.31, 95% CI 0.16-0.60), whereas highest tertile (19.6 g/d) did NOT differ (OR 0.91, 95% CI 0.47-1.78, P-trend=0.42). Limits: cross-sectional, no dose-response, mushroom species and cooking preparations unidentified.
