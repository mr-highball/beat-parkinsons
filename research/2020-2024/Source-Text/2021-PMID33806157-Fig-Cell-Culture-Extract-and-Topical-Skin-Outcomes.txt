Title: An Extract from Ficus carica Cell Cultures Works as an Anti-Stress Ingredient for the Skin
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8064501/
PMID: 33806157
PMCID: PMC8064501
DOI: 10.3390/antiox10040515
Publication date: 2021-03-25 (first public electronic publication)
Source depth: complete PMC JATS and publisher HTML

Forty healthy exam-period students ages 20–27 used active cream or vehicle in two groups of 20 in a reported double-blind comparison. The article reports lower transepidermal water loss at recovery with active cream and separate cultured skin-cell measurements. The exposure is topical leaf-derived cell-culture extract, with no whole fruit ingestion or visual episode outcome.

Methods section 2.2 describes a double-blind comparison with twenty participants per group. It does not detail the allocation sequence. The volunteers were healthy, and 38 of 40 were women. The extract was soluble material from cell suspensions derived from common fig leaf explants. Fig. 9 reports a 12.2% reduction in transepidermal water loss for the extract group in the recovery phase. The discussion separately gives +48.8% for vehicle and −0.2% for active cream around the acute exam comparison. Keratinocyte and skin-explant assays for stress and barrier markers are separate experiments. These routes and endpoints give no basis for an effect of dried figs on Parkinson visual hallucinations.
