Title: Accelerated recovery nursing with a topical herbal pack after gastrointestinal anastomosis
Original title: 加速康复护理联合中药灸敷对胃肠吻合术后患者胃肠功能恢复的影响
Source: https://tougao.cdmc.edu.cn/CN/Y2022/V39/I3/232
Original PDF: https://tougao.cdmc.edu.cn/EN/article/downloadArticleFile.do?attachType=PDF&id=1101
Date basis: Publisher issue date 2022-06-10 and release date 2022-08-02. Journal of Chengde Medical University 39(3), pp. 232–236.
Coverage: Factual extract of the complete five-page original article and Tables 1–6.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

The authors report random allocation of 100 gastric cancer patients after laparoscopic gastrointestinal anastomosis. Of 50 assigned per group, 49 intervention and 48 control patients completed the comparison. Both groups received enhanced recovery care. The intervention added a heated topical pack containing dried ginger, Evodia fruit and salt. The comparator did not include sham heat or a constituent-matched pack. The article reports recruitment at Henan Provincial People's Hospital from September 2020 through December 2021. Its July 2021 received date precedes the stated end of recruitment and needs clarification.

Table 3 shows earlier postoperative first defecation in the intervention group, 42.23 ± 10.09 hours versus 60.19 ± 12.36 hours in the control group. Mean first flatus was 26.81 versus 39.05 hours. The authors mark both comparisons p<.01. Table 4 shows abdominal-pain scores of 1.65 versus 2.08 on postoperative day 3, and 0.82 versus 1.43 on day 5. The baseline and day 1 contrasts were inconclusive. Bloating and the overall gastrointestinal score were lower on days 3 and 5. The article supplies group-completer estimates and no separate chronic constipation severity score.

The topical combination and lack of a sham prevent attribution to ginger alone. Postoperative first stool measures acute recovery after surgery. It differs from the frozen grated-ginger and Parkinson constipation question.

Source lineage: The October 2022 UEG poster P1009 has the same lead author, 100 recruited patients, comparison, outcome sequence and result direction. Treat the poster and paper as likely reports of one trial. The April 2023 paper reports the same 49 and 48 denominators and closely related treatment, with hormones and postoperative complications as endpoints. Its abstract differs in the named anatomical span. The thesis and companion full text are needed to reconcile cohort overlap. A 2021 timing comparison reports three arms of 50 and is a separate protocol, with possible participant overlap unverified.
