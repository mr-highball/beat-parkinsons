The prospective analysis enrolled participants without diagnosed Parkinson disease at baseline. The bowel frequency and stool consistency analysis included 170,017 participants and identified 519 incident diagnoses. Relative to at least seven bowel movements per week, the adjusted hazard ratio for a subsequent diagnosis was 2.16 (95% CI 1.74 to 2.68) for three to six bowel movements per week, and 2.57 (2.00 to 3.31) for fewer than three. The separate laxative analysis had 490,797 participants and 2,735 diagnoses.

The exposure is baseline bowel habit and the outcome is incident diagnosis. The study supplies epidemiological context for constipation as a possible prodromal marker. It does not evaluate a food, establish that constipation causes Parkinson disease, or test whether bowel symptoms respond to an intervention.
