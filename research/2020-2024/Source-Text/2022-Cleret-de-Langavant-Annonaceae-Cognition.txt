Title: Annonaceae Consumption Worsens Disease Severity and Cognitive Deficits in Degenerative Parkinsonism
PMID: 36210778
PMCID: PMC10092620
DOI: 10.1002/mds.29222
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10092620/
First publication date: 2022-10-10 (original article JATS).
Coverage: Original article body, exposure definition, cluster comparisons and adjusted regression tables.

The observational analysis used 180 of 305 participants in a Guadeloupe and Martinique parkinsonism cohort enrolled from 2012 to 2016. Seventy-four had probable Parkinson disease and 106 had atypical parkinsonism. A retrospective food questionnaire covered fruit and juice, plus herbal teas, from Annona muricata, A. squamosa and A. reticulata. It did not report eating dried Ficus fruit as its exposure.

The two clinical profiles were formed without the Annonaceae exposure inputs. Table 1 reports a binary visual hallucination item in 176 participants. Presence was recorded in 18.00% of the milder profile and 46.75% of the severe profile. The adjusted comparison of that item between profiles remained below P = 0.001. This is the presence of the symptom by profile, not an estimate of episode frequency.

In Table 3, the odds ratios for membership in the more severe profile were 3.76 (95% CI 1.13–15.18) for above 0.2 cumulative fruit-years of fruit or juice and 2.91 (1.34–6.56) for any herbal tea exposure. The outcome combines motor, cognitive, functional and other features. The study does not estimate the independently adjusted effect of any individual plant or meal on visual hallucinations alone. Retrospective recall, cohort selection and confounding limit causal inference. Further comparisons need to preserve the original parent cohort and the identity of each plant and preparation.

Earlier comparison note retained for lineage and working-memory outcomes:
October 10, 2022 online. The analysis includes 180 of 305 people with degenerative parkinsonism, including 74 classified as Parkinson disease and 106 atypical. Attention and working memory were assessed through digit span. Fruit or juice exposure above the study threshold was associated with membership in the severe clinical cluster (reported OR 3.76, 95% CI 1.13–15.18). The result describes a combined clinical profile with retrospective consumption and selected neuropsychological participation. The exposure comprises soursop, sweetsop and custard apple, with no kiwi comparison. A later Parkinson-only geographic analysis uses the same registry and Caribbean group size.
