Title: A pilot study investigating the impact of a caffeine-nap on alertness during a simulated night shift
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32819191/
Publisher: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07420528.2020.1804922

Review depth: Original article methods and results, Figure 1 description, and independently matched source metadata.

Six healthy people (four women) completed two periods, consuming either 200 mL decaffeinated coffee with added anhydrous caffeine (200 mg) or the same decaffeinated coffee alone. Both periods provided the same 30-minute nap opportunity. Coffee was therefore a shared exposure. The design isolates added caffeine under the post-nap conditions.

The mixed model showed less subjective fatigue after the caffeinated condition on the Samn–Perelli scale during the first 45 minutes after the nap (condition F(1,35) = 36.46, p < .001). The pre-nap comparison was inconclusive and the condition-by-time interaction was not clear. Sleep-stage recording was complete in three people only.

The 2026 AASM review's Figure S123 shows six observations per condition and the review body lists N = 12. The original and the review's intervention table identify six unique participants. Table S9 and Figure S123 are a secondary analysis of this same pilot and should not be counted again. The short post-nap result is contextual for brewed coffee and Parkinson fatigue (0121) due to shared decaf, added caffeine, acute sleep inertia and healthy population.
