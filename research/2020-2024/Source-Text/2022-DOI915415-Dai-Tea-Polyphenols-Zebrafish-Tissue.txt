Title: Dai and colleagues' tea-polyphenol study of ovarian mitochondrial gene expression
Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/marine-science/articles/10.3389/fmars.2022.915415/full
DOI: 10.3389/fmars.2022.915415
Publication date: 2022-05-30
Reviewed: 2026-11-25
Evidence depth: Original article, methods, results and combination exposure definition.

The original concerns mitochondrial gene expression in ovaries from female zebrafish exposed to bisphenol A. Selected mitochondrial NADH dehydrogenase expression measures declined with bisphenol A, and the presence of commercial tea polyphenols increased several of those measures. The original does not identify the test preparation as brewed black tea. Its reported results do not concern a Parkinson model, bradykinesia, or tyrosine hydroxylase as an assessed endpoint.

The 2025 nutraceutical review refers to black-tea extract and tyrosine hydroxylase in the paragraph and table supported by reference 197. Reference 197 identifies this Dai article. It is an ovarian toxicology experiment. Its biological findings are contextual, with no transferable estimate for the Round 1 brewed black-tea movement claim.
