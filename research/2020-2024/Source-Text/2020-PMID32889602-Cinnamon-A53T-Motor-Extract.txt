Raha and colleagues gave ten-month-old A53T mice ground Ceylon Cinnamomum verum, 100 mg/kg/day by gavage in methylcellulose, for 60 days. Separate sodium benzoate was 50 mg/kg/day. This aged genetic model differs from the earlier acute MPTP experiments, while retaining an overlapping investigator program.

Compared with vehicle-treated A53T mice, cinnamon favored rotarod performance and shorter pole turning/descent times. Figure 4 gives five or six mice per group without unambiguous endpoint-specific denominators. These are coordination and timed agility/bradykinesia proxies, not clinical gait velocity. Methods mention monitoring open-field velocity, but Results/Figure 4 give movement maps and center-time frequency without a separate numerical velocity effect. The report does not establish blinded longitudinal correction of each animal's premeasured walking deficit.

The March 12, 2025 correction (PMID40072646; DOI10.1007/s11481-025-10187-5) replaces Figure 6 after duplicated panels in the isolated sodium-benzoate histology experiment. It does not replace Figure 4 motor results. Preserve that image-reporting reliability caveat without inventing an opposing motor estimate. Neither tissue markers nor separate benzoate arms are additional clinical cohorts.

Original study scope
In the separate aged A53T mouse study, cinnamon-treated animals showed favorable rotarod and timed pole outcomes versus the model control. Figure 4 concerns timing, coordination and open-field behavior. Rigidity and dyskinesia occur in disease or treatment background without separate measurements for the cinnamon groups. Fig is an illustration label rather than fig fruit. The 2025 correction concerns a separate Figure 6 histology panel and leaves the assessed Figure 4 outcome scope intact.

Continuing evidence gaps
Keep the observed pole timing and rod performance available for movement research. The separate rigidity, dyskinesia and fruit findings remain unmeasured.
