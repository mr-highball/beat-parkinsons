2024 | Uzzan et al. | Cold-pressed Nigella sativa oil, reward consumption and social approach

Source: primary full report, PMID 38339101, DOI 10.3390/ijms25031823, online February 2, 2024.

Standard powdered chow was compared with chow enriched with commercial cold-pressed Nigella oil. The investigators assayed thymoquinone in the oil. After four weeks, healthy experimental rats consumed more sucrose than control rats over a 24-hour test in both sexes. Figure 2 lists male p=.001 and female p=.0002. The three-chamber sociability index increased in males (p=.036), without a clear female difference. Novelty preference and pre-amphetamine open-field velocity and distance did not differ clearly. These unlesioned animals underwent an experimental reward-consumption assay. A clinical apathy measure and ground seed were not used.
