Title: Effect of onion (Allium cepa L.) peel extract on natural killer cell and cytokines in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
Authors: Hyunji Cho, Sohui Kim, Sung Hyen Lee, Yongsoon Park
Primary original article: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10861340/
Publisher HTML: https://e-nrp.org/DOIx.php?id=10.4162%2Fnrp.2024.18.1.33
Official trial registry: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05666752
Identifiers: PMID 38352207, PMCID PMC10861340, DOI 10.4162/nrp.2024.18.1.33, NCT05666752
Date: 2024, Nutrition Research and Practice 18(1):33–45
Coverage: factual extract of full original Methods and Tables 2–3, checked against the trial registry

The randomized study assigned 40 adults per arm to 1,000 mg/day of onion peel extract or placebo for eight weeks. The study population had a recent history of an upper respiratory infection and elevated perceived stress. The researchers extracted dried outer onion peels from Muan, Korea in hot water, concentrated and freeze-dried the extract, and measured quercetin at 38 mg/g. This preparation omits the bulb flesh.

WURSS-21 symptom scores were a secondary outcome. Table 3 reports hoarseness means of 1.35 to 0.73 in the extract group and 0.24 to 0.70 in placebo. The reported ANCOVA of baseline-to-week-eight changes adjusting for perceived stress score gave p=.038. The between-arm total respiratory symptom result was p=.164. The primary natural killer cell activity through eight weeks did not separate the full groups with the adjusted model. The main text evaluates ten symptom items and does not include rated listener comprehension or objective articulation. No cooked bulb intervention or Parkinson cohort was included.
