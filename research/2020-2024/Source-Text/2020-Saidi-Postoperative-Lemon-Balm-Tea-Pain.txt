Title: Saidi et al. — post-orthopedic-surgery lemon balm tea, pain and satisfaction
DOI: 10.34172/jhp.2020.43

Original PDF: https://herbmedpharmacol.com/PDF/jhp-9-339.pdf
First published: 2020-07-01 per Crossref. October 2020 issue, volume 9(4), pp. 339–345.
Coverage: Complete original article, especially methods, Tables 2–3 and trial identification.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

The authors report 96 postoperative participants in lemon balm tea, chamomile tea and black tea groups. The comparison addresses short-term orthopedic pain and satisfaction. Table 2 reports a larger reduction in pain with lemon balm tea than black tea at the first six measurements. The seventh to ninth comparisons were inconclusive. The same direction at the first three measurements applies to the reported satisfaction comparison in Table 3, with no clear contrast at the later measurements.

The abstract groups its description around the first three measurements. Use the separate tables for the two endpoints. The printed self-citation uses a conflicting DOI ending in .45. The title page, publisher and Crossref agree on .43.

The trial ID IRCT20170122032101N2 and ethics code IR.SKUMS.Rec.156 match the 2023 state-anxiety report. The data do not supply a Parkinson symptom or depression endpoint. Once-assigned teas and their sensory differences also constrain interpretation of self-reported outcomes.
