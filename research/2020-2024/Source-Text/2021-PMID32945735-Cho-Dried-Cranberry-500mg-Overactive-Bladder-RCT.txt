Title: Efficacy of Daily Intake of Dried Cranberry 500 mg in Women with Overactive Bladder: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo Controlled Study.
PMID: 32945735
PMCID: PMC8299659
DOI: 10.1097/JU.0000000000001384
First published: 2021
Authors: Cho A, Eidelberg A, Butler DJ, Danko D, Afshinnekoo E, Mason CE, Chughtai B
Journal: The Journal of urology
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8299659/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial (Cho et al., 2021, J Urol, PMC8299659) enrolling 98 adult women (>=18 years) with overactive bladder (OAB) randomized to 500 mg/day dried cranberry powder capsules vs 500 mg placebo for 24 weeks; 77 completed all visits and 60 were included in the per-protocol (PP) analysis. In PP analysis, dried cranberry significantly reduced daily urgency episodes by -2.81/day (-57.3%; 95% CI -4.82 to -0.80, p=0.0069), daily micturitions by -1.91 (-16.4%; 95% CI -3.74 to -0.88, p=0.0406), and Patient Perception of Bladder Condition (PPBC) scores by -0.66 (-39.7%; 95% CI -1.23 to -0.08, p=0.0258), while nocturia, mean voided volume, and other OAB surveys did not differ (p>0.05). CRUCIAL LIMITATION: Intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis showed NO statistically significant between-group differences (p>0.05). Direct dried-cranberry urgency signal outside Parkinson disease, tempered by ITT null and attrition.
