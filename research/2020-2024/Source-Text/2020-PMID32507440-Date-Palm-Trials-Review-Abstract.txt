Title: Clinical effects of date palm (Phoenix dactylifera L.): A systematic review on clinical trials
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32507440/
Publisher: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ctim.2020.102429
PMID: 32507440
DOI: 10.1016/j.ctim.2020.102429
Publication year: 2020
Citation: Bagherzadeh Karimi A, Elmi A, Zargaran A, et al. Complementary Therapies in Medicine 51:102429.
Text coverage: Original summary of the authors' public abstract with a publisher locator. Study tables remain unverified.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

The authors evaluated English- and Persian-language clinical studies of date-palm preparations published from 2000 through August 2019. They included 26 studies across obstetric and gynecologic uses, oncology, dermatology, hematology, gastroenterology, endocrine or metabolic questions, and infertility. They report that 38% were randomized controlled trials and that 80% had performance and detection bias. The studies used multiple plant parts, so an aggregate literature count cannot identify a whole dried fruit trial or its effect.

This source is an index to possible clinical research. The abstract does not supply a dried-date contrast for clinical Parkinson pain or apathy, exact preparation-level pain findings, or the individual records from Persian-language sources. Verify eligible primary studies and their endpoints from the complete review tables and original reports. A category summary does not establish that an individual symptom was unstudied.
