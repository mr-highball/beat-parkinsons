Twenty female Wistar rats were placed into four groups of five. The article compares feed and water, dopamine, and two carrot-juice amounts. It does not specify a Parkinson disease model. The tests include passive avoidance, rotarod, grip and beam walking. The Discussion reports shorter latency to re-enter the shock-associated dark compartment after carrot juice and interprets this as improved memory. A food-specific cognitive direction cannot be assigned from that interpretation here. The authors describe some motor changes and qualify their beam-walking result.

Unheated juice and model learning and motor tasks do not provide a roasted-carrot or Parkinson executive-function comparison. No patient estimate can be transferred from this report.
