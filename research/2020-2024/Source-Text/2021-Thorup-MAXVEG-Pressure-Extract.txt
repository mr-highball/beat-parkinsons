Title: Strong and Bitter Vegetables from Traditional Cultivars and Cropping Methods Improve the Health Status of Type 2 Diabetics: A Randomized Control Trial
PMID: 34073610
PMCID: PMC8227621
DOI: 10.3390/nu13061813
First public publication: 2021-05-26
Source: https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/13/6/1813
Format: Factual extraction from the original main text, sections 2 and 3.3 and Table 4.
Thorup and colleagues enrolled 92 adults with type 2 diabetes and report 82 completers. The two active diets prescribed 500 g/day of bitter/strong or mild/sweet vegetables for 12 weeks. Both included red cabbage alongside several brassicas and roots. The control continued its habitual diet with a target of 120 g/day in the study design. The vegetables were delivered uncooked, and participants received recipes and cooking guidance. The amount and preparation of red cabbage actually eaten are unspecified.
Twenty-four-hour diastolic change in mmHg (mean ± SEM) was +1.76 ± 1.06 control, −1.15 ± 0.93 mild/sweet and −2.12 ± 1.28 bitter/strong. Table 4 reports p < .05 for the bitter/strong versus control contrast. The corresponding systolic changes were +3.16 ± 2.04, −1.22 ± 1.50 and −3.23 ± 2.23, with an omnibus p = .07.
Section 3.3 states no significant within-group pressure declines, although Table 4 marks within-group diastolic p < .05 in the bitter/strong group. Section 3.3 also says 12 months, whereas the methods and Table 4 specify 12 weeks. The aggregate ambulatory measurements provide no standing or lightheadedness outcome. The study does not isolate red cabbage or enroll a Parkinson cohort.
Registration: NCT01397942, first publicly posted 2011.
