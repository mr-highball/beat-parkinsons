Weemering and colleagues randomized 43 adults with ALS at four sites. The 24-week blinded phase assigned 21 to synthetic deuterated linoleic acid ethyl ester and 22 to encapsulated USP safflower oil, with matching appearance, size and taste. Participants initially took nine study capsules daily with planned reduction to six after four weeks and further reductions if needed. The paper identifies the comparator as linoleic-acid-enriched safflower oil. It gives no verified pressing method. A feeding tube at entry or anticipated need for one during the trial was an exclusion.

Table 1 classifies bulbar disease onset at baseline in 3 of 21 RT001 participants and 6 of 22 safflower participants. This classification describes pretrial disease presentation. Table 2 summarizes adverse events and Table 3 gives the 24-week efficacy comparisons.

In the repeated-measures model, total ALSFRS-R changed by −4.91 points with safflower oil (95% CI −7.23 to −2.58) and −3.00 with RT001 (−5.31 to −0.70). The RT001-minus-oil difference was 1.90 points (−1.39 to 5.19, p=.25). Total ALSAQ-40 changed by 18.86 with safflower versus 14.20 on RT001. The treatment-minus-oil difference was −4.66 (−14.67 to 5.35). Tube feeding was one component of a composite progression endpoint. The reported composite-free survival was 39.0% with safflower and 50.0% with RT001, with hazard ratio 0.57 (0.21 to 1.51). These estimates leave swallowing, speech and the individual tube-feeding component unresolved. The paper uses an active comparator and a population with progressive ALS. The change within the oil arm cannot by itself identify an effect caused by safflower.
