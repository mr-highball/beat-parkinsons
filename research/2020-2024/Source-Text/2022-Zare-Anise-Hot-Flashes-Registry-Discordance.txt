Hot flash outcome and registration discordance in the Tehran trial
Source: https://brieflands.com/journals/semj/articles/117385
Coverage: factual extract from primary report and official IRCT comparison

Elham Zare, Fatemeh Nahidi, Faraz Mojab and Hamid Alavi Majd. Shiraz E-Medical Journal 23(7):e117385. First online April 26, 2022. DOI 10.5812/semj-117385.
The study randomized 72 women to Pimpinella anisum ethanol extract capsules or placebo. The paper describes enrolment in Tehran between May 2018 and February 2019, 330 mg nominal extract capsules three times per day for eight weeks, and four weeks follow-up. Hot flash frequency and severity improved per report. It says there were no side effects in either group and gives no separately timed or quantified daytime drowsiness, insomnia, or dream enactment outcome.
The cited IRCT201103284317N4 was registered in 2011, describes expected recruitment in 2009–2010 and plans four treatment weeks. The 2012 Qazvin report also ran four weeks and enrolled 72, but does not independently identify this registry code in its accessible article. Treat the two journal reports as reporting different recruitment dates with uncertain registry assignment. Do not merge cohorts or infer a registry-prespecified eight-week safety or efficacy endpoint.
