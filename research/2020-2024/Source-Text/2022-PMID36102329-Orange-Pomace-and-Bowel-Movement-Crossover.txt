Title: Orange pomace and bowel movements in the Alexander crossover trial
Source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/bowel-habits-faecal-microbiota-and-faecal-bile-acid-composition-of-healthy-adults-consuming-fruit-pomace-fibres-twoarm-randomised-doubleblinded-placebocontrolled-trials/90DBA560250B1E0B038220F27D262CE7

Publication date: 2022-09-14
Date basis: First online publication. Print issue appeared in 2023.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24
Review depth: Full publisher article, including design, primary and secondary outcomes and the authors' description of Supplementary Table 7.

The orange crossover randomized 91 healthy adults to four-week periods of orange juice alone and the same juice with processed orange pomace, separated by a three-week washout. An independently enrolled cohort of 90 tested apple preparations. The orange cohort's treatment-by-time test for weekly bowel movements gave p=.46 and the between-treatment comparison of week-four change gave p=.20. For participants with up to seven bowel movements per week at baseline, the interaction gave p=.45. The exploratory interaction among participants with more frequent baseline bowel movements gave p=.008 without a significant comparison at an individual time point.

The authors report that a smaller proportion of participants in the added-pomace period had at least one day without a bowel movement per week, with an overall product effect of p=.002 independent of time. Supplementary Table S7 instead gives p=.004 for the main effect and p=.40 for the product-by-time interaction. The supplement records a pre-existing imbalance, and the baseline pomace cells sum to 86 despite a column heading of 84. Week-four counts were 33/89 for juice and 26/84 for juice with pomace. See the local extraction 2022-Alexander-Pomace-S7-Factual-Extract.txt. Stool consistency and ease of passage gave no clear difference. Gastrointestinal discomfort scores suggested more symptoms with pomace at week four, an inconclusive contrast (p=.066).

The findings distinguish a favorable secondary regularity measure from inconclusive primary frequency and stool-form outcomes. They compare processed fibre added to juice in healthy adults. The study provides no clinical Parkinson constipation comparison or whole-orange arm.
