Title: Hydroalcoholic Melissa officinalis extract and fluoxetine in male mice
First published: 2020
DOI: 10.29252/iau.30.4.418
Source: https://tmuj.iautmu.ac.ir/files/site1/user_files_5255d5/admin-A-10-1-637-ee6f0f2.pdf
Archive scope: Original seven-page bilingual publication, pp. 418–424. Methods and original figures checked.

Five groups of seven male mice received an oral leaf extract, fluoxetine or saline for seven days. Methods and both figure labels identify extract groups of 600, 800 and 1,000 mg/kg. The dried leaf was extracted with 70% ethanol and the resulting extract redissolved after evaporation. The Persian and English abstract doses agree with the labels when read in the original page images. The tested material is a concentrated extract.

Figure 1 shows tail-suspension immobility above saline at 800 mg/kg, with a saline comparison marker. The 600 and 1,000 mg/kg bars are below saline. Figure 2 shows forced-swim immobility above saline at 800 mg/kg as well, again with a saline comparison marker. In that figure, the fluoxetine mean is close to or above saline. The 1,000 mg/kg bar is below saline, but its annotation compares it with fluoxetine, while the Results narrative describes a significant saline comparison. The 600 mg/kg tail-suspension bar carries a fluoxetine comparison marker. The figures and accompanying narrative do not support dose-wide improvement or formal equivalence to fluoxetine. Possible mechanisms discussed by the authors were not measured clinical outcomes. The behavior assays involved no Parkinson cohort or brewed infusion.
