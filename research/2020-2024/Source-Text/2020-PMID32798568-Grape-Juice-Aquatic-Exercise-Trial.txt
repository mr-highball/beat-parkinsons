Title: Effect of exercise and grape juice on epigenetic modulation and functional outcomes in PD: A randomized clinical trial
PMID: 32798568
DOI: 10.1016/j.physbeh.2020.113135
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32798568/
Original publisher-formatted PDF: https://www.sucodeuvapuro.com.br/?jet_download=a456df2f345d26876b56d0ee7b299ca06d6c1836
First published: 2020-08-14. Physiology & Behavior 227, article 113135.
Archive scope: Factual notes from complete seven-page article. Methods, CONSORT and outcome tables were checked directly. Reviewed 2026-11-20.

Both groups performed four weeks of aquatic exercise. One group also consumed juice made from Vitis labrusca, with the variety printed as Bordon. The study has no raw-grape arm and no independent anxiety or rigidity outcome. UPDRS motor scores in Table 2 characterize the sample at baseline. Table 3 reports group-by-time p values of .11 for six-minute walk distance, .28 for Timed Up and Go, and .70 for Berg Balance score. Berg Balance is a performance measure and separate from counted falls. Blood BDNF and histone H4 acetylation changed over time, with no clear incremental juice comparison in the reported study.

Figure 1 shows 24 randomized and 12 allocated per arm. Nine exercise-only and ten exercise-plus-juice participants were analyzed. The abstract describes these 19 as randomized, and a 2023 secondary review reverses the two arm counts in its narrative. The flow chart and original Tables 2–3 control the analysis denominators. The eligibility text lists Hoehn and Yahr stages 1 through 3 and Table 2 includes one stage 4 participant in the juice arm.
