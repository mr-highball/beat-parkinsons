Title: Linalool identified in a Ceylon cinnamon bark essential oil
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10975636/
PMID: 38559956
PMCID: PMC10975636
DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.3c09595
Publication date: 2024-03-13
Date basis: First electronic publication.
Review depth: Full PMC structured original, source material, GC methods and Table 1.

A supplied Cinnamomum verum bark oil had a deposited voucher. The GC table reports 75.9% (E)-cinnamaldehyde and 7.0% linalool. The specimen provides an original chemical link between Ceylon cinnamon bark oil and linalool. These are relative oil proportions from a supplied preparation. Enzyme-based antiviral assays in the same article do not measure mood or anxiety.

For ground cinnamon claim 0243, linalool identification does not establish the amount, chiral identity, bioavailability or physiological effect of a culinary exposure. The 2022 commercial sample comparison did not detect linalool in its own headspace samples.

Additional composition and traditional-use assessment: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-report/assessment-report-cinnamomum-verum-j-s-presl-cortex-and-corticis-aetheroleum_en.pdf
The 10 May 2011 EMA assessment, section 1.1, cites linalool at 1–3% of the essential oil and distinguishes bark, distilled oil and comminuted herbal substance. The traditional indications reviewed do not establish a clinical anxiety effect.
