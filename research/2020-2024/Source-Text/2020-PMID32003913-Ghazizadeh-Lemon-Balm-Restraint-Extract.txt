Title: Melissa officinalis L. hydro-alcoholic extract inhibits anxiety and depression through prevention of central oxidative stress and apoptosis
PMID: 32003913
DOI: 10.1113/EP088254
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32003913/
Author manuscript: https://academy.miloa.eu/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Me%CC%81lisse-stress-rats.pdf
First published: 2020-02-21 online per NLM. Experimental Physiology 105(4):707–720.
Coverage: Accepted original manuscript, Methods, Results and Figure 4.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

The study assigned 60 male BALB/c mice to five groups of twelve. The restraint-stress groups received saline vehicle or oral hydroalcoholic lemon-balm extract. The extract was characterized by its measured rosmarinic acid content. Behavioral testing was blinded.

Relative to the restraint control, the middle and highest extract groups had shorter immobility on both forced-swim and tail-suspension assays. In the accepted Methods and Results these are 75 and 150 mg/kg, respectively. The lowest comparison did not show that result. The accepted Figure 4 caption calls the middle group M75 but gives 100 mg/kg. This inconsistency warrants checking the final figure or underlying records before reusing the middle-dose label. Open-field total locomotion did not differ significantly among groups. Oxidative and apoptosis markers and corticosterone also changed. Correlations with assay outcomes do not show that any single marker or constituent mediated the effect.

The intervention is a hydroalcoholic concentrate in a restraint-stress mouse comparison. Neither a brewed infusion nor clinical outcomes in Parkinson disease were measured.
