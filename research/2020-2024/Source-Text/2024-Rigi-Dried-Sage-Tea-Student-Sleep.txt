Common sage tea and sleep quality in students
Source: https://cmja.arakmu.ac.ir/article-1-1008-en.pdf
DOI: 10.32592/cmja.14.3.13 and 10.22034/cmja.14.3.13 as printed
Coverage: Original publisher body, Persian methods and Table 2

Rigi and Shahnavazi reported a double-blind comparison of 128 dormitory students, 64 per group, with allocation using room numbers. Over one month students brewed nightly tea bags containing 10 g of sage and 3 g of brown sugar, or 13 g of brown sugar as the control. The original does not explicitly establish the sage drying method. Table 2 gives mean PSQI 9.93 ± 2.72 to 8.21 ± 2.17 for sage and 9.89 ± 2.74 to 10.60 ± 2.88 for control. The prose instead gives 9.39 for the sage baseline. Either reading gives lower change scores for sage, with crude differences of −2.43 or −1.89 points. The follow-up Mann–Whitney result is printed .00 and the authors describe it as significant. A separate .003 Wilcoxon value cannot be assigned to the between-group contrast. The table gives no room-adjusted estimate or confidence interval. The PDF supplies IRCT20141125020082N2 and prints conflicting DOI prefixes 10.22034 and 10.32592. Taste, unequal sugar and transfer from young students and global sleep quality limit the Parkinson insomnia inference.
