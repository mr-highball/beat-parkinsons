Title: Characterization and Functional Properties of Lactobacilli Isolated from Kefir Grains
DOI: 10.1134/S0003683821040037
Source: https://www.pleiades.online/cgi-perl/search.pl?name=appbio&number=4&page=458&type=abstract&year=21
Published: 2021, Applied Biochemistry and Microbiology 57(4), pages 458–467.
Archive scope: Factual record from the publisher's author abstract and its explicit reuse in the 2023 original study, DOI 10.3390/foods12010223. Original methods and full strain-level tables remain open.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Begunova and colleagues report three lactobacilli isolated from Northern Caucasus kefir grains, including two classified as Lacticaseibacillus paracasei and one Lactobacillus helveticus. They screened antibiotic susceptibility, resistance markers and enzymatic activities. In a milk fermentation test, the individual isolates acidified more slowly than the kefir starter culture. The compared readouts include proteolysis, antioxidant capacity and biochemical activity relevant to ACE inhibition.

The 2023 paper names KF1 and ABK as previously reported isolates and explicitly reuses their enzyme data for Figure 3. FEBS poster P-07.2-08 describes two paracasei isolates from kefir with different susceptibility profiles. The poster gives no strain IDs, so exact linkage to KF1 and ABK remains possible and unverified. The tests concern isolates and milk fermentation. None compares plain kefir consumption with falls or fatigue in people with Parkinson disease.
