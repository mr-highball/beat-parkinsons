Title: A randomized double-blind active-controlled clinical trial on the efficacy of topical basil (Ocimum basilicum) oil in knee osteoarthritis.
PMID: 38769995
PMCID: PMC11103000
DOI: 10.3389/fphar.2024.1377527
First published: 2024
Authors: Askari A, Hasheminasab FS, Sadeghpour O, Naghizadehd MM, Ravansalar SA, Iraji A, Hashempur MH
Journal: Frontiers in pharmacology
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11103000/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Full-text verified double-blind active-controlled RCT (PMC11103000; IRCT2017081711341N7; authorship-only erratum PMID:39931694/PMC11808300) in n=100 randomized (n=86 completed: 43 basil oil, 43 diclofenac gel) knee osteoarthritis patients applying topical traditional basil oil (Ocimum basilicum macerated in sesame oil vehicle) or topical diclofenac gel tid for 4 weeks. No between-group differences at week 4 in VAS pain (basil 61.05 +/- 9.67 -> 44.07 +/- 11.87 vs diclofenac 59.07 +/- 9.47 -> 44.53 +/- 13.53, p = 0.880), WOMAC pain (8.59 -> 5.71 vs 8.17 -> 5.48, p = 0.887), WOMAC total (40.02 -> 29.27 vs 39.66 -> 27.52, p = 0.354), or knee flexion (p = 0.473), with within-group pain improvements in both arms (p < 0.001). Crucially, the 8-m walking test showed ZERO improvement even within-group (basil 13.34 +/- 6.81 -> 13.30 +/- 10.47 s, p = 0.975; baseline imbalanced vs diclofenac 10.30 +/- 2.03 -> 10.39 +/- 2.56 s, p = 0.006 baseline / 0.555 week 4). Lacks a placebo arm and combines basil with sesame oil carrier topically, preventing isolated culinary oral attribution.
