This is a separate outcome report from IRCT20170122032101N2, matching the earlier pain paper on hospital, 96 participants across three groups, tea assignments and ethics code. Block randomization assigned lemon balm tea, chamomile tea or black tea. The analyzed instrument was the state portion of the Spielberger inventory. Repeat assessments were made after each tea administration.

The lemon balm tea comparison was inconclusive at the first measurement and favored tea over active black tea at later time points. Table 3 separates the comparisons, including an inconclusive early chamomile–control result. All three groups improved over time. The paper states that taste, color and scent prevented masking. Keep the registered endpoints and the Persian table separate from background assertions about depression or sedatives.

The human population, postoperative state anxiety and active comparator limit transfer to brewed lemon balm and depressive severity in Parkinson disease. The two publications do not constitute independent replications.
