Cinnamon among five odors in postinfectious smell training
2020-06-25
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7904423/

Choi and colleagues compared three months of odor training with observation in 104 patients with postinfectious olfactory dysfunction. The 40 training participants used rose, lemon, cinnamon, orange and peach odorants; 64 were observed. The threshold-discrimination-identification total went from 17.5 to 22.1 and from 15.6 to 18.3, respectively. The reported adjusted repeated-measures group comparison gave p=.018. The Results print identical statistics for identification and discrimination while describing different significance for them. This contrasts a multi-odor program with observation, with no separate cinnamon exposure or Parkinson food outcome.
