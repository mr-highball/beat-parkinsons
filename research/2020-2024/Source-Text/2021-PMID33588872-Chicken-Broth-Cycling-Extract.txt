Title: Ergogenic effect of pre-exercise chicken broth ingestion on a high-intensity cycling time-trial

Barbaresi and colleagues compared chicken broth and a vegetarian placebo soup in a blinded randomized crossover of healthy recreationally trained men. Twenty men entered the study, 14 participated as described in the abstract and 13 were analyzed after one further exclusion. The serving averaged 607 mL and contained about 3.52 g of combined histidine-containing dipeptides. The intervention was broth before intense cycling. It supplied different food components from the vegetarian comparator, including protein or peptides.

Mean relative power in the final 8-minute cycling test was 5.0 versus 4.75 W/kg (p=.033, Figure 2). Circulating anserine and carnosine increased. The study found no significant supplementation interaction for red-cell glutathione, and the tested acid–base comparisons did not identify a clear mechanism. The power measurement addresses short physical performance and does not quantify perceived daily fatigue or fatigue severity in Parkinson disease.
