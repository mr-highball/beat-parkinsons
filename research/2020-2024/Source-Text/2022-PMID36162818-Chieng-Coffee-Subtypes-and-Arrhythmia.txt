Title: Habitual coffee subtype and cardiovascular outcomes in UK Biobank
First published: 2022-09-27
PMID: 36162818
DOI: 10.1093/eurjpc/zwac189
Source: https://academic.oup.com/eurjpc/article/29/17/2240/6704995
Archive scope: Original results and methods summarized from the author abstract and publisher full report.

Participants self-reported amount and primary subtype of coffee. The cohort study used diagnoses and death records to follow incident cardiac rhythm events, cardiovascular disease and mortality. In adjusted models, ground and instant coffee were associated with fewer arrhythmias at some intake levels. The decaffeinated subtype lacked the same arrhythmia association. All three subtypes had associations with lower incidence of cardiovascular disease and all-cause mortality at some intake levels. Separate models and observational confounding limit inference about caffeine or subtype effects. OP-162-1-GE reports closely related coffee-subtype work in the same UK Biobank, and OP-163-1-GE examines people with existing cardiac conditions. These are related reporting routes. Neither rhythm outcomes nor overall mortality substitute for a Parkinson symptom score.
