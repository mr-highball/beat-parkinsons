Title: Clinical trial: A Mediterranean diet is feasible and improves gastrointestinal and psychological symptoms in irritable bowel syndrome
PMID: 37969059
DOI: 10.1111/apt.17791
Trial registration reported by the investigators: ACTRN12620001362987
Publication date: 2023-11-15
Date basis: Wiley first online publication. February 2024 issue, pp. 492–503.
Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/apt.17791
Reviewed: 2026-11-22
Verified depth: Original peer-reviewed full text, intervention and Tables 2–3.

TANDIM randomized 59 adults with irritable bowel syndrome and mild or moderate anxiety or depressive symptoms to Mediterranean-pattern dietary counseling or their habitual diet for six weeks. Forty-eight completed. The unblinded trial assessed feasibility and measured bowel symptoms, anxiety and depression separately. Table 2 reports adjusted HADS anxiety of 7.2 versus 10.6 (p < .001). GSRS constipation was 1.4 versus 1.5 (p = .420) and hard stool scores were 1.3 versus 1.4 (p = .446). The improved overall IBS score differs from these individual constipation outcomes. The groups had different baseline IBS subtype distributions.

The dietary program changed multiple exposures. The authors discuss olive oil as part of the pattern. The randomized comparison does not isolate cold-pressed or extra-virgin oil. Participants had irritable bowel syndrome and the trial does not supply a Parkinson-specific estimate. The conference report PP1101, the peer-reviewed trial and the 2025 item-level analysis are the same cohort.
