Title: The effects of DL-3-n-butylphthalide in patients with mild cognitive impairment due to Parkinson’s disease: A multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial
Authors: C. Han, Y. Xia, J. Wu, Y. Sun, L. Kou, J. Huang, Q. Wang, E. Xu, H. Yu, Q. Ye, X. Luo, H. Ma, H. Chen, L. Wang and T. Wang
Citation: Movement Disorders. 2024;39(Supplement 1), abstract 764.
Source: https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/the-effects-of-dl-3-n-butylphthalide-in-patients-with-mild-cognitive-impairment-due-to-parkinsons-disease-a-multicenter-randomized-double-blind-placebo-controlled-trial/
Companion publisher collection: https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.29951
Text coverage: selected facts paraphrased from the original congress report.

The report describes a double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter randomized trial at ten academic sites in China. Participants aged 18–80 had mild cognitive impairment associated with Parkinson's disease. Entry MoCA was 20–25, and the DBS subgroup was required to have reached at least 26 before surgery. The report says enrolled participants were receiving stable dopaminergic medication. Participants received pharmaceutical NBP 200 mg three times daily or matching placebo for 48 weeks. The report lists 287 enrolled participants. ITT change in MoCA was 1.74 with NBP versus 0.90 with placebo (p = .0059); MMSE change was 1.00 versus 0.19 (p = .0001). Investigators monitored adverse events and summarize treatment-related events as uncommon and largely mild gastrointestinal effects. There is no individual severity comparison for dyskinesia, formal Part IV result, or food exposure in this abstract.

Potential registration match: ChiCTR1800017084. The design, diagnostic and screening criteria, dose schedule and follow-up, primary scales and senior investigator align. The congress report does not state the identifier.
