Title: Broccoli-Derived Exosome-like Nanoparticles Alleviate Loperamide-Induced Constipation, in Correlation with Regulation on Gut Microbiota and Tryptophan Metabolism
PMID: 37875137
DOI: 10.1021/acs.jafc.3c04150
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37875137/
Publisher: https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jafc.3c04150
First published: 2023-10-24. Journal issue 2023-11-08, 71(44), pp. 16568–16580.
Archive scope: Factual summary of the original author's abstract. Full tables, comparator details and supplements remain open. Reviewed 2026-11-20.

Duan and colleagues administered broccoli-derived exosome-like nanoparticles orally to mice with loperamide-induced constipation. The abstract reports shorter time to defecation, greater intestinal propulsion and increased fecal output. The microbial and tryptophan findings accompanied these changes. The abstract does not establish a human effect or isolate the microbial pathway as the clinical cause of improvement.

The tested nanoparticle fraction differs from eating broccoli, and the animal model differs from Parkinson constipation. This original is the source cited in PMID 39309692.
