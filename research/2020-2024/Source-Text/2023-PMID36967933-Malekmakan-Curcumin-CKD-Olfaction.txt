Title: Comparison between olfactory training and curcumin therapy in improving olfactory dysfunction in patients with chronic kidney disease
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10031463/
PMID: 36967933
PMCID: PMC10031463
DOI: 10.1016/j.heliyon.2023.e14478
Publication date: 2023-03-12
Date basis: Publication date in official NCBI PMC JATS XML metadata (Heliyon 9(3):e14478, March 2023).
Coverage: Paraphrased original methods and Tables 1-3 from official NCBI PMC JATS XML.

This double-blind, three-arm randomized placebo-controlled trial in Shiraz, Iran enrolled 60 patients with chronic kidney disease and olfactory dysfunction, analyzing 58 completers (curcumin n = 20, olfactory training n = 19, placebo n = 19; mean age 56.1 ± 2.5 years) over 12 weeks. Outcomes were the Iran Smell Identification Test (Iran-SIT), Questionnaire of Olfactory Disorders (QOD; lower score = better quality of life), and a subjective self-assessment rating.

In Table 2, curcumin reduced QOD scores from 17.5 ± 11.8 to 13.1 ± 9.7 (within-arm p = 0.045; reported change +3.5 ± 2.6 vs placebo -0.8 ± 1.2, 3-arm Kruskal-Wallis p = 0.003) and improved self-assessment from 8.5 ± 3.1 to 9.5 ± 4.0 (p = 0.047; 3-arm change p = 0.048), whereas placebo was unchanged across all measures (p > 0.05). Iran-SIT rose from 14.0 ± 4.1 to 14.7 ± 3.3 (within-arm p = 0.066; Table 2 prints a change of +1.8 ± 1.2 vs placebo -0.1 ± 0.4, Kruskal-Wallis p = 0.023, reflecting an internal arithmetic discrepancy with printed pre/post means). In Table 3, 4 of 20 curcumin participants (20.0%) shifted to normosmia (ordinal shift p = 0.032) versus 0 of 19 on placebo (p = 0.317), while olfactory training outperformed curcumin on Iran-SIT and QOD (p = 0.002). The trial studied isolated oral curcumin in chronic kidney disease rather than grated culinary turmeric in Parkinson disease.
