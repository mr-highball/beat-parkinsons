Title: Effectiveness of remotely delivered speech therapy in persons with Parkinson's disease – a randomised controlled trial
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11415969/
PMID: 39309726
PMCID: PMC11415969
DOI: 10.1016/j.eclinm.2024.102823
Publication date: 2024-09-11
Date basis: online publication in original JATS
Source depth: factual extract of full original results, Table 2 and linked design

Maas and colleagues randomized 214 people with Parkinson disease and reduced speech intelligibility to eight weeks of personalized remote speech therapy or waiting list. The arms included 109 and 105 participants. The primary measure was PDQ-39 quality of life. Table 2 reports distinct patient-rated ROMP speech, swallowing and drooling domains.

On the 9–45 drooling subscale, the eight-week median was 13 in both arms, with 104 treated and 100 control observations. The estimated between-group difference was −0.22 points (95% CI −1.1 to 0.7, p=0.682). Swallowing had a separate estimate of −0.58 points (95% CI −1.2 to 0.1, p=0.073). In the intervention group, the 32-week drooling score difference from week eight was 0.44 points (95% CI −0.2 to 1.1, p=0.200). The wait-list group supplies no 32-week contrast.

The drooling result is a secondary, patient-reported composite and its confidence interval spans zero. No food or raw-fig exposure was assigned. The original protocol identifies ROMP under complaints about speech and voice. The published trial separately tabulates all three patient-rated domains.
