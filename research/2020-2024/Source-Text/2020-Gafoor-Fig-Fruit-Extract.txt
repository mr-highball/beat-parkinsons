The investigators tested an oral concentrated ethanol extract made from dried fig fruit powder alongside vehicle, haloperidol alone and levodopa-carbidopa comparison groups in rats. The acute and 14-day comparisons used the same group scheme. The authors label the reported comparisons as six animals per group.

The original narrative reports shorter metal-bar catalepsy duration at both extract levels in the acute and longer experiments. Compared with haloperidol alone, the 14-day comparisons report more locomotor activity and longer time before falling from the rotating rod. For each of these narrative contrasts the lower extract group is reported at p < .01 and the higher group at p < .001. Tables 1 and 2 are needed for numeric effect sizes, uncertainty and the exact time points underlying the claims. The archived gallery caption identifies liver from a lead injury model while the narrative describes brain histology. The caption and image cannot independently verify the reported brain comparison.

Resting tremor appears in the introductory description of Parkinson disease. The behavioral methods measure retained bar posture, voluntary activity and rotating-rod performance. The comparison gives no rest-tremor assessment or clinical raw fig exposure. Rotating-rod latency and catalepsy remain distinct animal motor outcomes.
