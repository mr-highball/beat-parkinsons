Title: Society for Neurochemistry India conference abstract PP11 on grape seed powder and mood
Source: https://snci2024.puchd.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/SNCI_Abstract_Book_Print_compressed.pdf
Publication date: 2024-09
Date basis: The organizer's abstract book dates the meeting to September 26–28, 2024. Its first publication day was not established.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26
Review depth: original_conference_abstract

Printed page 108 reports a randomized placebo comparison of grape seed powder in 120 adults with mild cognitive impairment over 12 weeks. DASS-21 depression and anxiety changes reported for the seed-powder group were −1.68 ± 0.23 (p=.001) and −0.61 ± 0.60 (p=.041), respectively. The abstract describes slight placebo increases but does not give a clear group-by-time or adjusted between-arm estimate for these outcomes, arm-specific counts, or the meaning of the dispersion terms. The trial addresses oral seed powder, a materially different exposure from refined fixed oil. A matched full trial report and registration had not been verified. The authors' subsequent review is not an independent cohort.

Claim 0169 | Related evidence: The conference abstract reports a favorable within-arm depression change after oral grape seed powder. It provides no clear between-arm mood estimate, has a mild cognitive impairment population and does not test refined grapeseed oil.

Claim 0467 | Related evidence: The conference abstract reports a favorable within-arm anxiety change after oral grape seed powder. The reported excerpt lacks a verified between-arm anxiety estimate and does not test an oil or Parkinson population.
