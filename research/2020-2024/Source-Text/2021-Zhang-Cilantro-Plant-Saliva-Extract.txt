Factual extract: Twenty-six healthy students, 13 per group, performed 50 minutes of homework in rooms with or without living potted coriander and then watched plants or control surroundings for five minutes. Participants could see and smell the plants; no oral herb consumption was described. Mood was measured by POMS (including anger-hostility, fatigue and vigor), and saliva was collected using a chewed Salivette for amylase, cortisol and amino-acid assays. Results include lower anger-hostility and changes in concentrations of salivary analytes. These are not clinical Parkinson apathy, salivary flow, or drooling measures, and no fresh/dried culinary leaf exposure is established.
