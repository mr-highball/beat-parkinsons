Title: Melissa officinalis extract improved high-fat-diet-induced anxiety-like behaviors, depression, and memory impairment by regulation of serum BDNF levels in rats
PMID: 40255947
PMCID: PMC12009017
DOI: 10.22038/AJP.2024.24343
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12009017/
First published: September–October 2024 issue, Avicenna Journal of Phytomedicine 14(5):617–631. The index uses September 1 for sorting. The later NLM identifier does not set publication date.
Coverage: Original full-text XML, Methods, Results and Figure 4 caption.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

Eighty male Wistar rats received normal or high-fat diets. Following five weeks, each diet group was split into four groups of ten. Animals received intraperitoneal injections of a hydroalcoholic lemon balm extract or vehicle for two weeks. The route is central to interpretation because the experiment did not use oral tea or lemon balm as a food.

The high-fat diet reduced sucrose preference relative to normal diet. The Results describe a dose-related increase in sucrose intake after extract among high-fat diet rats. The low dose in normal-diet rats instead decreased sucrose preference versus normal-diet vehicle (p<.05 in the Results). The abstract emphasizes the favorable high-fat diet comparisons. Both directions are retained here with their separate controls. Serum BDNF changes accompany some behavioral changes and do not by themselves demonstrate mediation.

Sucrose preference is a rodent behavioral and intake measure. Differences in diet, thirst or metabolism can affect the test. Neither group supplies a human depression severity estimate or a Parkinson diagnosis.
