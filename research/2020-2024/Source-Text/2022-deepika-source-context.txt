Claim 0110 concerns refined coconut oil and postural instability severity.
Section on clinical evidence, ResearchGate HTML lines 1361–1389. The narrative cites a mixed-condition lifestyle report through Martin and Preedy's edited book. In the original Null et al. group report, coconut oil was permitted and specified in the breakfast smoothie instructions, and two Parkinson participants gave separate testimonials. The multiple-sclerosis testimonial belongs to another participant in the same 27-person program. Neither actual consumption by an individual, refining grade, nor measured postural instability can be established from the report. The review also summarizes Mischley et al. 2017, DOI 10.1155/2017/6405278 and PMID 29081890, a cross-sectional survey of 1,053 people self-reporting idiopathic Parkinson disease. That underlying paper used total PRO-PD and a general coconut-oil food-frequency item. It did not estimate a paired postural result or distinguish refined oil. The remainder of this coconut-oil clinical paragraph cites Alzheimer's or mild cognitive impairment cohorts. Postural instability is named in the review's Parkinson background. These are related citations and supply no independent estimate for the frozen pair.
