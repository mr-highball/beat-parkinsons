FiSK Junior oily-fish meals and socioemotional function
Original factual extract of a published primary study

Teisen MN and colleagues. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. 2020;112(1):74–83.
PMID 32529206
DOI 10.1093/ajcn/nqaa050
Registration NCT02809508
Record https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32529206/
Registration https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02809508

The Danish trial randomized 8–9-year-old children to approximately 12 weeks of oily fish or poultry. The fish provisions included salmon fillets for dinners and mixed fish products for lunches. The outcomes were analyzed for the assigned oily-fish group. A total of 199 children were randomized and 197 completed the trial.

The adjusted oily-fish versus poultry contrast was −0.63 points (95% CI −1.11 to −0.16) for the parent-rated Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire internalizing score, −0.89 points (−1.60 to −0.18) for total difficulties and −0.13 (−0.26 to 0.01) for the aggregate socioemotional score. Internalizing includes emotional and peer problems. No salmon-specific, preparation-specific or standalone anxiety effect was reported.
