Title: Dietary strawberry improves cognition in a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in older adults
Source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/dietary-strawberry-improves-cognition-in-a-randomised-doubleblind-placebocontrolled-trial-in-older-adults/6DCBA9C5A798C0E6149322BBBCAAD461
PMID: 33468271
DOI: 10.1017/S0007114521000222
First public publication date: 2021-01-20
Accessed: 2026-11-19
Text coverage: Distilled original Methods, mobility outcomes and separately published correction.

Miller and colleagues compared daily 24 g freeze-dried strawberry powder with a matched placebo for 90 days in healthy older adults (NCT02051140). The final per-protocol sample included 18 strawberry and 19 placebo participants. Entry screening excluded people with any falls in the prior year. There was no detected effect on fear of falling during the study (Falls Efficacy Scale International, p=.518), tabled postural sway, gait speed or gait variability. The instrument measures concern about falling. The study supplies no comparison of observed fall frequency and no Parkinson cohort.

The authors report a group-by-visit word recognition difference (p=.014) and a shorter-term spatial probe difference (p=.020). The Attention Network Test, task switching, trail making and digit span showed no significant treatment effects. The correction to Table 1 is a separate publication in the same study family. Correction DOI: 10.1017/S0007114521000842.
