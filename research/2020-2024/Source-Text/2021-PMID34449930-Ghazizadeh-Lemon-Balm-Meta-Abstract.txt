Title: The effects of lemon balm (Melissa officinalis L.) on depression and anxiety in clinical trials: A systematic review and meta-analysis
PMID: 34449930
DOI: 10.1002/ptr.7252
Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/ptr.7252
First published: 2021-08-27.
Text coverage: Original abstract and available figure captions, with marked cross-source interpretation.

The abstract reports a pooled depression standardized mean difference of -0.47 (95% CI -0.73 to -0.21). It identifies a limited number of heterogeneous trials. The included-study table and Figure 3 row labels were unavailable through the routes verified. The directly readable original anxiety sensitivity caption names five studies. A separate summary of six trials and 435 people and an independent summary of three depression trials support a likely depression membership of Haybar, Heydari and Beihaghi. This is a reconstruction. Exact depression weights and timepoint selection remain unverified.

The candidate originals address stable angina, adolescents with premenstrual symptoms and postpartum blues with capsules. The pooled result contributes no additional participant cohort. The conference and registry evidence for brewed tea must be assessed separately.
