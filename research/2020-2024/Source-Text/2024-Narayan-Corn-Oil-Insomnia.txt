Scope: Factual methods and outcome summary from the original report.

After a single-blind corn-oil run-in week, 34 adults with primary insomnia were randomized (18 CBD, 16 placebo). Eight run-in placebo responders were excluded before randomization. Thirty-two completed dosing and the reported primary analysis used 30 completers (15 per arm). The two-week randomized period used 150 mg CBD in oil or the vehicle alone. The original identifies the vehicle as corn oil and gives 1.5 mL nightly, approximately 60 minutes before bed. The dosing procedure is described as sublingual. The refining process for the corn oil is unreported.

Table 2 reports baseline-to-week-two Insomnia Severity Index means of 18.6 to 11.6 for CBD and 17.4 to 9.7 for corn oil. The endpoint difference (CBD minus corn oil) was −1.9 points (95% CI −5.0 to 1.1). Diary sleep-onset latency, subjective efficiency and waking after sleep onset showed no clear between-arm differences. The CBD group had higher objective actigraphy sleep efficiency at the end of week two, an estimated 6.85 percentage points (SE 2.95) above the corn oil group. That contrast tests CBD added to its oil vehicle. Both trial arms received the oil and the trial did not include a no-oil arm or establish a Parkinson insomnia effect.

The official registered protocol describes 200 mg CBD in 2 mL and three dosing weeks, while multiple outcome timepoints refer to two weeks. The published methods report the actual exposure and retain the registered identity. The public record does not resolve the timing or rationale for these changes.
