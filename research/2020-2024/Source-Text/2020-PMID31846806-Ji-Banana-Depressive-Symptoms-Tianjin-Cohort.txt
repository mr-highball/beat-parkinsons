Title: The association between banana consumption and the depressive symptoms in Chinese general adult population: A cross-sectional study.
PMID: 31846806
DOI: 10.1016/j.jad.2019.12.008
First published: 2020
Authors: Ji T, Li X, Meng G, Gu Y, Zhang Q, Liu L
Journal: Journal of affective disorders
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31846806/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Cross-sectional observational study of n=24,673 general adults in Tianjin, China assessing banana consumption via validated FFQ and depressive symptoms via Self-Rating Depression Scale (SDS >= 45; prevalence 16.1% men, 18.4% women). Results were sex-divergent: in men, moderate banana intake was inversely associated with depressive symptoms (<1/wk OR 0.86 [0.74-0.99]; 1-3/wk OR 0.76 [0.66-0.88]; >=4/wk OR 0.97 [0.82-1.16]), whereas in women, high banana intake (>=4/wk) was positively associated with higher depressive symptom odds (OR 1.22 [1.02-1.46]; 1-3/wk OR 0.99). Limits: cross-sectional design cannot establish causality or rule out reverse causation; non-PD cohort; unspecified preparation.
