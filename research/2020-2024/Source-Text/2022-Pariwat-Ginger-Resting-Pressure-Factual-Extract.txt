Dissolved ginger and resting pressure in older women
Source: https://tis.wu.ac.th/index.php/tis/article/download/4606/269
DOI: 10.48048/tis.2022.4606
Coverage: Primary publisher full text and tables

Pariwat, Masodsai and Chuanchaiyakul reported 32 older women completing a 30-day randomized parallel study. The intervention was commercial powdered ginger dissolved in water, 75 mg per kg of body weight after breakfast. In Table 2, resting systolic pressure (mean ± SE, mmHg) changed from 150.00 ± 4.21 to 147.30 ± 3.73 among hypertensive controls (n=10), and from 145.82 ± 3.31 to 142.54 ± 3.69 among hypertensive ginger recipients (n=11). The authors describe similar reductions in the two hypertensive arms. Their conclusion attributes a reduction to ginger, although the paper does not establish a treatment-specific systolic change. The investigators state that they could not mask the taste. The normotensive ginger arm changed from 117.40 ± 1.91 to 117.20 ± 1.59. The study gives no standing challenge, orthostatic lightheadedness measure, fresh-grated ginger intervention or Parkinson subgroup. The flow diagram and table reverse the two normotensive group denominators. Table 2 gives control n=6 and ginger n=5. The hypertensive group sizes agree.
