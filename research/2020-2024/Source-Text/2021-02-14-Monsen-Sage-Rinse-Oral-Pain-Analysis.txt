Sage tea mouth rinse and unreported pain comparisons in palliative care
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8295113/
PMID: 33586003
PMCID: PMC8295113
DOI: 10.1007/s00520-021-06021-2
Registration: NCT02067572
Coverage: Full original report, oral discomfort and primary outcomes

Monsen and colleagues randomized 88 palliative cancer patients to a sage tea-based mouth rinse or normal saline and 73 completed. Fewer than 20 percent reported mouth or swallowing pain at baseline, and fewer than 8 percent rated it at least 4 on the numerical scale. The investigators excluded those symptoms from subsequent analyses. The reported dry-mouth effects therefore provide no between-group pain result. The preparation was a rinse and the cohort had advanced cancer.

Table 3 reports separate outcomes. The EORTC mouth-soreness interaction was inconclusive (group by day p = .462), as was the NRS difficulty-swallowing interaction (p = .756). The trial separately reported interactions for EORTC dry mouth (p = .036) and NRS dry mouth (p = .045). Table 3 did not supply a comparison for the excluded numerical pain items. The registry lists the companion clinical and project reports separately.
