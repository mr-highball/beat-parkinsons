Study design and population
- Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group clinical trial conducted in overweight and obese adults (aged 35-70 years, BMI 25 to <40 kg/m^2) with angiographically confirmed coronary slow flow phenomenon (CSFP, CTFC >27, stenosis <40%, LVEF >=45%).
- Enrolled: 50 participants (25 nano-curcumin, 25 placebo) using stratified permuted block randomization (blocks of 2 or 4, stratified by sex and cardiovascular risk score).
- Analyzed completers: 42 participants (21 nano-curcumin, 21 placebo; 17 men and 4 women per arm; mean age 54 +- 9 vs 55 +- 8 years; 4 dropouts per arm due to COVID-19 or other medical conditions).
- Exclusion criteria included active GI bleeding/ulcers, NSAIDs, corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, anticonvulsants, curcumin supplements, or unusual turmeric consumption.

Intervention and comparator (12 weeks)
- Active arm: 80 mg/day nano-curcumin soft-gel capsules (Exir Nano Sina Co., Iran) containing curcuminoid powder (>=95% purity: 79.4% curcumin, 17.6% demethoxycurcumin, 3% bisdemethoxycurcumin) formulated with polysorbate, vitamin E, vitamin C, and natural oil.
- Placebo arm: Appearance-matched capsules (Exir Nano Sina Co., Iran) containing polysorbate 80, soy oil, sorbitol 70, methylparaben, propylparaben, and purified water.
- No gastrointestinal or other adverse events were reported during the 12-week trial.

Verified primary and secondary outcomes (Table 3 SF-36 and Table 4 BDI-II)
- Inflammatory primary endpoints: Circulating hs-CRP and visfatin showed no significant between-group improvement.
- Glycemic indices: Fasting blood glucose change -0.9 +- 12.2 vs +7.7 +- 12.4 mg/dL (p = 0.02); HbA1c change -0.1 +- 0.8% vs +0.5 +- 0.8% (p = 0.04) favoring nano-curcumin against deterioration in placebo.
- SF-36 Bodily Pain (0-100, higher = less pain): Nano-curcumin 66.3 +- 29.1 -> 80.2 +- 21.7 (change +13.9 +- 15.3, within-group p = 0.001) vs placebo 75.8 +- 18.4 -> 73.5 +- 20.0 (change -2.4 +- 9.3, within-group p = 0.20); between-group change difference -16.3 (95% CI -24.2 to -8.4, p < 0.001 t-test; post-intervention ANCOVA p < 0.001).
- SF-36 Physical Component Summary (PCS): Nano-curcumin change +8.2 +- 8.1 vs placebo -1.2 +- 6.5 (between-group p < 0.001; ANCOVA p < 0.001).
- SF-36 Mental Component Summary (MCS): Nano-curcumin change +6.8 +- 11.8 vs placebo -1.1 +- 10.4 (between-group p = 0.02; ANCOVA p = 0.01).
- SF-36 Vitality (energy/fatigue domain): Nano-curcumin change +2.6 +- 9.0 vs placebo -0.7 +- 6.6 (between-group change p = 0.18; ANCOVA p = 0.11; inconclusive).
- SF-36 Physical Functioning (change p < 0.001, ANCOVA p = 0.003), Role Physical (change p = 0.04, ANCOVA p = 0.06), Social Functioning (change p = 0.01, ANCOVA p = 0.002), Mental Health (change p = 0.12, ANCOVA p = 0.04).
- BDI-II Depression severity categories (Table 4): Minimal depression shifted from 12 (57.1%) to 16 (76.2%) in nano-curcumin vs 13 (61.9%) to 8 (38.1%) in placebo (baseline Mann-Whitney p = 0.66; post-intervention Mann-Whitney p = 0.046).

Relevance and limits for Parkinson's food experiments
- Independent double-blind human RCT showing improvement in SF-36 bodily pain (p < 0.001), PCS/MCS, and BDI-II depression category distribution (p = 0.046), alongside inconclusive SF-36 vitality (p = 0.18).
- Limits: Non-Parkinson cardiovascular cohort (CSFP); formulated nano-curcumin capsules with vitamin E/C and polysorbate rather than culinary ground or grated turmeric; 16% completer attrition without ITT imputation; originally powered for hs-CRP/visfatin rather than pain or depression.
