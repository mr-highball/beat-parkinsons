Title: Randomised Controlled Trial of Fish Oil Supplementation on Responsiveness to Resistance Exercise Training in Sarcopenic Older Women
Publication date: 2022-07-11
PMID: 35889801
PMCID: PMC9317261
DOI: 10.3390/nu14142844
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9317261/
Text coverage: Distilled primary results, including Table 3 and muscle outcomes.

Alves and colleagues randomized 34 sarcopenic older women. Two withdrew from the sunflower capsule group. Participants performed matched resistance training while assigned sunflower or fish oil. The published report shows a larger increase in peak torque on fish oil (32.5% versus 8.98%, main-text group comparison p=.003; abstract reports p<.001 for its 19.46 versus 5.74 Nm contrast) and greater change in six-minute walk performance (p=.009). MRI muscle cross-sectional area increased across both groups with an inconclusive group difference (p=.23). The protocol is closely matched to P169 in the 2020 ICFSR supplement. Participant-level overlap was not independently reconciled. These measures do not isolate the oil versus no oil or estimate a Parkinson executive-function or rigidity effect.
