Title: Evaluating Walnut-Fig Syrup as an Innovative Herbal Medicine for Functional Constipation in the Elderly
Source: https://www.cjmb.org/uploads/pdf/pdf_CJMB_575.pdf
DOI: 10.34172/cjmb.2022.32
Trial registration: IRCT20200714048108N1
Publication date: 2022-10-07, first online date printed in the article.
Citation: Joudikhajeh et al. Crescent Journal of Medical and Biological Sciences 9(4), 195–201.
Text coverage: Paraphrased original clinical methods, intervention boundaries, and aggregate results from the journal PDF.

The report randomized 90 adults aged 60–83 with functional constipation to a walnut–fig syrup or lactulose syrup, 45 per group. Parkinson's disease and serious psychiatric disorders were exclusionary. The report describes four weeks of treatment and checks at baseline, week two and week four. The trial compares a combination product with an active comparator. It has no fig-only or walnut-only arm and does not specify the fruit's dried status for ordinary culinary use.

Treatment response was 43 of 45 (95.6%) in the mixture group and 41 of 45 (91.1%) with lactulose. The reported between-group result was P=.677. Median duration of actual treatment was 3 days versus 4 days (P=.222). The paper separately reports P=.242 for the duration of response comparison. The result does not establish a superiority, equivalence or noninferiority conclusion. The report observed no side effects during this study.

The reported 2020–2021 recruitment and 2022 publication concern the study identified by IRCT20200714048108N1. The original registration describes a two-week intervention and follow-up one month after treatment. Preserve that difference when comparing protocol with the published analysis. This is one result family.
