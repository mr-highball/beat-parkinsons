Date basis: original article electronic publication date
Source depth: factual extract of original methods, diagnostic counts and results

Ko and colleagues retrospectively analyzed videofluoroscopy in 76 people with dysphagia and laryngeal penetration. Table 1 records three patients with Parkinson disease. Drooling was one of several symptoms that could lead to referral. The measured outcome was laryngeal penetration while swallowing standardized consistencies, including liquid, porridge and yogurt used as test boluses.

Across all diagnoses, 26 of 76 met the study's criterion for an effective reduction in the penetration ratio with the posture change. The paper gives no Parkinson-specific estimate for that result. It supplies no drooling severity outcome and no comparison involving raw fig.
