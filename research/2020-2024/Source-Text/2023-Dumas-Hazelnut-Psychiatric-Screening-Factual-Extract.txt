Title: Dumas and colleagues, hazelnut oil feeding and baseline psychiatric screening
Original source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10318304/
DOI: 10.1016/j.nbas.2023.100072
Public date: March 10, 2023
Coverage: Original factual extract from the verified NLM full text, Methods and Table 1.

Of 31 people screened, 12 were randomized and ten completed, five women and five men aged 66–75. One participant stopped because of limitations of the provided diet and one withdrew for an unspecified medical reason. The latter has no published symptom, period or oil attribution.

The high-oleic intervention oil was entirely hazelnut oil. The control oil blend included 43.8% hazelnut oil. The other experimental oil blend was made from palm, peanut and virgin olive oil. The full text does not identify the hazelnut oil pressing method.

Beck Depression and Anxiety Inventory measurements were collected at screening. Among the ten completers the means were 1.9 ± 1.8 and 0.9 ± 1.9 respectively. The repeated cognitive study assessed N-back performance and related imaging. No post-diet Beck comparison, hallucination count, or systematic adverse-event table was published. The trial differs from the later registry NCT06192407.
