Repeated oral anise seed extract and observed drowsiness in mice
Source: https://asjp.cerist.dz/en/downArticle/255/11/2/173040
Coverage: factual extract from primary Methods and Tables 2 and 4

Latifa Fenaghra and Mustapha Khali. Revue Agrobiologia 11(2):2682–2691, December 29, 2021. The experiment used botanically identified Pimpinella anisum from Oued Chaaba, Algeria. Powdered seeds were macerated in water, filtered and concentrated. The extract is a fraction of ground seed.
Single-dose arms were evaluated separately. In the repeated-dose experiment, male mice received oral aqueous extract at 250, 500 or 1,000 mg/kg/day for 28 days (five per treated group); the control received vehicle. Twice-daily observations were made for abnormal clinical signs. Table 4 records drowsiness and decreased motor activity only for the 1,000 mg/kg group and expresses the observations as group-level presence or absence. It gives no affected animal count or magnitude, time of day, validated somnolence instrument or sleep-stage measurement. Repeated high-dose exposure produced pathologic liver observations as a coexisting toxicology signal. Ground raw seed was not administered directly, and the subjects were not human or Parkinson patients.
