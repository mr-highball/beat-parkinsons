Source: Zhang T, et al. Associations between different coffee types, neurodegenerative diseases, and related mortality: findings from a large prospective cohort study.
Journal: The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition (published 2024)
Identifiers: PMID:39168304 | DOI:10.1016/j.ajcnut.2024.08.012
Source URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39168304/
License note: Factual summary recorded from official NCBI PubMed metadata and abstract.

Study design and findings
- Prospective UK Biobank cohort (N = 204,847; 44.7% male; median follow-up 9 years; 1,093 incident Parkinson's disease cases, 1,696 ADRD cases, 419 neurodegenerative deaths).
- Multivariate Cox models compared 5 intake tiers across unsweetened caffeinated, sugar/artificially sweetened, and decaffeinated coffee.
- Highest intake of unsweetened caffeinated coffee (>= 3 cups/day vs non-consumers) was associated with lower incident Parkinson's disease (HR 0.71, 95% CI 0.56-0.91) and ADRD (HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.62-0.91), whereas neither decaffeinated nor sweetened coffee showed statistically significant associations (p > 0.05).

Relevance and limits for Parkinson's food experiments
- Large prospective cohort context distinguishing unsweetened caffeinated coffee from decaffeinated and sweetened coffee for Parkinson's incidence.
- Limits: Observational disease-incidence cohort rather than symptom-severity intervention in diagnosed Parkinson's disease.
