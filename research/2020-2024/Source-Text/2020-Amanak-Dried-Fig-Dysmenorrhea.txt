Coverage: Selected factual results and methods from the primary report, its Figure 1, Table 3, and the linked erratum.

The study randomized 107 midwifery students to placebo, cinnamon or dried fig (36, 36, 35). Eight were excluded after allocation. The analyses included 99 (32, 34, 33). Some running text in the original publication used incorrect denominators and is superseded by the 2021 correction. The intervention was six whole dried figs daily (reported total 150 g), two at each of three servings with warm water, for the first three days of three menstrual cycles. Comparators were cinnamon powder or empty capsules.
The original Table 3 summarizes self-reported perceived stress. Third intervention cycle scores presented for placebo, cinnamon and fig were 3.12, 2.59 and 2.07, with a reported post hoc p = .004 for placebo versus fig. The perceived stress result supplies neither a visual hallucination frequency measure nor a diagnosed Parkinson comparison.
