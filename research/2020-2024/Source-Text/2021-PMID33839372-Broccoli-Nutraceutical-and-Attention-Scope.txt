Title: Randomized controlled trial of an adjunctive sulforaphane nutraceutical in schizophrenia
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33839372/
Original article: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.schres.2021.03.018
Public article PDF: https://ae99858c-5e18-49e7-9038-4d0086fb2a8c.filesusr.com/ugd/7bf8c3_3b24712990f642fc9ae7cedc67660d3a.pdf
PMID: 33839372
DOI: 10.1016/j.schres.2021.03.018
Trial registration: NCT02810964
Registry: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02810964
Publication date: 2021-04
Source depth: Original article and posted registry results.
Reviewed: 2026-11-27

The randomized adjunct trial enrolled 64 adults with schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder, of whom 29 per arm completed follow-up. It compared a broccoli-derived sulforaphane precursor nutraceutical with placebo. The authors report no statistically significant between-group difference in the overall MATRICS cognitive battery or individual domains. Attention/vigilance appears in the baseline table. A separate follow-up estimate for that domain was not recovered from the report.

The registry reports the cognitive composite, with active arm mean T scores 25.0 at baseline and 26.8 at week 18, and placebo means 19.5 and 22.8, respectively. These raw means reflect a baseline imbalance and do not provide an adjusted attention comparison. This cohort is distinct from the Hei schizophrenia trial. The assigned nutraceutical, the population and the reported endpoint do not give an effect estimate for eating raw mature broccoli on attention in Parkinson disease.
