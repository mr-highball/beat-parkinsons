Title: Effects of Daily Low-Dose Date Consumption on Glycemic Control, Lipid Profile, and Quality of Life in Adults with Pre- and Type 2 Diabetes: A Randomized Controlled Trial.
PMID: 31952131
PMCID: PMC7019638
DOI: 10.3390/nu12010217
First published: 2020
Authors: Alalwan TA, Perna S, Mandeel QA, Abdulhadi A, Alsayyad AS, D'Antona G et al.
Journal: Nutrients
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7019638/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Randomized, parallel-group, open-label controlled trial (Alalwan et al., 2020, Nutrients, PMC7019638, n=100 Bahraini adults with type 2 diabetes randomized; n=96 analyzed: 46 dates, 50 control) comparing 3 whole dried Tamr-stage Khudary dates/day for 16 weeks versus no dates. Glycemic HbA1c did not worsen, while SF-36 Mental Health subscale improved significantly between groups (mean difference +5.64 points, 95% CI 0.57 to 10.70) alongside Total SF-36 (+30.66, 95% CI 12.45 to 48.23) and several QoL domains. Limits: open-label unblinded design in a population where dates carry strong cultural/religious positive meaning (authors explicitly caution expectancy bias), SF-36 Mental Health pools general emotional/depressive/anxious wellbeing rather than a dedicated clinical depression scale, non-Parkinson T2DM cohort.
