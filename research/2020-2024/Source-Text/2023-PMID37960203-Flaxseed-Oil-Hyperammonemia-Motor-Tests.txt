Title: Flaxseed Oil (Linum usitatissimum) Prevents Cognitive and Motor Damage in Rats with Hyperammonemia
PMID: 37960203
PMCID: PMC10647672
DOI: 10.3390/nu15214550
Publication date: 2023-10-27
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10647672/
Author article: https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/3c30/e1ba68468530c4f8c3eb20aa86883069e806.pdf
Text coverage: Primary Methods 2.2–2.5, Results 3.1, Table 1 and Figure 1.

Ocaña-Sánchez and colleagues used a capsule-derived flaxseed oil with 43% alpha-linolenic acid measured by mass spectrometry. They assigned 28 hyperammonemic rats to four groups of seven, including two administered the oil and one mineral-oil group. Seven additional rats served as a control group without the hyperammonemic diet. The methods give the brand and do not verify the pressing method.

Time to fall from the rotating rod was shorter in the hyperammonemia control group. The authors report that both oral oil groups avoided that reduction. The omnibus F(4,30) statistic was 7.144 (p=.0003). Across groups, open-field square crossings were inconclusive (F(4,30)=2.673, p=.051). Table 1 records changes in rearing and grooming separately from the rod result. The model concerns hepatic encephalopathy, and the assays measure coordination or general behavior. Neither reports a separate resting-tremor phenotype.
