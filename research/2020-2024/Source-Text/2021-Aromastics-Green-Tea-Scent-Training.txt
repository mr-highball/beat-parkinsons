Title: Olfactory training with Aromastics: olfactory and cognitive effects
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8051546/
PMID: 33864109
PMCID: PMC8051546
DOI: 10.1007/s00405-021-06810-9
First published: 2021-04-16
Text coverage: factual extraction from Methods, Results and Table 3.

The comparison assigned 55 adults to standard-frequency or higher-frequency odor training, with 32 and 23 participants respectively. Twenty-six participants had idiopathic, postinfectious, or posttraumatic smell loss. Both groups used combined odors. Green-tea scent entered the second set with menthol, thyme, tangerine and bergamot. Participants smelled the odors from a dispenser rather than drinking tea.

The original report favored standard over higher-frequency training for odor sensitivity. Its Table 3 reports a gain of at least 5.5 points on the combined Sniffin' Sticks threshold, discrimination and identification scale in 3 of 12 postinfectious participants with follow-up, 1 of 9 with idiopathic smell loss, and 0 of 3 after trauma. The difference by cause was inconclusive (p=.36). The study compares frequencies of the same multiscent package and gives no tea-only estimate or Parkinson comparison.
