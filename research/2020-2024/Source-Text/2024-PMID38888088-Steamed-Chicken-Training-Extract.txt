Title: Effect of resistance training and chicken meat on muscle strength and mass and the gut microbiome of older women

First published: 2024-06-18, Physiological Reports 12(12):e16100.
Archive scope: Original Methods 2.3–2.5, participant flow and change estimates in Results 3.2 and Table 3.
Reviewed: 2026-11-19

Uchida and colleagues randomized 93 older women across four training and diet groups, with 81 in the outcome analysis. Participants in each chicken group ate a 110 g breast serving with an average 22.5 g protein three times per week for 12 weeks. The abstract describes the meat as boiled. Methods 2.4 and the companion paper call it steamed. The comparator was a protein-free carbohydrate food with similar energy. The non-training and training doses differed in timing relative to exercise.

Table 3 reports greater whole-body lean mass change with chicken than comparator within both training strata. For maximal voluntary contraction, training with carbohydrate and training with chicken had mean changes of +77.2 ± 45.8 and +75.6 ± 54.9 Nm. The corresponding non-training changes were +28.4 ± 32.6 and +25.0 ± 39.2 Nm. The reported significant strength comparisons are each training arm against the sedentary arms. The table does not show added maximal voluntary contraction from adding chicken to training. This is narrower than the wording of the abstract. The report did not identify a significant diversity or community-composition change in the gut microbiome.

The trial reports body composition and strength in older women without an assessed Parkinson fatigue endpoint. UMIN000038253 links this paper and Fujie's secondary analysis as one trial family.
