Title: Almond Consumption Modestly Improves Pain Ratings, Muscle Force Production, and Biochemical Markers of Muscle Damage Following Downhill Running in Mildly Overweight, Middle-Aged Adults: A Randomized, Crossover Trial.
PMID: 39257478
PMCID: PMC11381864
DOI: 10.1016/j.cdnut.2024.104432
Registry ID: NCT04787718
First published: 2024
Authors: Rayo, Cervantes, Hong, Hooshmand, Jason
Journal: Current developments in nutrition
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39257478/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

8-week randomized crossover trial (NCT04787718; San Diego State University) in 26 healthy mildly overweight middle-aged adults (37 +/- 6 y) consuming 2 oz/d (57 g/d) raw, shelled, unsalted almonds or isocaloric pretzels before a 30-min -10% downhill treadmill run and 72-h recovery. Pain during maximal isokinetic contraction (VAS 0-100 mm, Table 4) was lower in the almond trial (Trial effect P = 0.026, eta^2 = 0.225), with post-hoc reductions at 24 h (25.1 +/- 22.5 vs 34.3 +/- 25.1 mm, -37%, P = 0.012) and 48 h (23.0 +/- 19.7 vs 30.6 +/- 23.0 mm, -33%, P = 0.038). Maximal knee flexion torque at 120 deg/s was greater in almond vs control (P = 0.004; +12% at 24 h, +9% at 72 h). No significant trial effects for resting individual muscle VAS (Table 3), vertical-jump pain, vertical jump force, CRP, or myoglobin. Limits: acute exercise pain, raw rather than roasted almonds, non-PD cohort.
