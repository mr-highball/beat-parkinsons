Title: The effects of aromatherapy with thyme oil on disease symptoms, vital findings, and hemodynamic parameters in COVID-19 patients.
PMID: 38129231
DOI: 10.1016/j.explore.2023.12.004
First published: 2024
Authors: Öner U, Cengiz Z
Journal: Explore (New York, N.Y.)
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38129231/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 7 review).

Primary Abstract:
To determine the effect of aromatherapy with thyme oil on disease symptoms, vital signs, and hemodynamic parameters in COVID-19 patients. We conducted the randomized controlled trial with 140 (experimental group=70, control group=70) COVID-19 patients. Patients admitted to the COVID-19 service of the Batman Training and Research Hospital were included in the sample between 31.01 - 31.08 2022. Patients in the experimental group inhaled thyme oil 3 times a day during 5 days. At the end of day 5, symptoms and hemodynamic parameters were measured as posttest. Vital signs were measured 3 times a day during 5 days. The control group only received routine treatment. Thyme oil was found to be effective in relieving symptoms of shortness of breath, dizziness, secretion, diarrhea, weakness, loss of appetite, cough, headache and muscle joint pain. Although there was improvement in the symptoms of nausea-vomiting, runny nose and loss of taste-smell, the effect was not statistically significant. Thyme oil significantly decreased body temperature, pulse rate and respiratory rate (p<0.05), increased SPO 2 (p<0.05), and did not affect systolic and diastolic blood pressure (p>0.05). It had a significant effect on the regulation of pH, decreased CO2 and increased O2 significantly (p<0.05). Thyme oil aromatherapy was effective in reducing symptoms, regulating vital signs and hemodynamic parameters. Accordingly, thyme oil is recommended as non-pharmacological treatment method in COVID-19 patients.

Verified Round 7 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Unblinded randomized trial (2024; n=70 patients with acute COVID-19) evaluating inhaled Thymus vulgaris essential oil aromatherapy vs routine care, reporting reduced self-rated dyspnea, fatigue, headache, and myalgia. STRICT ROUTE/PREPARATION & BIAS EXCLUSION: inhaled volatile essential oil aromatherapy (not ingested dried/fresh culinary thyme), unblinded subjective outcomes in acute viral illness (exposure_fit remote, reliability_fit remote, attribution_fit partial); cannot support oral dried thyme on PD fatigue or pain.
