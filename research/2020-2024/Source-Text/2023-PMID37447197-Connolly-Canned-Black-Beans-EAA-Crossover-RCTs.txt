Title: Effects of Consuming Ounce-Equivalent Portions of Animal- vs. Plant-Based Protein Foods, as Defined by the Dietary Guidelines for Americans on Essential Amino Acids Bioavailability in Young and Older Adults: Two Cross-Over Randomized Controlled Trials.
PMID: 37447197
PMCID: PMC10343739
DOI: 10.3390/nu15132870
Registry ID: NCT03649568
First published: 2023
Authors: Connolly G, Hudson JL, Bergia RE, Davis EM, Hartman AS, Zhu W, et al.
Journal: Nutrients
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37447197/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Two investigator-blinded randomized crossover trials (Connolly et al., 2023, Nutrients, PMC10343739, NCT03649568 and NCT04243395) in 55 adults (30 young, mean 26.0 y; 25 older, mean 64.2 y) comparing standardized meals with 2 oz-eq of drained, rinsed canned black beans (7.5 g protein, 3.02 g EAA), unprocessed lean pork (7.36 g EAA), whole eggs (5.38 g EAA), or sliced almonds (1.85 g EAA). Canned black beans produced markedly lower 300-min postprandial plasma EAA bioavailability (iAUCpos) than pork or eggs in young, older, and combined analyses (p<0.0001), indistinguishable from almonds. Relevant indirect pharmacokinetic context for levodopa-protein competition (showing relatively low large-neutral/essential amino acid load per serving of drained canned black beans), but no Parkinson's patients, levodopa dosing, or daily OFF-time endpoints were tested.
