Ninety-eight of the original 140 participants with Parkinson disease had the necessary longitudinal and imaging records. The study compares baseline MRI white matter hyperintensity volume with change in cognition and movement across two years. Examinations took place in the medication ON phase. Freezing was measured using the 0–24 Freezing of Gait Questionnaire summary score.

The total baseline white matter hyperintensity model in accepted-manuscript Table 3 gives a coefficient for freezing questionnaire change of -0.33, 95% CI -2.43 to 1.75, adjusted p = .75. Neither the tested regional burden models nor the overall motor results give a clear predictive association. Cognitive associations remain separate. The paper contrasts its freezing result with Chung and colleagues' retrospective study, which used visual ratings and incident freezing. The original methods and tables have no fresh lemon balm exposure and measure questionnaire severity rather than a direct count of episodes following ingestion.
