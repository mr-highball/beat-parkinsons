Title: Habitual tea consumption was associated with lower levels of depressive symptoms among older Chinese: Guangzhou Biobank Cohort Study.
PMID: 35477125
DOI: 10.1016/j.nutres.2022.03.010
First published: 2022
Authors: Chen YX, Jiang CQ, Zhang WS, Zhu F, Jin YL, Cheng KK, et al.
Journal: Nutrition research (New York, N.Y.)
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35477125/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Cross-sectional analysis of n=10,014 older Chinese adults in the Guangzhou Biobank Cohort Study assessing depressive symptoms via the 15-item Geriatric Depression Scale (GDS >=8 in 421/10,014 [4.20%]). Adjusted for confounders, occasional and regular tea consumers had lower GDS scores (beta -0.29 [95% CI -0.46 to -0.11] and -0.60 [-0.78 to -0.42]) and regular consumers had lower odds of depressive symptoms (OR 0.51 [95% CI 0.35-0.72]), with dose-response trends (p<0.001) and consistent findings across black, green, and oolong tea. Limits: cross-sectional observational design in non-PD older adults; residual confounding and social-interaction effects.
