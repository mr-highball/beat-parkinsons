Title: Odor familiarity and improvement of olfactory identification test in Chinese population
Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1278668/full
PMID: 37908819
PMCID: PMC10613687
DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1278668
First published: 2023-10-16
Text coverage: factual extraction from Methods, Figures 1–2 and Tables 1–2.

One hundred healthy volunteers rated familiarity with 121 odor labels. Green tea scored 66 on that survey. A separate 100 normosmic volunteers completed test versions whose odor targets or answer choices were modified. The final test did not include green tea. Cinnamon was an odor target; correct responses increased from 59% to 88% after changing answer choices. Mean scores across standard Sniffin' Sticks identification, modified distractors and the final Chinese adaptation were 11.95, 12.85 and 14.17, respectively. The test-version comparison was p<.0001.

The study changed the measurement instrument. The food labels identify candidate stimuli and answer choices. Participants did not receive a food intervention and no fatigue measure was reported. The trial supplies context for interpreting odor recognition across test versions rather than evidence of a food effect on clinical smell impairment.
