All 17 participants completed the study (9 on the curcumin formulation and 8 on cellulose placebo). Means are listed with standard deviations.

Change in 400-m walking speed, m/s: curcumin +0.014 (0.090), placebo +0.103 (0.100).
Change in handgrip, kg: curcumin +0.63 (4.90), placebo −0.25 (3.14).
Change in MMSE, points: curcumin +0.33 (3.94), placebo −2.75 (5.09).
Pain on a 0–10 scale: curcumin 0.97 (1.30) at baseline and 0.20 (0.40) at 90 days, placebo 1.30 (1.70) and 1.60 (1.70), respectively.
Interleukin-6, ng/mL: curcumin 3.1 (1.4) to 3.4 (1.5), placebo 2.7 (1.7) to 3.3 (1.5).

The registry does not give a between-arm analysis for these outcomes. The walking change contrast is −0.089 m/s. An exploratory Welch interval derived from the reported change means, standard deviations and group sizes is approximately −0.188 to +0.010 m/s (95%, unadjusted). Both arms gained speed. Walking speed and grip therefore remain separate results. The difference in pain changes is −1.07/10 from rounded group means. Individual change variances and baseline-to-follow-up correlations are unavailable, so no corresponding interval is inferred.

The evidence concerns a formulated curcumin product in older volunteers. No Parkinson bradykinesia comparison or ground-turmeric ingestion was measured. The pain outcome gives a descriptive comparative signal in this cohort.
