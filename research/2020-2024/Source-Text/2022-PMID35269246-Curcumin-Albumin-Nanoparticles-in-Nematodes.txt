Original source scope
Nematode experiment on purified curcumin carried in albumin nanoparticles used body bends and a dopamine-linked basal slowing assay. The food-dependent slowing in cat-2 mutants increased with the nanoparticle preparation.

Evidence boundary or continuing gap
Nematode basal slowing reflects response to a food environment and must not be equated with clinical bradykinesia. There was no ground turmeric preparation or human Parkinson outcome.
