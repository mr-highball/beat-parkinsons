Title: Exploring the Potential of Wild Olive (Acebuche) Oil as a Pharm-Food to Prevent Ocular Hypertension and Fibrotic Events in the Retina of Hypertensive Mice

The study assigned 90 male mice to six groups, 15 per group. Mice with and without experimentally induced hypertension received a standard diet, a wild acebuche olive oil diet, or a cultivated extra-virgin olive oil diet for six weeks. Analysis groups for individual figures used subsets of these animals.

At six weeks the untreated hypertensive group had an intraocular pressure near 17.5 mmHg compared with 13.5 mmHg in the normotensive control group. The corresponding hypertensive wild-oil and cultivated-oil groups were near 13.5 and 15.5 mmHg. Figure 1 used at least seven animals per group and also reported retinal imaging changes. The cultivated-oil group still had an approximately 35% decrease in fluorescein signal relative to the normotensive control, compared with approximately 60% in the untreated hypertensive group. Several retinal collagen endpoints favored wild oil. The authors reported no intraocular pressure change with either oil in normotensive animals.

The 2022 congress abstract O5-03 presents the same research team's two oils, hypertensive mouse model and retinal outcomes.
