Original source scope and evidence boundary
The investigators assigned 60 women with nonalcoholic fatty liver disease equally to sesame or sunflower oil for 12 weeks. Both arms followed the same prescribed calorie restriction. Fifty-three completed the intervention. The abstract conclusion favors sesame oil for fatigue, anxiety and depression. It reports p=.002, p=.011 and p=.013 respectively in a sentence describing changes following sesame oil, without identifying the test for each value. Stress did not show a clear change. Arm means, adjusted between-arm contrasts, scale details and uncertainty remain unverified. The 2022 companion reports (PMID 35685535 and 35804451) identify both oils as refined and odorless. The 2025 biomarker report (PMID 40618149) confirms the same grant, participant flow and trial framework. The public abstract for the mood paper lacks its own registration field. Matching authors, funding award, randomized and completed counts, arms and duration support assigning the report to the IRCT20140208016529N6 cohort family. Its original Methods would confirm the allocation of these mood and fatigue outcomes. The result is an indirect active-oil comparison for claims 0039, 0262 and 0404. It cannot establish a cold-pressed oil effect or an oil-versus-no-oil contrast in Parkinson disease.

Original abstract and contrast limits
Source: https://doi.org/10.1108/NFS-12-2022-0407
DOI 10.1108/NFS-12-2022-0407
Checked: 2026-11-15
Coverage: Factual extract.

60 women with NAFLD randomized to sesame or sunflower oil alongside a reduced-calorie diet for 12 weeks. 53 completed. The abstract concludes that anxiety and depressive symptoms decreased with sesame versus sunflower. The results paragraph includes p=.011 for anxiety and p=.013 for depressive symptoms without establishing whether these were between- or within-group tests. Arm data and statistical adjustment are unavailable. Matching companion trial reports refined, odorless oils.
