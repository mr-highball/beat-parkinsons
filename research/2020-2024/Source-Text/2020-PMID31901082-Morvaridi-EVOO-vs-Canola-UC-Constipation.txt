Title: The effects of extra virgin olive oil and canola oil on inflammatory markers and gastrointestinal symptoms in patients with ulcerative colitis
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31901082/
PMID: 31901082
DOI: 10.1038/s41430-019-0549-z
Publication date: 2020-01-03
Date basis: First online publication date in Europe PMC / PubMed (European Journal of Clinical Nutrition 74:891–899).
Coverage: Verified official author abstract only; publisher full text remains access-limited.

Morvaridi and colleagues enrolled 40 adults with ulcerative colitis in a crossover clinical trial comparing extra-virgin olive oil with canola oil across two 20-day intervention periods; 32 participants completed both rounds. Outcomes included blood inflammatory markers (ESR, hs-CRP), Mayo score and the Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale (GSRS).

The author abstract reports statistically significant reductions after extra-virgin olive oil consumption for ESR (p = 0.03), hs-CRP (p < 0.001) and GSRS items including bloating, constipation, fecal urgency, incomplete defecation and total GSRS score (each p < 0.05). Because the full text and numeric outcome tables remain behind the publisher paywall, exact baseline/post constipation scores, between-oil crossover contrasts against canola oil, washout timing and adverse events remain unverified. Population is ulcerative colitis rather than Parkinson disease.
