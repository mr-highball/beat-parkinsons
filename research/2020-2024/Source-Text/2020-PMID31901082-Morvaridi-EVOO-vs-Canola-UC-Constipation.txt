Morvaridi and colleagues enrolled 40 adults with ulcerative colitis in a crossover clinical trial comparing extra-virgin olive oil with canola oil across two 20-day intervention periods; 32 participants completed both rounds. Outcomes included blood inflammatory markers (ESR, hs-CRP), Mayo score and the Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale (GSRS).

The author abstract reports statistically significant reductions after extra-virgin olive oil consumption for ESR (p = 0.03), hs-CRP (p < 0.001) and GSRS items including bloating, constipation, fecal urgency, incomplete defecation and total GSRS score (each p < 0.05). The publisher's Figure 2 caption compares the EVOO and canola periods within participants and reports less constipation after EVOO (paired t-test p<.001). Bloating (p=.04), fecal urgency (p<.001) and incomplete defecation (p=.04) also favored EVOO. The Figure 3 caption reports a lower total symptom score after EVOO (paired p<.001).

Figure captions establish a signed active-comparator contrast in ulcerative colitis. Exact effects and intervals, period and sequence adjustment, washout sufficiency, and safety data remain unverified from the accessible original text. The planned COLONIC comparison is a separate study. The population here differs from people with Parkinson disease. Evidence after culinary olive oil is relevant to bowel symptoms while the Parkinson-specific direction retains a weak transfer.
