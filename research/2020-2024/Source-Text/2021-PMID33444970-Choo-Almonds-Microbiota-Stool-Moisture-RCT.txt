Title: Almond consumption affects fecal microbiota composition, stool pH, and stool moisture in overweight and obese adults with elevated fasting blood glucose: A randomized controlled trial.
PMID: 33444970
DOI: 10.1016/j.nutres.2020.11.005
First published: 2021
Authors: Choo, Tran, Luscombe-Marsh, Stonehouse, Bowen
Journal: Nutrition research (New York, N.Y.)
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33444970/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

8-week parallel RCT in 69 overweight/obese adults with elevated fasting glucose (age 60.8 +/- 7.4 y) randomized to 56 g/d almonds vs isocaloric high-carbohydrate biscuit snack. Almond intake altered fecal microbiota composition (P = 0.011), increased alpha-diversity (P < 0.01), and enriched Ruminococcaceae without changing fecal SCFAs or gut permeability. Fecal pH (P = 0.0006) and stool moisture content (P = 0.027) decreased significantly in the almond arm vs biscuit control. Limits: biochemical stool moisture proxy rather than clinical constipation severity; thermal preparation unstated in abstract.
