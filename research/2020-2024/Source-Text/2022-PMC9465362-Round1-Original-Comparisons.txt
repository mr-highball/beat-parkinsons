Round 1 full publication review. The archive retains the focal exposure and outcome boundaries for each queued claim.

The review separates biogenic amines in processed foods from studies of biologically active compounds in animals. Nutmeg, clove and rosemary appear in food preservation or compositional studies. Kefir appears as a fermented dairy product with reported amine content; mango appears via isolated mangiferin in rodents.

Claim 0042 | grated nutmeg | depressive symptom severity | related
The nutmeg experiment concerns extract added to dry fermented sausage and changes in amine concentrations during storage. It does not estimate depressive symptoms after grated nutmeg.
Claim 0128 | fresh rosemary | depressive symptom severity | related
Rosemary oil and ethanol extract appear in refrigerated-food preservation comparisons. The outcomes are amine concentrations, without a fresh rosemary depression test.
Claim 0232 | dried mango | depressive symptom severity | related
The review's mango-related entry concerns isolated mangiferin and neurochemical outcomes in aged mice. Dried mango and measured depression were not assessed.
Claim 0526 | plain kefir | fall frequency | related
Kefir is listed by measured biogenic amine content. The source gives no plain kefir and falls comparison.
Claim 0771 | ground clove | fall frequency | related
Clove's eugenol is discussed for antimicrobial action and amine control during food preservation. There is no culinary clove fall-frequency assessment.
