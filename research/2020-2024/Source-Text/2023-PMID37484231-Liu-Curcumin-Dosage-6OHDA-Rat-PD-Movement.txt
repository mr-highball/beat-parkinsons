Study design and exposure
- Wistar rats (n = 6/group for behavioral analyses) divided into 6 groups: control, 6-OHDA stereotactic lesion model, levodopa comparator, and intragastric oral curcumin at 40, 80, or 160 mg/kg/day for 2 weeks (plus a 1-week vs 2-week duration comparison at 160 mg/kg/day).
- Behavioral outcomes: apomorphine-induced rotation test, fixed-speed and accelerating rotarod latency, and open-field total distance and average movement speed, alongside SNc tyrosine hydroxylase immunofluorescence.

Key findings
- 6-OHDA lesioned controls developed severe apomorphine-induced rotations, shortened constant and accelerating rotarod latencies, and decreased open-field total distance and average speed.
- Intragastric curcumin at 160 mg/kg/day for 2 weeks significantly alleviated behavioral movement deficits (including open-field speed/distance and rotarod performance) and outperformed 1-week dosing, whereas 40 and 80 mg/kg/day showed limited efficacy.

Relevance and limits for Parkinson's food experiments
- Direct oral curcumin dose-response evidence for movement speed/bradykinesia proxies in a classic 6-OHDA rat Parkinson model (supporting 0507 context/signal).
- Limits: Purified >98% curcumin administered by high-dose intragastric gavage (160 mg/kg/day) rather than culinary ground turmeric; small animal groups (n = 6/group); no human Parkinson clinical bradykinesia measurement.
