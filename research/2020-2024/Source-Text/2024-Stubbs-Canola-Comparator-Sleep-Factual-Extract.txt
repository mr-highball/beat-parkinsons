Title: Daytime dysfunction in the canola comparator preprint
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11451762/
Format: Factual extract from the original record.

Stubbs and colleagues, September 2024 preprint, NCT05585762. Thirty healthy older adults were randomized to matched ketone ester or canola-oil comparator drinks and 23 completed. Table 1 reports daytime dysfunction changes +0.09 (SD .30, n = 11) for canola and +0.08 (SD .51, n = 12) for ketone ester on a 0–3 PSQI component (reported Welch p > .999999). Global PSQI changes −0.27 versus +1.83 had unadjusted p = .047. The 2025 peer-reviewed report describes no clear difference across PSQI global or component changes. These are versions of one cohort. Daytime dysfunction includes more than sleepiness and there was no oil-free arm or Parkinson group. Canola refining is unspecified.

Table 1 in the exploratory preprint reports change in physical Pittsburgh Fatigability Scale of −1.6 ± 5.2 for the canola comparison and +0.1 ± 3.6 for ketone ester (p = .711). Grip changes were +0.6 versus −1.0 kg (p = .728). Trail Making Test A changed by −1.6 versus −5.8 seconds (p = .422), and POMS tension-anxiety by −0.73 versus +1.75 points (p = .211). These exploratory measures compare active ketone treatment with a canola-containing comparator in healthy older adults, without a separately assigned oil-free arm.
