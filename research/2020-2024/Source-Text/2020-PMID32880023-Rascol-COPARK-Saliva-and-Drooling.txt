Title: Excessive buccal saliva in patients with Parkinson's disease of the French COPARK cohort
Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00702-020-02249-0
PMID: 32880023
DOI: 10.1007/s00702-020-02249-0
Publication date: 2020-09-02
Source depth: Institutional author postprint body and tables, version-of-record abstract, publisher figure correction, original congress abstract 765
Date basis: Published online by Springer September 2, 2020. Volume 127 issue follows later in 2020.
Design: Observational comparison within a French Parkinson disease cohort, with age- and sex-matched control participants, on/off observations and a follow-up subset

Of 683 people with Parkinson disease enrolled, 671 had baseline on-medication UPDRS item 6 available. The journal reports 35% with item 6 scores 1–2 and 3% with scores 3–4. The two severity groupings are study-defined and the lower scores may include occasional minor drooling. The alternate SCOPA-Aut question was available in 302 Parkinson participants and 100 of 177 controls, with any drooling in 49% and 19%, respectively. This question uses a different threshold and sample.

In 229 participants with usable ratings in both medication states, mean UPDRS item 6 was 0.90 off and 0.54 on, reported p below .01. Among 392 people with paired follow-up ratings, the score rose from 0.47 to 0.64 over about 24 months. The postprint reports 2% to 3% in its more severe drooling category in this subset. These are observational comparisons of medication states and time. Table 2 links saliva complaints and eating-in-public difficulty in an unadjusted analysis. The adjusted estimate for that item was 1.09 (0.92–1.28). It is a question about social difficulty and supplies no dietary exposure.

The publisher correction changes the legend of Figure 2. The unfilled segment is no excessive saliva, singly hatched is sialorrhea, and crosshatched is drooling. The 2020 congress abstract 765 describes the same study family and has different SCOPA-Aut denominators and on/off summaries. Its broader SCOPA numbers are 576 Parkinson participants and 146 controls, versus 302 and 100 in the journal postprint. Treat these as differing reports within COPARK until their analysis definitions can be reconciled. No raw fig or other defined food was tested.
