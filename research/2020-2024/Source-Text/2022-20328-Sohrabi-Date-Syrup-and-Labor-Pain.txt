Title: The effect of oral honey-saffron syrup and date syrup on labor pain in nulliparous women
Source: https://doi.org/10.22038/ijogi.2022.20328
DOI: 10.22038/ijogi.2022.20328
Publication date: April 2022, journal issue
Registration: IRCT20190924044873N1
Coverage: Paraphrase of the original Persian pain paper, Tables 2–3, and verified companion registry lineage.
Original pain tables: https://ijogi.mums.ac.ir/article_20328_b4127a4ff9ad328f6991bcceb2b5a8eb.pdf#page=7

The Sanandaj comparison reports pain scores for 180 analyzed participants across date syrup, honey-saffron syrup and a placebo syrup. At the end of stage two, the corresponding 0–10 pain means were 7.25, 7.67 and 8.47. The report gives p=.015 for date syrup versus honey-saffron syrup at stage two and inconclusive differences at the other reported post-intervention stages. The two syrup groups were each reported lower than placebo at the three post-intervention stages.

The labor-duration paper describes 189 allocated and 180 analyzed. It shares the registry and ethics IDs and counts in the same trial family. The registry lists duration as the primary outcome and has no separately listed pain result. It describes open masking, while the companion paper states assessor masking. The study compares syrup formulations in acute labor. Food texture, energy matching, the pain setting and the population differ from the frozen claim.
