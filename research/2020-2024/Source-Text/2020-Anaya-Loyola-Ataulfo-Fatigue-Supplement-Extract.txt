Title: A mango (Mangifera indica L.) juice by-product reduces gastrointestinal and upper respiratory tract infection symptoms in children
Authors: Miriam A. Anaya-Loyola and colleagues

Coverage: factual extract of author Methods and official supplement Table 3S, prepared 2026-11-14

The product was peel and residual pulp from Ataulfo mango after juice production, dried at 45 °C and milled. Children aged 6 to 8 years received 2 g/day dissolved in 50 mL water or flavored water, for two months. The study randomized 80 and analyzed 65 (32 control, 33 mango) after 15 participants were excluded for parasitic infection. Questionnaires monitored different infection symptoms and included whether fatigue occurred. This endpoint quantified incidence rather than severity.

The four fatigue values in Table 3S, in the order of its columns, were 6 (18.75%) control and 3 (9.09%) JBP at baseline (p=.3708), then 3 (9.38%) control and 0 (0%) JBP at month two (p=.1823). Table 3S labels both arm sizes as 32, while the Methods say 33 in JBP and 3/33 gives the 9.09% printed in that table. The observed differences in incidence provide no statistically clear between-group contrast and do not represent raw fruit or clinical Parkinson fatigue severity.
