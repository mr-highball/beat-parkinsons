2023-03-25 — Wood et al., wild blueberry powder parallel randomized trial
Wood E et al. Wild blueberry (poly)phenols can improve vascular function and cognitive performance in healthy older individuals: a double-blind randomized controlled trial. American Journal of Clinical Nutrition 117:1306–1319. PMID36972800, PMC10315404, DOI 10.1016/j.ajcnut.2023.03.017; first available online 2023-03-25. Primary https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10315404/ ; accessible original publisher PDF https://www.apacare.ch/media/53/83/31/1761655847/wood-wild-blueberry-poly-phenols-can-improve-vascular-function.pdf?ts=1763047661 (local Wood-2023.pdf). Registration NCT04084457.

Healthy 65–80-year-olds consumed 26 g/day freeze-dried wild blueberry powder (302 mg anthocyanins; equivalent 178 g fresh weight), mixed with water, or matching appearance/taste/macronutrient/fiber/vitamin-C placebo for 12 weeks. Participants were instructed to keep their sachets frozen. This is powdered berry in a drink, not direct whole fresh-fruit intake. Methods describe 66 randomized (35 blueberry, 31 placebo), 61 retained at study completion after 3 withdrawals and 2 exclusions (baseline table 32 and 29), and 54 with 12-week measurements analyzed per protocol, 27 per arm, after seven missed visits due to COVID disruptions. Actual registry enrollment 53 differs.

Original Results pp. 1311–1312, Fig. 2: immediate recall 5.92 blueberry vs 5.28 placebo words, F(1,46)=4.321, p=.043; delayed recall 7.48 blueberry vs 9.43 placebo, F(1,47)=5.042, p=.029. Task-switching accuracy intervention effect was described as 8.5% higher vs placebo, F(1,46)=5.05, p=.029 in Results, whereas Fig. 2 caption reports p=.026. The article separately says in Discussion p. 1316 that delayed recall did not differ significantly, conflicting with the Results and Figure 2; preserve as an unresolved internal discrepancy. Task-switch model residuals failed normality Shapiro–Wilk p<.001. Other cognition and PANAS mood outcomes were not statistically different (cited supplemental Table S4). Flow-mediated dilation was 0.86 percentage points higher (95% CI 0.56–1.17, p<.001) and 24h systolic BP −3.59 mmHg (95% CI −6.95 to −0.23, p=.037), compared to placebo. No differences in cerebral blood flow or gut microbial composition.

Original data availability p. 1317: manuscript data, codebook, and analytic code available on request pending application and approval; no public individual dataset verified. Sponsor Wild Blueberry Association of North America supplied product and unrestricted funding, paper reports funder no role in design or analysis. Same cohort as registration, no claim-level inference here.
