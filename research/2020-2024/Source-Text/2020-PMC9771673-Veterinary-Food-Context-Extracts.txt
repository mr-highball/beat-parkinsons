Reviewed: 2026-11-19

The Prunus entry covers animal toxicity of leaves and seeds and includes apricot, cultivated sour cherry and European plum in the list. These are plant-part identities rather than ripe-fruit treatment comparisons. The separate phloridzin entry describes a historical chemical attribution to cherry, plum and pear without a fruit preparation study. Patulin is listed as a possible contaminant of barley and other grains.

The animal poisoning entry lists onion and chives as dietary hazards for dogs or cats. The parsley entry distinguishes cultivated Petroselinum crispum from broader common-name cross-references and describes furanocoumarins and photosensitization. The pearl-millet entry mentions a reported effect on lactation in sows. The low-purine diet entry lists lentils among foods limited when managing certain canine urinary stones. None of these veterinary descriptions reports a food effect in people with Parkinson disease.

The polyradiculoneuritis entry links a reported risk factor, raw chicken intake in dogs, to a proposed microbial trigger in Australia. The pneumonia entry describes lipid aspiration in cats after forced oil administration, with cod-liver oil given as an example. Separately, the Phocanema-related entries mention eating infected fish as a potential parasite exposure in humans. The peanut entry distinguishes extracted oil used as a pharmaceutical solvent from kernels and meal used in feed and from the latter materials' fungal contamination context.

Animal coat colors, crop names applied to other species, and the dictionary's unrelated fish, prawn and clinical terminology are recorded separately in the experiment disposition table. No internal link between entries is treated as a tested exposure–outcome comparison.
