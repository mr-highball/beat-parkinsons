Title: Exploring plants with flowers, original review and culinary identity scope
PMID: 38002124
PMCID: PMC10671036
DOI: 10.3390/foods12224066
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10671036/
Published: 2023-11-08.
Coverage: Complete official review body and all four original tables. This is a review of species and plant uses.

Table 1 records rosemary leaves, stems and flowers in culinary and other uses. It summarizes broad digestive use without a constipation comparison. Table 2 separates composition measures for rosemary flowers, leaves and whole plants. Table 3 lists Crocus sativus among sources of selected carotenoids. None of those rows measures saffron and clinical fatigue or insomnia. The introduction identifies cauliflower as an edible flower vegetable, with no anxiety comparison.

Table 1 lists olive oil in cooking dandelion flowers. This is a culinary pairing without assigned oil grade, dose or a clinical outcome. The constipation entry in the same row refers to the reviewed dandelion uses. Roasted dandelion roots and chicory are reported as coffee analogues. Chlorogenic acid in the composition tables comes from the named non-coffee plants. These contexts cannot establish brewed coffee exposure.

The review distinguishes Mexican bush sage, Salvia microphylla and red salvia from culinary Salvia officinalis. Its Aniseed swab is Agastache foeniculum. Pimpinella anisum appears in a cited aromatic-compound study rather than a measured preparation in this review. Chinese parsley is coriander, cherry pie heliotrope is not Prunus fruit, mango is a calla-lily cultivar, and orange often describes flower color or a lily name. COD in the bioremediation tables is chemical oxygen demand. Date is chronological. No asparagus intervention is measured.
