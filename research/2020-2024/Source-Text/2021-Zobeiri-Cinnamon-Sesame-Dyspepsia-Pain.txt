Public source https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC8137295
PMID 34093719, PMC8137295, DOI 10.1155/2021/6634115

First published 2021-05-13. Evaluation of the Effectiveness of Cinnamon Oil Soft Capsule in Patients with Functional Dyspepsia. The original Methods describe a Cinnamomum zeylanicum bark macerate in white-sesame oil and a sesame-oil capsule comparator. Table 3 field epigastric/upper abdominal pain provides the listed decreases from baseline. Numerical differences leave the incremental effect of the cinnamon macerate unresolved. No sesame-free arm is present. The original registration specifies bloating as sole primary; article describes the gastrointestinal symptom sum as primary. Same cohort as IRCT20170802035460N2.

Zobeiri and colleagues report 64 functional dyspepsia participants, 29 receiving cinnamon macerate and 35 sesame oil alone. Cinnamon bark was steeped in white-sesame oil and filtered. Both groups took soft capsules for six weeks. Table 3 gives mean decreases on the 0–4 epigastric or upper abdominal pain item of 0.4348 and 0.6154, respectively, p=.69. Both arms received sesame oil and the article leaves its roasting status unspecified. The two experiment claims use the same study. The publication and registration describe different primary outcomes.
