Nada M. Mostafa, Ahmed M. Mostafa and Mohamed L. Ashour. Antioxidants 10(12):1993, 2021-12-15. DOI 10.3390/antiox10121993. PMID 34943096 and PMCID PMC8698347.

Four groups of six male Sprague–Dawley rats were compared. The orally tested oil was factory-sourced cold-pressed oil, distinct from the hydrodistilled preparation used for chemical comparison. Cold-pressed oil was given before and with scopolamine. Comparator groups received vehicle, scopolamine or donepezil with scopolamine. The assessed behaviors were maze memory and passive avoidance. Sustained attention, a clinical Parkinson population and cracked culinary pepper were outside this experiment.

Section 3.1 reports 262.9% more time in the former platform quadrant with the pepper-oil intervention relative to scopolamine. Passive avoidance latency was 19.17% below the healthy group. Figures 1–4 attribute their material to the 2018 Bombax ceiba leaf extract paper, DOI 10.25135/rnp.47.17.10.062. The two articles report matching comparator changes in target quadrant time, crossings and avoidance latency, and donepezil avoidance latency relative to healthy control. The plant-treatment percentages differ. The available reports leave individual comparator animals and measurements unidentified across papers. Repeated estimates and figure attribution warrant preserving uncertainty about independence.
