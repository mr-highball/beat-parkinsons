BRIEF SUMMARY

The purpose of this research study is to determine whether istradefylline improves
cognition in individuals with Parkinson disease with cognitive impairment.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

Istradefylline has been approved by the U. S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to
reduce "off" episodes in Parkinson disease. The period when levodopa has a positive
effect on Parkinson's symptoms is called on-time. Once the medication stops working, a so
called "off" episode starts, where symptoms recur. Usual care for treatment of Parkinson
disease with cognitive impairment is use of cognition enhancing medications also called
cholinesterase inhibitors. In this study, participants will receive usual care, and in
addition, they will be asked to take istradefylline daily for 26 weeks.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change in Executive Function - Card Sort Test
    Description
      Executive function will be assessed using the Card Sort Test from the NIH Toolbox
      Cognition Battery. It measures the number of times the participant successfully figures
      out the rule and matches 10 cards in a row (maximum of 6). So the score range is 0-6.
      Interpretation: Low numbers (e.g., 0-2 categories) reflect poor abstract reasoning and an
      inability to use feedback to guide behavior. A score of 6 is the standard normal score
      for younger adults and a score of 3 or higher is normal or adjusted cut-off for adults
      60+.
    Time frame: Baseline to 26 weeks
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Change in Neurocognitive Outcomes
    Description
      NIH Toolbox Cognition Battery (NTCB): Scores are reported as T-scores normed to a mean of
      50 with a standard deviation (SD) of 10, with higher scores indicating better cognitive
      functioning. In this dataset, the lowest T-score was 28 and the highest T-score was 66.
    Time frame: Baseline to 26 weeks
  -
    Measure: Change in Recall
    Description
      Immediate and delayed recall will be assessed using the Hopkins Verbal Learning Test -
      Revised, which will be administered by a trained clinician. Higher scores indicate better
      recall. aw metrics must be converted into standard scores because raw totals decrease
      naturally with normal aging. Examiners utilize the normative data inside the Official PAR
      HVLT-R Manual Supplement or automated PARiConnect software to convert raw metrics into
      T-scores. T-scores adjust for age (and sometimes gender and education level depending on
      the specific norming data used). T-Score Range: Typically spans from 20 to 80. Average
      Performance: 40 to 59, Borderline/Low Range: 30 to 39, Clinical Impairment: Below 30
    Time frame: Baseline to 26 weeks
  -
    Measure: Change in Oral Fluency
    Description
      In the Controlled Oral Word Association Test (FAS, animals) (COWAT) the participant is
      asked to make verbal associations to different letters of the alphabet by saying all the
      words which they can think of beginning with a given letter. Higher scores indicate
      better oral fluency. Because it is a free-generation task, there is no formal maximum
      "ceiling" score, but performance typically falls into predictable ranges. Average
      Performance: Healthy young-to-middle-aged adults typically generate an aggregate total of
      35 to 45 words across all three letters combined. Scoring beneath the 25th percentile
      suggests a low-average performance. If the trajectory drops significantly
      further-specifically below the 5th percentile (equivalent to ≤ 1.5 standard deviations
      under the median)-clinicians look closely for signs of mild cognitive impairment (MCI),
      aphasia, or neurodegenerative conditions.
    Time frame: Baseline to 26 weeks
  -
    Measure: Change in Executive Function - Trail Making Test
    Description
      TMT which is a timed test of attention and executive function.It primarily assesses
      visual scanning, attention, and processing speed and requires participants to connect
      numbered targets in sequential order. It additionally assesses cognitive flexibility and
      executive function and requires participants to alternate between numbers and letters in
      sequential order. Raw scores are the number of seconds needed to complete the test with
      higher scores indicating poorer executive function. A standard cutoff or cap is typically
      applied at 300 seconds (5 minutes) per section to halt the test if a patient struggles.
      The raw scores are converted into T-scores based on Heaton norms for age, sex, race, and
      education. T-scores have a mean of 50 and standard deviation (SD) of 10, with higher
      T-scores indicating better performance. T-scores ≥40 are generally considered within
      normal limits, while T-scores <40 indicate below-expected performance, with lower scores
      reflecting greater impai
    Time frame: Baseline to 26 weeks
  -
    Measure: Change in Cognitive Status-Montreal Cognitive Assessment
    Description
      Participant's cognitive status will be assessed using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment
      score. Higher scores indicated better cognitive status. The Montreal Cognitive Assessment
      (MoCA) is scored between 0 and out of a maximum of 30 points, with a raw total score of
      26 or above traditionally considered clinically normal. 18-25 points is considered Mild
      Cognitive Impairment (MCI), 10-17 points is considered Moderate Cognitive Impairment, and
      less than 10 is considered Severe Cognitive Impairment
    Time frame: Baseline to 26 weeks
  -
    Measure: Change in Higher Cortical Functions-The Dementia Rating Scale-2 (DRS-2)
    Description
      The Dementia Rating Scale-2 (DRS-2) measures deficits in a large range of higher cortical
      functions and differentiates deficits of varying severity levels. The Dementia Rating
      Scale-2 (DRS-2) is scored out of a maximum of 144 total raw points. A higher score
      indicates better cognitive function, while lower scores signify greater cognitive and
      behavioral impairment. 124-144 points indicated Normal/non-impaired, 120-123
      Borderline/mild, less than 120 points Impaired. The maximum possible total score for the
      Dementia Rating Scale-2 (DRS-2) is 144, and the minimum possible total score is 0. There
      are five domains that equal 144 points. Each domain has a different maximum score, but
      they all have a minimum score of 0: Attention: Maximum score of 37,
      Initiation/Perseveration: Maximum score of 37, Construction: Maximum score of 6,
      Conceptualization: Maximum score of 39, Memory: Maximum score of 25.
    Time frame: Baseline to 26 weeks

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  Meet criteria for probable Parkinson disease dementia or PD-MCI (mild cognitive
       impairment)

    -  Age greater than 50

    -  Hoehn and Yahr stage < 4 in "on" state

    -  Currently taking carbidopa/levodopa

    -  Antiparkinsonian medications stable for at least 4 weeks prior to baseline visit

    -  Cholinesterase inhibitor dose stable for 8 weeks prior to baseline visit

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  Meet criteria for dementia with Lewy bodies, including dementia onset prior to or
       within 1 year of parkinsonism onset

    -  Presence of troublesome dyskinesias

    -  Pregnancy or possibility of becoming pregnant during the study period.

    -  Moderate or severe hepatic impairment

    -  dementia too severe to complete study measures or to adhere to medication schedule
Healthy volunteers: No
