BRIEF SUMMARY

The principal motor features of Parkinson's disease (PD) include rigidity, tremor,
bradykinesia, and postural instability. These motor deficits might cause gait dysfunction
including reduced gait velocity, stride length, arm swing, and increased gait
variability. Among these, increase in gait variability increased fall risk and served as
a marker of disease progression. Previous studies reported that some factors might
influence gait variability. However, which one contributes most has not yet been
verified. Therefore, the purpose of the present study is to identify the more related
influencing factors on gait variability in people with PD.

    Description: Using NeuroCom Smart Balance Master to measure

    Description: The score ranged from 0 to 72. Higher scores show increased severity.

    Description: The score ranged from 0 to 28. Higher scores show better balance.
    Time frame: 10 minutes

    Measure: New freezing of gait questionnaire
    Description: The score ranged from 0 to 29. Higher scores show increased severity in freezing of gait.
    Time frame: 15 minutes
  -
    Measure: Montreal Cognitive Assessment
    Description: The score ranged from 0 to 30. Higher scores show better cognitive function.
    Time frame: 20 minutes
