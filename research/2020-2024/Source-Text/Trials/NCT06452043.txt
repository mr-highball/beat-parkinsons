BRIEF SUMMARY

The goal of this clinical trial is first to determine the feasibility and to learn if a
nature-based program has positive effects on the physical and psychosocial health in a
group of participants with Parkinson's disease. The main questions for this purpose are:

Will the program improve:

  -  The physical outcomes such as mobility, strength and balance?

  -  The psychosocial health outcomes?

Participants will participate in one session per week of the intervention and be assessed
before and after the program. First, the adherence will be measured as the proportion of
supervised outdoor sessions completed. Dropout rate will correspond to participants who
will not finish the 8-week protocol, including pre- and post-intervention assessments.
Satisfaction will be evaluated with a post-intervention questionnaire rating the exercise
component, nature-based activities, and the PARC program on a 4-point Likert scale (0-3),
capturing clarity, usefulness, difficulty, and acceptability. Adverse effects will be
documented throughout the intervention. Qualitative benefits such as increased hope and
improved social interaction will be collected after sessions.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION

The chronic and neurodegenerative disease of Parkinson's (PD) is often characterized by
movement and balance disorders, which are accompanied by an increased risk of falls.
Falls are one of the most common problems that can lead to subsequent injuries (e.g.,
femoral head fracture) and associated mortality rates. Falls are frequent, debilitating,
and have deleterious effects on self-confidence and the quality of life of individuals
with PD. Additionally, according to Parkinson Canada, a high proportion of individuals
living with this disease suffer from depression and/or anxiety. Therefore, it is
essential to implement intervention programs aimed at improving the physical and
functional capacity as well as the well-being of this population to prevent falls and
promote sustainable health.

In recent years, an increasing number of studies have demonstrated the benefits of
contact with nature on the physical and mental health of individuals. Specific and
positive effects on psychological symptoms (e.g., depression) as well as cognition
(memory) in adults have also been shown according to this recent evidence. This
stimulates our research team to test the effects of a physical intervention in nature on
the physical and mental health of individuals suffering from PD. One of the perspectives
of this new intervention in nature is also to evaluate its impact on fall risks in this
population. The ultimate goal of this project is to contribute to the sustainable health
of the population.

The overall objective of this project, based on a pilot study, is to assess the
feasibility and the effects of an 8-week nature-based intervention program on the
physical and mental health of individuals with PD. The feasibility indicators are:

Adherence - proportion of supervised outdoor sessions that will be completed by
participants; Dropout Rate - proportion of participants who will not complete the full
8-week protocol, including pre- and post-intervention assessments; Satisfaction -
participant satisfaction will be measured with a brief post-intervention questionnaire
using a 4-point Likert scale assessing clarity, usefulness, difficulty, and acceptability
of the NBI/PARC program; Adverse Effects - any adverse events will be documented
throughout the intervention; and Perceived Benefits - qualitative feedback will be
collected on perceived benefits such as increased hope and improved social interaction.
In addition, various physical measures (mobility, strength, and balance) and mental
measures (level of depression, stress) will be studied in this project to better
understand the effects of this type of intervention.

REGISTERED OUTCOME MEASURES

Primary outcomes
  -
    Measure: Retention rate
    Description
      roportion of participants completing the full 8-week protocol including pre- and
      post-intervention assessments.
    Time frame: 8 weeks
  -
    Measure: Adherence to supervised outdoor sessions
    Description: Proportion of scheduled supervised outdoor sessions completed by each participant.
    Time frame: 8 weeks
  -
    Measure: Dropout rate
    Description: Proportion of participants who discontinue participation before completing the protocol.
    Time frame: 8 weeks
  -
    Measure: Participant satisfaction
    Description
      Participant Satisfaction was measured using the "Participant Satisfaction Likert Scale",
      a 4-point scale ranging from 0 (strongly disagree) to 3 (strongly agree). Higher scores
      indicate greater satisfaction with the intervention, including clarity of instructions,
      perceived usefulness, exercise difficulty, and overall acceptability.
    Time frame: Post-intervention
  -
    Measure: Adverse effects
    Description: Number and type of adverse events reported during the intervention.
    Time frame: 8 weeks
  -
    Measure: Qualitative feedback on perceived benefits
    Description
      Qualitative feedback collected after each session through brief open-ended questions
      documenting perceived benefits such as increased hope, enhanced social interaction, and
      other emergent themes.
    Time frame: through study completion, an average of 8 weeks.
  -
    Measure: Well-being state (WHO-5)
    Description
      The World Health Organization Well-Being Index (WHO-5) is a widely used self-reported
      questionnaire designed to measure subjective well-being. It assesses the individual's
      overall psychological well-being and quality of life. The WHO-5 consists of five simple
      questions that ask respondents to rate their well-being over the past two weeks. The
      questions cover aspects such as positive mood, vitality, and general interest in life.

      The questionnaire is scored on a scale from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating
      better well-being.
    Time frame: change from baseline at 0 to 8 weeks
  -
    Measure: Depression state (PHQ-9)
    Description
      The Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9) is a self-administered tool used to screen,
      diagnose, monitor, and measure the severity of depression. It consists of nine questions
      based on the diagnostic criteria for major depressive disorder in the DSM-5 (Diagnostic
      and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition).Respondents rate how often
      they have experienced each symptom over the past two weeks on a scale ranging from 0 (not
      at all) to 3 (nearly every day). The scores are then added together to produce a total
      score ranging from 0 to 27, with higher scores indicating greater severity of depressive
      symptoms
    Time frame: change from baseline at 0 to 8 weeks
  -
    Measure: Social Provisions (SPS-10)
    Description
      The Social Provisions Scale-10 (SPS-10) is a shorter version of the original Social
      Provisions Scale (SPS). It is a brief self-report questionnaire designed to measure
      perceived social support across five dimensions. The SPS-10 is derived from the longer
      SPS but consists of a subset of items that capture key aspects of social support.

      Similar to the original SPS, respondents rate their agreement with each item on a Likert
      scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 4 (strongly agree). The SPS-10 provides a
      quick and efficient way to assess an individual's perceived social support network and
      their satisfaction with various aspects of their social relationships.
    Time frame: change from baseline at 0 to 8 weeks
  -
    Measure: Connection to nature (CNS)
    Description
      The Connection to Nature Scale (CNS) is a psychological assessment tool designed to
      measure an individual's subjective connection to nature. It evaluates the degree to which
      people perceive themselves as a part of the natural world and the extent to which they
      appreciate and value nature in their lives.

      Respondents are asked to rate their agreement with each statement on a Likert scale,
      ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The scores are then totaled to
      provide an overall measure of an individual's connection to nature.
    Time frame: change from baseline at 0 to 8 weeks
Secondary outcomes
  -
    Measure: 5 times Sit-To-Stand (FTSTS)
    Description
      f the 30 second sit to stand test was not applied for some patients, we used also this
      test which it measures the amount of time it takes for a patient to sit and stand five
      times in succession with arms folded across their chest. 2 trials were applied in this
      test and the best time in seconds used for analysis.
    Time frame: change from baseline at 0 to 8 weeks
  -
    Measure: Functional mobility test (TUG)
    Description
      To assess mobility. Equipment: A stopwatch. Directions: Patients wear their regular
      footwear and can use a walking aid, if needed. Begin by having the patient sit back in a
      standard arm chair and identify a line 3 meters, or 10 feet away, on the floor.
    Time frame: change from baseline at 0 to 8 weeks
  -
    Measure: Maximal isometric hand grip strength
    Description
      Jamar dynamometer to assess maximal isometric hand grip strength and frailty criteria. 3
      trials of maximal contraction up 5 seconds. The best value was retained and corrected by
      body mass index from Fried classification.
    Time frame: change from baseline at 0 to 8 weeks
  -
    Measure: Postural control
    Description
      Measures of center of pressure (COP) from plateform of force during different balance
      tasks
    Time frame: change from baseline at 0 to 8 weeks
  -
    Measure: Walking pattern
    Description
      Gait parameters from GaitRite system (14-ft walkway) measurement across two speeds:
      preferred and fast, with a sampling frequency of 100 Hz.
    Time frame: change from baseline at 0 to 8 weeks
  -
    Measure: 10 m walk test
    Description
      The test was performed along a 14 m walkway, with timing initiated at the 2 m mark and
      stopped at the 12 m mark to ensure steady state walking. Each participant completed two
      trials at both comfortable (preferred) and maximal walking (fast) speeds, and the best
      time for each condition was retained for analysis.
    Time frame: change from baseline at 0 to 8 weeks
Other outcomes
  -
    Measure: Modified Falls Efficacy Scale (M FES)
    Description
      The confidence in performing daily activities without falling was evaluated using the
      French version of the Modified Falls Efficacy Scale (M FES), consisting of 14 items rated
      from 0 (no confidence) to 10 (complete confidence), with higher scores indicating greater
      falls related self efficacy.
    Time frame: change from baseline at 0 to 8 weeks
  -
    Measure: Frailty criteria from Fried (inclusion criterium only)
    Description
      The presence of frailty criteria according to Fried was assessed with self-report
      questions and two physical tests from five characteristics : 1) Low physical activity (to
      capture history of sedentary behavior or activity: ex. Do you get any physical exercise
      for the sake of exercising? How often do you leave your house?); 2) fatigue (complaint of
      exhaustion with normal activity such as walking outside, climbing stairs: ex. I felt that
      everything I did was an effort in the last week?); 3) weight loss (unintentional weight
      loss 10 lbs. or more in past year or more than 5% in past year); 4) weakness (grip
      strength evaluated with manual dynamometer; in average <30 kg for men and <18 kg for
      women, but data is normalized by body mass index); 5) slowness or slow walking speed
      (usual gait speed over 4.57 meters (15 feet); ex. >6.5 seconds, dependent of height).
    Time frame: change from baseline at 0 to 8 weeks
  -
    Measure: Hoehn and Yahr Scale
    Description
      It is a five-point scale used by doctors and researchers to rate the degree of disability
      and severity of symptoms caused by Parkinson's disease:

      Stage 1: Unilateral involvement only Stage 1.5: Unilateral and axial involvement Stage 2:
      Bilateral involvement without impairment of balance Stage 2.5: Mild bilateral disease
      with recovery on pull test Stage 3: Mild to moderate bilateral disease; some postural
      instability; physically independent Stage 4: Severe disability; still able to walk or
      stand unassisted Stage 5: Wheelchair bound or bedridden unless aided
    Time frame: Only at baseline from Sample characterization when Parkinson was included
  -
    Measure: Cognitive status
    Description
      Mini-mental state questionnaire (MMSE or Folstein), which is used for grading the
      cognitive state of patients in this study. It's assessing the attention and orientation,
      memory, registration, recall, calculation, language and ability to draw a complex
      polygon.

      It is ranged from 0 to 30, where 30 means no impairment.
    Time frame: Only at baseline from Sample characterization

ELIGIBILITY

Eligibility criteria
  Inclusion Criteria:

    -  diagnosed with Parkinson's disease (while medicated: ON);

    -  capable of walking independently;

    -  presenting mild to moderate disability symptoms according to the Hoehn and Yahr
       scale (stages 1-3);

    -  having a frailty score of less than 2/5 (Fried);

    -  having a cognitive status ≥26 based on the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE)
       questionnaire.

  Exclusion Criteria:

    -  Cancer;

    -  Red flags (infection, tumor, etc.);

    -  Severe psychiatric disorders;

    -  Palliative care;

    -  Severe systemic syndromes or diseases that may prevent tests and exercises from
       being performed;

    -  Stroke - very acute phase (1 week) and this until medial hemodynamic stability.
