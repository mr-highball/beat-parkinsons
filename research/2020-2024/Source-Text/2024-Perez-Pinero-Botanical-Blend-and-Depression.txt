Pérez-Piñero S et al. Dietary supplementation with plant extracts for amelioration of persistent myofascial discomfort in the cervical and back regions: a randomized double-blind controlled study. Front Nutr. 2024;11:1403108. First published 2024-06-03.
DOI: 10.3389/fnut.2024.1403108
PMID: 38887495
Registration: NCT05597189
Primary source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2024.1403108/full
Coverage: original Methods and Results, especially Tables 8 through 10.

The botanical product combined standardized rosemary, ashwagandha and sesame extracts, with two doses and a placebo over twelve weeks for myofascial pain. Table 9 analyzed 42 placebo, 39 low-dose and 42 high-dose participants. BDI-II means changed from 7.4 to 6.1, 7.3 to 4.8 and 8.8 to 5.8 respectively. The published overall between-group p=.149 is inconclusive. The baseline BDI-II ≥4 analysis used 35, 31 and 32 participants and reported a between-group p=.016. The current registration does not state that subgroup rule.

Table 8's PROMIS depression between-group p=.798 is inconclusive. All groups' scores increased, while Methods defines higher scores as more symptoms and Results interprets the change as improvement. The publication does not reconcile those statements. The study cannot identify the contribution of rosemary from the blend and does not study Parkinson disease or fresh culinary leaf.
