Title: The effect of Salvia officinalis extract on symptoms of flushing, night sweat, sleep disorders, and score of forgetfulness in postmenopausal women
Publication year: 2020
PMCID: PMC7114003
PMID: 32318472
Trial registration: IRCT2015111713940N2
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7114003/
Reviewed: 2026-11-26
Text coverage: Original methods and Table 2, exhaustion and urinary incontinence items.

Sixty-six postmenopausal women were randomized to sage extract or placebo. Table 1 describes 31 extract participants and 27 placebo participants. The table gives three-month means of 1.16 (SD 0.89) versus 1.43 (SD 1.39) for combined physical and mental exhaustion, with published p=.634. Some comparisons in the original do not reconcile from the displayed summaries, and within-arm improvement does not demonstrate a treatment effect. This trial was previously assessed under its registration number. The exposure is an extract and the population is postmenopausal, leaving the fresh-sage and Parkinson fatigue comparison unresolved.

Table 2 reports urinary incontinence scores before and after 12 weeks. The extract arm starts at 1.31 (SD 1.13) and ends at 0.81 (SD 1.11). The starch-placebo arm starts at 1.49 (SD 1.07) and ends at 1.07 (SD 1.49). The published post-treatment arm comparison is p=.369. Individual item denominators are unclear. The results text lists urinary incontinence among the items without persuasive between-arm improvement. Incontinence and urgency frequency are different outcomes. The study assigned common sage extract and evaluated postmenopausal participants.
