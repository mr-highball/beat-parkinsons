Title: Saffron ameliorated motor symptoms, short life span and retinal degeneration in Parkinson's disease fly models
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34224829/
PMID: 34224829
DOI: 10.1016/j.gene.2021.145811
First public: 2021-07-02
Coverage: Factual extract of Sections 2.1–2.3 and 3.1–3.2 and Figures 1–2 in publicly readable article text.

Inoue and colleagues suspended purchased saffron in distilled water and added it to Drosophila feed for the in vivo comparison. The isolated crocetin arms were distinct. The laboratory used a separate extract for in vitro work. The source does not establish a consumer milling grade. Figure 1 followed climbing in flies expressing human A30P or G51D alpha-synuclein variants. Male climbing groups contained 24–30 individuals, and the plotted repeated trials within a group do not add independent animals. At measured time points, saffron and isolated crocetin attenuated the loss of climbing performance. Figure 2 reports longer survival in G51D but no clear extension in A30P. Eye outcomes were separate. Climbing and survival did not measure muscle rigidity, subjective fatigue, sleep or depression.
