Title: Effect of Long-term Supplementation With Marine Omega-3 Fatty Acids vs Placebo on Risk of Depression or Clinically Relevant Depressive Symptoms and on Change in Mood Scores: A Randomized Clinical Trial
PMID: 34932079
PMCID: PMC8693224
DOI: 10.1001/jama.2021.21187
First published: 2021
Authors: Okereke OI, Vyas CM, Mischoulon D, Chang G, Cook NR, Weinberg A, Buring JE, Manson JE
Journal: JAMA
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34932079/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 4 review).

Source basis: PubMed primary abstract. Landmark adversarial RCT (VITAL-DEP, NCT01696435): 18,353 US adults aged >=50 years randomized to 1 g/day marine omega-3 fish-oil capsules (465 mg EPA + 375 mg DHA) vs placebo over median 5.3 years. Found NO improvement in PHQ-8 mood scores (+0.03 points, 95% CI -0.01 to 0.07, P=0.19) and a small statistically significant INCREASE in incident/recurrent depression events (HR 1.13, 95% CI 1.01-1.26, P=0.03). Isolated fish-oil capsules rather than whole herring, strongly countering marine omega-3 antidepressant extrapolation.
