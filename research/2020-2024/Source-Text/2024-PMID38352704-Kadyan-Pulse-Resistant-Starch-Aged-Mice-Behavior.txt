Title: Resistant starches from dietary pulses improve neurocognitive health via gut-microbiome-brain axis in aged mice.
PMID: 38352704
PMCID: PMC10864001
DOI: 10.3389/fnut.2024.1322201
First published: 2024
Authors: Kadyan S, Park G, Hochuli N, Miller K, Wang B, Nagpal R
Journal: Frontiers in nutrition
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38352704/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Preclinical mouse study (Kadyan et al., 2024, Front Nutr, PMC10864001) feeding 5% w/w resistant starch from lentil, chickpea, pinto bean, or black-eyed pea (BEP RS) for 20 weeks to aged humanized-microbiome mice (n=14-16/group). Full-text results confirm that BEP RS produced NULL behavioral effects across every motor, anxiety, and cognitive test: grip strength, hanging wire, rotarod, open-field mobility/distance/center time, T-maze alternation (improved only by lentil and pinto bean), novel object recognition (ANCOVA p=0.295), and object location memory (p=0.654), altering only hypothalamic/frontal cortex inflammatory and senescence mRNA transcripts. Limits: purified resistant starch rather than whole cooked/canned peas, non-PD aging model, and null behavioral outcomes.
