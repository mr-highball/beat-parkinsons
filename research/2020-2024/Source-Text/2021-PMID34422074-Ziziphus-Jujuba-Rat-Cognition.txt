Title: Antiamnesic and Neuroprotective Effects of an Aqueous Extract of Ziziphus jujuba Mill. (Rhamnaceae) on Scopolamine-Induced Cognitive Impairments in Rats
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8373493/
PMID: 34422074
PMCID: PMC8373493
DOI: 10.1155/2021/5577163
Publication date: 2021-08-10
Date basis: Publication date in official Europe PMC and NCBI PMC XML records (Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine).
Coverage: Paraphrased original methods and results from official NCBI PMC JATS XML.

This preclinical rat experiment evaluated an aqueous leaf extract of Ziziphus jujuba Mill. (Rhamnaceae, jujube / Chinese red date — a botanically distinct genus and family from culinary dried date palm fruit, Phoenix dactylifera) at 29, 57, and 114 mg/kg p.o. for 15 days in scopolamine-treated rats (n = 8 per group across 7 groups, compared with distilled water, donepezil 1.2 mg/kg, and tacrine 10 mg/kg).

Outcomes were limited to spatial and working memory tasks (Morris water maze and Y-maze) plus hippocampal and prefrontal oxidative-stress, inflammation, and apoptosis biochemistry. While the introduction cites traditional Cameroonian use of Z. jujuba for pain and other ailments, the experiment neither tested Phoenix dactylifera date fruit nor performed any nociception, analgesia, or Parkinson symptom measurement.
