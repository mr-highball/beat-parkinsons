Title: Melissa officinalis Regulates Lipopolysaccharide-Induced BV2 Microglial Activation via MAPK and Nrf2 Signaling
PMID: 39467696
PMCID: PMC11729339
DOI: 10.4014/jmb.2409.09020
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11729339/
First published: 2024-10-29 online. Journal of Microbiology and Biotechnology 34(12):2474–2483, December issue.
Coverage: Factual extract of the original Methods, Results, assay tables and figure captions.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

Choi and colleagues tested a freeze-dried 70% ethanol extract of dried Melissa officinalis leaves in LPS-stimulated BV2 microglial cells. The extract contained about 45.5 mg rosmarinic acid per gram. The experiment tested the extract mixture. The paper quantified rosmarinic acid for characterization and did not establish that it alone caused the response.

In Figure 2 the LPS-only condition produced about 28 μM nitric oxide, versus about 4 μM in the highest tested extract condition. Assay variability is reported across three independent experiments. The lowest tested concentration did not clearly reduce COX-2 or the p38 and JNK measures. Figure 7 shows higher nuclear Nrf2 and cytoplasmic HO-1 with extract. The model uses an inflammatory stimulus in cultured cells without Parkinson participants, a brewed exposure, or a mood endpoint. It provides a mechanistic lead, not a depressive-symptom result.

Locators: Methods section on extract preparation, Table 3, Figures 2–4 and 7. A Chinese Parkinson Nrf2 review independently names the herb in its abstract. Its underlying references have not yet been authenticated, so a citation from that review to this experiment remains unconfirmed.
