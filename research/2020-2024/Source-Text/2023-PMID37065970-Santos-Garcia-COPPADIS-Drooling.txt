Title: Prevalence and Factors Associated with Drooling in Parkinson's Disease: Results from a Longitudinal Prospective Cohort and Comparison with a Control Group
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10101739/
PMID: 37065970
PMCID: PMC10101739
DOI: 10.1155/2023/3104425
Publication date: 2023-04-06
Date basis: electronic publication in original JATS
Source depth: factual extract of original methods, results and Figure 1

The prospective COPPADIS cohort classified daytime drooling by item 19 of the Non-Motor Symptoms Scale. The item combines reported frequency and severity on a 0–12 scale. The authors used any positive item score to define drooling and assessed swallowing separately.

Drooling was recorded for 277 of 691 participants with Parkinson disease at baseline, 264 of 604 at one year and 242 of 502 at two years. Control proportions were 5 of 201 at baseline and 4 of 124 at two years. Among the 481 Parkinson participants with all three visits, 306 experienced drooling at one or more visits and 123 at all three. The report also relates drooling to dysphagia and other nonmotor measures. The denominators change across visits.

The design describes prevalence and symptom associations. The authors identify AEMPS postauthorization cohort code COH-PAK-2014-01. No raw-fig intake or randomized culinary comparison was measured. The English NILS cohort, PMID 32130515, is a separate study.
