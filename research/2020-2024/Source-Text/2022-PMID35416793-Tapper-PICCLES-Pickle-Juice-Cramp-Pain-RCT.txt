Title: Pickle Juice Intervention for Cirrhotic Cramps Reduction: The PICCLES Randomized Controlled Trial.
PMID: 35416793
PMCID: PMC11214544
DOI: 10.14309/ajg.0000000000001781
Registry ID: NCT04650295
First published: 2022
Authors: Tapper EB, Salim N, Baki J, Zhao Z, Sundaram V, Patwardhan V et al.
Journal: Am J Gastroenterol
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11214544/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Randomized, open-label controlled trial (Tapper et al., 2022, Am J Gastroenterol, PICCLES trial, PMC11214544, NCT04650295, n=82 randomized / 74 completed adults with cirrhosis and frequent muscle cramps) comparing sips of dill/kosher pickled cucumber brine versus tap water at cramp onset for 28 days. Primary endpoint of cramp VAS pain severity improved significantly with pickle juice (-2.25 +/- 3.61 vs -0.36 +/- 2.87 points, p=0.03), whereas proportion of cramp-days with VAS <5 (p=0.2), sleep quality (p=0.1), and EQ-5D quality of life (p=0.3) were null. Limits: liquid pickle brine rather than eaten solid pickled cucumber flesh, open-label design, cirrhosis peripheral cramps rather than Parkinson pain or central basal-ganglia dystonia, and mechanistic attribution points to acetic acid (vinegar) triggering an oropharyngeal neural reflex rather than Cucumis sativus phytochemistry.
