Title: Sclareol exerts an anti-inflammatory effect, possibly through COXs inhibition pathway: In vivo and in silico studies

Coverage: Paraphrased original methods, Tables 2–4 and discussion from official NCBI PMC JATS XML.

This preclinical mouse experiment and molecular docking study evaluated oral sclareol (a labdane diterpene alcohol derived from clary sage, Salvia sclarea L., rather than culinary garden sage Salvia officinalis) in formalin-induced paw inflammation and nociceptive licking. Forty adult Swiss albino mice were randomized into eight groups (n = 5 per group): vehicle control, sclareol at 5, 10, or 20 mg/kg p.o., celecoxib 42 mg/kg p.o., ketoprofen 42 mg/kg p.o., and sclareol 10 mg/kg combined with either NSAID, dosed 30 minutes before sub-plantar 0.5% formalin injection.

In Table 2 (mean ± SEM paw licks, n = 5), vehicle mice showed 42.20 ± 2.10 licks in the early phase (1–10 min) and 22.40 ± 2.17 in the late phase (21–30 min). Sclareol reduced licking dose-dependently across 5 mg/kg (15.60 ± 1.99 early; 5.40 ± 1.93 late), 10 mg/kg (13.80 ± 2.16; 3.60 ± 1.58), and 20 mg/kg (10.80 ± 0.96; 1.60 ± 1.27; p < 0.05 vs vehicle). In Table 3, paw edema at 120 min fell from 2.00 ± 0.00 mm (vehicle) to 0.50 ± 0.18 mm (5 mg/kg), 0.50 ± 0.25 mm (10 mg/kg), and 0.40 ± 0.27 mm (20 mg/kg; p < 0.05). The study tested an isolated clary-sage constituent in acute mouse inflammatory pain rather than fresh culinary sage in Parkinson disease, measured zero fatigue outcomes, and is included in an October 2026 journal batch corrigendum (PMID:42898092).
