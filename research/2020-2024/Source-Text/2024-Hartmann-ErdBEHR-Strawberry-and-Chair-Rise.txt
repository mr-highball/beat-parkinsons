Title: High-dose polyphenol-rich nutrition improves lipid and inflammation profiles and can trigger apoptotic signaling in healthy older adults (the ErdBEHR study)
Author: Hartmann and colleagues
Source: https://agingbiologyjournal.org/Archive/Volume3/20240020/
DOI: 10.59368/agingbio.20240020
Publication date: 2024-02-15
Registry identifier: DRKS00026998
Full article PDF: https://agingbiologyjournal.org/Archive/Volume3/20240020/agingbio.20240020.pdf

Text coverage: Distilled from the publisher's full paper, including Methods and Table 2, and the supplementary PDF, especially Table 1.

Five groups underwent ten weeks of intervention. The lower-dose group received 500 g of fresh strawberries weekly. A second group received an escalating amount of fresh strawberries alone, and selected high-intake arms added freeze-dried strawberries or that preparation plus capers in olive oil. The main analysis includes 168 adults aged 50–80 and reports 156 regular completions. Assignment was open to participants, while physicians and laboratory staff assessed without allocation information. A strawberry producer provided partial funding and helped with recruitment.

Participants completed a 30-second chair-rise test. Table 2 reports a common modeled increase of 0.16 on the Poisson model scale (SE 0.029, p < 0.001, n = 156), with group-by-visit p = 0.999. The paper describes average improvement of about two repetitions. Supplementary Table 1 shows changes of 0.152 (SE 0.065) for the lower fresh-fruit group and 0.171 (SE 0.070) for the higher fresh-fruit-only group. These are group-specific model estimates and their standard errors, without a statistically discernible dose effect. With all groups exposed to strawberries, the common time change cannot isolate a fruit effect from other changes over time.

In the highest combined-exposure group, LDL decreased relative to the low fresh-fruit group in the pairwise comparison (p = 0.007), with overall interaction p = 0.248. The prespecified hsCRP analysis did not show a significant intervention effect. The paper reports an exploratory signal in participants with elevated starting CRP. Neither the main report nor the supplementary material reports prospective falls after strawberry exposure. The cohort was not recruited for Parkinson disease and chair rises cannot determine fall frequency.
