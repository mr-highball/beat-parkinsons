In a survey of three rural Barbadian communities, 154 people completed the questionnaire and 116 reported using botanical medicines. Table 3 lists 69 taxa, each reported indication and the recorded preparation or frequency when specified. The citation counts are totals for each plant and cannot supply a per-indication denominator or symptom effect.

The ginger row pairs grated or whole rhizome decoction or infusion with maintenance of health. The neighboring joint or back pain use specifies rhizome decoction, infusion or stew, with grating unspecified. Parsley infusion is reported for headache, clove decoction for toothache, and turmeric decoction, infusion or stew for joint or back pain. Grated nutmeg infusion is reported for upset stomach. The nutmeg headache preparation is topical. The botanical entries called Bay Leaf, Sage and Black Cumin are Pimenta racemosa, Lantana camara and Nigella sativa. Those identities differ from culinary Laurus nobilis, Salvia officinalis and Cuminum cyminum.
