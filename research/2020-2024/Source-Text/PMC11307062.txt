Title: Localized Pantothenic Acid (Vitamin B5) Reductions Present Throughout the Dementia with Lewy Bodies Brain
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11307062/
PMID: 38820022
PMCID: PMC11307062
DOI: 10.3233/jpd-240075
Publication date: 2024-05-24
Date basis: publisher PDF and JATS pre-press date; issue release 2024-07-23
Source depth: complete original PMC JATS body; local research digest

The original measures pantothenic acid in 20 dementia with Lewy bodies and 19 control donors, with lower concentrations in six of ten regions, including primary visual cortex. It mentions visual hallucinations as a possible interpretation of the anatomy. Food composition separately confirms that dried figs contain this nutrient. The tissue comparison has no individual food intake, supplementation, or clinical episode counts.

Table 3 lists primary visual cortex means of 48.6 in cases and 91.5 in controls, in the paper's reported tissue concentration units (p = 0.008). One comparative table caption says urea, while the Methods, Results, and table heading identify pantothenic acid as the measured analyte. The earlier Parkinson disease dementia report PMC8468190 uses another sample. See the USDA dried fig composition entry FDC174665.
