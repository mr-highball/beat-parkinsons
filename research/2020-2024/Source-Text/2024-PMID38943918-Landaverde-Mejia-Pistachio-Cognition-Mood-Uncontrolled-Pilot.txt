Title: Pistachio (Pistacia vera L.) consumption improves cognitive performance and mood in overweight young adults: A pilot study.
PMID: 38943918
DOI: 10.1016/j.foodchem.2024.140211
First published: 2024
Authors: Landaverde-Mejia K, Dufoo-Hurtado E, Camacho-Vega D, Maldonado-Celis ME, Mendoza-Diaz S, Campos-Vega R
Journal: Food chemistry
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38943918/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 6 review).

Primary Abstract:
This pilot study evaluated the impact of pistachio consumption on cognitive performance and mood in overweight young adults. Pistachios were characterized (chemical and nutraceutical), and a baseline-final, uncontrolled nutritional intervention was performed (28 g of pistachio/28 days). Psychometric tests were applied to estimate cognitive performance and mood; anthropometric evaluation, biochemical analysis, and plasma antioxidant activity were included. The main component of nuts was lipids (48.1%). Pistachios consumption significantly (p ≤ 0.05) reduced waist circumference (-1.47 cm), total cholesterol (-10.21 mg/dL), LDL (-6.57 mg/dL), and triglycerides (-21.07 mg/dL), and increased plasma antioxidant activity. Pistachio supplementation improved risk tolerance (p ≤ 0.006) and decision-making strategy (p ≤ 0.002; BART-task), executive functions (BCST-task; p ≤ 0.006), and selective and sustained attention (Go/No-Go-test; p ≤ 0.016). The mood state was positively modulated (p ≤ 0.05) for anxiety, anger-hostility, and sadness-depression. These results show for the first time the benefits of pistachio consumption on cognitive performance and mood in overweight young adults.

Verified Round 6 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Single-arm, UNCONTROLLED baseline-final pilot intervention (Food Chem 2024;457:140211; n=28 overweight young adults; 28 g/d pistachios for 28 days, no comparator arm, no randomization, no blinding). Reported pre-to-post reductions in waist circumference (-1.47 cm), TC, LDL, TG, pre-post shifts in EVEA mood subscales (anxiety, anger-hostility, sadness-depression; p<=0.05), and pre-post gains on Go/No-Go attention (p<=0.016), BCST executive card-sorting (p<=0.006), and BART decision-making (p<=0.002-0.006). CRITICAL METHODOLOGICAL LIMITATIONS: lack of a control arm renders 28-day repeat cognitive testing (especially BCST rule discovery and Go/No-Go) heavily confounded by test-retest practice effects (as directly proven in Cogan 2022 where no-nut controls improved at p<0.001 over 4 weeks), and uncontrolled pre-post mood changes are confounded by concurrent weight/waist loss (-1.47 cm) and Hawthorne/expectation effects. Kept non-directional (claim_effect=none) pending completed randomized controlled trials (NCT06992544, NCT06900452).
