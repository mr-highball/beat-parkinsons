S9.664 uses olive oil as the vehicle for rotenone in a mouse Parkinson model. The experimental comparisons concern exercise or an inhaled gas challenge. The linked full article confirms that the model control also received injected olive oil, while the recorded outcomes included locomotion, coordination and tissue measures. The study cannot attribute its results to dietary oil or to an anxiety comparison. See 2022-PMID36359351-Olive-Oil-Vehicle-Extract.txt. S2.132 used olive oil as an oral vehicle for astaxanthin in experimental cardiac work. The oil was matched in the corresponding comparator.

S1.97 uses theoretical conformational analysis of rubiscolin, a peptide originally associated with spinach leaf Rubisco. It names parsley and several other leaves as possible related sources on the basis of sequence similarity. That inference is neither an analytical measurement of a culinary parsley serving nor a symptom assessment. S6.409 separately examines spinach thylakoid membranes in thermal stress. The distinct 2007 isolated-peptide mouse study is a behavioral association that still lacks a Parkinson dyskinesia or leafy-food comparison.

S9.719 studies resveratrol in rats after sciatic nerve injury, including axon and Schwann-cell markers and a functional index. S2.146 studies the compound in pea mitochondria. S9.660 identifies grapes among reported sources of resveratrol. Grape snail experiments represent other members. S9.596 studies antioxidants in mate or membrane models, including chlorogenic acid and egg phospholipids, with no coffee intervention. S1.46 uses a purified egg-white lysozyme reagent.

S7.478 uses an oral oat-stem lignin preparation in mice undergoing radiation. Separate agriculture or plant-system reports examine barley, oats, apple, pear, cherry, millet and tomato through seed, grafting, ripening, lighting or photosynthesis measures. A preparation measured in one member cannot be assigned to neurologic outcomes in another.
