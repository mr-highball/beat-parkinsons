Title: Cranberries for preventing urinary tract infections.
PMID: 37068952
PMCID: PMC10108827
DOI: 10.1002/14651858.CD001321.pub6
First published: 2023
Authors: Williams G, Hahn D, Stephens JH, Craig JC, Hodson EM
Journal: The Cochrane database of systematic reviews
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10108827/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Cochrane Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis (Williams et al., 2023, Cochrane Database Syst Rev, PMC10108827) of 50 RCTs (8,857 randomized participants; 26 trials n=6,211 meta-analyzed for symptomatic culture-verified UTIs). Cranberry products reduced symptomatic culture-verified UTIs overall (RR 0.70, 95% CI 0.58-0.84; moderate certainty), specifically in women with recurrent UTIs (RR 0.74, 95% CI 0.55-0.99; n=1,555), children (RR 0.46; n=504), and post-intervention susceptible patients (RR 0.47; n=1,434). CRUCIAL BOUNDARY FOR PARKINSON DISEASE: little or no benefit in adults with neuromuscular/neurogenic bladder dysfunction with incomplete emptying (3 trials, n=464, RR 0.97, 95% CI 0.78-1.19) or institutionalized elderly men and women (3 trials, n=1,489, RR 0.93, 95% CI 0.67-1.30). Gastrointestinal side effects did not clearly differ from placebo/no treatment (10 trials, n=2,166, RR 1.33, 95% CI 1.00-1.77). Context-only synthesis.
