Journal: European Journal of Epidemiology (published 2024)
Identifiers: PMID:39294525 | DOI:10.1007/s10654-024-01152-2
Source URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39294525/

Study design and findings
- Prospective French E3N women's cohort (N = 71,542; 1993-2018; mean follow-up 18.8 years; 845 validated incident PD cases) with 5-year and longer exposure lags plus premotor constipation/depression adjustment.
- Total milk showed a J-shaped association (p-nonlinearity = 0.045) with positive linear risk among drinkers (HR/1-SD = 1.09, 95% CI 1.01-1.18, p = 0.024).
- Plain milk (alone or with cereals) was positively associated with PD incidence (HR/1-SD = 1.08, 95% CI 1.02-1.14, p = 0.014), whereas milk added to tea/coffee/chicory showed a U-shaped relation with lower risk at moderate intake (HR = 0.77, 95% CI 0.61-0.97, p = 0.030). Other dairy products were not associated with PD incidence.

Relevance and limits for Parkinson's food experiments
- Long-lag prospective epidemiological context for cow's milk and dairy exposures, showing divergence between plain milk and milk consumed with caffeinated beverages.
- Limits: Observational disease-incidence cohort in women rather than symptom-severity intervention in diagnosed Parkinson's disease.
