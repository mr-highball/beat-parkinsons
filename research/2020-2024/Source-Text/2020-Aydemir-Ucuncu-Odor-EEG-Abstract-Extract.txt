Thyme and clove oils as EEG identification odors
Indexed author abstract with Crossref publication date
Aydemir and Üçüncü’s October 5, 2020 conference record uses EEG responses during exposure to rosemary, thyme, mint and clove oil odors for participant identification. Parkinson disease appears as context for EEG use. The measured outcome is recognition of individual signal patterns. The archived original abstract supplies no ingested thyme or clove exposure, clinical odor threshold, or symptom comparison.
