Title: Coadministration of 6-Shogaol and Levodopa Alleviates Parkinson's Disease-Related Pathology in Mice
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11392672/
PMID: 39092515
PMCID: PMC11392672
DOI: 10.4062/biomolther.2024.075
Publication date: 2024-08-02
Date basis: First online epub date in official NCBI PMC JATS XML metadata (Biomolecules & Therapeutics, September 2024 issue).
Coverage: Paraphrased original methods and results from official NCBI PMC JATS XML.

This preclinical mouse experiment tested oral isolated 6-shogaol (20 mg/kg, a dehydrated ginger constituent) alone and coadministered with oral levodopa (80 mg/kg) for 26 days in MPTP/probenecid-induced Parkinson model mice. Behavioral outcomes included motor performance, depression-like tests, memory testing, and a blinded buried-food test of olfactory impairment alongside striatal and nigral immunohistochemistry.

Levodopa alone improved motor behavior but had no significant effect on non-motor deficits, dopaminergic neuronal loss, or neuroinflammation. Oral 6-shogaol alone and 6-shogaol plus levodopa ameliorated olfactory impairment (buried-food latency), depression-like behavior, and memory deficits while preserving striatal dopaminergic neurons and attenuating glial neuroinflammation. The experiment used an isolated ginger constituent in a mouse toxin model rather than culinary grated or ground ginger in humans, and did not score levodopa-induced abnormal involuntary movements (dyskinesia) or clinical passive muscle rigidity.
