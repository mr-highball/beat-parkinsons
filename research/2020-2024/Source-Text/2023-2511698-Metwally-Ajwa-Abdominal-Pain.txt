The completer analysis covers 131 children across three whole-date, five whole-date and no-date daily groups. Table 3 gives rounded post-intervention abdominal pain means of 0.2 in all three groups. Table 4 records pain improvement in 15 of 47, 15 of 42 and 13 of 42, respectively (three-group p=.883). Improvements in gastrointestinal totals and constipation supply separate outcomes. Those totals cannot substitute for the abdominal pain row.

The clinical registry describes nonrandomized allocation, whereas the report describes random assignment. The journal biomarker paper has matching arm sizes and identifies Ajwa dates. Count the linked biomarker and behavioral reports with this cohort. The drying process and maturity stage remain unverified. The child population, abdominal symptom and completer design limit applicability to Parkinson pain.
