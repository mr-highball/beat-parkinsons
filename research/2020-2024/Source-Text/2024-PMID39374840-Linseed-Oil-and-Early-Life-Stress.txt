Title: Linseed oil in a maternal-separation rat study
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39374840/
PMID: 39374840
DOI: 10.1016/j.brainres.2024.149268
Publication date: 2024
Coverage: Journal author abstract plus original related conference and accessible dissertation text from the same investigative team.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26

The journal abstract reports that an isocaloric diet enriched with linseed oil attenuated the elevated-plus-maze anxiety-like signal following maternal separation. It also describes sex-specific inflammatory observations. The available first-author dissertation clarifies that the enriched diet contained both linseed and soybean oils, and the comparator diet contained soybean oil. In maternally separated females, the light–dark result did not show attenuation. The dissertation appendix gives a null diet main effect on open-field crossings (p=.595). The publicly available PDF supplied the methods and appendix but omitted the main results pages and figures. Exact sex-specific contrasts and the oil processing grade remain unverified.

A 2022 e-poster by the same investigative team (number 11323, pp.258–259) gives elevated-plus-maze stress-by-diet interactions for time in closed arms of p=.060 in males and p=.078 in females, with eight animals per group. It reports increased total distance after separation in both sexes. Possible cohort overlap keeps the report in the same evidence family pending verification. None of these sources measures anxiety in people with Parkinson disease.
