Abstract 281, by Li and colleagues, concerns tea consumption and incident type 2 diabetes. The cohort enrolled 5,199 adults without diabetes at baseline, with a separate meta-analysis of 19 cohorts. The meta-analysis lists black, green and oolong tea in subgroup analysis. It gives no Parkinson symptom comparison, and the tea categories do not identify a brewing method.

Abstract 280, by Ciborowski and colleagues, tests a capsule containing cinnamon with red capsicum before a high-carbohydrate meal. The combined exposure and measured plasma metabolites give no isolated cinnamon result or clinical Parkinson fatigue, rigidity, pain, anxiety or walking measure.

Abstract 116, by Ali Mondal and colleagues, uses intraperitoneal olive oil in a vehicle group of a mouse liver-fibrosis experiment. It does not test an assigned cold-pressed culinary preparation or a gait or anxiety intervention. Abstract 544, by Imai and colleagues, compares a vegetable juice preload, a fresh tomato and broccoli preload, and water with a rice meal. Twenty-four healthy women completed the crossover. The endpoint is postprandial glucose. Cooked tomato, steamed broccoli and Parkinson rigidity or falls are unmeasured.

Relevant near matches include a cherry fluorescent label, Salmon sequence-analysis software and an acridine orange stain. Dietary food categories, diabetic gait and falls measures, and a separate analysis of Parkinson incidence cannot be combined into an ingredient-specific Parkinson symptom result.
