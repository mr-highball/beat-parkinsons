Yoshihara and colleagues report experiments in 85 anesthetized male rats. After prolonged direct laryngeal contact with capsaicin or acid, airflow elicited fewer reflex swallows. The abstract distinguishes the 60-minute result from a short capsaicin exposure, which did not clearly reduce the later airflow-triggered response. It also reports increased laryngeal vascular permeability after sustained capsaicin. The 85 is the study total, not the number in any single contrast.

The 2018 swallowing congress member ENS1-4 and 2019 JPS member 1P-350 have overlapping investigators and essentially the same prolonged stimulation, outcomes and setting. The journal report expands this line with additional measurements. The participant-level relationship is uncertain, so keep the original presentations and journal as one research family. Local contact in an anesthetized animal differs from oral culinary consumption, and neither intake of ground cayenne nor clinical Parkinson severity was compared.
