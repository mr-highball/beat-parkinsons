Title: Psycho-sensory modalities of visual hallucinations and illusions in Parkinson's disease
Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/am/pii/S0035378721005907
PMID: 34238577
DOI: 10.1016/j.neurol.2021.04.007
Publication date: 2021-07-05
Source depth: complete primary accepted manuscript; local research digest

The authors analyzed 54 symptomatic participants nested in the previously reported 82-person Parkinson cohort, with 26 classified as predominantly illusions and 28 as predominantly hallucinations. Seven experienced both. The Psycho-Sensory Hallucinations Scale measured phenomenological features. Hallucinations were reported more often and lasted longer than illusions in the group comparisons (p = 0.02 for each). The reports also distinguished nocturnal occurrence and content. These associations compare symptom groups rather than a dietary exposure.

The manuscript assigns the ancillary outcomes to NCT03454269 and cites NCT01114321 for the parent cohort. Its outcome-frequency comparison is informative about measurement, with no intervention assignment, fig intake contrast or timing against a meal. The ocular baseline, this nested analysis and subsequent telephone study belong to the same cohort family. Open institutional manuscript: https://hal.science/hal-03552255.
