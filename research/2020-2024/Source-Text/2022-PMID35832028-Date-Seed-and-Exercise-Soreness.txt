Title: The effects of date seed (Phoenix dactylifera) supplementation on exercise-induced oxidative stress and aerobic and anaerobic performance following high-intensity interval training sessions

This randomized double-blind trial assigned date-seed powder or placebo alongside high-intensity interval training in physically active adults. Thirty-four of 36 participants completed it, 17 in each group. Baseline 0–10 muscle-pain VAS was 6.35 versus 6.41. After two weeks the means were 5.06 versus 5.82 (p = .070). At 24 hours they were 3.35 versus 5.00 (p < .001), with SDs 1.05 and 1.11. The reported supplement-by-time interaction was p < .001. The original results distinguish the immediate and later comparisons.

The authors cite registration IRCT20150205020965N9. The same identifier appears in the published protocol (PMID 35769196) and the companion inflammation and muscle damage report (PMID 36881179). These records are one trial family. The measured effect concerns recovery after exercise under seed powder. It does not establish pain effects of eating dried date flesh in Parkinson disease.
