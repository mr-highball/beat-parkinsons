Title: Evaluating efficacy and safety of Saffron add-on treatment in improvement of motor and depressive symptoms of patients with Parkinson’s disease
Publication: Lalezari and colleagues, Journal of Herbal Medicine 48, 100968, 2024
Publication year: 2024
DOI: 10.1016/j.hermed.2024.100968
Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hermed.2024.100968
Publisher results and available excerpts: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2210803324001258
Linked prospective registry: IRCT20090117001556N145, https://irct.ir/trial/65247
Coverage: Original author abstract, visible trial design, Hamilton results paragraph, and registry comparison. Complete methods, arm tables and figures remain open.

At one Tehran hospital, the double-blind parallel trial compared routine Parkinson therapy plus saffron capsules and routine therapy plus placebo. The saffron capsule dose was 15 mg twice daily during eight weeks. There were 25 participants in the saffron group and 27 in the placebo group. The Hamilton depression interaction favored saffron in the published analysis (F=8.24, p=.006). The available original results paragraph also gives p=.045 for the week-eight between-group level comparison. The accessible abstract reports an inconclusive MDS-UPDRS Part III total (F=.53, p=.424), inconclusive GDS-30 and individual depressed-mood item, and an inconclusive Hoehn and Yahr comparison.

The official registry names the Hamilton scale and Part III total and leaves its secondary outcome list blank. No individually reported motor score for rigidity, voice or facial expression was found in the available material. The publication and registry are one Tehran family, distinct from the later Isfahan whole-powder tablet study. The available methods do not resolve whether the capsules contained ground stigma or an extract. The Hamilton change should be read alongside the other depression measures and the unresolved capsule formulation.
