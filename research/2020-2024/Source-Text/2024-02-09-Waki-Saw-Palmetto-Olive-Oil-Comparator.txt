Olive oil capsules as comparator in a saw palmetto trial
Source: https://ffhdj.com/index.php/ffhd/article/view/1312
DOI: 10.31989/ffhd.v14i2.1312
Registration: UMIN000046386
Coverage: Original paper and official registry comparator discrepancy

Waki and colleagues assigned 54 men to a phytosterol-enriched saw palmetto extract or a capsule described in the publication as olive oil. The longitudinal IPSS and OABSS composite results favored the active extract, and urine volume and flow comparisons were inconclusive. The report acknowledges that the comparator may influence symptom scores. Its cited UMIN registry instead lists polyethylene glycol-400 as placebo. There is no isolated extra-virgin oil effect estimate, no oil grade, and no Parkinson cohort.

The full article reports 27 men analyzed per arm. IPSS and OABSS group comparisons are reported as p < .01 and p < .05. CLSS and objective urine volume, maximum flow and average flow comparisons were inconclusive. The official register names residual urine and salivary DHEA-S. The paper reports other saliva outcomes and gives no residual urine or DHEA-S result. It provides no separate urgency or void-frequency treatment estimate. The published comparator claim remains uncertain because the registry lists a different formulation.
