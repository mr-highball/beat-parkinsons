Title: Sumac fruit powder and depressive symptoms during calorie restriction
Source: https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.6737
DOI: 10.1002/ptr.6737
Publication date: 2020
Date basis: Dated published trial.
Coverage: Authenticated author-posted original, including comparison Table 4, checked against the official citation.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26

Table 4 gives mean BDI-II change −10.41 (SD 11.08) with sumac fruit powder and −9.74 (SD 8.50) with placebo, between-group p=.78. Both groups followed calorie restriction. The trial does not establish clinical equivalence or estimate Parkinson-specific depression.

The randomized study reports 62 participants with an intention-to-treat analysis and 57 completing. The exposure was 3 g/day sumac fruit powder. Table 1 gives two placebo baseline entries. The comparison above uses the Table 4 placebo baseline 26.38 (SD 10.92), the value in the outcome table.
