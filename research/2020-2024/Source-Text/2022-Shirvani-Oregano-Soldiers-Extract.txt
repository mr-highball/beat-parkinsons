Title: Oregano (Origanum vulgare) Consumption Reduces Oxidative Stress and Markers of Muscle Damage after Combat Readiness Tests in Soldiers
Authors: Hossein Shirvani, Behzad Bazgir, Alireza Shamsoddini and colleagues
Journal: Nutrients 15(1), 137; first published 28 December 2022
PMID 36615794; PMCID PMC9823977; DOI 10.3390/nu15010137
Original full text: https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/15/1/137
Coverage: original Methods sections 2.1–2.6 and Results 3; first-public date and article identity confirmed at publisher.

Twenty-four healthy male Iranian soldiers were randomized to two equal groups of 12. Origanum vulgare collected in the mountains near Saqez was dried at room temperature for ten days and then oven-dried 48 hours at 32°C, ground in mortar and encapsulated at 500 mg. The part of plant used is not explicitly identified in section 2.1: it should not be described specifically as leaves. Placebo was 500 mg starch. A sample of 50 g underwent aqueous distillation for separate GC-MS chemical profiling; that distilled oil was not the stated capsule intervention. Powder or starch was given immediately after the second blood draw, following the army combat readiness test. Blood was drawn pre-exercise, immediately post-exercise and 60 and 120 minutes post-test. Neither anxious symptoms nor clinical fatigue scales are included in reported outcome methods.

Creatine kinase group-by-time interaction was p<.0001; post-hoc between-group tests at 60 and 120 min were each p<.0001, in the direction of higher CK in placebo; LDH group-by-time p<.0001. These are exercise biomarker outcomes, not self-reported anxiety or fatigue. Baseline CK comparison p=.83, LDH p=.18. Healthy soldiers differ from a Parkinson cohort, the plant-part definition and ordinary meal use are uncertain, and no measured anxiety direction is available.
