# Food experiment evidence for 2020–2024

## Marjoram tea and motor and nonmotor symptoms

Chbili and colleagues reported a 30-day randomized placebo-controlled pilot in August 2021. Sixty people with idiopathic Parkinson’s enrolled and 51 completed the study. The infusion was made from dried marjoram leaves and strained. In the tea arm, mean UPDRS Part III scores fell from 18.76 to 16.52 (within-arm p = .069). The paper describes .07 alongside that result and concludes there was no motor improvement, so a firm motor direction requires the analysis details. The tea arm’s Non-Motor Symptoms Scale total and Beck Depression Inventory scores fell (within-arm p < .0001 for each). The accessible material does not give separate controlled estimates for the frozen symptoms or compare consumption of the fresh or dried leaves themselves. The 2025 antioxidant pilot reports different methods and a later cohort. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34455218/) · [Publisher preview and preparation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1353802021003072) · [Later trial](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#marjoram-tea-and-oxidative-stress-biomarkers)

## Marjoram tea biomarker trial registration

PACTR202205801626909 was registered May 12, 2022. The primary outcome was blood superoxide dismutase after 30 days. Secondary outcomes were catalase, carbonylated proteins and uric acid. The comparator used processed, depleted *Origanum syriacum* stems. The registry lists an actual start of May 9, 2022, actual last follow-up of June 1, 2022, 55 as actual target enrollment, and status Recruiting with a blank publication field. A linked 2025 paper reports different conduct dates. [Official registration](https://pactr.samrc.ac.za/TrialDisplay.aspx?TrialID=23704) · [Published results and discrepancy](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#marjoram-tea-and-oxidative-stress-biomarkers)

## Bay leaf infusion registrations and lipid outcomes

Two Sousse registrations from May 2022 test ten days of bay leaf (*Laurus nobilis*) tea in healthy volunteers. PACTR202205671550114 lists the LDL receptor as its primary outcome. PACTR202205585377035 lists the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio. Chbili and colleagues’ April 4, 2024 lipid report analyzed 55 of 62 participants, with 30 assigned to bay leaf infusion and 25 to placebo among completers. The paper reports LDL cholesterol and LDL receptor differences after treatment. The report is one component of the same project as the 2026 stress and anxiety publication. The protocols give no ground-leaf exposure, Parkinson population or swallowing or drooling endpoint. [Lipid registration](https://pactr.samrc.ac.za/TrialDisplay.aspx?TrialID=23695) · [Inflammation registration](https://pactr.samrc.ac.za/TrialDisplay.aspx?TrialID=23718) · [2024 primary article](https://najfnr.com/home/article/view/415) · [2026 project companion](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#bay-leaf-infusion-and-stress-in-healthy-adults)

## Earlier Sousse bay leaf infusion reports

The March 26, 2020 lipid paper reports a ten-day pre-post comparison in 30 healthy adults aged 20–57. HDL cholesterol increased (p = .01), and LDL cholesterol and triglycerides had no clear change. The May 15, 2020 Research Square preprint (rs-28846/v1) also describes 30 people in one intervention arm and reports LDL receptor and oxidation data. The May 28 v2 PDF consists of a withdrawal notice. The 2024 randomized paper identifies v1 as its preprint deposit. The LDL receptor values and baseline superoxide dismutase in the preprint closely match its intervention arm, despite differing reported study dates and design. Treat this as overlapping evidence. The 2022 anxiety paper also reports a ten-day pre-post comparison in 30 healthy adults aged 20–57, with lower stress, anxiety and plasma cortisol. Its participant overlap with the lipid reports remains unverified. PubMed’s DOI for the 2022 anxiety paper leads to a different article, so use the PMID and title to identify it. [March 2020 lipid report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32213118/) · [May 2020 preprint v1](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-28846/v1/73ccb30d-bcc0-460d-a64c-1e96045cb3cc.pdf?c=1631834665) · [May 2020 v2 withdrawal](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-28846/v2_covered.pdf?c=1631834987) · [2022 anxiety report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36881915/) · [2024 report, Table 2 and p. 95](https://www.najfnr.com/home/article/download/415/277/2738)

## Nanomicelle curcumin as an add-on

Ghodsi and colleagues’ pilot randomized 60 people with controlled idiopathic Parkinson’s symptoms to 80 mg/day nanomicelle curcumin or placebo alongside their dopaminergic therapy for nine months. The original IRCT20171123037600N1 registration names the SinaCurcumin formulation and lists MDS-UPDRS at baseline and three, six and nine months. Its secondary outcomes field is empty. The May 18, 2022 report also describes assessing PDQ-39 and adverse effects. The overall MDS-UPDRS Part III group-by-time comparison gave p = .04, while post hoc comparisons at individual visits gave no clear differences. The report abstract gives p = .25 for nausea and vomiting and p = .42 for reflux. The available results concern the formulated compound and aggregate clinical scales. The published full-text tables were inaccessible. [Trial abstract, PMID 35636380](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35636380/) · [Original registration](https://irct.ir/trial/28424)

## Phospholipid curcumin and composite outcomes

Donadio and colleagues first published on May 13, 2022. The one-year nonrandomized study analyzed 19 people who took a phospholipid curcuminoid formulation (Maxicur containing Meriva, standardized to 18–22% curcuminoids) and 14 untreated participants who opted for adjustment of usual medication. The Methods describe 21 people initially choosing supplementation and 19 choosing the comparison strategy. Table 1 gives the analyzed counts. The supplement group kept dopaminergic doses stable. Table 2 gives mean changes of +5 versus +11 points for the off-medication MDS-UPDRS III examination, −0.5 versus +6 points for COMPASS-31, and −2 versus +8 points for the Non-Motor Symptoms Scale. The p-values in that table compare each group with its own baseline. OFF identifies the medication state at the motor examination. The article supplies no duration of daily off time (`0506`) and no individual outcomes for rigidity (`0359`) or bradykinesia (`0507`). [Primary article, PMID 35556131](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9210322/) · [University of Bologna publisher PDF, Tables 1–2](https://cris.unibo.it/bitstream/11585/897697/2/nlac034.pdf#page=4)

## Capsaicin wafer and swallowing

A small randomized crossover trial evaluated a capsaicin wafer in people with neurodegenerative diseases. It reported a swallowing-timing signal in the ALS subgroup and did not find that signal in the Parkinson’s subgroup. The preparation and measured outcomes differ from consuming ground cayenne and reporting swallowing difficulty severity. [Higashi et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.18926/amo/63412)

## Habanero extract in a Parkinson swallowing crossover

Abreu’s 2019 University of São Paulo dissertation studied 13 orally fed participants with Parkinson’s, with or without a dysphagia complaint, in a crossover with two visits seven days apart. Participants took their usual levodopa before imaging. The test bolus was a 5 mL spoonful of a homogeneous paste made from barium sulfate, Thick&Easy thickener and water. One drop of the placebo or a capsaicin-containing extract from EMBRAPA Habanero (*Capsicum chinense* CNPH 15.192) was added to that spoonful. The reported 2,612 µg/g figure describes the extract drop, and the thesis does not specify the final capsaicin concentration in the bolus. [Primary dissertation, printed pp. 27–30](https://teses.usp.br/teses/disponiveis/17/17151/tde-27052020-075810/publico/LuanaOliveiraAbreu.pdf)

Table 9 reports capsaicin versus placebo means of 318 versus 324 ms for pharyngeal onset (p = .53), 179 versus 461 ms for laryngeal vestibule closure onset (p = .62), 487 versus 525 ms for closure duration (p = .38), and 301 versus 310 ms for upper esophageal sphincter opening duration (p = .34). The placebo SD for laryngeal closure onset was 908 ms. These acute physiological comparisons leave ground cayenne and swallowing difficulty severity (`0724`) unresolved. The thesis names ethics process HCRP 14755/2014, with a February 2015 approval letter in Appendix A. Searches by investigator, title and ethics number located no public trial registration or journal or conference outcome report. The repository metadata gives a December 12, 2019 defense and a July 9, 2020 public harvest timestamp matching the DOI registration date. The cohort and bolus are distinct from Higashi’s wafer crossover. [Primary dissertation, Table 9 and Appendix A](https://teses.usp.br/teses/disponiveis/17/17151/tde-27052020-075810/publico/LuanaOliveiraAbreu.pdf) · [Repository metadata](https://bdtd.ibict.br/vufind/Record/USP_2c1cd7bf2b07159fdc0281f44ff05775/Details) · [DOI deposit metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.11606/d.17.2019.tde-27052020-075810)

## Spices in odor identification tests

A study compared people with Parkinson’s and healthy controls on recognition of odor stimuli, including common spices and herbs. It measured performance on an olfactory test. Identification of pepper, sage, caraway, cinnamon, cardamom, mint or marjoram is not evidence about consuming the ingredient. [Study report](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/2272691)

## Cinnamon odor identification in the Medellín clinic

Agudelo Uribe and colleagues’ 2024 paper compared 29 neurology patients with Parkinson’s and 76 without it. In Table 4, 4 of 29 and 37 of 76 correctly identified the cinnamon odor (13.8% and 48.7%, reported p = 0.001). Coffee identification was 6 of 29 and 49 of 76. Clove identification was 3 of 29 and 22 of 76 (reported p = 0.07). The groups differed in age. The investigators presented odors from packaged products as stimuli and recorded free responses. The comparison gives no ingestion result for ground cinnamon and olfactory impairment (`0979`). The thesis is dated 2023 and its repository metadata records availability in June 2024. Its title and abstract describe the same clinic study, so the thesis and article count as one cohort. [Published study, Table 4 and Figure 3](https://www.medigraphic.com/pdfs/revneuneupsi/nnp-2024/nnp241b.pdf#page=5) · [Thesis metadata](https://redcol.minciencias.gov.co/Record/UDEA2_ed4bdc07ecf3fc430a9234c98df9c9c8/Details)

## Sesame oil as a trial carrier

A randomized trial compared cannabinoid extract in a sesame-oil vehicle with a sesame-oil placebo in people with Parkinson’s. Sesame oil was part of both arms, so the between-group comparison cannot estimate its independent effect or address the experiment’s oil processing choices. [Trial report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11102313/)

## Sesame oil in an experimental model

A rat study of manganese-induced parkinsonism examined locomotor and rotarod outcomes after sesame oil exposure. Those model outcomes cannot establish a change in fall frequency in humans with Parkinson’s who consume toasted sesame oil. [Study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38548024/)

## Edible oil categories and Parkinson’s

A case-control analysis evaluated reported oil and mayonnaise use in people with Parkinson’s and controls. The investigators reported associations with broad oil categories and the odds of a Parkinson’s diagnosis. The comparisons do not establish a symptom effect of an individual oil or its processing method. [Study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37997257/)

## Saffron capsules and depressive symptoms

A double-blind add-on trial at Roozbeh Hospital in Tehran included 52 participants with Parkinson’s and depression. It compared 15 mg saffron capsules twice daily with placebo during standard treatment for eight weeks. Change on the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale favored saffron (time-by-treatment p = 0.006). The Geriatric Depression Scale, MDS-UPDRS Part I depressed mood item, and motor examination did not show clear between-group differences. The public report gives no fatigue-specific result. The acknowledgments identify Green Plant Life as the study medication supplier. The available report and registry do not specify the capsule brand or ground stigma versus extract. The full methods and tables were unavailable during review. The capsule regimen leaves the effect of ground saffron in meals open. [Lalezari et al. 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2210803324001258) · [Separate Isfahan trial](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#saffron-powder-and-depressive-symptoms)
