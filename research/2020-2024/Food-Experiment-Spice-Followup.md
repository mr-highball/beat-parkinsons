# Spice evidence follow-up, 2020–2024

## Gardenia-derived crocin and depression-like behavior in mice

Tang and colleagues published on June 3, 2020. They selected MPTP mice with prolonged forced-swim immobility and administered isolated gardenia-derived crocin intragastrically. Figure 1 reports higher sucrose preference and less forced-swim and tail-suspension immobility after crocin, with no detected differences in open-field movement or novelty-suppressed feeding. The Methods and Figure 1 give different doses, and the abstract gives a different treatment duration from both. The experiment supplies an animal behavior result for an isolated constituent also present in saffron. The human whole-saffron trials provide the clinical evidence for `0063`. [Publisher record, PMID 32495180](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12035-020-01941-2) [Author-uploaded primary report, Figure 1 and Discussion](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/373192674_Crocin_Reverses_Depression-Like_Behavior_in_Parkinson_Disease_Mice_via_VTA-mPFC_Pathway)


## Injected curcumin and rotarod performance in rats

Fikry and colleagues first published on January 23, 2022. In rotenone-exposed rats, intraperitoneal curcumin increased mean rotarod retention from 15.80 ± 0.69 to 46.75 ± 1.03 seconds (mean ± SEM, p < .0001, ten rats per group). The vehicle control averaged 87.85 seconds. This partial improvement on a balance and coordination task gives no dietary turmeric estimate for rigidity or bradykinesia. [Primary report, PMID 35068069](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8981438/) [Local Figure 1 text](Source-Text/PMC8981438.txt#L167)

## Heated ginger extract in a mouse Parkinson model

Huh and colleagues first published on June 20, 2023. In a mouse model induced with *Proteus mirabilis*, the researchers administered an ethanol extract of heated, dried ginger or isolated 6-shogaol in separate oral groups. Both groups had shorter pole descent times than the model controls. The ginger extract group also improved open-field distance and rotarod retention. The report measures intestinal barrier proteins and brain pathology. These outcomes concern a concentrated extract in a mouse model and leave the clinical effects of grated ginger on constipation or rigidity unresolved. [Primary report, Methods and Figure 1, PMID 37337830](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10315336/)

## Saffron extract with butyrate and vitamin D in a Parkinson chart review

Alexoudi and colleagues published on November 23, 2023. In an uncontrolled chart review of patients with Parkinson disease and chronic constipation, 29 of 32 included patients completed three months of a combination containing saffron extract, butyrate triglyceride, and vitamin D3. Reported weekly stool frequency rose from 3.76 to 5.41 (p = .001) and the mean UPDRS Part III score changed from 35.72 to 32.97 (p = .005). The study gives no saffron-only or separate rigidity, insomnia, fatigue, falls, or depression estimate. The paper cites a saffron-versus-imipramine depression trial when discussing Parkinson depression, but that trial enrolled people with major depression and did not identify a Parkinson cohort. [Primary report, PMID 38146566](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10749423/) [Local Results and Table 1](Source-Text/PMC10749423.txt#L113) [Cited depression trial, PMID 15341662](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15341662/)

## Isolated piperine and bowel outcomes in 6-OHDA rats

Yu and colleagues first published online in December 2023. Their rat experiment reports better gastrointestinal function, including fecal water content, and reduced apomorphine-induced rotations after isolated piperine. The paper describes piperine as an alkaloid of long pepper and assesses brain and bowel tissue, microbiota, and autophagy. The experiment gives related preclinical bowel evidence for `0227` and no culinary black pepper constipation or swallowing outcome in people. [Primary abstract, PMID 38158101](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38158101/) [Publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378874123014988)

## Intranasal curcumin formulations in MPTP mice

Lei and colleagues first published on June 25, 2024. Groups of six MPTP mice received isolated curcumin intranasally, either unencapsulated or in one of two nanoparticle formulations. The results emphasize the coated particle group on rotarod, pole, and forepaw suspension tasks. The text does not give separate outcome estimates for unencapsulated curcumin. The experiment addresses delivery and movement in mice and leaves oral culinary turmeric and clinical rigidity unresolved. [Primary report, Figure 2, PMID 38918856](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11197265/) [Local full text](Source-Text/PMC11197265.txt#L131)

## Injected curcumin and mouse movement and memory assays

Wang and colleagues first published on June 25, 2024. They assigned 72 male C57BL/6J mice to saline control, MPTP or MPTP plus curcumin, with 24 animals per group and eight per group in each behavioral test. Table 1 gives mean pole descent times of 6.29 versus 8.38 seconds, rotarod retention of 59.13 versus 34.75 seconds, and Y-maze alternation of 57.85% versus 47.21% for curcumin versus MPTP alone. The study reports p < .05 for the pairwise comparisons. It tested intraperitoneal isolated curcumin following MPTP. Pole descent and rotarod are movement proxies, and Y-maze alternation concerns working memory. These measures leave the effects of culinary turmeric on bradykinesia and attention unresolved. [Primary publisher PDF, Methods and Table 1](https://jpmed.qdu.edu.cn/CN/PDF/10.13362/j.jpmed.202403015#page=3) · [Publisher metadata](https://jpmed.qdu.edu.cn/CN/10.13362/j.jpmed.202403015)

## Oral piperine and odor-guided behavior in transgenic mice

Li and colleagues published the final article online August 25, 2021. Six-month-old male Thy1-SNCA transgenic mice received oral isolated piperine (25, 50 or 100 mg/kg for six weeks). The buried-pellet task used sweetened corn, and the investigators also tested exposed pellets and sucrose preference. Figure 1 shows a shorter search latency for buried food and a changed response to attraction and irritation odor stimuli after piperine. The legend reports n = 10–19 for the initial assays and n = 5–10 for the later-age buried-food experiment. The authors also report better pole and rotarod performance in the longer experiment with 100 mg/kg. They discuss piperine as a constituent of long pepper. This is a model olfaction result related to `0260`, which asks about ground black pepper in people. [Primary full report, Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9037522/) · [Publisher PDF and figures](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/15548627.2021.1937897)

## Isolated piperine with levodopa in a 6-OHDA model

Hu and colleagues first published August 1, 2023. Six weeks of levodopa plus isolated piperine increased levodopa plasma AUC over 10 hours by 17.59% and striatal levodopa concentration by 2.43-fold compared with levodopa alone in lesioned rats. Figure 2 reports that the combined group had lower total abnormal involuntary movement scores than the levodopa group in the experiment. The scoring included axial dystonia, reverse rotation, limb and masticatory movements, but Figure 2E reports the total score. The pharmacokinetic comparison is related to `0129` and the movement composite to `0486`. Neither comparison measures daily off time or a separate dystonia result after cracked black pepper in people. [Local primary report, Methods and Figures 2–3](Source-Text/PMC10848080.txt#L137) · [Publisher article](https://doi.org/10.1111/cns.14383)

## Plasma piperine and curcumin in the PPMI cohort

Xia et al. reported a matched PPMI analysis of 640 participants at the July 24–28, 2023 Aligning Science Across Parkinson’s meeting. The Parkinson group had 32% lower piperine (nominal p = .0007, adjusted p = .22), 9% higher curcumin (nominal p = .04, adjusted p = 1.00), and a 36% lower piperine-to-curcumin ratio (nominal p = .0001, adjusted p = .032). The 2024 MDS report gives matching numbers and investigators from the same cohort. The comparisons use plasma measurements and disease status, and the reports supply no black-pepper or turmeric intake, preparation, or symptom outcome. [Original July 2023 proceedings, printed page 56](https://parkinsonsroadmap.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/ASAP-Collaborative-Meeting-Series_San-Diego_1-31-23.docx.pdf#page=56) · [2024 congress abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/lower-plasma-piperine-levels-in-parkinsons-disease-a-ppmi-metabolomics-analysis/) · [Later PPMI symptom analysis](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#plasma-piperine-and-motor-scores-in-ppmi).

## Cadi Ayyad curcumin conference lead

The August 12–13, 2023 Pakistan Neuroscience Society conference abstract by Oukhrib and colleagues describes an evaluation of preventive and therapeutic effects of isolated curcumin in Parkinson animal models and mentions a clinical study. It gives no clinical eligibility criteria, enrollment, preparation, registration, or results. The same group's June 2023 reserpine study tests graphene oxide nanoparticles. Its 2025 doctoral defense announcement specifies an animal model, leaving the clinical study mentioned in the 2023 abstract unresolved. Searches by the authors, institution and title in English, French and Arabic yielded no identifiable clinical registration or results report. [Conference abstract, PASTIC record 158551](https://psa.pastic.gov.pk/article/158551/evaluation-of-the-preventive-and-curative-effects-of-curcumin-on) · [Graphene oxide animal report, PMID 37356558](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37356558/) · [Later thesis announcement](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Spice-Followup.md#cadi-ayyad-curcumin-thesis-announcement)

## Injected thymol and balance tasks in 6-OHDA rats

Nourmohammadi and colleagues first published February 10, 2022. In Figure 8, injected isolated thymol produced no clear increase in rotarod retention time. The authors report greater travel distance at one dose in the shorter rotarod condition (p < .05) and no clear distance difference in the longer condition. They also report improvements in several other movement tasks. The methods give conflicting dose lists. These results concern injected thymol and laboratory motor tasks, which are related to clinical postural instability (`0421`). No dried thyme or human postural-instability outcome was tested. [Primary report, PMID 35144603](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8832724/) · [Licensed results text, Figure 8](Source-Text/PMC8832724.txt#L227)

## Isolated carvacrol in later rodent models

Akan et al. first published on July 15, 2023. Their 6-OHDA rat experiment reports nigrostriatal tissue and channel expression outcomes after isolated carvacrol. Shah et al. first published April 2024. In rotenone-exposed mice, the journal abstract reports better motor performance after oral isolated carvacrol and no separate estimate for stride length, dystonia or levodopa off time. [2023 primary abstract, PMID 37452911](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37452911/) · [2024 primary abstract, PMID 38649135](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38649135/)
