# Seafood Exposure Speech

## Methylmercury exposure and dysarthria in a 2023 preprint

The 2023 preprint presents the same analysis as the later journal report, including 197 historically methylmercury-exposed residents and 130 control-area residents. Table 5 reports examiner-detected dysarthria in 24.9% and 1.6%, respectively. The exposure was historically contaminated seafood. Species, cooking method, formal intelligibility scores and an analysis in Parkinson disease are unavailable. ## Dysarthria after historical methylmercury exposure

The 2023 journal publication reports the same 327-person comparison as the preprint and identifies the study as a reanalysis of earlier survey participants. Table 5 lists examiner-rated dysarthria in 24.9% of the exposed residents versus 1.6% of controls. The survey also lists participant-reported difficulty speaking in a separate table. Industrial contamination and historical geography define the exposure groups. The study gives no species-specific seafood exposure, grilled sardine comparison or Parkinson speech result. It is distinct from the Bordeaux contaminated-sardine botulism outbreak. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10748001/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2023-Methylmercury-Speech-Journal-Extract.txt)

The journal Methods identifies the same 197 exposed residents as in the 2007 survey. It selected 130 control-area residents aged at least 50 from the earlier pool and retained some with neurological complications. These changes affect comparability.
