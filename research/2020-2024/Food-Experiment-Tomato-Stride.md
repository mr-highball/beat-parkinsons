# Tomato and stride follow-up

## Gait measures in the Nutri-Park dietary assessment

In Gatti and colleagues' 2020 cross-sectional study, 66 Parkinson outpatients and 58 controls completed dietary assessment and clinical testing. Table 2 reports a six-metre-course measure labeled stride length, mean 0.48 m (SD 0.13) versus 0.56 (0.12), p = .001. It also reports 13.49 versus 11.17 steps and gait speed 0.93 versus 1.06 m/s. The methods do not define how the authors obtained the length. The reported step counts and distances are compatible with average step length. The dietary variables and example protein redistribution menu do not connect individual cooked tomato intake with the gait measures. ## Dietary lycopene and gait speed in Framingham adults

Sahni and colleagues, online 2020 and print 2021. In the Framingham Offspring community cohort, a 10 mg/day higher food-frequency-questionnaire-derived lycopene estimate was associated with 0.0043 m/s/year greater change in gait speed after adjustment (SE 0.0010, p < .01, Table 3). Walking speed was the outcome and the source of lycopene was not separated into food items or cooking techniques. The paper supplies no Parkinson-specific or stride-length estimate. Its validation cohort did not provide an independent lycopene comparison, since that food-frequency analysis did not have the individual lycopene variable.
