# Red cabbage and orthostatic symptoms

## 2021 MAXVEG ambulatory pressure

Both active 12-week arms of the diabetes trial included red cabbage alongside other vegetables. The bitter/strong diet had lower 24-hour diastolic pressure change than habitual diet, with inconclusive systolic differences across the three arms. The methods and Table 4 identify a 12-week assessment. Section 3.3 says 12 months and disagrees with Table 4 about within-group diastolic significance. The assigned diets leave red-cabbage intake and preparation unresolved, and the reported pressure outcome has no standing-symptom measure. [Original text and Table 4](Source-Text/2021-Thorup-MAXVEG-Pressure-Extract.txt) · [2011 registration](../2010-2014/Source-Text/Trials/NCT01397942.txt)

## 2022 Fermented and pickled mixed vegetables

The study reports separate six-week pressures for fermented cabbage or cucumber, pickled cabbage or cucumber, and usual diet. The endpoint comparison across arms had p=.093, while p=.037 describes the within-control systolic change. The official registry lists the same median pressures with different denominators in the two food arms. No standing measurement or postural symptom is reported. [Original text and Table 3](Source-Text/2022-Galena-Fermented-Vegetables-Pressure-Extract.txt) · [Posted registration results](../2015-2019/Source-Text/Trials/NCT03407794.txt)

## 2024 VESSEL mixed soup

The crossover used boiled and blended vegetables. The adjusted ambulatory systolic pressure difference favored the mixed cruciferous soup by 2.5 mmHg. The original gives no cabbage cultivar and does not report standing symptoms. The 2025 glycaemic report is a companion analysis from the same trial. [Original text and Table 2](Source-Text/2024-Connolly-VESSEL-Pressure-Extract.txt) · [2019 registration](../2015-2019/Source-Text/Trials/ACTRN12619001294145.txt) · [2025 companion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232361/)
