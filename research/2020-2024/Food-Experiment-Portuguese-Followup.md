# Portuguese-language dietary studies, 2020–2024

## Food frequency and Parkinson’s stage in Belém

Fernandez and colleagues surveyed 40 Parkinson’s outpatients at Hospital Ophir Loyola in 2019. The article appeared in the August 2021 issue and entered SciELO on September 20, 2021. The questionnaire separately listed pineapple, kiwi, banana, watermelon, orange, tangerine, acerola, chia, flaxseed, several nuts, oats, brown rice, olive oil and fish. Tables 2 and 3 respectively give Hoehn and Yahr stage by nutritional status and food frequency for the pooled cohort. The published results leave individual food frequencies by stage or symptom unresolved. ## Reported food relief and worsening in Brasília

Bruna Caroline Pereira Santos recruited through Brazilian Parkinson’s social media groups for a descriptive UniCEUB study. Fifteen adults who reported a Parkinson’s diagnosis and treatment completed the study. The thesis was presented July 5, 2021 and the repository lists public availability September 21, 2021. The questionnaire and April interviews asked about diet, medication timing, and perceived relief or worsening after foods. [Repository record, dates](https://repositorio.uniceub.br/items/edcc369e-b91b-45c4-a273-3330e5c7cbc9/full) · [Primary thesis, Methods and Appendix A](https://repositorio.uniceub.br/bitstreams/8162b242-d2ab-4efe-8c0e-3fece4267208/download)

Five of 15 reported relief after a food. Page 13 names ginger and `açafrão` in connection with body aches, and granola with intestinal relief. The author says respondents attributed this relief to other sensations beyond direct Parkinson’s symptoms. The report does not distinguish saffron from turmeric, specify whether ginger and `açafrão` were taken together, give preparation or dose, count responses per ingredient, or measure pain severity or cause. The findings are human self-report leads for `0725` (grated ginger and pain severity) and `0812` (ground turmeric and pain severity). [Primary thesis, p. 13](https://repositorio.uniceub.br/bitstreams/8162b242-d2ab-4efe-8c0e-3fece4267208/download#page=13)

Figure 3 shows eight reports for red meat, three for milk, two for gluten and two for sugar under perceived worsening. It does not identify the particular symptom or food preparation for each report. A separate dairy question on p. 13 gives four of nine dairy consumers reporting worse digestion. The Discussion gives five of nine for general bodily and digestive worsening. Those are differently described counts from the same thesis. Figure 2 gives seven of 15 reporting constipation and one of 15 reporting choking, without linking them to individual foods. [Primary thesis, pp. 13–14 and Discussion](https://repositorio.uniceub.br/bitstreams/8162b242-d2ab-4efe-8c0e-3fece4267208/download#page=13)

## Food patterns and clinical measures in Recife

Silva’s UFPE dissertation was defended February 17, 2022 and entered the public repository July 28, 2022. The study enrolled 79 people with Parkinson’s at two Recife sites between January and June 2019. It collected a 90-item food frequency questionnaire, Hoehn and Yahr stage, and UPDRS II and III. Table 4 lists foods by group. The questionnaire distinguishes sweet potato and white potato, several beans, many individual fruits, coconut water, and some cooking methods for meat. Table 5 gives prudent-pattern scores of −0.22 in mild disease and 0.21 in moderate or severe disease (p = .056). Table 6 associates the traditional Brazilian pattern with lower odds of excess body fat after adjustment (OR 0.54, 95% CI 0.30–0.97). Tables 4–6 give food-group loadings, pattern comparisons and body fat estimates. They leave individual-food associations with UPDRS items or other symptoms unresolved. Appendix B gives the clinical instrument and Annex A gives the dietary questionnaire. [Repository record, public availability](https://repositorio.ufpe.br/handle/123456789/45291?mode=full) · [Dissertation, Tables 4–6 and Annex A](https://repositorio.ufpe.br/bitstream/123456789/45291/1/DISSERTA%C3%87%C3%83O%20Julianne%20Cibele%20Rodrigues%20da%20Silva.pdf) · [OpenAlex W7120900008](https://openalex.org/W7120900008)

Bezerra and colleagues published another analysis of this cohort on October 7, 2022. The authors report the same recruitment sites and dates, sample count and demographics, and ethics protocol 98691118.2.0000.8807. Its figures show item-level daily consumption by sex. Table 1 compares composite food scores across Hoehn and Yahr stages, with p = .588 for each score. The published results give no estimate for individual foods against symptoms. [Companion article, Table 1 and Figures 1–2](https://scielo.br/j/rn/a/9YJHynP3vGS5NDRzYvWwQWs/?lang=en) · [Local article text](Source-Text/2022-Bezerra-Recife-Parkinson-Food-Frequency.txt) · [OpenAlex W4312755632](https://openalex.org/W4312755632)

The 2019 Silva cohort differs from [Arruda’s 2021 single-clinic cohort](https://repositorio.ufpe.br/handle/123456789/45662) by enrollment dates, number of sites, age criteria and sample count. Both recruited from the UFPE outpatient program. Whether any individual enrolled in both studies is unresolved.

## Food patterns and meal preparation in Botucatu

Komuro’s UNESP dissertation was defended February 16, 2023. The repository lists an April 10, 2023 availability date for the item, a 24-month embargo from defense, and separate partial and complete PDFs. The public partial version contains the abstract and overview. The complete version, consulted for the tables below, carries the same dissertation date. [Repository metadata and embargo](https://repositorio.unesp.br/entities/publication/6b75b739-e05d-4fda-8821-357c2d38c895/full) · [Partial dissertation](https://repositorio.unesp.br/server/api/core/bitstreams/28bd0e61-d028-46af-b28e-9c65e261a761/content) · [Complete dissertation](https://repositorio.unesp.br/server/api/core/bitstreams/0b0f13a5-3845-46c6-8538-069afb386a51/content) · [OpenAlex W7120508956](https://openalex.org/W7120508956)

Eighty movement-clinic patients completed the 71-item food frequency questionnaire and 68 also had anthropometry. Article 2, Table 1, reports food-item loadings for four patterns. Its questionnaire separates sweetened and unsweetened coffee, olive oil, and some cooked and fried preparations. UPDRS appears in the sample description. Article 3, Table 1, links the soft-food pattern to dyslipidemia (p = .0092). The text describes food preparation difficulties and proposes that disease limitations influence food choice. The tables give no test of individual foods improving or worsening a symptom and no food-by-UPDRS estimate.

## Caregiver dietary education in Amapá

Rocha and colleagues described two dietary education sessions with 14 and 30 caregivers of people with Parkinson’s or Alzheimer’s. The November 2023 congress chapter reports caregiver knowledge, household meal preparation, and qualitative responses. It contains no patient symptom measurement or Parkinson’s-only food exposure comparison.
