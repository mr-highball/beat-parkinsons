# Paprika composition, measured wet voice and treadmill step-length adaptation

## Capsaicinoid amounts differ across paprika flavor grades

Arrizabalaga-Larrañaga and colleagues measured capsaicinoids and carotenoids in 136 commercial paprika samples, covering sweet, hot and bittersweet types. In supplementary Table S2, La Vera samples had mean total capsaicinoids of 9 mg/kg for sweet, 31 mg/kg for bittersweet and 942 mg/kg for hot, with substantial dispersion. An independent product survey confirms smoke-drying of La Vera paprika. These are measurements of the spice itself, not evidence about effects after eating it. ## Wet voice during a post-stroke capsaicin inhalation case

After one week, endoscopy found silent aspiration unchanged for IDDSI Level 1 liquid and a better secretion rating. The subsequent endoscopy followed added swallowing therapy and tested other consistencies.

Pekacka-Egli and colleagues recorded clinical observations each session during nebulized capsaicin treatment for post-stroke dysphagia. Table 1 includes pre- and post-treatment voice quality. Wet voice improved or cleared in some observed episodes. Cough, throat clearing and secretions were the clinical target, and follow-up endoscopy after one week showed ongoing silent aspiration on the specified liquids. The uncontrolled record offers no spoken intelligibility comparison and used inhaled capsaicin in post-stroke care. ## Measured step-length asymmetry after capsaicin and heat

In a 2024 primary report, Jackson and colleagues used vision-guided treadmill step lengths to train a novel asymmetric walking pattern. Healthy young participants trained during localized capsaicin-plus-heat pain or no stimulus, with a distinct heat-only experiment. Baseline stepping and acquisition were comparable. The capsaicin-plus-heat group retained less of the learned asymmetry when tested the next day, while heat alone did not clearly reduce retention. This concerns retention of a deliberately asymmetric pattern and does not give a change in habitual stride length or a Parkinson outcome. ## Capsaicin as a cough stimulus during Parkinson rehabilitation

In Troche and colleagues’ randomized study, 65 participants with Parkinson disease received expiratory strength training or sensorimotor airway training. The sensorimotor training combined visual feedback, airflow coaching and a subthreshold inhaled capsaicin cue. Both groups also completed capsaicin provocation testing. Sensory urge and cough flow improved under the combined training. That contrast does not isolate an effect of capsaicin, and the tested outcomes concern reflex and voluntary cough rather than speech intelligibility. [Primary trial](https://www.nzbri.org/resources/publications/696/Troche_Movement-Disorders_2022.pdf)
