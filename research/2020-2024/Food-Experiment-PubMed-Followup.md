# Food experiment PubMed follow-up

## Yogurt as a diagnostic swallowing bolus

Suárez-Patiño and colleagues studied simultaneous surface electromyography and videofluoroscopy in 10 adults with dysphagia, six with Parkinson’s disease and four with multiple sclerosis. The study used 5 mL of yogurt as a thick test bolus alongside water and a cracker. Table 3 reports muscle timing during the yogurt swallow. The study relates muscle activation to bolus transit during a diagnostic procedure. It gives no strained-yogurt preparation or handwriting measurement for frozen claim `0832`. [Primary paper, PMID 36511672](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9814368/) · [Local full text](Source-Text/PMC9814368.txt)

## Commercial yogurt as a swallowing comparator

Oh and colleagues enrolled 30 people with Parkinson’s disease and 50 healthy controls. During videofluoroscopy with commercial yogurt, the Parkinson’s group showed longer pharyngeal delay and higher penetration–aspiration scores than controls. In the Parkinson’s group, a formulated swallowing supplement performed better than commercial yogurt on pharyngeal wall coating, pharyngeal transit time, and aspiration subscales. The accessible results concern bolus performance during a diagnostic exam. Frozen claim `0832` asks about the effect of strained yogurt on handwriting. The publisher’s change history records a 2025 correction to an author’s name and affiliation. [Primary paper, PMID 32980984](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10072-020-04730-w) · [2025 correction](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10072-025-08285-6)

## Leek and bean sprouts in a jejunal-tube phytobezoar

Ueno and colleagues reported a man with Parkinson’s disease on continuous intestinal levodopa whose jejunal tube had migrated and knotted. Endoscopy found a phytobezoar containing leek and bean sprouts. His postprandial abdominal pain resolved after tube removal, and his Parkinson’s symptoms had stayed stable during the episode. The case involves mixed vegetables, an unspecified preparation, and a device complication. Frozen claims `0661` and `0741` ask about rigidity after roasted leek and apathy after braised leek, respectively. [Primary case report, PMID 33867393](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8580754/)

## Yerba mate and odds of a Parkinson’s diagnosis

Sáenz-Farret and colleagues compared 215 people with Parkinson’s disease with 219 controls in Argentina, Paraguay, and Uruguay. In a retrospective questionnaire study, greater yerba-mate infusion concentration per serving was associated with lower odds of a Parkinson’s diagnosis (adjusted odds ratio 0.62, 95% CI 0.47 to 0.84). Daily volume and duration of use were similar between groups. Yerba mate (*Ilex paraguariensis*) is outside the frozen ingredient set. The endpoint was diagnosis odds. [Primary abstract, PMID 35849689](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35849689/)
