# Butylphthalide and composite neuropsychiatric symptoms in Alzheimer disease

Gu and colleagues reported this randomized comparison in 2024. Eighty-four patients with Alzheimer disease and behavioral or psychological symptoms were assigned equally to donepezil and risperidone with or without oral butylphthalide 0.2 g three times daily for three months. The article states that BEHAVE-AD includes hallucinations, but Table 2 reports only the combined scale: before and after scores were 11.21 ± 3.12 and 4.75 ± 2.35 with adjunct butylphthalide, versus 11.30 ± 2.87 and 6.06 ± 2.54 without it. The reported Chinese Neuropsychiatric Inventory scores are also composite. Table 3 reports mean adverse-symptom scale scores of 12.86 ± 3.20 versus 11.93 ± 3.19 at one month and 15.39 ± 4.24 versus 15.04 ± 4.13 at three months. The paper does not report hallucination items or visual-hallucination frequency separately, and its participants had Alzheimer disease, not Parkinson disease. The pharmaceutical exposure is not dried celery leaf. [Original Chinese paper, Methods and Tables 2–3](https://js.dnzh.org/FileUpload/PdfFile/20241015160030.pdf)
