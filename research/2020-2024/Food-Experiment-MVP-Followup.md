# Modifiable Variables in Parkinsonism food and symptom follow-up, 2020–2024

These reports analyze the same observational MVP cohort as the [2017 baseline paper](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#fish-yogurt-and-overall-symptom-scores-in-the-mvp-cohort). The analyses may include some of the same people, with different inclusion rules, survey dates and sample sizes.

## Fresh vegetables, motivation and other individual symptoms

Laurie K. Mischley’s MVP ten-year summary is dated November 2024 on its cover. The earliest verified public records are the investigator’s 27 December 2024 full-text upload and an announcement linking to the study site, timestamped 27 December 2024 UTC. The study’s download page is undated. The report’s printed pages 63–64 show baseline food-frequency and individual PRO-PD symptom correlations among participants with Parkinson’s disease. The heatmap displays categories such as fresh vegetables, quinoa, coffee, green tea, black tea and oils against distinct symptom rows, including motivation, fatigue and handwriting. It gives colors for most pairs. The accompanying table prints absolute correlation coefficients for 14 selected pairs. [Dated investigator deposit](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/387441305_Modifiable_Variables_in_Parkinsonism_MVP_Study_10-Year_Summary_Report) · [Investigator’s announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/laurie-mischley-nd-phd-mph-8b8aa511_modifiable-variables-in-parkinsonism-mvp-activity-7278255975415824384-YtNf) · [Report, printed pp. 63–64](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/14919326-8cbc-4a3e-88d4-0f609019e178/MVP%20Study%2010-Year%20Summary-c634e33.pdf#page=64)

The prose associates higher fresh-vegetable frequency with greater motivation and describes it as less apathy. The selected pair has an absolute correlation of 0.166368. The food-frequency prompt pools fresh vegetables in half-cup servings, and the PRO-PD motivation and initiative slider is a patient-reported item. The report provides no pair-specific sample size, uncertainty interval, p-value, covariate adjustment or multiplicity analysis for that result. This is directionally compatible with reduced apathy at the pooled food-group level. The data shown cannot resolve raw kale [0532](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0532.txt) or raw cucumber [0204](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0204.txt). The fatigue-row colors for coffee and green tea fall slightly below zero on the baseline heatmap. Black tea is at the zero swatch. These are qualitative readings of the display, with no printed coefficients or uncertainty. The survey pools coffee and espresso in one field and gives no green tea preparation details. The figure provides indirect context for brewed coffee [0121](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0121.txt) and brewed green tea [0948](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0948.txt). The same list pairs quinoa with better handwriting (absolute correlation 0.167154), and canned fruit with worse balance (0.184712), rising (0.183893), dressing/eating/grooming (0.180439), fall frequency (0.164066) and gait (0.162459). It also pairs ice cream with more hallucinations (0.162057), and lists wine with gait (0.172034), fatigue (0.171974) and stooped posture (0.162574). The coefficients are absolute. The displayed wine correlations lack a signed numeric estimate. The PRO-PD handwriting item also includes typing. The frozen quinoa claims measure walking slowness and rigidity, and the orange-falls claim specifies raw orange. The text refers to approximately 300 people with six or more points, and the figure labels show 403 in each extreme group. The basis of those counts is unclear. The bars give no individual symptom estimates or adjusted per-food progression coefficients. ## Composite scores in the 2020 and 2023 congress reports

At the 2020 MDS Virtual Congress, Mischley and Farahnik reported abstract 201. Their cross-sectional survey included 1,795 people with idiopathic Parkinson’s disease, with 1,527 complete cases. Regression adjusted for age, gender, income and time since diagnosis. Higher frequency of fresh vegetables, fresh fruit, non-fried fish, olive oil, coconut oil and fresh herbs was associated with lower total PRO-PD. Canned fruit or vegetables, yogurt, milk, chicken and several other foods were associated with higher total scores. The abstract reports 23 foods with p < 0.05 and gives no individual symptom analyses. [MDS abstract](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/association-between-diet-parkinsons-disease-progression/)

The 2023 World Parkinson Congress program lists *The Relationship Between Diet and Parkinson Symptoms Over Time*, P26.06, on 6 July 2023. The poster, credited to Mischley, Fox and Farahnik, appears on printed page 65 of the 2024 report. Its two cross-sectional regressions use baseline (2013–2022) and 2022 survey data. In Table 2, fresh-vegetable frequency was associated with lower composite PRO-PD at baseline (estimate −29.9, SE 6.06, n = 1,787) and in the 2022 survey (−51.8, SE 10.1, n = 706), each p < 0.001 after Holm–Bonferroni correction. The poster says its models account for age, gender, income and years since diagnosis. Respondents could contribute to both surveys. The models do not estimate within-person change in apathy or other symptoms. [Congress program](https://cdn.ymaws.com/wpc2026.org/resource/resmgr/pdf_files/WPC2023_FinalProgram_2.pdf#page=106) · [Poster and Table 2](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/14919326-8cbc-4a3e-88d4-0f609019e178/MVP%20Study%2010-Year%20Summary-c634e33.pdf#page=66)

The investigator’s PRO Diet handout, posted publicly by PD Active in June 2024, reproduces baseline food-association weights, including fresh vegetables −30 and green tea −11. Its footer dates the underlying model 19 August 2023 and specifies baseline data. It supplies no separate fatigue or motivation outcome. ## Olive oil, coconut oil and item-level symptoms in the baseline graphic

The baseline Parkinson heatmap in the 10-year MVP report gives weak inverse signs for olive-oil frequency with self-rated movement slowness and walking difficulty, and for coconut-oil frequency with subjective sleepiness. Native color comparison found the same category and symptom tile values on both printed copies. The report does not identify processing grades, pair sizes, adjusted effects or uncertainty. The slowness item represents self-rated slowness of movement and does not provide an examiner-scored bradykinesia result. The walking item represents difficulty walking, with no timed speed. The coconut symptom sits adjacent to fatigue and has its own plotted item. The printed copies share one baseline cohort. [Original report, PDF pages 64–65](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/14919326-8cbc-4a3e-88d4-0f609019e178/MVP%20Study%2010-Year%20Summary-c634e33.pdf#page=64) · [Native color receipt](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/followup/gait-and-dream-nov15/mvp-item-colors.txt)

## Coconut oil with motivation and self-reported REM behavior in the baseline graphic

The two printed views of the same baseline Parkinson data give weak inverse colors for unspecified coconut-oil frequency and both the motivation or initiative and REM behavior items. The matching signs come from one cohort and do not establish individual participant changes. The plotted REM item does not supply a count of dream-enactment events. The visible motivation result is a patient-reported proxy for apathy. Neither correlation specifies an oil grade, reports pairwise uncertainty or isolates oil from other behaviors. One separate ketogenic/MCT trial found no clear apathy contrast and did not isolate virgin oil.
