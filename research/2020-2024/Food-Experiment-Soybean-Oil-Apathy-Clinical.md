# Soybean-oil and apathy follow-up

## 2022 — Oxford cohort's cooking-fat field and apathy analysis

Lawrie and colleagues analyzed 162 participants with idiopathic Parkinson’s disease in the Oxford Parkinson’s Disease Discovery Cohort. The assessments for this cross-sectional analysis occurred from April 2017 through February 2020. Diet in the prior year was recorded with the EPIC food frequency questionnaire and processed with FETA. Separate past-week apathy was assessed with MDS-UPDRS Part I item 1.5. The investigators modeled presence (45) or absence (117) of apathy because few participants had moderate symptoms. The lifestyle-adjusted overall-sample p values were .612 for MIND, .687 for the Dietary Inflammatory Index and .369 for the Healthy Diet Indicator 2020 (Table 4). These models used scores for entire dietary patterns. The article provides no analyzed subgroup, intake quantity or apathy estimate for soybean oil. [Institutional copy of primary report, Methods and Table 4](https://radar.brookes.ac.uk/radar/file/e74b0c76-bd44-44b2-8f7e-e3622a259d4d/1/Dietary%20Patterns%20and%20Nonmotor%20Symptoms%20in%20Parkinson%20s%20Disease%20A%20Cross%20Sectional%20Analysis.pdf) · [PubMed, online April 7, 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35512773/)

The standard EPIC-Norfolk questionnaire and FETA instructions permit free-text identification of vegetable oil used for frying, roasting or grilling. The public fat lookup table lists soya oil as code 17044 and unspecified vegetable oil as 17046. FETA processes a single frying-fat code. Its instructions use 17046 for unspecified oil and allow that default when the input gives an unresolvable mixture. The baking vegetable-oil response maps to 17046 in the published instructions. The frying field records a usual fat type without its refinement method or a standalone oil-dose measurement. The standard instrument can therefore record some named soybean-oil use. The public study report gives no count for this code or evidence that the cohort retained enough named responses for an analysis. Individual records are request-only through the cohort’s Data Access Committee. The article cites publisher supplements, which could not be accessed. ## 2022 — MVP diet-pattern questionnaires and motivation item

Fox, Park and Mischley’s MVP analysis scored motivation or initiative and loss of interest as separate PRO-PD items. The MEDI and MIND pattern scores were each associated with lower reported motivation difficulty (both p < .001 in Table 2). The supplements include olive-oil questions and butter, margarine or cream groupings. They give no soybean-oil item. Nutrition question-level results in Supplementary Table S4 use the aggregate PRO-PD score. The biomarker analysis by Roberts and colleagues uses data collected under the same named MVP parent study, with a single-practice blood-testing inclusion criterion. Participant overlap between these reports is unquantified. ## 2023 — Soybean oil emulsion and effort for a milk reward

In a maternal-separation model, male mice consumed a soybean-oil emulsion (Intralipid) or sweetened milk for 30 minutes before a five-minute extinction test. Each animal underwent the alternative prefeeding after retraining, with counterbalanced order. The report gives a prefeeding effect on lever presses (F[1,44] = 15.138, p < .001) and no clear interaction with both strain and maternal separation (F[1,44] = .1271). The soybean emulsion served as the alternate preload, and the reward was milk. This tests sensory-specific satiety within a study of food-directed behavior. It did not estimate the effect of refined soybean oil on clinical apathy.
