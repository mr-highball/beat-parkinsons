# HAL and French-language dietary research

## 2021 herbalists’ accounts of Parkinson care

Teresa Peintner’s University of Westminster report is dated April 2020. HAL records public release on June 30, 2021. The qualitative study interviewed two medical herbalists about their practice. Appendix II contains their completed questionnaires, including one respondent’s use of turmeric in an enhanced-absorption capsule and green tea in capsule or tea form. Appendix I lists saffron as used by that respondent, while the saffron row in their questionnaire is blank. [Report, pp. 40–42](https://hal.science/tel-03267487/document#page=47) · [HAL release metadata](https://api.archives-ouvertes.fr/search/?q=halId_s:tel-03267487)

The questionnaires record practitioner-reported use. The report provides no patient-level outcome data that could link green tea to fatigue, turmeric to pain severity or saffron to a change in symptoms. The mechanisms in Appendix I cite separate literature and do not measure clinical effects in these practitioners’ patients. [Report, results and appendices](https://hal.science/tel-03267487/document)

## 2021 chocolate craving and anxiety measures in a French case-control study

De Chazeron and colleagues compared 200 people with Parkinson’s with 200 matched controls. Table 2 reports chocolate craving in 26 Parkinson participants (13%) and nine controls (4.5%). Table 3 reports higher anxiety ratings among participants with food addiction in the pooled sample. Chocolate craving is also more frequent in that group, but the study does not report anxiety by chocolate craving or intake within the Parkinson group. The survey does not distinguish dark chocolate from other types, and craving is different from an observed effect of eating it. [Primary report, Tables 2–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8149641/) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC8149641.txt)

## 2021 cardamom and ginger odors in a Parkinson smell test

Jacob and colleagues’ May 17, 2021 study used an olfactometer to test five scents, including ginger, cardamom, garlic, coffee and vanilla. The abstract and Table 1 list 32 idiopathic Parkinson cases and 63 controls, while the prose and some percentages suggest different denominators. Table 5 reports lower recognition pressure as stimulus concentration rises. In the Parkinson group, mean pressure ranged from 20.0 to 10.8 psi for ginger and from 19.7 to 8.9 psi for cardamom across the three concentrations. The reported areas under the case–control curves were 0.928 and 0.955, respectively. [Publisher record](https://journaljpri.com/index.php/JPRI/article/view/2398) · [Article copy, Tables 1 and 5](https://www.academia.edu/108559796/Olfactory_Function_Assessment_Standardization_of_a_New_Quantitative_Technique_for_the_Indian_Population)

The outcomes reflect how readily participants detect the diagnostic odor under different stimulus strengths. The test involved smelling a preparation through the nose and gives no measure of olfactory improvement after eating ground cardamom or grated ginger.

## 2021 levodopa and curcumin formulation study and later report

Cardoso, Mogharbel and colleagues presented a laboratory formulation at the Coimbra Health School annual meeting in 2021. The official abstract appeared August 3, 2021. The April 28, 2022 journal article links that meeting presentation to its later report. Both sources characterize particles carrying curcumin and levodopa and report cell compatibility. The experiments concern formulation and cellular outcomes, without evidence of symptom changes in people or animals or consumption of ground turmeric. [Conference abstract](https://academic.oup.com/eurpub/article/31/Supplement_2/ckab120.070/6336146) · [Journal report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9101601/) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC9101601.txt)

## 2022 public release of a Parkinson meal-diary study

Pauline Lestra’s thesis is dated October 18, 2019. HAL records its public release on February 14, 2022. The flowchart in Section 3.1 shows 103 people screened, 71 excluded and 32 included, with 21 without restless legs syndrome and 11 with it. The surrounding prose reports 33 while giving those same group sizes. The flowchart and the denominators in Table 7 agree on 32. [Thesis and tables](https://dumas.ccsd.cnrs.fr/dumas-03572161/document) · [HAL release metadata](https://api.archives-ouvertes.fr/search/?q=halId_s:dumas-03572161)

Table 7 reports meal and snacking patterns in the two groups. Six of 11 in the restless legs group and 13 of 21 in the other group reported night eating, including one participant with night eating only in the restless legs group. The reported p = 0.0216 pertains to the table’s categorization by day and night, and the small cells limit interpretation. The diary form solicited names of foods. The published analysis does not link an individual food, its preparation or intake to a change in restless legs or other Parkinson symptoms. [Section 3.5 and Table 7](https://dumas.ccsd.cnrs.fr/dumas-03572161/document)

## 2023 vanilla and lemon odors before meals in a swallowing case

Sarah Mangini’s thesis is dated June 8, 2023, with public release in DUMAS on November 27, 2023. One older patient with idiopathic Parkinson’s underwent supervised premeal exposure to vanilla and lemon odors three times weekly over one month. Both aromas were administered in each session. The patient smelled them through the nose. The repeated swallowing tests used madeleine, yogurt and water as boluses. [Primary thesis, Sections 4.2 and 6](https://dumas.ccsd.cnrs.fr/dumas-04163056/document) · [HAL release metadata](https://api.archives-ouvertes.fr/search/?q=halId_s:dumas-04163056)

Table 1 gives swallowing times before and after of 16.96 to 3.21 seconds for madeleine, 6.49 to 5.41 for yogurt, and 5.37 to 1.40 for water. Madeleine chewing took longer, from 19.45 to 25.20 seconds. The qualitative swallowing comparison gives McNemar p = 1. The abstract calls the improvement in swallowing handicap significant. Section 6.2 and the discussion report Wilcoxon p = 0.5, and Figure 4 shows the Dysphagia Handicap Index changing from 69 to 59. The single-patient before-and-after observations have no concurrent control or separate aroma arms. The measured outcomes cannot identify a food-specific treatment effect or answer the baked potato claim. [Primary thesis, Table 1, Figure 4 and discussion](https://dumas.ccsd.cnrs.fr/dumas-04163056/document)

## 2023 Toulouse mealtime protocol and congress poster

The Toulouse team first registered this observational study on June 12, 2023 as NCT05898139. The World Parkinson Congress program lists its July 2023 poster as P26.03. HAL holds a later copy of that protocol poster. The study uses mealtime videos and interviews with patients with Parkinson’s or atypical parkinsonism and their caregivers to describe strategies for feeding and swallowing. There is no assigned food or preparation. [First registered version](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05898139?tab=history) · [Official congress program](https://cdn.ymaws.com/wpc2026.org/resource/resmgr/pdf_files/WPC2023_FinalProgram_2.pdf) · [Protocol poster](https://hal.science/hal-05734351/document)

The registry now records 22 participants and completion on October 13, 2025, with no posted results in the December 22, 2025 update. The poster predates recruitment and gives no outcome data. The meal videos and interviews are a potential source for identifying preparations actually used. The public protocol provides no estimates for baked potato. [Current study record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05898139)

## 2024 dairy and Parkinson incidence in the French E3N cohort

Hajji-Louati and colleagues reported 845 incident Parkinson cases among 71,542 women in the E3N cohort. The March 13, 2024 preprint, March 17 conference abstract and September 18 journal article describe the same analysis. In the journal report, the hazard ratio per standard deviation of plain milk intake was 1.08 (95% CI 1.02–1.14). For milk added to coffee, tea or chicory, the intermediate category had HR 0.77 (0.61–0.97) and the highest category HR 0.98 (0.79–1.21), each relative to the lowest. These are associations with incident diagnosis. The publication reports no food-by-symptom comparison among people with Parkinson’s and no independent effect of the individual drinks grouped with milk. The separate EPIC4ND analysis used a larger multinational cohort. [Local preprint text](Source-Text/2024-Hajji-Louati-E3N-Dairy-Preprint.txt) · [March conference abstract](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1654039/consommation-de-lait-et-autres-produits-laitiers-e) · [September journal report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39294525/) · [EPIC4ND catalog note](Food-and-Nutrition.md#2024--dairy-in-the-epic4nd-cohort)

## 2024 safflower and corn oils in a mouse DHA and exercise study

Kerdiles and colleagues’ paper was first published online March 1, 2024 and assigned to a February 2025 issue. The mouse study used a unilateral 6-OHDA lesion and compared DHA-enriched and control diets with and without voluntary exercise. Table 1 lists 25 g/kg each of corn oil and safflower oil in the control feed, versus 10 and 19 g/kg in the DHA feed, respectively, together with added microencapsulated DHA. The two oils changed together within the diet formulation. Their refining grades were unreported. [Primary report, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11317935/)

The combined DHA-plus-exercise arm improved forelimb stepping asymmetry relative to other lesioned arms after eight weeks. The paper reports p < 0.001 for the reported restoration comparison. It found no significant intervention impact on open-field locomotion. The experiment gives a related motor endpoint in an animal model, with no isolated oil arm or human hand-dexterity or bradykinesia comparison. [Primary report, Figure 3 and behavioral results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11317935/)

## 2020 public deposit of the Rennes olive phenol thesis

The French national export gives July 10, 2019 as the defense date for Morgane Lambert de Malezieu’s thesis, NNT `2019REN1S044`. The earliest verified HAL deposit is January 7, 2020. The Quebec co-supervisor's institutional repository lists July 9, 2024 as its own deposit date and makes the full thesis readable. ## Beef fat selection after dopamine depletion in rats

The bioRxiv version of Joshi and colleagues' study posted November 19, 2020. The journal report first published May 25, 2021. Its seven-day free-choice test offered standard chow, rendered beef fat, sucrose solution and water. After histologic exclusions, the substantia nigra experiment analyzed 22 lesioned animals and 25 sham animals. The lesioned rats ate less chow and more beef fat, with lower total calorie intake. The authors attribute the mean increase in beef fat to six lesioned animals. The study altered dopamine signaling and measured the resulting food choice. It did not assign beef fat as a treatment or measure motor changes caused by eating it. A July 14, 2021 report from the same team used beef tallow, chow, sucrose solution and water while testing dopamine receptor inhibition in the nucleus accumbens. It provides a separate food-choice comparison. [2020 preprint record](https://api.biorxiv.org/details/biorxiv/10.1101/2020.11.17.384651) · [2021 full report, Methods and Figure 2](https://pure.knaw.nl/ws/files/436253007/joshi_et_al.pdf) · [Receptor study, PMID 34273421](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34273421/)

The 2021 Strasbourg–Amsterdam thesis `2021STRAJ109` covers these experiments and separates them from a Parkinson model study of locomotion, pain and depressive-like behavior. That study used brain lesions as its interventions and appeared in February 2020. Strasbourg lists the full dissertation as embargoed through December 31, 2026. The co-awarding university provides the front matter and the chapter corresponding to the separate lesion study. [Strasbourg thesis record and embargo](https://theses.unistra.fr/search/notice/view/2021STRAJ109) · [Amsterdam thesis record](https://pure.amsterdamumc.nl/en/publications/dopaminergic-control-of-food-choice-preference-and-tvta-influence/) · [Amsterdam chapter 4](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/65469320/Chapter_4.pdf) · [Lesion study, PMID 32087289](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32087289/)

## Genetic proxies for coffee and dairy intake in Courage-PD

Domenighetti’s thesis `2021UPASR032` was defended December 15, 2021 and deposited on HAL in October 2022. The October 7, 2021 coffee report compared genetic proxies for coffee consumption with Parkinson diagnosis across 7,369 cases and 7,018 controls. Table 3 gives an odds ratio of 1.69 (95% CI 0.51–5.63) per log unit of daily cups. Removing two variants associated with alcohol intake gave 1.09 (0.26–4.45). The January 8, 2022 dairy report analyzed a lactase-persistence variant in 9,823 cases and 8,376 controls. It estimates an odds ratio of 1.70 (1.12–2.60) per serving of genetically predicted dairy consumption in the main analysis. Both studies use diagnosis as the endpoint. Neither estimates the effect of consuming a specified beverage or dairy product on fatigue, speech or another Parkinson symptom. The papers use samples drawn from the same consortium with different inclusion criteria. [HAL thesis record](https://theses.hal.science/tel-03793817) · [Coffee study, Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9211765/) · [2021 publication date](https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/publications/mendelian-randomisation-study-of-smoking-alcohol-and-coffee-drink/) · [Dairy study, PMID 34997937](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34997937/)
