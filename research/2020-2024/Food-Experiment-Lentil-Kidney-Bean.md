# Lentil and kidney bean evidence

## Lentils in a shared Parkinson vegetarian intervention menu

Hegelmaier and colleagues listed lentils among foods available in a 14-day ovo-lacto vegetarian inpatient intervention. Six people with Parkinson's received the diet and ten received the diet with bowel cleansing. Motor examination totals decreased in both small before-and-after groups (diet alone p = .033, combined p = .005). The UPDRS III includes facial expression, rigidity and gait-related items. Appendix A supplies a menu ingredient list and the supplementary individual trajectories are for total scores. The publication does not isolate lentil intake, canning or boiling, or any of the three individual motor outcomes. [Primary article, Methods, Figure 4 and Appendix A](Source-Text/PMC7072275.txt#L288)

## Legume dinner example and measured gait in Nutri-Park

Gatti and colleagues measured gait speed and diet in 66 Parkinson outpatients and 58 controls. The example dinner for protein redistribution lists 150 g legumes as an alternative to meat, fish, dairy products or eggs. Table 2 compares mean gait speed of 0.93 versus 1.06 m/s for Parkinson participants and controls (p = .035). In levodopa-treated participants Table 4 reports a cross-sectional UPDRS III divided by years of disease of 2.41 versus 4.38 in participants following a protein redistribution diet versus others (p = .023). Neither comparison assigns species, preparation or individual intake to the menu example. [Licensed primary text, Tables 1–4](Source-Text/2020-Gatti-Nutri-Park.txt) · [Existing study note](Food-Experiment-French-Reference-Followup.md#example-protein-redistribution-menu-and-measured-outcomes-in-milan)

## Bean and legume questionnaire items in the 2022 MVP cohort

Fox and colleagues' 2022 MVP cohort analysis included 1,205 respondents reporting Parkinson's or other parkinsonism. Supplementary Table S4 estimates total PRO-PD burden by pooled food category. For the Mediterranean screener's three or more servings of legumes per week the estimate is −43.6 points (printed interval −98.2 to 11.0, adjusted p = .6). For the MIND bean frequency item it is −111 points (printed interval −195 to −27.8, adjusted p = .07). Preserve the printed interval and multiplicity-adjusted p value. Table 2 relates whole dietary patterns to anxiety, daytime sleepiness, insomnia, drooling and general hallucination scores. Neither table pairs a specific bean or lentil preparation with one of those symptoms. General hallucination severity also differs from frequency of visual hallucinations. [Primary article, Table 2](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/14/23/5185) · [Licensed Supplementary Table S4 transcription](Source-Text/PMC9739738-Supplement.txt)

## Isolated lentil lectin in an alpha-synuclein antibody assay

Vojdani and colleagues tested food antigen binding to laboratory-made antibodies. Isolated lentil lectin bound the anti-alpha-synuclein epitope 118–123 antibody (optical density 2.9 in Figure 2A) and showed dilution and inhibition responses. The paper's generic bean agglutinin was concanavalin-A, a jack-bean lectin. These measurements use prepared antigens and reagent antibodies without human food intake or symptom outcomes. [Primary article text, Figures 2, 4 and 6](Source-Text/PMC8147930.txt) · [Existing assay note](Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#shrimp-antigens-in-a-laboratory-assay)

## Lentil and bean lectin activity before and after cooking

Adamcová and colleagues tested representative raw and prepared pulses with hemagglutination assays using untreated or trypsin-treated erythrocytes. Assay choice affected which lentil samples registered activity. The study detected activity in several raw lentil varieties and reports that the selected preparation eliminated detectable activity in the tested lentils and beans, except chickpeas. This is an analytical comparison. The result provides a preparation distinction for lectin hypotheses without measuring any Parkinson symptom. [Primary article text, Tables 2 and 3](Source-Text/PMC8618113.txt)

## Lentils with rice adapted for cerebral palsy dysphagia

Merino and colleagues evaluated sensory characteristics of five texture-modified dishes. Fifty-nine judges without dysphagia gave the adapted lentils-with-rice dish a mean liking score of 4.7/9 (SD 2.3), the lowest mean among five dishes. Comparisons with ratings from an earlier cerebral palsy dysphagia panel found no clear lentil-rice difference at the three acceptance levels. The odor scores characterize the dish. These sensory studies did not enroll people with Parkinson's or measure objective swallowing physiology or changes in smell function. [Primary article text, Methods and Tables 1–3](Source-Text/PMC7926527.txt) · [Original dish-design and cerebral palsy panel study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ifset.2020.102383)

## Immediate allergy in children after kidney bean meals

Laiseca García and colleagues' report first appeared online May 3, 2022. One child developed hives after eating cooked white kidney beans. Another had hives and abdominal discomfort after white kidney beans and facial angioedema after red kidney beans. Testing included cooked red and white kidney beans and showed IgE cross-reactivity. This shows a specific adverse reaction can occur even with cooked food in allergic individuals. The observations do not estimate Parkinson drooling or visual hallucinations. [Primary case report, PMID 35503406](https://www.jiaci.org/revistas/vol33issue2_6-7.pdf)

## Gastroenteritis outbreak after inadequately cooked kidney beans

Watier-Grillot and colleagues published their report in 2023 about an outbreak in Brittany in 2018. They counted 200 cases among about 1,700 customers. The survey of 92 cases and 113 controls found associations with chili con carne (adjusted OR 32.8, 95% CI 13.8–77.8) and pan-fried corn and kidney beans (4.9, 1.8–13.1). The beans were undercooked and investigators measured remaining hemagglutinating activity. Cases were defined through acute gastrointestinal symptoms. This study provides evidence about foodborne illness from undercooking and no measure of Parkinson drooling or visual hallucinations. [Primary article text, Table 1 and Methods](Source-Text/PMC10467087.txt)

## Daily cooked lentils in a metabolic randomized trial

Chamberlin and colleagues compared meals providing 980 g/week cooked whole green lentils against control meals for 12 weeks in adults with metabolic risk. Thirty-eight of 47 randomized participants completed the intervention. The trial measured fasting cholesterol, postprandial glucose and inflammation, and self-reported abdominal discomfort, cramping, bloating and flatulence. The groups did not differ in reported gastrointestinal symptom severity overall (p = .32). Its enrolled population and outcomes did not include Parkinson-specific symptom assessment, including daytime sleepiness or insomnia. [Primary article text, Methods and Supplementary Figure S5 description](Source-Text/PMC10857178.txt)

## Legume targets in Parkinson constipation interventions

Rusch and colleagues' five-week pilot included a goal of at least three weekly servings of legumes in a broader Mediterranean diet. The eight Parkinson participants completed the pilot and reported less constipation. A later eight-week randomized study reported constipation-score changes of −0.5 with the Mediterranean program and standard care and −0.8 with standard care alone (p = .60). The studies estimate a dietary pattern and standard-care comparisons and do not give species- or preparation-specific intake effects. Constipation is outside the nine frozen lentil and kidney bean endpoints. [Primary pilot](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2021.794640) · [Primary randomized trial text](Source-Text/PMC11396875.txt)

## Boiled white kidney bean challenge and acute systemic symptoms

Matsui and colleagues’ October 2023 case report describes recurrent symptoms after white bean soup in a seven-year-old child with Down syndrome. A supervised challenge using boiled white Phaseolus vulgaris reproduced cough, oxygen desaturation, hives, a change in alertness described as abnormal sleep, and mild hypotension. The case confirms an acute reaction in the child and does not establish hallucination or drooling outcomes after canned beans in Parkinson disease. [Licensed primary case report](Source-Text/PMC10715741.txt)

## MIND sleep trial protocol with a grouped legume exposure

The 2024 protocol cites IRCT20181111041611N8 and gives August 7, 2023 as the registration date. It compares two low-calorie diets in women with type 2 diabetes and insomnia. The MIND intervention changes multiple food categories including beans and lentils. The protocol does not assign boiled lentils separately. The primary article and registry identifier link this protocol to the 2025 outcomes. [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2024-MIND-Insomnia-Protocol-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Original methods paper and reported IRCT accession](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11457347/)

## Registered MIND diet comparison in women with insomnia

The official registry marks this MIND diet comparison prospective and gives August 7, 2023 for registration. It gives August 19 as the initial enrollment date, while the later protocol lists October 12 as the planned start. WHO's 2023 snapshot shows recruiting and the current Iranian record shows complete. The study assigns multi-food diets rather than a lentil preparation.
