# Parkinson clinical nutrition in the Japanese proceedings

## Tube-feeding survival after oral intake ceased

The 2022 Utano report followed 78 patients with Parkinson’s, including 44 given tube feeds and 34 given intravenous fluids alone. Among the tube-fed Parkinson patients, median survival from cessation of oral intake was 1.1 years (IQR 0.5–3.1). The meeting abstract gives no named formula and reports a statistically significant between-group difference in overall disease duration only in the multiple system atrophy cohort. The 2018 and 2019 Utano abstracts cover overlapping hospital records, with different Parkinson counts and inclusion rules. [2022 O-30-07](https://web.archive.org/web/20230724134216id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/4/Supplement1/4_391/_pdf/-char/ja#page=202) · [Earlier Utano reports](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-NHO-Middle.md#2018-and-2019-tube-feeding-after-oral-intake-ceased)

## Calorimetry and weight gain during combined feeding

A 62-year-old woman with Parkinson’s, rigidity and dyskinesia weighed 30.4 kg at admission. Weight fell below 30 kg again during earlier calorie increases. Indirect calorimetry estimated resting expenditure at roughly 2,200 kcal/day. With combined oral and tube feeding adjusted to roughly 2,000 kcal/day and other ongoing care, she weighed 38 kg by discharge on day 288. The abstract does not name a food or report a separate effect on rigidity or dyskinesia. [2023 O-12-3](https://web.archive.org/web/20240601082754id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/5/Supplement1/5_255/_pdf/-char/ja#page=69)

## Meibalance 500R and jejunal feeding tolerance

A report describes an 86-year-old patient with Parkinson’s disease, dementia, worsening swallowing and prior aspiration pneumonia. The team changed the administration of Meibalance 500R from pump feeding to a 90-minute manual drip via gastrojejunostomy. The abstract reports no dumping symptoms, diarrhea or malabsorption and supplies no quantified swallowing or aspiration result after the change. It describes a commercial formula and its delivery. [2024 O-29-1](https://web.archive.org/web/20250323185706id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/6/Supplement1/6_302/_pdf/-char/ja#page=194)

## Formula viscosity and vomiting during tube feeding

Masataka Sakisaka reported an 88-year-old woman with Parkinson’s disease and cerebral amyloid angiopathy who had delayed gastric emptying, vomiting and aspiration pneumonia during gastrostomy feeding with thick or viscosity-changing formula. After switching to a low-viscosity formula containing lactic fermentation products, episodes decreased and nutrition was easier to continue. The team also adjusted feeding speed and frequency after leakage at the gastrostomy site. A 2023 manufacturer-hosted report from the treating author describes the same patient and gives nominal viscosities of 15 and 50 mPa·s for the dilute and preceding formulations. The case supplies no isolated estimate for viscosity, the fermentation products or the delivery schedule. [JSPEN 2024 P-19-5, printed p937](https://web.archive.org/web/20250323185258id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/6/Supplement1/6_761/_pdf/-char/ja#page=177) · [2023 treating-author report](https://www.meiji.co.jp/meiji-nutrition-info/pdf/science/case/lacticbacteria_report.pdf#page=4)

## Specimen delays in a tube-feeding case with low glucose

A separate 2024 case describes weight loss and low recorded glucose in a tube-fed patient with Parkinson’s. Weight recovered after increasing feed energy. Occasional low glucose readings continued, then normalized when specimens were collected immediately before feeding under revised handling. The authors suspected variable one- to six-hour delays in sending specimens for processing. The report gives no comparative ingredient exposure and raises a sample-handling explanation for some readings in this patient. [JSPEN 2024 P-01-5, printed p765](https://web.archive.org/web/20250323185258id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/6/Supplement1/6_761/_pdf/-char/ja#page=5)

## Semisolid tube formula during rehabilitation after trauma

A patient with Parkinson’s and multiple traumatic injuries had suspected aspiration pneumonia. The team reported less sputum and improved oral hygiene after replacing the tube feed with an unnamed semisolid formula. Swallowing rehabilitation and a palatal prosthesis accompanied advancement of oral textures through 0j and 1j to code 2. No individual food or comparative airway outcome was reported. [JSPEN 2024 P-15-7, printed p904](https://web.archive.org/web/20250323185258id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/6/Supplement1/6_761/_pdf/-char/ja#page=144)

## Pork mousse and texture-modified meat in severe Parkinson dysphagia

Komatsu and colleagues report one man in his seventies with Hoehn and Yahr stage 5 Parkinson’s disease. An initial jelly trial provoked coughing at larger amounts. He swallowed approximately 1.5 g without cough or wet voice and cleared audible residue with an additional swallow. On day 25, pork mousse left marked oral and pharyngeal residue with coughing. On day 36, videofluoroscopy showed more effective chewing and transport with a different texture-designed meat food. Alternating with jelly cleared pharyngeal residue. The team subsequently used this meat with tea jelly during direct therapy without reported aspiration. The abstract gives no species or brand for the second meat and no tea type. Rehabilitation, timing and accompanying jelly differed across trials. [JSPEN 2020 P-39-11, printed p1276](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/2/Supplement1/2_840/_pdf/-char/ja#page=437)

## Oral Engelead jelly during Parkinson dysphagia rehabilitation

Shirai and colleagues followed a 77-year-old man with Parkinson’s disease, aspiration pneumonia and suspected sarcopenic dysphagia. Oral Engelead jelly began on day three alongside enteral nutrition and swallowing rehabilitation. On day 12, endoscopy showed pyriform sinus residue without aspiration. The report does not identify the bolus used in that examination. Enteral feeding ended on day 42. On day 55, oral intake was FOIS level 6 and tongue pressure was 26.6 kPa, compared with 21.2 kPa on day 12. The manufacturer offers apple and grape formulations. The case gives neither flavor nor a jelly-specific outcome. Kanzaki and colleagues separately used Engelead jelly in place of dyed water as an endoscopic test bolus in 64 gastrostomy patients, ten with Parkinson’s disease. They pooled findings across diagnoses and printed a pneumonia count that exceeds its stated group denominator. [JSPEN 2020 P-41-10, printed p1298](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/2/Supplement1/2_840/_pdf/-char/ja#page=459) · [JSPEN 2020 O-32-1](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/2/Supplement1/2_350/_pdf/-char/ja#page=217) · [Manufacturer](https://www.otsukakj.jp/products/engelead/)

## Rice porridge and chopped food in two Parkinson cases

A 2020 poster describes an 85-year-old man with Parkinson’s who tolerated full rice porridge, chopped food and thickened liquids without cough during rehabilitation. After gastrostomy for medication support, he later ate soft rice, bite-size chopped food and thickened liquids. A separate 77-year-old man with Parkinson’s, aspiration pneumonia and septic shock progressed from enteral feeding to full rice porridge by discharge after multidisciplinary care. The foods are recorded as components of oral feeding care. Neither case isolates a food effect. [JSPEN 2020 P-18-6](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/2/Supplement1/2_840/_pdf/-char/ja#page=194) · [JSPEN 2020 P-39-9](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/2/Supplement1/2_840/_pdf/-char/ja#page=435)

## Soba request in drug-induced parkinsonism

An older man with schizophrenia, drug-induced parkinsonism, sarcopenia and terminal aspiration pneumonia requested soba during end-of-life care. The report records assisted oral eating during three days. It does not specify whether soba was served or eaten, nor give ingredients, preparation or a food-specific swallowing measure. [JSPEN 2020 P-36-12](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/2/Supplement1/2_840/_pdf/-char/ja#page=403)

## Ornithine and pressure injury in a 2020 Parkinson case

Watanabe and colleagues added 2.5 g/day ornithine to enteral nutrition on day 79 in a 67-year-old man with Parkinson’s disease, pneumonia and a sacral pressure injury. The authors observed disappearance of necrotic tissue from day 92 and improved signs of inflammation and infection from day 98. The DESIGN-R score was 37 on day 175, after reaching 50 before supplementation. Fine movements associated with Parkinson’s later became more noticeable and energy provision was increased when weight fell. The report evaluates pressure injury during several changes in nutrition, debridement and infection care, without an isolated Parkinson symptom effect. [JSPEN 2020 P-55-2](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/2/Supplement1/2_840/_pdf/-char/ja#page=602)

## Outpatient nutritional status and disease duration

Tsuruta and colleagues’ 2021 JSPEN study of 91 Parkinson outpatients was reproduced with presentation materials by the Hokkaido neurological institute in 2022. By CONUT and BMI, 55% had normal status, 37% were at risk and 8% were malnourished. CONUT correlated with disease duration (r = .31, p < .05). Nutritional risk and malnutrition together were reported for 39% of those with disease duration of two years or less (n = 39) and 61% of those with six years or more (n = 18). The report gives no exposure to a specific food. Their earlier inpatient series and their 2026 title on inpatient mood and nutritional status are separate records, with any individual overlap unverified. [JSPEN 2021 O-56-3, printed p688](https://web.archive.org/web/20220806222420id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/3/Supplement1/3_451/_pdf#page=238) · [2022 institutional reproduction](https://www.hokkaido-find.jp/sys/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/FIND_08_2022.pdf#page=26) · [2026 abstract metadata](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=202602269607661691)

## Rigidity and akinesia after short-bowel surgery

Eshima and colleagues reported an 83-year-old woman with stage IV Parkinson’s who had 140 cm of small bowel remaining after emergency resection. She resumed oral intake on postoperative day five and oral levodopa/carbidopa on day eight. Limb rigidity and akinesia worsened relative to baseline and made self-feeding difficult. The team proposed increasing levodopa/carbidopa. From postoperative day two to day 14, plasma tyrosine fell from 76.7 to 39.8 nmol/mL and tryptophan from 36.9 to 17.7 nmol/mL. The authors discussed medication interruption, haloperidol for postoperative delirium, impaired drug absorption and changes in amino acid availability. The abstract reports no dietary intervention for rigidity or a test that distinguishes among these explanations. [JSPEN 2021 O-03-2, printed p462](https://web.archive.org/web/20220806222420id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/3/Supplement1/3_451/_pdf#page=12)

## Tube water temperature and stool consistency

Dota and colleagues followed nine older patients with neurological disease receiving enteral nutrition, including four with Parkinson’s. The group switched from water at about 30 °C to water at 40–43 °C. At month 12, normal or soft stool scores rose from 61.3% to 86.8% in the Parkinson subgroup and fell from 60.0% to 40.2% in the other five patients. The full cohort fell from 58.9% to 56.7%. The reported percentages describe stool consistency, and the abstract supplies no water volume, bowel movement counts, concurrent laxative changes, individual outcomes, untreated group or estimate with uncertainty. This was a change to tube-delivered water. In a separate 25-person tube-feeding ward study, Fukumizu and colleagues reported that the share of Bristol types 6 and 7 fell from 98.5% to 68.7% over 12 weeks of bowel monitoring and medication adjustments. The abstract mentions Parkinson bowel dysfunction in discussion and gives no Parkinson subgroup result. [JSPEN 2021 O-48-4, printed p654](https://web.archive.org/web/20220806222420id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/3/Supplement1/3_451/_pdf#page=204) · [O-24-3, printed p548](https://web.archive.org/web/20220806222420id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/3/Supplement1/3_451/_pdf#page=98)

## Oral intake alongside gastrostomy

Shiraishi and colleagues reviewed 33 people with neurological diseases who received gastrostomies at one center from 2014 to 2020. Concurrent oral intake after gastrostomy was reported for 57% overall, including 46% of the 13 people with Parkinson’s, 100% of 10 people with ALS, and 0% of four people with progressive supranuclear palsy. A second abstract from the same institution and inclusion period gives the same oral-intake percentages. It adds that nutritional indicators improved in 93% overall and 23% of the Parkinson group returned home. The reports give no specific foods, food-specific aspiration results or parallel group without gastrostomy. [JSPEN 2021 O-49-1, printed p657](https://web.archive.org/web/20220806222420id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/3/Supplement1/3_451/_pdf#page=207) · [PEG and Home Care 2021 O2-3, printed p124](https://peg.or.jp/heq25/file/abstract.pdf#page=126)

## Guar fiber, heat and massage in one constipation case

Satō and colleagues recorded bowel movements in one patient with Parkinson’s at the hospital that also reported the water-temperature series. Over the course of abdominal heat and massage, partially hydrolyzed guar gum and ongoing bowel care, the patient went from daily manual evacuation to a spontaneous bowel movement about every three days at one month. Suppository and enema use fell to about monthly. Drops used every other day fell to twice weekly and a separate oral laxative was stopped after two months. Stool consistency was reported as Bristol 5–6. The report gives no fiber dose or component-specific result. Whether the patient was in the water-temperature series is unknown. The organizer’s indexed abstract supplied the methods and results during this pass. [Japan Chronic Phase Medicine 2021 2-9-3, printed p147](https://site2.convention.co.jp/jamcf29/program/ippan_syoroku.pdf) · [Proceedings index](https://mp.medicalonline.jp/products/article_list.php?magazine_code=ej7aaomc&number=suppl&volume=&year=2021)

## Body composition in a Parkinson cohort

Kanda and colleagues compared 15 participants with BMI under 18.5 and 44 with higher BMI in a 59-person Parkinson cohort. The low-BMI group included 14 women and one man, compared with 20 women and 24 men in the other group. The groups differed in body composition, transthyretin and hemoglobin. The authors reported no differences in motor scores, disease stage, disease duration, quality of life or levodopa-equivalent dose. The comparison used contemporaneous measurements and reports no food intake data or longitudinal weight-change estimate. [JSPEN 2021 O-80-3, printed p789](https://web.archive.org/web/20220806222420id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/3/Supplement1/3_451/_pdf#page=339)

## Electrical stimulation and swallowing measures

Ogino and colleagues treated three men with Parkinson’s and prior aspiration pneumonia with neck interferential current stimulation. After four weeks, oral-intake levels were unchanged in all three. Penetration–aspiration scores for a 3 mL liquid bolus changed from 2 to 2, 7 to 1, and 1 to 1. Laryngeal elevation delays shortened in two participants and lengthened in the third. The citric-acid concentration needed to provoke cough fell in all three. Citric acid was inhaled as an assessment probe. The uncontrolled study used electrical stimulation as the intervention. [JSPEN 2021 O-99-1, printed p870](https://web.archive.org/web/20220806222420id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/3/Supplement1/3_451/_pdf#page=420)

## Jelly and paste after gastrostomy complications

Yakabe and colleagues reported a man in his sixties with newly diagnosed Parkinson’s disease, aspiration pneumonia and several comorbidities. Swallowing endoscopy found saliva aspiration and a risk of silent aspiration. Gastrostomy was followed by peritonitis. Oral training with an unspecified swallowing jelly began on day 36 and stopped on day 61 when appetite and alertness declined and sputum increased. Tube feeding continued. Oral intake resumed on day 106 and progressed to a paste-texture diet by transfer. Jelly composition and food-specific airway invasion outcomes were unreported. [JSPEN 2021 O-101-6, printed p882](https://web.archive.org/web/20220806222420id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/3/Supplement1/3_451/_pdf#page=432)
