# Pinto beans and Parkinson symptom endpoints

## Pooled beans and Parkinson sleep, drooling, and handwriting outcomes

[Fox et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9739738/) (first published 6 December 2022, PMID 36501214) analyzed a cross-sectional questionnaire from 1,205 people with self-reported Parkinsonism, including 1,145 idiopathic Parkinson's cases. The MIND and Mediterranean diet scores include broad bean or legume categories and do not identify pinto beans, boiling or canning, or verified individual pinto consumption. Symptom severity was self-rated with PRO-PD scales over the preceding week. The nonmotor subscore included insomnia and drooling; the motor subscore included handwriting, without a separate small-handwriting estimate.

In adjusted whole-diet analyses, each point of MIND score was associated with a −2.14 insomnia score (95% CI −2.93 to −1.35, adjusted p<0.001) and −1.20 drool score (95% CI −1.91 to −0.498, adjusted p=0.009). Mediterranean score estimates were −1.07 for insomnia (95% CI −1.75 to −0.399, adjusted p=0.02) and −0.182 for drool (95% CI −0.785 to 0.422, adjusted p=0.9); the displayed intervals and multiplicity-adjusted p values have different adjustment conventions. The whole-diet, cross-sectional associations cannot identify which foods were responsible, determine causation, or establish either direction of any pinto-bean claim. The generic bean item was analyzed against total symptom burden, not the named individual outcomes; see the pre-existing [source and supplement archive](Source-Text/PMC9739738.txt) and [supplement transcription](Source-Text/PMC9739738-Supplement.txt).
