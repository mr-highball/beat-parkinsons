# Ginger preparations and outcome measures

## Constipation domains in the original Parkinson gastrointestinal scale

June 16, 2021. The original Gastrointestinal Dysfunction Scale for Parkinson’s Disease has 12 items. Its constipation domain includes bowel frequency, straining, stool hardness and incomplete evacuation. The development thesis specifies six-month recall. NCT07882108 lists the GIDS-PD at baseline and follow-up during a 12-week ginger extract intervention. The registration does not specify item-level contrasts, a change to recall, or posted results. The scale validation and trial are distinct datasets. The institution's original thesis is https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/a4ebe64a-b9fd-47bc-8550-1ec2fef1d833/content. ## Dried ginger extract and isolated shogaol in patent animal comparisons

May 16, 2024. In an inventor-reported 6-OHDA and levodopa mouse study, Example 6 records repeated axial, limb and orolingual abnormal involuntary movement scores. Table 12 reports AIM area under the curve (mean ± SE) of 276.75 ± 26.00 with levodopa, 144.00 ± 15.56 with dried-ginger extract, 169.58 ± 22.61 and 106.25 ± 12.02 in the two isolated shogaol arms, and 171.00 ± 13.67 with the amantadine positive control. The disclosed example does not establish animal sample size, administration route or masking. The priority-related later Korean publication identifies a dried-rhizome ethanol extract. The family disclosures reproduce this experiment and do not provide replication.

In a separate 6-OHDA mouse experiment, Example 3 reports fecal water of 57.31 ± 2.46% with levodopa and 69.54 ± 1.51% with levodopa plus isolated shogaol. KR20240067803A, published May 17, 2024, reproduces Table 4. This is a fecal water measure and the treatment in this experiment is the isolated constituent. The related Kim articles use a different model and the 2026 publication's bead-expulsion test differs from this endpoint. Animal and control overlap is unverified. Neither experiment estimates effects of the culinary preparation in Parkinson patients.
