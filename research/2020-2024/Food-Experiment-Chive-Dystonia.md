# Chives, related Allium species and dystonia

## 2020 — Chives on a multi-food Parkinson study menu

Hegelmaier and colleagues listed chives with the vegetables in Appendix A Table A1. The intervention included six people with Parkinson’s disease receiving a vegetarian diet and ten receiving that diet with bowel cleansing. The menu did not identify a species, preparation or dose for chives or provide individual intakes. The study reported longitudinal total motor scores and a baseline summary of Part IV covering motor complications. It did not report dystonia separately. The study documents that chives were offered in this intervention, without an ingredient-specific or dystonia-specific estimate. [Original local text, Methods and Appendix A](Source-Text/PMC7072275.txt) · [Previous chive evidence note](Food-Experiment-Chive-Outcomes.md)

## 2021 — Dried Chinese chive shoot fractions and muscle cell proliferation

Oh, Kim and colleagues dried authenticated Chinese chive (*Allium tuberosum*) shoots at 40 °C and obtained a methanol extract that was partitioned into solvent and aqueous fractions. They identified isolated compounds from the water fraction. Some compounds increased BrdU incorporation in cultured C2C12 skeletal muscle cells in Figure 6B, and the article describes changes in myogenic markers. The cell outcomes address proliferation and differentiation. No muscle contraction, dystonia or whole-animal motor effect was assessed. [Original local text, Methods and Figure 6](Source-Text/PMC7956299.txt) · [Original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7956299/)

## 2022 — Separate dystonia measures in a Parkinson cohort

The subthalamic stimulation and movement study used the Burke–Fahn–Marsden dystonia rating scale for physical distribution and severity alongside the distinct MDS-UPDRS item 4.6 for painful OFF dystonia. The latter measures the fraction of OFF time spent with painful dystonia. The authors distinguish historical and observed dystonia and their interim postoperative analysis uses item 4.6. Neither endpoint can be recovered from a total motor score alone. The treatment was stimulation. [Original local text](Source-Text/PMC9541849.txt) · [Original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9541849/) · [Original scale publication](https://www.movementdisorders.org/MDS-Files1/Resources/PDFs/MDS-UPDRS.pdf)

## 2023 — Onion peel extract and drug-induced catalepsy

Bhandarkavathe and Nesur tested an ethanolic Soxhlet extract of shade-dried mature onion peel (*Allium cepa*) in a haloperidol Wistar rat model, six rats per arm. The catalepsy table shows day-seven bar holding of 97.9 ± 11.07 seconds in the model control versus 82.4 ± 5.49, 61.8 ± 5.25 and 23.2 ± 5.91 seconds for 125, 250 and 500 mg/kg, respectively. The table marks all three as lower than the model group at day seven. The adjacent prose reverses the latency direction, although the discussion describes less cataleptic behavior. This recording measures maintenance of an imposed posture. Rigidity and patterned dystonia were not measured separately.
