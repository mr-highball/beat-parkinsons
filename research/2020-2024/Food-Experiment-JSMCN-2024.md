## Lactulose syrup and spontaneous bowel movements

Kyoko Matsuba at Minamitama Hospital studied Clinico lactulose syrup in Parkinson outpatients reporting constipation with Mini-Mental State Examination scores of at least 21. The main outcome was spontaneous bowel movements without additional laxative or enema use. The abstract reports significant improvements in spontaneous bowel movement frequency and Bristol stool form, an increase in total bowel movements and fewer rescue interventions as trends, and no significant differences on the Japanese constipation assessment scale (CAS-MT) or PDQ-39. It gives no sample size, administered dose, duration, comparator, effect estimates or exact p-values. Clinico described its product as a food-grade syrup containing 50% lactulose in August 2023. The manufacturer's labeling does not supply the study dose. Lactulose is a milk-lactose derivative outside the frozen ingredient set. [Primary O-164, printed S45](https://www.eiyou.or.jp/gakujutsu/27th/pdf/jsmcn_Vol27.Supplement_vo.pdf#page=131) · [Presenting hospital's annual report](https://www.eisei.or.jp/media/nenpou_2023.pdf) · [Clinico formulation notice](https://www.clinico.co.jp/pdf/press/lactulosekinousei20230818.pdf)

Kubo, Ito, Matsuba and Shimono published a closely matching Eisei Clinic pilot in May 2024. They recruited 29 patients and analyzed 25 completers after three weeks of baseline diaries and three weeks of open-label syrup use. The article reports a starting dose of 10 g of lactulose in 15 mL daily, with adjustment allowed, and a mean intake of 12.6 g/day. Mean spontaneous bowel movements rose from 3.08 to 3.79 per week (P = .011), and mean Bristol score rose from 3.22 to 3.66 (P = .005). Median rescue use was zero in both periods, with the upper quartile falling from five to three over three weeks (P = .046). Two participants discontinued after worsening stool characteristics. Regular laxatives were continued, rescue use was unrestricted, and diet and fluid intake were uncontrolled. The article provides no placebo group or longer follow-up. The reports share an author, the cognitive cutoff and core outcomes. The conference abstract omits counts and includes CAS-MT and PDQ-39, which the article does not report. Treat the reports as potentially overlapping until participant or protocol linkage is confirmed. [Full journal article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11108808/) · [Hospital-hosted original](https://www.eisei.or.jp/eisei-hospital/media/The_effects_of_lactulose.pdf)

The 2022 fiscal-year hospital report lists an internal exploratory talk by Matsuba on March 29, 2023. The following annual report lists the January 2024 meeting presentation and names Kubo and Ito as co-presenters. The paper was submitted on February 22, 2023, and identifies ethics approval 2022–003. Its text gives no syrup brand. The manufacturer's logistics page dates the bottled syrup to 2017. The August 2023 announcement concerns a labeling change. The reports appear to belong to the same study family. The conference program supplies no protocol identifier, and the product labels and rescue-treatment summaries cannot establish identical analysis populations. ## Thickened water and texture changes during home support

Shigeko Yamamoto reported an 80-year-old man with Parkinson's, dementia, diabetes and severe visual impairment who was discharged on a thickened chopped diet after a decline in swallowing. His spouse initially had trouble recreating the advised texture and thickened water. During repeated home visits, a dietitian and the spouse adjusted thickness and food texture. He progressed to bite-sized foods without reported aspiration and resumed his preadmission home diet after a later urinary infection admission. The report names thickened water and broad meal textures, without ingredient-level contrast, standardized reassessment or an independent effect of one preparation. [Primary O-244, printed S65](https://www.eiyou.or.jp/gakujutsu/27th/pdf/jsmcn_Vol27.Supplement_vo.pdf#page=151)

## Retained medication and oral intake after enteral support

Narumi Nagura and colleagues followed a 76-year-old man with Parkinson's and poor oral intake. Endoscopy on day 12 found medicines retained in the mouth and pyriform sinuses, and his Food Intake Level Scale score was 1. After nasogastric medication and enteral feeding, the team observed qualitative easing of OFF symptoms and resumed direct swallowing rehabilitation. Some oral intake returned by day 25, gastrostomy was placed on day 45, and his Food Intake Level Scale score was 5 at transfer on day 75. The authors reported poor swallowing endurance despite retained function on videofluoroscopy. They did not identify a formula, food, or separate effects of medication delivery, feeding and rehabilitation. [Primary P-011, printed S99](https://www.eiyou.or.jp/gakujutsu/27th/pdf/jsmcn_Vol27.Supplement_vo.pdf#page=185)

## Energy intake and weight during long hospital stays

Shuhei Nishino and colleagues reviewed 24 Parkinson inpatients followed for six months. Six gained and five lost at least 5% of body weight. For weight gain, the ROC cutoff for mean energy intake in the first month was 35.4 kcal/kg (AUC .92, 95% CI .80–1.00). The six-month mean cutoffs for gain and loss were 32.4 kcal/kg (AUC .89, 95% CI .76–1.00) and 28.2 kcal/kg (AUC .82, 95% CI .62–1.00). These thresholds come from the same small retrospective cohort. There was no controlled food, preparation or Parkinson symptom comparison. [Primary O-346, printed S91](https://www.eiyou.or.jp/gakujutsu/27th/pdf/jsmcn_Vol27.Supplement_vo.pdf#page=177) · [Research institution's project listing](https://www.omu.ac.jp/life/nutrimedsci/folder/index.html)

## Maitake extracts in a cell-free alpha-synuclein assay

Misaki Ono and colleagues compared freeze-dried maitake hot-water and ethanol extracts in a cell-free alpha-synuclein fibril assay. At 72 hours, thioflavin T fluorescence relative to the untreated sample was 1.5 ± 0.3% for the hot-water extract and 0.1 ± 3.5%, 68.1 ± 8.2%, and 88.9 ± 20.0% for extracts made with 60%, 80%, and 100% ethanol, respectively. The assay measures fluorescence in an experimental solution without a human or animal symptom endpoint. The team's 2023 presentations cover precursor mushroom screening and maitake hot-water extracts. A May 2024 program lists a closely related presentation from the same group, with cohort or experimental duplication unresolved pending comparison of the actual abstracts. ## Yogurt and azuki preparations in separate swallowing reports

Akihito Ueda and colleagues used yogurt fermented with *Lactococcus cremoris* FC to deliver oral magnesium oxide in a 90-year-old woman with relapsing polychondritis, diabetes and a postoperative tracheostomy. The abstract reports mean bowel movement frequency of 2.2 per day during oral medication with yogurt and 1.5 per day during nasogastric medication. Recovery, route, and the yogurt exposure changed together. Her case gives no Parkinson diagnosis and no strained yogurt or handwriting assessment.

Takuya Yoshida and colleagues studied a dessert made from smooth azuki red-bean paste layered around rice-porridge jelly in place of mochi. The physical measurements included hardness, adhesion and cohesion. Twenty healthcare staff rated seven sensory items. Compared with a commercial texture-modified food, the dessert had a higher appearance rating and similar ratings on the other items. The study did not measure swallowing events in Parkinson patients. A funder report and presentation provide earlier or additional material for the same product.
