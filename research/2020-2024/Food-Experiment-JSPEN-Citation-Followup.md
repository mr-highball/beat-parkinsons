# Japanese nutrition and rehabilitation reports in Parkinson’s

## Formula choice during continuous intestinal levodopa

Takahashi and colleagues reported a woman in her seventies with advanced Parkinson’s and oral intake below estimated requirements. Nutrition support progressed through parenteral, nasogastric, gastrostomy and oral routes. After starting intestinal levodopa-carbidopa gel, the team replaced a formula that gelled in the stomach (Mermed) with a semisolid Racol NF formula for easier home delivery. The report gives about 10–15 minutes for direct gastric administration of the latter. Alertness and wearing-off improved after starting the drug infusion, and she progressed from soft meals to a regular diet with swallowing care. A regulatory table identifies soybean, perilla and palm oils in Racol NF. The case gives no formula-specific motor effect, oil grade, named oral food or daily OFF time. [Original 2020 clinical report](https://doi.org/10.11244/ejspen.2.4_270) · [Regulatory formulation](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-JSPEN-Followup.md#racol-nf-semisolid-formula-composition)

## Three oral meals after pneumonia and neurology referral

Tazumi and colleagues retrospectively compared 19 Parkinson’s patients before and 11 after a new neurology referral pathway for severe dysphagia following pneumonia. The proportion taking three meals orally at discharge rose from 21.1% to 63.6% (p = .027). The median maximum daily levodopa equivalent during the period of poor intake rose from 50 to 219.7 mg (p = .021). Cohorts differed in disease duration and the intervention changed access to specialist care. The study reports no controlled food exposure or instrumental evidence of the cause of change. The team’s 2019 poster examined a broader mixed parkinsonism population and is a likely precursor. [Original 2021 clinical report](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/3/5/3_291/_html/-char/en) · [2019 poster O-58-4, page 397](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/1/Supplement/1_347/_pdf/-char/ja#page=397)

The 2020 paper reports the same 136-person mixed cohort as the 2019 poster. Eighty-three had Parkinson’s recorded at admission and 40 of those were eating all three meals orally at discharge. Initial swallowing impairment and prior independence predicted oral intake in the combined cohort. The 2020 and 2021 papers share a hospital and part of their accrual intervals, so individual overlap remains possible. [Original 2020 paper](https://doi.org/10.11244/ejspen.2.1_16) · [Society publication page](https://www.jspen.or.jp/publication/journal/posts/59)

The symposium abstract printed in 2020 studied 17 people with parkinsonism and severe dysphagia after pneumonia from June 2018 through May 2019. Seven resumed oral intake. The 136-person publication used admissions from 2015–2017. The symposium dates and seven oral recoveries match the 2021 paper’s 11-person Parkinson post-referral group, which suggests overlap. Individual linkage is unreported. [2020 S-10-9, PDF p72](https://web.archive.org/web/20220728002617id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/2/Supplement1/2_91/_pdf/-char/ja#page=72)

## Breakfast white rice and daytime sleepiness

Mine and colleagues treated a man in his sixties with stage III Parkinson’s who reported severe drowsiness after meals and medication. The team first introduced sleep-management measures. At week four they cut his breakfast serving of white rice by half. Sleep and exercise management continued. Clinicians observed better alertness at week five and the patient later reported less daytime sleepiness. His Parkinson’s Disease Sleep Scale-2 total decreased from 43 to 21 and UPDRS Part III from 50 to 41. He kept three daily meals and gained 2 kg. A standardized daytime sleepiness assessment was not completed during the exacerbation. One uncontrolled case with concurrent care cannot isolate white rice, carbohydrate amount or fullness as the explanation. The frozen claims specify brown rice and different symptoms. [Original 2022 report, pp. 733–740](https://doi.org/10.32178/jotr.41.6_733)

## Fatigue and orthostatic symptoms during multidisciplinary care

Yasuda and colleagues followed a 77-year-old with stage III Parkinson’s, recent weight loss, fatigue and lightheadedness. She received soft meals, supervised medication administration and physical rehabilitation. The MDS-UPDRS Part I fatigue item went from 3 to 0 and lightheadedness from 3 to 1. Daytime sleepiness increased from 1 to 2. The report gives no food ingredient or isolated dietary contrast. She later used home meal delivery and had fewer medication errors. [Original 2024 clinical report](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjrmc/61/6/61_23053/_pdf)
