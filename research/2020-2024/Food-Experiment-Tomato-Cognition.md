# Tomato preparations and cognitive outcomes, 2020–2024

## MITOS tomato-paste registration

The University of Barcelona crossover registration NCT05891977 was first posted June 7, 2023, after an initial submission on May 17. It enrolled healthy adults and prespecified a secondary executive-function comparison using the Wisconsin Card Sorting Test, together with separate attention and memory outcomes. The public record was completed and had no tabulated results when retrieved on November 15, 2026 UTC. The clinical article was published in May 2026. The published trial tested concentrated tomato paste consumed with or without further cooking and used the modified version of the test. ## Registered tomato congee trial in healthy older adults

TCTR20201020003 lists registration on October 20, 2020 and enrollment on January 26, 2021, with 66 planned participants. The dose groups took 200 or 400 mg/day of the preparation named tomato-pomace extract in the registry. The eight-week N100 and P300 evoked potentials are primary. A computerized cognitive battery, safety and biomarker measures are secondary. The completed registry has blank results fields. The later publication calls its ingredient a boiled tomato powder, gives December 20, 2020 as the ethics date, and reports 60 completers. The official enrollment field comes after the reported ethics date. The purpose is cognitive processing in healthy volunteers. ## Multicomponent fruit and vegetable capsules and executive testing

Carrillo et al. tested encapsulated, homogenized fruit and vegetable juice and pulp powders in a double-blind crossover with two 16-week periods and four weeks of washout. Tomato was one of many ingredients and lycopene was among the listed bioactive constituents. Raspberry, blackberry and carrot were also listed in unspecified proportions. The contrast concerns the combined dehydrated capsule. The comparator contained microcrystalline cellulose. The abstract describes 108 healthy adults, and the Methods describe 92 participants included in the study after exclusions. Table 3 reports TESEN execution speed falling from 406.83 to 355.68 on the product and 402.28 to 393.85 on placebo. The displayed significance labels test pre-to-post changes within periods. The paper reports p < .01 for change in product-period speed and p = .25 in the placebo period. The product-period execution score rose from 19.69 to 25.62 and the placebo-period score from 19.88 to 21.38. The table gives no uncertainty for a direct crossover treatment contrast. The study does not isolate cooked tomato or include people with Parkinson disease.
