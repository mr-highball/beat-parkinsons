# Grape preparations and hand function

## Grape seed extract and simple and complex finger tapping

First published online January 16, 2020. Sixty healthy adults aged 18–30 received 400 mg grape-seed polyphenol extract or maltodextrin for 12 weeks. The baseline-adjusted simple tapping estimates in 30 s at weeks 6 and 12 were 123.24 and 125.71 with extract versus 124.36 and 121.43 with placebo. Treatment-by-visit p=.005. Complex sequences showed no clear interaction. The Methods identify NCT03526406. A preliminary page instead names NCT03579095, whose official record assigns American ginseng. The study used isolated seed extract and healthy participants. [Original manuscript Methods and Table 3](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/88613/3/Bell%20et%20al%2C%20Nutritional%20Neuroscience%2C%20author%20final%20copy.pdf) · [Local text](Source-Text/2020-Bell-Grape-Seed-Tapping-Table-Extract.txt)

## Bilberry and grape juice with pegboard testing

First published November 22, 2021. Sixty older men with subjective memory complaints entered a randomized comparison of bilberry and red grape juice with placebo for nine weeks. The dominant-hand Grooved Pegboard change comparison gave p=.41. The mixed juice and non-Parkinson population leave raw grape dexterity unresolved. The null estimate does not establish an opposing direction. [Primary mixed juice report and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8607697/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2021-Bohn-Bilberry-Grape-Pegboard-Table-Extract.txt)

## Registered Enovita grape seed extract and finger tapping

First posted March 13, 2024. This 76-person record lists four parallel arms including placebo, Enovita grape seed, bilberry and ginkgo extracts. Bilateral finger-tapping performance was planned. The 2026 Papa paper explicitly names the registration but reports continuous performance and mood in an extract versus placebo subset. Numerical tapping outcomes are not verified.
