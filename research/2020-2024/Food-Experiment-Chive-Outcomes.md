# Chives in a Parkinson diet and composition study in 2020–2024

## Facial expression in the chive-containing Parkinson menu

Hegelmaier and colleagues first published on 6 February 2020. Their 14-day ovo-lacto vegetarian intervention lists chives among the vegetables in Appendix A. Six participants with Parkinson's received the diet alone, and ten also received bowel cleansing. The menu gives neither the chive species and preparation nor individual amounts. Both groups received the same menu.

Methods section 2.6 explicitly lists facial expression as an item of the UPDRS III exam. Figure 4 reports lower total motor scores at one year in the diet and diet-plus-cleansing groups (p = .033 and .005). The supplementary caption describes individual UPDRS III totals during the 14-day intervention. The published results supply no separate facial-expression estimate. Changes in other items may drive the motor total, leaving the direction of the facial result and any dried-chive effect on claim 0179 open.

Table 1 provides baseline Bristol Stool Scale and UPDRS IV data for the larger case-control cohort. The longitudinal intervention results report stool microbial measures and the motor examination total without an item-level dyskinesia result or a constipation-severity comparison after eating chives.

## Ferulic acid in common chive tissue

Dai and colleagues first published on 25 February 2024. They separated the round green and bottom white parts of postharvest common chives (*Allium schoenoprasum*) and used UHPLC-MS/MS after extraction to quantify free and bound phenolics. Section 3.2.2 and Figure 3N give ferulic-acid ranges of 22.22–28.79 mg/kg fresh weight in green tissue and 0.85–1.93 mg/kg in bottom white tissue across the study conditions. The study varies storage temperature and time and provides no culinary dried-leaf composition or intake measure. It reports chemical content without a participant outcome. The tissue concentrations establish a constituent link to the separate studies of purified ferulic acid.
