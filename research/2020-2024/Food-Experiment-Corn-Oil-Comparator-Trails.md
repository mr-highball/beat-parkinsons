# Corn oil and gait comparator in an older adult rehabilitation pilot

## Four-metre gait speed with corn oil and maltodextrin

Tessier and colleagues reported on a 16-week blinded, randomized pilot involving 20 older adults in rehabilitation, with ten completing the study. The comparator included liquid corn oil and maltodextrin. The other regimen included fish oil, vitamin D, whey and leucine. The oil was dispensed in amber bottles and the paper does not give a refining grade. Four-metre walking speed was measured during the six-minute walk. The control group average gait-speed change was +0.12 m/s (95% CI +0.01 to +0.23), and the multinutrient group average change was +0.03 m/s (95% CI −0.16 to +0.22). These are within-group changes from a small feasibility study with substantial attrition and a rehabilitation co-intervention.

The observed direction in speed provides another comparison for 0324. The study recorded speed and other mobility tasks. Stride and the isolated oil effect remain undetermined. ## Placebo composition in a post-COVID olfaction trial

Lerner and colleagues used the Brief Smell Identification Test in a randomized trial of oral fish-oil capsules after COVID-related olfactory symptoms. The mean change at six weeks was +0.68 under placebo and +1.12 under omega-3 (p=.221). The trial register and full protocol identify matching manufacturer-supplied capsules. Their composition fields do not identify a corn-oil ingredient. Earlier studies of postoperative and postviral smell outcomes used saline-only or smell-training-only control groups. These reports trace clinical smell outcomes while exposure attribution for 0823 remains open. ## Corn oil sham comparison in a mouse olfactory training experiment

Published February 7, 2020. Zhou and colleagues randomized 31 mice to a corn-oil sham injection, induced olfactory loss without training, or induced loss plus odor training. The day-28 buried-pellet times were 26.63 ± 7.60 s in sham controls, 176.63 ± 61.06 s in the injured untrained arm, and 67.79 ± 32.54 s in the injured trained arm. The reported differences concern injury and training, with no oil-free control or feeding intervention. Refining and Parkinson status are outside the comparison.
