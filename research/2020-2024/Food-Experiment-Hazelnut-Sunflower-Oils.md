# Hazelnut and sunflower oil evidence

## Refined sunflower oil comparator in the BENEFIC cognitive analysis

Fortier and colleagues published combined BENEFIC results October 26, 2020. The report gives 83 completers, 44 in the matched high-oleic sunflower beverage group and 39 in the MCT group. The same formulation used in the 2019 first phase was refined, bleached, winterized and deodorized. In Table 2, sunflower-arm Trail Making switching time changed from 130 to 131 seconds (between-arm p = .664), and total errors across conditions from 1.7 to 2.5, compared with 2.7 to 1.8 in the MCT group (adjusted between-arm p = .017). Among completers, 40% of sunflower comparator and 74% of MCT participants reported at least one adverse event. The 2021 white-matter subsample is a report from this trial. It enrolled adults with mild cognitive impairment and gives no effect of cold-pressed sunflower oil in Parkinson’s. [Aggregate report, Table 2](https://alz-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/alz.12206) · [2021 white matter subsample](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8596139/) · [2015 registration and 2019 first phase](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Hazelnut-Sunflower-Oils.md#benefic-registration-and-first-sunflower-comparator-report)

## Native and repeatedly heated sunflower oil in rat behavioral testing

Zarei and colleagues first published November 10, 2020. Wistar rats received diets with native or repeatedly heated sunflower or canola oil, or their heated oils with added ginger or turmeric. After 90 days the original abstract reports poorer memory and motor coordination in heated-oil groups versus their corresponding native-oil controls (p < .05). The tests included novel object recognition, spontaneous alternation and rotarod. Processing before experimental heating and exact task-specific results were unavailable in the accessible abstract. No Parkinson model or oil-free arm was identified. [Original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33170419/)

## Sunflower oil comparator in the Shiraz major depression trial

Foshati and colleagues published the mood report August 3, 2021. Seventy-three adults with major depressive disorder were randomized to 25 mL/day of sunflower oil or extra-virgin olive oil for 52 days. The primary abstract reports no clear BDI-II change within either arm, with a between-arm comparison of p = .021. HAMD-7 declined more with olive oil (p = .001). An accepted manuscript from the same MSc project was publicly posted April 21 and published as version of record May 4, 2021. It reports anthropometric, lipid and inflammatory outcomes in 62 people and does not provide the depression scale results. The official 2016 registry matches the intervention and planned sample and lists metabolic outcomes. Product processing and arm-level mood score values remain unverified. [Original mood report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34358723/) · [Companion metabolic report](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ijcp.14254) · [2016 registration](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Hazelnut-Sunflower-Oils.md#shiraz-major-depression-and-sunflower-oil-trial-registration)

## Sunflower oil capsules and cognition and mood in mild cognitive impairment

Mengelberg’s 2018 doctoral thesis was deposited publicly March 9, 2020. The journal report first appeared March 25, 2022, from the same ACTRN12614000874617 trial. Seventy-two older adults with mild cognitive impairment were randomized and 60 analyzed after 12 months. The thesis identifies 3 g/day of sunflower oil supplying 1857 mg/day linoleic acid, with orange oil and vitamin E common to both the DHA/EPA and comparison capsules. Table 2 of the journal report gives sunflower-arm Trail Making B percentile scores of 49.8 to 56.8, with no treatment difference (p = .91). On the 20-second dominant-hand Coin Rotation Task, age- and sex-standardized scores in the sunflower arm changed from −0.25 to −0.12, with no clear treatment difference (p = .38). The overall depression and anxiety treatment tests give p = .95 and p = .64. APOE ε4 interactions favored DHA on depression (p = .04) and anxiety (p = .02). The cohort and design do not establish a sunflower-oil effect in Parkinson’s and the preparation grade is unknown. [Public thesis deposit](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/items/2639b071-e66e-453c-9f5c-a85b535beb54/full) · [Thesis Methods §7.5](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/server/api/core/bitstreams/75f1ac35-0219-4a6b-9048-0ae5691e99c2/content) · [Journal Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9321856/) · [2014 registration](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Hazelnut-Sunflower-Oils.md#sunflower-comparator-in-two-australian-and-new-zealand-registrations)

## Cold pressed hazelnut oil and drug induced sleep in a liver injury assay

Mulkijanyan and colleagues presented PP23 in September 2024 in Tbilisi. In mice with chemically induced liver injury, oral cold-pressed hazelnut oil plus the injury exposure yielded pentobarbital-induced sleeping times of 8.8 and 10.6 minutes at days 3 and 10, compared with 14.9 and 21.5 minutes in injured comparators and 9.6 minutes in intact controls. The Methods and Table 1 give different oil volumes, and the abstract does not give uncertainty estimates for the sleep times. This is a drug-induced sleep assay of liver injury and does not measure spontaneous insomnia or daytime sleepiness. No toasted oil or Parkinson model was used. [Conference proceedings, pp. 71–72](https://ikip.tsmu.edu/files/ABSTRACT_BOOKGIBS_2024Final_Version1_1727420154.pdf#page=71)

## Heated sunflower oil in an intergenerational rat study

Keremane and colleagues published December 20, 2024. The study compared heated sunflower and canola oils with their corresponding native oils in an intergenerational feeding design. The abstract reports that heated-oil diets increased anxiety-like behavior and decreased memory and motor coordination (p < .05). It gives the elevated plus maze, Y-maze, novel object recognition and rotarod among outcome tests. Processing grade and arm-level estimates were unavailable in the accessible abstract. This distinct experiment used no Parkinson model. [Original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39704875/)

## Sunflower seed oil rinse and salivary output in healthy adults

The German register posted DRKS00011922 on April 5, 2017. In the September 19, 2020 results, three adults completed saline rinses followed by a washout and sunflower seed oil rinses. Collected saliva was higher with oil than saline in all but one session comparison. The trial was fixed-sequence, studied the oral microbiome and measured neither swallowing performance nor voice or Parkinson’s. [Local primary text](Source-Text/PMC8060239.txt) · [Official registration](https://drks.de/search/en/trial/DRKS00011922) · [2017 registry record](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Hazelnut-Sunflower-Oils.md#sunflower-seed-oil-rinse-registration-in-healthy-volunteers)

## Cold pressed sunflower oil rinse and subjective swallowing

Ludwar and colleagues first published their journal report in December 2020. Twenty-four of 26 randomized participants completed a medication-related dry mouth crossover. Participants swished cold-pressed Wiberg sunflower oil made from roasted kernels and spat it out. After a week, eight reported easier swallowing with oil versus two with mineral water (33% versus 8%, p = .031, unadjusted). Table 3 also records that four reported easier talking with oil versus eight with water, with no statistically clear difference (p = .250). The primary dryness change was −1.53 with oil and −0.36 with water (p = .067). Stimulated and unstimulated saliva-flow changes favored water (p = .010 each). Participants knew the product used, and the secondary swallowing comparison did not adjust for multiple outcomes, time period or carryover. There was no instrumental swallowing, vocal loudness, ingestion or separately reported Parkinson outcome. [Primary Methods and Table 3](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/odi.13752) · [2020 conference abstract](https://iadr.abstractarchives.com/abstract/20iags-3301613/efficacy-of-oil-pulling-for-relief-of-medication-induced-xerostomia)

## Sunflower oil as a comparator in an executive task trial

Power and colleagues first published December 7, 2021. Sixty cognitively healthy older adults were randomized to sunflower-oil capsules or capsules with fish oil, carotenoids and vitamin E. After 24 months, stage-eight spatial working memory errors were essentially unchanged in the sunflower group (11.06 to 11.18), while the active group had fewer errors (11.32 to 7.05, reported group-by-time effect size η² = .090, 90% CI .005–.189). Immediate memory improved in the oil group, which the authors note may reflect test practice or influential active-arm outcomes. The product's extraction grade was unspecified. The design supplies no untreated group or Parkinson-specific estimate. [Primary Methods, Table 2 and Figure 2](https://profjohnnolan.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/Power-et-al.-2022-1.pdf) · [ISRCTN10431469](https://doi.org/10.1186/ISRCTN10431469)

## Sunflower oil meal and postmeal attention

Madison and colleagues published in 2020 from NCT04247763. Fifty-one women contributed attention data in a randomized crossover of meals with high-oleic sunflower oil or a higher-saturated-fat formulation. The high-saturated-fat meal impaired discrimination of targets in a continuous performance task relative to the sunflower-oil meal (reported p = .02). The attention result concerns an acute meal comparison. The registry's fatigue assessment times were before the meals and at later study follow-ups. Neither arm establishes a direction for Parkinson fatigue or a cold-pressed oil's effect on executive impairment. [Primary methods and results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7266694/) · [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04247763)

## Sunflower oil compared with saline in rat motor tests

Casanova and colleagues published their tolcapone delivery study May 17, 2022. The control group included separate sunflower-oil and saline subgroups of four rats each. The groups had no statistically clear difference on either catalepsy test or on akinesia. The experiment separately compared rotenone injury and tolcapone formulations. The vehicle comparison used injection and an unspecified oil preparation. Catalepsy is an indirect motor proxy and the study provides no clinical rigidity or dystonia outcome. [Local primary text, Figure 3 and Results](Source-Text/PMC9143005.txt#L212) · [Published article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9143005/)

## Sunflower oil oral placebo and depression ratings

Schienle and Jurinec published separate trials in adults treated for major depression. The first combined a CBT course and sunflower oil framed as a natural remedy. The second used a sunflower-oil placebo with a positive suggestion during the wait for psychotherapy, or standard waiting. Mean BDI-II change over the waiting period was −3.30 versus −0.05 (p < .001), although there was no clear group difference at either the pre or post visit alone. The oil and suggestion were combined, with no oil-plus-neutral-instruction arm. A distinct 2022 open-label CBT trial used sunflower oil disclosed as placebo. None gives a Parkinson effect or identifies refining grade. [First primary paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165032720326938) · [Waiting-period primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12144-022-04206-4) · [Open-label CBT trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9228278/)

## High oleic sunflower oil comparator in prostate cancer

Savard and colleagues published October 3, 2023. The high-oleic sunflower oil and EPA capsule groups each included 65 men around prostatectomy. Table 3 gives sunflower-group HADS depression 3.2 at baseline and 2.3 at 12 months, HADS anxiety 6.3 and 4.2, and Fatigue Symptom Inventory severity 8.3 and 8.0. No group-by-time difference was reported for these measures. A perceived cognitive impact subscale improved over time only in the sunflower group, while the other cognitive subscales did not show an interaction. Recovery and repeated assessment occurred in both groups, and there was no no-oil group. The study gives no Parkinson estimate or processing grade. [Local primary article and Table 3](Source-Text/PMC10587967.txt) · [Published article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10587967/) · [Trial registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02333435)

## Sunflower oil comparator during a weight loss diet

Vahedi and colleagues published in May 2023. Sixty women with nonalcoholic fatty liver disease were assigned sesame or sunflower oil alongside the same calorie-reduction advice. Fifty-three completed the study. The authors report lower fatigue, anxiety and depression in the sesame arm compared with sunflower oil. A companion report identifies both oils as refined and odorless. The mood article’s public abstract does not provide numerical changes in the sunflower arm or the refining method. The population, dietary co-intervention and comparator limit the application to Parkinson symptoms. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1108/NFS-12-2022-0407) · [Companion methods and registry](Food-Experiment-Cinnamon-Sesame.md#sesame-oil-preparation-in-a-fatty-liver-trial-family)

## Hazelnut oil in an older adult working memory trial

The 2023 pilot compared high-oleic and high-palmitic diets in older adults. The high-oleic fat supplement was entirely hazelnut oil, the high-palmitic supplement combined palm, peanut and olive oils, and the control diet included hazelnut and sunflower oils. Functional MRI showed a difference in activation during the working-memory task, while the N-back performance comparison did not clearly differ (p = .09). A subsequent University of Vermont trial, NCT06192407, posted January 5, 2024 and plans N-back accuracy and imaging outcomes. The November 4, 2026 registry update lists recruiting and no posted results. The high-oleic arm specifies 100% hazelnut oil mixed with meals. Pressing, visual hallucinations and Parkinson disease remain outside the registry fields. Neither study enrolled a Parkinson cohort or tested a frozen hazelnut-oil symptom endpoint. In the March 10, 2023 full report, 12 participants were randomized and ten completed. One withdrew for difficulty tolerating the diet restrictions and the other for an unspecified medical reason. The latter had no reported event type, study period, or oil attribution. The high-oleic supplement was all hazelnut oil and the control blend was 43.8% hazelnut oil. Beck depression and anxiety scores were collected only at screening. There was no reported visual hallucination instrument or adverse-event table. The paper gives a funding identifier and no separate trial registration for this cohort. [2023 primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10318304/) · [Local original-methods extract](Source-Text/2023-Dumas-Hazelnut-Psychiatric-Screening-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06192407) · [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT06192407.txt)

## Hazelnut oil chocolate and measured salivary secretion

Burkard and colleagues published April 9, 2024. In a controlled sensory comparison, participants licked and expectorated fat matrices with identical sugar content. One matrix contained cocoa butter and the other replaced part of the cocoa butter with hazelnut oil. Supplementary Figure S5c shows no clear difference in saliva per gram of sample. The reported F(1,33) = 3.58 concerns salivary secretion's contribution to soluble solids, and is not an oil effect on drooling. The study reports no oil extraction grade, ingestion requirement, clinical sialorrhea or Parkinson cohort. [Local primary article](Source-Text/PMC11372135.txt#L99) · [Publisher article and Supplementary Figure S5c](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-58838-6)

## Refined sunflower cooking oil and executive function in the DICe pilot

Raysa El Zein reported the DICe results in an August 2022 Bournemouth University doctoral thesis, publicly deposited September 5. It is the completed NCT03727386 study registered under 1718/IRASREZ/1. The thesis reports 31 participants randomized, 18 to coconut and 13 to sunflower, with 28 completing. The analyzed group included older adults with and without mild cognitive impairment. The Parkinson exclusion in the registration and lack of separate MCI estimates limit transfer to Parkinson symptoms.

Participants were allocated 30 mL/day for six months. Methods §4.1.1 and protocol Appendix 1 identify the sunflower comparator as KTC sunflower oil supplied from Sainsbury’s. A 2018 KTC specification describes physically refined, degummed, bleached, winterized and deodorized sunflower oil. The trial report gives no lot certificate. In the D-KEFS Trail Making Number–Letter Switching task, the reported between-group comparison was 81.1 seconds (SD 18.9) in the sunflower group and 110.1 seconds (SD 45.6) in the coconut group (p = .037). Table 5.8 gives p = .07 for the sunflower group’s within-arm pre–post change. Across Trail Making conditions the oil-by-time interaction was p = .026. Table 5.13 reports a descriptive sunflower effect size d = .61 in the favorable direction on the switching task. The pilot used an active oil comparison, so it does not isolate sunflower against no oil. It neither establishes a result in Parkinson’s nor matches the frozen cold-pressed preparation. [Bournemouth thesis record](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/37440/) · [Thesis pp. 59, 96–99 and 108](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/37440/1/EL%20ZEIN%2C%20Raysa_Ph.D._2022.pdf) · [Official trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03727386) · [2018 KTC technical specification](https://www.tfsltd.co.uk/media/filer_public/f5/d0/f5d0b0d4-a0c8-4072-872c-6503e8b4dd2d/sunflower_oil_-_june_2018.pdf)

## Sunflower oil expectancy trials and three month follow-up

The 2020 Jurinec and Schienle trial randomized 126 adults with major depression among a CBT course, the same course plus daily sunflower oil described as a natural remedy, and waiting. The 2021 report followed 82 from the two course arms. At three months, 74% of the oil-plus-suggestion course group and 53% of the course group were below the BDI-II clinical cutoff. The oil, suggestion and therapy engagement changed together. A separate 2022 open-label add-on trial randomized 60 adults to CBT with or without sunflower oil disclosed as placebo and linked to relaxation practice. Mean BDI-II changes were −7.93 versus −5.47 (p = .001) by course end and the between-group reduction at three months was inconclusive (p = .052). The design lacked a group receiving the oil without the added expectancy and practice cues. Neither trial reports refining grade or a Parkinson cohort. [2020 original](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33065817/) · [2021 same-trial follow-up](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7912085/) · [2022 separate original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9228278/) · [Local comparison text](Source-Text/2020-2022-Schienle-Jurinec-Sunflower-Expectancy-Extract.txt)

## Sunflower oil emulsion in the DEPOXIN pediatric trial

Trebatická and colleagues first published the 60-participant clinical report on March 7, 2020. Table 2 shows mean Children’s Depression Inventory scores of 24.9 and 22.2 at baseline and week 12 for the sunflower emulsion group. The paired change is reported as −2.9 and the within-arm Friedman p is .233. The fish-oil comparison shows 27.8 and 21.3, with a reported paired change of −7.5. The unadjusted week-12 change contrast had p=.064 and the baseline-adjusted overall ANCOVA p=.021. Companion studies use the same trial ID and report laboratory outcomes. The sunflower grade was unspecified and the design compared two oils, with no Parkinson population. ## Depression reporting in the sunflower-carrier inhalation trial

Lucena and colleagues first published online on April 24, 2021. The author abstract analyzes the trial of lavender aroma versus the sunflower-oil carrier in 35 women with postmenopausal insomnia, 17 and 18 per arm. Both groups received sleep-hygiene guidance. The reported depression p = .025 describes change in the lavender arm. The abstract supplies no numerical between-arm depression estimate. Registry RBR-5q5t5z reports 36 included and 33 who performed the intervention. Its figures and the article's 35 are kept under their original denominators. The cohort lacks a Parkinson diagnosis and the protocol does not test oral refined oil.
