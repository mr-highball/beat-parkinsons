# Sesame oil cake and Rhus gall cognition trials

## Sesame oil cake extract and memory tests

Jung and colleagues. Nutrients. 2021;13(8):2606. DOI `10.3390/nu13082606`, PMCID PMC8399671. The 12-week randomized double-blind pilot used an extract of the solid sesame material left after oil production and assessed older adults with memory impairment. The authors report benefits on memory measures, especially verbal learning. The discussion explicitly states that attention was not assessed. The published outcome is separate from its 2019 registration, and the preparation differs from toasted oil. No clinically attributable direction for claim 0528 follows. [Original article](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/13/8/2606) · [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03826121)

## Rhus javanica gall extract and attention tasks

Heuer and colleagues. Functional Foods in Health and Disease. 2023;13(10). First public October 20, 2023. DOI `10.31989/ffhd.v13i10.1213`. Eighty adults were randomized and 68 completed the six-week trial of oral Galla Rhois extract or cornstarch. The intervention is medicinal insect-induced galls of Rhus javanica. Culinary ground sumac is typically Rhus coriaria fruit. In the per-protocol report the adjusted ASRS score was 35.14 versus 38.73, p = .006. Number-count accuracy and time gave p = .67 and p = .24. Tower of Hanoi completion time gave p = .03, with accuracy p = .09. The abstract makes broader quality-of-life claims than the Table 5 subscales support. The endpoints and plant materials should be kept separate. The trial did not include a Parkinson cohort or ground fruit. [Original published full text](https://ffhdj.com/index.php/ffhd/article/download/1213/1932/8012)
