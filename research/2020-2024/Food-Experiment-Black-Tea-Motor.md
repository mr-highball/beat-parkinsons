# Caffeine and Parkinson motor outcomes

## Short caffeine trial and total motor responder status

In 2024 Hamdan and colleagues reported a double-blind trial of 50 mg isolated caffeine twice daily, or starch placebo, for 21 days. Twelve of 15 caffeine completers and two of 12 placebo completers exceeded the four-point total motor improvement threshold described in the methods (p = .004, OR 20, 95% CI 2.77–144.31). Baseline motor severity categories differed across arms. Tea was among the beverages participants were told to avoid. The methods discuss scoring rest tremor and bradykinesia, while the published results give no separate estimate for either. The article calls the scale MDS-UPDRS III and describes a 108-point maximum. The adverse-event narrative gives four of 15 caffeine participants, whereas Table 6 uses four of 12. ## PROBE-PD prevalence results and separate black-tea questionnaire

Brazil's PROBE-PD protocol asks about black tea and records the motor examination. The official registry listed no posted results on November 17, 2026. A separate 2024 study abstract reports Parkinson and other parkinsonism prevalence across four sites. The October 2025 conference schedule lists a South Brazil age- and sex-specific follow-up. The public prevalence abstract does not give an exposure-by-severity analysis, and the 2026 author preprint covers household insecticides. A black-tea-specific resting tremor finding remains open.
