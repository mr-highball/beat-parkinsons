# Dietary exposure and symptom evidence

## Cooked black beans and chewing to self-rated swallow readiness

First published October 22, 2021. Seventeen adult volunteers chewed 5 g cooked black-bean samples with different electric-field or calcium processing histories. They spat out each bolus when they judged it ready to swallow. Median chewing times were 28.5, 29.0 and 24.75 seconds across samples A, B and C. Other analyses concerned particle size, salivary enzyme activity and digestion in vitro. The protocol did not require swallowing. It did not measure aspiration or clinical swallowing difficulty. ## Time-sampled chewing of cooked black beans

The journal issue and university listing are January 2022. An earlier precise public date has not been verified. The original abstract reports 22 participants chewing samples of cooked black beans for up to 30 seconds. PEF-treated beans without added calcium gave smaller particles at 18 and 24 seconds than two calcium-containing preparations (p < .05). The abstract does not establish an observed swallow or clinical swallowing difficulty. Participant overlap with the earlier 17-person study remains unverified.
