# Pomegranate exposure and outcomes in Parkinson research and related studies

## Pomegranate on a Parkinson diet menu

Hegelmaier and colleagues’ February 2020 study included pomegranate in the fruit list for a two-week mixed vegetarian intervention. Six participants received the diet and ten received the diet plus bowel cleansing. The study reports composite UPDRS III motor changes and microbiome measures. Appendix A identifies pomegranate generically and supplies no preparation, portions, individual intake or ingredient-level comparison. Drooling, visual hallucination frequency, daytime sleepiness and apathy were outside the reported Part III outcome. [Local primary article, Figure 4 and Appendix A](Source-Text/PMC7072275.txt) · [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.3390/cells9020376)

## Registered nanoemulsified pomegranate seed oil in Parkinson disease

The December 2, 2021 registration for NCT05142085 (INNN0121) plans a randomized blinded comparison of nanoemulsified pomegranate seed oil capsules and neutral mineral oil over six months. The structured enrollment estimate is 170, while the narrative and arm descriptions give different counts. The co-primary measures are motor MDS-UPDRS and the original Non-Motor Symptoms Scale (NMSS). NMSS items cover saliva, daytime sleepiness, hallucinations and loss of interest. Its aggregate scores combine severity and frequency, so separate item components would be needed for the frozen outcomes. The description also mentions a Parkinson sleep scale and monitoring adverse events. The November 2026 public API gives status unknown, last verified November 2021, no posted results, and twelve references labeled background. The registered completion dates are estimated. The same-sponsor COGMEG5 registration is a separate cognition and fatigue study in older adults with cognitive impairment. [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT05142085.txt) · [Current official record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05142085) · [NMSS instrument](https://www.movementdisorders.org/MDS/MDS-Rating-Scales/Non-Motor-Symptoms-Scale-for-Parkinsons-Disease-NMSS.htm) · [COGMEG5](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06520878)

## Nanoemulsified seed oil in a separate Mexican rat study

Sánchez-Camacho and colleagues’ November 25, 2024 study compared GranaGard before or after a rotenone regimen in rats. Motor performance and biochemical responses varied among animals and by measured feature. Their exploratory multivariate results suggested stronger preservation of some motor features with earlier use than recovery after exposure. The authors, institutional affiliations and methods differ from the Boll human registration, and the article gives no human outcomes. GranaGard was also tested in a separate multiple sclerosis cognition pilot. Neither publication supplies a Parkinson symptom result for raw fruit or for NCT05142085. [Local 2024 rat article](Source-Text/PMC11640963.txt) · [Multiple sclerosis pilot](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34243101/)

## Pomegranate juice and olfaction in the Poznań study family

The July 14, 2021 conference record and August 25 journal paper by Kujawska and colleagues use the same exposure schedule and share authors and outcomes. Count them as one report family. In the journal paper, concentrate increased vertical activity by 160% versus the toxin arm, while the reported 57% increase in horizontal activity was inconclusive. Olfactory discrimination improved and midbrain dopamine depletion was attenuated. The 2019 and 2021 publications share a grant, an animal approval and much of the protocol. Reported cohort accounting differs, and independent replication between those reports is unverified. A separate 2021 study by Fathy and colleagues used a paraquat mouse model and compared juice and seed extract arms, with motor and tissue outcomes. These studies did not measure the four frozen nonmotor symptoms. [Conference record](https://sciforum.net/paper/10672) · [Local journal article, Figures 2–4](Source-Text/PMC8467386.txt) · [Local paraquat article](Source-Text/PMC8074500.txt) · [2019 report](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Pomegranate.md#pomegranate-juice-and-postural-stability-in-poznań)

## Whole pomegranate fruit and acute saliva in children

Kannaiah’s June 25, 2024 dental crossover report compared simple fruit, fresh juice and processed juice. The 20-child pomegranate group’s Table 10 lists flow of 2.49 ± 0.16 mL/min for unstimulated baseline saliva and 3.30 ± 0.15 mL/min for stimulated saliva at 30 minutes, without a fruit-specific flow split. The paper separately tabulates pH for each form. The flow comparison changes the collection conditions as well as time. It cannot establish a reduction in resting saliva or drooling. The cohort was pediatric. [Primary journal page and full-text Table 10](https://doi.org/10.21088/ijde.0974.6099.17224.1)

## Pomegranate rind extract and sleep changes in premenstrual syndrome

NCT06201702 and the September 14, 2024 article document the same randomized trial in 60 women. The extract arm received pomegranate fruit rind and the control arm received no intervention. In Table 2, the sleep-change subscale averaged 8.40 after extract versus 9.63 in controls (p = .146). Fatigue was also inconclusive at the endpoint (19.73 versus 20.80, p = .394), with a baseline imbalance. No dedicated daytime sleepiness or apathy measure was reported. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06201702) · [Publisher, Table 2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032724015684) · [Publication metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39284531/)

## Registered pomegranate juice and sleep during opioid treatment

NCT05861544 first posted May 17, 2023. The study registered oral pomegranate juice for adults receiving methadone or buprenorphine. The protocol uses a global Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index that includes a daytime dysfunction component. It also planned fatigue and mood ratings, including vigor, without an apathy scale. The 2025 result papers report outcomes from this registration. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05861544) · [Published protocol](https://www.ijocs.org/clinical-journal/pomegranate-juice-consumption-by-patients-under-medication-for-addiction-treatment-as-regulator-of-craving-and-blood-redox-status-16972.html) · [Dated outcomes](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Pomegranate.md#pomegranate-juice-and-sleep-quality-during-opioid-treatment)

## Pomegranate peel in a botanical alertness trial

Published July 4, 2021. Thirty-six healthy adults were randomized to a four-botanical blend including pomegranate peel, caffeine or placebo. Day-21 predose mean alertness was 7.58 (SD .84) on blend, 6.16 (1.81) on caffeine and 6.42 (1.49) on placebo. Follow-up comparison with placebo gave p=.03, while the group-by-session interaction was p=.07. The blend, healthy population and MAPSS alertness subscale leave raw arils and Parkinson daytime sleepiness unresolved. [Primary trial and Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8336296/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2021-Tartar-Botanical-Alertness-Table-Extract.txt)

## Investigator conference report of nanoemulsified seed oil use

Ruth Gabizon described the Nano-PSO GranaGard program at the PICAD and MeCoND conference on February 11, 2023. Printed proceedings page 96 says that patients with several neurological conditions, including Parkinson disease, were receiving the formulation. It gives no Parkinson cohort size, comparison, outcome estimate or trial identifier. The numerical verbal outcome refers to a separate multiple sclerosis study. The proceedings provide a possible clinical program lead and leave its relationship to NCT05142085 / INNN0121 unverified.
