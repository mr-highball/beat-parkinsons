# Food experiment source follow-up

## BerryCare thesis and implementation reports

The University of Kentucky lists Moellering’s BerryCare thesis as a 2019 thesis with a public availability date of July 25, 2021. The repository abstract describes the older adult nutrition collaboration. Its PDF download returned HTTP 403 on both a web preview and the native retrieval. Brewer and colleagues’ qualitative partnership article was first public on July 15, 2022 and appeared in its journal issue in 2024. Its interviews and focus groups examined program implementation. The registration, thesis and papers are linked as reports from the same program. ## BerryCare gait speed and fresh blackberries

Koempel and colleagues published the senior-center results on December 8, 2023. The Short Physical Performance Battery included an eight-foot walk. The mean baseline gait speed subscore was 2.6 ± 1.3 out of four among the 28 enrolled older adults (Table 2). Attrition reached 82%, and the authors did not estimate a baseline-to-follow-up change. A separate plate-waste exercise confirmed consumption of plain blackberries from the garden. Fresh berry consumption and a relevant gait instrument were present in the project, while an analyzable within-person change and a Parkinson-specific cohort were unavailable. [Primary paper, pp. 3–5](Source-Text/2023-Koempel-BerryCare.txt) · [Publisher](https://www.cmjpublishers.com/ajser-140-abstract/) · [Official registration](../2015-2019/Source-Text/Trials/NCT03477305.txt)

## Red raspberry, spatial working memory and other measured outcomes

In summer 2023 Xiao, Shukitt-Hale and Burton-Freeman reported interim results of NCT03879213 to the North American Raspberry & Blackberry Association. Figure 7 shows fewer between-search errors and better strategies at the six-hour assessment after a meal containing red raspberry powder. The plotted six-hour means are about 6.9 versus 9.5 for eight-box between-search errors, with the comparison marked p < .05. [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2023-Xiao-Raspberry-Spatial-Working-Memory-Factual-Extract.txt) The investigators list Profile of Mood States, psychomotor and additional cognitive tests and state that the remaining outcomes did not attain statistical significance. They do not give a separate numerical anxiety or tension contrast. This is an earlier report from the same study family as the 2026 journal article. It gives a second source for the reported null mood outcomes in a non-Parkinson population and for the timing of the working-memory signal. [Investigator report, pp. 9–11 and 17](https://www.raspberryblackberry.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/NARBA-2023-BRAMBLE-Summer-Vol-38-Digital-Issue.pdf) · [2019 registration](../2015-2019/Source-Text/Trials/NCT03879213.txt) · [2026 journal article](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Kiwi-and-Caneberries.md#red-raspberry-powder-and-post-meal-spatial-working-memory)

## Raspberry and blackberry in diet-induced hypertension

Meister and colleagues first published November 23, 2022. The publisher preview identifies separate freeze-dried blackberry and raspberry arms and a combination arm in a high-fat, high-sucrose mouse model. Its Figure 2 summary gives systolic pressure as 98.9 ± 6.25 mmHg after raspberry alone (p < .0001) and 106.0 ± 9.10 mmHg after the combination (p = .002), versus 120.6 ± 6.79 mmHg with the high-fat, high-sucrose diet alone. The abstract also reports a combination-arm increase in nitric oxide metabolites. A March 2022 cardiac tissue paper has the same grant, investigators and feeding schedule. It is a likely companion report and is not counted independently. The induced diet model and vascular endpoints differ from symptomatic standing blood pressure in Parkinson’s. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36435288/) · [Publisher Figure 2 preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0955286322002935) · [Cardiac tissue report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35487829/)

## Raspberry and blackberry in angiotensin II-treated rats

In a June 2022 conference abstract, Danh and colleagues reported final systolic pressure 20.8% lower in angiotensin II-treated rats given raspberry than in angiotensin II rats without raspberry (p = .03). The 2024 conference abstract reports lower week-seven systolic pressure in raspberry-only (p = .0001) and mixed blackberry and raspberry (p = .004) groups versus the angiotensin II group. The abstracts share investigators, funding, exposure timing and model. Cohort overlap remains unresolved, so they are tracked as a potentially overlapping study family. These experiments measure induced hypertension in rats. [2022 primary abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9193387/) · [2024 primary abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2475299124014409)

## Hardy kiwifruit original report access

Kitamura and colleagues’ April 2, 2021 paper has DOI 10.1016/j.bbrc.2021.03.116. The investigators froze Shinjo-grown hardy kiwifruit, thawed and peeled the fruit before pressing, and froze the separated juice again. The primary abstract reports a motor outcome after pretreatment with this juice in an MPTP model. The Figure 2 preview reports longer pole-descent and beam-completion times after MPTP versus control and identifies a catalepsy test. The related 2022 conference abstract also names rotarod. The preview cuts off the arm-specific juice estimates, and a public underlying dataset was not located. The funder project links the original research and additional outputs.
