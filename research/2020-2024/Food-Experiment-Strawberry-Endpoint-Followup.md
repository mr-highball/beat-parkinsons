# Strawberry attention and mobility trials, 2020–2024

## Freeze-dried strawberry, attention and fall-related measures

Miller et al. published online January 20, 2021, reporting 37 healthy adults aged 60–75 who completed a 90-day randomized placebo-controlled trial. The intervention was 24 g/day freeze-dried strawberry, not raw fruit. The report found no significant effect on the Attention Network Test, task switching, trail making or digit span. Fall history was checked at entry and people with a fall in the previous year were excluded. The Falls Efficacy Scale assessed fear of falling, not fall frequency, with no group effect (p = .518). Gait and postural sway measures also showed no significant benefit. These findings do not estimate falls in Parkinson disease. A separately published April 2021 erratum corrects Table 1 formatting, not a second trial. ## Strawberry powder and memory interference

Krikorian et al. published October 19, 2023, a 12-week placebo-controlled trial of 13 g/day whole-fruit strawberry powder in overweight middle-aged adults with subjective cognitive decline. The strawberry group had fewer California Verbal Learning Test intrusion errors after adjustment for baseline (F(1,27)=5.69, p=.02). The authors interpret this as memory interference and executive control; it is not a direct measured attention outcome. The trial did not study raw fruit or Parkinson participants.
