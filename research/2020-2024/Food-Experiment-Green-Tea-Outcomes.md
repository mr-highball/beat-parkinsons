# Green tea preparation and olfaction follow-up

## Registered sleep and morning refreshment trial

**UMIN000052248** first posted September 19, 2023. The registry describes a four-week randomized parallel trial with a target of 60 generally healthy Japanese adults reporting sleep problems. The primary measure was sleep EEG. The registry lists an OSA-MA factor translated as “refreshing” in English and 疲労回復, recovery from fatigue, in Japanese. This is the previous night's sleep and morning refreshment scale. The registry gives no posted outcome values and still labels publication unpublished. The later Baba paper cites the registry and identifies the test food as encapsulated matcha. [Official UMIN registration](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000059633) · [Japanese trial record and outcome wording](https://rctportal.mhlw.go.jp/detail/um?trial_id=UMIN000052248) · [Linked paper and outcome table](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/16/17/2907)

## Four-week matcha capsule trial and refreshment on waking

Baba, Takihara and Okamura first published August 31, 2024 (PMID **39275223**, DOI **10.3390/nu16172907**). Their original double-blind trial analyzed 23 participants per arm. Volunteers received 2.7 g/day of powdered matcha in capsules or starch capsules. The paper reports 71.5 mg/day of caffeine in the matcha, with a starch control. OSA-MA Table 3 reports mean change in morning refreshment of +3.47 ± 6.9 points with matcha and +5.12 ± 5.9 with placebo (mean ± SD, between-group p=.39). The change in the separate Epworth daytime sleepiness score was −2.0 ± 3.7 versus −2.9 ± 5.6 (p=.54). The paper's narrative describes lower OSA-MA values, whereas both numerical refreshment scores increased in Table 3.

Table 2 reports no clear between-group difference in total sleep time, sleep latency, wake after sleep onset or sleep efficiency. Time spent in bed after waking was shorter in the matcha group at the last assessment (148 versus 320 seconds, p=.04). Waking behavior, sleepiness and morning refreshment are distinct endpoints. The four-week study supplies capsule and sleep context for the brewed-green-tea Parkinson fatigue claim without measuring that preparation or clinical fatigue severity. [Original tables and methods](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/16/17/2907)

## Twelve-month matcha capsule trial in older adults

Uchida and colleagues first published August 30, 2024 (PMID **39213264**, DOI **10.1371/journal.pone.0309287**). Of 99 randomized adults with subjective or mild cognitive decline, 49 were assigned 2 g of matcha powder per day in nine capsules and 50 received placebo. The main mixed-effects analyses used 46 and 45 participants, respectively. The paper reports a 0.86-point between-group change difference on the secondary Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index in a favorable direction (95% CI −0.002 to 1.71, p=.088). The interval includes zero. The two registered primary cognition and daily living outcomes showed no clear differences. The study permitted habitual green tea intake and evaluated neither Parkinson fatigue severity nor a brewed ingredient intervention. [Original methods and outcomes](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0309287) · [Table 4](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0309287.t004) · [2019 registration](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Green-Tea-Outcomes.md#registered-matcha-capsules-for-cognitive-decline-and-sleep)

## Smell threshold and identification after isolated caffeine

Stafford and Orgill randomized 40 healthy adults to a caffeine capsule or placebo. Among 39 analyzed, there was no overall clear threshold effect. Odor identification change averaged −1.13 with caffeine and +0.03 with placebo (p=.06 for the overall condition effect). An exploratory subgroup of eight nonhabitual caffeine consumers produced opposing threshold and identification signals. The subgroup identification contrast was p=.059. This was single-dose isolated caffeine in healthy adults, without a standardized brewed green tea comparison. [Original table, figures and full text](Source-Text/PMC7683449.txt) · [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-020-05695-6)

## Fatigue history before intravenous green tea extract

The 2022 acute liver injury case concerns an intravenous preparation containing concentrated green tea extract and other agents, as well as patient-reported recent tea drinking. The full case records that fatigue dated back about five years and identifies nausea and upper abdominal pain after the intravenous exposure. Fatigue therefore cannot be counted as a newly reported effect of either recent intake. [Original Case Description](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9745259/)

## Powdered green tea plus exercise trial registration

UMIN000055115 was first posted on August 7, 2024. The cluster trial registered exercise and group sessions in both arms, with green tea powder or placebo powder over 24 weeks in older adults. Grip strength was planned as one physical-function measure, alongside leg strength, walking and balance. The listed outcomes supplied no handwriting size or separate fatigue scale. See the later results for the tested population and endpoints. ## Brewed green tea versus water for postoperative fatigue

Liu and colleagues randomized 80 gastric cancer surgery patients to brewed Laoshan green tea or an equal volume of water under an enhanced recovery program. Seventy-seven were analyzed, 38 in the tea group and 39 in the water group. The brew used a 1:200 tea-to-water ratio. It was prepared at 80 °C for five minutes, filtered and cooled. Participants received 500 mL on postoperative day 1 and 1,000 mL daily from day 2 until discharge.

The open-label trial gave fatigue secondary-outcome status. An independent investigator recorded daily fatigue scores using a model cited to Christensen and colleagues. Figure 3C shows mean scores with standard errors. The tea group had lower scores on days 3–6, with the day 3 comparison marked p<.01 and days 4–6 p<.001. The caption reports p<.001 for the repeated-measures comparison over days 1–6. The daily means and between-arm estimates are presented graphically without an exact numerical table. The results concern postoperative recovery, with pain and concomitant care relevant to interpretation.

The official WHO mirror of ChiCTR1800018294 lists fatigue as a secondary outcome and dates the registration to September 10, 2018, after first enrollment on September 21, 2017. The release establishes a registered fatigue field, with prospective specification remaining unverified. The publication’s Methods give September 2017 to May 2018, while Results describe September 2017 to September 2018 screening. The protocol and dated recruitment reconciliation remain open. [Primary methods and results](Source-Text/PMC8563416.txt#L281) · [Publisher Figure 3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41430-021-00868-8/figures/3) · [Fatigue model source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7104617/) · [Dated registry](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Green-Tea-Outcomes.md#retrospective-registration-for-brewed-green-tea-after-gastrectomy)

## Separate gastric cancer biomarker study citing the tea registry

Li and colleagues cite ChiCTR1800018294 for a prospective integrin-β6 biomarker cohort of 34 radical gastric cancer resections from October 2020 to June 2021. Their paper also reports a separate retrospective series of 135 resections from January to December 2017. The WHO registry record identifies the randomized 2017–2018 tea intervention and does not describe that prospective biomarker cohort. The studies share an institution and some authors. The prospective dates establish different cohorts. Overlap with the retrospective series and the reason the biomarker paper used the number remain unestablished. It contributes no independent green-tea fatigue comparison. ## SleepLift registration and public supplement protocol

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted SleepLift (NCT05323084, PBRC 2020-039) on April 12, 2022. Adults aged 50 or older with insomnia received a supplement and placebo in randomized order for seven days per condition, separated by a two-week washout. The protocol lists four scheduled Multidimensional Fatigue Inventory assessments. Its planned active recipe specifies 5 g green tea extract, 1 g chicory extract, 5 g collagen peptides, and 7 g equal-parts sugar and erythritol. The protocol identifies decaffeinated green tea containing EGCG and gives no measured EGCG, caffeine, or theanine dose. It describes 60 mL water added during production and a finished volume of 53 mL per bottle. Potassium sorbate and flavor are 0.2% and 0.1% in the protocol. The current registry arm descriptions instead list 60 mL and 2% and 1%, respectively. The three named ingredient masses agree. The recorded differences in finished volume and excipients remain unresolved. The protocol carries December 2021 approval and was uploaded to the registry in September 2023. Its first public date is unverified. [Registration and record history](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05323084?tab=history) · [Public protocol, p. 6](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/84/NCT05323084/Prot_SAP_000.pdf#page=6) · [Local registry and protocol fields](Source-Text/Trials/NCT05323084.txt)

## SleepLift posted fatigue results by crossover condition

The public API records September 19, 2024 as the first result posting. The currently posted results include 9 supplement and 10 placebo observations, following one placebo-first loss to follow-up during the crossover washout. The results report least-squares means and standard errors for three one-week Multidimensional Fatigue Index subscales on a 4–20 scale. Higher scores indicate greater fatigue.

| Subscale | Supplement baseline → week 1 | Placebo baseline → week 1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General fatigue | 15.1 ± 1.0 → 14.0 ± 1.0 | 14.7 ± 1.0 → 13.4 ± 1.0 |
| Physical fatigue | 11.1 ± 1.3 → 9.5 ± 1.3 | 10.3 ± 1.3 → 11.3 ± 1.3 |
| Mental fatigue | 11.7 ± 1.0 → 12.4 ± 1.0 | 12.5 ± 1.0 → 11.7 ± 1.0 |

The registry's fatigue objects give these summary means without inferential comparisons. The 2026 publication reports adjusted contrasts from the same cohort. The combination of extracts and collagen does not isolate brewed green tea. Direct comparison of the original results text with the current record is unavailable. ## Intranasal white tea extract and odor tasks in stressed mice

Stressed mice given white tea extract by the nasal route had improved odor-task performance relative to stressed controls. The paper provides functional odor data in a different preparation, route and disease model from brewed green tea in Parkinson disease.
