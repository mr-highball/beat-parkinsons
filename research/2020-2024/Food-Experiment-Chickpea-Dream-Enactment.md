# Chickpeas and dream enactment

## 2020 Diet patterns and probable dream enactment

Molsberry et al., published online 2020-08-19, DOI 10.1212/WNL.0000000000010523, PMID 32817391, PMCID PMC7713746. The 47,679 cohort participants did not have diagnosed Parkinson disease at selection. Alternate Mediterranean diet scoring included a general legume component along with eight other components. Probable REM sleep behavior disorder was screened with a sleep-partner question requiring dream enactment at least three times. The authors reported no association of baseline or long-term alternate Mediterranean diet adherence with probable RBD; this is a binary screening association, not a chickpea-specific association or episode-frequency estimate. Preparation was not established and no effect of canned chickpeas can be inferred.

## 2023 Legume intake and prodromal score in isolated REM sleep behavior disorder

Lee et al., published online August 2023 (publisher issue lists August 2; PubMed lists August 3), DOI 10.1016/j.parkreldis.2023.105794, PMID 37549588, enrolled 101 patients with polysomnography-confirmed isolated RBD; the accessible publisher abstract and article snippets describe associations of a general recommended-food score and legume consumption with an estimated prodromal Parkinson probability, not change in dream-enactment episodes. For participants aged under 70, legume intake was negatively correlated with estimated probability (p=0.032); consumption was higher with fewer prodromal markers (p=0.016). The overall high versus low food-score groups did not differ in estimated probability. The published abstract names no chickpea, canning, or individual chickpea consumption, and the population does not have diagnosed PD. The analysis is observational and its endpoint cannot be relabeled a protective effect on RBD frequency. The complete publisher full text and ingredient-specific questionnaire are not accessible in this pass.

A 2022 Korean Neurological Association conference abstract reports the same 101-person sample and p-values and should be treated as an earlier report of the same study, not independent replication: https://new.neuro.or.kr/workshop/202202/file/2022_fall_Abstract_book.pdf (printed p.184).

### Follow-up on Lee's measurement and access

Publisher issue page lists online 2023-08-02, while PubMed and OpenAlex list 2023-08-03; dates conflict. The publisher's indexed study-population snippet says patients with RBD and neurodegenerative disorders including Parkinson disease were excluded; the 101 subjects were selected as isolated RBD, not diagnosed Parkinson cases. A news description identifies the legume component as soybeans/cooked soybeans, miso/soybean paste, tofu and soy milk, and the four other marker components as olfactory loss, sleepiness, depression and constipation. Those details are secondary; Lee's complete methods and underlying questionnaire remain inaccessible. The cited Kim et al. 2011 original RFS paper independently lists those exact four soy-derived legume items (Table 1); it is strong instrument context, not proof of Lee's exact version without the full Lee protocol. Neither source establishes canned chickpea exposure or a frequency outcome for dream enactment.
