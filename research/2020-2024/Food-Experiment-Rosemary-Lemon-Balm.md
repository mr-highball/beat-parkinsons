# Rosemary and lemon balm evidence

## Lemon balm extract and neuropsychiatric outcomes in mild Alzheimer dementia

Noguchi-Shinohara and colleagues published October 29, 2020. Twenty-three participants with mild Alzheimer dementia were randomized to rosmarinic-acid-rich lemon balm extract or placebo. At 24 weeks the NPI-Q total changed by −0.5 versus +0.7 points (p = .012), with no clear group differences on composite cognitive measures. The trial used an extract of a different herb from rosemary, and its composite scales give no separate speech intelligibility estimate. [Local CC BY article text](Source-Text/PMC7596544.txt) · [Original report](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-73729-2)

## Second case of a migrating rosemary twig

In August 2021 a second case report described a 54-year-old man with an abscess from which surgeons removed an elongated object resembling a rosemary twig. They found no visible gastrointestinal perforation and inferred migration from the object’s location and the earlier 2007 case. [Local CC BY case text](Source-Text/PMC8387063.txt) · [2007 case](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Rosemary-Lemon-Balm.md#gastric-perforation-and-liver-abscess-after-swallowing-a-rosemary-twig)

## Isolated rosmarinic acid and motor measures in Parkinson models

Cai and colleagues published May 16, 2022. Pre-exposure isolated rosmarinic acid improved pole descent, rotarod retention and open-field movement at one or more tested levels in MPTP mice. In a separate model reported online in December 2022, Presti-Silva and colleagues found no detectable model-induced deficit on pole, rotarod or grip tests. Their model increased open-field movement and rosmarinic acid reduced that increase. The studies differed in baseline motor deficits and intervention protocols. Rosemary and lemon balm both contain rosmarinic acid, and the studies provide no measure of a culinary preparation, episodic freezing or human gait. [Cai full text](Source-Text/PMC9149082.txt) · [Presti-Silva author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36526017/) · [Earlier catalog comparison](Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#rosmarinic-acid-and-motor-tasks-in-mptp-mice)

## Nursing staff rosemary registration and doctoral thesis

Brazil's ReBEC registered the open single-group trial RBR-88hrnnw on September 23, 2022. The registration covers sleep, mood and cardiovascular outcomes. Pretto's 2023 doctoral thesis reports a decrease in resting pressure, better subjective sleep and shorter sleep latency after oral dry rosemary extract. Adverse experiences included sleep-related symptoms, anxiety, headache and stomach pain. The thesis, 2025 sleep paper and 2026 cardiovascular paper use the same 2022 intervention cohort. The studies provide no posture-specific pressure change or REM dream-enactment measure. [Official registration](https://ensaiosclinicos.gov.br/rg/RBR-88hrnnw) · [Local selected registry fields](Source-Text/Trials/RBR-88hrnnw.txt) · [University thesis](https://repositorio.ufsm.br/handle/1/31328) · [2025 sleep report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41020583/) · [2026 cardiovascular report](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Rosemary-Lemon-Balm.md#rosemary-extract-and-resting-blood-pressure-in-nursing-staff)

## Rosemary hydrosol and insomnia-model outcomes

Li and colleagues reported a rosemary hydrosol experiment in a chemically induced rat insomnia model online in 2023, before the January 2024 issue. The study measured sleep-related outcomes and serotonergic markers. The extract route, population and outcomes leave dried rosemary and dream enactment in Parkinson disease untested. The 2018 university-student rosemary trial used subjective sleep quality and likewise gives no dream enactment count. [Hydrosol study author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37532071/) · [Student trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29389474/)

## Rosemary extract and composite mental health in a prison trial

ReBEC registered RBR-102nsx22 on June 24, 2022. Sztormowski and colleagues published November 18, 2022. The report gives statistically clear baseline-to-follow-up decreases on the SRQ-20 mental disorder screen at two levels of rosemary extract. The accessible abstract gives no corresponding between-arm depression estimate. The intervention used extract capsules in incarcerated adults and prison workers. [Official registration](https://ensaiosclinicos.gov.br/rg/RBR-102nsx22) · [Journal report](https://doi.org/10.9734/ejmp/2022/v33i121110)

## Phospholipid lemon balm extract and depression scores

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT05602688 on November 2, 2022. In the October 2023 trial report, 100 adults with emotional distress or poor sleep were randomized to an aqueous lemon balm extract with a phospholipid carrier or placebo. At three weeks the mean DASS-42 depression score had fallen from 23.3 to 6.9 in the extract group and from 22.7 to 17.1 with placebo. The group-by-time interaction was p < .001. The participants had no Parkinson study inclusion and the intervention used a formulated extract. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05602688) · [Local CC BY article text, Table 3](Source-Text/PMC10620697.txt#L178)

## Lemon balm anise and rose formula and depression in IBS

Azimi and colleagues first published May 29, 2024. Eighty-eight of 96 adults with constipation-predominant IBS completed the randomized comparison of a lemon balm, anise and damask rose capsule against cornstarch. The abstract favors the formula but gives a depression interval that crosses zero. The original tables are needed to resolve the contrast and the effect-size label. There was no lemon-balm-only arm or Parkinson population. [Original author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38937193/)

## Rosemary extract and bidirectional gut motility

Naqvi and colleagues published May 25, 2024, before the October issue. The hydroethanolic rosemary leaf extract increased laxation at lower tested levels in mice and reduced transit and secretions at higher tested levels in the antidiarrheal experiments. Isolated diosmetin produced antidiarrheal and antispasmodic effects. The preparations and model outcomes differ from eating ground rosemary and measuring Parkinson constipation. [Original author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38801915/) · [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2024.118395)

## Brewed lemon balm and depression in a registered crossover

Official study UMIN000055134, first public 2024-08-02. Planned enrollment 42 female students with premenstrual symptoms. Registered intervention 120 mL lemon balm tea daily versus hot water in a crossover. DASS-21 depression, anxiety and stress are secondary outcomes. Final follow-up is recorded as 2025-01-31. As of 2026-11-15 the results are listed as unpublished and the results fields are blank. Register history: https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_his_list.cgi?recptno=R000062988. [Local factual text](Source-Text/Trials/UMIN000055134.txt) · [Primary source](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/icdr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000062988)

The November 16, 2026 replay still lists study completion, publication status unpublished and no actual enrollment or outcome estimates. The planned enrollment was 42. The comparison assigns brewed tea versus hot water in a non-Parkinson population and lists DASS-21 depression as a secondary outcome.
