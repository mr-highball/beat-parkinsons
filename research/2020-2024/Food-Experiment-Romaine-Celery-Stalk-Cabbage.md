# Food experiment evidence for romaine lettuce, celery stalk, cabbage and Brussels sprouts

## 2020 Celery on a Parkinson diet menu

Appendix A of Hegelmaier and colleagues' vegetarian intervention lists celery without a part or preparation. It identifies Lollo rosso and iceberg lettuce and separately lists Chinese cabbage. The diet and diet-plus-enema arms showed improved aggregate UPDRS III scores. Raw celery stalk intake and item-level gait speed, rest tremor and swallowing outcomes were unavailable. [Earlier local evidence note](Food-Experiment-Celery-Spearmint.md#2020-celery-and-mint-leaves-in-a-parkinson-diet-intervention) · [Primary local full text](Source-Text/PMC7072275.txt)

## 2020 Thawed Brussels sprouts and retronasal flavor

Duffy, Hayes and Sharafi tested thawed frozen Brussels sprouts without additional cooking in 97 adults, alongside kale and asparagus. Opening the nose increased vegetable flavor intensity, while the association between change in flavor intensity and liking was null for Brussels sprouts (rho = .001, p = .94). The experiment measured flavor perception during sampling. It gives no evidence about smell function after eating roasted sprouts. [Primary report, Methods and Results](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jafr.2020.100044)

## Kale flavor sampling and retronasal perception

The same Duffy study offered 4 g frozen-thawed kale, allowed participants to spit or swallow and measured flavor while blocking and releasing nasal airflow. This was sensory sampling without roasting or a measured change in olfactory function after consumption. The cohort did not address Parkinson smell loss. [Primary report, Methods](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jafr.2020.100044) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2020-Duffy-Kale-Retronasal-Extract.txt)

## 2021 Raw romaine with a meal in healthy young men

Shokraei and colleagues randomized meal order in a crossover with 16 healthy young men. A high-fat meal contained raw romaine, raw watercress or a cellulose control. Postprandial glucose and insulin area under the curve were lower with romaine than control. Lipids and measured inflammatory cytokines showed no significant between-meal differences. The trial did not measure facial expressiveness, rest tremor, dyskinesia or handwriting. [Primary report, Methods and Figure 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8281573/) · [Local article](Source-Text/PMC8281573.txt)

## 2021 Brussels sprout and cabbage fibers in Parkinson-derived fecal samples

Baert and colleagues list Brussels sprouts and white cabbage among vegetable sources for fiber fractions tested ex vivo with Parkinson and control fecal samples. Soluble fractions produced more butyrate than insoluble fractions overall. Parkinson status was associated with lower acetate across the vegetable experiments, while its adjusted butyrate effect was inconclusive. The participants supplied fecal samples without an assigned culinary exposure. The experiment measured short-chain fatty acids without dyskinesia, drooling or smell assessments. [Primary report, Table 2 and Supplementary Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8363715/) · [Local article](Source-Text/PMC8363715.txt)

## 2021 Fresh and pasteurized sauerkraut registration

DRKS00027007 first registered October 29, 2021 and lists a crossover comparing fresh and pasteurized sauerkraut in healthy adults. The planned outcomes concern gut microbiota, metabolism and inflammation. The registry marks the study complete and links the 2025 publication. [Official registration](https://drks.de/search/en/trial/DRKS00027007/details) · [2025 outcome report](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Romaine-Celery-Stalk-Cabbage.md#2025-sauerkraut-and-microbiome-outcomes)

## 2023 Fresh and dried red cabbage extracts in a Parkinson-cell model

Vega-Gálvez and colleagues tested extracts from fresh and convectively dried red cabbage in SH-SY5Y cells exposed to preformed alpha-synuclein fibrils. Figure 5 reports reduced fibril-associated cytotoxicity after 24 hours for all extracts and after 48 hours for the fresh, 50 °C and 90 °C extracts only. This is a cellular comparison of extracts. The study did not measure Parkinson symptoms, food consumption or a clinical preparation effect. [Primary report, Figure 5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10526076/) · [Local article](Source-Text/PMC10526076.txt)

## 2023 Red cabbage anthocyanin extracts in aging mice

The study compared orally administered red cabbage anthocyanin-rich preparations in normally aging mice. The authors report improved measures of cognition and no motor effect in their behavior testing. The mice did not have a Parkinson model, and the accessible primary abstract gives no depression or rigidity result. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37781995/)

## 2023 Raw, steamed and boiled Brussels sprout volatiles

Wieczorek and colleagues measured glucosinolates and volatile compounds in Brussels sprouts prepared raw, steamed or boiled. Their chemical profiles differed across preparations and sulfide levels generally rose with cooking. The study did not test roasted sprouts, human smell ability or Parkinson dyskinesia. [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00217-023-04276-z)

## 2024 Cooked mixed-vegetable bolus with celery

The authors tested ten fork-mashable dishes in five healthy adults and measured chewing and properties of boluses ready to swallow. The ratatouille ingredient list includes celery alongside other vegetables. The study did not isolate celery, use raw stalks or enroll people with Parkinson dysphagia. [Primary report, Methods and food compositions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11202756/) · [Local article](Source-Text/PMC11202756.txt)

## 2024 Cooked cruciferous soup and ambulatory pressure

Connolly and colleagues compared a soup containing cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower and kale with a root-and-squash soup in adults with mildly elevated pressure. Among 17 analyzed, the primary 24-hour systolic contrast was −2.5 mmHg (p = .002). The assigned foods were boiled and blended, and the results article does not identify red cabbage. No upright lightheadedness or Parkinson outcome was measured. [Primary report, Methods and Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12916-024-03577-8)

## 2022 Registry for fermented food and dysphagia research

UMIN000046319 was first publicly disclosed April 1, 2022. The official records describe an umbrella study in people needing support, including a gut microbiota outcome after fermented food and separate swallowing rehabilitation outcomes. The national portal lists carbonated versus ordinary thickened water under the displayed interventions. The sauerkraut pilot cites this registration, while its article gives the particular food and comparator. The umbrella record gives no sauerkraut-specific drooling outcome. [Official UMIN disclosure](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000052859) · [National registry details](https://rctportal.mhlw.go.jp/detail/um?trial_id=UMIN000046319)

## 2024 Sulforaphane and motility in Parkinson-model nematodes

Wang and colleagues compared isolated sulforaphane and formononetin in two Parkinson-model nematode experiments. Figure 6 reports higher thrashing rates with sulforaphane than with the model condition. The figure labels the measure dyskinesia, and its operational endpoint is thrashes per 20 seconds. There was no levodopa-induced abnormal-involuntary-movement assay or steamed Brussels sprout exposure. [Primary report, Figure 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11596823/) · [Local article](Source-Text/PMC11596823.txt)
