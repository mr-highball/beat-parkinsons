# Culinary umbellifers and Parkinson symptom comparators

## Chervil, lovage and coriander in a Parkinson diet intervention

- **2020-02-06. Hegelmaier and colleagues.** Appendix A, Table A1 names chervil, lovage and coriander among the seasonings in a 14-day vegetarian diet. The table does not specify plant parts, whether these herbs were fresh or dried, individual exposure or dose. Six patients received the diet and ten received the diet plus bowel cleansing. The paper reports lower UPDRS III motor scores at one year for the combined sample (p = 0.0004), diet arm (p = 0.033) and combined intervention arm (p = 0.005). The examination included facial expression, speech, walking and rigidity. Figures 4 and S2 give total motor scores without individual item changes. The diet and bowel intervention do not isolate effects of individual herbs. [Original intervention](https://doi.org/10.3390/cells9020376) · [Local source text and Appendix A](Source-Text/PMC7072275.txt).

## Chervil soup and acute voice changes

- **2020-08. Van Der Brempt and colleagues.** A 43-year-old patient experienced immediate gastrointestinal symptoms after homemade garden chervil (*Anthriscus cerefolium*) soup and later throat itching, uvular swelling and a voice change after the soup. The report compares laboratory responses to raw and cooked extracts and describes a stronger signal for the raw extract. The symptom was an acute change in voice during an allergic reaction. Voice volume was not measured, the soup was cooked and the case did not involve Parkinson disease. [Original conference case](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.waojou.2020.100312).

## Fresh cilantro oral challenge

- **2020-06-24. Wong-Pack and colleagues, abstract 34.** A 20-year-old patient with previous workplace and salsa-associated reactions received one-eighth of a fresh cilantro leaf as the first dose in an oral challenge. Facial and neck redness, hives, oral itching and lip tingling developed within 20 minutes. Vomiting, diarrhea and chest tightness followed. The abstract does not report difficulty swallowing or a Parkinson diagnosis. The [2013 mixed-meal case](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Umbellifer-Herbs.md#coriander-with-exercise-associated-swallowing-symptoms) reported difficulty swallowing with exercise and had a less specific ingredient exposure. [Original conference proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13223-020-00445-x) · [Local source text, abstract 34](Source-Text/PMC7313110.txt).

## Lovage root mixture and urgency severity

- **2020-08-21. Alidjanov and colleagues.** The post hoc analysis of the [Wagenlehner phase III trial](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Umbellifer-Herbs.md#lovage-root-mixture-in-acute-cystitis) included 657 participants in its full analysis set. Mean scores on the typical-symptom composite fell from 10.2 to 2.1 in the root mixture arm and from 10.1 to 2.1 in the fosfomycin arm between days 1 and 8. At baseline, 99.2% reported urgent urination. Figure 2 shows lower urgency severity over time in both arms. Supplementary Table 4 was listed, but attempts to retrieve the linked file from the publisher and PMC did not return a readable PDF. The questionnaire measures symptom severity during acute infection. The frozen claim asks about urgency frequency after dried lovage leaf in Parkinson disease. [Original post hoc analysis and supplement listing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7458871/) · [Original article PDF with Figure 2](https://icurology.org/Synapse/Data/PDFData/2020ICU/icu-61-498.pdf).

## Topical dill preparation and sleep in hemodialysis

- **2024-01-12.** This three-arm randomized trial enrolled 111 patients receiving hemodialysis and analyzed 106. The dill preparation used juice from fresh aerial parts in a sesame-oil base applied to the skin. Table 6 reports follow-up Pittsburgh sleep scores of 3.24 (SD 2.41) with the dill preparation, 4.54 (SD 3.11) with sesame oil, and 5.05 (SD 3.21) with usual care (overall p = 0.032). The baseline table prints 3.22 (SD 6.14) for the dill group, which conflicts with the narrative claim that each group started above 4. This inconsistency prevents a secure reading of the within-group change. The route and general sleep measure also differ from the frozen claim about eating dried dill weed for Parkinson insomnia. [Original trial](https://doi.org/10.1155/2024/3077603) · [Local source text and Table 6](Source-Text/PMC10805558.txt).

## Chervil mentioned in a Parkinson constipation handout

- **2024-10, edition printed on the handout.** The reference center lists chervil (*cerfeuil*) among fiber-containing herbs in a dietary handout for constipation in Parkinson disease and related syndromes. The AP-HP website path carries a February 2025 upload date, and the same edition is also hosted by CHU Toulouse. The entry offers context for 0766 and supplies no participant outcomes, preparation-specific comparison, or evidence for the direction of the frozen worsening claim. [Original patient handout, CHU Toulouse copy](https://www.chu-toulouse.fr/IMG/pdf/407025_-_livret_a5_ams_parkinson-syndrome_constipation-10-24.pdf) · [AP-HP copy](https://pitiesalpetriere.aphp.fr/wp-content/blogs.dir/58/files/2025/02/407025-Livret-A5-AMS-Parkinson-Syndrome-Constipation-10-24.pdf).

## Japanese patent and nigral coriander experiments

- **2021-06-17, publication. JP2021091675A.** The patent application uses the Thai culinary name *phak chi* and describes a heated water extract of fresh coriander leaves, stems and roots. The reported rat experiment co-injected the extract and 6-OHDA into the substantia nigra and compared tyrosine hydroxylase staining after two weeks. Relative staining was 82.37% for 6-OHDA alone (n = 4) and 98.36% with extract (n = 4), with the control scaled to 100% (n = 6). The claimed food compositions are proposed uses. The experiment did not test oral exposure or gait. The application cites the [Korean aerial-part patent](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Umbellifer-Herbs.md#coriander-aerial-part-extract-in-a-korean-patent) and describes a distinct preparation and route. [Original Japanese application and figures](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2021091675A/ja) · [English machine translation](https://patents.google.com/patent/JP2021091675A/en).
- **2022-08-03. Saeki and colleagues.** The publisher abstract describes heated coriander leaf extract co-injected with 6-OHDA in the rat substantia nigra and reports effects on tyrosine hydroxylase staining, intracellular zinc and a reactive oxygen species signal. It shares authorship and a closely related experiment with the patent disclosure. The abstract identifies leaf extract, and the patent reports whole fresh material including stems and roots. Full experimental provenance was unavailable from the abstract, so the two reports cannot establish independent replication. Neither supplies a human gait outcome. [Original publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11130-022-00999-8).
