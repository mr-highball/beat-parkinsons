# Avocado oil preparation and Parkinson symptom outcomes

## Cold-pressed avocado oil and prostate histology in rats

Silva et al.'s 2020 primary methods identify 7% cold-pressed avocado oil in feed, replacing 7% soybean oil in the control and induced-control feeds. The 28 Wistar rats were allocated across four groups with and without androgenic stimulation and dietary oil. This paper reports serum hormonal and metabolic measures. Its endpoints do not include bladder function. [2020 primary full paper](https://www.nutricionhospitalaria.org/filesPortalWeb/3514/MA-03155-01.pdf?sP0WhxJk52SLTRl5TftjnvR0TGz1T6A8=)

The February 22, 2023 paper describes the same group structure and a histology analysis. In the induced groups, epithelial thickness was 19.44 ± 2.62 µm with avocado oil and 27.02 ± 4.10 µm without it. The abstract gives 12 weeks, while a related 2014 conference abstract describes 20 weeks and assigns slightly different epithelial values. Shared investigators, sample size, study design and overlapping averages suggest related reports. Animal overlap and the 2023 oil processing details have not been independently verified from the 2023 abstract. The tissue result supplies no urgency frequency, micturition diaries, cystometry, or Parkinson endpoint. [2023 original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37871299/) · [2014 congress report](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Avocado-Oil-Outcomes.md#brazilian-congress-report-on-prostate-histology)

## Registry outcomes for avocado oil as a cannabidiol comparator

NCT04590495 first posted October 19, 2020 and posted results September 15, 2022. The structured study record names CBD oil and placebo. The linked WVU protocol describes planned oral avocado oil with possible food coloring, and peppermint-flavored oil to mask both treatments. The trial reports identify avocado oil with mint flavor. The protocol names the CBD supplier and gives no avocado supplier or processing grade. The registry labels the secondary outcomes as baseline changes, but its displayed means match the papers' endpoint means instead of their baseline-to-endpoint differences. Stanford Sleepiness Scale endpoint scores were 2.67 ± 1.32 for CBD and 2.63 ± 1.42 for placebo (registry p=.9925). Correct Digit Symbol Substitution Test counts were 74.5 ± 8.1 and 71.8 ± 12.1. The registry's adjusted paired-test p=.6331 differs from the journal's repeated-measures negative-binomial p=.43 for the same test. The sources report different analysis methods and different test durations. The registry and WVU protocol (Table 2) specify 60 seconds, while the journal Methods specify 90 seconds. Neither addresses an independent effect of avocado oil. [Official results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04590495?tab=results) · [WVU protocol and analysis plan](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/95/NCT04590495/Prot_SAP_ICF_000.pdf) · [Local selected fields](Source-Text/Trials/NCT04590495.txt)

## Avocado oil comparator and daytime sleepiness or symbol substitution

Rudisill et al. published simulator outcomes on November 24, 2022 and subjective-state and psychomotor outcomes on January 17, 2023 from the same 40-person trial. Healthy university students received 300 mg CBD (n=21) or avocado oil (n=19). Both preparations were flavored with mint oil. The simulator paper describes delivery by oral syringe. Neither public paper supplies pressing or refining details for the avocado oil, and the trial has no arm without either study formulation. [2022 primary simulator paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9949874/) · [2023 primary sleepiness and psychomotor paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175152/)

After dosing, breakfast, a 120-minute wait and a driving task, mean Stanford Sleepiness Scale scores rose from 1.9 ± 1.0 to 2.6 ± 1.4 with the avocado comparator and from 2.0 ± 1.0 to 2.7 ± 1.3 with CBD (baseline-adjusted p=.99). Oil comparator scores on the Digit Symbol Substitution Test rose from 65.7 ± 11.6 to 71.8 ± 12.1 correct symbols. Corresponding CBD scores were 68.0 ± 10.7 and 74.5 ± 8.1 (p=.43). Participants transcribed symbols under a time limit, and the score counts correct symbols. It provides no estimate for manual dexterity as a separate impairment or for character size. The authors discuss practice effects in the repeated tests. [2023 Tables 1 and 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175152/) · [2022 posted registry results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04590495?tab=results)

## Oil rinsing and measured salivary flow

Ludwar et al.'s December 2020 crossover studied 26 participants with medication-induced dry mouth. They used topical sunflower oil rinsing and mineral water for one week each. The investigators measured whole stimulated and unstimulated salivary flow. Oil reduced subjective dryness from 6.46 to 4.93 (within p=.003), but the comparison with water was p=.067. Self-reported easier swallowing favored rinsing with oil (p=.031), while the paper did not identify clinical salivary improvement with oil. The exposure was a different oil used as a rinse and the population had dry mouth. The findings do not give ingested avocado oil or a Parkinson drooling measure. [Primary full article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1111/odi.13752)

## Fibromyalgia trial registration with avocado oil in the placebo

The Australian and New Zealand Clinical Trials Registry registered CANN-RELIEF on April 3, 2023. It describes a double-blind parallel feasibility trial. The comparator was swallowed at bedtime and consisted of MCT oil with small unspecified amounts of walnut and avocado oil. Pressing grade, oil volume by ingredient, and an avocado-free matched comparator were not supplied. Sleep quality and adverse events were tracked. The 2026 results identify study-level counts from the same trial. ## Avocado oil capsules in a preliminary adolescent comparison

2024-10-25. Filizzola and colleagues reported 34 participants started and 30 completed a 12-week comparison of two amounts of oral avocado oil with mineral-oil control. The analyzed arms contained nine, 12 and nine adolescents. The paper measured body composition and blood pressure and gives no daytime sleepiness scale or adverse-event table. It gives the same ethics code as the later Brazilian registry but differs in eligibility, capsule schedule and comparator amount. Pressing method was unreported. [Original preliminary report](https://revistamundodasaude.emnuvens.com.br/mundodasaude/article/view/1600) · [Local source text](Source-Text/2024-Filizzola-Avocado-Oil-Factual-Extract.txt)

## Avocado oil mentioned in a Parkinson ketogenic anxiety follow-up

First published June 13, 2024. Seven Parkinson participants continued from an earlier 12-week uncontrolled ketogenic pilot. At 24 weeks the Parkinson Anxiety Scale mean change was −6.29, SD 2.87 and median −7 (p=.0313). Participant 019 mentioned either coconut or avocado oil in discussing adaptation. The interview does not establish a particular refined oil dose, the individual PAS change, or any avocado-oil-specific intervention. [Original Table 2 and interviews](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11457624/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2024-Tidman-Ketogenic-Parkinson-Anxiety-Table-Extract.txt)

## Ketogenic Parkinson pilot and baseline to twelve week anxiety

First published February 18, 2022. In the one-group 12-week Parkinson diet pilot, the reported change in total PAS was −3.06 points (SD 4.06, n=16, p=.0086). The later seven-person follow-up recruited from this pilot, so the two do not constitute independent replications. Neither assigns an avocado-oil-only contrast or identifies refined oil consumption. [Primary pilot report and Parkinson Anxiety Scale table](https://doi.org/10.2217/nmt-2021-0033) · [Local text](Source-Text/2022-Tidman-Ketogenic-Parkinson-Pilot-Anxiety-Extract.txt)

## Avocado oil mayonnaise in a non Parkinson ketogenic case

First published January 9, 2023. A case report in a 47-year-old woman with Down syndrome and Alzheimer disease describes less observed anxiety after broader ketogenic dietary changes. The example menu includes avocado-oil mayonnaise. No anxiety scale, independent oil comparison or refining method was provided, and the participant did not have the target Parkinson phenotype. [Primary case and food menu](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9870323/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2023-Bosworth-Avocado-Mayonnaise-Case-Extract.txt)

## Avocado oil comparator and acute tranquility

In the 40-person CBD trial, mean tranquility scores were 297.3 to 301.3 in the mint-flavored avocado oil comparator and 303.0 to 302.4 with CBD. The adjusted comparison is p=.85. This is one more outcome from the existing NCT04590495 study family. It has no oil-free arm, confirmed refined grade or Parkinson anxiety result. [Original Methods and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10175152/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2023-Rudisill-Avocado-Comparator-Tranquility-Table-Extract.txt)

## Registered avocado and mango meals with cognitive testing

First posted April 29, 2022. The completed trial compared an avocado-and-mango diet with energy-matched controls in 82 adults with prediabetes. Registered secondary outcomes include Trail Making, Stroop and verbal fluency. The registry had no posted results. The protocol assigns whole fruits. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05353790) · [Local source text](Source-Text/2022-NCT05353790-Avocado-Mango-Cognition.txt)

## Registered whole avocado and executive testing in older adults

First posted November 18, 2023. AvoCog plans 70 adults aged 65–85, whole avocado daily for 12 weeks versus usual diet, executive-task MRI and NIH Toolbox outcomes. Parkinson disease is excluded. The official August 22, 2024 update showed Unknown status without posted results when checked November 16, 2026.
