# Olive Oil Urinary Comparators

## Registry comparator composition for the saw palmetto urinary trial

The public UMIN record was disclosed June 1, 2022, last modified June 17, and lists completed status. It plans 50 middle-aged men with an IPSS primary outcome, randomized for 12 weeks to saw palmetto or placebo listed as polyethylene glycol-400. The linked original 2024 article reports 54 participants and olive-oil placebo capsules. The formulation discrepancy remains unresolved and the registration provides no direct result for extra-virgin olive oil. ## Olive oil capsules as comparator in a saw palmetto trial

Waki and colleagues assigned 54 men to a phytosterol-enriched saw palmetto extract or a capsule described in the publication as olive oil. The longitudinal IPSS and OABSS composite results favored the active extract, and urine volume and flow comparisons were inconclusive. The report acknowledges that the comparator may influence symptom scores. Its cited UMIN registry instead lists polyethylene glycol-400 as placebo. There is no isolated extra-virgin oil effect estimate, no oil grade, and no Parkinson cohort. ## Parkinson diet patterns and reported urinary symptoms

Fox and colleagues analyzed 1,205 participants reporting Parkinson disease or another parkinsonian disorder. Table 2 associates the composite MIND score with lower general urinary symptom severity (coefficient −1.44, reported interval −2.23 to −0.649, adjusted p = .004). The Mediterranean pattern estimate was −0.133 (interval −0.806 to 0.539, adjusted p = .9). Both scores represent many foods, with no isolated oil grade, urgency frequency or causal dietary estimate.
