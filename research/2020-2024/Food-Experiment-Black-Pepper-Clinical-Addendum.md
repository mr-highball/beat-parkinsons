# Further black pepper trial outcomes

Review date 2026-11-14 (US Eastern).

## Three-botanical Kleeb Bua Daeng formula and total MoCA

Musigavong and colleagues published a double-blind randomized trial on 2022-03-25. Forty adults with mild cognitive impairment were randomized equally to oral Kleeb Bua Daeng 1 g twice daily or matching placebo for three months. Thirty-eight completed the study. The formula has 1:1:1 dried Nelumbo nucifera petal, Centella asiatica aerial parts and Piper nigrum fruit. The reported month-3 MoCA contrast was −0.207 points (95% CI −1.96 to 1.54, p=.817). Neither isolated attention subscore nor effect of pepper alone was reported. The paper identifies TCTR20220209007 and no PD subgroup. [Musigavong mild cognitive impairment article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8975679/), [local text](Source-Text/PMC8975679-Kleeb-Bua-Daeng-MoCA.txt).

## Topical oil blend and chemotherapy-induced neuropathic pain

Langley-Brady and colleagues published the related trial report online on 2023-01-22. They randomized 27 participants stratified by baseline pain to the topical essential-oil blend (13) or placebo (14) every eight hours for six weeks. The abstract reports no significant between-group difference in pain or quality of life over seven weeks. It reports p=.001 for a comparison involving concomitant pain medication. The complete tables and medication comparison methods were not accessible, so its magnitude and robustness remain open. The paired registry confirms that black pepper was one of six essential oils compared with jojoba. No oral exposure, isolated pepper or PD-specific outcome was evaluated. [Langley-Brady primary abstract](https://augusta.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/a-pilot-randomized-controlled-trial-evaluating-essential-oils-for/), [local text](Source-Text/PMID36693769-Mixed-Essential-Oils-Pain-Extract.txt).

## Five-extract metabolic pilot and reported pain events

Chavanelle and colleagues’ May 3, 2021 translational report includes an uncontrolled pilot of 14 overweight men. Its five-extract product contained black pepper extract. The paper mentions low back pain and headache among adverse events. It gives no pain scores, no number for either event, no Parkinson group and no separate cracked pepper exposure. The results can flag adverse-event context, with ingredient attribution unresolved. [Primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8285600/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2021-Chavanelle-TOTUM-63-Pain-Events.txt)

## Injected standardized pepper extract in experimental pain

Venkatakrishna and colleagues’ February 5, 2022 report studied a β-caryophyllene-rich black pepper seed extract in several mouse nociception assays. Figure 4 reports higher tail-flick latency for the injected extract at early follow-up. It is a controlled nonhuman reflex measurement with a concentrated injected extract. The same manufacturer’s oral diabetic-rat study is a separate experiment. [Original](<https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8827412/>) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2022-Venkatakrishna-Injected-Pepper-Extract-and-Nociception.txt)

## Oral standardized pepper extract in diabetic rat thermal testing

Ramanaiah and colleagues’ June 15, 2022 experiment assigned a standardized pepper seed extract to male diabetic rats for six weeks. Figure 4 shows longer tail-flick latency with all three extract doses compared with untreated diabetic controls, with five per group. The same experiment also changes glucose and nerve conduction outcomes. It cannot isolate a pain mechanism from broader changes in the diabetic model. [Original](<https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.2147/DMSO.S366609>) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2022-Ramanaiah-Oral-Pepper-Extract-and-Diabetic-Rat-Tail-Flick.txt)

## Piperine blend and exercise for low-back pain

Shavlovskaya and colleagues compared exercise plus Cartilox with exercise alone in 60 people with musculoskeletal pain. Table 4 lists lower day-14 numeric rating scores with the blend and zero at day 30 in both arms. The narrative gives a percentage inconsistent with its numerator. Patients also described transient early pain increases with the combined intervention. The blend, optional concomitant topical medication, and reporting differences limit ingredient attribution. [Original](<https://www.mediasphera.ru/issues/voprosy-kurortologii-fizioterapii-i-lechebnoj-fizicheskoj-kultury/2022/4/1004287872022041020>) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2022-Shavlovskaya-Cartilox-and-Pain-Scores.txt)

## Four-plant Qurs-e-Mafasil trial and pain severity

Ansari and colleagues’ February 3, 2024 first online rheumatoid-arthritis report compared a four-plant formulation containing black pepper fruit with celecoxib. Seventy were randomized and 50 completed. The final between-group joint pain VAS difference was 3.00 mm (95% CI −1.82 to 7.84, p=.219). The published abstract and accessible original sections leave the full noninferiority analysis and arm-level tables open. A separate 2012 trial used the same formulation name for a different recipe without black pepper. [Original](<https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378874124001326>) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2024-Ansari-Qurs-e-Mafasil-and-Joint-Pain.txt)

## Knee osteoarthritis pain in the Persian Mixodin report

Gorgian and colleagues’ Winter 2020 Persian report randomized 60 adults to combined turmeric, ginger and black-pepper extract or naproxen for four weeks. Table 2 gives week-four VAS 4.53 (SD 0.68) versus 4.37 (0.89). The reported group comparison p=.747 is inconclusive, and the time test was p=.001. Table 3 gives a group p=.762 for function. The 2020 English PGE2 article matches study authors, group sizes, interventions and duration. A shared cohort is likely and is counted once for interpretation. Neither arm isolates pepper.
