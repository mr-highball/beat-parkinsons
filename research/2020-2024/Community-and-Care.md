# Community and Care

### 2020 — Integrated outpatient palliative care

A randomized trial in people with Parkinson’s and related disorders reported better quality of life at six months with integrated outpatient palliative care than standard care alone. The trial evaluated a team-based service and included caregiver outcomes. [Primary source](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/2760511)

### 2021 — Racial differences in age at recorded diagnosis and treatment

Xie and colleagues reviewed records for 2,033 people with Parkinson’s at a U.S. academic center, including 725 Black patients. Black patients were four years older at first recorded diagnosis at that center and were less likely to receive medication for parkinsonism or mood disorders than White patients. A retrospective single-center study cannot determine community diagnostic delays or identify which barriers produced the differences. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33905958/)

### 2023 — National Medicare study found persistent gaps in specialist care

A study of 685,116 Medicare beneficiaries with a Parkinson’s diagnosis in 2019 found that 40% had no neurologist visit during that calendar year and 9.1% visited a movement disorder specialist. The authors reported differences by race and ethnicity, sex, and rurality. Administrative claims measure recorded visits, and its one-year window differs from earlier four-year utilization studies. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37429849/)
