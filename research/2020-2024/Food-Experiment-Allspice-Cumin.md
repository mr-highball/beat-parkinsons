# Allspice and cumin evidence

## Allspice in a pumpkin spice meal

Huang and colleagues' 2022 crossover report enrolled 25 adults with overweight or obesity. The pumpkin spice meal included cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg and allspice as a blend. Brachial flow-mediated dilation at 24 hours was 7.9% after the blend and 6.5% after the control meal (p = .027). Their 2023 analysis of plasma phytochemicals in 24 participants uses the same registration and specifies 6 g total blend with unknown component proportions. The trial provides an oral culinary-mixture comparison for metabolic and vascular outcomes. There was no allspice-only group and no Parkinson symptom scale. [Primary 2022 article text](Source-Text/PMC8902252.txt) · [Primary 2023 analysis, PMID 37310415](https://doi.org/10.1002/mnfr.202200785)

## Postoperative bowel motility after cumin extract capsules

Amin and colleagues randomized 74 abdominal-surgery patients to cumin extract or starch capsules. Two participants were excluded before analysis. Table 2 reports first flatus at 9.03 ± 3.41 versus 11.72 ± 4.21 hours (p = .004) and first defecation at 16.97 ± 5.02 versus 26.00 ± 9.87 hours (p < .001). The abstract has a conflicting inequality, so the numerical comparison follows Table 2. The study tested short-term postoperative recovery. The frozen claim concerns culinary ground cumin and constipation severity in Parkinson’s. [Primary article text, Table 2](Source-Text/PMC11229490.txt#L163)

## Registered cumin capsule surgery protocol

The Iranian registry lists October 24, 2021 as the registration date, while recruitment was in progress. The protocol targeted 70 adults, specified two doses of cumin extract or starch, and included time to first flatus and first defecation among the outcomes. Bowel activity was checked every two hours for 24 hours. The registry now shows recruitment complete. The published sample is larger than planned and the paper reports both bowel milestones. [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/IRCT20150713023190N10.txt) · [Official IRCT entry](https://irct.ir/trial/59216)

## Oral eugenol timing and motor tasks in toxin models

Vora and colleagues compared eugenol begun before and during MPTP exposure against eugenol begun after exposure. All tested pretreatment doses shortened narrow-beam crossing time relative to MPTP alone. All three post-treatment doses prolonged crossing time. The two higher pretreatment doses reduced catalepsy scores, while post-treatment had no clear benefit on that test. The highest post-treatment dose also worsened locomotor activity. A different 6-OHDA rat study reported improved behavior with oral eugenol and with eugenol plus levodopa, using a different model and design. These findings qualify any directional inference from the component studies. Both used isolated eugenol and neither measured human gait, falls, bradykinesia or rigidity after ground allspice. [Vora primary article, Figures 2–4](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ddt/16/4/16_2022.01026/_pdf) · [Vasconcelos primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32353201/)

## Dietary cuminaldehyde and spatial memory in aging mice

The 2021 mouse study tested cuminaldehyde and reported differences in Morris water maze outcomes. It studied learning and spatial memory in aging mice. It used a constituent and supplied no attention-specific or toasted cumin outcome in people with Parkinson's. [Primary article text, Figure 4](Source-Text/PMC7880363.txt)

## Cumin preparations in additional rodent anxiety tasks

Khichi and colleagues reported open-arm behavior after a cumin extract and a diazepam comparison in 2022. The paper describes an ethanolic extract in its study account and calls the preparation aqueous in its conclusion. Arshad and colleagues' separate 2023 report used an ethanolic extract in healthy male mice and reported effects on elevated plus maze and light-dark behavior. These studies do not involve Parkinson's or test toasted seed. [Khichi primary paper and Table 1](https://www.njppp.com/fulltext/28-1641797056.pdf) · [Arshad primary paper](https://www.xisdxjxsu.asia/V19I03-54-1.pdf)

## Severe allergic reaction reported after allspice

Regula and colleagues published a clinical report titled severe allergic reaction to allspice in May 2022. A 2015 ACAAI program lists a similarly titled presentation by three of the later authors, suggesting a shared case. The program supplies no symptom data and publisher routes for the journal full text returned HTTP 403. The public primary records establish the reported allergy topic, with detailed preparation and any swallowing outcome unverified. [Primary clinical citation, PMID 35577235](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35577235/) · [2015 program, P114](https://college.acaai.org/sites/default/files/2015ProgramGuidef.pdf)

## Acute swallowing symptoms after mixed seasoned meals

A 2024 allergy congress case described oral itching, tongue swelling and inability to swallow after sauce containing chili oil, with similar symptoms after a tomatillo sauce containing cumin, chili, onion, garlic and pepper. Multiple ingredients were tested. The source does not isolate toasted cumin as the cause of the mixed meal reaction. Acute allergy symptoms differ from chronic Parkinson dysphagia. [Primary congress report, M362](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anai.2024.08.746)

## Dry-roasted cumin and oil-cooked cumin volatiles

A Japanese food-science report compared forms of *Cuminum cyminum* and measured aromatic compounds after dry roasting and cooking in oil. Cuminaldehyde accounted for about 80% of the extracted odor-active compounds in both preparations under that study's methods. This is a relative composition result, without a clinical concentration or symptom outcome. [Original study and Japanese abstract](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1390853649779015296)

## Original eugenol beam results by treatment timing

Advance publication August 24, 2022. Vora and colleagues compared isolated oral eugenol by treatment timing in a parkinsonian mouse model. The original narrow-beam result shows faster crossings in the pretreatment comparisons and slower crossings in the post-treatment comparisons. The paper describes one model-only control group and different observation schedules. It does not describe a separate time-matched model-only group for each schedule, which limits causal interpretation of the timing contrast. Other motor tasks and group contrasts differ by dose and endpoint, and the abstract emphasizes high-dose worsening of akinesia. The beam time is a model behavior. It gives no human walking-speed comparison, effect for ground allspice, or measurement of culinary exposure. [Original source](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ddt/16/4/16_2022.01026/_pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2022-Vora-Eugenol-Original-Motor-Timing.txt)

## Rodent eugenol motor tests and the related UFC dissertation

The journal article first published April 30, 2020. It describes selected behavioral outcomes in hemiparkinsonian rats receiving isolated eugenol, alone or with levodopa. The institutional dissertation describes a closely related model and names open-field exploration, rotarod and apomorphine-induced rotation. A timed gait or stride assay is not identified. The university dates the dissertation to February 6, 2018. Its first repository availability is unverified. Exact cohort overlap and the full journal tables remain unresolved. These reports give no culinary allspice or clinical Parkinson walking-speed comparison. [University record and dissertation](https://repositorio.ufc.br/handle/riufc/40992). [Original source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32353201/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2020-Vasconcelos-Eugenol-Motor-Tests.txt)
