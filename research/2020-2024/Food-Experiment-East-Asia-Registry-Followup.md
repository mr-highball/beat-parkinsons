# Japanese and Korean dietary and botanical registrations and outcomes, 2020–2024

## Matured hop bitter acids and cognition

jRCTs031190162 was first disclosed January 1, 2020. The double-blind parallel trial targeted 40 adults with Parkinson’s and cognitive impairment. Matured hop bitter acid tablets were compared with placebo over 24 weeks, with primary measures including MoCA-J, Stroop, verbal fluency and visual paired associates. The registry marks the trial complete and provides no outcome estimate. The dietary constituent was given as a tablet, and the population differs from the investigators’ separate trials in healthy adults with subjective cognitive decline. [jRCT trial record](https://jrct.mhlw.go.jp/latest-detail/jRCTs031190162) · [MHLW registration mirror](https://rctportal.mhlw.go.jp/detail/jr?trial_id=jRCTs031190162) · [Separate healthy-adult study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32474473/)

## Majainhwan chart review and constipation

KCT0005033 registers a chart review published by Cho et al. in 2022. The researchers analyzed 68 records from a Korean medicine hospital, including 48 people with Parkinson’s disease and 20 with parkinsonism. Patients received a multi-herb formula after changing from earlier laxatives. The authors classified response as effective in 38 of 44 outpatients and 23 of 24 inpatients, with five diarrhea cases. The chart review combined two diagnoses and lacked a concurrent control. [CRIS trial record](https://cris.nih.go.kr/cris/search/detailSearch.do/16725) · [Primary report, DOI 10.13048/jkm.22043](https://www.jkom.org/upload/jkm-43-4-8.pdf)

## Korean medicine and constipation microbiome observations

CRIS first registered KCT0005744 on January 8, 2021. This single-center prospective observational study targeted 20 participants with Parkinson’s and constipation. It planned to document Korean medicine treatments used in usual care alongside clinical and microbiome measures. The public record gives no posted results or standardized single ingredient. [CRIS trial record](https://cris.nih.go.kr/cris/search/detailSearch.do/17971)

## Kestose and inulin for wearing-off

jRCTs041220054 was first registered August 15, 2022. The Fujita Health University single-arm study targets 20 adults with Parkinson’s and wearing-off. It will assess oral kestose and inulin together, with symptom-diary off time and gut microbiome changes as primary outcomes. The registry reported recruiting in its October 2026 update and gives no outcomes. This is related to dietary prebiotic fiber and supplies no estimate for a frozen culinary preparation. [Japanese jRCT trial record](https://jrct.mhlw.go.jp/latest-detail/jRCTs041220054) · [English jRCT trial record](https://jrct.mhlw.go.jp/en-latest-detail/jRCTs041220054)

## Injected botanical mixture for pain

KCT0007254 registers a trial of an injected multicomponent pharmacopuncture product versus manual acupuncture or usual care for Parkinson’s pain. The 2023 protocol targeted 90 participants with Parkinson’s pain and planned a numeric pain intensity endpoint. The product includes botanical extracts such as peach kernel together with other ingredients. The protocol supplies no result and the exposure differs from eating fruit. [CRIS trial record](https://cris.nih.go.kr/cris/search/detailSearch.do/22000) · [Published protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9914000/)

## Roasted Japanese Mucuna powder crossover

Sakata et al. compared a single dose of roasted, ground Japanese *Mucuna pruriens* seeds with levodopa/carbidopa in a crossover of seven people with Parkinson’s. The authors reported about twice the ON duration with the seed powder and higher plasma levodopa, with no observed increase in dyskinesia. The active levodopa exposure, small sample, and acute endpoints limit extrapolation. Hasshou beans are *Mucuna pruriens*. Frozen broad-bean statements use *Vicia faba*. [Primary report, DOI 10.2169/internalmedicine.3171-23](https://doi.org/10.2169/internalmedicine.3171-23)

## Hydrogen-steam processed Mucuna powder

Neshige and Neshige described a retail and retrospective clinical review of a processed *Mucuna pruriens* powder. More than 200 people purchased and used the product, and four cases had rectal exposure. The authors gave incidental clinical assessments for selected cases and no prospective comparator or systematic outcome for the oral users. This is a distinct preparation and study from the Japanese crossover. [Primary report, PMID 38645304](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11031812/)
