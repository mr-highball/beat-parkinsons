# Dill and black sticky rice in a clinical stroke trial

## Registered clinical trial of the dill and rice extract

TCTR20201224001 lists December 24, 2020 as its registration date, with planned first enrollment March 15, 2021 and target enrollment 60. The official export lists matching placebo and two combined Khao Kum rice and dill extract arms, 600 and 1,200 mg/day. Its primary outcome is hematological and clinical chemistry change at six weeks. NIHSS score is among the six-week secondary outcomes. The export displays enrollment by invitation, a December 29, 2022 completion date in the results section, and blank public results fields. The linked publication describes NIHSS as central to assessing efficacy and reports 51 randomized. No one-week NIHSS endpoint appears in the official export. ## Randomized dill and rice extract trial after minor stroke or TIA

On November 19, 2024, Kongbunkiat and colleagues reported 51 randomized adults after minor ischemic stroke or transient ischemic attack, with 13 completing in each of three arms. Subjects also received standard care. The capsule contained 600 or 1,200 mg/day of black sticky rice and dill extracts or a starch placebo. The report describes drying and ethanol extraction of authenticated dill and does not name the plant part. Its extract ratio was undisclosed. The 2019 animal ratio and food preparation therefore remain distinct.

Because total baseline NIHSS scores differed between groups, Table 8 compares percent changes from baseline. At one week the 1,200 mg/day group changed −87.50% versus −36.03% with placebo (p = .045). The 600 mg/day comparison was p = .433. The two-week and six-week comparisons for both doses were inconclusive. The discussion says the improvement occurred at two weeks. Table 8, §3.3 and the abstract place it at one week. The registry lists NIHSS at six weeks as secondary. No arm estimate for a distinct NIHSS motor item or bradykinesia appears in the report. Table 9's feeding item records self-feeding independence and found no clear comparison by dose or visit. It supplies no dysphagia result.

The study reports uneven withdrawals, including eight in placebo and two per treatment arm, and inconsistent enumeration of withdrawal reasons in prose. Figure 1 and the participant totals show 39 completers. Adverse symptoms included nausea and vomiting in some treated participants and headache and dizziness in one high-dose withdrawal. The short-term neurological composite yields a related comparison for claim 0115. The blend, stroke population, endpoint, attrition and timing differences limit its relevance to fresh dill and Parkinson bradykinesia.
