# Parkinson swallowing and food in the 2023 and 2024 JSDR proceedings

The Japanese Society of Dysphagia Rehabilitation published these abstracts in journal volumes 27(3) and 28(3). The meetings ran September 2–3, 2023 and August 30–31, 2024. Page links use the PDF page number, which is one above the printed S-page number. The abstracts and registry entries are summarized in original text here. No permissive redistribution license was verified for the proceedings. [JSDR official meeting archive](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/meeting/meeting_annual.html)

## Water bolus size and residue in the 2023 endoscopic study

Curtis and Troche, ENS4-01, studied 23 people with Parkinson disease using flexible endoscopic evaluation of swallowing and 5 mL, 10 mL, 20 mL and patient-selected cup sips of water. The analysis covered 243 swallows. Larger volumes were associated with less frequent residue on the epiglottis, laryngeal vestibule, vocal folds and subglottis (p = .049, .008, .002 and < .001, respectively), but larger amounts when residue was present at several pharyngeal or laryngeal sites. The abstract's conclusion also reports fewer penetration and aspiration events and more material in penetration events when present. It gives no event counts or effect sizes for those conclusions. It reports no carbonation or food ingredient comparison. [2023 proceedings, ENS4-01, S61](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no29_abstracts.pdf#page=62)

## Carbonated water and boiled beans after surgery for Wallenberg syndrome

Tanimura and colleagues, ENS4-05, described a 77-year-old man with stage IV Parkinson disease and new, severe dysphagia after a lateral medullary stroke. He resumed some porridge in a side-lying position after inpatient swallowing rehabilitation. His swallowing subsequently deteriorated until he could manage only small amounts of jelly. After diagnosis of Wallenberg syndrome, cricopharyngeal resection and vocal-fold adduction were followed by coordinated nutrition, bowel and sputum management and swallowing rehabilitation. At one month he could consume carbonated water and yogurt, and at ten months he could also take boiled beans, cheese and bananas in a right side-lying position. The bean species and banana preparation were unspecified. These foods documented regained oral intake after surgery and rehabilitation. No food-specific change in dysphagia was measured, and the case cannot establish whether boiled black beans worsen swallowing difficulty for `0077`. [2023 proceedings, ENS4-05, S63](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no29_abstracts.pdf#page=64)

## Rice balls and whole-swallowed side dishes in advanced Parkinson disease

Okada and colleagues, O10-04, followed one patient in their 80s with roughly 30 years of Parkinson disease and Hoehn and Yahr stage IV. On admission the patient ate about half of the rice-ball staple and sometimes swallowed bite-sized regular side dishes whole, with choking reported. The side dishes involved in choking were unspecified. Videofluoroscopy showed tongue pumping, impaired bolus formation and pharyngeal residue. After 18 months of medication adjustments, wheelchair fitting, mealtime supervision, speech-language therapy and food-form changes, whole-swallowing ceased and the patient ate all of the served food. BMI rose from 17.0 to 18.6, while Food Intake Level Scale score fell from 8 to 7. The report gives no new individual side dish names or effects. [2023 proceedings, O10-04, S101](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no29_abstracts.pdf#page=102)

## Thickened cup ramen during rehabilitation for Parkinson dysphagia

Jinba and colleagues, P12-09, reported a man in his 70s with stage III Parkinson disease, an acute deterioration with psychiatric symptoms and aspiration pneumonia. After pneumonia treatment and indirect swallowing therapy, the team used a crushed, thickened preparation of his requested instant cup ramen in videofluoroscopy and then in supervised direct training under conditions set by the assessment. Subsequent oral intake increased until he could finish the texture-code-3 side dishes served to him. The ramen was an individually adapted test and training food in a wider program. The report provides no ramen ingredient list or comparison isolating its effect. [2023 proceedings, P12-09, S260](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no29_abstracts.pdf#page=261)

## Texture and bolus controls outside Parkinson disease

Riera and colleagues, ENS3-01, used 12 healthy volunteers and measured oral processing across five foods, including boiled potato, bread, banana, carrot and cracker. The textural analyses covered several foods and showed reduced hardness and increased adhesiveness after oral processing. Sugita and colleagues, O21-03, measured shape retention of 3D-printed mashed-potato flakes with two commercial gelling agents in a bench study. The records concern boiled potato or a mash formulation, with no people with Parkinson disease or baked-potato symptom comparison for `0290`. [2023 proceedings, ENS3-01, S59](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no29_abstracts.pdf#page=60) · [2023 proceedings, O21-03, S129](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no29_abstracts.pdf#page=130)

## Parkinson subgroup in the Mataro swallowing physiology comparison

Tomsen, Ortega and Clavé reported a five-group analysis of swallowing biomechanics and pharyngeal sensory physiology. A March 2023 Dysphagia Research Society poster preceded the JSDR abstract, ENS1-03, which included 50 patients with Parkinson-related dysphagia. The February 12, 2024 journal abstract lists 78 Parkinson participants, while its body and Table 1 list 50, matching the JSDR abstract. The study combined existing clinical studies, and the available text does not explain the two counts. Treat these presentations and the article as one analysis family. [March 2023 official DRS poster proceedings](https://www.dysphagiaresearch.org/resource/resmgr/conference/2023/General_Poster_Sessions__1__.pdf) · [2023 JSDR, ENS1-03, S54–55](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no29_abstracts.pdf#page=55) · [2024 article, PMID 38345868](https://doi.org/10.1111/nyas.15103)

The journal methods describe water with radiographic contrast and several commercial thickening agents at 5, 10 and 20 mL. The kinematic analysis used a 10 mL nectar-viscosity contrast bolus, without culinary nectar. Parkinson and the other dysphagia groups showed delayed laryngeal vestibule closure and impaired swallowing safety compared with healthy volunteers. Laryngeal vestibule opening was delayed in the Parkinson group (p = .0065). The paper reports disease-group comparisons and gives no comparative symptom result for an individual culinary ingredient. [2024 article, Methods and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1111/nyas.15103)

## Fujita Parkinson swallowing CT registration

UMIN000050721 was first disclosed March 30, 2023. Fujita Health University registered 320-row detector swallowing CT in patients with Parkinson disease who were still taking three meals orally. Laryngeal closure timing and vestibular volume were primary outcomes. Other registered measures included upper esophageal sphincter opening, pharyngeal volume, penetration and aspiration and patient-reported dysphagia. The protocol describes the imaging examination without identifying a culinary ingredient. [Original registry, UMIN000050721](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/icdr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000057788) · [2025 abstract and 2026 registry results](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-JSDR-Recent-Abstracts.md#thin-and-honey-thick-liquid-in-parkinson-swallowing-ct)

## NCNP carbonation and swallowing registrations

The National Center of Neurology and Psychiatry registered UMIN000051163, public May 29, 2023, and UMIN000051942, public August 18, 2023. Both records list Keigo Nakayama, target 50 people with Parkinson disease and describe counterbalanced 10 mL barium preparations made with tap water or carbonated water during videofluoroscopy. UMIN000051163 specifies a crossover design and includes viscosity measurements and the Penetration–Aspiration Scale as primary outcomes. UMIN000051942 codes the design as parallel despite describing opposite bolus sequences and lists videofluoroscopy as the primary outcome. These are separate registry entries for closely aligned protocols. No participant overlap can be established from the registries. [UMIN000051163 original record](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000058349) · [UMIN000051942 original record](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/icdr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000057259) · [Subsequent registry status](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-JSDR-Recent-Abstracts.md#ncnp-carbonation-study-status)

## Porridge retention and nutrition supplements in a 2024 Parkinson case

Okada and colleagues, O15-02, described a patient in their 80s with stage IV Parkinson disease, low weight and esophageal-phase impairment. Videofluoroscopy showed esophageal retention with porridge. Alternating swallows with jelly or liquid reduced retention. The team introduced one high-energy jelly and two Meibalance Mini drinks daily within an hour after exercise. At ten months, the abstract reports that all of the supplements were consumed, weight increased from 39.8 to 44.2 kg and Geriatric Nutritional Risk Index rose from 90.1 to 93.8. The case does not separate the effects of meal timing, exercise, the products and other care. Overlap with the same group's later 2025 patients is possible given the descriptions, but the abstracts do not establish identity. [2024 proceedings, O15-02, S109](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no30_abstracts2.pdf#page=110) · [2025 two-case report](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-JSDR-Recent-Abstracts.md#water-and-jelly-between-porridge-swallows)

## Soft-cooked egg and preferred snacks in stage V Parkinson disease

Makino, P19-05, described an older nursing-home resident with Hoehn and Yahr stage V Parkinson disease, previous chronic aspiration pneumonia and low intake on blended institutional meals. A dietitian and clinical team introduced a soft-cooked onsen egg, an unnamed nutritional formula and preferred foods tailored to his condition. The patient gradually ate more, stopped losing weight and was later able to take rice crackers, cookies and manju under clinical assessment and assistance. The report mentions aspiration and fever during the course without attributing either to a named food. Neither a standardized food-specific dysphagia score nor a comparison group was reported. [2024 proceedings, P19-05, S305](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no30_abstracts2.pdf#page=306)

## Lollipop used during a neck-position rehabilitation case

Ikegawa and Tatsuta, P6-10, used sucking a lollipop as one swallowing-training activity for an older bedridden woman with Parkinson disease. A dentist and physical therapist provided tongue and neck therapy. Neck extension improved from 40 degrees to neutral and tongue motion improved. Candy ingredients and candy-specific swallowing or aspiration outcomes were unspecified. The 2025 second version repeats the authors, observation dates and result and describes the same case. [2024 proceedings, P6-10, S246](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no30_abstracts2.pdf#page=247) · [2025 proceedings, P-114, S279](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no31_abstracts.pdf#page=280)

## Thickened water and jelly after gastrostomy

The team reporting P21-06 evaluated a person with Parkinson disease and deconditioning who was fully tube-fed and had a pressure ulcer. During endoscopy, 3 mL of moderately thickened water was swallowed without observed penetration or aspiration. Direct training and medication and nutrition review followed. Jelly was introduced after six months, high-energy jelly was used at lunch later, and full oral intake was reported by 15 months. P21-07 on the same page was a different Parkinson case. It reported pharyngeal residue with a texture-code-3 diet that cleared with repeated swallows. In a separate report, P21-12, a man with Parkinson disease and orthostatic hypotension progressed to a mousse texture during supervised training with side-lying, repeated dry swallows and blood-pressure monitoring. The studies describe individual swallowing evaluations and combined care without specifying active food ingredients or isolating a food effect. [2024 proceedings, P21-06 and P21-07, S314](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no30_abstracts2.pdf#page=315) · [2024 proceedings, P21-12, S316](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no30_abstracts2.pdf#page=317)
