# Blackberry exposures and pain outcomes

Reviewed 2026-11-14 (US Eastern).

## Registry for blackberry enriched milk and exercise soreness

NCT05463835 was first posted on 2022-07-19. The single-blind proof-of-concept RCT reports enrollment of 32 men aged 18–45. It compared RubusElite blackberry-purée high-protein milk with high-protein milk and low-protein milk. The registered muscle soreness measure explicitly used a 0–10 pain scale through 72 hours after exercise. No summary results were posted on ClinicalTrials.gov. The university thesis below identifies the same registration and supplies the consumed freeze-dried form and outcome data. [RubusElite official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05463835), [local text](Source-Text/NCT05463835-RubusElite-Registry-Extract.txt).

## RubusElite thesis muscle soreness results

Carey's 2023 thesis became publicly available in the University College Cork repository on 2024-05-27 according to CORA metadata. Chapter 5 presents the trial registered as NCT05463835. Thirty-two moderately active men completed it (12 blackberry milk, 10 high-protein milk, 10 low-protein milk). Each participant consumed two 500 mL servings per day over ten days. Each experimental serving used 25 g freeze-dried blackberry purée powder. The study methods give a target of 750 mg polyphenols per serving. Batch testing gave a mean measured concentration of 28.3 ± 2.9 mg/g and an estimated intake of 1,413 ± 147 mg/day. The high-protein comparison used the same starting milk without added blackberry, but the finished drinks differed in calories and carbohydrate per serving (341 versus 265 kcal and 43 versus 24 g carbohydrate in Table 1). The low-protein commercial milk was a second comparator.

Immediately after the exercise challenge, mean 0–10 muscle soreness scores were 2.3 ± 1.7, 4.0 ± 2.2 and 3.9 ± 1.6 in the three groups, respectively. The blackberry drink was lower than high-protein milk (p=.038) and low-protein milk (p=.036) at this time point. Across the post-exercise observations, the treatment term was p=.007, with a lower mean soreness level on the blackberry drink than either comparison (p<.001 and p=.001, respectively). The treatment-by-time interaction was p=.914. Isolated between-arm comparisons at 24, 48 and 72 hours were inconclusive (p=.158, .291 and .114). These data describe post-exercise soreness levels. The original limitations section (printed p. 233) says soreness was not measured at baseline, so pre-existing soreness differences cannot be accounted for. The groups received different calories and carbohydrate, the investigator was aware of the assigned drink, and the treatment-by-time result does not establish a faster recovery slope. The experiment used a freeze-dried dairy blend and participants did not represent a Parkinson cohort. [Carey thesis, Chapter 5](https://cora.ucc.ie/items/2591e1ee-e355-4831-9429-158b32fd2872), [local chapter text](Source-Text/Carey-RubusElite-2024-Chapter-5.txt).

The [Palavra Parkinson cohort](Food-Experiment-Date-Pain-Followup.md) supplies a complementary chronic-pain and diet measurement. Reported group differences concern total sugar in a 145-item food-frequency study. That paper supplies no quantified blackberry item or frozen-blackberry pain contrast.

## Microfiltered juice made from frozen tropical highland blackberries

Quesada-Morúa and colleagues published this randomized dietary crossover on 2020-06-28. Thirteen healthy adults completed both periods. The fruit was ripe *Rubus adenotrichos* cultivar Vino, stored at −20 °C before crushing, enzyme treatment and microfiltration. The beverage contained 50% juice by volume and was taken as 250 mL with each of three daily meals on the high-fat and high-carbohydrate background. Water with optional sugar was the comparator. The authors measured lipid, glucose and antioxidant enzyme endpoints. They did not report a pain scale or PD cohort. [Quesada-Morúa highland blackberry article](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/JBR-190516), [local text](Source-Text/2020-Quesada-Frozen-Highland-Blackberry-Juice-Extract.txt).
