# Mango fruit, sleep and fatigue

## Recife Parkinson diet dissertation and symptom instruments

Bezerra's Universidade Federal de Pernambuco dissertation, approved 10 February 2020, uses data collected in Recife in 2019. Figure 2 and the surrounding Results place mango among the most frequently eaten foods for women, with 31.3% reporting daily consumption. Annex B lists mango separately under fruit and lists fruit juice separately under drinks. The FFQ mango entry gives frequency and no preparation, cultivar or time with respect to meals and medication. A second instrument collected a 24-hour food recall.

Methods on printed pages 33–34 specify use of Hoehn–Yahr and UPDRS domains II and III, with domain totals reported. The template in Appendix B includes item 41 under Part IV, asking a binary question that groups insomnia and hypersomnolence. The data collection methods used only domains II and III. This prevents treating the appendix's item 41 as a collected outcome. UPDRS motor examination uses fatigue or movement decrement within some examination anchors, distinct from separately rated fatigue severity. The results provide no individual mango–sleep or mango–fatigue estimate. ## Ataulfo juice byproduct and pediatric infection fatigue

Anaya-Loyola and colleagues analyzed an intervention with dried and milled peel and remnant pulp of Ataulfo mango. The study randomized 80 schoolchildren aged 6–8. Thirty-two controls and 33 children assigned to the by-product completed two months of 2 g/day product in water or flavored water alone. The questionnaire tracked fatigue incidence as one of several infection symptoms. Table 3S reports 6 (18.75%) controls versus 3 (9.09%) in the mango group at baseline (p=.3708), and 3 (9.38%) versus 0 at month two (p=.1823). The table header labels the mango arm n=32, although the proportions and complete-case Methods indicate n=33. The supplement gives incidence rather than severity. It does not establish an effect on Parkinson fatigue or on consumption of raw mango. ## Recife Parkinson cohort journal report

The journal report, published online on 7 October 2022, analyzed 79 adults with diagnosed Parkinson disease, 59.5% men and mean age 65.4 years. It reports daily mango frequency of 31.3% among women. Clinical measures comprised Hoehn–Yahr and UPDRS II and III. Figure 2 reports only the food-frequency statistic. Published association models use aggregated food scores and body composition. The labels “risk” and “protection” concern chronic noncommunicable diseases as categorized by the investigators. The paper gives no mango-specific result for insomnia or fatigue, no preparation for the mango item, and derives from the 2020 thesis cohort. ## Fresh versus dried mango with glucose and satiety outcomes

Stamper and colleagues compared 100 kcal portions of fresh mango, dried mango or white bread in a crossover among 34 healthy adults. The named cultivars included Tommy Atkins, Kent and Keitt. The abstract calls the design randomized and the Methods place white bread first. The report measured postprandial glucose and appetite over a 90-minute session following each snack. It verifies separate fresh and dried fruit arms. Fatigue severity, insomnia and mood were outside the outcome battery, and the study did not enroll a Parkinson cohort. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10374964/) · [Trial registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03956602) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2023-Fresh-Dried-Mango-Satiety-Extract.txt)

## Previously frozen Tommy Atkins pulp and cycling performance

Nieman and colleagues published online on 21 December 2023. Twenty-two trained cyclists completed a crossover of 330 g/day of cubed, previously frozen Tommy Atkins mango pulp supplied in two servings versus water for two weeks. Participants could add the pulp to yogurt or smoothies. In the 2.25-hour standardized cycling session, mango and water had similar average watts (p=.756), cycling distance (p=.238), and speed (p=.325). The trial measured inflammatory lipid mediators as a core outcome. There was no separate time-to-exhaustion comparison or patient-reported fatigue-severity result. The treatment sequence also included different products after exercise. ## Mango frequency and self reported sleep among Ugandan students

Kiggwe and colleagues surveyed 403 students and published their preprint on 25 September 2023. Table 6 gives a Pearson correlation of −.057, without a significance marker, between self-reported time sleeping during weekends in a semester and mango frequency. The original PDF clips the later columns. The recovered Research Square HTML and the journal's separate Table 6 page both give r = −.094 for holiday weekend sleep and r = −.085 for the study's sleep-time composite. The table marks these two correlations at nominal one-tailed p < .05. The semester estimate has no significance marker. These are unadjusted questionnaire correlations. Both sources concern student questionnaires, with no quantified insomnia, Parkinson population, cultivar, raw preparation or timing of mango use. ## Non-soluble mango leaf trials and distinct fatigue measures

López-Ríos and colleagues ran two acute pilot studies with concentrated leaf extract in healthy people. In study 1, Table 2 gives mean POMS fatigue of 2.10 before and 1.12 at 90 minutes after the extract. Placebo means went from 2.05 to 0.37 as printed. The p = .015 under the extract column is a within-condition comparison. The public table gives no corresponding exposure-by-time contrast. Study 2 found no clear leaf extract POMS effect at 60 minutes. The same report found an in vitro COMT result for isolated mangiferin, with no clinical levodopa or oral mango-flesh comparison. [Primary article, Methods and Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2020.112996) · [Publisher-formatted table](https://www.tyrin.eu/user/documents/upload/Studie_a_vyskum/1_Central%20nervous%20system%20activities%20of%20extract%20Mangifera%20indica%20L..pdf) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2020-Lopez-Rios-Leaf-Pilot-Fatigue.txt)

Wightman and colleagues tested one 300 mg dose in a 70-person crossover. The article reports no intervention effect for mental fatigue during the cognitive demand battery. The result differs from the within-condition fatigue result in the smaller pilot. [Primary report, Sections 3.2 and 3.3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7468873/) · [Local licensed report](Source-Text/PMC7468873.txt) · [Dated registration](Source-Text/NCT04299217-Mango-Leaf-Registry-Extract.txt)

Dodd and colleagues independently compared 300 mg of the extract and placebo in a 114-person crossover. Supplementary File 4 gives p = .309 for the treatment main effect and p = .308 for treatment by time for task-related mental fatigue. The POMS Fatigue–Inertia results give p = .354 for treatment and p = .669 for treatment by time. These trials study acute responses to leaf extract in healthy people.
