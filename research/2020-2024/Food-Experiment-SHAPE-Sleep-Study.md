# SHAPE Sleep Study

## Corn oil label in an early SHAPE conference listing

The ISBNPA 2020 listing describes a 16-week trial in postmenopausal women. One six-person arm received 25 g whey protein with 4.3 g of comparator oil described as corn. The listing reports an overall intervention effect for sleep efficiency (p = .03) and the global sleep-quality score (p = .01). It supplies no numerical whey-plus-oil versus whey-only estimate. The published May 2020 report has the same five group sizes, 6, 4, 10, 6 and 8, and describes soybean oil in the comparator arm. The discrepancy leaves the conference listing insufficient to establish actual corn-oil exposure. ## Soybean oil label in the published early SHAPE report

The May 2020 abstract describes 34 participants across five groups. The six-person placebo fat arm received whey protein and 4.3 g/day soybean oil. The authors report an overall Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index change and a sleep-efficiency result of p = .06. Group-matched outcomes and denominators link this record to the ISBNPA description, which differs in oil label and some reported aggregate results. The published abstract includes the incomplete identifier NCT0303041. The official SHAPE record with matching investigator, title and arm design is NCT03603041. ## SHAPE dissertation sleep tables and comparator identity

The December 2020 Hawley dissertation identifies soybean oil capsules in the whey plus placebo fat arm. Thirty-nine women completed the study procedures. In Table 7, mean global Pittsburgh sleep scores changed from 7.7 to 6.3 in the whey-plus-soybean arm and from 6.7 to 4.6 in the whey-only arm. The table leaves a dedicated adjusted comparison between those two arms unresolved. Oil refining is unspecified. This postmenopausal sleep trial does not provide a Parkinson insomnia result. The original body, published 2020 abstract and 2021 follow-up consistently identify soybean oil despite the earlier corn label. ## SHAPE follow-up sleep and mood outcomes

The 2021 report describes 39 postmenopausal women across five groups, including seven receiving whey protein with 4.1 g/day soybean oil. It reports a time effect for subjective sleep. The reported global sleep-score changes were minus 20.2% for the whey-plus-oil group and minus 30.3% for whey alone. The abstract calls the between-group sleep evidence inconclusive and supplies no adjusted oil-specific contrast. The companion body-composition abstract uses the same participants and formulation. These are multiple records from the SHAPE trial, without a confirmed corn-oil or Parkinson insomnia comparison.
