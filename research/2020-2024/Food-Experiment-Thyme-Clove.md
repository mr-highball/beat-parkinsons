# Thyme and clove evidence, 2020–2024

## Clove odor identification and the Parkinson freezing phenotype

Glover and colleagues published their cross-sectional report on August 12, 2021. It included 60 people with Parkinson’s and freezing, 31 with Parkinson’s without freezing and 33 controls. Table 2 reports a between-Parkinson-group clove odor identification contrast of 12 percentage points (p = .212). Classification incorporated freezing history or examination, and testing occurred during the ON-medication state. Clove was a scratch-and-sniff test item, and its recognition was the compared outcome. The study gives no freezing frequency response to consuming ground clove. [Primary report, Tables 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8397477/) · [Local CC BY article](Source-Text/PMC8397477.txt)

## Thyme lozenges and acoustic measurements after vocal loading

Frechen and von Latoszek published this randomized double-blind study online in 2021. Eighteen vocally healthy professional voice users received a proprietary thyme lozenge or placebo after vocal loading. Subjective mouth dryness and hoarseness improved in the thyme group. Objective voice measures gave no clear between-group change. Listener-rated intelligibility, Parkinson’s hypophonia and eating fresh thyme were outside the study design. The 2019 study used a similar product and larger sample. Neither study establishes a change in speech intelligibility. [Primary author abstract and publication date](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34852952/) · [2019 perceptual outcomes](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Thyme-Clove.md#thyme-drops-after-an-induced-vocal-fatigue-task)

## Thyme among twelve odors in post-COVID smell training

Altundag and colleagues followed 75 people with post-COVID parosmia, of whom 38 chose modified olfactory training and 37 did no training. Thyme was one of four odors in the middle training block. Clove was one of four in the first block. The 2022 report measured a total Sniffin’ Sticks threshold, discrimination and identification score. At nine months, the trained group’s mean was 27.9 versus 14.0 in controls. The comparison was nonrandomized and the protocol changed several odors together. It did not estimate a separate thyme effect and involved neither eating fresh thyme nor Parkinson’s. [Primary report, Methods and Table III](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/lary.30101)

## Dried thyme and anxiety in university students

The trial first appeared on ClinicalTrials.gov on August 14, 2023, after its recorded completion in December 2022. The current registry reports actual enrollment of 86 and triple masking. The article describes 106 randomized participants and double blinding. The arm descriptions give oral thyme capsules versus starch placebo, while the registry intervention-name field links the thyme label to both arms. The published Methods distinguish dried powdered thyme leaf and starch. Among 106 randomized students, the published HADS anxiety means changed from 7.33 to 5.82 with thyme and 7.62 to 7.39 with starch, with a printed group-by-time p = .030 in Table III. The [table consistency check](#consistency-check-on-the-thyme-anxiety-tables) records incompatible dispersion and category counts. The preparation and anxiety measure are close to claim 0362, and the sample had no Parkinson’s subgroup. The self-reported prospective and retrospective memory questionnaire is distinct from an objective attention task for claim 0887. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05987228) · [Local registry fields](Source-Text/Trials/NCT05987228.txt) · [Primary report, Methods and Tables II–III](https://www.europeanreview.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/10806-10814.pdf)

## Eugenol and isolated rat bladder contraction

Araújo and colleagues published the bladder study December 31, 2024. Eugenol applied to rat detrusor strips reduced both phasic and sustained contractions evoked by potassium and carbachol. This is an ex vivo constituent comparison. It gives no clinical measure of urgency, no Parkinson’s subgroup and no oral ground-clove exposure. [Primary report, Methods and Figures 1–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11710953/)

## Earliest recovered presentation of the clove oil rat study

Hamdi, Ouachik, Messaoud and Hafidi presented the clove oil work in Hammamet, March 21–24, 2022. Oral abstract 97, on printed page 51, describes 6-OHDA-exposed SH-SY5Y cells and rats, with actimetry and cylinder outcomes at days 8 and 15. The abstract reports better performance in the treated rats. A 2024 poster by the same research team gives the same test families and lesion schedule. The chronology and protocols indicate reports of the same experimental work, so the two presentations are linked to the existing 2023 paper instead of counted as independent experiments. [2022 original proceedings, oral abstract 97](https://www.atsb-bio.org/archive/31eme%20Congres%20ATSB%202022/Oral%20ATSB%202022.pdf) · [2024 original proceedings, poster 49, p. 140](https://atsb-bio.org/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Book-of-Abstracts-ATSB-2024.pdf) · [2023 full report and 2024 thesis](Food-Experiment-Botanical-Model-Followup.md#clove-essential-oil-and-motor-tests-in-rats)

## Oral isolated eugenol before and after toxin exposure

The Vora study compared oral eugenol given before and during MPTP exposure with eugenol begun after MPTP exposure. Pretreatment shortened beam crossing and improved catalepsy at the higher tested exposures, while post-treatment prolonged crossing and the highest tested exposure worsened locomotion. The frozen clove claims concern ingestion of the ground spice in people with Parkinson’s. The animal results depend on when isolated eugenol was given, and the tasks do not directly measure falls, clinical rigidity or bradykinesia. [Primary study, Figures 2–4](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ddt/16/4/16_2022.01026/_pdf) · [Related eugenol comparisons](Food-Experiment-Allspice-Cumin.md#oral-eugenol-timing-and-motor-tasks-in-toxin-models)

## Registered clove aromatherapy for postoperative headache

IRCT registered IRCT20211124053172N1 on January 1, 2022. The three-arm design specifies inhaled clove, inhaled lavender and a control in urological surgery, with a visual scale for headache after spinal anesthesia. The registration describes routine care in one field and saline inhalation in another for controls. The linked 2025 article reports statistically inconclusive group contrasts. ## Pediatric dental comparisons for topical clove products

In a randomized 2021 study with 15 children per arm, topical lignocaine gave lower Wong–Baker pain scores than clove oil (means 2.13 and 4.93, p < .001) and lower observer scores (4.93 and 6.67, p = .026). Clove oil and ice were statistically inconclusive on both pain scales. A separate 2020 study compared a combined clove–papaya gel, benzocaine and precooling in children. Its published abstract ranks the facial pain means in that order with benzocaine highest and ice lowest, and gives no clear overall difference. The combination study cannot isolate clove. [2021 primary report, Tables 1–4](https://jdapm.org/search.php?code=0223JDAPM&id=10.17245/jdapm.2021.21.4.329&vmode=PUBREADER&where=aview) · [2020 author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32611866/)

ISRCTN10078508 first appeared October 18, 2023 after its recorded 2022 study period. The registry gives a double-blind description and the 2024 journal article describes an assessor-blinded design with no participant or clinician masking. The study assigned 60 children to topical clove oil or benzocaine gel. The between-group observer and self-rated pain comparisons were inconclusive (p = .326 and .714). There was no inactive comparator or equivalence margin. These three trials address immediate procedural pain in children and provide no clinical Parkinson pain estimate. [Official registry, PDF](https://www.isrctn.com/pdf/ISRCTN10078508) · [Local registry fields](Source-Text/Trials/ISRCTN10078508.txt) · [2024 primary report, Tables 2–3](https://doi.org/10.5114/jos.2024.145771)

## Placebo data from the oral clove hangover trial

2020-07-02. The later inflammation analysis identified the original Mammen study and reanalyzed its placebo condition. It describes a severity score averaged across 15 hangover symptoms. It does not furnish a clove-versus-placebo pain contrast. This is the existing 16-person cohort and does not add an independent clinical effect to claim 0956. [Primary reanalysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7408936/) · [Local source text](Source-Text/PMC7408936.txt)

## Eugenol and isolated rat bladder contraction

Published December 31, 2024. Araújo and colleagues found concentration-dependent inhibition of phasic and tonic contractions in isolated rat detrusor strips. The paper reports complete suppression at the highest tested 3 mM bath concentration for the potassium and carbachol stimuli. The laboratory comparison uses isolated eugenol and measures muscle force. Urinary urgency episodes and ground clove intake were not measured. [Original journal report](https://www.einj.org/upload/pdf/inj-2448326-163.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2024-Araujo-Eugenol-Rat-Detrusor.txt)

## Thyme and clove oils as EEG identification odors

Aydemir and Üçüncü’s October 5, 2020 conference record uses EEG responses during exposure to rosemary, thyme, mint and clove oil odors for participant identification. Parkinson disease appears as context for EEG use. The measured outcome is recognition of individual signal patterns. The archived original abstract supplies no ingested thyme or clove exposure, clinical odor threshold, or symptom comparison. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1109/SIU49456.2020.9302456) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2020-Aydemir-Ucuncu-Odor-EEG-Abstract-Extract.txt)

## Consistency check on the thyme anxiety tables

Alqudah et al. randomized 106 university students in 2023. In Table II, normal-range anxiety counts were 27 of 53 with dried thyme and 30 of 53 with starch at baseline. After one month the counts were 40 and 31, respectively. The printed mild range overlaps the moderate and severe ranges. Table III prints mean baseline thyme HADS-A 7.33, SD 0.8, while Table II lists six observations of at least 15. Those six alone require a sample SD of at least sqrt(6 × (15 − 7.33)^2 / 52) = 2.60, before counting any remaining observations. The unchanged published mean and SD belong to the same stated measurement and cannot jointly fit the threshold counts. The control mean change of 0.23 and marginal SDs of 0.9 and 0.4 also imply a paired t-statistic magnitude of at least 1.29 at n = 53, inconsistent with p = .71 under the stated calculation. The directional category counts are descriptive. Participant-level data or a published correction are needed to reconcile the inferential results. The cohort was university students. [Original source](https://www.europeanreview.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/10806-10814.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2023-Alqudah-Thyme-HADS-Table-Audit-Extract.txt)
