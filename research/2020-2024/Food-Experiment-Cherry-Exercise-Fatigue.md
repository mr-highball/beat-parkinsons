## Tart cherry concentrate and rugby player fatigue

Morehen and colleagues published online August 6, 2020. Eleven male rugby players completed a single-blind crossover of Montmorency concentrate and matched placebo around two successive matches. On the one-item five-point fatigue scale, mean ratings were 3.3 versus 3.3 at 24 hours and 3.5 versus 3.4 at 48 hours. The article reports no clear comparison or group change for fatigue, with limited washout. The concentrated drink and match-related fatigue differ from the frozen Parkinson claim. [Primary source](https://researchonline.ljmu.ac.uk/id/eprint/14053/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2020-Morehen-Rugby-Cherry-Fatigue-Extract.txt)

## Tart cherry extract and strength after resistance exercise

Thirteen resistance-trained men completed a crossover of proprietary tart cherry extract powder capsules and rice-flour placebo. At 24 hours grip change was +1.7 kg with extract and −2.0 kg with placebo, p=.017. The soreness comparisons and pairwise jump comparisons were inconclusive. The authors described central-fatigue interpretation as speculative; no perceived fatigue scale is reported. Sponsor supplied the capsules. [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8204440/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2021-Hooper-Cherry-Grip-Extract.txt)

## Tart cherry capsules and exercise exhaustion in hypoxia

Thirteen healthy adults completed a double-blind crossover in hypoxia. The tart cherry capsule condition had cycling exhaustion time of 940 seconds (SD 84), compared with 912 (SD 63) with flour capsules, p=.01. Perceived exertion was a stopping criterion. The oral preparation was a manufactured capsule and the result is exercise endurance under hypoxia. [Primary source](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/15/2/388) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2023-Horiuchi-Cherry-Hypoxia-Extract.txt)

## Tart cherry juice and electrically measured muscle fatigue

Twelve recreational cyclists completed a randomized crossover of tart cherry juice and a high-glycemic sports drink. Neither the low-frequency muscle fatigue measure nor perceived exertion showed a clear beverage difference. Cycling time-trial performance was 17 minutes in both conditions (p=.27). Low-frequency fatigue increased after exercise and recovered by 24 hours across conditions. The test measured evoked force after cycling, with no Parkinson cohort or dried whole fruit intervention. [Primary source](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0307263) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2024-Gao-Cherry-Electrical-Fatigue-Extract.txt)

## Recovery outcomes from the same hypoxic cycling cohort

Twelve of the 13 previous participants supplied additional data. No clear difference was reported for total exercise energy expenditure between the cherry and placebo hypoxic conditions (p=.41) or for recovery hypotension between the reported trials. The normoxia visit used no supplement. The paper adds observations from the same cohort, with no independent fatigue sample. [Primary source](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/ham.2024.0076) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2024-Horiuchi-Oliver-Cherry-Cohort-Extract.txt)
