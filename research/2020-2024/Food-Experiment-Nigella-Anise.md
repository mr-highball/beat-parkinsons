# Nigella seed and anise seed evidence

## Nigella oil adjunct in a depression trial

The 2021 Iranian registration planned 50 participants, six weeks and DASS-21 depression and serum BDNF outcomes. Rafiee Zadeh and colleagues reported 52 men with major depression receiving sertraline for the ten-week comparison of added nigella oil extract versus placebo. Table 2 gives endpoint means of 18.6 versus 23.4 (p < .001) and prints changes of −5.50 versus −0.24 (p < .001). The oil arm’s printed baseline of 23.1 implies a change of −4.5 points, one point smaller than its printed mean change. Both presentations favor the oil arm, and the magnitude needs clarification. The study used an oil extract capsule outside Parkinson’s. [Official registration](Source-Text/Trials/IRCT20200217046523N17.txt) · [Primary report and tables](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9081508/)

## Nigella combination and depression in hypothyroidism

The Iranian registry describes an eight-week mix of nigella, ajwain and lime alongside levothyroxine. In the 2024 publication, BDI-II fell within both groups. Table 5 shows 11.09 versus 10.51 at follow-up (p = .79). The preparation was a three-ingredient powder, with no ingredient-specific effect or Parkinson cohort. [Official registration](Source-Text/Trials/IRCT20210101049909N1.txt) · [Licensed article, Table 5](Source-Text/PMC11287032.txt#L222)

## Nigella enriched bar and sprint fatigue index

A 2021 double-blind trial randomized 54 military cadets to an enriched bar or an unenriched bar during two weeks of training. The enriched bar contained nigella, arginine and beta-alanine. The adjusted sprint power-based fatigue index changed −1.63 versus +0.34 watts per second (p = .016). The comparison does not isolate nigella or measure a patient-reported fatigue score. [Licensed primary text, Table 2](Source-Text/PMC8269545.txt#L178)

## Nigella oil and sleep in nonrestorative sleepers

CTRI/2021/05/033780 and CTRI/2021/05/033761 are distinct registrations for the 90-day questionnaire trial and seven-day actigraphy trial. They share a site, investigator team and proprietary oil. The 2023 randomized trial of 72 participants reported favorable Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index scores after 45 and 90 days on a proprietary black cumin oil extract. It measured daytime dysfunction as a sleep-quality component and reported no daytime drowsiness with fatigue by day 14. The latter was an adverse event observation. A separate 2024 seven-day actigraphy trial assigned 70 participants and reported favorable sleep efficiency, sleep onset latency and total sleep time with the same formulation. Its correction changes a placebo p value in Figure 2A. Participants had nonrestorative sleep and the intervention was a thymoquinone-rich oil. Neither trial tested ground seed or Parkinson insomnia. [2023 report](Source-Text/PMC10149792.txt#L208) · [2024 report](Source-Text/PMC10822901.txt#L212) · [Correction](Source-Text/PMC10897832.txt#L75)

## Nigella and sleep quality after menopause

Asadi and colleagues assigned 60 postmenopausal participants to nigella, zolpidem or placebo for eight weeks. The Methods describe an oily seed extract and give inconsistent amounts. Table 2 prints PSQI 7.2 to 6.6 for nigella and 11 to 9.5 for placebo, with overall p=.45. The prose gives a nigella end point of 6.0 and the table gives a positive change for a falling score. The categorical sleep-quality p=.07 is a separate within-arm result. Table 1 shows 7 of 20 with good sleep at baseline and 8 of 20 at follow-up. Those paired marginals differ by one. The standard uncorrected McNemar statistic can therefore be at most 1, which cannot give p=.07. A paired cross-tab or a published correction is needed. [Local factual text](Source-Text/2020-Asadi-Nigella-PSQI-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Original, Table 2](https://publish.kne-publishing.com/index.php/JFRH/article/download/4672/4256/6483)

## Registered nigella oil and depression during hemodialysis

The 2022 protocol and May 2020 Iranian registry specify a randomized nigella oil comparison in adults with diabetes receiving hemodialysis, with HADS depression among the outcomes. Rahmani and colleagues’ 2022 results paper gives the same trial identifier and reports 46 randomized, with 2 g/day oil versus paraffin for 12 weeks. It reports metabolic, oxidative-stress and inflammatory results. The HADS result remains unavailable in this publication. [Official registry snapshot](Source-Text/Trials/IRCT20200411047027N1.txt) · [Licensed protocol](Source-Text/PMC8815251.txt) · [Primary outcomes paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9033343/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2022-Rahmani-Nigella-Dialysis-Outcome-Extract.txt)

## Nigella oil and reported smell loss during COVID-19

The 2021 open-label comparison of oral oil against usual care reported shorter self-reported anosmia duration (8.5 versus 10.4 days, p < .01). In a separate 2022 double-blind trial with six seed-dose groups and placebo, persistence of smell loss at day 10 varied from 6% to 30.8% (overall p = .529). The studies used different designs and neither measured objective Parkinson hyposmia. [2021 primary Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8364675/) · [2022 licensed text, Table 2](Source-Text/PMC9517329.txt#L146)

## Nigella nasal drops and registered sleep outcomes

NCT05494164 enrolled 102 adults with chronic rhinosinusitis. It planned a comparison of nigella nasal oil plus standard care against standard care, with a Sleep Quality Scale and symptom domains. The 2024 article reports combined SNOT-22 sleep and emotional-domain findings. Separate results for the registered Sleep Quality Scale and smell item remain to be verified. Intranasal oil and rhinosinusitis differ from the frozen oral seed and Parkinson outcomes. [Official registry snapshot](Source-Text/Trials/NCT05494164.txt) · [Investigators’ article deposit](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.11056516)

## Nigella oil and pellet reaching in healthy mice

Hossain and colleagues assigned eight healthy male mice per arm to oral oil or saline. Reaching success was similar on the first two trials and was higher in the oil group by the eighth day in Figure 2. This is acquisition of a forelimb skill in healthy mice with oil, without a Parkinson hand-dexterity comparison. [Licensed primary text](Source-Text/PMC10473895.txt)

## Nigella oil before or after rotenone in rats

The Egyptian Journal of Histology’s September 2024 online record compared oil before and during rotenone exposure and oil after exposure against time-matched toxin groups. Figure 1 reports higher open-field activity and shorter forced-swim immobility in both oil comparisons. The pre-exposure comparison had a larger difference. The abstract states 60 animals and an oil dose in mg/kg, whereas the methods describe eight animals in each of six groups and a dose in mL/kg. Those discrepancies limit inference. Open-field activity and swim immobility are adjacent endpoints for bradykinesia and depressive symptoms. [Original article, pp. 350–356](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_378047_2ffcaf2f472addd55f6ff77ae67848ca.pdf) · [Publisher-deposited date](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.21608/ejh.2024.304554.2104)

## Anise extract and cognition or motivation proxies in mice

In the 2021 oral extract study, novel-object recognition and Morris water maze measures showed no clear memory effect. The study reported shorter forced-swim and tail-suspension immobility. Those are rodent depression-related proxies and give no measure of apathy, attention or executive function. [Licensed primary article](Source-Text/PMC8399924.txt)

## Anethole studies in rotenone-exposed rats

Vastegani and colleagues’ first report found a higher thermal pain threshold and favorable recognition or avoidance results with isolated anethole. The April 2023 corrigendum is indexed without its details in the accessible abstract. The November 2023 PLoS report found longer stride length and rotarod fall latency after anethole, using timed apparatus and footprint measures. A May 2023 report gives related motor and mood-proxy findings, with a March 2025 correction to Figure 8a. The papers share investigators, dosing schedule and toxin protocol. Cohort overlap is unverified. The outcomes do not establish everyday falls, clinical executive function or ground anise effects. [2022 primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36075399/) · [2023 corrigendum](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37129969/) · [PLoS full text, Figures 3–4](Source-Text/PMC10653401.txt#L193) · [2023 original](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37204660/) · [2025 corrected figure](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11011-025-01547-4)

## Ground anise preparation and abdominal pain during COVID-19

This randomized 2024 report gave aniseed powder or placebo in addition to standard care for acute COVID-19 with gastrointestinal symptoms. Table 2 reports an adjusted relative risk of 0.55 (95% CI 0.46 to 0.72) for abdominal pain under the authors’ model. The 2022 registry specified gastrointestinal symptoms and listed standard care alone as control, whereas the publication describes a placebo powder comparison. These results concern acute gastrointestinal pain outside Parkinson’s. [Official registry snapshot](Source-Text/Trials/IRCT20120506009651N3.txt) · [Licensed primary text](Source-Text/PMC10824915.txt#L222)

The figure and authors describe a favorable abdominal-pain trajectory, but Table 2 labels its ratio anise versus placebo while calling the event improvement. A ratio under one then points the opposite way. The report does not establish the modeled event coding. The sachet contained anise powder with rock sugar. Interpret the table as a reporting uncertainty and use the separately randomized dyspepsia study for the clearer pain-severity comparison.

## Nigella oil and movement in an MPTP preprint family

The 2021 Research Square preprint and earlier 2019 AfricArXiv version use the same animal approval identifier, and the later preprint cites an overlapping 2019 meeting abstract. The report describes less tremor and preserved striatal cell density in the oil pretreatment group, alongside an unexpected increase in recognition performance after toxin in the no-oil group. The preprint is not an independent replication of the earlier version. No direct freezing or Parkinson clinical bradykinesia measure was identified. [2021 version](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-522823/v1) · [Earlier version](https://doi.org/10.31730/osf.io/v7fu4) · [Meeting abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ibror.2019.09.025)

## Registered nigella oil massage and fatigue severity

NCT06298760 reports 150 participants assigned to reflexology with nigella oil, placebo reflexology, knee massage with nigella oil, placebo knee massage or standard care. It prespecified the Fatigue Severity Scale. The linked journal article’s abstract was indexed in December 2024, while its current publisher metadata lists November 2025 online and January 2026 issue publication. The abstract reports lower fatigue with oil procedures and describes reflexology with oil as the most favorable. Full numerical oil versus matched-placebo procedure comparisons remain open. [Official registry snapshot](Source-Text/Trials/NCT06298760.txt) · [Primary result](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39642267/)

## Anise seed preparations and memory tasks in two animal studies

The 2021 article administered total anise seed extract to healthy Swiss mice. Its authors report no clear overall memory improvement, although they describe an individual Morris maze quadrant result. The 2023 study tested a chromatographic fraction of aqueous dried-seed extract in scopolamine-amnesic mice and reports differences on maze and hole-board tests. It gives internally inconsistent group and toxicity descriptions. The later experiments use a different model and formulation from the earlier work, with author overlap. Neither tests a ground-seed clinical executive-function outcome. [Original 2021 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8399924/) · [Original 2023 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10005257/) · [Licensed 2023 text](Source-Text/PMC10005257.txt)

## Registered Nigella and vitamin D arms for acute COVID

First posted July 29, 2021. The four-arm comparison describes standard care, Nigella, vitamin D3 and their combination, with proposed enrollment of 25 per group. The publication gives 30 per group and inconsistent daily oil dosing descriptions. The public record shows Unknown status and no posted results. The 2022 linked article provides fatigue counts. [Local factual extract](Source-Text/Trials/2021-NCT04981743-Nigella-COVID.txt) · [Official Egyptian study registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04981743)

## Nigella oil alone and fatigue during acute COVID

In the 2022 open-label randomized four-arm study, fatigue at day four was reported in 12/30 assigned Nigella plus standard care and 25/30 on standard care alone. The corresponding baseline counts were 28/30 and 30/30. Table 5 reports adjusted OR 0.15 (95% CI 0.04–0.48). The Baraka softgel contains seed oil. The article's stated total conflicts with its frequency and capsule arithmetic, so the exact administered amount remains uncertain. The study measured symptom presence during acute infection. It did not measure Parkinson fatigue severity or assign ground seed. ## Ground Nigella seed arms and day-ten fatigue

Table 2 of the 2022 Saudi randomized trial reports day-ten general fatigue in 1/17 placebo participants and 1/25, 2/37 and 3/26 across the ground-seed groups. The study reports no clear between-group symptom finding. Attrition changes the denominators and the placebo baseline fatigue cell is internally inconsistent. The protocol tested capsules in acute COVID and measured fatigue presence. This is separate from the Egyptian oil trial. [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2022-Ground-Nigella-COVID-Fatigue-Table-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Saudi randomized trial, original Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9517329/)

## Anise oil in a combined premenstrual insomnia comparison

First published November 29, 2024, assigned to a 2026 issue. In a randomized PMS trial 32 combination and 35 control participants completed follow-up. The softgel contained anise, evening primrose and clary sage oils, with sunflower oil control. Table 5 means for insomnia from baseline across two cycles were 5.19, 3.56, 2.44 versus 5.46, 2.86, 2.23. The report lists between-group p=.810, no significant group-by-time interaction and a time effect p<.001. No treatment component can be isolated. The oil blend and population differ from ground anise seed and Parkinson insomnia. [Publisher primary report and Table 5](https://www.eurekaselect.com/article/144661) · [Local text](Source-Text/2024-Simbar-Anise-Oil-Insomnia-Table-Extract.txt)

## Adverse events in the aniseed powder trial

The acute-COVID trial randomized 45 adults to ground aniseed and rock sugar and 180 to placebo, with open-ended adverse-event collection. The authors report two anise and three placebo participants with mild or moderate adverse events in the abstract. The results list throat itching and thirst in the anise group and cough and cramps in the placebo group. The symptom counts are not unique participant counts. The protocol did not specify falls and the clinical report supplies no fall-frequency result. [Original trial Methods and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10824915/) · [Earlier registration](Source-Text/Trials/IRCT20120506009651N3.txt)
