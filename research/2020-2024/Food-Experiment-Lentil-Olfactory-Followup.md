# Lentils, dietary patterns and olfactory outcomes

## Dietary pattern and tested hyposmia in community cohorts

The 2020 Molsberry study selected participants without diagnosed Parkinson disease. Repeated food-frequency questionnaires contributed to dietary indices. The alternate Mediterranean score includes legumes as one component. In a subset, the investigators assessed hyposmia using a 12-item Brief Smell Identification Test and a cohort reference threshold. Overall diet-index adherence did not show an association with hyposmia. The published comparison does not identify lentils or boiling, and it concerns a community cohort without diagnosed Parkinson disease at selection. ## Lentil odor and persistent smell problems after COVID-19

A 2022 exploratory survey included 47 participants with persistent post-COVID taste or smell problems. Eleven described a rubber-like or elastic odor in legumes, pulses and brown bread as a group. One report specifically mentions beans, peas and lentils, alone or in soup. The study records altered odor perception of foods. It does not identify confirmed boiling, an intervention assigning lentil consumption, an objective smell score or a Parkinson cohort.
