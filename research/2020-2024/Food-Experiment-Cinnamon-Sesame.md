# Cinnamon and sesame oil evidence

## Cinnamon patch and overactive bladder

Chen and colleagues published October 12, 2020. Sixty-six outpatients with overactive bladder were randomized to a topical cinnamon patch or placebo patch. In Table 2, the end-of-treatment Overactive Bladder Symptom Score was 6.33 versus 9.73, and its urgency-frequency item was 2.82 versus 4.18 (both p < .001). The ordinary daytime voiding frequency item was inconclusive (0.97 versus 1.00, p = .79). On the separate urgency severity scale the active arm changed from 2.67 to 1.64. The six-week report includes a two-week treatment assessment and a four-week follow-up. The experiment used topical patches and supplies no estimate for ingested ground cinnamon in Parkinson disease. [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phymed.2020.153380) · [Publisher article tables in a public copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/538233481/chen2021) · [Earlier registration](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Cinnamon-Sesame.md#registration-of-a-patch-trial-for-overactive-bladder)

## Registration of a cinnamon-containing formula for oral frailty

UMIN first disclosed UMIN000039882 on March 19, 2020, as shown in the registry history. This is a single-group observational oral frailty study of Ninjin’yoeito. The later report identifies the same registry and gives an earlier ethics approval date. The registry date and report date reflect separate public releases. [Official registration](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000045481) · [Registry history](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_his_list.cgi?recptno=R000045481) · [Dated study report](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Cinnamon-Sesame.md#cinnamon-containing-formula-and-oral-function)

## Cinnamon powder capsules and ambulatory pressure in mild hypertension

The 2021 pilot randomized 40 adults with stage 1 hypertension to dried *Cinnamomum zeylanicum* powder capsules or lactose placebo. At day 90 both groups had mean 24-hour systolic pressure of about 130 mm Hg. Table 2 reports no clear between-group difference for any day-90 pressure variable. Some within-group tests were statistically significant. The trial gives no standing-pressure challenge or lightheadedness score. [Original article, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7885002/)

## Oral cinnamon powder and void counts in experimental cystitis

Chen and colleagues reported the mouse follow-up in December 2021. Cyclophosphamide-treated controls had 62 ± 6 voids over 24 hours compared with 10 ± 1 in unexposed controls. Oral *Cinnamomum cassia* bark powder and two isolated cinnamaldehyde levels lowered the abnormal frequency and increased urine volume per void. The highest isolated-compound group and solifenacin did not give the same clear comparisons. The assay counts voids under acute inflammatory bladder injury. It gives no measure of perceived urgency or Parkinson-related bladder dysfunction. [Local CC BY report, Results and Figure 2](Source-Text/PMC8716214.txt#L124)

## Oral cinnamon and bladder sensation in a second animal model

A February 2023 preprint and March 2023 journal article describe a separate study in female rats. Oral true cinnamon reduced visceromotor responses to distension after acute cystitis and modestly increased them in uninflamed animals. Isolated cinnamaldehyde delivered into the bladder lowered the micturition volume threshold in uninflamed animals. The studies distinguish bladder nociception, volume threshold, void counts and perceived urgency. [Preprint](https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202302.0057.v1) · [Original journal figures](https://doi.org/10.3390/nutraceuticals3010012)

## Sesame oil preparation in a fatty liver trial family

The original symptom abstract concludes that anxiety decreased compared with sunflower oil. The p value is reported separately and cannot be assigned to a between-group comparison from the abstract alone. The 2025 report from the registered trial confirms that participants used the oils unheated. [Contrast and cohort details](Food-Experiment-Sesame-Oil-Anxiety.md#refined-sesame-and-sunflower-oil-anxiety-report)

The Iranian registry recorded IRCT20140208016529N6 on December 12, 2020. Two 2022 reports and a 2023 fatigue and mood paper share investigators, group sizes, intervention, diet and trial setting. The 2022 blood-pressure paper identifies both oils as refined and odorless. The 2023 publication reports favorable fatigue, anxiety and depression results for sesame oil compared with sunflower oil during calorie restriction, with p = .002, .011 and .013 in its summary. Stress was inconclusive. The accessible registry lists liver and metabolic outcomes. The 2023 arm means and whether those symptom outcomes were prospectively specified remain open. The cohort had fatty liver disease and the methods describe refined sesame oil. ## Sesame oil and mood in animal comparisons

Khan and colleagues compared sesame oil, shark fish oil and their combination using laboratory behavior tests. The rat study measured forced-swim behavior and locomotor tasks, with no Parkinson modeling or clinical depression outcome. Bristy and colleagues reported that sesame oil changed climbing time in a separate small experiment. Their reported forced-swim immobility contrast against the active control was inconclusive (p = .22). [Primary fish and sesame comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32276892/) · [Bristy et al., Table 3](https://www.heraldopenaccess.us/openaccess/therapeutic-use-of-vegetable-oils-as-functional-food-in-alleviation-of-experimental-depression)

## Sesame oil carrier and symptom outcomes in a Parkinson trial

The ClinicalTrials.gov results for NCT03582137 report four falls in 31 cannabinoid recipients and three in 30 placebo recipients. Both oral formulations used sesame oil. The mean changes on the depression scale were −1.73 and −2.04, and each reported interval included zero. The registry gives aggregate motor and neuropsychiatric scores. The published table and registry do not identify visual hallucination frequency, a separate rigidity comparison, or daily OFF time. These data compare cannabinoids with a vehicle held across arms. The cooking grade of the oil is unspecified. [Official posted results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03582137?tab=results) · [Original trial, Tables 2–3](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.29768) · [Existing trial family note](Food-Experiment-Oil-Followup.md#sesame-oil-carrier-in-a-randomized-cannabinoid-trial)

## Isolated sesamol and whole sesame oil in manganese models

The 2022 rat experiment found shorter catalepsy duration after isolated sesamol in grid and bar tests (46.5% and 62.5% lower, respectively, against the manganese model group). A 2024 study by overlapping authors tested whole sesame oil and reported improved locomotion and rod performance in a separate manganese exposure experiment. Catalepsy and rod retention provide distinct laboratory outcomes, and neither test measures human dystonic posture or clinical rigidity after toasted or cold-pressed oil. [2022 local CC BY report, Figure 4](Source-Text/PMC9785377.txt#L111) · [2024 author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38548024/)

## Sesame oil as a rotigotine vehicle in rats

Kim and colleagues compared subcutaneous rotigotine suspensions in sesame oil or an aqueous formulation in a 2022 rat pharmacokinetic study. The sesame-oil suspension had a higher peak (103.43 versus 61.5 ng/mL) and faster decline in plasma. Total exposure was inconclusive (104.3 versus 95.1 ng·day/mL). The study supplies no oral toasted sesame intervention or daily OFF-time measurement. [Local CC BY report, Figure 5 and Table 4](Source-Text/PMC9784203.txt#L169)

## Sesame oil swishing and reported speech difficulty

First published October 29, 2021. The authors describe 62 people with oral submucous fibrosis equally randomized to sesame oil swishing or placebo capsules and no dropout. Table 3 lists speech difficulty present/absent/reduced as 29/3/0 versus 24/2/0 at baseline and 11/14/7 versus 13/7/8 at day 90. The implied counts differ from the stated 31 per arm and the control total changes during follow-up. A reliable between-arm effect cannot be calculated from these data. It is subjective speech difficulty, and the paper does not confirm ingestion, toasted oil or Parkinson disease. [Original Methods and Table 3](https://www.scielo.br/j/pboci/a/fkJKytSzks83kFk4zNgmRDb/?lang=en) · [Local text](Source-Text/2021-Shrivastava-Sesame-Speech-Table-Extract.txt)

## Sesame oil used to measure odor identification

Dong and colleagues substituted sesame oil for coffee in a 16-item smell test of rural Chinese older adults. They attribute sesame and soy sauce replacements to Shu and Yuan. The 2007 first-online Shu and Yuan abstract confirms five modified odors but names no sesame item. The experiment assessed recognition of odor delivered to the nose and did not measure a change from eating the oil. [Original olfactory test Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8072018/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2021-Dong-Sesame-Odor-Method-Extract.txt)
Published June 25, 2024. Among 36 participants with Parkinson disease and seven with multiple system atrophy, correct identification of the smelled sesame oil item was 44.4% and 100%, respectively (p=.010). Group totals imply 16/36 and 7/7 if there was no missing item data. The oil was a diagnostic stimulus, with no oral intake, cold-press specification or treatment effect. [Original primary report and Figure 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11219208/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2024-Liu-Sesame-Odor-Figure2-Extract.txt)
