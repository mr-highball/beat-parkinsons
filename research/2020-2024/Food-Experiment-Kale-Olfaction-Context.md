# Kale and olfactory rehabilitation

## Optional kale in post-COVID sensory rehabilitation

Kumari and colleagues published on July 12, 2023. Fifteen people entered an unblinded, multisensory post-COVID smell and taste rehabilitation program with camera feedback; 11 completed objective follow-up. Kale appears among optional bitter foods. The report gives no count or preparation for kale exposure and no kale-specific arm. Olfactory identification and subjective measures improved during the whole program. The design leaves individual ingredient effects unmeasured and involved post-COVID cases. ## Roasted kale aroma in a laboratory sensory test

The article first appeared online August 10, 2021. The investigators prepared a tea material using repeated roasting and drying of kale leaves. Chemical profiling identified 25 aroma compounds in raw kale and 37 in the roasted tea material. Two trained assessors sniffed separated compounds at a gas chromatography port. The investigators listed 18 aroma-active compounds, including two dimethylpyrazines with roasted notes and a dilution factor of 256. The measurements characterize aroma of the prepared product. The assessors were not studied for a change in clinical smell loss after eating kale. ## Maternal kale powder and fetal facial reactions

First online September 21, 2022. The experiment analyzed 180 ultrasound scans from 99 fetuses. Maternal capsule exposures used 400 mg powdered kale (34 fetuses) or carrot (35) and an unexposed comparison (30). The investigators observed more cry-face patterns with kale flavor and more laughter-face patterns with carrot flavor. The outcome is fetal facial movement. Roasting was not specified and no patient olfaction or Parkinson outcome was measured.
