# Bell pepper preparation and symptom outcomes

## Paprika listed as a vegetable in a Parkinson diet study

Hegelmaier et al. published their Parkinson dietary pilot on February 6, 2020. All 16 intervention participants received the same 14-day ovo-lacto vegetarian diet, with 10 also receiving bowel cleansing. Table 3 lists paprika under vegetables and separately lists chili and pepper among spices. In this German menu, the placement supports identifying paprika as vegetable sweet pepper. There is no cultivar, preparation or separate consumption measure. [Methods and Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7072275/) · [Local licensed full text](Source-Text/PMC7072275.txt#L285)

Total UPDRS III improved across the intervention participants (p=.0004). The authors report within-group comparisons of p=.033 for the diet-only six and p=.005 for the ten also receiving bowel cleansing. These are changes after a whole dietary pattern, with no separate pepper contrast. The motor examination included gait, finger taps and hand movements. Figure S2 shows individual **total** changes, without separately published gait or dexterity scores. The study does not measure handwriting size, apathy or daytime sleepiness as separate outcomes. [Figure 4 and Results](https://doi.org/10.3390/cells9020376) · [Primary article supplements](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4409/9/2/376/s1)

## Nonpungent pepper extract and physical activity in older adults

Yokoyama et al. published this double-blind parallel trial on January 14, 2020. Eighty adults aged 50 or older were enrolled, 75 completed and 69 formed the main analysis sample. The activity analysis used the 47 participants with adequate activity-meter data, 26 on capsinoids and 21 on placebo. The trial used 9 mg per day of purified capsinoids from nonpungent CH-19 Sweet fruit. Each capsule contained rapeseed oil in both arms. The investigators screened out people with motor disorders or walking aids. The 10 m walking test and grip, standing, jumping and functional reach were **baseline eligibility measurements** used to exclude those with high fitness. The Fitness Age Score cutoff was −0.071 for participants over 60 and 2.01 for those in their 50s. The published follow-up gave no walking-time or handgrip comparison. [Primary full paper, sections 2.1.2–2.1.5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7019503/) · [Local licensed full text](Source-Text/2020-Yokoyama-CH19-Sweet-Activity.txt) · [First registry posting](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Bell-Pepper-Outcomes.md#nonpungent-capsicum-activity-trial-registration)

- In the 47-person activity analysis, light-to-moderate activity changed from 103.0 ± 28.2 to 108.2 ± 28.3 MET-hours/week with capsinoids and from 104.6 ± 19.8 to 115.2 ± 23.6 with placebo. The overall light-to-moderate activity comparison reported no significant group difference. Estimated energy expenditure changed from 594.6 ± 167.1 to 619.9 ± 155.5 kcal/day with capsinoids and from 565.0 ± 115.6 to 653.3 ± 166.3 with placebo. The change was larger with placebo at weeks 8 (p=.010) and 12 (p<.001). [Primary Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7019503/)
- In the inactive subgroup defined by the 468.7 min/day median sedentary time, light-to-moderate activity changed from 84.5 ± 17.2 to 99.2 ± 24.9 MET-hours/week (13 capsinoids participants) and 99.7 ± 23.3 to 103.8 ± 21.9 (11 placebo participants). The main text gives p=.050 for the larger change with capsinoids at week 12, and Table 4 marks the same result with p<.05. The subgroup's week-12 activity energy expenditure favored capsinoids (p<.001), in the opposite direction to the full activity sample. Daily activity volume and energy expenditure do not measure walking slowness. [Primary Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7019503/)
- The 69-person mood analysis used Japanese POMS2, including fatigue-inertia and vigor-activity. Other than a week-six anger-hostility result, the authors report no significant between-group comparisons. The article supplies no apathy-scale result. Vigor ratings do not provide a direct apathy estimate. [Primary section 3.1.4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7019503/)
