# Additional date palm and pain research, 2020–2024

- [2024 review of preparation and cohort lineage for labor pain](Source-Text/2024-PMC10759543-Date-Syrup-Labor-Pain-Lineage.txt). Two independent favorable syrup trials and one fresh-fruit study with pooled pain categories account for the review’s four pain publications.

## Public Belgian Parkinson dietary files

Baert and colleagues made three workbooks public on July 13, 2020, before the July 21 article. The study collected two nonconsecutive days of dietary records. The released dietary sheet contains 34 participant rows of energy and nutrient totals. It omits the original food records. The questionnaire and medication workbooks concern dietary habits, demographics, medication and food–drug knowledge. The files do not provide individual dates eaten or Parkinson’s pain or apathy severity. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7385303/) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC7385303.txt) · [Dataset version 1](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.12646451.v1)

## Sugar intake and chronic pain in an Australian Parkinson cohort

Palavra and colleagues studied 103 people with Parkinson’s and 81 controls. They used a 145-item food frequency questionnaire and measured chronic pain with a visual analogue scale. Within the Parkinson’s group, those reporting chronic pain consumed a mean of 164.0 g/day total sugar versus 124.6 g/day among those without chronic pain (adjusted p = .040 for the association). The main text describes pain over the previous year, while Table 2 labels the same 75 Parkinson’s participants as reporting pain over the previous three months. The retrieved supplementary table gives nutrient associations and no individual dried-date exposure or date–pain estimate. The investigators offer raw data through author access. [Primary article and supplement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8138322/) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC8138322.txt#L323)

## Individual date intake and premenstrual symptom domains

AlQuaiz and colleagues surveyed 1,831 women in Riyadh. Table 3 lists 1,288 frequent consumers of dates and 543 infrequent consumers, without specifying drying stage. Table 5 reports associations for abdominal, back or joint pain, severe headache and other symptom domains. The only published date-specific estimate concerns appetite, weight gain or bloating (adjusted OR 0.69, 95% CI 0.49–0.98). The pain models give no date-specific estimate. [Primary report, Tables 3–5](https://www.dovepress.com/getfile.php?fileID=86198)

## Pooled dried fruit and pain in genetic analyses

Huang and Xie’s March 23, 2023 analysis used the UK Biobank dried-fruit genetic instrument `ukb-b-16576` and estimated lower odds of low back pain in FinnGen (IVW OR 0.435, 95% CI 0.287–0.659). Zhou and colleagues’ August 24 analysis used the UK Biobank dried-fruit exposure and reported an inverse association with the number of chronic pain sites (IVW β −0.22, SE 0.07, adjusted p = .008). The exposure is a broad fruit group. The outcomes concern pain diagnosis or number of affected sites in general populations, without a Parkinson’s pain-intensity estimate. [Huang and Xie primary report and data IDs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10076586/) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC10076586.txt) · [Zhou et al. primary report, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10490345/) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC10490345.txt) · [UK Biobank exposure field](https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/ukb/field.cgi?id=1319)

## Dates and joint symptoms in a Moroccan rheumatology survey

Takhrifa and colleagues asked 168 patients with rheumatoid arthritis or spondyloarthritis whether individual foods improved or aggravated joint symptoms. The instrument distinguishes dates from other dried fruit. Neither appears among the ten foods plotted in Figure 1. The paper gives an overall pain VAS and the patient-reported effects of diet in aggregate, without a dates-specific count, VAS comparison, or fruit preparation. The omitted figure categories leave the date direction unresolved. [Primary paper and questionnaire](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10924651/) · [Figure 1](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC10924651.1/cureus-0016-00000053868-i01.jpg) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC10924651.txt#L107)

## Pooled dried fruit and Parkinson susceptibility

Zeng and colleagues’ May 22, 2024 Mendelian-randomization analysis estimated higher Parkinson’s disease odds for genetically proxied dried-fruit intake (IVW OR 2.019, 95% CI 1.052–3.875). The MR-Egger and weighted median intervals both span the null. Shi and colleagues’ September 6, 2024 UK Biobank analysis reported IVW OR 1.69 (95% CI 1.08–2.65) in Table 2. Its discussion describes this as a risk association, while the results explicitly identify household income as robust after false discovery rate correction. These analyses reuse related UK Biobank summary-data resources. They estimate disease susceptibility and provide no separate food-species or pain and apathy symptom results. [Zeng primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11151297/) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC11151297.txt#L123) · [Shi primary report, Table 2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-024-00780-5.pdf) · [Later analyses](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Date-Pain-Followup.md#dried-fruit-phenotypes-in-later-parkinson-genetic-analyses)

## Date palm pollen and exercise-related soreness markers

IRCT registered IRCT20190103042219N2 on May 2, 2020. Its design summary describes a blinded three-arm comparison in 36 female athletes using two doses of date-palm pollen and starch placebo before eccentric exercise. Abdollahi and colleagues’ January 1, 2021 report matches the investigator, population, allocation, doses, and exercise. It reports no clear effect on the measured blood, thigh circumference, leg extension, and jumping outcomes. The registered outcomes and accessible article abstract give no direct pain rating. The registry’s detailed placebo arm states 200 mg of starch, while its summary and the published abstract give 100 mg. This is a separate comparison from the date-seed powder trial that used a direct soreness scale. [Primary registration](https://irct.ir/trial/47211) · [Primary report, DOI 10.1007/s11332-020-00723-6](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11332-020-00723-6)

## Barhi date feeding and nutrient intake

Al-Sayyed and colleagues’ 2020 paper describes nutrient intake during the 36-person Barhi date comparison introduced at the 2018 conference. The paper analyzes dietary intake, without estimating pain severity. The 2025 publication cites both earlier reports. [2018 conference report](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Date-Pain-Followup.md#whole-barhi-dates-and-menstrual-characteristics-conference-report) · [2020 primary report, DOI 10.2174/1573401316666200326110555](https://www.benthamscience.com/article/105489) · [2025 symptom report](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Date-Pain-Followup.md#whole-date-feeding-and-premenstrual-symptoms)

## Whole Mazafati dates and dysmenorrhea registration

IRCT retrospectively registered IRCT20180708040381N1 on February 5, 2022. It gives 2011 as the conduct year. The protocol summary describes 26 students and a comparison of consecutive menstrual cycles with and without whole Mazafati dates. The structured target enrollment is 43, and the listed intervention assigns ten dates during the second cycle. It specifies a visual analogue pain measure and analyst masking. The record marks recruitment complete. Its Persian data-sharing section states that study documents would be available after publication and identifies the investigator responsible for access. A public outcome report and the exact fruit drying stage remain unverified. [English registration](https://irct.ir/trial/61664) · [Persian registration](https://fa.irct.ir/trial/61664)

## Date-containing drinks and menstrual pain

Zeny Fatmawati and colleagues reported lower dysmenorrhea scores after a drink containing dates, lemongrass, and coconut water in 60 adolescents from one school in September 2021. Both reports describe the same school, enrollment count, lead authors, intervention, and pain scales. Treat them as reports of one cohort. The September report gives pre/post coded pain means of 2.70 and 1.43 (p < .001). The December report gives a Wilcoxon comparison. There was no concurrent control. [September 2021 primary report](https://thejmch.com/index.php/thejmch/article/view/682) · [December 2021 primary article record and abstract](https://garuda.kemdiktisaintek.go.id/documents/detail/2395226)

Ery Fatmawati and Muliawati separately reported a before-and-after comparison in 20 students who drank a ginger, lemongrass, and date mixture. Table 2 gives 14 of 20 with mild pain and three with no pain after the drink. At baseline, 14 of 20 had moderate or severe pain. The article page dates public release to January 10, 2024 and the PDF gives a December 2023 issue date. Both studies use mixtures and measure a fluctuating symptom without a control group. They provide indirect context for `0502` and, for the ginger-containing mixture, `0725`. [Primary article and publication date](https://jurnalmadanimedika.abdimasmadani.ac.id/JMM/article/view/356) · [Primary PDF, Table 2](https://jurnalmadanimedika.ac.id/JMM/article/download/356/198/)

## Date palm pollen and fatigue in multiple sclerosis registration

IRCT registered IRCT20230706058689N1 on December 29, 2023. The record describes a six-week double-blind pollen capsule versus starch placebo comparison with planned enrollment of 50 people with multiple sclerosis and a Fatigue Severity Scale outcome. The summary reverses the group labels used in the detailed arm descriptions. The registry marks recruitment complete and no outcome report was located. The pollen preparation and neurological condition differ from Parkinson’s, and fatigue is a distinct outcome from apathy. [Primary registration](https://irct.ir/trial/74234)

## Registered date seed powder and exercise study

IRCT prospectively registered IRCT20150205020965N9 on February 19, 2021, with a target sample of 36. It describes allocation stratified by sex and aerobic fitness and use of coded date seed powder and placebo. The current English record, last updated June 18, 2022, lists oxidative, inflammatory, and exercise outcomes. Its outcome sections have no separate muscle soreness or visual analogue pain measure. The published protocol includes muscle pain among the primary measures. [Primary registry record](https://irct.ir/trial/53070) · [Published protocol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35769196/)

## Date seed powder and exercise-related muscle soreness

Moslemi and colleagues randomized 36 healthy recreational runners to date seed powder or placebo for two weeks alongside high-intensity interval training. Thirty-four completed the study, 17 per group. Table 4 reports mean 0–10 visual analogue scale pain scores of 6.35 versus 6.41 at baseline, 5.06 versus 5.82 immediately after the intervention (p = 0.070), and 3.35 versus 5.00 at 24 hours (p < 0.001). The group-by-time interaction was also p < 0.001. Pain was one of several primary measures, and the sample size was calculated for malondialdehyde. The 2022 protocol, July 2022 outcome paper, and 2023 biomarker report share registration IRCT20150205020965N9. They describe one trial of powdered seeds and acute exercise soreness in healthy participants. [Primary outcome paper, Table 4, PMID 35832028](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114522002124) · [Protocol, PMID 35769196](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35769196/) · [Biomarker companion, PMID 36881179](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36881179/)

## Fruit extract fractions in chemical pain models

Yunusa and colleagues compared methanol and n-hexane fruit extracts in Wistar rats using acetic acid writhing and formalin pain behavior. Each model had three animals per arm. The authors reported lower pain behaviors with both extracts. Their comparison of the strongest responses used different doses for the two preparations. The 2021 issue was published online January 1, 2022. The small groups, solvent extracts, and induced responses limit applicability to [0502](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0502.txt). [Primary article, DOI 10.4314/gjs.v62i2.1](https://www.ajol.info/index.php/gjs/article/view/222330)

Albermani and colleagues compared an oral methanolic fruit extract with saline and meloxicam in a separate Wistar rat experiment. The 2023 report describes fewer observable acetic acid-induced contractions and less formalin-related paw behavior at higher doses. The published description has sparse group-level outcome and design details. This is another acute animal experiment using a solvent extract. [Authors’ copy of the primary report, *Online Journal of Veterinary Research* 27(7), 447–450](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374005760_Analgesic_and_anti-inflammatory_effects_of_Phoenix_dactylifera_in_Wistar_rats)

## Ajwa seed fractions and writhing

Begum and colleagues tested fractions from two sources of Ajwa seeds in an acetic acid writhing model. Figures 2–3 and the discussion report fewer writhes after selected fractions. The highest reported inhibition for the n-hexane fractions was 53.7% and 62.9% for the two seed sources, versus 66.67% for aspirin. The paper’s broad abstract claim about surpassing standard medications is stronger than those comparisons. Seed fractions and evoked animal behavior leave the clinical dried-fruit hypothesis unresolved. [Primary article, Figures 2–3 and discussion, DOI 10.30848/PJB2024-3(24)](https://www.pakbs.org/pjbot/papers/1710930725.pdf)
