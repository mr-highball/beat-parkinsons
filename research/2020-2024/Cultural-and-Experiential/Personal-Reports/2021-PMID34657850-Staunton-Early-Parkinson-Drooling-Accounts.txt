PMID: 34657850
PMCID: PMC8842769

Source kind: Participant experiences gathered through a qualitative clinical study
Publication date: 2021-10-13
Date basis: publisher first-online date. The archive reports a 2022 issue date.
Source locator: Staunton and colleagues, concept-elicitation interviews, regional saturation analysis, Figure 4 on print page 146 and Supplementary Table 1.
Community/context: Thirty-five people with early Parkinson disease were interviewed, 19 in the United States and 16 in Europe.
Reported direction: Participants reported experiencing excess saliva or drooling. The reports do not attribute improvement or worsening to a food.
Authenticity: Original publication, original figure and supplemental table. The summary reproduces no individual identifying details.
Efficacy evidence: Qualitative accounts of symptom presence and burden. The study supplies no assigned intervention, symptom-change estimate or raw fig comparison.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20, original body, figure and Supplementary Table 1.

Figure 4 lists 13 of 35 interviewees (37%) who reported excessive saliva or drooling. Seven raised it spontaneously and six after prompting. In the European subgroup, the authors say no one volunteered the concept without prompting. The study separately considered social-media material and expert input. The 37% refers to the interviewed sample, and its denominator does not include posts or experts. The final conceptual model places the symptom in its motor group. That placement reflects the authors' classification and does not establish mechanism or population prevalence.
