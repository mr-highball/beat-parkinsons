Title: Saliva, dribbling and related symptoms described in early Parkinson interviews
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9338202/
PMID: 35778541
PMCID: PMC9338202
DOI: 10.1007/s40120-022-00375-3
Source kind: Participant experiences gathered through a qualitative clinical study
Publication date: 2022-07-01
Date basis: electronic publication in original JATS
Source locator: Morel and colleagues, Supplementary Material 1, PDF pages 6 and 24.
Community/context: Fifty people with early Parkinson disease and nine relatives were interviewed in a mixed-methods study.
Reported direction: Participants described dribbling, saliva buildup during speech and differences in saliva consistency at different times of day.
Authenticity: Traceable original article and speaker accounts in the primary supplement. No individual identifying details or verbatim interview excerpts are reproduced.
Efficacy evidence: Subjective symptom descriptions. The study supplies no dietary assignment, controlled comparison or raw-fig outcome.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20, original article and original supplement.

The saliva-control examples include dribbling and saliva pooling during speech. A separate oral-dryness account describes a dry mouth at night and thick saliva on waking. The same person reported a runny nose around meals and distinguished it from saliva. The mealtime report is a related nasal symptom, with no demonstrated food-linked change in drooling.
