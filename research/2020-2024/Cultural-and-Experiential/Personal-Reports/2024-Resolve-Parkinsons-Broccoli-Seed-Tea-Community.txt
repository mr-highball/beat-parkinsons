Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://doi.org/10.18103/mra.v12i1.4955
Source kind: Patient-led personal self-experimentation report and community summary (DOI:10.18103/mra.v12i1.4955; cross-linked to research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2024-Wright-Broccoli-Seed-Tea.txt)
Publication date: 2024-01-31
Date basis: Publication date January 31, 2024 in Medical Research Archives (Wright 2024, DOI 10.18103/mra.v12i1.4955; synthesizing three years of ongoing patient-led community observations documented at resolveparkinsons.org and archived locally at research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2024-Wright-Broccoli-Seed-Tea.txt).
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Mixed/divergent by symptom domain (frequent patient self-reports of non-motor relief for constipation, fatigue, urinary urgency, mood/apathy, brain fog, and sleep; virtually no motor improvement for bradykinesia, rigidity, or tremor; transient worsening of urinary urgency or tremor reported when exceeding weekly doses).
Authenticity: Verified open-access patient-led report by Albert F. Wright (Resolve Parkinson's) summarizing anonymized weekly symptom logs of 17 volunteers with Parkinson disease; privacy-safe summary omitting individual handles or personal details.
Efficacy evidence: Low (unblinded, uncontrolled self-selected n=1 self-experiments using crushed broccoli seeds co-administered with 10-15% white mustard seed powder; does not establish clinical efficacy of culinary broccoli florets).

Within online Parkinson disease communities (including Resolve Parkinson's and patient forums), self-experimenters frequently discuss broccoli sprouts and freshly brewed Broccoli Seed Tea (BST) combining crushed broccoli seeds with white mustard seed powder (as exogenous myrosinase) to activate Nrf2. In the published 17-volunteer community log compilation (Wright 2024, DOI 10.18103/mra.v12i1.4955), participants reported frequent non-motor attenuation (fatigue 16/16, urinary urgency 13/14, constipation 9/11, apathy/mood 8/10, brain fog 6/8, sleep impairment 6/8) returning 2-4 weeks after stopping, alongside near-total absence of motor improvement (bradykinesia 0/13, rigidity 0/13, tremor 1/10) and occasional transient worsening of urinary urgency or tremor at higher doses. Community commenters also note digestive upset when starting high doses and emphasize that BST is an unvalidated adjunct, not a replacement for levodopa.
