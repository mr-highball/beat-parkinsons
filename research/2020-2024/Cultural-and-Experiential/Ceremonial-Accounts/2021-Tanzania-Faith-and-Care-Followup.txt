Title: Faith, care and an unnamed herbal wash in rural Tanzania
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Qualitative interviews with people with Parkinson disease and caregivers
Source: Fothergill-Misbah et al., Journal of Neurodegenerative Disorders 4(1):122–128. DOI: 10.36959/459/606. https://scholars.direct/Articles/neurodegenerative-disorders/jnd-4-018.pdf
Publication date: 2021-02-15
Date basis: Online publication date printed on the publisher's original
Reviewed: 2026-11-17
Existing accession: research/2010-2014/Cultural-and-Experiential/Ceremonial-Accounts/2011-Tanzania-Healing-and-Prayer-Accounts.txt

Context: Interviews in Hai District represented nine people with Parkinson disease. Three spoke directly and six were represented by caregivers. The paper explicitly follows part of the 2011 report's cohort and also includes more recently diagnosed people. The exact overlap of interviewees is unspecified.

Exposure: All but one participant described visiting Christian or Muslim faith healers. Participants described prayers alongside medication. One reported washing with unspecified herbal leaves.
Reported direction: Prayer and support were described as helpful for coping. The herbal wash was described as unsuccessful for an unspecified outcome. Some participants described improvement after pharmaceutical treatment.
Authenticity: Seven-page publisher original inspected, especially Methods on p.123 and the healing and follow-up passages on p.125. This is a factual paraphrase of the source, published under its stated Creative Commons Attribution terms.
Efficacy evidence: These qualitative reports describe practices and perceptions. The study supplies no controlled comparison, separately measured rest-tremor change or numerical adverse-event contrast.
Open trail: Plant identity, preparation and intended outcome for the wash remain unspecified. The earlier interviews and this follow-up should be interpreted as overlapping qualitative reports.
