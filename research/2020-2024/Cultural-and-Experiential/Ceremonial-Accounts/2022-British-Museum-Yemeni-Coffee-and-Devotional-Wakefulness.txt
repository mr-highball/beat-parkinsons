Community and context: The museum curator describes reported use by members of the Shadhiliyya Sufi order in Al-Mukha, Yemen, during dhikr remembrance and devotional recitation. The origin story is presented as attributed tradition in the late fourteenth or early fifteenth century.
Exposure and reported symptom: Brewed coffee beans taken orally in the context of an extended devotional practice. The document gives no standardized bean preparation, dose, participant count, clinical diagnosis or fatigue instrument.
Reported direction: Greater wakefulness during devotion is attributed to the beverage. The account describes resistance to sleep rather than a measured change in persistent fatigue.
Efficacy evidence: This is an institutional historical interpretation. The publication provides no comparative observation, first-person account from the historical attendants, measured fatigue outcome or chronology that establishes who first used coffee. It records claims about wakefulness and devotion without a clinical Parkinson inference.
Authenticity: The newsletter and passage are directly inspectable. The specific origin event is reported historically and does not constitute an independent efficacy family with later retellings.
