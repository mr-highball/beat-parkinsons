Title: Contemporary patient and caregiver surveys of aniseed (Pimpinella anisum) use for constipation and digestive complaints in Saudi Arabia, Algeria, and Jordan
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11074969/
Source kind: Cross-sectional ethnobotanical and CAM utilization surveys
Publication date: 2024
Date basis: Peer-reviewed publication dates: Saudi survey 2024 (PMID 38716111 / PMC11074969), Algerian survey 2024 (PMID 38369815 / PMC10878339), Jordanian mothers survey 2021 (PMID 33538314).
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Surveyed patients and caregivers commonly report using aniseed infusions for functional GI complaints including constipation, bloating, colic, and infant sleep; self-reported usage surveys do not measure controlled symptom efficacy.
Authenticity: Verified across three peer-reviewed PubMed/PMC surveys (PMC11074969, PMC10878339, PMID 33538314).
Efficacy evidence: Observational self-reported utilization surveys only; prevalence of folk use does not establish clinical efficacy or Parkinson's safety.

Three peer-reviewed Middle East and North Africa (MENA) utilization surveys document widespread contemporary self-care use of Pimpinella anisum (aniseed) for gastrointestinal symptoms including constipation. In Riyadh, Saudi Arabia (Babelghaith et al. 2024, PMC11074969; n=828 participants), anise was used by 55.5% of functional GI disorder CAM users (n=722), where IBS (29.9%) and constipation (29.8%) were the leading targeted conditions; the authors' safety table explicitly notes clinical dosing data remain limited and allergic GI/skin/respiratory reactions can occur. In Saida, Algeria (Tazi et al. 2024, PMC10878339; n=160 GI patients), Pimpinella anisum was the third most cited medicinal plant (49 citations, 8.5%, UV=0.38). In Jordan (Al-Alami et al. 2021, PMID 33538314; n=520 mothers of infants <3 months), aniseed was used by 70.2% for bloating/colic, 60.8% for infant sleep, and 48.1% for constipation, with authors urging better awareness of infant safety and lack of formal efficacy guidelines.
