Reported direction: Authors suggest potential digestive and swallowing benefits for the adapted diet.
Authenticity: Identifiable journal viewpoint contextualizes familiar Thai foods and meal preparation.
Efficacy evidence: Author expectations and a proposed score. No treated Thai cohort, follow-up results or matched culinary ingredient comparison were reported.

The article proposes an adaptation that would use familiar ingredients
and preparation methods. Its separate descriptions of Korean and
Indo-Mediterranean patterns provide comparisons across settings.
Those descriptions do not document the same culinary practice or an
observed symptom effect in all three settings.

The article places basil and turmeric in a discussion of dyspepsia and
bloating. The primary citation tested adjunctive curcumin in functional
dyspepsia. It included no basil arm, and its reported between-group
dyspepsia result was inconclusive. The supplementary ten-item score
proposes ways to describe diet adherence without measuring efficacy.
