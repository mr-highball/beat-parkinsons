Title: Household Complementary-Medicine Familiarity and Digestive Use Context for Star Anise in Dammam, Saudi Arabia
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9416104/
Source kind: Cross-sectional community knowledge and utilization survey
Publication date: 2022
Date basis: Peer-reviewed survey publication date (Ahmad et al., 2022, J Pharm Bioallied Sci, PMC9416104; field survey conducted March 2020)
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: Generally favorable perceived household usefulness across grouped CAM herbs; no isolated star anise symptom outcome
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed cross-sectional community questionnaire (n=375 completed respondents of 559 invited, 67% response rate)
Efficacy evidence: Descriptive local-knowledge utilization survey only; groups star anise with other household remedies without isolating symptom-specific efficacy or sampling Parkinson's disease

Community/context: General adult population survey in Dammam, Saudi Arabia (March 2020; n=375 respondents, 97.1% Saudi nationals; Ahmad et al., 2022, PMC9416104).
Exposure and reported symptom: Among respondents/families using complementary herbal home remedies, star anise was cited by 84.5% alongside olive oil (95.7%), green tea (89.1%), thyme (85.3%), cinnamon bark (84.8%), and black seed (78.4%). Grouped indications across household herbs included colds (28.3%), cough (14.0%), constipation (11.97%), menstrual discomfort (10.8%), diarrhea (9.87%), and fever (8.62%). About 75% reported observing no side effects.
Evidence and limits: Questionnaire does not disaggregate star anise from other listed herbs for constipation or menstrual pain, does not specify botanical authentication (Illicium verum vs Pimpinella anisum or adulterants), and does not assess Parkinson's disease.
