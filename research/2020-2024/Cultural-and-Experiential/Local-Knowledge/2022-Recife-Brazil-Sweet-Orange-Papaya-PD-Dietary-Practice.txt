Title: Real-world daily dietary consumption of sweet oranges and papaya among outpatients with Parkinson's disease in Recife, Brazil
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://scielo.br/j/rn/a/9YJHynP3vGS5NDRzYvWwQWs/?lang=en
Source kind: Local-Knowledge
Publication date: 2022-10-07
Date basis: Publication date (2022-10-07) of Bezerra et al., Revista de Nutricao 35:e220058 (DOI:10.1590/1678-9865202235e220058).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Descriptive real-world PD dietary baseline: sweet oranges (21.2% men, 24.9-31.3% women) and papaya (19.1-27.6%) ranked among the most frequently consumed daily fruits in Brazilian PD outpatients; no symptom-change contrast.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed open-access Brazilian nutrition survey (CC BY 4.0) of 79 clinically diagnosed PD outpatients (mean age 65.4 y; UPDRS-III 25.3 +/- 12.8).
Efficacy evidence: Descriptive dietary consumption profile documenting cultural staple intake of raw sweet orange (Citrus sinensis) and papaya (Carica papaya) in PD without isolating symptom effects; distinguishes culinary sweet orange from Kampo dried peel (Citrus unshiu / chenpi) and bitter orange (Citrus aurantium).

In a cross-sectional dietary survey of 79 clinically diagnosed Parkinson's disease outpatients in Recife, Brazil (Bezerra et al. 2022, Rev Nutr 35:e220058), raw sweet oranges (Citrus sinensis) and papaya (Carica papaya) were among the top daily fruits consumed alongside bananas and mangoes (oranges 21.2% in men and 24.9-31.3% in women; papaya 27.6% in men and 19.1% in women). Across Mediterranean, Latin American, and Asian culinary cultures, sweet orange is eaten raw as a staple vitamin-C and flavanone-rich fruit, distinct from bitter/Seville orange (Citrus aurantium) and East Asian dried citrus peel preparations (chenpi / Citrus unshiu used in Kampo formulas such as yokukansankachimpihange). While confirming routine dietary feasibility in PD, descriptive intake frequencies do not establish symptom efficacy.
