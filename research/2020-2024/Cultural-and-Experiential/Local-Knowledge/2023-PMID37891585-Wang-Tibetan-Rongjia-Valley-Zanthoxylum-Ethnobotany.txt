Efficacy evidence: Community dietary-salience record only; demonstrates widespread traditional food consumption without testing Parkinson symptom outcomes.

Wang et al. (Journal of Ethnobiology and Ethnomedicine, 2023; PMID:37891585; PMC10612173) conducted structured interviews and voucher collection with 161 Tibetan informants across three villages in the Rongjia River Valley near Mount Everest (2021-2023), recording 2,499 use reports across 50 wild edible plants. Zanthoxylum bungeanum Maxim. ranked second overall in relative frequency of citation (RFC = 0.75), valued by local households as an essential culinary seasoning and traditional healthcare food supporting dietary resilience. Informant consensus confirms long-standing culinary familiarity in Himalayan communities while providing no controlled evidence for Parkinson motor, sensory, or swallowing symptoms.
