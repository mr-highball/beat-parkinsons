Title: Nordic Parkinson patient and caregiver mealtime observations on green peas and haricot verts texture
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijgfs.2022.100528
Source kind: Local-knowledge record
Publication date: 2022
Date basis: Peer-reviewed mixed-methods study publication date (April 18, 2022).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Practical lived experience: haricot verts (green beans) were avoided in severe PD dysphagia due to stringy choking risk; whole green peas were difficult to grip with fine-motor impairment and adapted as purees/dips.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed qualitative focus groups with Scanian Parkinson coalition spouses (n=5) and a person with PD (n=1), contextualized by a Swedish older-adult survey (n=97) (Forsberg et al. 2022, Int J Gastron Food Sci 28:100528).
Efficacy evidence: Experiential mealtime ergonomics and texture-safety guidance only; does not test whether eating peas or green beans alters underlying Parkinson motor or swallowing disease severity.

In a Swedish survey of 97 older adults (age 65-84 y), haricot verts (green beans, 73%) and green peas (72%) ranked among the most liked vegetables. However, in follow-up focus groups with five spouses caring for partners with Parkinson disease and one person diagnosed with Parkinson disease, participants experiencing severe chewing/swallowing difficulties reported avoiding haricot verts because their stringy fibers created choking hazards, while whole green peas were difficult to pick up and transfer to the mouth due to fine-motor impairment and tremor. Participants suggested serving difficult vegetables such as peas as smooth purees or dips for people with advanced motoric eating or swallowing challenges. This documents practical food-texture adaptation rather than causal symptom modification.
