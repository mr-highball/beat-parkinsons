Title: Nunatsiavut Inuit Local Ethnobotanical Knowledge of Wild Blueberry Shrubs as Foundational Food and Medicine
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8888477/
Source kind: Peer-reviewed ethnobotanical community interview study (PMID:35273405, PMC8888477)
Publication date: 2021-12-01
Date basis: Published online 2021 in Economic Botany (PMID:35273405, PMC8888477; documenting contemporary and intergenerational Inuit plant knowledge across Hopedale, Postville, and Rigolet, Labrador).
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Cultural food-and-health heritage (documents high cultural, nutritional, and intergenerational value of wild blueberry shrubs in Subarctic Inuit communities; does not report Parkinson-specific symptom efficacy).
Authenticity: Verified peer-reviewed open-access ethnobotanical study in Economic Botany (Norton, Cuerrier & Hermanutz, 2021, DOI 10.1007/s12231-021-09530-7).
Efficacy evidence: Low (qualitative cultural/ethnobotanical food-ways documentation; does not evaluate clinical efficacy for Parkinson disease symptoms).

Norton, Cuerrier, and Hermanutz (Economic Botany, 2021; PMID:35273405; PMC8888477) interviewed Inuit community members across three communities in Nunatsiavut (Hopedale, Postville, and Rigolet, Labrador, Canada), identifying 66 culturally important plant taxa with strong cross-community consensus (75% of taxa reported in at least two communities). Edible berry-producing shrubs—prominently wild blueberries (Vaccinium spp.)—formed the cornerstone of local food harvesting, intergenerational knowledge sharing, environmental awareness, and traditional food-as-health values. This record provides verified North American Indigenous cultural context for blueberry consumption without claiming clinical efficacy for Parkinson disease symptoms.
