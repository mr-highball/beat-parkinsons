Title: Stigma and healing practices in Kenyan Parkinson disease fieldwork

Context: Fieldwork in 2018–2019 involved 55 people with Parkinson disease, 23 caregivers, 22 healthcare professionals and three herbal healers. The stigma analysis focuses on patient and caregiver experiences. Earlier meeting reports cover related fieldwork. Their differing informant counts cannot be pooled as independent samples.

Exposure: Some accounts described advice to substitute special prayers or other alternative practices for medication. An account also described an unspecified herbal treatment. A separate passage describes concealment of hand tremor in public settings.
Reported direction: Some symptoms were described as worsening in the context of alternative care. The unspecified herb was described as costly and ineffective. The tremor concealment describes stigma.
Authenticity: Methods and alternative-care passages of the archived CC BY 4.0 primary article were inspected. Participant names and codes are omitted.
Efficacy evidence: The report lacks a controlled comparison, a measured separate tremor response and attribution to a specific practice. The medicine changes and wider circumstances are part of the accounts.
Open trail: Botanical identity, preparation, affected symptom and separate effects of each practice remain unresolved.
