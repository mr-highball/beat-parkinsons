Title: Korean and Japanese Everyday Culinary-Functional Perceptions of Perilla frutescens Leaves (Kkaennip and Shiso)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10043449/
Source kind: Local culinary-functional knowledge documented in clinical literature
Publication date: 2023
Date basis: Peer-reviewed publication year (Kim et al. 2023, Front Pharmacol / PMC10043449; corroborated by Japanese odor-perception study PMC13550764, 2025).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Mixed/contextual: everyday culinary consumption is culturally perceived as aromatic, digestion-friendly, and anti-inflammatory for joint comfort, while controlled Japanese sensory testing of ground shiso aroma found pleasantness ratings without statistically significant short-term mood/affect improvement.
Authenticity: Traceable open-access publications (Kim et al. 2023, PMC10043449; Japanese shiso aroma trial jRCT1060240004, PMC13550764) inspected during Round 7 review.
Efficacy evidence: Cultural perception and acute healthy-volunteer sensory ratings do not establish efficacy for Parkinson motor or non-motor symptoms.

Local Culinary & Experiential Context:
In Korean food culture, fresh green Perilla frutescens var. frutescens leaves (kkaennip) are consumed daily as ssam wraps with grilled meats, pickled banchan, and soups, widely regarded in local dietary lore as aiding fat digestion and soothing joint or inflammatory discomfort (background documented in Kim et al. 2023, PMC10043449). In Japan, fresh purple and green shiso (P. frutescens var. crispa / acuta) are prized for their refreshing citrus-mint aroma attributed to perillaldehyde and limonene. However, balanced experimental observation in 41 healthy Japanese women exposed to warm ground shiso leaf steam (jRCT1060240004, PMC13550764) reported higher subjective pleasantness/refreshment without statistically significant improvement in validated negative-affect or mood scores, illustrating the gap between culinary enjoyment and measurable psychiatric efficacy.

Parkinson Community Coverage Note:
Searches of the Parkinson's UK public forum (https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/) identified zero firsthand patient or caregiver threads discussing fresh or dried shiso/perilla leaves for pain, mood, apathy, or motor symptoms.
