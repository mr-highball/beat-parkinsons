Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Local knowledge in an ethnobotanical field survey
Community/context: Taounate province in northern Morocco. Scope is the interviewed population.
Exposure and reported symptom: Figure 5 on journal page 540 lists wild carrot, Daucus carota. Figure 5B allocates its reported uses to digestive conditions (56%) and genitourinary conditions (44%). The text describes leaves and stems (78%) and seed (22%). The visually checked figure labels the 78% portion as stems. The discrepancy leaves leaf use uncertain.
Reported direction: Documented uses. The survey figure does not report measured symptom change.
Authenticity: The publisher PDF and the fieldwork figure were checked directly.
Efficacy evidence: There is no reported cognition or roasted-root practice in the fieldwork. Page 545 separately cites a seed-extract memory study in the literature table. That table labels the row in vitro while the narrative describes rats. It is a secondary description separate from the interview findings.
Related research: research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2024-PMID39275238-CIRCUS-Protocol-and-Baseline.txt. The protocol measured a different population and planned cognitive tasks. Round 1 claim 0325 asks about roasted carrot and executive function in Parkinson disease.
Access and follow-up: Figure text and legend disagree on leaf inclusion. The cited animal result requires its own original assessment for any efficacy use.
