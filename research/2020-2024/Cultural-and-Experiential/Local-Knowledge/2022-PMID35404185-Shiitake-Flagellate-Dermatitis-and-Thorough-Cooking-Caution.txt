Title: Culinary safety knowledge on raw/undercooked shiitake flagellate dermatitis and uncut-cap obstruction in older adults
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35404185/
Source kind: Clinical toxicovigilance and culinary-safety record
Publication date: 2022
Date basis: Publication year of Boels et al. 2022 (Clin Toxicol, PMID:35404185; reporting 2014-2019 French Poison Control Centre surveillance), cross-referenced with Japanese whole-shiitake bowel-obstruction cases (PMC6688735, online 2017) and Gordon et al. 2026 (PMC13445236).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Adverse preparation-dependent safety warnings: raw or briefly cooked shiitake triggers pruritic whiplash-like flagellate dermatitis (59 French PCC cases 2014-2019, dose-dependent duration p=0.007, attributed to heat-labile lentinan), raw/unheated powder provoked eosinophilia/GI symptoms in 5/10 healthy adults (Levy 1998), and swallowing whole uncut tough shiitake caps without dentures caused surgical small-bowel obstruction in older adults (PMC6688735)—all mitigated by fine slicing and thorough cooking.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed national toxicovigilance series (Boels et al. 2022, PMID:35404185; matching 2021 ANSES bulletin) and surgical case reports (PMC6688735), integrated with 2026 thermal-cooking LC-MS/MS evidence (Gordon et al. 2026, PMC13445236).
Efficacy evidence: Preparation safety and food-handling guidance only; establishes why shiitake should always be sliced finely and cooked thoroughly (which denatures dermatitis-linked lentinan while preserving or increasing extractable L-ergothioneine), without establishing Parkinson motor or non-motor efficacy.

Title and source: Culinary preparation safety guidance regarding raw/undercooked shiitake flagellate dermatitis and bolus/bowel impaction, documented via Boels et al., Clin Toxicol 2022 (PMID:35404185), Levy et al., J Allergy Clin Immunol 1998 (PMID:9600497), Spim et al. 2021 (PMID:34375514), and Japanese whole-shiitake obstruction cases (PMC6688735 / Katata 2010, locally archived).
Community/context: As fresh shiitake spread globally into Western salads, rapid stir-fries, and pizza toppings, poison-control networks documented rising outbreaks of "shiitake flagellate dermatitis"—linear, whiplash-shaped erythematous itchy papules appearing 1-7 days (median 48 h) after eating raw or inadequately cooked shiitake (59 confirmed French PCC cases in 2014-2019; median duration 10 days, scaling significantly with portion size, p=0.007; 10% dermatitis also observed in the Spim 2021 shiitake-bar trial). Separately, Japanese hospital series recorded acute choking involving shiitake (Katata 2010) and surgical small-bowel obstruction after edentulous older adults swallowed whole uncut shiitake caps without dentures (PMC6688735).
Practical cooking alignment: Thorough thermal cooking (well-browned pan-sauteing, roasting, or thorough simmering) denatures the heat-labile lentinan implicated in flagellate dermatitis while preserving—and even increasing extractable—L-ergothioneine (Gordon et al. 2026, PMC13445236), and slicing caps finely (removing tough woody stems) mitigates chewing and dysphagia hazards in older adults with Parkinson's disease.
