Title: Shiitake eating difficulty in a Japanese elder-care interview
Source: https://roushikyo-digital.com/archive_previous_pos/news-6359/
Publication date: 2023-10
Date basis: October 2023 issue of Roushikyo Monthly. The publisher's copy was posted online November 10, 2023.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Secondhand personal account in an elder-care interview
Community/context: Japanese elder-care periodical interview with a dietitian about food texture and individual eating difficulty.
Exposure and reported symptom: Shiitake eaten as food. Cooking method, texture and dose are unspecified. An older person reported to have Parkinson disease was said to be unable to eat shiitake, while tolerating fried tofu.
Reported direction: Reported difficulty eating one food. Change in swallowing severity is unspecified.
Authenticity: The Japanese publisher page and issue date were checked directly. The difficulty is a single account relayed by the interviewer’s source. Diagnosis and clinical outcome were not independently evaluated.
Efficacy evidence: No swallowing examination, aspiration measurement, before-and-after change, food-only contrast or general effect estimate.
Related research: research/2010-2014/Source-Text/2010-Kameda-O220-Printed-Choking-Scope.txt and research/2025-2029/Source-Text/2025-PMID40604458-Shiitake-Smoothie-FEES.txt.
Access and follow-up: First interview section on supporting older people’s meals. Determine texture and preparation in other primary reports. The account groups chewing and swallowing challenges and does not identify which prevented eating.
Graph ingredients: shiitake mushroom
Graph symptoms: swallowing difficulty severity

The same interview describes a different person with Parkinson disease who found fried tofu difficult. The two accounts illustrate differences in food tolerance. They do not compare the effect of a shiitake intervention.
