Archaeological and historical-ecology syntheses document pervasive, sustainable Indigenous oyster fisheries persisting for 5,000 to 10,000+ years across North America (including Pacific Northwest Coast Salish and Southeastern Atlantic coastal communities) and Australia, where oysters were actively managed and woven into social, nutritional, and ceremonial traditions (Reeder-Myers et al., Nat Commun 2022, PMC9065011), paralleled by millennia of Mesolithic-Neolithic European flat oyster (Ostrea edulis) harvesting in Denmark (Robson et al., PNAS 2024, PMC11573498). Seasonality reconstruction from the Late Archaic St. Catherines Shell Ring in Georgia (5,000-3,000 BP; Cannarozzi & Kowalewski, PLoS One 2019, PMC6867601) shows harvesting was concentrated from late fall through late spring, avoiding summer months—an empirical seasonal practice congruent with modern microbiology demonstrating peak Vibrio vulnificus and V. parahaemolyticus proliferation in warm summer estuarine waters.
