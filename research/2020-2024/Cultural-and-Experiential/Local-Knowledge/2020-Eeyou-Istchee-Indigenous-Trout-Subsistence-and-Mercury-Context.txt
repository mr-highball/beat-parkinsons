Freshwater salmonids including lake trout, brook trout, and rainbow trout have long served as foundational traditional foods and cultural harvest species for Indigenous communities across boreal Canada (Eeyou Istchee / James Bay Cree; Moriarity et al. 2020, PMID:32947681) and the Great Lakes (30-year Native American fish harvest contaminant monitoring, PMID:37343914). Community nutrition programs emphasize the high protein, selenium, and omega-3 value of traditional fish harvests while issuing species- and size-specific guidance to limit methylmercury and legacy contaminant intake from large, long-lived predatory lake trout compared with smaller trout or farmed rainbow trout. This local-knowledge record contextualizes both culinary heritage and water-body contaminant distinctions without asserting therapeutic or adverse Parkinson symptom effects.
