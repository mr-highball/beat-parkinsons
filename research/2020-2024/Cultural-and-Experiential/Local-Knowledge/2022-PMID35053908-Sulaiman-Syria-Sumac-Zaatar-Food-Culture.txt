Title: Everyday Culinary Use of Rhus coriaria (Sumac) and Za'atar in Coastal Syria (Sulaiman et al., 2022)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35053908/
Source kind: Ethnobotanical field survey
Publication date: 2022
Date basis: Peer-reviewed journal publication year (Foods, 2022; fieldwork March 2020 to March 2021).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Staple culinary condiment in za'atar and everyday food resilience; no symptom-treatment claim.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed field study of 50 informants across 26 villages in Tartus Governorate, Syria (PMC8775266, DOI:10.3390/foods11020177).
Efficacy evidence: Culinary food-heritage context only; highlights everyday spice consumption rather than clinical Parkinson intervention.

Sulaiman et al. (Foods, 2022; PMID:35053908; PMC8775266) interviewed 50 villagers across 26 rural settlements in coastal Syria (2020-2021) documenting 75 wild edible food plants used during food-insecurity pressures. Rhus coriaria was among the four most frequently cited species overall, primarily prepared in everyday za'atar blends and traditional regional dishes rather than isolated medicinal regimens. This record situates ground sumac as a widely consumed Levantine table spice without attributing Parkinson symptom relief to culinary intake.
