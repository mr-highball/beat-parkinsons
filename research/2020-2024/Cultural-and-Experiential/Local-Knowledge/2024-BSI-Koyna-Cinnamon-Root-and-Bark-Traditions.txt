Title: Separate cinnamon root and bark uses in the Koyna Valley ethnobotanical survey
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Local knowledge and ethnobotanical survey
Source ID: BSI-KOYNA2024-CINNAMON
Source: https://bsi.gov.in/uploads/documents/publications/e-publications/english/Ethnobotanical_studies_on_Koyna_Valley%2C_Maharashtra.pdf
Citation: Ethnobotanical Studies on Koyna Valley, Maharashtra. Botanical Survey of India. English edition ISBN 978-81-970647-2-2, printed p. 38.
Publication date: 2024-07
Date basis: Earliest verified publication of this account. The survey does not date the origins of these uses.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26
Ingredients: cinnamon
Symptoms: constipation severity, pain severity
Community and context: The Koyna Valley field compilation identifies true cinnamon, Cinnamomum verum, by its local name Dalchini.
Exposure and symptom: Oral root paste is described as a customary constipation use. The same species entry assigns bark powder to joint pain and associates a bark and ginger combination with other abdominal complaints. It lists culinary bark separately.
Reported direction: The described customary uses seek to ease constipation with root paste and joint pain with bark powder.
Authenticity: English edition page 38 verified against the official Botanical Survey publication. The publisher notes that the e-book was released without editorial review.
Efficacy evidence: The account supplies no participant counts, symptom changes, comparator or diagnosed Parkinson cohort. The source identifies different plant parts for constipation and joint pain.
