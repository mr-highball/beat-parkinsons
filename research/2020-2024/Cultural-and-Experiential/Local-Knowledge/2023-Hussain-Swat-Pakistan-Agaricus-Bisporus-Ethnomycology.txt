Title: Ethnomycological Documentation of Wild Agaricus bisporus Use in Swat, Pakistan
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10137476/
Source kind: Peer-reviewed ethnomycological field study (PMID:37107503, PMC10137476)
Publication date: 2023-04-19
Date basis: Publication year 2023 in Foods (PMID:37107503, PMC10137476; field interviews with 62 local informants in District Swat, Pakistan).
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Favorable traditional food/medicinal recognition (widely cited edible and traditionally valued wild mushroom among local communities, without Parkinson-specific symptom claims).
Authenticity: Verified peer-reviewed open-access ethnomycological survey (Hussain et al., Foods 2023, DOI 10.3390/foods12081705).
Efficacy evidence: Low (traditional community foraging and general culinary/folk-use survey; contains no clinical symptom measurement or Parkinson disease cohort).

Hussain et al. (Foods, 2023; PMID:37107503; PMC10137476) interviewed 62 local informants across ethnic communities in Swat, Pakistan, documenting 34 wild mushroom species used for food and folk medicine. Agaricus bisporus ranked among the most widely cited edible and traditionally used species (43.5% informant citation frequency; 41 total ethnomycological citations across local communities). Informants categorized A. bisporus primarily as a valued cooked culinary food and general folk-health mushroom, with no specific traditional claim for Parkinson disease symptoms.
