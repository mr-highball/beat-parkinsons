Karremans (Plants, 2024; PMID:39599412; PMC11598392) traces the historical domestication and spread of Vanilla planifolia from its native Middle American range through 19th-century botanical and colonial cultivation networks (Réunion, Java, Guadeloupe, Martinique) enabled by hand pollination. This agricultural history clarifies why commercial culinary vanilla derives overwhelmingly from narrow clonal lineages of cured V. planifolia pods used in tiny flavoring quantities, distinct from alcohol- or glycerol-based imitation essences and high-dose pure vanillin.
