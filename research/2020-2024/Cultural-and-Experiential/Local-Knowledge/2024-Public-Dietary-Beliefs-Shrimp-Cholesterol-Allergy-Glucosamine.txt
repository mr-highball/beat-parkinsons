Title: Public dietary perceptions surrounding shrimp cholesterol avoidance, crustacean tropomyosin anaphylaxis, and glucosamine tolerance
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11527777/
Source kind: Local-knowledge record
Publication date: 2024-10-01
Date basis: Published open-access clinical review of shrimp allergy (Su et al., Front Allergy 2024; PMC11527777), paired with De Oliveira e Silva 1996 (PMID:8901790) and Villacis 2006 (PMID:17083356).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Contrasts widespread consumer avoidance of shrimp for cholesterol (which randomized whole-shrimp feeding trials show does not worsen LDL:HDL ratios) and shell-derived glucosamine fear (tolerated in DBPCFC) with authentic IgE-mediated crustacean anaphylaxis affecting up to ~2% of US adults.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed clinical nutrition and allergy records inspected during Round 7 review.
Efficacy evidence: Safety and consumer-perception context only; does not establish therapeutic efficacy for Parkinson symptoms while documenting a genuine severe allergy hazard.

Public dietary attitudes toward shrimp center on three recurring concerns. First, many consumers avoid boiled or steamed shrimp because of its high dietary cholesterol content (~189 mg/100 g), yet controlled human feeding trials (De Oliveira e Silva et al. 1996, PMID:8901790, 300 g/day steamed shrimp; Isherwood et al. 2010, PMID:20196970, 225 g/day prawns) found neutral or improved LDL:HDL ratios and lower triglycerides given shrimp's very low saturated fat. Second, IgE-mediated shrimp allergy—driven mainly by heat-stable muscle tropomyosin—is a real and potentially life-threatening hazard affecting up to ~2% of US adults (Su et al. 2024, PMC11527777; EuroPrevall ED10 = 2.5 g shrimp protein, Ballmer-Weber et al. 2015, PMID:25589011) that can acutely cause oropharyngeal angioedema, dysphagia, or anaphylactic hypotension. Third, although patients often avoid shell-derived glucosamine supplements out of shellfish-allergy concern, a double-blind challenge in 15 shrimp-allergic adults showed all 15 tolerated 1,500 mg shrimp-derived glucosamine without reaction (Villacis et al. 2006, PMID:17083356).
