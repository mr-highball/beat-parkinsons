Title: Beniaich et al. — Fez-Meknes Moroccan herbalist survey of traditional remedies for insomnia, asthenia and oral conditions
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Ethnobotanical survey of traditional practitioners
DOI: 10.1007/s11356-021-14439-8
PMID: 34031820
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34031820/
Publication date: 2022
Date basis: Environmental Science and Pollution Research publication in 2022 (online first 2021) reporting field questionnaires among contemporary Moroccan herbalists.
Reviewed: 2026-11-21 UTC
Location inspected: PubMed bibliographic record and full abstract (PMID 34031820).

Community/context: Field questionnaire survey of 120 herbalists of varied ages, sexes, and socioeconomic backgrounds across the north-central Fez-Meknes region of Morocco (80% willingness to share remedy inventories).
Exposure and reported symptom: Documented 86 plant species across 60 families used traditionally for asthenia/fatigue (47 plants), insomnia (25 plants), and oral/gum infections (21 plants). Most frequently cited plants included culinary herbs in the experiment vocabulary: Rosmarinus officinalis (rosemary), Allium cepa (onion), Origanum vulgare (oregano), Origanum majorana (marjoram), and Ocimum basilicum (basil), alongside Lavandula dentata, Matricaria chamomilla, Marrubium vulgare, and Lepidium sativum. Leaves were the most commonly used plant part and infusion was the most common preparation.
Reported direction: Practitioner-reported traditional use for easing insomnia, asthenia/fatigue, and oral infections; individual symptom scores were not measured.
Evidence and limits: Practitioner citation inventory with no treated patient cohort, comparator arm, blinding, dose standardization, or Parkinson diagnosis. Abstract lists top-cited species across the three indications combined without breaking out per-species insomnia vs asthenia citation counts.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed ethnobotanical survey (DOI 10.1007/s11356-021-14439-8) recording binomial names, plant parts, and preparation methods from 120 local herbalists.
Efficacy evidence: Practitioner citation frequency documents regional cultural use but supplies no controlled clinical evidence for Parkinson insomnia or fatigue.
Related research: Connects with Moroccan and Mediterranean ethnobotanical holdings in research/2020-2024/Cultural-and-Experiential/Traditional-Practices/ and culinary herb sleep/fatigue assessments for rosemary, basil, marjoram, oregano, and onion.
Access and follow-up: Full article tables behind Springer paywall; exact per-species citation breakdown between insomnia and asthenia for rosemary, marjoram, oregano, basil, and onion remains an open trail.
