Title: Classical Ayurvedic tradition of Mudga (Vigna radiata) soup (Yusha) and khichdi as light convalescent digestive food

Date basis: Publication date of Shirkande et al. 2024 (J Ayurveda Integr Med, PMC11250864) documenting classical Ayurvedic Mudga Yusha (green gram soup) dietary prescriptions from Charaka and Sushruta textual traditions, alongside Katkar et al. 2026 (PMC12874578).

Reported direction: Customary digestive and convalescent support: classical Ayurveda regards green gram (Mudga) cooked as spiced soup (Mudga Yusha) or soft rice-legume khichdi as the lightest, most easily digested pulse (laghu) for fragile digestion (agnimandya) and recovery, without any classical Parkinson-specific efficacy claim.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed open-access Ayurvedic clinical literature (Shirkande et al. 2024, PMC11250864; Katkar et al. 2026, PMC12874578) inspected during Round 5 review.
Efficacy evidence: Traditional medical classification and single-patient PMS case context (bundled with barley porridge and carminative spices); does not establish clinical efficacy of boiled whole mung beans for any Parkinson symptom.

In classical South Asian Ayurvedic dietary texts (Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita lineages), mung bean or green gram (Vigna radiata, Sanskrit: Mudga) is uniquely esteemed among pulses (Shami Dhanya) as laghu (light to digest) and wholesome for daily and convalescent diets (Pathya). Traditional preparations cook split or whole mung beans into thin spiced soups (Mudga Yusha) or soft rice-and-mung-dal porridge (khichdi) tempered with ghee and carminative spices such as cumin, dry ginger, coriander, and rock salt to support weakened digestive fire (agni) without causing the heaviness or flatulence attributed to denser beans. Shirkande et al. (2024, PMC11250864) documented a traditional Mudga Yusha regimen bundled with barley porridge and spices in a single adolescent PMS case, and Katkar et al. (2026, PMC12874578) noted green gram within broader traditional Ayush Ahara diets for aging strength. Traditional Ayurvedic concepts of Vata/Pitta balance do not equate to modern Parkinson symptom trials, and controlled human clinical trials isolating boiled whole mung beans in PD remain absent.
