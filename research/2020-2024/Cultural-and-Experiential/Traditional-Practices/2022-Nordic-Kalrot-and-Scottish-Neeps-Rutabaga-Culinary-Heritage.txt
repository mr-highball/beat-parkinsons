Title and source: Nordic and Scottish culinary traditions of rutabaga / swede / neep (Brassica napus L. var. napobrassica), documented via Forsberg et al. 2022 (Int J Gastron Food Sci 28:100528, DOI:10.1016/j.ijgfs.2022.100528), Molmann et al. 2021 (PMID:33011991), and Galanty et al. 2024 (PMC11012840).
Community/context: Originating as a natural amphidiploid hybrid of cabbage (B. oleracea) and turnip (B. rapa) first recorded in Sweden around 1620 (Swedish dialect rotabagge, standard Swedish kalrot, Finnish lanttu, Scottish neep), rutabaga became a cornerstone winter survival and festive root across Fennoscandia (Swedish rotmos, Finnish Christmas lanttulaatikko casserole) and Scotland (boiled and mashed "neeps and tatties" with haggis for Burns Night; clapshot), as well as an emergency wartime subsistence crop across Europe.
Exposure and reported symptom: Traditionally boiled/mashed with butter and pepper, diced into stews, or oven-roasted. Forsberg et al. 2022 noted rutabaga among traditional Nordic vegetables and documented in Swedish older adults and Parkinson focus groups that advanced PD motoric eating and dysphagia difficulties narrow acceptable vegetable textures toward soft, smooth, easily chewed preparations—favoring thoroughly boiled/mashed root over hard raw or stringy forms. Folk nutrition notes both fiber-mediated bowel regularity and cruciferous gas/bloating or historical goitrogen warnings (which modern systematic review confirms are mitigated by thorough cooking and adequate iodine).
Evidence and limits: Culinary and meal-usability context only; no controlled clinical intervention tested boiled or roasted rutabaga on any Parkinson symptom.
