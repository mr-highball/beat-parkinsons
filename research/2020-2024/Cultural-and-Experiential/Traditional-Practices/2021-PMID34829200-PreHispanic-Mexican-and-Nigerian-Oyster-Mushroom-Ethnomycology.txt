Title: Pre-Hispanic Mexican culinary traditions and Nigerian ethnomedicinal uses of Pleurotus ostreatus (oyster mushroom)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34829200/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2021-11-01
Date basis: Peer-reviewed publication date of Uriarte-Frías et al. (J Fungi 2021;7(11):911, PMC8619982), paired with Agunloye et al. 2021 (PMID:32496927), González-Bonilla et al. 2022 (PMID:36004709), and Effiong et al. 2023 (PMC10824988).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional food-as-medicine staple in pre-Hispanic and rural Mexican diets (Puebla) and West African functional food use; Nigerian ethnomedicinal reports cite local use of Pleurotus ostreatus for memory/dementia management without human clinical confirmation.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed ethnomycological and nutritional papers indexed in PubMed/PMC (PMID:34829200, PMID:32496927, PMID:36004709, PMID:38292699).
Efficacy evidence: Ethnomycological and cultural food-heritage records only; distinguishes pre-Hispanic/local traditional practices from publication date and does not establish clinical Parkinson's efficacy.

Multiple peer-reviewed sources document traditional culinary and ethnomedicinal uses of Pleurotus ostreatus (oyster mushroom) across diverse regions. In Mexico, Uriarte-Frías et al. 2021 (J Fungi, PMID:34829200, PMC8619982) and González-Bonilla et al. 2022 (Int J Med Mushrooms, PMID:36004709, Puebla) record edible oyster mushrooms as part of pre-Hispanic Mexican food traditions valued for metabolic health and nutrition. In West Africa, Agunloye et al. 2021 (J Diet Suppl, PMID:32496927) report that P. ostreatus has been used locally in Nigeria in traditional attempts to manage memory decline and Alzheimer-like cognitive complaints (investigated preclinically in transgenic Drosophila), while Effiong et al. 2023 (Front Nutr, PMID:38292699, PMC10824988) highlight P. ostreatus as an accessible nutrient-rich functional food. Documenting these traditions separates historical cultural heritage from modern controlled evidence and does not establish clinical efficacy for Parkinson's symptoms.
