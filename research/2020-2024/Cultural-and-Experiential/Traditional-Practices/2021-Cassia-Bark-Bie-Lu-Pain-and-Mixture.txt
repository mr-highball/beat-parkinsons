Title: Cassia bark pain indications in the Chinese materia medica tradition
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9037793/
DOI: 10.1039/d1ra04965h
Source kind: Traditional practice documented in a scholarly review
Publication date: 2021-08-12
Date basis: First online date of the inspected review. The cited historical practices have separate, much earlier attributed dates.
Reviewed: 2026-11-22 UTC
Community/context: The 2021 article attributes the passage to Bie Lu in the Chinese materia medica tradition. The original edition and historical chronology were not checked independently.
Source locator: Traditional uses and Table 2 in the source review, including separate Bie Lu and Yi Yu Lu discussions.
Exposure and reported symptom: Cassia bark is recorded for historical headache and waist pain indications. Other descriptions concern combined remedies. A Yi Yu Lu case uses spleen-fatigue terminology within a historical diagnostic framework.
Reported direction: Intended relief for the named pain indications. No quantified patient outcome or controlled comparison is reported.
Authenticity: The full peer-reviewed review and the separate historical attributions were inspected. The underlying manuscripts and their variants remain open.
Efficacy evidence: Historical indications and secondary interpretation. The record contains neither a confirmed Parkinson diagnosis nor a measured Parkinson pain, fatigue or rigidity result.

The bark identity and mixture limitations matter. A historical descriptor about fatigue in a specific organ framework cannot be assumed to denote reported fatigue severity. Ground cinnamon added to food is a different exposure from the remedies and combined prescriptions summarized by the review.
