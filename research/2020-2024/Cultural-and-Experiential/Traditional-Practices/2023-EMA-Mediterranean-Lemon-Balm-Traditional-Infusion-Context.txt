Title: European, Mediterranean, and Persian traditional herbal infusion uses of lemon balm (Melissa officinalis L.) for mild mental stress, sleep, and digestive complaints

Across Mediterranean, Central/Southern European, Arabic, and Traditional Persian medicine traditions—documented from Historia Plantarum (~300 BCE) through modern European pharmacopeias—the fresh and dried leaves of Melissa officinalis L. (lemon balm, honey leaf, Badranjboyeh, matičnjak) are widely prepared as aromatic herbal infusions or extracts for mild mental stress, restlessness, nervous irritability, difficulty sleeping, headaches, and mild gastrointestinal complaints such as bloating and spasmodic discomfort (Bano et al., 2023, PMC10620697; Mathews et al., 2024, PMC11510126). The European Medicines Agency (EMA HMPC) recognizes Melissa officinalis leaf infusions, dry extracts, and fluid extracts as traditional herbal medicinal products for relief of mild symptoms of mental stress, to aid sleep, and for mild digestive disorders, while noting lack of clinical confirmation in neurodegenerative disorders. Clinical and pharmacovigilance commentaries also preserve practical safety boundaries: potential thyroid-hormone interaction requiring caution in thyroid disease, rare withdrawal symptoms (restlessness, anxiety, insomnia, postural hand tremor) reported after abrupt cessation of heavy 4-cup/day habitual tea intake (Demirci et al., 2015, PMC4384870), and preparation variability in rosmarinic acid between culinary fresh leaves, brewed infusions, and concentrated extracts.
