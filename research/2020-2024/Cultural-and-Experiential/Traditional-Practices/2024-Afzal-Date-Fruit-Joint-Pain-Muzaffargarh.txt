The authors interviewed 168 respondents across Muzaffargarh District, Punjab, Pakistan. That count covers the survey as a whole. Table 6 lists cultivated date fruit, voucher MG-01, oral use and either eating it fresh or taking it as an infusion. Joint pain is among several listed uses. The row does not give a separate preparation, participant count, diagnosis, measurement or adverse outcome for that use.

The survey authenticates a reported community practice. The authors give no observed change in pain severity, no dried fruit exposure identified for joint pain and no Parkinson-specific assessment. This is one cultural source for Round 1 claim 0502, dried date and pain severity. A separate common-fig entry concerns other uses and supplies no fig-fruit or resting-tremor outcome.

Related research: 2024-Morocco-Date-Palm-Survey-Context.txt in this collection distinguishes respondents' own customary practices from a headache claim cited by the authors. 2021-PMID33959162-Date-Seed-Powder-and-Abdominal-Pain.txt under Source-Text describes seed powder and an abdominal event. Different preparations and source populations need separate assessment.
