Title: Classical Persian Medicine recommendations of black pepper (Piper nigrum) for CNS-related tremor
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35244296/
Source kind: Traditional-practice scholarly review
Publication date: 2022
Date basis: Peer-reviewed journal publication date (Phytotherapy Research 2022; PMID 35244296, DOI 10.1002/ptr.7425); surveys classical texts by Rhazes (854-925 AD), Avicenna (980-1037 AD), and Jorjani (1042-1136 AD).
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Classical Persian Medicine texts list black pepper among warming functional foods and dietary spices recommended for preventing and alleviating brain-based CNS tremors.
Authenticity: Verified via PubMed abstract (Iranshahy, Javadi & Sahebkar, Phytotherapy Research, 2022; PMID 35244296).
Efficacy evidence: Historical medical-text documentation only; classical "brain-based tremor" categories do not equate exclusively to modern Parkinson's disease and do not establish clinical efficacy.

Iranshahy, Javadi, and Sahebkar (2022, Phytotherapy Research, PMID 35244296) surveyed classical Persian Medicine textbooks and pharmacopeias by Rhazes (854-925 AD), Avicenna (980-1037 AD), and Jorjani (1042-1136 AD) for plant- and animal-based foods recommended to prevent or alleviate brain-based CNS tremors (historically compared to Parkinson's disease). Black pepper (Piper nigrum, noted for its alkaloid piperine) appears among the dietary functional foods recommended in Persian Medicine for CNS tremor alongside garlic, cinnamon, saffron, black cumin, chickpea, and pistachio. Classical nosology predates modern Parkinson's diagnosis and does not substitute for controlled clinical trials.
