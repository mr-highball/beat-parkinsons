Title: Ayurvedic and Siddha culinary-medicinal traditions of Murraya koenigii (Kadi Patta / Karuveppilai) versus commercial curry powder
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35215300/
Source kind: cultural
Publication date: 2022-02-02
Date basis: Peer-reviewed ethnopharmacology review publication dates (Tan et al., Pharmaceuticals, PMC8880493, February 2022; Gupta et al., PMID:21849142, 2011); traditional South Asian culinary and Ayurvedic usage spans centuries.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: favorable traditional digestive/tonic culinary uses, with no traditional or clinical claim of Parkinson's disease efficacy
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed pharmacognosy and Ayurvedic literature syntheses.
Efficacy evidence: Traditional culinary-medicinal documentation only; strictly separates botanical Murraya koenigii leaf from multi-spice commercial curry powder blends.

In South Asian cooking and classical Ayurveda and Siddha traditions, fresh or shade-dried leaves of Murraya koenigii (L.) Spreng (Kadi Patta / Karuveppilai / sweet neem) are routinely tempered in hot oil or ghee (tadka) or ground into chutneys and spice powders (kariveppilai podi). Traditional texts classify the bitter-astringent aromatic leaf as a digestive appetizer (Deepana/Pachana) used for nausea, mild abdominal discomfort, diarrhea or sluggish digestion, and general post-illness nourishment, while leaf pastes have folk topical use for minor bruises and aches (Gupta et al., 2011, PMID:21849142; Tan et al., 2022, PMC8880493). Critically, botanical Murraya koenigii leaves are entirely distinct from commercial 'curry powder' blends (dominated by turmeric/curcumin, coriander, cumin, fenugreek, and chili), which often contain zero curry leaf; observational curry-powder cohorts (such as Ng et al. 2006) cannot be attributed to curry leaf. Traditional dietary uses do not establish efficacy or safety in Parkinson's disease.
