Baraniak & Kania-Dobrowolska (2022, Foods, PMC8871380) and Calva-Cruz et al. (2023, Foods, PMC10379428) document the pre-Hispanic culinary and ceremonial role of grain amaranth species (Amaranthus hypochondriacus, Amaranthus cruentus, and Andean Amaranthus caudatus / kiwicha) among Aztec, Mayan, and Incan societies, where popped and boiled grain preparations served as nutrient-dense staple foods valued for sustenance and recovery from undernutrition. Botanical reviews emphasize distinguishing culinary grain amaranth from non-grain leafy or weed species (such as A. spinosus, A. tricolor, and A. viridis) used separately in South Asian and African folk medicine. No pre-Hispanic or modern traditional records link cooked or boiled grain amaranth to Parkinson's disease symptoms.
