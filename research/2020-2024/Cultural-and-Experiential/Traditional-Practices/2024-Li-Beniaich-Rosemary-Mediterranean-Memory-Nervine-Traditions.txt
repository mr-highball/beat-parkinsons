Title: Mediterranean and North African culinary-medicinal traditions of oral rosemary leaf for remembrance, nervous anxiety, and sleep
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37532071/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2024
Date basis: Peer-reviewed ethnobotanical review and survey publication dates (Li et al., 2024, PMID:37532071; Beniaich et al., 2022, PMID:34031820; Pengelly et al., 2012, PMID:21877951).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Favorable traditional reputation for memory ('rosemary for remembrance'), nervous calm, insomnia, and asthenia; strictly distinguished from inhaled essential-oil aromatherapy and paired with high-dose cognitive-impairment caution.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed ethnobotanical and clinical literature inspected during Round 6 review (PMID:37532071; PMID:34031820; PMID:21877951).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional culinary-medicinal documentation only; does not establish clinical efficacy in Parkinson's disease and notes inverted-U dose risk in older adults.

Rosmarinus officinalis L. (Salvia rosmarinus) has been embedded in Mediterranean culinary culture and European-North African folk medicine for centuries as an emblem of memory ('rosemary for remembrance') and a traditional oral infusion or seasoning for nervous tension, insomnia, low mood, asthenia, and digestive discomfort (Li et al., 2024, PMID:37532071; Beniaich et al., 2022, PMID:34031820; Pengelly et al., 2012, PMID:21877951). Ethnobotanical surveys in Morocco (Fez-Meknes) document ongoing oral leaf decoction/infusion use for insomnia and fatigue alongside culinary seasoning. Modern clinical evaluation requires strictly separating oral fresh/dried/ground rosemary leaf from inhaled rosemary essential-oil aromatherapy and topical balms, while recognizing that high oral leaf doses (6,000 mg) impaired memory speed in older adults (Pengelly et al., 2012) and oral leaf capsules caused high rates of heartburn in depressed adults (Azizi et al., 2022). Traditional practice does not establish PD clinical efficacy.
