Title: Northern Italian Bologna ethnobotanical record of Cichorium intybus (radicchio selvatico) bitter leaves for digestion, laxative use, and topical pain wraps
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10936038/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2024
Date basis: Peer-reviewed Journal of Ethnobiology and Ethnomedicine publication date (2024-03-12; field interviews conducted 2010-2016 across 22 Bologna municipalities; PMID:38475780, PMC10936038).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional bitter-leaf dietary use (raw salads and boiled soups) for digestive, laxative, and hepatic-purifying support, plus external leaf-and-flower wraps for painful body parts; distinct from roasted chicory root coffee substitutes.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed open-access ethnobotanical survey of 1,172 informants aged 50-85 in the Bologna district of Emilia-Romagna, Northern Italy (Chiocchio et al. 2024, PMC10936038, voucher BOLO0053713), inspected directly in local archive during Round 6 review.
Efficacy evidence: Ethnobotanical folk-knowledge inventory only; documents wild/field Cichorium intybus culinary and external wrap traditions without controlled clinical measurement or Parkinson disease data.

In a large ethnobotanical field survey interviewing 1,172 older rural informants across plain, hill, and mountain municipalities of the Bologna district in Northern Italy (2010-2016; Chiocchio et al. 2024, Journal of Ethnobiology and Ethnomedicine, PMC10936038; cross-linked with local archive research/2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC10936038-Chiocchio-Bologna-Ethnobotany.txt), Cichorium intybus L. (local names cicoria, radicchio selvatico, radecc, radecc amer; voucher BOLO0053713) was widely recorded as a bitter edible and folk-medicinal plant. Informants reported eating young leaves raw in salads (harvested before flowering) or boiled in soups for perceived digestive, mild laxative, blood- and liver-purifying, and renal-stimulating properties, while separately preparing roasted C. intybus roots as a wartime and everyday coffee substitute. External wraps of leaves and flowers were also noted once as a folk application on painful body parts. This record preserves Northern Italian bitter-leaf culinary heritage while keeping roasted chicory root uses and topical wraps distinct from clinical effects of raw or grilled cultivated radicchio in Parkinson disease.
