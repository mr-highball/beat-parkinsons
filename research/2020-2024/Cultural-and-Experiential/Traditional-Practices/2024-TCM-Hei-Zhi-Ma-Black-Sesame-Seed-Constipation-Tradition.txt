Title: Traditional Chinese Medicine (Hei Zhi Ma) food-medicine tradition of black sesame seed for intestinal dryness and aging
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11051911/
Source kind: Traditional-Practice
Publication date: 2024-04-11
Date basis: Publication date of peer-reviewed TCM pharmacology paper (Sun et al., Molecules 2024;29(8):1713, PMID:38675536, PMC11051911) documenting traditional Chinese medicine food-medicine classification of black sesame seed (Hei Zhi Ma).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) and East Asian dietary heritage classifies roasted or ground black sesame seed (Hei Zhi Ma / Sesamum indicum) as a homologous food-medicine tonic to nourish liver-kidney yin and lubricate intestinal dryness in chronic constipation and age-related frailty, often paired with mulberry leaf or honey in multi-herb formulas; no controlled human PD efficacy proof.
Authenticity: Documented in peer-reviewed open-access biomedical literature (Molecules 2024; PMC11051911) summarizing classical Chinese materia medica food-medicine practice.
Efficacy evidence: Customary cultural and traditional medicine context only; traditional diagnoses such as intestinal dryness or kidney-yin deficiency do not equate to diagnosed Parkinson disease, and traditional preparations frequently co-administer other herbs (such as mulberry leaf, honey, or walnut).

Across Traditional Chinese Medicine and East Asian culinary medicine, black sesame seed (Hei Zhi Ma, Sesamum indicum L.) is classified as an edible-medicinal substance traditionally roasted, ground into paste or sweet soups, and consumed to moisten the intestines, ease dry-stool constipation, and nourish liver and kidney essence in older adults. As documented in contemporary TCM multi-omics investigations (Sun et al. 2024, PMC11051911), classical constipation formulas frequently pair black sesame with other botanicals such as mulberry leaf (Morus alba, SH formula) rather than administering sesame monotherapy. In Middle Eastern and Mediterranean diets, ground roasted sesame paste (tahini / ardeh) is similarly a staple calorie- and mineral-dense culinary food. While culturally widespread, customary food-medicine use does not establish efficacy for Parkinson disease constipation or motor symptoms, and clinical case reports caution that excessive intake of unchewed whole seeds can rarely cause colonic seed phytobezoars.
