Title: Japanese kome-abura and South Asian culinary traditions of rice bran oil (Oryza sativa) and gamma-oryzanol
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11083700/
Source kind: Ethnographic and food-history scholarly record
Publication date: 2024
Date basis: Publication year of Tufail et al. 2024 comprehensive review in Foods (PMID:38731675, PMC11083700), documenting traditional Asian rice-bran utilization and modern culinary/industrial rice bran oil integration.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Widely valued neutral high-smoke-point frying/cooking oil across Japan (kome-abura for tempura) and India, paired with traditional pharmacopeial/nutraceutical interest in gamma-oryzanol for lipids and menopausal/autonomic wellness; no traditional claim for Parkinson disease symptoms.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed food-science and culinary-history synthesis (Tufail et al., Foods 2024; PMID:38731675, PMC11083700) cross-referenced with Cicero & Gaddi 2001 (PMID:11406848).
Efficacy evidence: Culinary heritage and phytochemistry context only; underscores the major gap between unrefined/physically refined oil vs chemically refined oil and isolated gamma-oryzanol tablets, and provides no clinical Parkinson efficacy proof.

Title and source: Culinary history and regional integration of rice bran oil (Japanese kome-abura / komeyu; Oryza sativa bran oil) across East and South Asia, inspected via Tufail et al. 2024 (Foods 13(9):1305; PMID:38731675, PMC11083700) and Cicero & Gaddi 2001 (PMID:11406848).
Community/context: Rooted in centuries-old Japanese zero-waste traditions using rice bran (komenuka) for pickling (nukazuke) and skin cleansing (nukabukuro), commercial extraction of kome-abura matured in mid-20th-century Japan as a prized neutral, high-smoke-point (~232-250 C) oil for crisp, light tempura and karaage, and later expanded widely across India for high-heat kadhai frying and everyday tadka tempering.
Exposure and reported symptom: Refined culinary rice bran oil is distinct from whole rice bran fiber, ethanol rice-bran extracts, and isolated pharmaceutical gamma-oryzanol tablets (historically used in Japanese clinical practice for hyperlipidemia and menopausal/autonomic complaints). Chemical alkali refining can remove a large fraction of crude oil gamma-oryzanol unless physical refining is used.
Evidence and limits: Documents culinary acceptance and cardiometabolic/antioxidant rationale without establishing any direct clinical benefit for Parkinson motor or non-motor symptoms.
