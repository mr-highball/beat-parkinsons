Title: Central and Eastern European Traditional Phytotherapy of Horseradish Root (Armoracia rusticana) for Respiratory, Sinus, Digestive, and Urinary Complaints
Collection: Cultural and experiential

Publication date: 2020
Date basis: Publication year of peer-reviewed Biomolecules horseradish review (Popovic et al. 2020, PMID:32098279, PMC7072351), cross-referencing Herz et al. 2017 (PMC5274677) and Neumaier 2007 (PMID:17619392).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional oral and poultice use for sinus/bronchial congestion, sluggish digestion, rheumatic pain, and urinary infections, alongside European herbal safety cautions for GI and urinary mucosal irritation; no traditional Parkinson motor indication.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed biomedical and phytotherapy reviews (Biomolecules 2020 PMC7072351; eCAM 2017 PMC5274677; MMW Fortschr Med 2007 PMID:17619392) verified during Round 4 review.
Efficacy evidence: Traditional European phytotherapy context only; does not establish clinical efficacy for isolated grated horseradish in Parkinson's disease and notes mucosal irritant risks.

European phytotherapy and ethnopharmacological reviews (Popovic et al. 2020, PMC7072351; Herz et al. 2017, PMC5274677; Neumaier 2007, PMID:17619392) record long-standing Central and Eastern European domestic medicinal use of fresh grated Armoracia rusticana root, oral mixtures (with honey or milk), and topical neck/chest poultices for acute sinus congestion, bronchitis, cough, sluggish digestion (as a pungent stomachic and sialogogue), localized rheumatic aches, and lower urinary tract infections. Grating fresh root activates myrosinase hydrolysis of sinigrin and gluconasturtiin into volatile allyl isothiocyanate (AITC) and 2-phenylethyl isothiocyanate (PEITC), accounting for sharp trigeminal nasal/oral pungency and antimicrobial activity. Modern German phytotherapy formalized dried horseradish root only in a fixed 2-herb tablet with Tropaeolum majus (nasturtium). Traditional European herbal monographs (including Commission E guidance) explicitly caution that excessive raw horseradish root can irritate gastrointestinal and urinary tract mucous membranes and is contraindicated in active peptic ulcer disease or acute nephritis.
