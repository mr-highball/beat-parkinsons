Reported direction: Participants describe customary purposes. The fieldwork records no measured Parkinson symptom change.
Authenticity: The authors interviewed 86 people, including 19 recognized experts, and gathered 469 practices for 69 contexts. They documented 67 species and 731 plant use reports.
Efficacy evidence: The interviews give access to locally recognized preparations and uses, while symptom changes following a comparison were outside the study design.

Sweet basil appears in childhood restlessness or jitteriness accounts and in a separate cold-related bath context. The original distinctions do not establish an insomnia diagnosis or measured improvement. Coconut oil is a component of monoi, described in cosmetic and external applications and reported by 19 people in 23 use reports. Turmeric appears in locally described childhood preparations, including the category he'a, with purposes recorded in local terms. These uses give leads for provenance and preparation, without an edible-oil or isolated-turmeric comparison for Parkinson falls or pain.
