Title: South Asian (Methi Saag / Kasuri Methi) and Persian (Shanbalileh) culinary-medicinal traditions of fenugreek leaves
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11387638/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2024
Date basis: Peer-reviewed open-access publication year (Bakhtiar et al., Sci Rep 2024, PMC11387638; Salam et al., Sci Rep 2023, PMC10148852; Gaikwad et al., 2025, PMC11947087).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Contextual culinary leafy-green vegetable and folk digestive/antipyretic/analgesic tradition; zero traditional or clinical claim of Parkinson's disease efficacy.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed phytochemical and ethnobotanical publications inspected during Round 4 review (PMC11387638; PMC10148852; PMC11947087; PMID:9406901).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional culinary-medicinal documentation only; strictly separates cooked/dried fenugreek leaves from fenugreek seed clinical trials and notes botanical species nuance for Kasuri methi.

Fresh cooked and shade-/air-dried leaves of Trigonella foenum-graecum L. occupy a distinct culinary and ethnobotanical niche across South Asia (cooked methi saag, aloo methi, methi paratha, and dried methi leaves), Iran (fresh and dried shanbalileh essential to stews such as ghormeh sabzi and herb frittatas), Egypt, and Greece (Bakhtiar et al., 2024, PMC11387638; Salam et al., 2023, PMC10148852). Folk and regional traditions value the bitter aromatic leaves as a nutrient-rich vegetable (iron, calcium, beta-carotene, vitamin C) and use leaf preparations for mild indigestion, anemia, fever, stomach discomfort, wound/skin poultices, and folk pain relief in Iranian traditional medicine (Javan et al., 1997, PMID:9406901). Two botanical distinctions are critical for evidence review: first, culinary fenugreek leaves (~86% fresh moisture, ~1.1% fiber, rich in leaf phenolics/chlorophyll) are chemically distinct from mucilage-rich fenugreek seeds (~45-50% fiber, 4-hydroxyisoleucine, high endosperm saponins) used in nearly all clinical trials; second, commercial Indian 'Kasuri methi' is frequently the related species Trigonella corniculata L. rather than T. foenum-graecum (Gaikwad et al., 2025, PMC11947087). No traditional or clinical human record establishes efficacy of cooked or dried fenugreek leaves for Parkinson's disease symptoms.
