Title: Ginger-processed Eucommia bark in Chinese historical back and knee pain accounts
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Traditional practices
Source: https://tjzhongyiyao.tjutcm.edu.cn/html/tjzyy/2024/3/20240320.htm
DOI: 10.11656/j.issn.1672-1519.2024.03.20
Publication date: 2024-03
Date basis: Tianjin Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine 41(3), 393–397. Historical attributions are discussed in this modern article and have their own source dates.
Reported direction: Intended relief of back and knee soreness in a traditional context. The text does not establish an attributable response to ginger.
Authenticity: The modern Chinese-language journal article and its preparation, indication and formula sections were checked directly. Ancient editions cited in the article were not independently verified for this record.
Efficacy evidence: Historical therapeutic indications and formula descriptions. No measured Parkinson pain response or isolated ginger comparison.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25

The authors describe Jiang Duzhong (姜杜仲), a form of dried Eucommia ulmoides bark treated with ginger juice. They locate ginger-juice processing in the historical literature and describe references to low back and knee soreness. Their account connects one intended use with the traditional cold-dampness category. Several related practices use additional substances or different processes. The article reports that the 2020 Chinese Pharmacopoeia specifies raw sliced bark and salt-processed bark, distinct from the historical ginger-juice preparation.

Ginger juice here serves in the preparation of bark. The traditional indication has no confirmed Parkinson diagnosis, validated pain score or controlled response. The related 2024 Eucommia review, PMC10834829, lists ginger juice in Table 3. Its separately discussed black and green leaf teas are Eucommia preparations, with a different plant identity from Camellia sinensis tea.
