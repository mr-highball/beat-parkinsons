Title: Global ethnopharmacological review of Allium cepa (onion) for headache, rheumatism, gastrointestinal, and nervous complaints
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38262524/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2024-02-01
Date basis: Peer-reviewed publication date of Elattar et al. (J Ethnopharmacol 2024;117779), paired with Teshika et al. 2019 (PMID:30040448) and Chakraborty et al. 2022 (PMC8789449).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional folk remedies across indigenous cultures cite Allium cepa bulbs for headache, rheumatism/joint pain, gastrointestinal complaints, fatigue/asthenia, and sleep/insomnia (also documented in Moroccan Fez-Meknes herbalist survey Beniaich 2022, PMID:34031820).
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed ethnopharmacological reviews indexed in PubMed/PMC (PMID:38262524, PMID:30040448, PMID:35087593).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional ethnopharmacological compilation only; authors explicitly note that most evidence remains traditional or in vitro and requires clinical validation. Does not establish PD efficacy.

Comprehensive ethnopharmacological reviews of Allium cepa L. (Elattar et al. 2024, J Ethnopharmacol, PMID:38262524; Teshika et al. 2019, Crit Rev Food Sci Nutr, PMID:30040448; Chakraborty et al. 2022, PMID:35087593, PMC8789449) compile centuries-old indigenous and traditional uses of onion bulbs across Mediterranean, South Asian, Middle Eastern, and African practices—including folk applications for headache, rheumatism, menstrual and abdominal pain, digestive sluggishness, respiratory catarrh, and nervous exhaustion. Separately, a field survey of 120 herbalists in Fez-Meknes, Morocco (Beniaich 2022, PMID:34031820, archived locally) recorded Allium practices in traditional insomnia and asthenia lore. Elattar et al. explicitly caution that traditional reputation outstrips human clinical trials; historical use does not prove clinical efficacy or safety in Parkinson's disease.
