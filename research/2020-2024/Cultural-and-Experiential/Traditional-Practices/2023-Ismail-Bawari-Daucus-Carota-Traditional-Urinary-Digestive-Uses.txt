Across South Asian, Mediterranean, and European food-medicine traditions, cultivated and wild Daucus carota roots have been used primarily for urinary/renal support, digestion, and general nourishment. In Ayurvedic and Indian folk medicine, carrot root is traditionally linked to Ashmari bhedan (urinary calculus prevention/dissolution), diuresis, and renal protection (Bawari et al., 2020; Sodimbaku et al., 2016). Mediterranean ethnobotany records wild carrot as carminative, diuretic, and anti-inflammatory (Ismail et al., 2023), while rural Italian surveys in Bologna document everyday culinary carrot consumption traditionally associated with eye health and nutrition (Chiocchio et al., 2024). Distinguishes wild subspecies and root extracts from ordinary culinary steamed, raw, or roasted orange carrots.
