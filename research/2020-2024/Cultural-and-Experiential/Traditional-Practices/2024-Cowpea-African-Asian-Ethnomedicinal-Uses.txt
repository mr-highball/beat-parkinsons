Sardar et al. (2024, Heliyon, PMC11422034) compile traditional African and Asian ethnomedicinal accounts of Vigna unguiculata (cowpea / black-eyed pea). Boiled or prepared seeds are traditionally regarded as a diuretic, used to strengthen the stomach, ease constipation, treat boils, and manage urinary complaints such as hematuria/schistosomiasis; by contrast, non-seed plant parts—roots, leaves, or wild subspecies—are separately documented in folk traditions for chest pain, headaches, epilepsy, or antidote preparations. Traditional dietary records in West Africa also note preparation methods (dehulling and thorough cooking, cf. Ndubuaku 1989, PMID:2596840) used to reduce post-meal flatulence and abdominal discomfort. None of these customary practices evaluate Parkinson's disease or establish clinical symptom efficacy, and V. unguiculata is botanically distinct from L-DOPA-rich Mucuna pruriens and Vicia faba.
