Title: Traditional uses of sage, cinnamon and rosemary in five Guadalajara neighborhoods
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Ethnobotanical survey of traditional practices
Source: https://www.blacpma.ms-editions.cl/index.php/blacpma/article/download/399/399/790
DOI: 10.37360/blacpma.24.23.1.6
Publication date: 2024-01-30
Date basis: Date printed on the journal's original article.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Location inspected: Original field report, tables of preparation and use, including printed pages 88, 92, 96 and 102–103.

Community/context: Field inventory across five old Guadalajara neighborhoods. The study documented 137 medicinal species. Reported indications and presences vary by neighborhood.
Exposure and reported symptom: The paper identifies common sage (Salvia officinalis, salvia real), true cinnamon (Cinnamomum verum, canela) and rosemary (Rosmarinus officinalis, romero). The cinnamon entry includes the local category of altered nerves. Sage entries emphasize digestion and include a cancer-prevention attribution. Rosemary entries list ritual and medicinal uses, including paralysis, without a Parkinson diagnosis. Preparation tables list infusion and decoction for all three. The route table lists oral use for sage and cinnamon and wash or bath entries for rosemary. These tables do not identify a tested preparation for each indication.
Reported direction: The uses were intended to address listed problems. The paper does not provide a person-level outcome, stability of effect, harm count or inactive comparison for anxiety, depression, Parkinson tremor, rigidity or insomnia.
Evidence and limits: Altered nerves is a culturally specific expression and cannot establish a clinical anxiety diagnosis. Paralysis is a reported indication without a verified neurologic phenotype. Neither the herb preparation nor the measured outcomes match the frozen Parkinson questions.
Authenticity: Original Spanish-language tables and publication information inspected.
Efficacy evidence: This inventory documents use and does not establish symptom changes.
