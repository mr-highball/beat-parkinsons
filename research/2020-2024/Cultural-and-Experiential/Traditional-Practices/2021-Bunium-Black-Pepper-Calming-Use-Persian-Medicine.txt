Title: Reported Iranian calming use of black caraway and black pepper
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2021/5577594
Source kind: Traditional practice, attributed by contemporary research authors
Source ID: IRAN-BUNIUM-PIPER-2021-CALMING
Publication date: 2021-06-12
Date basis: Date of inspected publication PMID 34235220. The selected formulation's rationale cites a 2008 Persian translation of the Canon of Medicine.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24
Ingredients: black pepper
Botanical exposure: Bunium persicum and Piper nigrum. Bunium persicum is also called Persian black cumin or black caraway, a different species from culinary Cuminum cyminum.
Symptoms: anxiety severity
Community and context: The authors attribute a calming use for a combined preparation to Iranian traditional medicine. They cite the third volume of the 2008 Sharafkandi translation for the traditional basis of formulation selection.
Exposure and reported symptom: A mixed preparation, with no reported direct patient anxiety measurement. The associated laboratory study tests concentrated extracts for stress-associated learning and memory in mice.
Reported direction: The combination was traditionally described as calming. This statement does not identify an effect of a single ingredient.
Efficacy evidence: No assigned clinical population, comparative anxiety result, or Parkinson observation in the traditional account.
Authenticity: The contemporary article and its reference list were inspected. The authors do not give an exact primary page for the calming statement. The relevant Persian edition passage remains unverified. The separate culinary cumin citation PMID 21639683 reports urinary stress biomarkers and memory, distinct from this mixture.
