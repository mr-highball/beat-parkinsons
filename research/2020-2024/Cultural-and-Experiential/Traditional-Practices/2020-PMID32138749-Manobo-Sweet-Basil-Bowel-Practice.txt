Community/context: Documented among Agusan Manobo communities in Agusan del Sur, Philippines. The study included 335 key informants and used interviews, group discussion, observations and guided walks. Plant identifications are tied to reported vouchers where available.

Exposure: The Table 4 sweet basil entry identifies Ocimum basilicum L., locally sangig, with leaves and shoots prepared as a decoction or added to soup for several groups of indications. The table does not identify whether the leaves were dried for constipation. A separate row identifies another plant as useful for fatigue.

Reported direction: The sweet basil entry lists constipation as one intended-use indication along with diarrhea, vomiting, hemorrhoids and other complaints. These capture distinct uses and cannot be read as a paired response. The aggregate use count of 385 is across indications and is not a count of constipation responses.

Authenticity: Checked the published table, specimen entry and Methods. The study documents community-reported knowledge and identifiable botanical material.

Efficacy evidence: No before-and-after bowel frequency, patient diagnosis, control group or Parkinson cohort is reported for this row. Its role is traditional-use context, with no directional clinical weight.

Open trail: Look for a traceable observation of constipation response, separated by preparation, diagnosis and symptom outcome.
