Title: Iranian Traditional Medicine Framing of Malus domestica (Apple) as a Heart and Brain Anti-Fatigue Tonic
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10912872/
Source kind: Peer-reviewed traditional-medicine and animal research article (PMID:38444713, PMC10912872)
Publication date: 2023-12-01
Date basis: Publication year 2023 in Iranian Journal of Pharmaceutical Research (PMID:38444713, PMC10912872), citing classical Iranian Traditional Medicine / Persian Medicine herbal classifications distinct from publication date.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Favorable traditional attribution (customary Persian Medicine classification of apple as a heart and brain tonic believed to enhance vitality and reduce physical and mental fatigue).
Authenticity: Verified via indexed PubMed/PMC record (Alembagheri et al., IJPR 2023; PMID:38444713; DOI:10.5812/ijpr-140323).
Efficacy evidence: Low (traditional Persian Medicine framing accompanied only by a rat forced-swimming assay; does not establish clinical efficacy for Parkinson disease fatigue).

Alembagheri et al. (Iranian Journal of Pharmaceutical Research, 2023; PMID:38444713; PMC10912872) document that in Iranian Traditional Medicine (Persian Medicine), Malus domestica (apple), alongside Myrtus communis (myrtle) and Syzygium aromaticum (clove), has historically been classified as a brain and heart tonic ('mofarreh' / cardiac tonic) believed to relieve fatigue by supporting cardiac vitality and tissue circulation. Alongside this historical framing, the authors evaluated an aqueous apple extract in rat weight-loaded forced swimming tests (~1.8-fold swimming duration increase vs control). Classical traditional framing provides cultural context only and does not prove clinical benefit for Parkinson disease fatigue.
