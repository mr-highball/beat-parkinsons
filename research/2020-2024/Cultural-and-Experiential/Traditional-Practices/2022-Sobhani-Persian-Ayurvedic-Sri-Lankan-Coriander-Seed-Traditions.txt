Title: Persian (Geshniz), Ayurvedic (Dhanyaka), Sri Lankan, and Mediterranean traditional uses of coriander seed for headache, anxiety, insomnia, mood, digestion, and joint pain
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9314633/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2022
Date basis: Publication year of Sobhani et al. 2022 (Journal of Food Science, PMID:35279837, PMC9314633); incorporates Napagoda et al. 2018 (PMID:29974006) and Tavakkoli-Kakhki et al. 2014 (PMID:25068136).
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional calming, headache/migraine-relieving, carminative digestive, and anti-inflammatory/joint-pain uses of dried Coriandrum sativum fruits/seeds; no Parkinson-specific clinical efficacy proof.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed ethnobotanical review (Sobhani et al. 2022, PMC9314633) corroborated by quantitative Sri Lankan ethnobotanical survey (Napagoda et al. 2018, n=458, PMC6008615) and Iranian Traditional Medicine food compilation (Tavakkoli-Kakhki et al. 2014, PMC4110782).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional ethnobotanical context only; modern non-PD clinical trials exist for migraine syrup (Delavar Kasmaei 2016) and student anxiety/depression/sleep (Alqudah 2024), whereas Parkinson's human clinical trials remain absent.

Peer-reviewed ethnobotanical sources (Sobhani et al. 2022, J Food Sci, PMID:35279837 / PMC9314633; Napagoda et al. 2018, PMID:29974006 / PMC6008615; Tavakkoli-Kakhki et al. 2014, PMID:25068136 / PMC4110782; Emamghoreishi et al. 2005, PMID:15619553) document multi-century culinary and traditional-medicine uses of dried Coriandrum sativum L. fruits (commonly called coriander seeds) across Persian, Ayurvedic (Dhanyaka), Sri Lankan, and Mediterranean traditions.

Recorded traditional indications for dried coriander fruits/seeds include:
- Traditional Persian/Iranian medicine (Geshniz): prescribed for headache/migraine, anxiety, insomnia, palpitations, and listed among medicinal foods recommended for depressed patients (Tavakkoli-Kakhki 2014); also noted as a folk caution regarding maternal pregnancy intake and offspring memory (Zargar-Nattaj 2011).
- South Asian & Sri Lankan ethnobotany: toasted/ground coriander seed infusions and decoctions used as carminative digestives for flatulence and abdominal discomfort, and in a Gampaha District Sri Lanka survey of 458 respondents (Napagoda 2018), Coriandrum sativum ranked highest in relative frequency of citation (RFC = 0.23) among traditional anti-inflammatory remedies for joint swelling and pain.

Plant-part and evidentiary boundaries: Coriander seeds/fruits (rich in linalool and petroselinic acid) must be distinguished from fresh coriander leaves (cilantro). Historical traditional use informs hypothesis generation but does not itself establish clinical efficacy in Parkinson's disease.
