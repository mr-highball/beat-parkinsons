Title and source: Yuli-Posadas RA et al., "TOCOSH FLOUR (Solanum tuberosum L.): A Toxicological Assessment of Traditional Peruvian Fermented Potatoes" (PMC7353511, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7353511/, 2020-06-02; cross-referencing ethnomicrobiology review PMC11406881, 2024).
Source kind: Peer-reviewed ethnomedicinal and toxicological record.
Publication date and date basis: Published 2020-06-02 (documenting centuries-old Andean Highland practices; retrieved 2026-11-26).
Community/context: Indigenous Quechua and Aymara communities of the Peruvian and Bolivian Andes.
Exposure and reported symptom: Two ancestral transformations of Solanum tuberosum are documented: (1) tocosh, produced by submerging potatoes in running cold mountain water for months of lactic-acid bacterial fermentation and consumed traditionally for gastritis, ulcers, and digestive protection; and (2) chuno, freeze-dried via alternating frost, treading, and sun drying to leach bitter, toxic steroidal glycoalkaloids (solanine and chaconine). Neither preparation resembles ordinary hot boiled, baked, or roasted potatoes, and no Parkinson symptom diagnosis is recorded.
Reported direction: Traditional gastrointestinal soothing (tocosh) and glycoalkaloid detoxification (chuno); no clinical Parkinson claim.
Evidence and limits: Animal toxicology and ethnobotanical records do not establish efficacy for human Parkinson symptoms.
Authenticity and efficacy: Authentic peer-reviewed documentation; reinforces real-world food safety around avoiding green/sprouted high-solanine potatoes while distinguishing traditional fermented tubers from modern culinary preparations.
Related research: Cross-linked to Bush 2023/2026 (PMID:37049425/43030167), Cao 2022 (PMID:35458108), and Nielsen 2013 (PMID:23661325).
