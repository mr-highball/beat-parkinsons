Title: Levantine (Za'atar), European (Thymi herba), North African, and Iranian (Avishan) Traditional Uses and Nomenclature Boundaries for Thyme (Thymus vulgaris)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38039009/
Source kind: Traditional-practice and ethnobotanical nomenclature record
Publication date: 2023
Date basis: Peer-reviewed publication year documenting Levantine/Jordanian traditional context (Alqudah et al. 2023, PMID:38039009; corroborated by Iranian Avishan species audits PMC3992233/PMC3685784 and Algerian GI survey PMC10878339).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional accounts across the Levant, Europe, and North Africa credit thyme teas and culinary za'atar with easing productive cough, bloating/constipation, low mood, and poor memory/sleep; balanced against major modern clinical limitations (Alqudah 2023 arithmetic/table contradictions) and critical species confusion with Iranian Zataria multiflora.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed clinical and ethnobotanical publications (Alqudah 2023, PMID:38039009; Tazi 2024, PMC10878339; Salmalian 2014, PMC3992233; Direkvand-Moghadam 2012, PMC3685784) inspected during Round 7 review.
Efficacy evidence: Traditional dietary and herbal-tea uses do not establish clinical efficacy for diagnosed Parkinson disease.

Cross-Cultural Traditional Heritage:
1. Levant & Jordan (Za'atar): Dried thyme leaves (commonly Thymus vulgaris or regional Origanum syriacum / Thymbra spicata in za'atar blends) are consumed daily with olive oil and sesame seeds and traditionally credited in Jordanian folk medicine with supporting memory, calming nerves, aiding sleep, and soothing digestion (ethnomedicinal rationale cited in Alqudah et al. 2023, PMID:38039009).
2. Europe (EMA HMPC Thymi herba): European herbal monographs recognize dried aerial parts of Thymus vulgaris L. / T. zygis L. as a traditional herbal expectorant for productive cough associated with colds, grounded in decades of customary tea/syrup use rather than neurological indications.
3. North Africa (Algeria): In a 2024 survey of 160 Algerian GI patients (Tazi et al. 2024, PMC10878339), Thymus vulgaris ranked second among all self-selected medicinal herbs (65 citations, 11.3%), primarily brewed for abdominal bloating (57%) and constipation (30%).

Critical Iranian Nomenclature Boundary ('Avishan'):
In Persian ethnomedicine and Iranian clinical literature, the vernacular term 'Avishan' or 'Shirazi thyme' frequently refers to Zataria multiflora Boiss., a distinct Lamiaceae genus whose essential oil profile differs from Thymus vulgaris L. Direct OA full-text inspection in Round 7 confirmed that two prominent dysmenorrhea pain RCTs titled 'Thymus vulgaris' (Salmalian 2014, PMC3992233; Direkvand-Moghadam 2012, PMC3685784) actually tested Zataria multiflora.
