Title: South Asian Ayurvedic, Unani, Ladakh Amchi, and Classical Persian Traditional Uses and Safety Boundaries of Cardamom
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9685814/
Source kind: Traditional-practice and ethnobotanical record
Publication date: 2022-11-10
Date basis: Publication date of peer-reviewed traditional/pharmacological review (Ashokkumar et al., PMC9685814 / PMID:36438833), integrating Ladakh Amchi ethnobotany (Ballabh et al., 2008, PMID:18550306), Iranian ancient PD review (Nobari et al., 2023, PMC10090798), and clinical ground-cardamom anaphylaxis reports (PMID:29214141; Pinhal 2025).
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional favorable digestive, breath-freshening, carminative, calming, and Amchi urinary/diuretic uses contrasted against historical multi-herb Amomum PD compounding and rare unfavorable IgE-mediated ground-cardamom allergy/anaphylaxis.
Authenticity: Traceable to peer-reviewed ethnobotanical and pharmacological reviews (PMC9685814; PMID:18550306; PMC10090798) and documented clinical allergy case reports.
Efficacy evidence: Traditional and cultural practice record only; does not establish clinical efficacy for ground green cardamom (Elettaria cardamomum) on Parkinson's motor, cognitive, sleep, drooling, or urinary symptoms.

Across Ayurveda ('Ela'), Unani ('Heel Khurd'), and culinary spice traditions documented in peer-reviewed ethnobotanical syntheses (PMC9685814, 2022), green cardamom pods and seeds (Elettaria cardamomum) are chewed or brewed as aromatic carminatives, breath fresheners, mild digestive/salivation stimulants, and calming spices. Ladakh Amchi traditional healers additionally catalog cardamom inside polyherbal kidney and urinary-tract diuretic remedies (Ballabh et al., 2008, PMID:18550306), a diuretic tradition that conceptually runs opposite to reducing urinary urgency frequency. A 2023 review of Parkinson's disease ('Ra'sha') in Iranian ancient medicine (Nobari et al., PMC10090798) records an unvalidated five-herb powder listing 'Amomum Cardamom' (100 g) alongside rose, cinnamon, myrtle, and star-thistle—illustrating both multi-herb compounding and historical genus ambiguity between Elettaria and Amomum. Balancing favorable culinary reputation, clinical allergy case reports document rare IgE-mediated urticaria and anaphylaxis after ingesting ground cardamom (PMID:29214141; Pinhal 2025).
