Title: Persian (Avicenna/ITM) and Traditional Chinese Medicine (Fan Hong Hua) practices for saffron stigmas in mood, pain, and neurological vitality
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10127217/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2023
Date basis: Peer-reviewed open-access review publication years (Yang et al., Exp Ther Med 2023, PMC10127217; Iranshahy & Javadi, Phytother Res 2022, PMID:35244296); classical Persian and Chinese medical texts date to medieval and imperial eras.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Favorable traditional reputation for uplifting mood, calming anxiety/palpitations, easing spasms and pain, and supporting brain/nerve vitality; no classical controlled clinical Parkinson's disease proof.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed ethnopharmacology and neurodegenerative reviews inspected during Round 6 review (PMC10127217; PMID:35244296).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional culinary-medicinal record only; distinguishes classical Persian/TCM indications from modern randomized controlled evidence and highlights culinary-dose vs therapeutic-dose differences.

Across classical Iranian Traditional Medicine (including Avicenna's Canon of Medicine), Ayurvedic rasayana formulations, and Traditional Chinese Medicine (as Fan Hong Hua), dried stigmas of Crocus sativus L. have been prized both as a culinary spice and as a medicinal botanical to relieve melancholy and depression, calm nervous anxiety, promote circulation, ease menstrual and musculoskeletal spasms/pain, and strengthen neurological vitality (Yang et al., 2023, PMC10127217; Iranshahy & Javadi, 2022, PMID:35244296). Culinary dishes typically incorporate only a few threads per serving (~5-20 mg), whereas modern clinical trials administer 28-100 mg/day of standardized extracts or milled stigma powder, and high doses (>400 mg/day) carry hemodynamic/hypotensive cautions (Modaghegh et al., 2008, PMID:18693099) and toxicity above 5 g/day. Traditional reverence does not independently establish efficacy for Parkinson's motor or non-motor endpoints.
