Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed comprehensive review (Ejaz et al. 2023, PMID:37554989, PMC10406440) inspected during Round 7 review.
Efficacy evidence: Traditional culinary and herbal context only; modern human oral trials of summer savory for GERD and knee OA were statistically null.

Traditional & Culinary Context:
Summer savory (Satureja hortensis L., Lamiaceae) is an annual culinary herb native to the eastern Mediterranean and Caucasus (Ejaz et al. 2023, PMC10406440). In Iranian cuisine, fresh S. hortensis leaves (Marzeh) are eaten raw daily as part of sabzi khordan (fresh herb platter) and used in traditional Iranian medicine as a carminative, stomachic, antidiarrheal, and antispasmodic remedy, as well as a folk remedy for muscle and bone aches (Hajhashemi et al. 2000, PMID:10904162; 2002, PMID:12241981). In Georgia, dried summer savory (Kondari) is essential to lobio (kidney-bean stew) and khmeli-suneli spice blends, paralleling German Bohnenkraut ('bean herb'), reflecting widespread culinary lore that summer savory eases bean-related gas and intestinal cramping.

Botanical Boundary:
Culinary summer savory (Satureja hortensis) must be strictly separated from perennial winter savory (Satureja montana) and endemic Iranian Satureja khuzestanica, which differ substantially in carvacrol concentration and account for most Iranian clinical trials labeled generically as 'savory' (Jafari et al. 2016, PMID:27342885).
