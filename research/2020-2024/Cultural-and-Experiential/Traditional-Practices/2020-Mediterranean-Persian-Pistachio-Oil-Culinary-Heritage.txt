Title: Persian and Mediterranean culinary finishing-oil heritage of pressed Pistacia vera kernel oil and separation from wild Pistacia species

Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Customary gourmet culinary finishing oil and traditional emollient/topical skin-care oil; zero traditional or clinical claim of Parkinson's disease efficacy.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed open-access Mediterranean food-science and ethnobotanical literature verified during Round 6 review (PMC7181075; PMC8747606; PMC12346616).
Efficacy evidence: Culinary and artisanal food-heritage context only; strictly distinguishes culinary Pistacia vera kernel oil from wild Pistacia atlantica ('baneh') and Pistacia lentiscus (mastic) oils and supplies no symptom comparison.

Mechanically pressed from raw or roasted Pistacia vera kernels in Mediterranean (Greece, Sicily/Bronte, Spain) and Middle Eastern culinary traditions, emerald-green pistachio oil is prized as an unheated gourmet finishing oil drizzled over salads, vegetables, fish, and pastries, with minor traditional cosmetic/emollient skin uses (Valasi et al., 2020, PMC7181075; Rabadan et al., 2018, PMID:29853404). Roasting kernels prior to pressing increases chlorophyll and carotenoid pigment transfer and deepens color and toasted aroma, while pressing leaves behind kernel fiber, protein, and hydrophilic anthocyanins/polyphenols in the defatted pistachio flour cake (Mendoza-Perez et al., 2025, PMC12346616). Botanical precision is essential in regional literature: traditional Middle Eastern and North African medicinal oils frequently derive from wild pistachio (Pistacia atlantica) or mastic tree berries (Pistacia lentiscus), which are distinct botanical species with different terpene and fatty-acid compositions from culinary Pistacia vera nut oil. No traditional or clinical record establishes any Parkinson's motor or non-motor efficacy for culinary pistachio oil.
