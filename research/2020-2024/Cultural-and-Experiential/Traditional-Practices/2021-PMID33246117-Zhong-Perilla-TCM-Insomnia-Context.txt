Reviewed: 2026-11-21 UTC
Location inspected: PubMed bibliographic record and full structured abstract (PMID 33246117).

Community/context: Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) pharmacology framework describing Perilla frutescens (shiso) as regulating Qi, relieving depression syndrome (viewed as a core pathogenesis of insomnia), and promoting Qi circulation to relieve pain, paired with a Chinese laboratory investigation.
Exposure and reported symptom: Traditional attribution covers insomnia, depressive mood, and pain. The paired experiment tested inhaled steam-distilled Perilla frutescens essential oil (PFEO; 54.37% perillaldehyde, 7.42% 1,4-cineole, 5.09% D-limonene, 4.94% eucalyptol by GC-MS) in a PCPA-induced insomnia mouse model, measuring open-field activity, pentobarbital sleep-onset rate, sleep latency, sleep duration, hypothalamic/cortical 5-HT and GABA, and GABAA alpha1/alpha2 expression.
Reported direction: Traditional TCM indication is easing insomnia, depression, and pain; experimental inhalation significantly reduced autonomic activity, increased sleep-onset rate, shortened sleep latency, prolonged sleep duration, and elevated 5-HT, GABA, and GABAA alpha1/alpha2 levels in PCPA insomnia mice.
Evidence and limits: Preclinical mouse insomnia model using inhaled essential oil rather than oral culinary dried shiso leaves or people with Parkinson disease. Abstract does not supply group sizes or exact numeric variance tables.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed Journal of Ethnopharmacology article (DOI 10.1016/j.jep.2020.113627).
Efficacy evidence: Supplies both documented TCM cultural provenance and primary controlled rodent sleep-assay data, but transfer to oral culinary shiso in Parkinson-related insomnia remains indirect (preclinical non-PD, inhaled essential oil).
Related research: Already summarized under formal shiso follow-up notes at research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Shiso-Followup.md#steam-distilled-leaf-oil-and-induced-insomnia; complements independent oral perilla leaf extract rodent sleep studies (Kwon 2017 DOI 10.20307/nps.2017.23.1.53; Bormate 2024 DOI 10.1016/j.jff.2024.106345).
