Efficacy evidence: Customary regional medicinal/food context only; provides no clinical efficacy evidence for Parkinson's disease symptoms

In an ethnobotanical survey of northeastern Morocco (Bencheikh et al., 2024, Front Pharmacol, PMID:39318777, PMC11420034), herbarium-verified Asparagus officinalis L. (local Moroccan name: Sekoum, voucher HUMPOM898, Use Value UV=0.0558) is traditionally prepared from stems and leaves and taken orally as a decoction, infusion, or cooked food for liver-supportive and general cleansing/diuretic folk purposes. Unlike Ayurvedic nervine traditions centered on Asparagus racemosus (Shatavari) or East Asian texts using Asparagus cochinchinensis roots, this Moroccan record specifically documents culinary-species A. officinalis. No Parkinson's disease patients or controlled symptom outcomes were evaluated.
