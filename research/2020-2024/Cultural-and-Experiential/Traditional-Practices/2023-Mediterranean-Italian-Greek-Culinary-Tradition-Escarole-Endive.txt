Title: Mediterranean Italian and Greek culinary traditions of raw curly endive and cooked escarole (Cichorium endivia)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10376668/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2023
Date basis: Peer-reviewed food-science publication year (July 2023).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional bitter leafy vegetable eaten both raw in olive-oil salads (curly endive / frisee) and braised or stewed in soups and bean dishes (escarole) as part of Mediterranean digestive and healthy-aging foodways; no Parkinson-specific claim.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed Mediterranean food-science and ethnobotanical publications (Donadio et al. 2023, Antioxidants, PMC10376668; Su et al. 2002, Eur J Clin Nutr, PMID:12428183; Guarino et al. 2019, Scientifica, PMC6636500; El-Seedi et al. 2013, J Ethnopharmacol, PMID:23228916).
Efficacy evidence: Cultural culinary heritage only; does not establish clinical efficacy for raw or cooked endive in Parkinson disease.

Across Southern Italy (notably Campania), Greece, and the wider Mediterranean basin, Cichorium endivia L. is maintained in two traditional culinary cultigroups: curly-leafed endive (var. crispum, frisee), most commonly eaten raw in dressed salads, and smooth broad-leafed escarole (var. latifolium, scarola), widely consumed both raw and cooked—braised with olive oil and garlic or simmered in traditional bean-and-greens soups (Donadio et al. 2023, PMC10376668; Guarino et al. 2019, PMC6636500). Greek diaspora dietary surveys likewise document endive and chicory among traditional bitter greens (horta) paired with olive oil (Su et al. 2002, PMID:12428183), and Egyptian ethnobotanical surveys record traditional folk medicinal uses of Cichorium endivia (El-Seedi et al. 2013, PMID:23228916). While traditional bitter-green foodways value escarole and endive for appetite and digestion, this culinary heritage does not establish controlled symptom effects in Parkinson disease.
