In a 2024 field survey of households and traditional healers (11% traditional healing occupation) in Benishangul Gumuz Regional State, Western Ethiopia (Mohammed, Teferra, and Sime, Food Science & Nutrition, PMC11016411), two native Abelmoschus esculentus cultivars (Kenketse and Sharma) were documented as multipurpose staple foods and folk remedies. Pods are traditionally chopped and boiled (often ~30 minutes to retain texture and nutrients) or stewed with pulses and served with injera. Informants reported ethnomedicinal uses of okra preparations for constipation, stomach pain, back pain, diabetes, heart discomfort, wounds/cuts, diarrhea, and malaria. Across broader West African, Middle Eastern (bamia), and South Asian/Ayurvedic traditions (Elkhalifa et al., 2021, PMC7865958), okra's acidic rhamnogalacturonan mucilage is similarly classified as a cooling gastrointestinal demulcent and mild bulk-forming digestive aid. None of these customary traditions describe Parkinson's disease specifically or provide controlled outcome comparisons.
