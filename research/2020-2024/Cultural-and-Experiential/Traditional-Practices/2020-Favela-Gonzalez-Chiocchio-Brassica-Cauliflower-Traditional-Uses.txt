Reported direction: General nutritive, digestive, and restorative traditional foodway context; no traditional Parkinson-specific therapeutic claim.

Efficacy evidence: Culinary and ethnobotanical background only; does not establish clinical Parkinson symptom benefit and differs from concentrated broccoli-sprout sulforaphane supplements.

Ethnobotanical and food-science literature records Brassica oleracea var. botrytis (cauliflower) primarily as a staple seasonal cruciferous food valued for general nourishment, vitamin C, fiber, and restorative winter cooking rather than as a targeted neurological remedy. In rural Emilia-Romagna/Bologna ethnobotany (Chiocchio et al., 2024), cooked cauliflower appears in domestic nutritive foodways, while broader reviews (Favela-Gonzalez et al., 2020) note widespread culinary use of Brassica species for antioxidant and general metabolic health. Traditional culinary use does not equate to clinical testing of purified sulforaphane supplements or Parkinson symptom modification.
