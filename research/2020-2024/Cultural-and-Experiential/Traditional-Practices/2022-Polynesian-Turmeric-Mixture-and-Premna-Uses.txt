Title: Turmeric co-use and the Premna constipation entry in Raiatea and Tahaa
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Ethnobotanical interviews with traditional healers
Source: https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1729/12/5/733
Publication date: 2022-05-14
Date basis: Original paper publication. The interviews took place in 2017–2018.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Location inspected: Methods, main Table 1, and complete supplementary Table S1. Primary archive PMID 35629400 is linked below.

Community/context: Eighteen traditional healers in Raiatea and Tahaa identified plants used for conditions described in their local practices. The paper describes 63 species and focuses its main table on the most cited material.
Exposure and reported symptom: In the cystitis context, healers reported use of Malay apple leaves (Syzygium malaccense) with turmeric rhizome (Curcuma longa). The reported plant use includes both exposures and leaves no ground-turmeric-only pain comparison. The supplementary constipation record concerns Premna serratifolia. It gives no evidence involving nutmeg or culinary sumac.
Reported direction: Intended therapeutic use. Symptom changes attributable to individual plants are unreported.
Evidence and limits: The original combines ethnobotanical interviews with separate laboratory work. Laboratory findings do not measure treatment outcomes in the reported community or in people with Parkinson disease.
Authenticity: Published paper and supplemental plant identity and use table inspected. The supplement's species names and use rows were checked separately.
Efficacy evidence: Relevant customary use context. No patient-level Parkinson pain or constipation comparisons.
