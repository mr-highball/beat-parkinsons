Title: Traditional Persian Medicine uses of sweet almond (Prunus dulcis) for brain health, sleep, mood, and bowel function
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33368772/
Source kind: Traditional-practice scholarly review
Publication date: 2021
Date basis: Peer-reviewed journal publication date (Phytotherapy Research 2021; PMID 33368772, DOI 10.1002/ptr.7006); summarizes classical Persian Medicine textbooks.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional texts describe sweet almond (Badam/Lawz) as a brain tonic/nootropic, sedative-hypnotic for sleep, anxiolytic, mild laxative, and nervous-system supportive food-medicine.
Authenticity: Verified via indexed PubMed record (Karimi et al., Phytotherapy Research, 2021; DOI 10.1002/ptr.7006) and cross-cited in clinical trials (Mohajeri 2026, Ghafarzadeh 2019).
Efficacy evidence: Customary traditional-practice documentation only; classical text attribution does not establish clinical efficacy in Parkinson's disease.

Karimi and colleagues (2021, Phytotherapy Research, PMID 33368772) surveyed classical Persian Medicine texts alongside modern phytotherapy reports for sweet almond (Prunus dulcis, termed Badam or Lawz). Classical sources classify sweet almond as a nutritive food-medicine used to moisten dry temperaments, act as a brain tonic and memory enhancer (nootropic), promote restful sleep (sedative-hypnotic), ease anxiety, soften stools as a gentle laxative, and support general nerve strength. Preparation in traditional practice frequently uses peeled soaked raw kernels, sweet almond powder, syrup, or expressed sweet almond oil rather than roasted whole kernels, and historical diagnostic terms do not equate to modern Parkinson's disease.
