Title: Egyptian (Hilba), Persian (Shanbalileh), and Mediterranean traditional practices for fenugreek seeds
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9182856/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2022
Date basis: Peer-reviewed open-access review publication year (Visuvanathan et al., Plants 2022, PMC9182856; Younesy et al., 2014, PMC3955423); historical citations span antiquity to modern household use.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Favorable traditional uses for menstrual/abdominal pain, digestion, lactation, and metabolic support; no established Parkinson's disease efficacy.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed open-access ethnopharmacology and clinical background literature inspected during Round 4 review (PMC9182856; PMC3955423; PMC13388466).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional and historical ethnobotanical record only; does not constitute controlled Parkinson's disease clinical evidence.

Historically referenced in ancient Egyptian medical texts (Ebers Papyrus) and Greco-Roman herbals, Trigonella foenum-graecum seeds remain widely used across Egypt (as Hilba seed infusion/tea), Iran (as Shanbalileh seeds in postpartum dishes such as kachi and traditional analgesic remedies), North Africa, Ethiopia, and the Eastern Mediterranean (Visuvanathan et al., 2022, PMC9182856; Younesy et al., 2014, PMC3955423; Otegenova et al., 2026, PMC13388466). Documented traditional indications include relieving menstrual cramps (dysmenorrhea), aiding childbirth and lactation, soothing digestive discomfort and constipation, and serving as a general metabolic tonic, as well as kidney-warming applications for cold-related abdominal pain in traditional Chinese medicine. While modern randomized trials have examined ground seed powder for primary dysmenorrhea and standardized seed extracts for menopausal mood/sleep and adjuvant PD motor scores, traditional usage does not verify efficacy, optimal preparation (ground vs toasted), or safety in Parkinson's disease.
