Title: Mediterranean and North African culinary and herbal-tea traditions for sweet marjoram (Origanum majorana L.)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32882360/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2021
Date basis: Peer-reviewed ethnopharmacology review publication year (Bouyahya et al., J Ethnopharmacol 2021, PMID:32882360), incorporating EMA HMPC final assessment report EMA/HMPC/63479/2015 (2016) and Beniaich et al. Fez-Meknes survey (2022, PMID:34031820).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional regional use of dried aerial-part infusions and culinary fresh/dried leaves for gastrointestinal discomfort, mild nervous complaints, insomnia, low mood, and asthenia, without controlled efficacy verification for fresh culinary leaves.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed ethnopharmacology review (PMID:32882360) and European Medicines Agency assessment report (EMA/HMPC/63479/2015) with verified botanical taxonomy (Origanum majorana L., distinct from Origanum vulgare L.).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional-use documentation only; provides cultural background for Tunisian Parkinson's marjoram-tea pilot studies while underscoring the lack of controlled clinical data for culinary fresh or dried leaves eaten whole.

Sweet marjoram (Origanum majorana L., syn. Majorana hortensis) is widely used across North Africa, the Levant, and Mediterranean Europe both as a culinary seasoning (fresh and dried leaves) and as a hot-water herbal infusion for stomach pain, intestinal spasms, mild digestive complaints, hypertension, mild nervous tension, low mood, insomnia, and asthenia/fatigue (Bouyahya et al., 2021, PMID:32882360; Beniaich et al., 2022, PMID:34031820). The European Medicines Agency assessment report (EMA/HMPC/63479/2015) documents traditional herbal-tea preparations of dried flowering shoots and notes no documented overdose, mental impairment, or abuse cases in reviewed clinical safety sources. Public Parkinson's UK Community Forum searches conducted during Round 5 returned zero threads discussing marjoram. Traditional regional infusion use in Tunisia directly motivated the Chbili/Chahra clinical pilot studies of strained dried marjoram leaf tea in idiopathic Parkinson's disease (PMID:34455218; PMID:41052623), which remain distinct in botanical preparation from culinary fresh leaves.
