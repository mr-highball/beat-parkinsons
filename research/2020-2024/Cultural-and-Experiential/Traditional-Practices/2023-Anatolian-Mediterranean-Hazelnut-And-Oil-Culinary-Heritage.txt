Title: Anatolian Black Sea and Italian Mediterranean culinary traditions of raw/roasted hazelnuts and expressed hazelnut oil
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10255299/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2023
Date basis: Peer-reviewed Nutrients review publication date (Rondanelli et al., 2023, PMC10255299), cross-referenced with Black Sea cultivar studies (Oztolan-Erol et al., 2021, PMC8282208).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Customary Mediterranean and Anatolian health-promoting food tradition (raw/roasted kernels with skin and culinary oil); no specific Parkinson disease symptom efficacy claim.
Authenticity: Traceable open-access peer-reviewed review (Rondanelli et al., 2023, Nutrients, PMID:37299385, PMC10255299, DOI:10.3390/nu15112421) and Black Sea germplasm study (Oztolan-Erol et al., 2021, PMC8282208).
Efficacy evidence: Culinary heritage and bromatological matrix record only; does not establish clinical Parkinson symptom modification.

Community and practice context: Black Sea Anatolian Turkey (Ordu, Giresun, Trabzon, Samsun, Akcakoca — supplying ~56-75% of world hazelnut production via traditional cultivars Tombul, Palaz, Cakildak, Sivri) and Piedmont/central Italy (Tonda Gentile delle Langhe / Romana) within the broader Mediterranean dietary pattern.

Preparation practices and matrix separation: Rondanelli et al. (2023, PMC10255299) document traditional consumption of hazelnuts as whole raw snacks, roasted kernels (with or without the astringent brown pellicle/skin), confectionery pastes, and expressed hazelnut oil. Critically, traditional roasting alters volatile aroma compounds (increasing filbertone) and facilitates blanching/removal of the brown skin, which carries most of the kernel's condensed tannins and polyphenols, while pressing into hazelnut oil yields a high-oleic (~78-84% MUFA), vitamin-E-rich culinary oil stripped of dietary fiber and polar skin phenolics.

Evidence limits: While customary inclusion of mixed nuts in Mediterranean diets is linked to cardiovascular health, neither Anatolian nor Mediterranean culinary tradition isolates raw hazelnuts, roasted hazelnuts, or hazelnut oil as a proven Parkinson disease therapy.
