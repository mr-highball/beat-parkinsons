Rosso et al. (2024, Frontiers in Plant Science, PMC11447754) document the long-standing traditional cultivation and everyday culinary consumption of edamame (immature green vegetable soybean, Glycine max) across East Asia—most prominently boiled or steamed in the pod as a protein-, fiber-, mineral-, and isoflavone-rich snack or side dish—and its modern global adoption as a whole plant-protein staple. The record distinguishes culinary immature green edamame from dry mature grain soybeans and purified isoflavone supplements, and provides cultural foodway context without making any clinical Parkinson's symptom treatment claim.
