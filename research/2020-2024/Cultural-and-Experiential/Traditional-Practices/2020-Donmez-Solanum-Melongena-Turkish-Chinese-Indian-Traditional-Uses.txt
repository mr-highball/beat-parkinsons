Title: Ethnopharmacological record of Solanum melongena (eggplant) in Turkish, Chinese, and Indian traditional medicine
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32687958/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2020
Date basis: Peer-reviewed Journal of Ethnopharmacology publication year (PMID:32687958, DOI:10.1016/j.jep.2020.113143).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional multi-indication remedies (rheumatism, neuralgia, toothache, asthma, hemorrhoids) across Asian and Anatolian traditions; no Parkinson's-specific clinical efficacy claim.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed ethnopharmacology article (Donmez et al. 2020, Journal of Ethnopharmacology, PMID:32687958) verified via NCBI PubMed during Round 4 review, supplemented by historical Sanskrit cultivation context in Wang et al. 2021 (PMC7823263).
Efficacy evidence: Historical and traditional folk-medicine documentation only; does not establish clinical efficacy for baked, grilled, or roasted eggplant in Parkinson's disease.

Donmez et al. (2020, Journal of Ethnopharmacology, PMID:32687958) document that Solanum melongena L. (eggplant/aubergine)—cultivated across Asia for over 1,500 years since early Sanskrit agricultural records (Wang et al. 2021, PMC7823263)—has been traditionally employed in Turkish, Chinese, and Indian folk and medical systems for rheumatism, neuralgia, toothache, itching, bleeding, asthma, bronchitis, cholera, and hemorrhoids, using fruit, calyx, and methanolic/aqueous preparations. While reflecting broad traditional culinary and analgesic/anti-inflammatory folklore, these traditional accounts do not separate baked, grilled, or roasted culinary fruit or provide controlled evidence for Parkinson's motor or non-motor symptoms.
