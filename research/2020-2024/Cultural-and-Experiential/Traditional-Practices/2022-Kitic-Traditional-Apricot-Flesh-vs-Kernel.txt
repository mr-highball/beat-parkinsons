Title: Ethnobotanical and Pharmacopeial Use of Prunus armeniaca Fruit vs Bitter Kernels for Constipation and General Health
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9325146/
Source kind: Peer-reviewed ethnobotanical and pharmacognosy reviews and CAM usage surveys (PMID:35890519, PMID:30744678, PMID:38323080, PMID:32379648)
Publication date: 2022-07-19
Date basis: Primary ethnobotanical overview published 2022 in Plants (PMID:35890519, PMC9325146), synthesized with Western Himalayan Gujjar fieldwork (2019, PMID:30744678), Turkish CAM constipation surveys (2019-2020, PMID:30973435, PMID:32379648), and Chinese pharmacopeia seed review (2024, PMID:38323080).
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Favorable traditional attribution for fruit laxation, paired with strict traditional/toxicological warning separating culinary flesh from cyanogenic bitter seeds (Kuxingren / Armeniacae semen amarum).
Authenticity: Verified directly via indexed PubMed and PMC records (Kitic et al. 2022; Rana et al. 2019; Toygar et al. 2020; Tang et al. 2024).
Efficacy evidence: Low (traditional ethnobotanical documentation and patient prevalence surveys; does not establish clinical Parkinson symptom efficacy and requires strict exclusion of toxic bitter apricot kernels).

Kitic et al. (Plants, 2022; PMID:35890519; PMC9325146) review the ethnobotany of Prunus armeniaca L. across East and Central Asia, the Himalayas (documented in Gujjar semi-pastoralist fieldwork by Rana et al. 2019, PMID:30744678), and the Mediterranean/Middle East, where culinary fruit flesh is traditionally valued as a mild laxative, antipyretic, and nutritive food. Modern Turkish hospital surveys confirm apricot remains the single most frequently used dietary phytotherapy for constipation among oncology patients (39.4% of CAM users, Toygar et al. 2020, PMID:32379648) and IBS outpatients (Erdogan & Kurcer 2019, PMID:30973435). Crucially, Chinese and Korean pharmacopeias separately classify the dried bitter seed (Armeniacae semen amarum / Kuxingren; Tang et al. 2024, PMID:38323080) for cough and colon lubrication, which contains cyanogenic amygdalin capable of causing acute cyanide poisoning (Abraham 2016, PMID:25708890; Talay 2025, PMID:39207944) and must never be conflated with culinary apricot flesh.
