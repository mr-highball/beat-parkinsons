Title: Traditional Chinese and Vietnamese culinary use of boiled mung bean (lu dou) broth for clearing heat and refreshment
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9642937/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2021-12-01
Date basis: Publication date of Mehta et al. 2021 (Biotechnologia, PMC9642937) and Hien et al. 2002 (Integr Cancer Ther, PMID:14664747) documenting traditional Asian cooling and detoxification uses of Vigna radiata.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Customary summer cooling and detoxification food: boiled mung bean soup/broth is traditionally consumed in China and Vietnam for relieving summer heat and general refreshment, with no verified clinical efficacy for Parkinson fatigue or motor symptoms.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed open-access review (Mehta et al. 2021, PMC9642937) and clinical paper (Hien et al. 2002, PMID:14664747) inspected during Round 5 review.
Efficacy evidence: Traditional food-medicine homology framing only; explicitly distinguished from a retracted 2015 mouse sports-drink fatigue paper (Qi & Ying 2015, PMID:26998181, retracted 2016 PMID:28932324) and from preclinical seed-coat extracts (Chen 2026).

Across China and Vietnam, boiled whole mung bean soup or sweet broth (lu dou tang) is a longstanding summer beverage and food-medicine homology staple traditionally described as clearing heat, relieving summer fatigue, and supporting detoxification (Mehta et al. 2021, PMC9642937; Hien et al. 2002, PMID:14664747). In Beijing culinary tradition, fermented mung bean starch liquid (douzhi) is also consumed as a distinct sour beverage, separate from boiled whole mung beans. Traditional heat-clearing concepts do not constitute clinical evidence for Parkinson fatigue, constipation, or neurodegeneration; notably, a 2015 mouse swimming-fatigue article citing traditional Chinese anti-fatigue claims for a mung-bean ("green beans") sports drink (Qi & Ying 2015, PMID:26998181) was formally retracted in 2016 (PMID:28932324) and is excluded from evidence synthesis.
