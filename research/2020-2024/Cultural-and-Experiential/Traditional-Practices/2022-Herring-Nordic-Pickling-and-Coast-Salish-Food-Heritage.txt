Title: Nordic Baltic herring preservation traditions and Coast Salish Indigenous Pacific herring food heritage

Reported direction: Staple coastal protein and omega-3-rich food heritage across Northern Europe and the Pacific Northwest; no traditional Parkinson's symptom treatment claim.

Herring species (Atlantic/Baltic herring Clupea harengus and Pacific herring Clupea pallasii) represent foundational coastal staple foods across distinct hemispheric foodways. In Northern Europe and Finland, traditional preservation of Baltic herring (Clupea harengus membras) relies on salt-and-acetic-acid pickling, canning in marinades or tomato sauces, baking, smoking, and lactic fermentation such as Swedish surstromming, providing year-round protein and marine lipids (Foods 2022, PMC9222561). On the Pacific Northwest coast, ethnographic records and ancient-DNA analysis of archaeological Puget Sound faunal assemblages (910-685 cal BP to post-contact) confirm that Coast Salish Indigenous Nations—including Nisqually, Puyallup, Klallam, Twana, Skokomish, Suquamish, and Lummi communities—harvested phenologically diverse winter and spring Pacific herring and herring roe over multiple centuries as a central pillar of regional food security and social well-being (Communications Biology 2022, PMC9357025). Neither tradition attributes specific anti-parkinsonian medicinal activity to herring, though both highlight herring as a concentrated whole-fish source of complete animal protein and n-3 PUFAs.
