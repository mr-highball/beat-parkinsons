Fejes et al. (2024, Frontiers in Nutrition, PMID:39279902, PMC11392787) review the traditional culinary and folk medicinal status of raw white garlic (Allium sativum L.) across global food cultures alongside all English-language human studies restricted specifically to raw white garlic (12 clinical trials and 10 observational cohorts).

While traditional medicine systems have used garlic for over 5,000 years for cardiovascular vigor, digestive ailments, and infection resistance, the authors emphasize that culinary processing dramatically alters organosulfur chemistry (aligning with Lawson & Hunsaker 2018 PMC6073756 showing allicin bioequivalence falls to ~30% in roasted garlic and ~16% in boiled garlic) and that raw-garlic human evidence remains concentrated in metabolic/lipid/blood-pressure endpoints rather than neurodegenerative or Parkinson's disease symptoms.
