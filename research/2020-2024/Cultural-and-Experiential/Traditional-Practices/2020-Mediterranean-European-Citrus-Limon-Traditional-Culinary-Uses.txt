Title: Traditional Mediterranean, European, and Ayurvedic culinary-medicinal uses of Citrus limon (lemon)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7020168/
Source kind: Traditional-Practice
Publication date: 2020-01-17
Date basis: Publication date of peer-reviewed review by Klimek-Szczykutowicz et al., Plants (Basel) 2020;9(1):119 (PMID:31963590, PMC7020168).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Customary digestive, antiscorbutic, throat-soothing, and culinary acidulant uses documented across Mediterranean and Ayurvedic traditions; no traditional claim specific to Parkinson's disease.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed ethnobotanical and phytochemical review (PMC7020168) documenting Citrus limon juice, peel, and culinary processing stability.
Efficacy evidence: Customary dietary and folk-medicine record only; documents traditional digestive/throat contexts and confirms thermal degradation of vitamin C versus heat-stable citric acid without establishing clinical PD efficacy.

Across Mediterranean, European, and Ayurvedic food-medicine traditions summarized in Klimek-Szczykutowicz et al. (2020, PMC7020168), fresh Citrus limon fruit, squeezed juice, and zest are widely used as daily culinary acidulants, antiscorbutic foods, digestive stimulants (such as warm lemon water or peel infusions), and warm throat-soothing beverages with honey. In Ayurvedic dietary frameworks, sour citrus ('amla' taste) is traditionally used to stimulate digestive fire and salivation. Modern food-chemistry data reviewed in PMC7020168 further document that while lemon juice is rich in citric acid and vitamin C, heat and prolonged processing substantially reduce ascorbic acid content while leaving citric acid acidity intact. These traditions provide cultural and preparation context rather than controlled evidence for Parkinson's symptoms.
