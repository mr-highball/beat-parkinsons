Title: Customary culinary and topical emollient use of Corylus avellana hazelnut oil versus modern human tactile and dietary trials
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10310855/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2023
Date basis: Peer-reviewed Scientific Reports publication date (Samain-Aupic et al., 2023, PMC10310855), cross-referenced with Dumas et al. (2023, PMC10318304) and Rondanelli et al. (2023, PMC10255299).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Customary culinary oil and cosmetic/emollient carrier without demonstrated standalone neurological, tactile, or Parkinson disease symptom benefit.
Authenticity: Traceable open-access peer-reviewed primary human study (Samain-Aupic et al., 2023, Scientific Reports, PMID:37386024, PMC10310855, DOI:10.1038/s41598-023-37361-0) and compositional/dietary literature (PMC10255299, PMC10318304).
Efficacy evidence: Contextual culinary/emollient record only; topical hazelnut base oil alone did not improve finger tactile discrimination in controlled human testing, and no Parkinson disease folk-remedy tradition for expressed hazelnut oil was verified.

Community and practice context: European and Mediterranean culinary use of cold-pressed and toasted Corylus avellana seed oil as a finishing/salad oil alongside cosmetic and massage-carrier traditions valuing its light texture, high oleic acid content, and alpha-tocopherol.

Comparison with modern controlled testing: Because hazelnut oil is widely used as a topical base oil in European cosmetic and massage care, Samain-Aupic et al. (2023, PMC10310855, n=44 healthy women, 30 days) tested unperfumed hazelnut seed base oil vs the same oil plus added aromatics and found that touch detection and finger spatial discrimination improved ONLY with the perfumed oil, not with hazelnut base oil alone. Similarly, dietary crossover trials using 100% hazelnut oil as a high-oleic dietary oil (Dumas 2023, PMC10318304) found no statistically significant working-memory performance advantage (p=0.09) and did not enroll Parkinson patients.

Public community search check: Systematic searches of Parkinson's UK Discourse and HealthUnlocked community forums found virtually zero firsthand Parkinson patient discussions targeting culinary cold-pressed or toasted hazelnut oil as a symptom intervention, confirming an open evidence gap.
