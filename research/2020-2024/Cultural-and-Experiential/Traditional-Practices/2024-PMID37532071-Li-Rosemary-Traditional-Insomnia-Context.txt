Community/context: Ethnobotanical background noting widespread traditional use of Rosmarinus officinalis (rosemary) in China and Western countries for insomnia, depression, and anxiety, paired with an experimental rat study in China.
Exposure and reported symptom: Traditional indications are insomnia, depressive symptoms, and anxiety. The paired laboratory study tested low-, medium-, and high-dose rosemary aqueous distillate (hydrosol; 19 GC-MS constituents including caryophyllene and citronellol) versus blank, model, and diazepam controls in PCPA-induced insomnia Sprague-Dawley rats using open-field tests, RNA-seq, ELISA (5-HT, GABA, DA), Western blot, RT-PCR, and immunohistochemistry.
Reported direction: Traditional use is for easing insomnia, depression, and anxiety; in the rat model, all three rosemary hydrosol dose groups showed improvement compared with the PCPA insomnia model group via serotonergic synapse (HTR1A/5-HT/ADCY5/cAMP/PKA/GABAA) signaling.
Evidence and limits: Preclinical rat study using distilled rosemary hydrosol rather than culinary dried/fresh rosemary in humans or people with Parkinson disease. Authors explicitly caution that the study was conducted in rats and results may not apply to humans.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed Journal of Ethnopharmacology article (DOI 10.1016/j.jep.2023.116984).
Efficacy evidence: Documents cross-cultural traditional use and controlled preclinical rat insomnia signaling, but does not establish human Parkinson efficacy or culinary rosemary dose transfer.
Related research: Complements historical rosemary tremor texts (1597 Langham, 1664 Choice Manual), Fez-Meknes Moroccan herbalist survey (PMID 34031820), and archived rosemary Parkinson/depression literature under Source-Text/.
