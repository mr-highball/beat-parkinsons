Title: Lagos ethnobotanical interviews and mango indication boundaries
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Ethnobotanical survey of traditional practices
Source: https://www.mdpi.com/2223-7747/11/5/633
Publication date: 2022-02-25
Date basis: First electronic publication of the primary interview survey.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Location inspected: Methods and Table 1 of the original article. The study accession is documented in the linked source note.

Community/context: The authors interviewed 100 informants across five Lagos State locations, including people involved in herbal sales, traditional practice, farming and hunting.
Exposure and reported symptom: Ninety-five informants reported uses of Mangifera indica. Table 1 attributes 76.84% of the mango use reports to malaria and 23.16% to fever. Insomnia occurs elsewhere in the inventory under other taxa. The printed mango row does not report raw fruit taken for sleep.
Reported direction: No mango-specific insomnia outcome reported. The survey records intended uses for other indications.
Evidence and limits: Citation frequencies measure recall or agreement on use. No Parkinson insomnia diagnosis, therapeutic outcome or raw fruit diet comparison is reported.
Authenticity: The survey methods and printed mango row were verified against the publisher copy.
Efficacy evidence: The inventory cannot estimate an effect of mango on Parkinson symptoms.
