Title: Mediterranean, European, North African, and Persian ethnomedicinal traditions of brewed chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla) infusions
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9032859/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2022
Date basis: Peer-reviewed ethnomedicinal review publication year (Life 2022; PMID:35454969, PMC9032859), cross-checked against EMA HMPC Matricariae flos monograph (https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/herbal/matricariae-flos).
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: Customary traditional oral infusion use as a mild calming/sleep aid, digestive antispasmodic/carminative, and remedy for menstrual or gastrointestinal discomfort; separate topical oil/compress uses for skin and joint complaints.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed open-access review (El Mihyaoui et al. 2022, PMC9032859) corroborated by Dai et al. (2022, PMC9822300) and the EMA HMPC European Union herbal monograph on Matricariae flos.
Efficacy evidence: Traditional and regulatory-monograph context only; does not establish clinical Parkinson efficacy.

Dried flower heads of German chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla L., syn. M. recutita) have been brewed as hot aqueous infusions across Mediterranean, European, North African (Babounj), Persian, and Asian folk traditions for nervous restlessness, sleep support, painful menstruation, nausea, bloating, and gastrointestinal spasms (El Mihyaoui et al. 2022, PMC9032859; Dai et al. 2022, PMC9822300). The European Medicines Agency Committee on Herbal Medicinal Products (EMA HMPC) monograph for Matricariae flos recognizes traditional oral use for mild gastrointestinal complaints (minor spasms, bloating, flatulence) and topical/oromucosal uses for minor inflammation, while noting that systemic anxiety or clinical insomnia indications lack formal EU traditional-use authorization under HMPC criteria. Traditional sources frequently distinguish oral flower tea from topical chamomile oil poultices or ointments and do not assess Parkinson's disease motor or non-motor endpoints.
