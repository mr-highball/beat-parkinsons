Title: Mediterranean and Middle Eastern Culinary and Traditional Medicinal Uses of Rhus coriaria (Sumac) (Alsamri et al., 2021)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33430013/
Source kind: Traditional-practice review
Publication date: 2021
Date basis: Peer-reviewed journal publication year (Antioxidants, 2021; PMID:33430013, PMC7828031).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional culinary souring spice and folk astringent/analgesic/digestive remedy; no traditional Parkinson-specific indication.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed review in Antioxidants (2021, DOI:10.3390/antiox10010073, PMC7828031); strictly concerns edible Rhus coriaria L., not toxic North American poison sumac (Toxicodendron vernix).
Efficacy evidence: Historical and culinary context only; traditional folk uses do not establish clinical Parkinson efficacy.

Alsamri et al. (Antioxidants, 2021; PMID:33430013; PMC7828031) summarize the widespread Mediterranean and Middle Eastern culinary use of dried ground Rhus coriaria L. fruit as a tart souring spice and appetizer, alongside traditional folk-medicine applications for diarrhea, oral/gastric ulcers, hemorrhoids, wound care, and inflammatory discomfort. Culinary R. coriaria is noted for high tannin, anthocyanin, and flavonoid content and favorable dietary safety, and must be strictly distinguished from unrelated toxic North American poison sumac (Toxicodendron vernix). Publication date (2021) is separate from historical Levantine/Persian herbals; traditional reputation does not prove efficacy in Parkinson's disease.
