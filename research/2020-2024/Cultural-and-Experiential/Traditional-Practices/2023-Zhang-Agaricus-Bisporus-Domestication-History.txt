Reported direction: Neutral culinary framing (world's most widely cultivated table mushroom—champignon de Paris/white button/cremini/portobello—eaten cooked/roasted for gastronomy rather than classical neuro-medicinal use, contrasting with Reishi or Lion's Mane).

Zhang et al. (BMC Genomics, 2023; PMID:37020265; PMC10077685) analyzed mitochondrial genomes of 352 global Agaricus bisporus strains, contextualizing its ~300-year domestication history originating in France (where champignon de Paris cultivation was formally documented in the early 1700s before expanding into Parisian limestone quarries and global production). Unlike Asian medicinal fungi such as Ganoderma lucidum (Reishi) or Hericium erinaceus (Lion's Mane), A. bisporus evolved culturally as an everyday cooked or roasted culinary food, and toxicology reviews (Lagrange & Vernoux 2020, PMID:32751277) advise cooking rather than raw intake to degrade heat-labile agaritine.
