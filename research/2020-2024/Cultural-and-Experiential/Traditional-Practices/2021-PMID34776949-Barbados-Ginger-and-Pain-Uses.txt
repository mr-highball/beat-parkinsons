Reported direction: Intended symptomatic relief. The interviews record practice without testing an effect.
Authenticity: Original survey Table 3. Study participants reported a plant, intended use and preparation when ascertainable.
Efficacy evidence: Reported customary use in three rural communities. No confirmed Parkinson diagnosis, comparative pain score or observed constipation effect.

For joint or back pain, respondents reported ginger rhizome in a decoction, infusion or stew. The grated-or-whole entry belongs to the health-maintenance row. Twenty-two people mentioned ginger across all uses. Table 3 does not give the number of people in the joint or back pain row. Turmeric is also named for joint or back pain in a decoction, infusion or stew. Parsley infusion is listed for headache and clove decoction for toothache. The entries do not specify ground clove, dried parsley or ground turmeric.

The grated nutmeg infusion is attached to an upset-stomach use. The separate headache use combines nutmeg with candle grease and is applied externally. The common names Bay Leaf, Sage and Black Cumin are assigned to Pimenta racemosa, Lantana camara and Nigella sativa. Retain these as identified taxa when following the reported practices.

Open trail: Seek independent first-person or trial evidence that pairs each culinary preparation with a measured symptom. Preserve the separation between survey citation frequency and symptom response.
