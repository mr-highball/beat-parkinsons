Title: Spanish Pimenton de la Vera PDO and Hungarian sweet paprika culinary traditions distinct from hot chili
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37509899/
Source kind: Traditional practice
Publication date: 2023-07-21
Date basis: Publication date (July 2023) of peer-reviewed Spanish traditional paprika consumption study Lami et al. (PMID:37509899, PMC10379102), supplemented by Govindarajan (1985, PMID:3899517) and Aranda et al. (2026, PMID:42895087).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Everyday culinary use of sweet dried or oak-smoked Capsicum annuum powder for red color, aroma, and mild warmth (mean 154.5 g/person/year in Spain), distinct from pungent capsaicin-rich chili traditions; no traditional PD therapeutic efficacy claim.
Authenticity: Grounded in peer-reviewed European food-heritage survey (Lami et al. 2023, Foods, PMC10379102) and Capsicum food-science reviews (Govindarajan 1985; Aranda et al. 2026).
Efficacy evidence: Documents widespread culinary consumption of sweet and oak-smoked paprika as carotenoid-rich (capsanthin) food seasonings with negligible capsaicin; provides no evidence of Parkinson's symptom efficacy while noting PAH and storage mycotoxin quality controls.

Peer-reviewed European food-heritage research (Lami et al. 2023, PMID:37509899, PMC10379102; Govindarajan 1985, PMID:3899517; Aranda et al. 2026, PMID:42895087) documents the distinct culinary tradition of sweet and oak-smoked paprika (Capsicum annuum L.), notably Spanish Protected Designation of Origin Pimenton de la Vera (average Spanish consumption 154.5 g/person/year), Hungarian sweet paprika, and Balkan roasted red-pepper preparations (Ajvar). Bred and processed specifically for deep red capsanthin/capsorubin pigmentation and mild sweet or smoky aroma rather than fiery pungency, sweet paprika contains roughly two orders of magnitude less capsaicinoids than hot chili/cayenne powders (Arrizabalaga-Larranaga 2020). Traditional oak-smoke drying enriches wood-smoke phenolics while requiring careful process control for polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) and post-drying moisture control against mycotoxins (Casquete 2017). Neither culinary tradition nor modern food-heritage records establish clinical efficacy for Parkinson's motor or non-motor symptoms.
