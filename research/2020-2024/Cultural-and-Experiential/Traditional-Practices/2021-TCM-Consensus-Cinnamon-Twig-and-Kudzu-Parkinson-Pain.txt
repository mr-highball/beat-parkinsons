Title: Cinnamon twig and kudzu in Chinese expert guidance for Parkinson pain
Source: https://guideline.whuznhmedj.com/content/8280
DOI: 10.13288/j.11-2166/r.2021.23.017
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Published Chinese medicine expert consensus with a public excerpt and later confirming table
Publication date: 2021-12
Date basis: Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine, 62(23), pp. 2109–2116. The first dated consensus cited by later summaries.
Text coverage: Public consensus metadata and abstract, the publicly attributed Chinese pain passage, the 2024 English mini review and the later primary-care consensus table.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25, source and translation scope
Reported direction: The consensus recommends a choice of traditional formulas for patterned upper-body or limb pain. It supplies no measured change from a standalone ingredient.
Authenticity: The consensus title and DOI are matched to a journal record. The pain phrase is available in a dated public account that cites the consensus, and the named combination appears in the later journal table. The original 2021 PDF remains unverified.
Efficacy evidence: Professional recommendation. The twig preparation, formulation and comparator differ from culinary ground cinnamon. No effect size or new treated cohort is reported.

The Chinese pain passage lists Ge Gen Tang or Gui Zhi Jia Ge Gen
Tang (桂枝加葛根汤) as formula options. The latter name denotes a
cinnamon-twig formula with kudzu, with adjustments within the
traditional pattern. The 2024 English mini review shortens the
wording to a cinnamon-twig option. The public Chinese excerpt and
the 2026 primary-care consensus table retain the compound formula.

This describes a professional approach to a specific pain pattern,
without attributing an effect to cinnamon alone. It does not
establish culinary bark exposure, an independently measured pain
benefit, or a comparison applicable to Parkinson constipation.
