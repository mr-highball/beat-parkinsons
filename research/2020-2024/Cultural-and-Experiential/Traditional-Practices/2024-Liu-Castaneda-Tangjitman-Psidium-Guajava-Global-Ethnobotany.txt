Title: Global 44-Country, Mesoamerican Mayan, and Southeast Asian Ethnobotanical Records of Psidium guajava
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11377350/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2024
Date basis: Publication year of primary global ethnobotanical systematic review (Liu et al. 2024, PMID:39246650, PMC11377350), synthesizing companion regional surveys (Tangjitman 2015 PMC4422539; Juarez-Vazquez 2013 PMID:23665055; Castaneda 2022 PMID:34656668).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Predominantly antidiarrheal and digestive-spasm traditional uses (mostly leaf decoctions; raw fruit minor), plus Mesoamerican Mayan ethnopsychiatric/CNS citations without controlled clinical Parkinson's evidence.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed open-access ethnobotanical systematic review (Front Pharmacol 2024;15:1459066; PMC11377350) and peer-reviewed field surveys (J Ethnobiol Ethnomed 2015 PMC4422539; J Ethnopharmacol 2013 PMID:23665055; J Ethnopharmacol 2022 PMID:34656668) inspected during Round 4 review.
Efficacy evidence: Ethnobotanical survey synthesis only; documents cultural use patterns and plant-part distribution without controlled clinical proof for raw guava fruit in Parkinson's disease.

A 2024 global ethnobotanical review of 189 studies across 44 countries (Liu et al., PMC11377350) quantified traditional antidiarrheal preparations of Psidium guajava: leaves accounted for 64.3% of citations (110 citations), bark 11.1% (19), fruit 7.6% (13), and root 5.3% (9), prepared mainly as decoctions (49.3%) or infusions (18.3%) rather than raw consumption (10.6%). Field surveys confirm high local consensus for digestive complaints in Guerrero, Mexico (Juarez-Vazquez et al. 2013, PMID:23665055) and among Karen communities in Chiang Mai, northern Thailand (Tangjitman et al. 2015, PMC4422539; Fidelity Level 95.45% for diarrhea, with guava rated non-toxic). Separately, a systematic ethnomedical review of traditional Mayan medicine in Mesoamerica (Castaneda et al. 2022, PMID:34656668) listed Psidium guajava among 155 species cited for ethnopsychiatric or CNS-related complaints (including anxiety, depression, insomnia, and memory loss), though the review noted scarce clinical studies and predominantly leaf-based preclinical literature. These accounts preserve cultural hypotheses while underscoring the botanical separation between leaf decoctions/unripe astringent remedies and culinary raw ripe guava fruit.
