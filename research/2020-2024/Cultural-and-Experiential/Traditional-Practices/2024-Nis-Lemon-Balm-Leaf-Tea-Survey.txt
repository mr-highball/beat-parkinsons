Title: Traditional Ethnobotanical Applications of Melissa officinalis for Niš district
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Ethnobotanical field survey reporting traditional preparations and intended uses
DOI: 10.46793/ChemN7.1.27SJ
Source: https://www.pmf.ni.ac.rs/chemianaissensis/wp-content/uploads/filebase/v7n12024/30%20Stankov%20Jovanovic%20et%20al.%20manuscript.pdf
Publication date: 2024
Date basis: Journal issue year on the original article and publisher archive. Fieldwork occurred in 2023–2024. An exact first public day was not established.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

In a 100-person online survey from southeastern Serbia, 14 participants reported using Melissa officinalis. Table 2 describes dried-leaf tea for all reported users, sometimes alongside oil or tincture. One entry lists depression among intended uses. Other entries include calming, sleep and gastrointestinal uses. No individual demographics are needed to preserve these preparation details. The Serbian-language summary supplies the local term matičnjak for the botanical.

Reported direction: Use for relief is reported. The article does not measure a change in depressive severity.
Authenticity: Original journal body, Table 2 and publisher issue. Serbian summary is part of the same report, not an additional sample.
Efficacy evidence: Regional convenience survey, no assigned comparisons, no Parkinson cohort and no standardized before-and-after depression result.
Related research: The earlier Niš psychiatric interviews recorded separately reported symptom changes and adverse experiences during use with prescribed treatment.
Open trail: Preparation strengths and individual outcome histories remain unavailable. The reported practices identify a culturally used preparation without supplying a clinical effect.
