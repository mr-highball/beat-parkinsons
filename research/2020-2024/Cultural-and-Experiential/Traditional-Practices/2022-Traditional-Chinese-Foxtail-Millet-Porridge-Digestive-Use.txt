Title: Traditional Chinese dietary medicine use of foxtail millet porridge (xiao mi zhou) for stomach and intestinal harmony
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9442030/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2022-08-23
Date basis: Publication date of Chen et al. 2022 (Front Nutr, PMC9442030) summarizing classical Chinese dietary medicine views of foxtail millet porridge (xiao mi zhou); archaeological cultivation spans ~8,000 years in northern China.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Customary digestive and convalescent benefit: boiled foxtail millet porridge is traditionally used in northern China to harmonize the stomach and intestines, ease indigestion, and nourish weakened or postpartum individuals; no classical Parkinson-specific symptom claim.
Authenticity: Traceable open-access scientific publication (Chen et al. 2022, Frontiers in Nutrition, PMC9442030) summarizing Chinese dietary medicine heritage, inspected during Round 5 review.
Efficacy evidence: Traditional dietary classification and cultural context only; does not establish human clinical efficacy for Parkinson constipation, insomnia, or neurological symptoms.

Foxtail millet (Setaria italica) has been cultivated for approximately 8,000 years in northern China, where slow-boiled millet porridge (xiao mi zhou) is its primary culinary form. As documented by Chen et al. (2022, Frontiers in Nutrition, PMC9442030), Traditional Chinese Medicine classifies warm foxtail millet porridge as a gentle medicinal food that coordinates the stomach and intestines, tonifies deficiency, stimulates appetite, prevents indigestion, and soothes digestive mucosa during convalescence and postpartum recovery. Popular Chinese dietary traditions also associate warm evening millet porridge with calm digestion and restful sleep, though traditional energetic categories do not map directly to modern diagnosed Parkinson disease or validated insomnia scales. Controlled human trials of boiled foxtail millet porridge for constipation or PD symptoms remain absent; Chen et al. (2022) tested freeze-dried boiled foxtail millet porridge only in diphenoxylate-constipated mice.
