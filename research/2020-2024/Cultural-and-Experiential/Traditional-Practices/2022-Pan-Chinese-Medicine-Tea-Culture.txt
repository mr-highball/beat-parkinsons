Title: Traditional Chinese Tea Culture and Herbal Framing of Black and Green Tea for Calmness and Alertness
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8861626/
Source kind: Peer-reviewed cultural and historical review (PMID:35193642, PMC8861626)
Publication date: 2022-02-22
Date basis: Publication year 2022 in Chinese Medicine (PMID:35193642, PMC8861626; summarizing textual traditions spanning >3,000 years, distinct from modern publication date).
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Favorable traditional attribution (customary Chinese herbal and cultural view that drinking tea promotes a peaceful, relaxed, refreshed, and cheerful state, while cautioning against overly strong or overheated tea).
Authenticity: Verified peer-reviewed open-access cultural/historical synthesis in Chinese Medicine (Pan et al., 2022, DOI 10.1186/s13020-022-00571-1).
Efficacy evidence: Low (traditional/cultural framing and narrative review; does not establish clinical efficacy for Parkinson disease symptoms).

Pan et al. (Chinese Medicine, 2022; PMID:35193642; PMC8861626) review >3,000 years of written Chinese tea history, traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) meridian classifications, and modern global consumption where fermented black tea accounts for over 90% of Western tea sales (including Qi Men, Darjeeling, Assam, and Uva black teas). Within traditional cultural and herbal framing, tea drinking is described as fostering peacefulness, relaxation, refreshment, and mental clarity, while advising moderation and avoiding overly strong or scalding infusions. These historical accounts provide cultural context rather than clinical Parkinson symptom trials.
