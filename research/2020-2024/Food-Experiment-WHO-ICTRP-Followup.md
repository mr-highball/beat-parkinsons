# Dietary and botanical registry leads, 2020–2024

## Coconut-derived MCT as a carrier in a marigold extract trial

CTRI/2022/06/043208 was registered June 13, 2022. Anzar et al. reported a five-week study in 2023 with 30 adults with cognitive difficulties associated with Parkinson’s, stroke or both. The marigold extract capsules used coconut-derived medium-chain triglyceride oil as a carrier. The placebo capsules contained MCT oil. A count of Table 2A gives cognitive improvement by clinician classification in four of eight Parkinson-only participants in the extract group and zero of six in the placebo group. The single participant with Parkinson’s and stroke was assigned extract and classified as improved. The article's Methods say 15 participants per group while Figure 1 and the treatment tables give 16 and 14. The tables supply no executive-domain estimate and both arms used an MCT carrier. The trial leaves [0598, refined coconut oil and executive function](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0598.txt) unresolved. A separate report linked to the same registration describes safety and laboratory measures. ## Resveratrol with balance and rhythmic training

IRCT20240821062827N1 was registered December 10, 2024. This open randomized study compares an eight-week exercise and balance program with supplemental resveratrol against no added intervention in women with Parkinson’s. The stated outcomes are serum SIRT1 and an activities-of-daily-living index. The registry status shows recruitment complete, while the WHO ICTRP snapshot carries an older recruiting status. No result report was located using the identifier, title or sponsor. The study uses a formulated compound and concurrent exercise. [Primary registration](https://irct.ir/trial/78659)

## Reishi extract and endurance exercise

IRCT20200708048053N1 was registered August 12, 2020. Its contact Farshid Ganji, 24 men with Parkinson’s, four groups of six and eight-week regimen match the 2021 report by Ganji, Barari and Abdi in *Razi Journal of Medical Sciences* (27[12], pp. 120–130). The article abstract lists starch placebo, exercise plus placebo, *Ganoderma lucidum* extract, and exercise plus extract. It reports group differences in plasma nerve growth factor and brain-derived neurotrophic factor. A closely matching proceedings citation by Ganji and Barari appears under Iranian year 1398 (2019–2020). The accessible sources supply no symptom estimate, and the journal full text requires access through the indexed copy. Reishi extract differs from the button and shiitake mushroom preparations in the frozen statements. [Primary registration](https://irct.ir/trial/49485) · [Published journal abstract](https://www.magiran.com/paper/2267658) · [Proceedings index](https://elmnet.ir/keyword/میانجی-های-عصبی) · [Separate reishi case report](Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#reishi-case-report)

## Probiotic and vitamin D combination

Zali et al. reported a randomized comparison of probiotic and vitamin D capsules versus placebo in 46 participants with Parkinson’s, 23 per group, over 12 weeks. The abstract reports favorable between-group results for clinical severity, Beck Anxiety Inventory and the Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale (p < .05) along with differences in inflammatory and oxidative biomarkers. IRCT20200921048793N1 lists study coauthor Mehri Salari as contact and specifies a six-strain capsule with vitamin D3. The registry’s intervention text says four months while its outcome timing says week 12, which matches the publication. The publisher abstract leaves symptom-level effect sizes and the relative contribution of vitamin D and the probiotic unresolved. [Primary registration](https://irct.ir/trial/51104) · [2024 primary report, PMID 38805039](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38805039/)

## Folate and vitamin B12 during levodopa treatment

The authors and Prapokklao Hospital affiliation, comparison arms, and homocysteine outcome link TCTR20181221001 to Anamnart and Kitjarak’s April 2021 trial report. Forty-two levodopa-treated participants with Parkinson’s were randomized to oral folate plus vitamin B12, entacapone or no added medication. The authors reported a decrease in homocysteine in the vitamin arm (p < .001), with an inconclusive change in the entacapone arm. The comparison addresses a laboratory marker during levodopa treatment. [Primary registration](https://www.thaiclinicaltrials.org/show/TCTR20181221001) · [2021 primary report, PMID 33992189](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33992189/)

## Mint essential oil in multisensory care

IRCT20220209053981N1 was registered February 24, 2022 with a target of 70 older people with Parkinson’s. The intervention is eight sessions in a multisensory Snoezelen room, including inhaled mint essential oil with other sensory stimuli. The registry identifies the oil as mint without a species, gives a Care Dependence Questionnaire outcome and lists recruitment complete. An accession, English and Persian title, and investigator search yielded no outcome report. The design does not isolate the effect of mint. [Primary registration](https://irct.ir/trial/61896)

## Modified Ukgansan with acupuncture

Jin et al. reported results for KCT0003444 on November 10, 2022. Sixty people with Parkinson’s were randomized to receive modified Ukgansan extract granules or no added granules, with acupuncture and usual medication in both arms. Table 5 reports mean week-seven total MDS-UPDRS changes of −4.43 with the formula and −5.33 in the control group (p = .801). The between-group motor part comparison was also inconclusive (p = .666). The authors reported exploratory PDQ-39 mobility and daily-living differences in a subgroup with anxiety. Table 2 identifies nine botanicals, including immature trifoliate orange (*Poncirus trifoliata*), a different species and preparation from culinary orange. The comparison tests the full formula. [2019 registration and protocol](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-East-Asia-Registry-Followup.md#hh368-added-to-acupuncture) · [Primary article, Tables 2 and 5](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2022.1025269) · [Local CC BY source text](Source-Text/PMC9685812.txt)

## Vitamin D3 trial registration in Thailand

TCTR20240708007 was registered July 8, 2024 to compare vitamin D supplementation with no added supplementation in Parkinson’s. The 2026 article identifies this accession and reports motor, cognitive and serum vitamin D results. [Primary registration](https://www.thaiclinicaltrials.org/show/TCTR20240708007) · [2026 report](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-WHO-ICTRP-Followup.md#vitamin-d-supplementation-and-clinical-outcomes)
