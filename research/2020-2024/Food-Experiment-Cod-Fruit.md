# Food experiment evidence for cod, pineapple, plum, and cantaloupe

## Pineapple plum and cantaloupe in a Parkinson dietary intervention

Table 3 explicitly labels cantaloupe melon. The preparation and participant portions are unspecified. Figure 4 and the described Supplementary Figure S2 report UPDRS III totals at different follow-up times. Neither reports an individual fruit contrast or rest-tremor item. [Original Table 3 and Figure 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7072275/)

Hegelmaier and colleagues' February 6, 2020 study lists pineapple, plums and cantaloupe melon in the same ovo-lacto-vegetarian inpatient menu. All 16 participants received the diet and ten also had bowel cleansing. At one year the total UPDRS III improved in both groups. Part III includes hand movements, finger tapping and rest tremor, and the supplement gives individual total scores. The study supplies no item-level motor results, individual fruit portions, preparation or food contrast. Dyskinesia, fatigue and urinary urgency were outside the reported motor outcome. [Existing local primary text, Table 3 and Figure 4](Source-Text/PMC7072275.txt#L285) · [Primary article and supplements](https://doi.org/10.3390/cells9020376)

## Plum extract under paraquat and rotenone

Tambe and colleagues' July 20, 2023 study tested a *Prunus domestica* polyphenol extract on primary midbrain cultures. The extract increased dopaminergic cell survival after paraquat in Figure 2, while Supplementary Figure S1 showed an inconclusive trend under parallel rotenone exposure. The results differed by toxin condition. Neither poached fruit nor a dyskinesia endpoint was studied. [Primary article, Figures 2 and S1](Source-Text/PMC10376534.txt) · [Earlier rotenone findings](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Cod-Fruit.md#plum-and-prune-extracts-in-rotenone-exposed-neurons)

## Pineapple juice sensitivity in a veterans cohort

The August 24, 2022 Veterans Affairs study used a food sensitivity questionnaire in 266 participants with interstitial cystitis or bladder pain syndrome, 68 with other pelvic pain and 91 controls. Table 3 records 42 reports of pineapple juice sensitivity in the first group and two in each comparator group. It reports the corresponding proportions as 15%, 2%, and 2%. The item concerns juice and the questionnaire asks about sensitivity affecting multiple bladder symptoms. There is no raw fruit or Parkinson urgency episode comparison. [Primary full text, Table 3](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/133828/cdc_133828_DS1.pdf)

## Bromelain enzyme blend in urinary infection models

The October 18, 2024 paper tested a bromelain-containing polyenzymatic mixture with N-acetylcysteine. It reports changes in bacterial biofilms and inflammatory or oxidative endpoints in ex vivo bladder tissue. The preparation was a mixture and the experiments did not measure urinary urgency or Parkinson outcomes. [Primary article text](Source-Text/PMC11505172.txt)

## Melon concentrate and exercise fatigue

The February 11, 2020 SODB exercise study included healthy men who trained during the intervention. Intergroup comparisons of the Prevost physical fatigue score gave no clear difference. A treated-group downward trend had p = .068, and some oxidative markers and exercise adaptation measures differed. The supplement was a dried melon juice concentrate and the study did not involve Parkinson or raw fruit. [Primary article, Section 3.2.4](Source-Text/PMC7068528.txt) · [Dated trial registration](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Cod-Fruit.md#melon-concentrate-exercise-trial-registration)

## Melon GliSODin and fatigue in locomotive syndrome

The May 12, 2022 randomized study used a Vauclusien melon SOD extract with wheat gliadin in women with musculoskeletal mobility limitations. The 46 completers' six-month Chalder Fatigue Scale change was −3.21 with the supplement and −0.59 with placebo (p = .15). Baseline fatigue was higher in the supplement arm. Motor-function changes were inconclusive. Non-fat mass, a body composition measure, increased more in the supplement group. The study did not test rest tremor, Parkinson or raw fruit. [Primary article, Table 2](Source-Text/PMC9143343.txt)

## Cod hydrolysate and physical function in older adults

Nygård and colleagues' January 27, 2021 report compared a marine protein hydrolysate manufactured from fresh or fresh-frozen Atlantic cod fillets with placebo in 86 older adults in its baseline analysis. No clear group difference emerged for change in the Short Physical Performance Battery (p = .41), grip strength or gait speed. Neither handwritten character size nor fatigue severity was measured, and the intervention was a supplement instead of whole cod. [Primary article text](Source-Text/PMC7912527.txt) · [Dated trial registration](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Cod-Fruit.md#cod-fillet-and-cod-hydrolysate-trial-registrations)
## Pineapple intake in a urinary incontinence survey

Guedes and colleagues’ journal issue is labeled 2021, and the original records acceptance in February 2022. In Table 5, one of eight participants with mild impact and one of 12 with severe impact reported pineapple one to two times per week. The study classified impact across incontinence subtypes, including four participants with urgency and 11 with mixed incontinence. It supplies no pineapple preparation, isolated urgency episode count or Parkinson analysis.
