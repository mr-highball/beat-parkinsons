# Soybean oil comparator and clinical endpoints

## Injurious falls in a soybean oil comparator arm

Shinto and colleagues published the NCT01953705 outcomes on 1 August 2024. Participants took three Nordic Naturals soybean oil softgels or three marine omega-3 softgels daily for three years. Safety and Tolerability reports injurious falls in 14 of 51 soybean-oil participants and 17 of 51 marine omega-3 participants. One fatal fall occurred in the marine omega-3 group. The headline safety percentages count people affected. Falls were adverse events in a trial focused on brain imaging. The paper gives no comparative rate of all falls or person-time estimate, and the oil processing grade remains unverified.

The 2019 protocol excluded people with Parkinson’s disease. The 2013 registration, 2019 protocol and 2024 trial results refer to one cohort. The group counts are descriptive clinical observations about this specific comparison. A separate 2013 Alzheimer pilot reported falls in the opposite nominal direction with a soybean oil blend.

## Soybean oil in texture modified mooncake

Wang and colleagues published online on 27 December 2022. The lab tested soybean oil and gum gradients in mooncake dough. With the selected formulation, the authors report reduced hardness and adhesiveness, and a red-bean-filled mooncake rated at IDDSI texture level 4. The indexed publisher Methods names Haitong Haitong Food Group as the oil supplier and gives no process grade. The accessible primary article gives a food consistency assessment without participant swallowing-severity outcomes.

## Developmental soybean oil comparator with adult rodent locomotion

D’Avila and colleagues first published on 24 December 2020. Rat mothers received either soybean oil or interesterified fat during gestation and lactation, and male offspring stayed on the assigned supplementation after weaning. The interesterified-fat group had decreased adult locomotion and poorer early sensorimotor and motor-learning performance compared with the soybean group. The relevant oral comparator is developmental rather than a later-life intervention and no human walking speed or bradykinesia was measured.

The same group’s 2022 paper used a maternal exposure before mating, with a mixed soybean-plus-fish-oil control and no continued supplemented offspring diet. The 2022 design differs materially from the gestation-through-adulthood comparison. The university lists a 2022 thesis by the 2020 paper’s lead author, so any matching thesis data should be tracked as parallel reporting.

## Soybean oil batch analysis and exploratory endpoints

Supplement 2 to the 2024 report names four tested soybean-oil placebo lots in eTable 2 and gives trace EPA and DHA concentrations, as well as oxidation measurements. The sample assays do not identify how the soybean oil was processed. The exploratory eTable 3 reports a composite of Trail Making Test B and digit-symbol z-scores. The adjusted annual difference was −0.03844 in the marine omega-3 arm relative to the soybean-oil comparator (95% CI −0.1179 to 0.04133, p=.35). Follow-up gait estimates were unavailable in the report or supplement. ## Registered soy lysolecithin trial and placebo description

UMIN000053685, R000060435, first disclosed February 22, 2024. The official record lists protocol fixation February 17, 2017 and last follow-up August 16, 2017, more than six years before disclosure. It describes healthy adults aged 40–74 without sleep problems, two randomized capsule groups for eight weeks, a primary memory scale and mental-function VAS among secondary endpoints. The public record does not identify the placebo oil or list a separate sleepiness outcome. Its results section marks the study unpublished. The later paper identifies the soy-oil placebo and reports sleep-quality scores. ## Soybean oil comparator and subjective sleep quality

First online August 24, 2024, print issue 2025. The healthy-volunteer trial assigned 23 participants per group to soy-derived lysolecithin or capsules providing 4.2 g/day soy oil, with unspecified refining. One placebo participant withdrew and Table 4 gives n=22 and 23. Sleep-quality VAS mean ± SE was 80.8 ± 3.2 at baseline and 80.7 ± 3.6 at week eight with oil, versus 76.5 ± 4.1 and 77.5 ± 3.8 with lysolecithin. One drowsiness report occurred in the lysolecithin arm. Neither the VAS nor that event is a comparative daytime sleepiness endpoint in Parkinson disease. No oil-free control was included.
