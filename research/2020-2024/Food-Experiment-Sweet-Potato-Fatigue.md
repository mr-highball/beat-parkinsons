# Sweet potato and measured exercise outcomes

## Boiled sweet potato and estimated exercise fitness

Okuneye and colleagues’ 2024 report assigned 15 healthy undergraduates boiled sweet potato with stew, boiled plantain with stew and a glucose reference across study sessions. Table 4 gives estimated oxygen uptake of 41.44 ± 5.8 after sweet potato and 39.23 ± 8.8 after plantain. Table 6 gives p = .611. The primary abstract’s favorable statement about glucose does not have a clear sweet-potato–glucose contrast in the table. Estimated oxygen uptake from a run does not measure fatigue severity. [Author-posted paper, Tables 4 and 6](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384861190_Glycemic_Response_of_Selected_Local_Foods_and_Effect_on_Cardiorespiratory_Fitness_of_Young_Adults_in_Lagos_Nigeria)

## Steamed sweet potato in a rice cake and exercise exertion

Ishihara and colleagues’ 2020 crossover compared a rice cake with steamed and mashed Naruto Kintoki sweet potato to carbohydrate gel in six trained men. Overall perceived exertion did not clearly differ (condition p = .80, condition-by-time p = .65). The food included glutinous rice flour, sugar and water and was steamed again. The experiment measured exertion during cycling. [Primary report, Table 3](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jnsv/66/2/66_128/_pdf) · [Local original factual extract](Source-Text/2020-Ishihara-Sweet-Potato-Exertion.txt)

## Sweet potato and cycling in a graduate thesis

The May 2023 Mississippi State thesis abstract describes 13 trained men studied after 42 days of daily sweet potato intake. The institutional abstract reports no detected cycling performance difference. The attached thesis body returned 403, so the preparation, dose and nature of the comparison remain unresolved. [Primary institutional thesis record](https://scholarsjunction.msstate.edu/td/5788/)
