# Ginger constipation comparators, 2020-2024

## Topical ginger and lavender abdominal massage for constipation

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT04513093 on August 14, 2020, after its reported May 21 completion. The registered trial assigned 20 nursing-home residents to abdominal massage with ginger and lavender oils and 20 to no intervention. It lists Bristol stool form and a 16-item constipation severity measure, with no posted results. The registry gives an actual start of January 14, 2018. The publication preview describes the nursing-home application in January–March 2019, leaving the difference in date scope unresolved. [Official registration and history](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04513093) · [Official registry data](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT04513093)

Aydınlı and Karadağ published the journal report on August 20, 2022 (PMID 36058824, DOI 10.1016/j.explore.2022.08.010). Its author abstract reports similar scores at first assessment, then lower constipation severity and higher Bristol stool-form scores with massage at weeks two and four, p < .05. The accessible original gives no arm means or precise p values. A matching 2020 doctoral thesis abstract, thesis 651147, describes the same investigators, treatment and 20 plus 20 participants. It is treated as part of the same study family. The trial compares a topical combination and physical massage with no intervention, so it cannot attribute the outcome to ginger independently. Oral grated ginger and Parkinson constipation were outside the reported comparison. [Original journal abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36058824/) · [Publisher preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1550830722001525) · [Matching thesis abstract record](https://tezara.org/theses/651147)

## Registered ginger extract comparison in constipation-prone adults

UMIN000048594 first posted on August 4, 2022, the listed anticipated start date. The completed randomized, double-blind comparison assigned four weeks of ginger or placebo capsules to healthy Japanese adults with three to five bowel movements per week. The registry gives 30 as target and 32 as enrolled. The primary endpoint is bowel movement frequency in week four. Constipation Assessment Scale items, GSRS, stool form and other outcomes are secondary. No quantitative results are posted there. [Original UMIN record](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000055354) · [Public health ministry record](https://rctportal.mhlw.go.jp/detail/um?trial_id=UMIN000048594)

Takenaka and colleagues published the linked trial on May 20, 2023 in *Japanese Pharmacology & Therapeutics* 51(5), 643–653 (J-GLOBAL 202302226756444258). The sponsor reported more bowel movements than placebo in week four in its 30-person description. The I908 product-notification summary reproduced by a separate database reports 100 mg/day Ginger Extract Powder E and week-four means of 6.8 versus 5.2 movements per week. The sponsor disclosure and secondary reproduction give no exact p value. The analyzed sample, dispersion definition, full journal tables and constipation-scale comparison remain unverified. The intervention is an extract capsule, and the trial enrolled healthy adults rather than a Parkinson cohort. [Publisher issue](https://www.lifescience.co.jp/yk/yk23/yke2305.html) · [J-GLOBAL publication record](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=202302226756444258) · [Sponsor study report](https://www.atpress.ne.jp/news/355917) · [I908 filing summary reproduction](https://db.plusaid.jp/foods/I908/)

## Registered ginger extract comparison in adults with frequent loose stools

UMIN000049830 first posted on December 19, 2022, the listed anticipated start date. It enrolled healthy Japanese adults with ten to twenty bowel movements in the screening week and recurring loose stools. The randomized, double-blind design compares four weeks of ginger capsules with placebo. Week-four bowel movement frequency is primary. GSRS domains including constipation are secondary. The completed registry gives a target of 30 and enrollment of 32, with no posted outcome estimates. [Original UMIN record](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/icdr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000056747) · [Ministry summary](https://rctportal.mhlw.go.jp/detail/um?trial_id=UMIN000049830)

The linked September 20, 2023 paper in *Japanese Pharmacology & Therapeutics* 51(9), 1389–1402 (J-GLOBAL 202302227744912460) is a separate trial from UMIN000048594. The sponsor reports using 100 mg/day of the same extract powder and **fewer** bowel movements than placebo in week four. Its qualitative result concerns adults selected for frequent loose stools. The exact journal means and constipation-domain results remain unavailable. These trials studied different starting bowel patterns and do not provide a single randomized test of an interaction with baseline stool frequency. [Study organization’s publication and registration link](https://www.orthomedico.jp/results/paper/%E3%82%B8%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B8%E3%83%A3%E3%83%BC%E3%82%A8%E3%82%AD%E3%82%B9%E3%83%91%E3%82%A6%E3%83%80%E3%83%BC-e-%E5%90%AB%E6%9C%89%E9%A3%9F%E5%93%81%E3%81%AE%E6%91%82%E5%8F%96%E3%81%AB%E3%82%88-2.html) · [Publisher issue](https://www.lifescience.co.jp/yk/yk23/yke2309.html) · [J-GLOBAL publication record](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=202302227744912460) · [Sponsor study report](https://www.atpress.ne.jp/news/366567)

## Registered ginger and Zataria comparison in irritable bowel syndrome

WHO ICTRP identifies IRCT20240609062057N3 as an IBS trial from Khomein Faculty of Medical Sciences. It first registered on December 31, 2024 and lists first enrollment on December 10. The target field is 66 and the narrative mentions 96. One intervention field describes the two herbs together, while the registry’s outcome field and eventual publication identify separate ginger and Zataria groups and placebo. The later report gives a composite gastrointestinal result and leaves the item-level constipation estimate unverified. [WHO ICTRP registration](https://trialsearch.who.int/Trial2.aspx?TrialID=IRCT20240609062057N3) · [Later primary results](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Ginger-Constipation-Comparators.md#ginger-and-zataria-ibs-trial-and-registration)

## Constipation adverse events in a ginger extract versus loratadine trial

Yamprasert and colleagues randomized 80 people with allergic rhinitis to ginger extract or loratadine. Six-week Table 6 lists constipation in 0 of 36 ginger and 3 of 36 loratadine participants. Eructation was reported in 26 and 6, respectively. No inactive arm or constipation severity endpoint was included. This is a comparison of reported adverse events in rhinitis. The separate official NCT02576808 registry first posted October 15, 2015, lists the study complete and has no registry results. The journal article supplies the observed outcomes. ## Combined perilla and ginger extracts in functional dyspepsia

Di Pierro and colleagues described 65 treated adults with functional dyspepsia. Seven stopped after the first week without a response and were omitted from the final 58-person single-group analysis. Enteric tablets provided 375 mg ginger extract and 150 mg perilla extract per tablet, taken twice daily for two weeks. Table II combined diarrhea and constipation in one score. The mean was 4.7 (SD 1.4) at baseline and 1.5 (SD 1.3) at week two, p<.01 versus baseline. The text reports 38 with constipation and 15 with diarrhea, with no separate effect estimate for either subgroup. The retrospective series had no comparison group and no separately assigned ginger exposure. [Original source](https://www.mednews.care/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/effectiveness-of-a-nutraceutcal-supplement-containing-highly-standardized-perilla-and-ginger-extracts-in-patients-with-functional-dyspepsia.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2020-Di-Pierro-Perilla-Ginger-Combined-Bowel-Score.txt)
