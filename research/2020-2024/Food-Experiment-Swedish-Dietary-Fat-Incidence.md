# Dietary fat and Parkinson incidence with extended Swedish cohort follow-up

## 2022 full report

Hantikainen and colleagues published this report online on April 13, 2022. It followed 41,597 eligible participants from the Swedish National March Cohort through 2016 for an average of 17.6 years, identifying 465 diagnoses. Baseline questionnaires estimated nutrient intake from food composition tables. Main models compared quartiles and adjusted for potential confounders. [Publisher article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10654-022-00863-8) · [Archived full text](Source-Text/PMC9288363.txt)

The adjusted highest versus lowest quartile estimate for saturated fat was HR 1.41 (95% CI 1.04–1.90, trend p=0.03). For total fat the adjusted estimate was HR 1.29 (95% CI 0.96–1.74, trend p=0.08). Monounsaturated and polyunsaturated fat and the substitution analyses did not show statistically significant associations. After excluding cases within five years of baseline, the saturated-fat extreme-quartile estimate was HR 1.39 (95% CI 1.01–1.92, trend p=0.07). The observational analysis addresses incident diagnoses and has no standalone food-specific or Parkinson symptom effect estimate. [Original Methods, Results and Table 2](Source-Text/PMC9288363.txt)

The 2018 presentation used follow-up through 2010, different exposure categories and reported 292 cases. Its significant associations for total fat and for replacing polyunsaturated with saturated fat did not persist in the longer 2022 analysis. Count both reports as one cohort when comparing independent evidence. [Earlier conference report](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Swedish-Dietary-Fat-Incidence.md)
