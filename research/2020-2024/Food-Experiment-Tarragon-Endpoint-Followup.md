# Tarragon endpoint follow-up

## Tarragon ethanol extract during voluntary wheel exercise

Allerton and colleagues randomized 52 diet-induced obese mice to voluntary wheel running or sedentary conditions with and without ethanolic tarragon extract PMI-5011. The primary paper, first published online in 2020, reports greater fat oxidation with extract during speed- and distance-matched wheel-running bouts. The comparison addresses exercise metabolism without a time-to-exhaustion or clinical fatigue result. A 2021 corrigendum is indexed and the publisher did not supply its text during this check. This provides separate indirect evidence for 0822. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000002516) · [Corrigendum record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34759194/)

## Angelica and tarragon blend in a mouse grip test

Korean patent publication KR20240032222A reports in Example 3 that a 7:3 blend of *Angelica gigas* and *Artemisia dracunculus* extracts was given orally to mice in the dexamethasone muscle atrophy model. Forelimb peak grip decreased in the dexamethasone-only group and was described as increasing with each dose of the blend. Figure 6 and Example 3-4 describe the apparatus and comparison. The in vivo grip experiment lacks an isolated tarragon arm and does not measure fine motor dexterity. It is a competing preparation and indication lead for 0504 with limited attribution.
