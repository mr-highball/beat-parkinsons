# Mango bark blend and exercise performance

## Mango bark and flower extract in a resistance-training trial

Salter and colleagues published this randomized trial on May 3, 2024. The supplement combined extracts of *Mangifera indica* stem bark and *Sphaeranthus indicus* flower heads, not mango fruit. Researchers randomized 120 previously untrained healthy young men to 425 or 850 mg of the blend with one resistance-training set, placebo with one set, or placebo with two sets. Ninety-nine completed the eight-week trial, with 23–26 per group.

For bench-press one-repetition maximum at day 56, the 425 mg group lifted an adjusted 10.6 kg more than the one-set placebo group (95% CI 2.6–18.6; p = .003); the 850 mg group lifted 13.8 kg more (95% CI 5.7–21.8; p < .001). Bench repetitions until failure also differed among groups at day 56 (p = .003). These are exercise performance measures, not a validated subjective-fatigue scale. Neither component was tested alone, and the study did not involve Parkinson disease or edible mango.

The 2021 IUPHAR abstract Sub-Code 1223 describes the same 120-person design, doses, exercise arms and shared investigators, making it a likely earlier report of this trial; it did not give results. A 40-person trained-cohort study described separately in the article should not be combined as the same sample.
