# Japanese diet and oral-function measurements

## Gummy chewing test and one-month diet history in Ishikawa

Ushimura and Sakurai’s August 31, 2024 paper compared ten adults with Parkinson’s and 24 comparison participants, aged 70–80. All ten Parkinson participants had unrestricted oral intake on the Functional Oral Intake Scale. Investigators measured tongue pressure and scored how participants chewed a commercial gummy jelly. Median chewing score was 2 versus 6 on a 0–9 scale (p = .046). Median tongue pressure was 33.8 versus 36.5 kPa (p = .423). This was a chewing test, without a food-specific aspiration outcome. [Primary report and publication dates](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsdr/28/2/28_90/_article/-char/en) · [Full methods and tables](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsdr/28/2/28_90/_pdf/-char/ja)

The BDHQ questionnaire captured a month of usual intake. The paper analyzed 45 energy-adjusted nutrient estimates. In the ten Parkinson participants, lower tongue pressure correlated with higher polyunsaturated fat intake (Spearman r = −.745, p = .013), and lower chewing scores correlated with higher soluble fiber intake (r = −.790, p = .006). The paper reports many correlations at a nominal .05 threshold and the design cannot determine temporal direction. The Tokyo government’s description of the BDHQ lists a pooled tuber item and a pooled cooking-oil item. Published Ishikawa tables provide no individual food data or baked-potato and swallowing comparison.
