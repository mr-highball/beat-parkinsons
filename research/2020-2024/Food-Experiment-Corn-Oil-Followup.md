# Corn oil and Parkinson’s symptom follow-up

## Seated blood pressure during the Parkinson public-speaking trial

Faria and colleagues used an oral corn-oil placebo and the same carrier in the cannabidiol arm of a Parkinson crossover study. The protocol measured wrist systolic and diastolic pressures and pulse around simulated public speaking. Participants remained seated for the speech. The authors reported no statistically significant differences for these physiological measures. The protocol does not supply posture-provoked blood pressure change or corresponding lightheadedness results. The public-speaking task elicited anxiety and examined tremor and tapping, with no intelligibility rating. Use this study for the orthostatic lightheadedness trail (0172), and retain the existing local tremor and tapping interpretation. [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269881119895536) · [University copy](https://amparo.numec.prp.usp.br/media/amparo_responde/papers/Resposta%2053%20-%20O%20Cannabidiol%20e%20o%20tremor%20no%20Parkinson.pdf) · [Prior local note](Food-Experiment-Oil-Followup.md#corn-oil-capsules-in-cannabidiol-trials-of-tremor-and-sleep)

## Adverse-event reporting in the Parkinson CBD sleep trial

The Almeida 2021 randomized trial enrolled 33 people with Parkinson’s and REM sleep behavior disorder. In the associated University of São Paulo thesis, the English manuscript appendix lists a report of dizziness in the CBD group. The report gives no standing provocation, timing in relation to posture, blood pressure change, or corn-oil-specific comparison. The shared corn oil capsule vehicle and related adverse-event reporting make this a follow-up lead for claim 0172. The thesis and journal report describe the same trial. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.28577) · [University thesis](https://teses.usp.br/teses/disponiveis/17/17140/tde-05072021-154535/publico/CARLOSMAURICIOOLIVEIRADEALMEIDAco.pdf)

## Cooking corn oil and seated blood pressure in Brisighella

Cicero and colleagues grouped community participants by their predominant reported seasoning and cooking fat and recorded seated blood pressure after five minutes of rest. Corn oil users had lower pressure than users of predominantly animal fats. Reported oil use was habitual and self-reported, the comparison was observational and standing blood pressure and lightheadedness were outside the measured endpoint. The finding gives a hemodynamic lead for 0172 without resolving its direction. [Primary text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7600239/) · [Local primary article text](Source-Text/PMC7600239.txt)

## Dietary corn oil and rat submandibular flow

Escandriolo Nackauzi and colleagues fed 36 male Wistar rats corn oil before continuing that diet or replacing its oil with olive or chia oil. They collected saliva from cannulated submandibular ducts. At day 1 the three group means were close. At day 30, the corn, olive and chia oil groups averaged 4.68, 6.08 and 7.82 μL/min, respectively. The public abstract reports higher flow in chia versus corn (p<.001) and chia versus olive (p=.038). It gives no corn-versus-olive test or matched oil-free contrast. Dietary replacement, gland secretion and intact rats limit applicability to the proposed reduction in Parkinson drooling, which also depends on oral handling and swallowing. [Primary report abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32436164/)

## Corn oil capsules and functional mobility in older Chinese adults

Xu and colleagues reported a secondary analysis of a randomized trial in 200 older Chinese adults. Over six months, the 4 g/day corn oil comparator group's mean Timed Up and Go moved from 12.08 to 12.37 seconds, while the fish oil group's time moved from 12.17 to 11.83 seconds (time-by-group p<.001). Timed Up and Go incorporates standing, walking, turning and sitting. It supplies neither stride length nor a count of falls. The relative change can reflect the benefits of fish oil, whose effects were the study objective. The combined outcome is a mobility lead for claims 0324 and 0546. The article lists registrations ChiCTR-TRC-14005084 and ChiCTR-IOR-16008435. [Primary report, Table 4](https://doi.org/10.1093/ageing/afac274)

## Registered corn oil vehicle in the insomnia pilot

ANZCTR first posted ACTRN12620000070932 on January 29, 2020. Both arms use corn oil, with cannabidiol added to the active arm. The record describes 200 mg in 2 mL with three weeks of dosing and gives primary endpoint timing based on two active weeks. The trial publication reports 150 mg in 1.5 mL for two randomized weeks after a one-week oil run-in. Public information does not resolve the amendment timing. [Official protocol](https://anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?ACTRN=12620000070932) · [Local record](Source-Text/2020-ACTRN12620000070932-Corn-Oil-Insomnia.txt)

## Corn oil capsules in a fish oil sleep comparison

In the 2022 double-blind trial, Yokoi-Shimizu and colleagues randomized 66 healthy adults reporting poor sleep to DHA/EPA fish oil or corn oil capsules for 12 weeks. Mat-measured sleep efficiency at baseline and week 12 was 95.6% to 96.0% with fish oil and 96.3% to 93.7% with corn oil. The week-12 comparison (32 per arm) was p=.018. The sleep initiation and maintenance questionnaire comparison was inconclusive (p=.351). The comparison estimates the two assigned supplements against each other. It gives no demonstrated effect of refined corn oil on Parkinson insomnia. [Original Tables 4 and 6](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/14/19/4136) · [Local text](Source-Text/2022-Yokoi-Shimizu-Corn-Oil-Sleep.txt)

## Corn oil vehicle in the randomized insomnia comparison

Narayan and colleagues randomized 34 participants after a run-in with corn oil alone. Thirty-two completed the study and the pilot report analyzed 30, 15 per arm. The Insomnia Severity Index means moved from 18.6 to 11.6 for CBD and 17.4 to 9.7 for corn oil, with a week-two contrast of −1.9 points (95% CI −5.0 to 1.1). Objective actigraphy sleep efficiency favored CBD at week two by 6.85 percentage points (SE 2.95). Both arms used corn oil and no oil refining details were reported. The design cannot attribute the observed changes to the oil itself. [Original Methods and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11063694/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2024-Narayan-Corn-Oil-Insomnia.txt)

## Cognitive companion of the insomnia pilot

The August 2024 first publication DOI 10.1007/s00213-024-06674-x is a second report from ACTRN12620000070932. The original supplement reconciles 44 starting the run-in, 34 randomized, 32 completing and 30 analyzed. The cognitive tests gave no clear treatment effects. Its Methods contain a dose-unit error, while 1.5 mL at 100 mg/mL and the abstract agree with the main report's 150 mg. Neither report isolates a corn oil effect. [Original companion and supplement](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00213-024-06674-x) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2024-Narayan-Corn-Oil-Cognitive-Companion.txt)

## Resistance exercise trial and unreported PSQI

Dalle and colleagues' first report on December 7, 2020 gives institutional identifier S61809, directly matching NCT03986307. Both trial arms used resistance training for 12 weeks and continued the assigned fish oil or 3.3 g/day corn-oil capsules for 14 weeks. The complete original and seven-page supplement contain no result for the registered Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index. This gap leaves the intended sleep comparison open. Neurodegenerative disease was excluded. [2020 primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7907485/) · [Original supplement](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC7907485.1/glaa309_suppl_supplementarty_material.pdf) · [Local text](Source-Text/2020-Dalle-Corn-Oil-PSQI-Reporting-Extract.txt) · [2019 registration note](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Corn-Oil-PSQI-Registration.md)

## Corn oil vehicle held constant in the phase 2a sleep comparison

First published June 8, 2023. The phase 2a report identifies pharmaceutical-grade corn oil as the common vehicle for AEF0117 and its matching placebo in cannabis use disorder. Extended Data Figure 7 gives treatment p=.0071 for actigraphy snooze duration and p=.0237 for number of wake bouts. Figure 8 gives no clear subjective waking-often difference between either dose and placebo despite an interaction. Refining is unreported. The comparison holds corn oil constant and gives no independent corn-oil or Parkinson insomnia result. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10287566/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2023-Haney-Corn-Oil-Vehicle-Sleep-Extract.txt)

## Registered phase 2b corn oil comparator and sleep scale

First posted April 12, 2022. A separate 333-participant phase 2b study compared AEF0117 dose groups with corn-oil placebo for 12 weeks in cannabis use disorder. The Medical Outcomes Study Sleep Scale is an Other outcome. Completed, without structured registry results. The sponsor later released a comparison. The trial does not give an oil-free arm or enroll people for Parkinson insomnia. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05322941) · [Local text](Source-Text/2022-NCT05322941-Corn-Oil-Sleep-Registration.txt)

## Mixed corn and canola oil control during bed rest

First posted January 11, 2023. The older-adult bed-rest protocol compares ketone monoester with an energy-matched control containing corn oil, canola oil and fructose. Sleep quality and disturbances are secondary measures. The completed record has no structured results. The comparator combines oils, their processing is unreported and the protocol does not isolate corn oil or study Parkinson insomnia. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05679596) · [Local text](Source-Text/2023-NCT05679596-Mixed-Oil-Sleep-Registration.txt)
