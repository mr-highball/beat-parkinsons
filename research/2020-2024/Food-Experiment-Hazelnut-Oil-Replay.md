# Hazelnut-containing exposures and measured neurologic or oral outcomes

## Whole hazelnuts on a Parkinson diet menu with total motor scores

Hegelmaier and colleagues offered whole hazelnuts in the nuts, seeds and kernels group of Appendix A. The separate oil list names olive, pumpkin seed, walnut and sesame oils. Six people with Parkinson’s disease received a multi-food vegetarian diet and ten received the diet with bowel cleansing. The paper names facial expression, rest tremor and rigidity among the clinical assessments included in UPDRS III. It reports improvement in the composite in both groups at one-year follow-up and a separate supplementary figure for immediate follow-up. No item-level results for these three signs or individual hazelnut consumption were published. ## Hazelnut spread carrier and measured wrist and digit function

Milton and colleagues assessed fine forepaw and wrist function in rats after partial cervical spinal cord injury. The oral carrier was Nutella, described as a hazelnut spread thinned with sunflower oil. It was shared by the groups receiving a drug in the carrier and relevant vehicle controls. The Irvine, Beattie and Bresnahan cereal-eating scale rated the shape of the forepaw grip and dexterous digit and wrist movements. The combination of the oral inhibitor and an injected peptide improved that score against control by 17 weeks after injury. The preparation was a mixed carrier, and no comparison isolated hazelnut oil, verified cold pressing, measured handwriting or used a Parkinson population. ## Hazelnut seed oil in bench mouthwash formulations

İnal and colleagues bought *Corylus avellana* seed oil and tested it alongside cinnamon leaf oil and a cellulose-based formulation in a January 2024 preprint. The vendor and species are reported. The authors discuss cold pressing in the introduction, while their materials do not verify pressing or roasting for the purchased lot. The selected formulation series was characterized by viscosity, stability and cell-based testing. The 2026 companion study measured formulation wetting on excised sheep tongues. No human saliva production, drooling, swallowing or speech loudness was assessed. ## Abdominal pain in two hazelnut food allergy cases

Bentrad and colleagues described a 13-year-old after hazelnut-and-almond cake and an 18-year-old after a hazelnut cookie and a peanut candy. Both experienced abdominal pain, urticaria and loss of consciousness during acute reactions. The second had a reported blood-pressure drop. The clinicians found sensitization to hazelnut and birch PR-10 proteins and attributed both cases to hazelnut. These are two conference case observations with complex foods and coexposures. There was no graded oil exposure, comparison group, standardized pain score, postural challenge or Parkinson cohort. [Original P192 case report](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.waojou.2020.100294)
