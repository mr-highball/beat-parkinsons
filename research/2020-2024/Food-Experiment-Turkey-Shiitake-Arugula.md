# Turkey, shiitake, and arugula evidence

## Fresh arugula within a nitrate-rich vegetable crossover

Ashworth and colleagues included fresh arugula with salad, lettuce and beetroot in a randomized crossover in 30 healthy young adults. Both vegetable and beetroot juice periods lowered post-lunch pressure, with no difference between those intervention periods and no clear fasting pressure change. The comparison estimates a dietary package under an acute schedule. It reports neither standing hypotension nor falls after eating arugula. [Original report, Methods and Table 1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32386891/) · [Author full text](https://eatrightmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Nitrate-Rich-Vegetables-Aug-2020.pdf) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2020-Ashworth-Arugula-Nitrate-Factual-Extract.txt)

## 2020 Rucola on a bundled Parkinson diet menu

Hegelmaier and colleagues listed rucola in Appendix A of a vegetarian diet pilot. Sixteen people with Parkinson's received the diet, and ten also received bowel cleansing. The paper lists facial expression among the UPDRS III examination items and reports aggregate results. It did not identify the arugula cultivar, specify raw preparation or report an arugula-specific contrast on facial expression, falls, fatigue or smell. [Existing local primary text, Appendix A](Source-Text/PMC7072275.txt)

## 2021 Acoustic speech measures after levodopa

Cavallieri and colleagues retrospectively analyzed off- and on-medication testing in 50 people with advanced Parkinson's. Maximum phonation time increased after levodopa in the women studied. Other acoustic effects were limited, and axial dyskinesia was associated with poorer on-state voice measures. The comparison studied medication state, not food exposure, and the measured acoustic features do not isolate functional speech intelligibility after roasted turkey. [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC7892955.txt) · [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2020.616062)

## 2021 Shiitake puree and gum printing for texture modification

Liu and colleagues tested printed shiitake preparations with arabic gum, xanthan gum, and kappa-carrageenan. Xanthan formulations and one kappa-carrageenan formulation reached the food texture tests for IDDSI Level 5. Some arabic-gum preparations did not meet the tested dysphagia classifications. The intervention was a modified puree and gum product. The authors measured material characteristics rather than clinical swallowing after roasted shiitake. [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC8465407.txt) · [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.3390/foods10092189)

## 2021 French shiitake dermatitis surveillance update

The French poison-centre network reviewed 125 exposures during 2014–2019 and classified 59 cases as shiitake dermatitis. The agency bulletin describes reports after raw or undercooked shiitake and food preparation details. The journal report was published in 2022 and uses the same exposure period and case counts. The surveillance concerns skin outcomes and does not measure Parkinson tremor or postural instability. [2021 primary agency bulletin](https://vigilanses.anses.fr/sites/default/files/VigilAnsesN13_March2021.pdf) · [2022 primary journal report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35404185/)

## 2023 Shiitake extract in neuronal senescence cells

Apparoo and colleagues measured ergothioneine across 14 mushroom species and selected shiitake for the cellular experiments. The ethanolic extract improved survival and reduced oxidative and senescence signals in HT22 cells exposed to tert-butyl hydroperoxide. The study used an extract and a general cellular stress model with no Parkinson patient, prepared shiitake exposure, dexterity, tremor or balance outcome. The paper first appeared electronically in 2023. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38036238/)

## 2024 Lentinan in alpha-synuclein experiments and Parkinson model mice

The investigators tested isolated lentinan in amyloid and alpha-synuclein aggregation assays, nematodes and mouse models. The paper reports improved behavioral deficits in the Parkinson-model mice and cites pole testing in its supplement. The accessible summary does not quantify a human tremor or dexterity outcome, and lentinan is a constituent rather than cooked or roasted shiitake. [Primary article and supplement listing](https://doi.org/10.1021/acschemneuro.4c00164)

## 2024 Erucin and movement responses in a polyglutamine worm model

The study found improved touch responses and less polyglutamine aggregation in affected nematodes treated with erucin. Wild-type touch responses did not change. The authors cite glucoerucin in rocket leaves as the compound's precursor. This is a distinct polyglutamine model, with no arugula feeding or Parkinson symptom outcome.
