# Parkinson feeding cases in the 2020 JSMCN supplement

## Vomiting and aspiration during tube-feeding changes

A 79-year-old woman with Parkinson’s and severe hiatal hernia experienced aspiration and choking with cardiac arrest during admission. Later attempts at enteral feeding were followed by vomiting and repeated aspiration pneumonia. The team changed to concentrated formula plus a viscosity-adjusting product and started mosapride. Vomiting decreased and enteral nutrition resumed. The report gives no named obstructing food, formula ingredient, or separate comparison for the thickener or medication. The Bristol stool scale on the same page belongs to a separate lactic-fermentation study. [2020 O-098, PDF p117](https://www.eiyou.or.jp/gakujutsu/23th/pdf/jsmcn_Vol23.Supplement_vo.pdf#page=117)

## Oral jelly and Ensure Hi during home nutrition care

A man in his seventies with about 20 years of Parkinson’s began home nutrition support weighing 35.1 kg. He initially took small amounts of jelly-texture food by mouth and received Ensure Hi through gastrostomy. During follow-up, oral energy intake rose from about 500 to 1,400 kcal/day. Weight reached 53.5 kg before a later sepsis admission, during which it was 44 kg. The team reduced the supplement as oral intake and weight increased. The abstract names neither a jelly ingredient nor a food-specific swallowing outcome. A separate case on the page describes packaged and delivered foods in a man with Parkinson’s and Lewy body dementia, with no quantified bowel response. [2020 P-153 and P-156, PDF p197](https://www.eiyou.or.jp/gakujutsu/23th/pdf/jsmcn_Vol23.Supplement_vo.pdf#page=197)
