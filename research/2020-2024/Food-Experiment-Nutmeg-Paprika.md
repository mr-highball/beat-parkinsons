# Nutmeg and paprika evidence

## Paprika xanthophyll exercise and cognition registrations

UMIN000055963 first disclosed October 28, 2024 an open randomized crossover of paprika xanthophyll against no treatment in healthy college students, with exercise oxygen consumption, heart rate, Stroop and Trail Making as primary measures. The register gives 25 enrolled and shows no posted results. The corresponding published article reports 21 analyzed and explicitly names the registration. [Official registry](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000063959) · [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu17172780)

UMIN000050969 first disclosed October 31, 2024 a four-arm randomized trial of paprika xanthophyll, nicotinamide mononucleotide, both or placebo in male athletes. It records 273 enrolled, 219 included in the intention-to-treat set, 194 in the full analysis set and 139 per protocol. The results were posted January 22, 2026 and link the December 2025 online publication. The publication describes collection in 2023, earlier than the registry's stated public disclosure. UMIN000049372 lists matching doses and a closely matching title but a separate ID and a December 31, 2024 disclosure. The Japanese ministry portal marks this second entry discontinued. Its administrative relationship to the published trial is unresolved. Count the paper and UMIN000050969 together. [Completed trial and posted results](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/icdr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000058095) · [Discontinued record](https://rctportal.mhlw.go.jp/detail/um?trial_id=UMIN000049372) · [Discontinued record history](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/icdr_e/ctr_his_list.cgi?recptno=R000056134)

## Nutmeg extract and task behavior after rotenone

Vijayan and colleagues’ 2020 report tested orally administered nutmeg seed extract in a rotenone rat model. The authors report changes in rewarded T-maze, Morris water maze and elevated-plus maze transfer-latency measures. These tasks combine motor, learning and exploratory performance. Group labels in the figures and methods need reconciliation before drawing finer conclusions. [Primary report, original pp. 5441–5447](https://velammalmedicalcollege.edu.in/college/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/2020-Names-of-the-indexing-databases.pdf) · [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.26452/ijrps.v11i4.3173)

## Nutmeg extract and motor and mood assays after rotenone

A 2020 abstract by Kanagasabapathy and colleagues reports better motor performance after nutmeg seed extract in rotenone-exposed rats using open-field, beam-walking and hanging tests. A 2021 abstract from the same group reports lower forced-swim immobility and higher sucrose intake after oral nutmeg seed extract in rotenone-exposed rats. The abstracts provide no clinical bradykinesia, gait freezing, depression or apathy estimates. They share investigators and a toxin model with the memory report and a 2022 thesis. The available abstracts and repository metadata do not establish whether the cohorts overlap. [Institutional packet reproducing the two primary abstracts](https://velammalmedicalcollege.edu.in/college/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/2020-Names-of-the-indexing-databases.pdf) · [2020 motor DOI](https://doi.org/10.31838/ijpr/2020.12.03.176) · [2021 nonmotor DOI](https://doi.org/10.31838/ijpr/2021.13.01.152) · [Thesis repository](https://shodhganga.inflibnet.ac.in/handle/10603/412614) · [University thesis metadata](https://indcat.inflibnet.ac.in/index.php/thesesrequest?id=th_455135)

## Nutmeg extract and motor assays after haloperidol

Palupi and Fekhayanti’s March 2024 study compared orally administered nutmeg seed ethanol extract with controls in a haloperidol BALB/c mouse model. With five mice per group, the highest extract group showed increased rotarod fall latency and cylinder movement (reported p < .05). These measures concern motor coordination and spontaneous movement in a pharmacological model. Neither directly measures clinical bradykinesia, gait freezing or rest tremor. [Primary text](Source-Text/2024-Palupi-Nutmeg-Haloperidol-Mice.txt) · [Primary paper in the university repository](https://eprints.uad.ac.id/61857/1/3.Palupi_citra.pdf)

## Nutmeg extract and essential oil in mouse mood assays

The 2022 Japanese pharmacology study reported lower tail-suspension immobility after nutmeg extract, without increased locomotor activity. The 2023 essential-oil screen reported lower tail-suspension immobility and greater exploration of open arms after intranasal nutmeg oil. These are behavioral assays in mice with different preparations and routes. [2022 primary full text](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/bpb/45/6/45_b21-01059/_html/-char/en) · [2023 primary full text](Source-Text/PMC10215971.txt)

## Nutmeg infusion and insomnia in a student comparison

The June 2024 Airlangga author report describes 42 female students split into 21-person groups, one receiving a powdered nutmeg seed infusion for two weeks. It reports a within-group change on an insomnia rating scale (p < .001) but gives no numerical between-group effect and limited assignment detail. It describes a two-arm, two-week design, while the 2017 Airlangga thesis abstract describes a one-arm, one-week design. Neither report tested Parkinson insomnia or spice used in food. [University author report](https://vokasi.unair.ac.id/pengaruh-seduhan-biji-pala-myristica-fragrans-houtt-dalam-mengatasi-insomnia-akibat-stres-pada-mahasiswi-surabaya/) · [2017 thesis note](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Nutmeg-Paprika.md#powdered-nutmeg-infusion-and-student-insomnia)

## Inhaled nutmeg oil and EEG sleep in eight adults

Supantini and colleagues’ August 2024 uncontrolled before–after study reports shorter EEG sleep latency, longer sleep duration and increased sleep efficiency after inhaled nutmeg aroma (paired p < .001 for each). The eight adults underwent no placebo comparison and the exposure was inhaled. [Primary article and results](https://journal.maranatha.edu/index.php/jmh/article/download/8854/2924/37864)

## Acute delirium after nutmeg ingestion

The 2023 case report describes severe agitation and delirium after excess nutmeg ingestion in a 19-year-old. The patient required intubation and recovered during observation. This describes an acute toxic exposure. [Primary full text](Source-Text/PMC10615644.txt)

## Upper limb tremor and dry mouth after accidental nutmeg overdose

The 2024 case report describes a previously healthy adult who accidentally put excess nutmeg in food and developed upper-limb tremor, dizziness and dry mouth. The patient also developed hyponatremia after drinking a large volume of water. The reported tremor had no Parkinson rest-tremor characterization. Dry mouth was reported by the patient and salivary flow and drooling were unmeasured. [Primary full text](Source-Text/PMC11571034.txt)

## Saliva incorporation while eating ground chili

A 2023 controlled oral-processing study reported that adding ground dried chili increased the amount and rate of saliva incorporated into the test food bolus while shortening oral exposure. This is a food-route measure for a pungent chili. The study did not measure drooling. [Primary full text](Source-Text/PMC10569983.txt)

## Mace milk preparation in uncontrolled primary insomnia

Kumar and colleagues published on 2023-06-26 a single-arm prospective study registered as CTRI/2018/09/015865. Thirty-two completed the mace aril milk preparation. The report records 25 participants improved on the difficulty-falling-asleep item, one unchanged, over the initial 30 days. The participants had primary insomnia, with a neurological cause excluded. The authors’ follow-up intervals are inconsistent between abstract and methods. Mace is the aril rather than ground nutmeg seed.
