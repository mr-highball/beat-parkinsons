# Ginger Pain Comparators

## Registered crossover of ginger and fennel for menstrual pain

The registry plans 378 participants, a randomized crossover with participant, investigator and assessor blinding, and three treatment conditions. It names a Neotea Thirikadum capsule described as Zingiber officinale, fennel and gram-flour placebo. The named study product formulation is not specified beyond this identification. Enrollment is listed from July 1, 2020. The linked 2026 paper reports 384 participants and uses both crossover and separate-group descriptions. ## Steamed ginger extract and postpartum pain

Jaiarree and colleagues report 99 women after first vaginal delivery, 33 in each steamed ethanolic ginger extract, placebo and paracetamol group. Perineal pain mean ratings for ginger versus placebo were 3.97 versus 6.30 at 24 hours, 2.48 versus 6.09 at 48 hours, and 1.42 versus 2.61 at 72 hours. Each overall comparison is reported at p<.001, with favorable post-hoc ginger comparisons. Uterine comparisons were inconclusive. The Methods describe ginger assignment in even weeks and placebo or paracetamol in odd weeks. The open design and allocation schedule leave time-related confounding possible. The trial used steamed extract, with no Parkinson cohort. [Original source](https://www.jmatonline.com/PDF/334-340-PB-13831.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/Jaiarree2023-Steamed-Ginger-Postpartum-Pain.txt)

## Gingerol enriched extract and mechanical pain in rats

Shen and colleagues assigned 36 male rats across sham and spinal-nerve-ligation groups, with two groups receiving a supercritical-fluid ginger extract standardized to 20% gingerols. The Methods describe four weeks of administration and the abstract describes five weeks for the overall study. Treated injured rats had higher mechanical withdrawal thresholds than untreated injured animals at the end. Figure 1 supplies the comparisons with no exact means table. The extract and animal nerve injury differ from grated ginger and Parkinson pain. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11118883/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/PMID38790607-Gingerol-Spinal-Nerve-Pain.txt)

## Steamed ginger extract and mild knee osteoarthritis

Baek and colleagues report 100 people with mild knee osteoarthritis randomized to steamed ginger extract GGE03 or placebo for 12 weeks. The author abstract says VAS pain and K-WOMAC scores favored the extract. The checked publisher body routes remained gated, leaving analysis denominators, numerical estimates and registration unresolved. The knee trial is distinct from the separate GGE03 dyspepsia trial and uses a processed extract.
