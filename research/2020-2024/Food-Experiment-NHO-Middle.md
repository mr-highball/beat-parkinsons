# Follow-up to Japanese National Hospital Organization swallowing sources, 2020–2024

## 2021 and 2022 rice meals and chewing speed in Fukuoka

Sano and colleagues' June 2021 Japanese Society of Gerodontology e-poster P一般-004 measured chewing in 24 self-feeding Parkinson inpatients recruited at Fukuoka University Hospital between September 2019 and August 2020. Chewing speed differed across mild and moderate motor impairment groups (p=.04), and in the mild group it was correlated with tongue pressure (r=.70, p<.01). The poster did not identify meal ingredients. [Primary e-poster](https://confit.atlas.jp/guide/event-img/gero32/1OnDemand112-132-04/createpdf/sub), [official meeting chronology](https://www.gerodontology.jp/event/congress/).

The December 9, 2022 article included 30 Parkinson inpatients from September 2019 through March 2021 with the same institution and ethics protocol H20-03-005. This indicates likely overlap with the earlier poster and the individual linkage is unreported. An additional comparator group comprised two people with PSP and seven with MSA-C. The authors recorded chewing during standard Japanese rice set meals, soft rice set meals or rice gruel set meals and illustrated these in Figure 1. They compared by motor severity rather than meal assignment. Parkinson patients in the mild and moderate groups had median chewing speeds of 77.0 and 66.9 chews/min (p=.04). Across the 30 PD patients, higher motor score correlated with lower chewing speed (rho=−.48, p<.01) and longer mealtimes (rho=.43, p=.01). Tables give no chewing or swallowing outcome by rice preparation or other food component. Rice grain variety and a baked potato bolus are unreported. [Primary article and Figure 1](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2022.1062134/full), [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36570461/).
