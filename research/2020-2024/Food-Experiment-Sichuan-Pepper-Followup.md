# Sichuan pepper follow-up

## Red and green pepper extracts in a Chinese stress-model experiment

Tan Bangyin and colleagues compared extracts from red *Zanthoxylum bungeanum* and green *Zanthoxylum schinifolium* in a 45-mouse chronic-restraint-stress experiment reported April 1, 2022. Five groups contained nine mice each and included stress and unstressed controls and a sertraline arm. The red-pepper extract reduced the percentage of time immobile during forced swimming. The green-pepper extract changed both the open-field and forced-swim outcomes. These were concentrated preparations evaluated by behavioral tests in a stress model. The experiment did not measure clinical depression after eating toasted pepper or enroll people with Parkinson’s. It provides two species-specific model comparisons for 0707. [Chinese primary report, methods and figures](https://www.aydxb.cn/upload/2024/07/17/20240717115303_479116.pdf) · [Institutional metadata](https://ir.kmmu.edu.cn/Document/Detail/9EF827713919B32A8830D24ED9862FD0.html)

## Sichuan pepper fruit essential oil in a separate mouse stress model

Tang and colleagues’ September 20, 2022 study administered an essential oil of *Zanthoxylum bungeanum* fruit orally to mice undergoing chronic unpredictable mild stress. In Figure 3, all three tested doses shortened immobility in forced swimming, while the lowest dose did not significantly change either sucrose preference or tail-suspension immobility. These assays track different features of stress-associated behavior. Neither the essential-oil route to exposure nor the stress model reproduces eating toasted pepper in Parkinson’s. This is another independent context for 0707. [Primary local full text, Figure 3 and Results](Source-Text/PMC9530639.txt) · [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2022.999962)

## Abdominal pain in a placebo-controlled chronic constipation trial

Manabe and colleagues reported a randomized double-blind trial in February 2023. Sixty-three participants with persistent chronic constipation were assigned to four weeks of daikenchuto or placebo. Figure 4 reports improvement of abdominal pain with the formula relative to its baseline. The placebo group did not show that within-group improvement. The primary endpoint was overall treatment efficacy. The reported abdominal-pain finding is a secondary outcome in chronic constipation after a combination formula with Japanese pepper. It provides indirect context for 0983 alongside the null IBS trial. [Primary report, Figure 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10037039/)

## Walking, insomnia and depression in a Japanese Parkinson case

An August 30, 2024 report describes a patient with advanced Parkinson’s, constipation, impaired walking and severe rigidity at baseline. The report identifies daikenchuto as the administered formula and documents daily rehabilitation after admission. The clinical timeline and text on pp. 42–43 describe improvement in depression, anxiety and insomnia at about one week. The patient could walk unaided at four weeks, and the reported Hoehn and Yahr stage went from IV at two weeks to III at four weeks. The contemporaneous rehabilitation, Parkinson’s treatment and combined formula prevent attribution to its Japanese *Zanthoxylum piperitum* component. No standalone freezing count, repeat rigidity-scale result or dyskinesia assessment is provided in this timeline. The symptom observations are relevant to 0250 and 0707. The motor description supplies adjacent context for 0038 and 0456. [Primary case report, pp. 42–43 and Figure 1](https://doi.org/10.57364/kmnn.9.1_41) · [Earlier catalog entry for the same case](Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#daikenchuto-and-constipation-in-a-single-parkinsons-case)

## Chronic constipation under the complete Daikenchuto formula

Manabe and colleagues randomized 63 adults with chronic constipation in 2023 to the complete Daikenchuto product or placebo. The primary overall treatment-effect rating favored Daikenchuto (1.7 ± 1.2 vs 1.0 ± 1.2, p=.021). The main paper reports better stool form and some lower gastrointestinal symptom measures. This constitutes evidence for the formula in chronic constipation, with no isolated Zanthoxylum or toasted-pepper arm. It supplements the earlier uncontrolled Parkinson observations. [Local factual text](Source-Text/2023-Manabe-Daikenchuto-Constipation-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Original trial](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgh3.12870)

## Conflicting immobility reporting in a climacteric mouse model

Song and colleagues described five groups of ten and administered a Sichuan pepper polyphenol extract in an ovariectomy and drug model. Approximate Figure 2 bars for low, middle and high extract groups are 105, 72 and 63 seconds, compared with 57 in negative controls and 118 in normal controls. The Results describe longer immobility with extract and the Discussion describes shorter immobility. The axis gives generic time and the Methods describe time until resting, while Figure 3 gives no clear voluntary activity comparison. These discrepancies leave the direction of the behavior in the model unresolved. No human Parkinson or toasted-spice comparison was tested. [Original article and figures](https://tjpr.org/admin/12389900798187/2020_19_6_22.pdf) · [Local text](Source-Text/2020-Song-Sichuan-Pepper-Immobility-Contradiction-Extract.txt)

## Topical mixed Sichuan pepper plaster and quality of life

First posted April 11, 2024. A traditional topical acupoint plaster contains ground Sichuan pepper mixed with other herbs and petroleum jelly. Primary outcomes concern pelvic pain. EQ-5D includes a combined anxiety/depression item among secondary quality-of-life measures. The recruiting trial has no structured results, isolated pepper arm, confirmed toasting or Parkinson population.
