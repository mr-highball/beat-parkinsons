# Black pepper and clinical outcomes

Review completed on 2026-11-14 (US Eastern).

## Registry for oral ground pepper added to turmeric

NCT05206266 was first posted on 2022-01-25. Its current enrollment is 30 adults with chronic pain. The detailed sequence descriptions specify oral turmeric with black pepper and turmeric alone, in randomized order, with one week between the active exposures. The summary also names placebo in its dose-assignment wording, while the detailed sequences and final paper specify the two active conditions. The version-history API returned HTTP 403. The registry has no posted tabular outcomes. See the 2026 article for what the investigators reported administering and measuring. [ClinicalTrials.gov chronic-pain record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05206266), [local text](Source-Text/NCT05206266-Black-Pepper-Pain-Registry-Extract.txt).

## Inhaled black pepper oil during a calculation task

Dilrukshi and colleagues published their controlled aroma exposure on 2024-01-02. Twenty healthy men experienced black pepper essential-oil aroma, ginger essential-oil aroma and scentless dipropylene glycol in counterbalanced order on separate days. The investigators reported no significant calculation performance difference. Mean self-rated concentration during the task was 53.4 (24.8 SD) on a 0–100 scale with pepper and 50.1 (22.9 SD) with dipropylene glycol. The aromas were perceptible and the study's autonomic stress response endpoints are separate from attention task performance. [Dilrukshi aroma article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10759736/), [local text](Source-Text/PMC10759736-Black-Pepper-Aroma.txt).

The same crossover used ginger essential oil as a reference aroma alongside a scentless control. During the arithmetic task, mean VAS nervousness was 35.0 with ginger and 32.4 with the scentless control, with no clear subjective difference among conditions. The task-related heart-rate increase was 46.8% smaller with ginger than with scentless control (p = .028). Acute autonomic changes during inhalation and Parkinson anxiety severity following oral grated ginger are distinct measurements. [Primary report, Methods and Table 3](Source-Text/PMC10759736-Black-Pepper-Aroma.txt)

## Three-botanical Kleeb Bua Daeng formula and sleep quality

Kessara and colleagues reported 50 adults with insomnia in the January–April 2021 issue. The participants received a four-week oral course of Kleeb Bua Daeng, a formulation including *Piper nigrum*, *Centella asiatica* and *Nelumbo nucifera*. The mean PSQI was 12.22 (SD 3.12) at baseline, 8.66 after one week, and 5.30 (SD 2.61) after four weeks. No concurrent comparator, PD subgroup or isolated analysis of pepper was reported. Reported reactions included urinary frequency, dizziness, hypotension, constipation and gastrointestinal disturbance. [Kessara Kleeb Bua Daeng article](https://he01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JTTAM/article/view/242718), [local text](Source-Text/Kessara-Kleeb-Bua-Daeng-Sleep-Extract.txt).

## Registered leaf tea and chamomile comparison

TCTR20241101001 gives November 1, 2024 as its registration date and November 7, 2023 as its enrollment date. The completed record planned 30 people with primary insomnia. It specifies coarsely ground, dried pepper leaves in 2 g tea bags, with chamomile flowers as an active comparator. ISI scores were planned at days 7, 14 and 21, with PSQI and stress as secondary outcomes. Its qualitative results field bears October 31, 2024, one day before the recorded registration date, and includes no numeric arm results. The 2025 article gives the measured ISI and PSQI comparisons.
