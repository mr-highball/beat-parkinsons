# Preparation chemistry and oral eugenol behavioral outcomes

## Eugenol in dried Guatemalan allspice fruit oil

Hydrodistillation of dried Guatemalan Pimenta dioica fruit yielded 1.51% essential oil by weight on average (reported variability ±0.26%). Gas chromatography–mass spectrometry assigned 65.9% of fruit-oil composition to eugenol, with other constituents present. Those figures describe the extracted volatile oil and its chromatographic composition. A dietary ground fruit exposure has a different matrix and dose. This connects the chemical name in the rodent papers with the culinary species for claims 0807 and 0809. Regional papers and the Spanish names pimienta gorda and pimienta de Jamaica also distinguish Pimenta dioica from related species. ## Capsaicin concentrations in hot and sweet smoked paprika

Arrizabalaga-Larrañaga and colleagues measured capsaicinoids and carotenoids in products from different origins and flavor classes. Supplementary Table S1 labels La Vera VH1–15 and VS1–15 as smoked. Measured capsaicin concentrations were 42.4–1,020 mg/kg in the hot VH samples and 0.8–7.6 mg/kg in the sampled sweet VS products. The same labels describe powders with different capsaicin exposures. This primary chemistry comparison supports keeping cultivar and pungency separate when using isolated capsaicin as a related lead for smoked paprika. The study supplies no anxiety or Parkinson outcome. ## Oral isolated eugenol and mouse behavioral safety outcomes

Dhara and Tripathi evaluated oral eugenol over 14 days in Swiss albino mice at 7.34–70 mg/kg in a safety study. The listed behavior assays include novelty-suppressed feeding, novel object recognition, tail suspension and social interaction. Their primary abstract reports no significant behavioral effects across the study and no changes in food or water intake. It supplies no arm-specific behavioral effect estimates. The study offers a null animal comparison for the isolated component under these conditions. It did not model Parkinson depression or administer ground allspice. [Primary indexed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32027395/) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/1440-1681.13276)

## Mature allspice fruit oil and acute toxicity outcomes

Pacheco-Hernández and colleagues first published electronically November 27, 2024. Their study separates mature *Pimenta dioica* fruit and leaves, with voucher FCME 18114, and hydrodistills each plant part. The drying step applies to recovered oils. The mouse experiment evaluates acute toxicity of those oils. Its measures leave visual hallucinations and handwriting after ground culinary allspice untested.
