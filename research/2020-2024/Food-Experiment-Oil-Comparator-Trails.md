The official ACTRN12617000027314 record gives July 16, 2021 for both the initial data-sharing statement and the supplied results. An alternate same-record view displays the data-sharing heading and an explicit statement that no supporting documents were provided. ## Frying frequency and functional measures in a Brazilian Parkinson cohort

Almeida et al. published the full cross-sectional report January 30, 2024. It reports the same ethics approval, 20 Parkinson participants, 20 controls and estimated AGE means as the earlier proceedings. The groups were paired by age and sex. The nutritionist consulted records for portion size and cooking methods and estimated dietary AGE over a 24-hour, a 48-hour and a usual-intake recall. In Table 1, frying is recorded as **days of the week**. The Parkinson group reported 2.00 ± 1.92 days versus 0.65 ± 0.88 among controls (p=.01). The published variable has no oil species, preparation, dose, heating history or indication of reuse. This was a group comparison by Parkinson diagnosis. The paper does not give a within-Parkinson analysis of frying days versus a motor symptom. [Primary article, Methods and Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10844933/) · [Local licensed full text](Source-Text/PMC10844933.txt)

The authors assessed Part III UPDRS, Short Physical Performance Battery, grip strength, cognition and mobility. The Parkinson group had lower mean dominant handgrip (18.8 versus 27.1 kg-force, p=.02) and Short Physical Performance Battery scores (8.5 versus 10.0, p=.01). For the 20 Parkinson participants, the paper reports a negative association of grip strength with estimated total daily dietary AGE (r=−.59, p<.05). The preliminary report printed −.26 for the corresponding relationship. The reports share observations and should count as one study. Their publications provide no reconstruction of the difference. Dietary AGE estimates cover all recalled foods and do not provide a separate estimate for frying days or an individual oil. [Primary article, Figure 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clinsp.2023.100320) · [2019 preliminary evidence](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Oil-Comparator-Trails.md#preliminary-report-on-dietary-glycation-and-handgrip-in-parkinson-disease)

## Cold pressed topical coconut oil for childhood bedwetting

Dehghanpour et al. randomized 120 children aged 6–14 with monosymptomatic nocturnal enuresis in Yazd, Iran. The article reports 104 included in the analysis. One group applied cold-pressed oil from fresh coconut topically and the other used topical paraffin. Table 2 gives 1.81 versus 3.38 mean enuresis episodes in the reported eighth-week frequency comparison. The difference was −1.577 episodes (95% CI −2.532 to −0.622, p=.001). Applying coconut oil to the skin was a different exposure route. The trial explicitly excluded children with urgency or abnormal voiding frequency and counted bedwetting. It supplies no estimate for oral virgin coconut oil or urgency in Parkinson disease. [Primary report, Methods and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9516406/) · [Local licensed full text](Source-Text/2022-Dehghanpour-Coconut-Topical-Enuresis.txt)

## Virgin coconut oil alongside levodopa in a rat experiment

Shehata et al. first published this four-arm study July 21, 2020. Forty adult rats received control conditions, oral virgin coconut oil, levodopa/carbidopa or the oil with levodopa/carbidopa over 30 days. The authors describe aggression and changes on open-field testing after levodopa and attenuation of some adverse findings with oil co-treatment. The public primary abstract does not provide an abnormal-involuntary-movement or dyskinesia rating, and it describes a model of drug-related toxicity in adult rats. The publisher’s publicly retrieved article material did not allow checking all figures and behavioral measures. The study does not establish the claim’s direction for clinical dyskinesia. [Primary publisher report](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopha.2020.110473) · [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32707436/)

## Parkinson voice study with unspecified dietary fat sources

Koyuncu et al. first published their randomized dietary comparison September 6, 2020. Sixty-eight of 74 enrolled patients completed a three-month assessment. Voice Handicap Index-10 totals changed from 21.2 ± 4.9 to 5.3 ± 1.2 in the ketogenic group and from 22.2 ± 5.1 to 20.9 ± 4.8 in the regular-diet group. The full methods describe a ketogenic protocol generally and do not list specific oils or their refining methods. VHI-10 records self-perceived voice burden. A standardized intelligibility or acoustic comparison was unavailable. [Primary publisher article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13760-020-01486-0) · [Readable primary paper](https://www.academia.edu/114148434/Effect_of_ketogenic_diet_versus_regular_diet_on_voice_quality_of_patients_with_Parkinson_s_disease)

## Self-reported voice handicap under a whole-diet intervention

Koyuncu and colleagues’ table reports Voice Handicap Index means of 21.2 to 5.3 over three months in the ketogenic arm and 22.2 to 20.9 in the regular-diet arm, with 34 completers per group. These are self-reported handicap scores. The intervention is a whole diet, no rye-specific food intake is given, and voice sound pressure or acoustic volume is not reported. [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13760-020-01486-0) · [Retrievable original body](https://www.academia.edu/114148434/Effect_of_ketogenic_diet_versus_regular_diet_on_voice_quality_of_patients_with_Parkinson_s_disease)
