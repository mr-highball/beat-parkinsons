# Watermelon seed, colocynth and exposure distinctions

## Watermelon seed in an experimental three-seed mixture

The three-seed mixture used almond, peanut and watermelon seed extracts in a chlorpromazine rat model. The authors report less catalepsy and higher measured activity in mixture-treated animals. The design leaves watermelon-specific effects and raw flesh ingestion unresolved. ## Bitter colocynth and motor behavior in rats

The rat study used bitter colocynth, Citrullus colocynthis, sometimes called desert watermelon. Its aqueous and ethanolic extracts were tested in a drug-induced movement model. The frozen watermelon exposure names a different species, Citrullus lanatus. [Original source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356214693_Investigation_of_Neuroprotective_and_Antiparkinson%27s_Activity_of_Citrullus_colocynthis_Fruit) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2021-Nimra-Fatima-Colocynth-Motor-Assays-Extract.txt)

## Acute drooling after a snack containing watermelon seed paste

A toddler developed excess drooling in an acute reaction after a mixed snack containing watermelon seed paste. Previous watermelon flesh was reportedly tolerated. The authors suspected seed allergy based on the exposure and skin testing. The report leaves the culprit unconfirmed by oral challenge.
