# Nutrition and swallowing studies traced through bibliographies, 2020–2024

## Example protein redistribution menu and measured outcomes in Milan

Gatti and colleagues enrolled 66 Parkinson outpatients and 58 controls for a cross-sectional study. Table 1 gives an example menu for a 70 kg patient and lists tea or coffee at breakfast, fruit, bread or toast, and several dinner options. The table supplies no participant-level food assignments or intake for those examples. The study describes dietary recall and measures total nutrient intake. Table 2 gives 0.93 versus 1.06 m/s mean gait speed in Parkinson and control groups (p = .035) without linking this outcome to an individual food. [Primary article, Methods and Tables 1–2](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/NHA-200083) · [Institutional publisher PDF and license](https://air.unimi.it/handle/2434/978328) · [Licensed local text](Source-Text/2020-Gatti-Nutri-Park.txt)

Among 57 levodopa recipients, Table 4 compares 15 people classified as following a protein-redistribution diet with 42 who were not. A cross-sectional motor score divided by reported years of disease was lower in the former group (mean 2.41 versus 4.38, p = .023). The dyskinesia, fluctuations and falls comparisons were inconclusive. The progression index is a surrogate based on one assessment, and a motor-score mean in Table 4 is inconsistent with the displayed median and range. These results leave any effect of a menu item, preparation or beverage on an individual Parkinson symptom unresolved. [Primary article, Table 4 and Discussion](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/NHA-200083)

## Bolus-specific aspiration during a Hamburg stimulation crossover

Pflug and colleagues published this randomized crossover on April 8, 2020. Eleven of 15 Parkinson participants completed the full protocol, and four withdrew during combined stimulation following reported adverse effects. The fixed endoscopy sequence included pudding, two water presentations, a 20 g biscuit and 28 g of buttered bread. Table 3 gives participant-level worst Penetration–Aspiration Scale scores by preparation and stimulation condition. Recounting scores of 6–8 gives water aspiration in 1/11 with stimulation off, 2/11 with subthalamic stimulation and 2/11 with combined subthalamic and nigral stimulation. Biscuit aspiration appears in one participant with stimulation off and none in the stimulation conditions. Bread and pudding had no scores in the aspiration range in these completers. [Primary article, Methods and Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261764/#acn351027-tbl-0003)

Nine patients had moderate or severe bread residue and eight had moderate or severe biscuit residue under subthalamic stimulation. The authors found no clear within-person change by stimulation condition. The preparation counts describe this selected cohort and fixed protocol. The study tested stimulation and did not include potato. [Primary article, Figure 2 and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261764/)

## Dietary recall and swallowing scores in Burgos

Rivadeneyra and colleagues assessed 43 people with Parkinson disease and 21 family controls using 24-hour dietary recall, body composition, activity and the Eating Assessment Tool (EAT-10). Table 1 reports EAT-10 medians of zero in both groups (p = .15). Dietary tables aggregate energy, protein, fiber, water and other nutrients. Table 2 compares motor scores and body composition by EAT-10 classification. These analyses do not link an individual ingredient or its preparation to the swallowing scores.
