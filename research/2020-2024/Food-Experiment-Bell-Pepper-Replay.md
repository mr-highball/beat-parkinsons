# Bell pepper source follow-through

## Sweet peppers and older-adult sarcopenia in the Tianjin journal report

Wang et al. first published the 2,451-person Tianjin cross-sectional analysis on March 26, 2021. The food-frequency questionnaire asked separately about sweet pepper and chili over the preceding month. It did not specify preparation. The investigators measured usual-pace four-meter walk time and dynamometer handgrip as components of sarcopenia, along with appendicular muscle mass. The 394 cases represented 16.1% of the sample. [Primary journal report](https://www.aging-us.com/article/104168/text) · [Licensed plain text](Source-Text/PMC8034967.txt)

For sweet pepper, Model 4 adjusted odds ratios for the sarcopenia composite were 0.73 (95% CI 0.56–0.96) at ≤1 time per week and 0.66 (0.45–0.95) at ≥2–3 times per week versus almost never (trend p<.01). The abstract and Table 2 assign these values to sweet pepper, while the surrounding prose mislabels them as chili. Model 4 chili odds ratios were 0.73 (0.55–0.97) and 0.60 (0.39–0.90), respectively. The paper did not report separate sweet-pepper effects on the gait and grip components. Table 1's gait and grip means are by sarcopenia diagnosis. The same-cohort 2019 abstract reports a nonsignificant sweet pepper result in a different analysis sample, and the 2015–2019 same-program grip-only analysis reports null sweet pepper trends. The publications do not establish a grilled bell pepper effect on walking or hand dexterity in people with Parkinson disease. [2019 abstract](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Bell-Pepper-Replay.md#tianjin-conference-report-of-sweet-peppers-and-sarcopenia) · [Earlier Tianjin grip study](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Bell-Pepper-Outcomes.md#sweet-pepper-intake-and-handgrip-in-tianjin-adults)

## Parkinsonism symptom outcomes and grouped vegetable questions

Fox, Park and Mischley published this analysis of 1,205 self-report parkinsonism participants on December 6, 2022. Supplement Table S1 lists handwriting or typing, walking and slowness, daytime sleepiness, and motivation or initiative. The Mediterranean diet question in Table S2 asks about more than two daily vegetable servings including at least one raw or salad portion. Table S3 contains the broader MIND Other Vegetables item. The 2015 original MIND cohort scoring paper included red and green peppers in its other-vegetables category. The 2022 study questionnaire displays a grouped question and does not identify a pepper intake item. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9739738/) · [Official supplement archive](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC9739738.1/nutrients-14-05185-s001.zip) · [Local main text](Source-Text/PMC9739738.txt) · [Local supplement text](Source-Text/2022-Fox-MIND-MEDI-Supplement.txt) · [2015 MIND scoring paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4581900/)

Table S4 estimated a difference of −190 points (95% CI −262 to −119, p<.001) in total PRO-PD for the MIND other-vegetables item and −95 points (−156 to −35.5, p=.02) for the Mediterranean vegetable question including a raw serving. Models had age, gender, income and years since diagnosis as covariates. Item-level dietary regressions target the overall symptom score. Specific symptom estimates by named vegetable, modeled gait speed, and handwriting size were unavailable.

## Two trials of oral capsiate and timed running

Costa et al. published their 12-man randomized double-blind capsiate trial on May 1, 2020. After 12 mg capsiate, mean 400 m completion time was 66.4 ± 4.2 vs 67.1 ± 4.8 seconds with placebo (p=.046), and 3,000 m time was 893.9 ± 46.8 vs 915.2 ± 67.6 seconds (p=.015). [Costa original trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7241615/)

Von Ah Morano et al. published a separate 21-man crossover December 24, 2020. Two 12 mg capsiate doses gave a total of 24 mg. Mean 10 km running time was 45.07 ± 6.41 vs 45.13 ± 6.73 minutes with placebo (p=.828). The 2022 meta-analysis classifies that protocol as 10 km cycling after a single 12 mg dose in its summary table. Use the original study for activity and total dose. Both are isolated-constituent studies in physically active adults with running endpoints. [Von Ah Morano original trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7823530/) · [Licensed plain text](Source-Text/PMC7823530.txt) · [Secondary comparison table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9655695/)

## Capsinoids and upper-body resistance performance

De Moura et al. reported that 6 mg of oral capsiate increased total bench-press weight in 20 resistance-trained men (2,454.6 ± 448.6 vs 2,354.7 ± 458.6 kg with placebo, p=.039). The 12 mg dose did not improve the result. In a separate triple-blind trial published online in 2023, 25 trained adults received 12 mg dihydrocapsiate or placebo. Bench-press throw peak velocities were 2.82 ± 0.77 and 2.82 ± 0.74 m/s. Estimated bench-press one-repetition maxima were 99.47 ± 43.09 and 99.60 ± 43.34 kg. Published comparisons were all p≥.703. The different nonpungent constituents and exercise tests provide adjacent motor evidence, with no estimate for hand dexterity, grilled bell pepper or a Parkinson sample. [2022 capsiate trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9458288/) · [2023 dihydrocapsiate trial](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1080/17461391.2023.2229854)

## Dihydrocapsiate in canola oil and perceived exertion

The 2022 ACTIFOX crossover included 24 sedentary men with overweight or obesity. The participants cycled for 60 minutes after 12 mg dihydrocapsiate or placebo. The authors reported no difference in perceived fatigue and did not show the rating data. Each active capsule contained purified dihydrocapsiate in canola oil. Placebo capsules used microcrystalline cellulose. This compares formulations under exercise rather than isolating cooked canola oil, raw bell pepper or Parkinson fatigue. [Primary ACTIFOX article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9302013/) · [Licensed plain text](Source-Text/PMC9302013.txt)

## Capsinoid journal report and muscle recovery

Liao and colleagues’ 2020 journal abstract reports a nine-person crossover comparing 12 mg of isolated capsinoids and placebo after cycling with the same meal. The authors report higher fat oxidation and inconclusive glycogen restoration. The 2018 preprint and 2019 registry have markedly similar sample and protocol details.
