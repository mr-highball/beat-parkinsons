# Turmeric formulations and walking, 2020–2024

## Curcugen and two walking measures in knee osteoarthritis

The independently randomized study first appeared in December 2021. The 40-meter time change did not clearly differ between curcumin and placebo (p=.604). The six-minute distance increased from 512.3 to 530.18 m with curcumin and decreased from 525.81 to 521.07 with placebo (Table 4 p=.013). The text gives a different p value for this endpoint. Endurance over six minutes and short-course walking speed are distinct. [Original trial, Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8746505/) · [Licensed source](Source-Text/PMC8746505.txt)

The separate small C3 Complex study reported 400-meter gait-speed changes numerically favoring placebo with no posted between-arm test. This extract was also tested outside Parkinson disease. [Official result and same-cohort article](Food-Experiment-Turmeric-Followup.md#posted-curcumin-walking-and-cognitive-outcomes)

## Water-dispersible turmeric extract and 80-meter walking time

Thanawala and colleagues studied a water-dispersible turmeric extract for 90 days in adults with chronic knee pain. The mean 80-meter fast-walk time change was −11.0 seconds (SD 26.1, n=49) versus −6.8 (SD 23.3, n=47). The reported nonparametric comparison was p=.0011. Medians were −0.2 versus 0 seconds. Baseline pain-related mobility and the formulated preparation limit extrapolation to Parkinson and fresh turmeric. The article's biomarker statement and later pain-data corrigendum do not alter the timed walk table. [Original 2021 report](https://www.dovepress.com/article/download/65012) · [Source summary](Source-Text/2021-Turmeric-WDTE60N-Gait.txt)
