# Mustard seed and caraway research

## Caraway as a dried odor in Parkinson disease

Baert’s 2021 dissertation and released data and the 2022 paper describe the same Belgian cohort. The article reports no clear difference in caraway identification. Counting correct Karwij labels in the released Kruidengeuren worksheet found zero of 34 Parkinson participants and five of 53 controls. This is a low-recognition item in both groups. The samples were dried spices presented for sniffing. Toasting and consumption were not tested. The earlier Ljubljana panel supplies a separate multiodor discrimination result. ## Caraway extract and carvone in separate mouse activity tests

Zrouri and colleagues’ oral caraway polyphenol fraction increased open-field tile crossings and time in the center. It also reduced forced-swim and tail-suspension immobility. Wojtunik-Kulesza and colleagues found that separately injected S-(+)-carvone reduced spontaneous movement after single or repeated exposure in mice. The experiments used different preparations, routes and tests. The behavioral measures provide no direct estimate of freezing episodes, stride length, fatigue or apathy. [2021 primary article, Figures 4–7](Source-Text/PMC8000502.txt#L123) · [2024 primary article, Table 2](Source-Text/PMC11434264.txt#L124)

## Mustard constituent and elicited rat swallows

Hossain and colleagues delivered allyl isothiocyanate topically to swallowing-related regions in rats. The compound triggered more reflex swallows up to the tested response peak. A local TRPA1 antagonist reduced the response. The article’s prose reverses the order of the two antagonist group means relative to the stated direction and Figure 4. This is a surface application of an isolated compound, and a reflex count cannot determine swallowing safety after eating ground mustard. The ESSD abstract presents the same investigators, regions and main chemical finding. A later pharyngolaryngeal injury experiment supplies a separate latency result. [Local primary article, Figure 4](Source-Text/PMC8891345.txt#L105) · [ESSD congress report](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00455-022-10456-y) · [Later injury model](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Mustard-Seed-Caraway.md#mustard-constituent-and-swallowing-after-rat-injury)

## Brown mustard leaf extract in a rat motor model

The 2021 report evaluated an extract made from *Brassica juncea* leaves in haloperidol-treated rats. It reports favorable broad motor tests with leaf extract, including locomotor and rotarod measures. This is a different plant part from the 2015 seed report. The evidence is recorded as plant-part context and gives no symptom-specific culinary seed comparison. [Primary article, Methods and Results](https://www.imrpress.com/journal/FBL/26/11/10.52586/5007)

## Ground caraway in a tuberculosis trial registration

NCT06157034 was first publicly posted on December 5, 2023. The registry lists actual study dates in 2021–2022, with completed status and no posted results. A ground raw *Carum carvi* capsule arm was compared alongside ginger, spearmint oil and placebo. The registered primary outcomes concern gastrointestinal symptoms during pulmonary tuberculosis treatment. No swallowing, Parkinson’s, fatigue or balance endpoint is listed. A later article reports the study outcomes. ## Mustard on a Parkinson diet menu and postural stability

The Hegelmaier Parkinson diet study lists mustard among available herbs and spices, with preparation and actual use unspecified. The motor total includes postural stability, and the report gives no individual stability result or randomized mustard comparison.
