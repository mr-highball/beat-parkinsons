# Herb and spice evidence follow-up, 2020–2024

## Lemon balm leaf capsules and outpatient depression

Araj-Khodaei and colleagues’ July 3, 2020 pilot (IRCT2014061718126N1) compared dried ground lemon balm leaves, lavender, and fluoxetine in 45 analyzed adults with mild to moderate major depression. Capsules were masked, and the Methods describe assigning patients in a repeating group order. The Results text assigns a week-eight change of −8.46 points to lemon balm. The group trajectory labeled lemon balm in Figure 2 appears to fall by about −7.8 points. The two readings are inconsistent and the paper provides no numerical outcome table to resolve the group values. The week-eight comparison was p = .848 and the group interaction was p = .192. The study had no inert control and did not use an equivalence design. Its clinical population and capsule preparation limit transfer to brewed lemon balm in Parkinson’s. [Local full text](Source-Text/PMC7333290.txt) · [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7333290/)

## Lemon balm extract and depression in type 2 diabetes

Safari and colleagues’ May 2, 2023 trial (IRCT201709239472N16) assigned 60 adults with type 2 diabetes and depressive symptoms to a hydroalcoholic lemon balm extract or toasted flour capsules. The analysis included 44 completers. Table 4 reports a mean Beck Depression Inventory-II change of −3.91 versus −0.04 points (p = .001) and an anxiety contrast of p = .04. Sleep quality did not differ statistically. The abstract and Methods specify 700 mg total daily in two 350 mg capsules, with Methods describing 12 weeks. A discussion sentence says 700 mg twice daily and the outcome table heading says eight weeks. The report gives both simple and permuted-block randomization descriptions. The formulation and population differ from [0133, brewed lemon balm and depression in Parkinson’s](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0133.txt). [Local full text](Source-Text/PMC10152712.txt) · [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10152712/)

## Parsley and marjoram preparations reported in a Tlemcen survey

Bachiri’s June 29, 2022 thesis was made available in the university repository on September 20. The survey included 31 respondents, 26 described as having Parkinson’s, and asked about fatigue, depression, broad motor problems, and rest tremor. Table 10 records two citations for parsley leaf infusions or decoctions and one for marjoram leaf or stem preparations. Ginkgo, moringa, and *Teucrium polium* were also listed. The parsley name *Apium petroselinum* corresponds to *Petroselinum crispum*. The questionnaire and results give no respondent-level link between a plant preparation and a symptom rating, no quantified improvement or worsening, and no verified fresh or dried status for these uses. Table 9 separates awareness, reported use and beliefs about efficacy. Its counts and the abstract’s 28% summary cannot establish a preparation-specific effect. The laboratory part of the thesis measured antioxidant activity in moringa preparations separately. ## Marjoram and seeds in a Tlemcen honey mixture

Benkhira and Bouayed Agha defended this thesis on June 23, 2024, and the university repository lists October 1, 2024 as the issue and availability date. It describes a survey of 150 people with Parkinson’s and a six-month oral honey mixture in 35 patients. The Methods list marjoram, anise seed, nuts, costus and ginkgo in the original mixture and report adding sesame and fennel seeds. The authors describe assessments every three months but give no individual ingredient quantities or details of leaf and seed preparation. Table 11 lists survey symptom categories, including akinesia, sleep disturbance, fatigue, pain and depression. It does not link the categories to mixture recipients or to changes after treatment. Table 14 reports 25 improved, nine rated controllable and three unchanged among 37 people in the combined apitherapy and phytotherapy category. The thesis does not reconcile the 35 and 37 denominators or link those ratings to particular symptoms. It describes verbal consent for the questionnaire. The available data leave the mixture’s symptom effects and individual ingredients unresolved. ## Sage extract and exhaustion in a registered menopause trial

Zeidabadi et al. published the sage and placebo symptom groups from IRCT2015111713940N2 on February 28, 2020. The study reports 66 assigned and 59 completing twelve weeks. The mean physical and mental exhaustion score on the Menopause Rating Scale went from 1.41 to 1.16 with dry sage extract and remained 1.43 with placebo. The post-treatment between-group comparison was inconclusive (p = .634). The reported within-sage change (p < .001) measures a different contrast. The 2021 companion publication uses the same registration and reports 99 randomized across the sage, Vitex and placebo arms, with calcium, phosphorus and vitamin D endpoints. It supplies no further exhaustion results. Both papers describe extract tablets in postmenopausal women. [Primary symptom report, Methods and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7114003/#T2) · [2021 companion report](https://bpsa.journals.ekb.eg/article_207155_97eea0a9bb75726a3341b77cc50bd598.pdf) · [2016 registration and chronology](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#sage-and-vitex-menopause-trial-registration)

## Sage-containing beverages and acute mental fatigue

Jackson et al. published results for NCT02202629 on July 28, 2020. Thirty-two healthy adults completed a randomized, double-blind crossover of a flavored placebo and three beverages containing beetroot, ginseng and sage extracts plus apple, blueberry or coffee berry extract. Mental fatigue ratings during the cognitive task battery were lower for the apple and coffee berry formulations than for placebo (adjusted p = .002 and .003). The coffee berry blend also had lower fatigue-inertia ratings (adjusted p < .001). The blueberry comparison supplied no clear fatigue signal. Sage was present in all three active blends, and the design gives no separate sage effect. These measurements concern acute mental fatigue after extract mixtures in healthy adults. [Primary report, Figures 2–3](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu12082254) · [Local results](Source-Text/PMC7468953.txt#L147) · [2014 registration](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#botanical-beverage-and-task-fatigue-registration)

## Fresh sage tip extract and menopausal fatigue

Dimpfel et al. randomized 80 menopausal women to a tablet containing a thujone-free extract of freshly harvested common sage tips or a matched placebo. Seventy-eight completed four weeks and 48 were in the per-protocol group. The Methods specify 280 mg of thick extract from freshly harvested sage tips, equivalent to 3,400 mg of tincture. The primary assessments concerned menopausal symptoms, hot flushes and EEG measures. The February 10, 2021 report describes a lower secondary Profile of Mood States fatigue score with sage versus placebo (p = .029) and prints values of 2.19 ± 1.34 and 1.47 ± 0.99 without a separate arm-by-time table or confidence interval for fatigue. The investigators also reported that the Menopause Rating Scale exhaustion item improved (p < .05) and that a separate sleep questionnaire item on mental exhaustion before sleep showed no change. The comparison uses a concentrated extract and a menopausal population, which limits its fit to [0883, fresh sage and Parkinson’s fatigue](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0883.txt). The article identifies EudraCT 2016-005033-77 and April 2017 regulatory and ethics approvals. An exact search of the public register returned no record for that identifier, leaving public posting and prespecification of fatigue unverified. [Primary report, Methods and Results](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2021.e05910) · [Public register exact search](https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/search?query=2016-005033-77) · [Earlier Swiss study of fresh sage extract](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#fresh-sage-extract-and-exhaustion-in-an-uncontrolled-menopause-study)

## Combined sage extracts and perceived exertion during cycling

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT04804657 on March 18, 2021, three days after the listed completion date. Reverse digit-span memory was primary and rating of perceived exertion was secondary. The register lists no posted summary results. Babault et al. published the crossover on November 30, 2021. Of 26 volunteers, 17 were analyzed. The intervention combined aqueous common sage leaf extract with Spanish sage essential oil. The published comparison found a 0.3-point lower rating of perceived exertion with the mixture across the study time points (95% CI 0.1 to 0.5 lower). The mean cycling power was similar under both conditions. Perceived exercise effort in healthy athletes measures a different outcome from fatigue severity in Parkinson’s, and the study supplies no effect for fresh culinary sage. ## FDA and French sage adverse-event follow-up

The FDA file posted November 4, 2022 contains four current case IDs linked through one worldwide report number in openFDA. Each lists sage as a secondary suspect in the quarterly file and includes fatigue among multiple reactions. Three update records first filed in 2019. The reported use was for nasopharyngitis and the record gives no verified preparation or Parkinson’s diagnosis. France’s 2023 ANSES assessment identified two supplement-product reports with likely attribution involving multi-ingredient products containing common sage. It did not attribute the effects to sage individually and gave no fatigue-specific or Parkinson’s outcome for those reports. ## Ginger and clary sage in a calcium-channel screen

Wang and colleagues screened 42 essential oil products in a cell assay. Ginger and clary sage (*Salvia sclarea*) oils were among the five products selected for further calcium-channel analysis. Purified 6-gingerol and sclareol were tested as channel antagonists. In the mouse component, purified sclareol preserved some locomotor measures in a 6-OHDA model. Rigidity was not separately assayed. The preparations and clary sage species differ from culinary grated ginger (`0626`) and ground sage (`0370`). [Primary report, Figures 2–5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8895113/)

## Curcumin extract for pain in adults with digestive complaints

Lopresti and colleagues compared an eight-week course of the turmeric-derived curcumin extract Curcugen with placebo and collected self-report data from 77 adults with digestive complaints. Overall gastrointestinal symptoms favored the extract. The time-by-group comparison for abdominal pain or constipation showed no clear difference, and the nominal SF-36 pain finding (p = .023) was inconclusive after adjustment for its baseline imbalance (p = .677). A secondary anxiety measure favored the extract (p = .023). This comparison used a standardized extract in a digestive-complaints cohort and is indirect evidence for ground turmeric and Parkinson’s pain (`0812`). [Primary report, Tables 2 and 3](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12906-021-03220-6)

## Curcuminoids plus acupressure in knee osteoarthritis

Mahanani and colleagues planned a three-week randomized comparison of standardized turmeric-rhizome curcuminoids at 30 mg three times daily plus acupressure against placebo capsules plus sham acupressure. The protocol included pain and joint stiffness and used olive oil during the procedure. Ginger appears in the background. The first author's Indonesian doctoral report describes lower pain and lower joint stiffness after the combined intervention. The public abstract reports p = .001 for joint stiffness and omits an estimate and test result for pain. The comparison tested both interventions together in knee osteoarthritis. It is indirect evidence for ground turmeric and pain (`0812`) and supplies no ginger or olive oil effect for `0359`, `0626` or `0725`. [Primary protocol, NCT06105840](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11231618/) · [Mahanani’s 2024 dissertation abstract](https://etd.repository.ugm.ac.id/penelitian/detail/244412) · [Pooled outcome abstract](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#pooled-curcuminoid-trials-in-knee-osteoarthritis) · [Earlier trial of extracted curcuminoids](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#turmeric-rhizome-curcuminoids-versus-diclofenac-in-knee-osteoarthritis)

## Ginger root extracts in rat neuropathic pain studies

In one spinal nerve ligation study, Shen and colleagues fed gingerol- or shogaol-enriched ginger root extracts to groups of male rats. Both extracts increased paw withdrawal thresholds and improved open-field anxiety-like behavior. A subsequent 17-rat study of the gingerol-enriched extract reported higher mechanical withdrawal thresholds and lower grimace scores. Vocalization differences were less consistent across measures. These preparations and behavioral proxies provide indirect evidence for grated ginger and pain (`0725`) or anxiety (`0937`) in Parkinson’s. [Primary sensory and open-field study, PMID 34748918](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8794052/) · [Primary sensory and grimace study, PMID 35873544](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9305072/) · [Later elevated plus maze study](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#gingerol-enriched-extract-in-neuropathic-pain-models)

## Ginger extract in migraine prevention

Helli and colleagues enrolled 103 people with episodic migraine in a three-month blinded comparison of standardized dry ginger extract and starch placebo. The authors reported lower headache severity and duration with ginger, with no clear difference in the number of attacks. An earlier Brazilian trial did not find a clear reduction in days with severe pain. The studies used different extract doses and headache endpoints in migraine. They do not measure Parkinson’s pain after grated ginger for `0725`. [Primary Iranian trial, DOI 10.5812/jjnpp-120449](https://brieflands.com/journals/jjnpp/articles/120449) · [Earlier primary reports](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#ginger-preparations-in-migraine-pain-studies)

## Sumac extract in Parkinson-derived cell cultures

This 2022 laboratory study applied a phenolic sumac extract to fibroblasts from people with early-onset Parkinson’s disease carrying PARK2 mutations and to cultured macrophages. The report describes changes in cellular oxidative and mitochondrial measures under the extract. The participants supplied cells and did not consume the preparation. Bradykinesia, postural instability, and rigidity were outside the measured outcomes for `0430`, `0605`, and `0855`. ## Rosemary capsules in major depressive disorder

Azizi and colleagues randomized 59 adults starting SSRI therapy for major depressive disorder to dried rosemary capsules or placebo. The administered capsules contained dried milled leaves. The ethanol extract in Methods was for analysis of plant chemistry. The analysis included 51 completers. Beck Depression Inventory II scores fell from 33.46 to 14.19 in the rosemary group and from 42.20 to 29.52 in the placebo group after eight weeks. The treatment-by-time comparison was reported as p = .005. Heartburn was reported by 11 of 26 rosemary participants and none of the 25 placebo participants. The baseline depression imbalance (p=.003), completer analysis and lack of an explicitly described baseline-covariate adjustment limit the estimate. The population and dried capsule preparation leave [0128, fresh rosemary and depressive symptoms in Parkinson’s](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0128.txt) open. [Primary report, Figure 2 and Tables 2–3, PMID 36343423](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ctcp.2022.101685) · [Accessible full text](https://www.naturalhealthresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Rosemary-Supplementation-as-an-Adjunctive-Therapy-to-SSRIs-Reduces-Anxiety-and-Depression-Symptoms.pdf)

## Cinnamon bark and ginger formula in a Parkinson dream enactment case

Tamada et al. reported four older adults with parasomnias. One, a 72-year-old man, had Parkinson’s and polysomnography-confirmed REM sleep behavior disorder. His wife had reported frequent episodes of shouting and hitting motions during dreams. The authors reported that dream enactment and daytime visual hallucinations ceased two days after he began Keishikaryukotsuboreito and that the parasomnia did not recur during six months of continued treatment with other medication held stable. The formula was a seven-ingredient extract granule containing cinnamon bark and ginger. This is a same-symptom, combined-exposure lead for [0981, ground cinnamon and dream enactment](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0981.txt). The single uncontrolled Parkinson’s case leaves the separate effects of cinnamon and the culinary preparation unresolved. The report appeared online in October 2021 and in the May 2022 journal issue. [Primary report, Patient 1 and Table 2, PMID 34670887](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/internalmedicine/61/9/61_7952-21/_pdf)

## A topical cinnamon-containing formula and constipation

Zou et al. randomized 90 inpatients with Parkinson’s and constipation classified as qi deficiency to topical multi-herb paste with heated stone massage, heated stone massage, or oral Maren capsules for two weeks. The topical formula included cinnamon bark, astragalus, magnolia bark, and mugwort. The investigators reported that the paste-plus-massage arm scored 1.45 points lower on the Wexner constipation scale than massage (95% CI, 0.21 to 2.70 lower) and 3.94 points lower than the Maren group (95% CI, 2.69 to 5.18 lower). The paper reports blinded outcome assessment and leaves participant blinding unclear. The topical mixture and the clinical subgroup limit applicability to [0737, ground cinnamon and constipation](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0737.txt). [Primary report, Methods, Discussion and Table 4](https://www.sciengine.com/doi/pdf/52601E2B480E4B07AF4C760C42FACE71)

## Ginger and cinnamon twig in a constipation case

Section 3 also describes baseline anxiety and a reported improvement in mood at follow-up. The article gives no follow-up anxiety scale or separate numerical anxiety measure for [0937, grated ginger and anxiety](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0937.txt). The two-week recorded tremor amplitude changed from a reported 3–6 cm to 1–3 cm, and the MDS-UPDRS total was 76 at baseline, 57 at two weeks and 52 at four weeks. The authors list fresh ginger rhizome, cinnamon twig, and an ingredient labeled 黑枣, without a botanical identification for the last one. ## Fresh ginger in a multiherb regimen with measured Parkinson gait

Lee et al. reported two Parkinson’s cases treated with herbal formulas, acupuncture, pharmacopuncture, and moxibustion while conventional Parkinson’s treatment continued. Case 1 started a 12-ingredient Jaeumganghwa-tang extract granule on November 4, 2022, after two earlier formulas that did not list ginger. Table 3 lists fresh ginger rhizome among the prescription ingredients. Measured stride length at baseline and months one, two, and three was 0.68, 1.02, 0.94, and 0.87 m. The first increase and subsequent decrease preceded the new formula. Stride length declined further during the final interval and remained above baseline. The UPDRS rigidity sum was 12, 4, 7, and 7, with no change in the final interval. Case 2 used a different formula containing peony and licorice. The multiple treatments and single exposed case leave direction unresolved for [0566, ground ginger and shortened stride](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0566.txt) and [0626, grated ginger and rigidity](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0626.txt). [Primary case report](https://doi.org/10.22246/jikm.2023.44.4.774) · [Publisher PDF, Tables 3, 5 and 7](https://www.jikm.or.kr/upload/pdf/jikm-44-4-774.pdf)

## Dried ginger as an optional component of a Parkinson decoction

Xu and colleagues’ 2021 study assigned 80 people with Parkinson’s to conventional treatment with or without a multiherb decoction. The base prescription contained neither culinary ginger nor cinnamon. Patients with a specified traditional pattern could receive both dried ginger and cinnamon bark. The paper gives no count of participants who received this modification. At four weeks, Table 3 reports overall UPDRS means of 45.88 and 53.90 for the herbal and comparison groups. The reported t statistic of 2.318 is inconsistent with the ordinary independent-sample calculation from the displayed means, standard deviations of 2.50 and 2.39, and group sizes of 40. The paper reports total symptom and sleep scales. It provides no estimate by ginger exposure and no separate dyskinesia, stride, or anxiety result. [Primary report, Methods and Table 3](https://lcbl.amegroups.com/article/download/46511/pdf#page=4)

## Charred ginger in a modified Zhigancao Tang trial

Wei and colleagues randomly assigned 60 people with Parkinson’s to usual medication or usual medication plus a 14-ingredient decoction containing charred ginger and cinnamon twig for 12 weeks. Table 2 reports mean total UPDRS scores of 46.28 to 44.72 in the combined group and 45.19 to 45.53 with usual treatment. The article marks the post-treatment comparison significant. An unadjusted comparison using the printed standard deviations and group sizes does not reproduce that result, and the table does not specify which of the paper’s two tests it used for this score. The reported outcomes are total and Part II and III UPDRS scores, traditional symptom scores, and serum markers. The report gives no stride length, dyskinesia item, or anxiety-scale estimate. CNKI identifies June 28, 2024 as the first public release on its platform, matching the publisher’s electronic publication field. The publisher records January 16, 2025 for publication on its current site and February 20, 2025 for the issue. ## Isolated 6-shogaol in two Parkinson animal models

Kim and colleagues’ August 2, 2024 study compared isolated synthetic 6-shogaol, levodopa, their combination, and control groups in a toxin-induced mouse model. Pole turn and descent times were shorter with either treatment alone than with the untreated model. The combination gave results similar to levodopa alone. Rotarod latency showed a clearer improvement in both levodopa groups than with 6-shogaol alone. The study’s nonmotor tasks concerned depression-like behavior, olfaction and memory. It measured neither stride length nor dyskinesia and used no anxiety-specific task. [Primary report, Figures 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11392672/) · [2026 follow-up](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#isolated-6-shogaol-and-levodopa-in-a-mouse-follow-up)

Rafeeq and colleagues’ October 9, 2024 rotenone rat study reported more total open-field crossings, longer rotarod latency and shorter catalepsy time with isolated 6-shogaol than with the rotenone control. Figure 4 shows shorter catalepsy time, despite the Results sentence that describes enhanced inflexibility. Figure 1 gives total square crossings and no separate result for the center or perimeter. The study does not report anxiety, dyskinesia, or stride length. [Primary report, Figures 1–4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11510247/) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC11510247.txt#L87)

## Isolated crocin and composite motor outcomes

Mohammad Soleymani et al. randomized 60 adults with idiopathic Parkinson’s to a blinded, controlled eight-week trial of isolated crocin capsules. Fifty-three completed it. The authors’ abstract reports improvement within the crocin arm on MDS-UPDRS Parts II and III and no significant change in the urinary 8-OHdG-to-creatinine ratio. The available abstract gives no between-group effect estimate for the clinical scales and no separate estimate for rigidity or facial expression. [Primary report and abstract, PMID 39025034](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.parkreldis.2024.107051)

## Daikenchuto and constipation in a single Parkinson’s case

A 2024 Japanese case report described improvement in constipation and other symptoms following administration of daikenchuto in one patient whose walking and rehabilitation had become difficult.
