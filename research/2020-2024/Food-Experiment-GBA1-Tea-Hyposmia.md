# Tea and hyposmia in a GBA1 Parkinson survey

## Tea exposure and clinical symptoms in a GBA1 family survey

Zhang and colleagues' Chinese multicenter intrafamilial survey was first published online on 19 November 2024. It included 59 nonmanifesting GBA1 carriers, 62 controls and 107 GBA1-associated Parkinson cases (81 early-stage). Tea use was among exposures considered against Parkinson disease; hyposmia was compared across clinical groups and was more severe in early-stage cases than in carriers and controls. The accessible abstract and publisher introduction report no tea–hyposmia estimate and do not identify tea type, preparation or smell-test method. The full methods and tables were not verified, so any unreported joint analysis remains open. No beverage effect can be inferred from the two separate comparisons.

The report and the [2025 cfDNA methylation study](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Tea-Hyposmia-Methylation.md#tea-and-hyposmia-among-cfdna-phenotypes) have different stated settings and designs; individual participant overlap is not documented in the accessible abstracts.
