# Food experiment evidence for soybean and rice bran oil

## Assigned soybean oil diet in a rotenone mouse comparison

Techaniyom and colleagues first published June 14, 2024. Both the unlesioned and rotenone controls received 5% soybean oil in feed. Low-dose perilla oil replaced it at 5%, while the other perilla and fish oil diets also differed in fat quantity. In the rotenone soybean group, mean pole descent was 7.83 seconds versus 4.73 in the unlesioned soybean group and 4.16 and 4.63 under middle- and high-dose perilla. Open-field stopping or resting lasted 135.0 seconds in the rotenone soybean group versus 72.76 in the equal-fat perilla group (p < .001). The equal-fat pole result is not reported as different. Cylinder rearing was 3.10 under rotenone soybean versus 8.30 under equal-fat perilla (p = .035). Open-field stopping includes exploration and anxiety-related behavior, and the design supplies a relative oil contrast under the toxin model. It does not isolate an effect against an oil-free diet or measure clinical freezing-of-gait episodes, walking speed, stride or falls. Thai Vegetable Oil supplied the soybean oil. A certifier lists refined A-Ngoon oil from that manufacturer, but the paper does not give a study SKU or lot. ## Soybean oil in neural assays and multicomponent diets

Deol and colleagues first reported January 8, 2020. Conventional and low-linoleic soybean oils in high-fat feed changed hypothalamic gene expression and oxytocin measures in male mice. The comparison feed also contained a small amount of soybean oil for essential fatty acids. Named disease-associated genes were identified by literature cross-reference. No motor or sleep behavior was measured. A November 28, 2024 hyperglycemia study gave mice a lard-rich feed with soybean oil and a streptozotocin manipulation, and reports differences in wire-hang, beam and Y-maze assays. The comparisons do not isolate soybean oil. [Deol primary text](Source-Text/PMC7041656.txt#L209) · [Hyperglycemia study, feed table and motor assays](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11650731/)

In Wang et al.’s August 2, 2023 rotenone rat report, soybean oil comprised 1% of the cinnamon nanoemulsion. The rotenone injection used sunflower oil. The authors observed reduced catalepsy with several cinnamon treatments and did not test the formulation without cinnamon. Li and colleagues’ April 8, 2024 butylphthalide study used soybean oil to carry the drug and describes soybean oil in the control. The abstract and detailed methods differ on the control route and saline wording. Movement and odor findings come from the drug study and give no verified dietary oil contrast. [Wang primary methods and outcomes](Source-Text/PMC10433916.txt#L125) · [Li primary methods and outcomes](Source-Text/PMC11057415.txt#L118)

## Rice bran oil placebo and missing Parkinson item data

TCTR20210807002 compares medical cannabis oil with rice bran oil placebo. In the current official XML, the rice bran oil matched color and texture and followed the same titration and observation schedule. The registry gives no oil lot, refinement, dose mass, cannabis carrier or item-level symptom results. Drowsiness, dizziness and dry mouth are listed without by-arm counts. The registry offers future individual data for two years after publication via IPDShare, but provides no protocol or dataset URL. The results-date field predates registration and the public meeting report. A separate Buriram trial has measured gait velocity and function, but the article explicitly identifies olive oil in both arms. ## Chamuangone-enriched rice bran oil and rat motor assays

Alotaibi and colleagues first published May 10, 2024. The assigned formulation was a *Garcinia cowa* leaf extract enriched to 1.97 mg/mL chamuangone in rice bran oil. Relative to haloperidol controls, the authors report longer strides, quicker ladder climbing, more open-field movement and lower catalepsy in multiple enriched-oil groups. Table 2 gives a mixed result at 1.5 hours, when the low-dose mean was 1.26 versus 1.15 in controls and the middle-dose mean was 1.14. At three hours the means were 0.84, 0.62 and 0.40 across dose groups versus 1.82 in controls. The figure legends and text report n = 6 in six groups, while Methods says 30 rats. The abstract describes dosing before haloperidol and the Methods gives a different sequence. The preparation lacked a plain rice bran oil arm and the study measured drug-induced rodent motor behavior, leaving cooking oil, Parkinson rigidity, gait, falls, and hand dexterity untested. [Publisher full report and license](https://www.itjfs.com/index.php/ijfs/en/article/view/2551) · [Institutional full report, Table 2 and Figures 1–3](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/122437)

## Rice bran extracts and Parkinson-related model outcomes

Sachin Kumar and Puneet Kumar reported in 2021 that an orally administered hexane rice bran extract partly reversed rotenone-associated changes in rat locomotion, coordination, grip and catalepsy. Zhang and colleagues’ 2021 report tested four individual phytosterols identified in rice bran and its oil in rotenone-exposed worms. Their dopaminergic protection results concern specific compounds and mechanistic measures. The preparations and models cannot establish a refined rice bran oil effect in people. [Rat extract primary report](https://www.benthamscience.com/article/113662) · [Worm phytosterol primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34570129/)

## Rice bran oil blend and stool outcomes

Li and colleagues first reported December 25, 2024. In loperamide-treated mice, a four-oil blend containing 26.02% rice bran oil increased stool water and frequency and shortened time to first dark stool compared with model controls. The medium and high doses outperformed the red pine oil comparator on the authors’ overall assessment. There was no rice bran oil-only group. Section 3.4 reverses the control and model direction in one sentence relative to the discussion and reported results. In a separate September 2022 human trial of defatted rice bran, normal Bristol type 4 stools were reported by 34.48% before and 48.28% after supplementation, and 35.48% and 32.26% in the control arm. The report supplies descriptive percentages, not a clinical Parkinson constipation estimate. ## Rice bran extract depression trial registration

NCT05180136 first posted January 6, 2022 and lists an eight-week comparison of rice bran extract and placebo in adults with depressive symptoms. The primary outcome was the Korean Hamilton Depression Rating Scale. The registry is completed and has no posted results. A later primary article reports questionnaire outcomes. The registration gives no apathy-specific test, no confirmed Parkinson group and no refined rice bran oil assignment. ## Soybean oil as the curcumin vehicle in a mouse motor study

Alves and colleagues’ original 2024 Methods and Table 1 give 50 mg/kg as the curcumin dose. Soy oil served as the oral vehicle and was also given in control and rotenone-only groups. The study reports beam, pole and other motor assays. It did not assess levodopa-induced dyskinesia or specify that the oil was refined. A later review table labels the 50 mg/kg entry as soybean oil.
