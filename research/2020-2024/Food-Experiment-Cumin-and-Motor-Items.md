# Cumin, edible oils and specific motor outcomes

## Crushed cumin within a randomized digestive symptom trial

First published September 22, 2024. The trial randomized 67 adults with functional dyspepsia and 55 completed. The active sachets used coarsely ground common cumin, anise, fennel, ajwain and sugar. The oat-and-sugar comparator included 10% of the active mix by weight. In Table 3, adjusted week-two dyspepsia scores were 6.01 versus 9.37 (p=.019). Adjusted GSRS constipation scores were 4.74 versus 7.98 at week one (p=.049) and 4.77 versus 7.43 at week two (p=.152). The authors stopped below their planned sample of 150 following an interim analysis. The results cover one mixture with an active component in its comparator, without isolating cumin, measuring gastric emptying or assessing Parkinson motor outcomes. The paper identifies IRCT20220304054179N1. [Original Methods and Table 3](https://brieflands.com/journals/jjnpp/articles/148163) · [Local text](Source-Text/2024-Samimi-Crushed-Cumin-Constipation.txt)

## Solvent-extracted sesame oil and rat catalepsy

Published September 30, 2022. The sesame seed oil was extracted with petroleum ether in a Soxhlet apparatus. In chlorpromazine-treated rats, oral oil groups had lower block-catalepsy scores and longer rotarod retention than the drug-only control. Figure comparisons used six animals per group. Rotarod retention is a performance proxy and the laboratory solvent extraction differs from cold pressing. This is an animal drug-induced model. [Original journal Methods and Figures 2 and 4](https://ijpsdronline.com/index.php/journal/article/download/3656/928) · [Local text](Source-Text/2022-Khot-Sesame-Oil-Catalepsy.txt)

## Egg frequency and self-reported slowness in Parkinson disease

First verified public report, December 27, 2024. The duplicate baseline heatmaps show a near-zero inverse sign for egg frequency and perceived slow movement. Both tiles match RGB 200/25/81 and the known positive canned-fruit/falls control is reproduced. The original supplies no numerical interval, isolated walking speed, variation in egg preparation or causal comparison. [Original MVP 10-year summary pages 64 and 65](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/14919326-8cbc-4a3e-88d4-0f609019e178/MVP%20Study%2010-Year%20Summary-c634e33.pdf#page=64) · [Local text](Source-Text/2024-MVP-Egg-Slow-Movement.txt)

## Turmeric extract and motor tasks in lesioned rats

First published in September 2021. The original abstract describes orally administered Curcuma longa extract in a 6-hydroxydopamine rat model and reports improvement in selected motor and cognitive tasks. The full Methods and outcome-specific numeric tables were inaccessible at review. This was an extract and a rodent behavioral comparison, without a clinical bradykinesia or dietary ground-spice result. [Publisher original](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13596-021-00606-2) · [Local text](Source-Text/2021-Bhowmick-Turmeric-Extract-Motor-Tasks.txt)

## Official registration of the Persian digestive mixture

The official recorded registration date is April 13, 2022. A separate first-public-posting date could not be verified. The registry describes a four-seed mixture with a 10% mixture comparator and prespecified postprandial fullness, pain and burning symptom outcomes. The WHO entry uses caraway wording, whereas the identifier-matched publication labels common cumin by its Latin name. The registry summary and article also differ on allocation ratio and reference ingredients. The registry has no structured results, while the trial report has published results. Actual enrollment dates are unavailable. [IRCT registration](https://en.irct.ir/trial/62308) · [WHO record](https://trialsearch.who.int/Trial2.aspx?TrialID=IRCT20220304054179N1) · [Local text](Source-Text/2022-IRCT20220304054179N1-Persian-FACT.txt)

## Official registration of curcumin and resistance training in Parkinson disease

The official recorded registration date is July 20, 2020. A separate first-public-posting date could not be verified. The record describes randomized capsule and exercise interventions, a baseline and eight-week UPDRS primary endpoint and laboratory secondary outcomes. The capsule has turmeric powder and enriched material. The intended arm descriptions and age eligibility contain discrepancies. Recruitment is listed complete, with no independently located primary results or posted diary of daily OFF time. Actual enrollment dates are unavailable. [IRCT registration](https://en.irct.ir/trial/45058) · [WHO record](https://trialsearch.who.int/Trial2.aspx?TrialID=IRCT20191231045968N1) · [Local text](Source-Text/2020-IRCT20191231045968N1-Curcumin-Resistance.txt)

## Oral curcumin dose and MPTP motor tasks

Published August 10, 2022. Gavage used isolated curcumin before and through MPTP exposure. Figure 1 reports favorable pole, rotarod and open-field outcomes at the middle experimental dose, with low and high doses inconclusive. These are animal motor proxies rather than clinical symptom measurements after ground turmeric. [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9400544/) · [Local text](Source-Text/PMC9400544.txt)

## Gavaged curcumin and mouse pole or open-field speed

Published June 14, 2023. Oral isolated curcumin was given concurrently in an MPTP mouse model. Figure 1 reports shorter pole descent and higher open-field mean speed and distance (each p < .01 versus toxin alone). The behavior in an experimental species and the concentrated intervention differ from the frozen claim. [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10303117/) · [Local text](Source-Text/PMC10303117.txt)

## Curcumin dose in lesioned rats and motor performance

First published in June 2023. The animal study used >98% isolated curcumin by gavage. Figure 1 reports improvements in fixed- and accelerating-speed rotarod and open-field distance after the higher exposure compared with lesioned controls. It tests a concentrated isolate and motor tasks. It does not measure culinary ground turmeric or separately rated clinical bradykinesia. [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10360947/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2023-Liu-Curcumin-Motor-Performance.txt)

## Culinary-spice review and the cited cumin motor experiment

The 2022 review cites a primary study of aqueous cumin fruit extract and movement outcomes in MPTP mice. Its use of dyskinesia elsewhere describes adverse outcomes of Parkinson medications. The review gives no toasted cumin or measured dyskinesia experiment. [Review and source trail](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9685814/) · [Licensed text](Source-Text/PMC9685814.txt)
