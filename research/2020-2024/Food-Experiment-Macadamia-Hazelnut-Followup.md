# Tree nut studies on movement, allergy and postmeal alertness

## Ground hazelnut and exploratory locomotion in mice

Olofinnade and colleagues first published their high-fat-diet mouse study on October 1, 2020, before the 2021 issue. The intervention used ground whole kernels of Italian and Turkish hazelnut cultivars. Open-field locomotion and rearing increased in supplemented groups relative to high-fat-diet controls. Open-field grid crossings quantify exploratory movement and do not measure freezing episodes or rest tremor. The study used no expressed oil or Parkinson disease model. [Primary paper, Sections 3.7 and 4.5](https://doi.org/10.2741/4906)

## Hazelnut processing in a skin prick and laboratory study

De Angelis and colleagues published a primary study in February 2022 involving 22 patients with suspected hazelnut allergy and tests of different kernel preparations. Skin-prick and immunoblot responses fell after selected combined heat and pressure treatments, with responses persisting for some proteins. The researchers did not perform a food challenge with the treated material. The tested exposure was processed kernel material rather than toasted expressed oil. [Local primary text](Source-Text/PMC8875159.txt) · [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8875159/)

## Macadamia confectionery allergy in children

Aota and colleagues reported two children with anaphylactic symptoms after ingestion of macadamia nuts in 2023. Both reacted in prick-to-prick tests to roasted nuts. One had used a moisturizer containing macadamia oil for a year, which the authors considered a possible sensitization route. In a distinct 2024 case, Kiyama and Kitazawa described excessive drooling, generalized erythema and shock after a three-year-old ate ice cream containing macadamia nuts. Prick-to-prick tests were positive to raw and roasted macadamia paste and negative to roasted hazelnut paste. No oral oil challenge or formal swallowing assessment was reported in these cases. [Aota original report](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspaci/37/1/37_99/_article/-char/en) · [Kiyama and Kitazawa local primary text](Source-Text/PMC11192167.txt) · [Kiyama and Kitazawa original report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11192167/)

## Registration for oral macadamia oil and postmeal sleepiness

Okayama Prefectural University publicly registered UMIN000055203 on August 23, 2024. The completed single-blind crossover enrolled eight healthy adults aged 18–29 and listed bread alone, two macadamia-oil meal configurations and a soy-oil meal. Planned outcomes included postmeal visual analogue ratings of sleepiness and fatigue, temperature, gas exchange and autonomic activity. The registry lists prior-night sleep timing and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index data as secondary information. These measures contain no dream enactment or intervention-period insomnia result. The matching sample and 150-minute measurement schedule link this record to the smaller experiment in the 2025 reports.
