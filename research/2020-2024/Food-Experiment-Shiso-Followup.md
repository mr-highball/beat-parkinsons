# Shiso and perilla follow-up

## Dried leaf powder and home blood pressure

Hashimoto and colleagues published their randomized placebo-controlled study on April 30, 2020. Sixty healthy Japanese adults received dried perilla leaf powder or pectin for six months. Complete serial home blood-pressure measurements were available in 30 participants. The overall analysis showed no clear difference. A subgroup with home systolic pressure at least 120 mmHg at month six had lower changes in morning and bedtime systolic pressure on leaf powder. The subgroup used an end-of-study pressure measure to classify participants. The study did not assess a pressure drop on standing, lightheadedness or fresh shiso consumption in Parkinson’s. [Primary report, Figure 1 and Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7248687/) · [Local full text](Source-Text/PMC7248687.txt)

## Steam-distilled leaf oil and induced insomnia

Zhong and colleagues first published this mouse experiment on November 25, 2020. Inhalation of steam-distilled perilla leaf essential oil shortened sleep latency and increased sleep duration in a chemically induced insomnia model using pentobarbital sleep assays. Reduced spontaneous movement accompanied these findings. The report does not test dried leaves by mouth, dream enactment, or Parkinson-related insomnia. [Primary report, PMID 33246117](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33246117/)

## Leaf oil and anxiety-like behavior under social defeat

In a 2024 experiment, intranasal *Perillae Folium* essential oil increased exploration of the center of the open field and of the open arms of an elevated maze in socially defeated mice. The distilled oil and nasal route differ from fresh leaf consumption. The mice were subjected to social defeat and had no Parkinson model. [Primary report, PMID 38224793](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38224793/)

## Seed oil and motor speed and open-field behavior

Rotenone-exposed mice eating the two higher cold-pressed perilla seed oil diets descended a pole in 4.16 and 4.63 seconds versus 7.83 seconds in rotenone controls. On the highest-oil diet, mice entered the central area more often and groomed less than rotenone controls. Exploration and grooming can reflect anxiety-like behavior and motor capacity. The gastrointestinal work measured colon length and microbiota rather than a clinical constipation outcome. The report first appeared June 14, 2024. Its seed oil and outcomes are separate from oral culinary leaves. [Primary report, Figure 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11561828/) · [Local full text](Source-Text/PMC11561828.txt)

## Acute leaf extract crossover registration

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT05603416 on November 2, 2022. The randomized crossover compared a proprietary perilla leaf extract with placebo in healthy adults after an experimentally large meal. The planned primary outcome was acute gastrointestinal discomfort over 90 minutes. The registry reports actual enrollment of 30 and displays no posted results. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05603416) · [Later primary reports](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Shiso-Followup.md#acute-leaf-extract-crossover-and-bowel-movements)

## Japanese registration for shiso as a food odor

Japan first published jRCT1060240004 on April 25, 2024, following registration on April 24. It planned 34 healthy women and measured odor pleasantness and changes in affect while smelling food samples. Its public status remains Recruiting. The 2026 paper identifies the registration and reports 41 participants. [Official jRCT record](https://jrct.mhlw.go.jp/latest-detail/jRCT1060240004) · [Published results](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Shiso-Followup.md#culinary-shiso-and-odor-pleasantness)

## Korean perilla-containing formula trial protocol

Lee and colleagues’ 2022 protocol for a randomized waitlist comparison of Banhahubak-tang in major depressive disorder planned 84 participants. Insomnia Severity Index and State-Trait Anxiety Inventory were secondary measures. The Korean Traditional Knowledge Portal indexes *Perilla frutescens* var. *crispa* among the formula components. The authors’ 2025 congress report describes 83 completers. [Protocol and identifier](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART002918289) · [Formula index](https://koreantk.com/ktkp2014/thesis/thesis-view.view?ctrlNo=HBSKB9_2022_v33n4_389) · [2025 result](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Shiso-Followup.md#korean-formula-and-insomnia-and-anxiety-outcomes)

## Perilla seed oil and constipation ratings in athletes and sedentary adults

The 2022 first-online Kawamura trial randomized 36 female athletes to perilla seed-oil jelly or placebo. In the analysis using each participant’s lowest treatment-period score, the constipation contrast favored 9 g/day versus placebo (p = .020), with a less clear 3 g/day comparison (p = .073). The groups received unequal jelly schedules. Fresh shiso leaf and Parkinson constipation were untested. [Local factual text](Source-Text/2022-Kawamura-Perilla-Oil-Athlete-Constipation.txt) · [Primary source](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114522001805)

## Perilla seed oil in a fixed-order constipation pilot

Kawamura and Sugita assessed eight sedentary women in an oil phase followed by a placebo phase after a ten-month washout. Constipation score within the oil phase fell at weeks two and three (p = .035 and .020) and had p = .052 at the endpoint. The design was nonrandomized, used within-phase comparisons and tested seed oil. Carryover and time effects remain possible. [Local factual text](Source-Text/2023-Kawamura-Perilla-Oil-Constipation-Pilot.txt) · [Primary source](https://doi.org/10.3390/dietetics2020015)

## Fermented perilla extract and rodent sleep

In the February 2023 thesis summary, oral Bacillus subtilis-fermented Perilla frutescens var. acuta extract increased NREM sleep and slow-wave activity in rodent recordings. A pentobarbital induction assay reportedly showed shorter sleep onset and longer sleep. The adenosine A1 antagonist DPCPX attenuated effects. This is part of the closely matching 2024 journal development family. Complete numerical results and independent cohort identity are unverified. [Ajou dissertation author summary](https://www.dbpia.co.kr/journal/detail?nodeId=T16655272) · [Local text](Source-Text/2023-Bormate-Fermented-Perilla-Sleep-Thesis-Extract.txt)
The 2024 article and the same lead author's February 2023 dissertation describe closely matching fermented perilla extract sleep experiments. The authors report NREM and slow-wave effects in rodents. Full outcome figures were unavailable through the checked journal route. The extract is a processed leaf preparation and the rodent endpoints do not establish a culinary shiso effect on Parkinson insomnia. [Journal accession and study-family link](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jff.2024.106345) · [Local text](Source-Text/2024-Bormate-Fermented-Perilla-Sleep-Article-Extract.txt)

## Jing Si herbal formula and sleep in functional dyspepsia

First posted July 17, 2023. IRB110-232-A lists 26 adults, an eight-herb formula with perilla leaves and planned day-28 PSQI. Its public registration followed registered 2021 study dates. Duration was four weeks. The matching 2024 Liu publication gives outcome data. The registry does not establish an amount of perilla leaf or dried source material. [Official dyspepsia registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05948215) · [Local text](Source-Text/2023-Jing-Si-Dyspepsia-Registration-Extract.txt)
First electronically available September 10, 2024. Thirteen participants per arm received 15 mL/day of an eight-herb concentrate or placebo for four weeks. Table 2 assigns the tea a median PSQI of 14 (IQR 7.5–19) to 12 (6.5–17), within-arm p=.207. Placebo changed from 11 (7–16) to 10 (7.5–14.5), p=.141. The baseline comparison gave p=.472. No adjusted between-arm PSQI change was identified. The measure covers overall sleep quality and the intervention does not isolate perilla leaf. Population was functional dyspepsia. [Primary publication and Tables 1–2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39256061/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2024-Liu-Jing-Si-Sleep-Table-Extract.txt)

## Registered long COVID herbal formula and sleep quality

First posted December 29, 2022. IRB111-232-A lists 32 enrolled and a crossover in long-COVID gut-brain conditions with day-28 PSQI. The narrative names perilla leaf in its eight-herb formula. The arm description calls the liquid packet a 15 mg tablet, leaving amount and form uncertain. The record marks the study completed, with no posted outcome. Different design, IRB ID and dates separate it from the 2024 publication for NCT05948215. Participant-level overlap was not established. [Official long-COVID registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05668104) · [Local text](Source-Text/2022-Jing-Si-Long-COVID-Registration-Extract.txt)

## Perilla seed peptides and exercise fatigue measures in mice

First published September 28, 2020. The original abstract reports greater exercise tolerance and changed fatigue-related markers after a preparation of hydrolyzed purple perilla seed peptides in mice. This is the same article indexed as AGR:IND607176280 and PMID:32984958. It uses seed and purified peptide material, with no dried leaf intake or reported Parkinson fatigue. [Original source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32984958/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2020-Liu-Perilla-Seed-Peptides-Exercise.txt)

## Original abstract scope for perilla oil under social defeat

The author abstract reports more center exploration and elevated-plus-maze open-arm activity after intranasal Perillae Folium essential oil in mice subjected to social defeat. The material is leaf and twig essential oil. Social interaction and immobility changes are separate endpoints. The original abstract gives no group size, effect estimates or interval. The publisher PII page was access-limited and alternate Elsevier metadata lacked figures. The tested exposure and model leave the fresh shiso claim unresolved. [Original source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38224793/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2024-Nguyen-Perilla-Oil-Social-Defeat-Abstract.txt)
