# Marjoram and olive oil symptom outcomes

## Marjoram pilot and coverage of individual Parkinson symptoms

Chbili and colleagues reported motor examination and nonmotor scale totals after 30 days of filtered dried-leaf marjoram tea. The treatment group's total UPDRS III change was inconclusive (p = .069), and the authors reported lower aggregate NMSS and depression scores in that arm. The reports available for this review give no separate speech, facial expression, pull-test, standing-lightheadedness or visual hallucination estimate. The original UPDRS motor examination and NMSS totals cannot substitute for these estimates. The publicly indexed 2025 study has a different ethics identifier and measures biomarkers. The 2021 publisher preview, metadata-only publisher API response and PubMed abstract did not supply item-level results. [Primary 2021 report](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1353802021003072) · [Existing clinical annotation](Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#marjoram-tea-and-motor-and-nonmotor-symptoms) · [2025 biomarker results](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#marjoram-tea-and-oxidative-stress-biomarkers)

## Extra-virgin olive oil intake and sleep in the OPERA sample

Muscogiuri and colleagues first published May 10, 2020. Among 172 adult volunteers, the bivariate proportional-odds models in Table 4 give OR 0.84 (95% CI 0.75–0.93) for using extra-virgin olive oil as the main culinary lipid and OR 0.89 (95% CI 0.83–0.96) for the higher-intake screener item. The sample was cross-sectional and the outcome was a total sleep-quality questionnaire. The model estimates do not identify an effect on insomnia severity after assigned oil consumption in Parkinson disease. [Primary report, Tables 4–5](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu12051364) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC7284844.txt)

## Lycopene-enriched olive oil and urinary or nocturnal outcomes

Carrasco and colleagues assigned no untreated comparison. All ten men with benign prostatic hyperplasia and ten healthy men received lycopene-enriched extra-virgin olive oil for 30 days. The questionnaire included urgency, frequency and nocturia, with results reported only for the total prostate symptom score. Its within-group changes were inconclusive. The recorded percentage of nighttime activity fell in both groups (p < .05), and sleep duration and depth showed no clear change. The publisher has an advance-print version and the indexed issue is November 2022. [Primary report, Figures 2–3 and Table 1](https://www.alternative-therapies.com/abstract/pdf/6322.pdf) · [Bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33421040/)

## Topical extra-virgin olive oil sleep trial registration

NCT05466045 first posted July 20, 2022. The registry planned topical extra-virgin olive oil or saline on itchy skin in people receiving hemodialysis, with sleep quality measured at baseline and at two and four weeks. The API record gives no posted results and has no update after first posting. A 2024 university thesis supplies the results for the same registration. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05466045) · [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT05466045.txt) · [Thesis results](#topical-extra-virgin-olive-oil-and-hemodialysis-sleep)

## Topical extra-virgin olive oil and hemodialysis sleep

Mei-Hua Pan's August 2024 thesis reports 81 participants in the registered comparison, 42 assigned to topical oil and 39 to saline. Figure 4-4 shows Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index means falling from 10.31 to 7.17 with oil and changing from 9.33 to 9.38 with saline. Table 4-7 gives the oil group's adjusted four-week change as −2.863 points (95% CI −4.851 to −0.874, p = .005) and the saline group's as +0.126 points (95% CI −0.202 to 0.455, p = .451). The table reports separate within-arm changes. The route, underlying condition and sleep-quality score differ from the frozen dietary Parkinson insomnia claim. [Original thesis, pp. 43–44 and registration appendix](https://irlib.ntunhs.edu.tw/retrieve/22065/112NTCN0563052-001.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2024-Pan-Topical-EVOO-Sleep-Thesis.txt) · [Official trial registration](#topical-extra-virgin-olive-oil-sleep-trial-registration)

## Refined olive oil cognition and task imaging

Kaddoumi and colleagues first published December 1, 2022. Twenty-five adults with mild cognitive impairment completed the six-month comparison of refined and extra-virgin olive oil. The Clinical Dementia Rating improved within both groups. Table 4 gives a refined-versus-extra-virgin difference-of-differences 95% CI of −0.344 to 0.068. The 1-back imaging task had no clear group-by-time activation interaction. The measured cognitive and imaging outcomes give no individual apathy, visual hallucination or clinical attention-impairment result. [Primary report, Methods, Results and Table 4](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu14235102) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC9736478.txt)

## Inhaled marjoram oil and mean arterial pressure

Lee and colleagues first published November 17, 2023. In an ICU nurse trial, Table 3 reports a mean arterial pressure change of −0.84 mmHg with marjoram aroma and −2.12 mmHg with the comparison aroma, with an inconclusive difference (p = .517). Staff rested before the measurement. The trial did not assess standing symptoms or intake of fresh marjoram. [Primary report, Methods and Table 3](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2023.1287282) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC10690939.txt)

## Olive oil grade and physical performance trial registration

NCT05485402 first posted August 3, 2022. The trial compares phenolic-rich extra-virgin olive oil with refined olive oil and includes a third oil-plus-prebiotic group. The protocol lists muscle mass and physical performance measures in adults with early sarcopenia. Its published gait outcome is available in the 2026 article. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05485402) · [2026 results](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Marjoram-Olive-Outcomes.md#refined-and-extra-virgin-olive-oil-and-gait-speed)

## Refined olive oil comparator in sleep actigraphy

Patan and colleagues studied healthy adults with refined olive-oil capsules as an oral placebo comparator for DHA- and EPA-rich products. The adjusted actigraphy means in the refined oil arm were 4.31 minutes sleep latency and 90.30% sleep efficiency at 26 weeks. The table compares this arm with marine oils, which cannot identify an olive-oil effect versus no added oil. The assigned grade was explicitly refined, and participants were healthy normal sleepers. [Local source text](Source-Text/PMC7830450.txt) · [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7830450/)

## Apathy ratings in a cannabis preparation carried in olive oil

Palmieri and Vadalà first posted May 26, 2022, and the matching article appeared in January 2023 (PMID 36655645). Both versions describe 30 Alzheimer patients receiving a cannabinoid preparation formulated with olive oil. The journal Methods specify sublingual administration and omit the oil grade. Figure 1 depicts lower caregiver-rated apathy during the uncontrolled treatment course. The analysis reports an unpaired test despite baseline and follow-up observations, and the text says one participant stopped while also saying everyone completed. The report supplies no standalone olive-oil arm or Parkinson patients. The preprint and published report describe the same cohort. NLM and the version-of-record PDF index DOI 10.7417/CT.2023.5009, while the publisher landing headline gives 10.7417/CT.2023.2497. ## Mediterranean diet score and apathy during quarantine

Di Santo and colleagues interviewed 126 older adults with subjective cognitive decline or mild cognitive impairment. Twelve met the Apathy Evaluation Scale threshold. MEDAS includes olive-oil intake within a wider diet score. Table 4 gives an unadjusted odds ratio of 3.02 (95% CI 0.86–10.63, p=.085) and an adjusted ratio of 2.76 (0.83–9.21, p=.099) for diet nonadherence and apathy. These contrasts are inconclusive. The Results paragraph calls its list of apathy associations significant, which differs from the reported diet comparison. The study does not estimate a refined olive-oil effect or measure a Parkinson group. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7591702/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2020-DiSanto-Mediterranean-Adherence-Apathy.txt)

## Registry for blended marjoram oil hand massage

First posted September 16, 2020. The trial assigned a blend of cedarwood, sweet marjoram and sweet orange oils in almond oil, almond oil alone, or usual care. The aromatic products were applied by hand and wrist massage in adults with rheumatoid arthritis. The protocol lists PSQI and Epworth sleepiness. It has no stated REM dream-enactment endpoint. A 2023 report expressly identifies this registration. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04549857) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2020-NCT04549857-Marjoram-Blend-Massage.txt)

## Reported sleep estimates after blended marjoram oil massage

Lu and colleagues randomized 102 adults with rheumatoid arthritis, and 95 completed. At one week Table 3 gives a PSQI change contrast between the oil blend massage and usual care of −1.19 points (95% CI −2.35 to −0.02, p=.046). The study includes a separate almond-oil massage arm. The oil blend combined three aromas, and PSQI and ESS results do not count dream enactment. The study supplies no effect of eating fresh marjoram in Parkinson disease. ## Injected marjoram leaf extract and mouse memory tasks

Wagdy and colleagues assigned eight Swiss albino mice per group to saline, injected LPS, hydroalcoholic marjoram leaf extract plus LPS, or extract alone. In the model arm versus the extract-plus-LPS arm, novel object discrimination ratios were 0.298 ± 0.011 and 0.545 ± 0.042 (p<.001), and Y-maze alternation values were 53.03 ± 3.032% and 68.87 ± 3.901% (p<.05). The unchallenged extract group had greater novel-object discrimination than saline. Both tasks assess aspects of memory in a neuroinflammation model. There was no separate sustained-attention test, oral fresh-leaf exposure or Parkinson cohort. [Original source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12906-023-03994-x) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2023-Wagdy-Marjoram-Mouse-Memory.txt)

## Olive oil vehicle and measured complex attention

Yoon and colleagues randomized 72 community participants aged 51–82 to hydroxytyrosol-rich desert olive tree pearls or cornstarch placebo for 12 weeks. Both products were gel granules dipped in olive oil. In Table 3, where lower complex-attention scores are better, the pearl group (n=35) changed from 19.1 to 13.5 and the placebo group (n=34) changed from 12.2 to 11.3. The adjusted group-by-time interaction was p=.049. The simple-attention and reaction-time interactions were p=.116 and p=.857. Different baseline scores, multiple endpoints and the common oil vehicle limit interpretation. The comparison tests the pearl formulation, with no oil-free group, verified refining grade or Parkinson subgroup. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10383185/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2023-Yoon-Olive-Vehicle-Attention-Factual-Extract.txt)

## MIND meals with extra virgin olive oil and planned attention measures

First posted May 31, 2024. The NURISH protocol compares meals following a MIND dietary pattern with healthy American-pattern meals in middle-aged adults. The MIND plan includes extra-virgin olive oil. Registered attention outcomes include flanker accuracy, reaction time and event-related P3 measures. Multiple ingredients change together. The protocol gives no isolated refined olive oil intervention, Parkinson cohort or posted results. ## Olive oil control in a registered attention and exercise study

First posted June 14, 2024. Healthy recreationally active women were assigned astaxanthin in an olive-oil softgel or an olive-oil placebo for four weeks. The registered cognitive outcomes include psychomotor vigilance, task switching and flanker performance. Both arms contain oil with no isolated refined-oil comparison or Parkinson subgroup.
