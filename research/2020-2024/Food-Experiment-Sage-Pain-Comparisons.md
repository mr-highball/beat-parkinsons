# Sage Pain Comparisons

## Sage essence massage and inhalation for labor pain

Nouri Monfaredi and colleagues published in 2022, volume 25(1), pp. 83–92 (DOI **10.22038/ijogi.2022.20104**). The accessible original abstract describes 96 participants randomized to sage essence inhalation, massage with sage essence or no assigned intervention. Both active groups reportedly had lower pain at the post-intervention assessments, with p=.001 for the group comparison and p=.917 between active methods. The trial lacked an inert aroma and sham massage. The abstract names Salvia essence without a verified Latin species. The full original tables, species and registry remain open. [Original English abstract](https://www.magiran.com/paper/2427530/the-effect-of-the-two-methods-of-massage-and-aromatherapy-with-salvia-essence-on-labor-pain-severity-in-nulliparous-women?lang=en) · [Published DOI](https://doi.org/10.22038/ijogi.2022.20104)

## Dry sage extract and joint and muscular discomfort

The 2020 Zeidabadi placebo-controlled comparison (PMID 32318472, IRCT2015111713940N2) assigned 66 postmenopausal women to dry sage extract or placebo and reports 59 completions. Table 2 reports a favorable post-treatment difference for the individual Menopause Rating Scale joint and muscular discomfort item (p<.001). The displayed values appear to reverse the order of means and dispersion. The text's approximate change also disagrees with subtraction of the values it calls means. The reported direction can be recorded, with a reliable numerical size of the difference still unresolved. This extract trial and the later fresh-tip extract study report a similar item direction in separate menopause cohorts. The tests do not cover fresh leaves in food or a Parkinson pain cohort. [Original trial and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7114003/#T2) · [Registration record](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#sage-and-vitex-menopause-trial-registration) · [Independent trial](Food-Experiment-Sage-Savory-Outcomes.md#fresh-sage-tip-extract-and-joint-and-muscular-discomfort)

## Echinacea and sage lozenges and acute throat pain

The 2023 original observational study (PMC10664317) followed 74 patients with acute sore throats using a combined echinacea and sage lozenge. Mean self-rated pain was 2.5 cm lower on the 10-cm scale after 90 minutes. At the end of treatment, 62 patients had a score below 1 cm. Without a comparator the changes cannot isolate the effect of the lozenge from the course of illness, and the two-botanical local preparation gives no separate sage effect or Parkinson pain result. [Original Methods and Figure 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10664317/)

## Sage tea mouth rinse and unreported pain comparisons in palliative care

Monsen and colleagues randomized 88 palliative cancer patients to a sage tea-based mouth rinse or normal saline and 73 completed. Fewer than 20 percent reported mouth or swallowing pain at baseline, and fewer than 8 percent rated it at least 4 on the numerical scale. The investigators excluded those symptoms from subsequent analyses. The reported dry-mouth effects therefore provide no between-group pain result. The preparation was a rinse and the cohort had advanced cancer. ## Oral fungal testing linked to the sage rinse project

The 2023 analysis used archived oral fungal samples from 45 of the 88 OralHAC participants. It identified 56 fungal isolates and reported in vitro susceptibility. Its outcomes are distinct from the randomized comparison and supply no sage-rinse pain estimate. ## Symptom reporting when an item is named or volunteered

The 2024 report uses different cohorts from 2013 and 2017 within the OralHAC project. When the form specifically listed a symptom, dry mouth, constipation, and sleep problems were reported more often than when they had to be volunteered in a single optional space. The observed rates depend on the instruments and cohort. This questionnaire comparison provides no food or rinse effect.
