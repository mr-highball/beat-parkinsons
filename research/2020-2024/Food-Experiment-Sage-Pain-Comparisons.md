# Sage Pain Comparisons

## Sage tea mouth rinse and unreported pain comparisons in palliative care

Monsen and colleagues randomized 88 palliative cancer patients to a sage tea-based mouth rinse or normal saline and 73 completed. Fewer than 20 percent reported mouth or swallowing pain at baseline, and fewer than 8 percent rated it at least 4 on the numerical scale. The investigators excluded those symptoms from subsequent analyses. The reported dry-mouth effects therefore provide no between-group pain result. The preparation was a rinse and the cohort had advanced cancer. ## Oral fungal testing linked to the sage rinse project

The 2023 analysis used archived oral fungal samples from 45 of the 88 OralHAC participants. It identified 56 fungal isolates and reported in vitro susceptibility. Its outcomes are distinct from the randomized comparison and supply no sage-rinse pain estimate. ## Symptom reporting when an item is named or volunteered

The 2024 report uses different cohorts from 2013 and 2017 within the OralHAC project. When the form specifically listed a symptom, dry mouth, constipation, and sleep problems were reported more often than when they had to be volunteered in a single optional space. The observed rates depend on the instruments and cohort. This questionnaire comparison provides no food or rinse effect.
