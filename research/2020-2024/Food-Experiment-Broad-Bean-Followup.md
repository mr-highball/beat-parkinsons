# Dyskinesia after broad bean ingestion in Parkinson’s disease

Lambea-Gil and colleagues reported a person with Parkinson’s who experienced generalized dyskinesias, sweating, tachycardia, and anxiety on two consecutive days after eating fresh sautéed broad beans (*Vicia faba*). The meals preceded symptoms by about 90 minutes. The patient and family reported a stable prescribed regimen. The authors considered levodopa in the beans a possible contributor. The report documents a repeated temporal association in one person. [Primary case report, PMID 34979720](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34979720/) · [University-hosted full article](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/111693/files/texto_completo.pdf)

This case adds to the acute dyskinesias seen in an earlier broad-bean feeding study and a 2013 case involving raw and stewed broad beans. The frozen food experiment has no *Vicia faba* exposure. ## Fava bean extract versus levodopa in a fixed sequence

Nikkhah and colleagues reported 30 people with Parkinson’s who all received fava bean extract first and conventional levodopa second. Short-term rigidity, gait and bradykinesia contrasts were inconclusive. The authors reported that conventional levodopa did better for rest tremor and freezing after one week (p = .025 and .046, respectively). The printed Table 2 means for those outcomes are lower in the extract column and the table lacks treatment-specific baselines, so the reported advantage cannot be independently reconstructed. The fixed treatment order, small sample, and levodopa already present in the extract limit inference about ordinary beans or an independent effect of other bean constituents. The matching 2015 conference abstract is the earliest results report located.
