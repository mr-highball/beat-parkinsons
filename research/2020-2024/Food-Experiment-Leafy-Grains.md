# Leafy vegetable and grain evidence, 2020–2024

## Spinach extract trial registered balance and published muscle strength

The SPISAR trial first appeared November 2, 2020 as NCT04612127. Balance is a secondary outcome in the registry. The 2021 journal report gives 23 spinach-extract and 22 placebo completers, all assigned moderate strength training. Table 3 shows greater improvement with the extract on several knee torque measures. The published paper did not give a balance result, and the registry had no results module. The trial used an extract rather than steamed spinach and supplies no separate Parkinson data. ## Buckwheat flour and maze learning in aged mice

The 2022 study compared several flours and starches in separate aging-prone mouse experiments. In the first experiment, mice assigned common buckwheat flour found the target hole sooner during the Barnes maze probe trial than aging-prone controls. In the second, the authors found a training advantage with buckwheat starch and flour and report separate probe and passive avoidance measures. This is spatial learning and memory evidence with milled products. It does not estimate attention or dyskinesia after toasted groats in Parkinson’s. [Primary article, Figures 1 and 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9269199/) · [Local CC BY text](Source-Text/PMC9269199.txt)

## Tremor with celiac disease and thyroid dysfunction

The first 2020 case describes neck tremor at rest that increased during movement, with ataxia and dystonia. The patient was advised to avoid gluten and simultaneously received rehabilitation, medication and thyroid treatment. The report describes some tremor improvement at four weeks. It gives no cooked wheat berry exposure or isolated dietary contrast and did not describe idiopathic Parkinson’s in this case. [Primary report, Case 1](https://applications.emro.who.int/imemrf/572/Pak-J-Neurol-Sci-2020-15-2-30-34-eng.pdf)

## Hordenine and gait in a Parkinson mouse model

Li and colleagues tested isolated hordenine, a constituent reported in germinated barley and beer, in several experimental models. Figure 5 describes better stride length and walking parameters with hordenine in MPTP mice. The compound was studied for motor outcomes. The paper does not test boiled barley or give a lightheadedness or anxiety estimate. [Primary paper, Figure 5](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1002/ptr.7790)

## Prodromal outcomes by diet pattern

Molsberry and colleagues evaluated diet indices in people without diagnosed Parkinson’s. Probable REM sleep behavior disorder was questionnaire-defined, and hyposmia was tested in a subset. The reported vegetable association is for a diet component and number of prodromal features. The study does not provide a chard-specific contrast for dream enactment or smell, and the grain component is pooled. [Primary report, exposure and outcome methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7713746/)

## Parkinson diet patterns motivation and pooled sofrito

Published December 6, 2022. In the Parkinsonism survey, Mediterranean and MIND scores were associated with lower self-rated loss of motivation. The estimates per index point were −1.39 and −2.46. The pooled sofrito question accepts tomato sauce and may cover several vegetables. Its comparison on the overall symptom score was inconclusive, with adjusted p=.5. It does not resolve braised leek intake or a leek-specific apathy outcome. [Original survey and supplements](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9739738/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2022-Fox-Parkinson-Motivation-Sofrito-Table-Extract.txt)

## Pearl barley as a shared carrier in an olanzapine experiment

Shagdarova and colleagues published October 16, 2024. Female mice received pearl barley porridge with peanut oil as the carrier for control, olanzapine or olanzapine plus one of three experimental additives. The open-field test counted sector crossings and rearings under light and dark conditions. The paper presents these as locomotor measures and gives comparisons of the additives. All groups shared the porridge. No separate barley, peanut-oil or clinical anxiety effect was estimated. [Original source](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11504887/fullTextXML) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2024-Shagdarova-Pearl-Barley-Olanzapine-Carrier.txt)

## Boiled waxy barley and tension–anxiety in healthy adults

Araki and colleagues published the exploratory randomized trial on July 17, 2024 (PMID 39064742, DOI 10.3390/nu16142298, UMIN000049817). Healthy Japanese adults received 100 g/day of packaged cooked Daishimochi waxy barley or cooked white rice for eight weeks. The public Japanese registration identifies the preparation as boiled barley. Participants reheated the assigned food by microwave and could add rice when their usual serving exceeded 100 g. The foods differed visibly. Research staff were masked through data fixation. Eight of 62 randomized participants were excluded for protocol deviations, leaving 27 analyzed per arm. [Primary report, §§2.1–2.3](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/16/14/2298) · [Official Japanese record](https://rctportal.mhlw.go.jp/detail/um?trial_id=UMIN000049817)

Figure 4 reports a more favorable between-group change in the self-rated POMS 2 tension–anxiety score with barley (Mann–Whitney p<.05). Table S3 gives baseline and final median T-scores of 42 (39–53) and 43 (39–49) with barley, and 40 (36–45) and 43 (40–48) with rice. The week-eight between-group comparison was p=.856. The change result and final-score result are distinct comparisons. Mood was in the authors’ primary questionnaire domain alongside respiratory symptoms, with several separately tested subscales. The paper gives no confidence interval for the anxiety subscale contrast or multiplicity adjustment in the named analyses. The registry history gives entry in December 2022 and public disclosure on December 31, 2024, after publication. The registry’s broad condition and stress outcome leaves the exact subscale prespecification uncertain. There is no Parkinson cohort or Parkinson-specific anxiety scale. ## Barley beta-glucan in a high-fat diet and stress model

Baroni and colleagues reported ICARE 2022 conference abstract A141 (PMID 37386671, DOI 10.1186/s44158-022-00070-7). The groups were ordinary diet controls (n=5), high-fat diet plus stress (n=6), and the same high-fat diet plus stress with 3% barley-derived beta-glucan (n=6). The authors reported more typical anxiety-related and spatial-memory behavior with the supplement versus the model group (p<.05), referring to the elevated plus maze and Y-maze. The abstract supplies no group behavioral values, separate test-specific p-values, or figures. This is a dietary constituent in an animal model of high-fat diet and stress. It supplies no boiled-barley or Parkinson anxiety estimate. [Original conference member A141](Source-Text/PMC9614771.txt#L5401) · [Complete original proceedings](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9614771/)

The NP03 poster in the 2021 meeting proceedings, published in 2022, reports closely matching investigators, timing, exposure and maze findings. The author institution categorizes Vascular Pharmacology DOI 10.1016/j.vph.2022.107031 as an abstract and provides no file. Available publisher metadata leaves its behavioral result and cohort relation unverified. The reports are counted as one study family pending cohort identification. ## Celiac-related neuropathy and Parkinson symptoms after combined care

The 2020 Pullman congress case reports improved neuropathy after immunoglobulin and gluten withdrawal in a person with biopsy-confirmed celiac disease and separately treated Parkinson disease. Parkinson symptoms remained largely stable with slow progression and later dyskinesia. The case supplies no wheat berry comparison or rest-tremor score. The results distinguish the reported response of neuropathy from the recorded Parkinson course.
