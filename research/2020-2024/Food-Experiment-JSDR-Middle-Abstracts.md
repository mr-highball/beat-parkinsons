# Swallowing and food in the 2021 and 2022 Japanese proceedings

## 2021 jelly and two liquid consistencies around intestinal levodopa

Dōtsu and colleagues compared videofluoroscopic dysphagia scores before and after levodopa-carbidopa intestinal gel in 12 people with Parkinson’s. Scores did not change significantly for any test consistency. After treatment, higher levodopa-equivalent daily dose correlated with higher oral-phase impairment scores with potage-thick liquid (r = .673), unthickened liquid (r = .663), and jelly (r = .597). The study compared medication periods and did not assign diets or identify culinary ingredients. A 2018 report from the same hospital with overlapping authors recruited six people starting intestinal gel from 2015 to 2017 and a separate group starting deep brain stimulation. The 2021 study enrolled from 2016 over four years. Patient-level overlap is possible. [2021 primary abstract JO15-1, p. S117](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no26-27_abstracts.pdf#page=118) · [2018 comparison O15-4, p. S108](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no24_abstracts.pdf#page=109)

## 2021 steamed bread with dietary support

Nakano and colleagues described a woman in her nineties with Parkinson’s, dementia, osteoporosis and renal impairment. She had stopped accepting blended food. During 28 nutrition visits over two years and nine months, her team adjusted textures and used a favored convenience-store steamed bread alongside a commercial concentrated liquid supplement. The authors reported that BMI rose from 17.8 to 24.5 and that she continued eating the bread near the end of life. They also described slower Parkinson progression without providing an objective progression score or a comparison. The flour, bread recipe, food-specific swallowing outcome, and the separate contribution of bread were unreported. [2021 primary abstract JP-102, p. S235](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no26-27_abstracts.pdf#page=236)

## 2021 sushi and modified steak in Parkinson cases

Nagamine compared typical modified meals, a softer code 3 meal, and sushi in two residents with dysphagia. One had Parkinson’s and the other had bilateral cerebellar infarction. The abstract reports no coughing or sensation of pharyngeal residue with sushi in either case. Chewing counts and the time from taking a bite to swallowing increased. It does not name the fillings, quantify each resident’s results, or give an instrumental aspiration assessment. The broader observation that 20 of 21 residents with food refusal accepted sushi pools diagnoses. [2021 primary abstract JP-177, p. S267](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no26-27_abstracts.pdf#page=268)

Izutsu and colleagues described a 70-year-old man with Parkinson’s and cognitive impairment who relied on both gastrostomy and oral feeding. The team adjusted the texture of a requested steak course after several trials. He finished the meal without a reported choking or aspiration incident. The preparation method, meat type, and instrumental aspiration results were unspecified. [2021 primary abstract JO26-6, pp. S150–S151](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no26-27_abstracts.pdf#page=151) · [Continuation, PDF p. 152](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no26-27_abstracts.pdf#page=152)

## 2021 medication jelly and thickened water across diagnoses

Watanabe and Kono compared a grape-flavored medication-aid jelly, water, and water prepared with a named thickener in 71 people referred for dysphagia. Five had Parkinson’s. The jelly had better overall endoscopic Hyodo swallowing scores than thickened water (P < .001). The abstract also reports a significant comparison among people with moderate to severe dysphagia. It does not give separate results for Parkinson’s, specify whole grape content, or test the effects of eating a frozen ingredient. [2021 primary abstract JO10-5, pp. S106–S107](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no26-27_abstracts.pdf#page=107) · [Continuation, PDF p. 108](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no26-27_abstracts.pdf#page=108)

## 2022 liquid bolus timing across Parkinson cohorts

Sugishita and colleagues analyzed liquid swallowing on videofluoroscopy in 84 participants with Parkinson’s and 151 orally fed participants without pathological findings. Onset of epiglottis and esophageal inlet motion was delayed in the Parkinson group, and the epiglottis delay was associated with pharyngeal impairment. The same authors reported 83 Parkinson participants and 151 nonpathological participants alongside a sarcopenia group in 2023. Their 2025 article analyzed 3 mL liquid boluses in 71 people with Parkinson’s selected for absence of aspiration on videofluoroscopy, 151 controls, and 45 with sarcopenia. The matching control count, authors and measurements indicate likely overlap. Neither the 2022 abstract nor the publicly readable 2025 abstract identifies the liquid’s composition. The studies compare diagnostic groups and supply no food effect. [2022 primary abstract O42-1, p. S197](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no28_abstracts.pdf#page=198) · [2023 companion report, p. S281](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no29_abstracts.pdf#page=282) · [2025 journal report](https://doi.org/10.2490/jjrmc.24023)

## 2022 prepared porridge, alternating liquid, and rehabilitation

Takuma described a 78-year-old man with Parkinson’s and severe weight loss after pneumonia. Prominent pharyngeal residue led the team to change mousse-texture meals to blended food. A later menu combined blended rice porridge with one modified-texture main dish and nutritional supplements. Repeat videofluoroscopy at one month showed no aspiration up to the soft-dish level, and alternating liquid swallows helped clear residual material. The report covers rehabilitation, posture, meal format, and caloric support together. It provides no isolated response to porridge, liquid thickening, or another ingredient. [2022 primary abstract P15-9, p. S281](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no28_abstracts.pdf#page=282)

## 2022 ground red chili in mochi and manometry in healthy adults

Taniguchi and colleagues reported a fixed-order comparison in 12 healthy adults who first ate five softened mochi portions without added chili and then five with chili. The 2024 funder report identifies the treated bolus as a 5 g rice cake mixed with 0.2 g of House Foods *ichimi togarashi*, a commercial ground red chili. The reports use the label capsaicin for the treatment. The capsaicin content and pepper cultivar remain unverified. The 2022 abstract and 2025 paper give the same sample, order, and chewing results. Mean chewing counts were 20.3 with chili and 22.4 without it. Mean chewing times were 14.2 and 15.4 seconds. Both reports give higher hypopharyngeal and proximal esophageal contractile integrals and longer upper esophageal sphincter relaxation with chili. The 2022 abstract gives 496.7 versus 429.8 milliseconds for relaxation, while the 2025 paper gives 486.7 versus 431.2 milliseconds. These physiological measures in healthy adults provide indirect context for `0724`, which specifies ground cayenne and swallowing difficulty in Parkinson’s. [2022 primary abstract P1-9, p. S210](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no28_abstracts.pdf#page=211) · [2024 primary funder report, Methods 2-2 and Figure 1](https://www.urakamizaidan.or.jp/research/jisseki/vol31/vol.31_urakami_08taniguchi.pdf) · [2025 primary journal report, PMID 39861968](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39861968/)

## 2022 mashed potato powder in a food-texture experiment

Sei and colleagues mixed dried mashed potato, carrot, or broccoli powders with water and a commercial gelling agent for 3D food printing. The potato formulation alone reached L4 in the swallowing-food pyramid. All three reached Universal Design Food category 4, and none met the cited Japanese approval criteria for foods for people with swallowing difficulty. The outcomes were physical measurements, including hardness, adhesiveness, and cohesiveness. This provides preparation context for `0290`. The investigators did not test baked potato or measure swallowing in any patient. [2022 primary abstract P1-2, p. S207](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no28_abstracts.pdf#page=208)
