# Posted registry outcomes for capsaicin cough and swallowing assessment

NCT03321019 posted results on October 10, 2024. People with Parkinson’s underwent an aerosolized capsaicin cough challenge and a separate fluoroscopic swallowing assessment. At the first visit, mean urge-to-cough sensitivity was 0.22 points per micromolar capsaicin among 100 participants with Parkinson’s and 0.31 among 19 healthy controls. Median Penetration–Aspiration Scale scores were 2 and 1, respectively. Controls attended one visit. In the Parkinson’s group, the aspiration outcome was available for 89 participants at the second visit and 70 at the third. The median remained 2 at both visits. These are descriptive comparisons between groups and visits. [Posted results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03321019?tab=results) · [Local registry text](../2015-2019/Source-Text/Trials/NCT03321019.txt)

Capsaicin was a diagnostic stimulus and the study evaluated swallowing separately. It did not test whether ingesting ground cayenne changes swallowing difficulty severity, the outcome in frozen claim `0724`. The prior NCT02183519 challenge measured cough airflow. A separate oral capsaicin-wafer study used a different preparation and reported no clear swallowing-timing signal in its Parkinson’s subgroup.
