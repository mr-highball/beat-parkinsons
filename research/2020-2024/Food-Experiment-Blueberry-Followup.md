# Berry trial follow-up, 2020–2024

## Blueberry powder and constipation in functional gastrointestinal disorders

NCT04824976 first posted April 1, 2021, following a January 1, 2020 actual start. The Bern crossover trial registered the Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale (GSRS) as primary and compared blueberry powder with a matched placebo in functional gastrointestinal disorders. The registry still listed unknown status and no posted results in November 2026. The 2023 report explicitly identifies the trial. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04824976) [Registry metadata](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT04824976)

Wilder-Smith and colleagues reported 43 evaluable patients with irritable bowel syndrome, functional dyspepsia or both on May 20, 2023. The six-week periods compared 30 g/day freeze-dried blueberry powder made with Tifblue and Rubel berries with an energy- and sugar-matched placebo. The GSRS constipation difference was −0.3 (95% CI −0.7 to 0.2, p = .19). Bristol stool scores in the constipation category occurred in 2 of 43 after blueberry and 7 of 43 after placebo (p = .10 for the overall three-category comparison). Overall abdominal symptom relief was reported by 53% and 30%, respectively (p = .03). That global endpoint appears as primary in the paper while the public registry lists the GSRS. The population and preparation differ from raw blueberry for Parkinson constipation, claim 0746. [Primary report, PMID 37242279](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10223779/) [Local CC BY results and Table 2](Source-Text/PMC10223779.txt#L256)

## Constipation during blueberry powder supplementation in mild cognitive impairment

NCT05172128 first posted on December 29, 2021. Doraiswamy et al. reported an open-label study of 36 g/day freeze-dried blueberry powder in ten people with amnestic mild cognitive impairment on August 23, 2023. One participant’s constipation and stool changes resolved after a week off the powder. Another participant’s mild constipation resolved spontaneously. The registry’s September 19, 2024 results list two participants affected and label the adverse-event assessment non-systematic. The study had no comparator, no Parkinson cohort, and no constipation severity outcome. [2023 primary report, Adverse Events](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10450204/) · [Local CC BY article text](Source-Text/PMC10450204.txt#L210) · [2021 registration and 2024 results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT05172128)

## Blueberry trials with planned gastrointestinal safety checks

The Berries and Steps pilot NCT05735587 was first posted February 21, 2023, before the recorded start on April 5. It compared freeze-dried blueberry powder and matched placebo over twelve weeks in sedentary older adults with depressive symptoms. The ABSORB crossover NCT05937165 first posted July 10, 2023 and tested two doses of freeze-dried blueberry powder for three days each. Its dated protocol planned to ask about gastrointestinal symptoms, including constipation, at visits. The dates printed on the protocols describe document versions. Their public results were first posted in 2025. [Berries and Steps registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05735587) · [ABSORB registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05937165) · [ABSORB protocol, safety monitoring](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/65/NCT05937165/Prot_SAP_000.pdf) · [Dated results note](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#blueberry-powder-and-constipation-in-older-adults)

## Mixed berries and constipation in distinct conference abstracts

2021-12-17. The original GSA program’s page 864 grouped blueberries, blackberries and raspberries to estimate changes in depressive symptoms. The constipation references on pages 20 and 791 came from separately authored palliative-care and pharmacoepidemiology abstracts. The conference context supplies no blueberry or constipation estimate applicable to 0746. [Original supplement and proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1093/geroni/igab046) · [Local source text](Source-Text/2021-GSA-Abstract-Book-Berry-Context.txt)

## Blueberries in a shared test meal and bowel diaries

2024-11-27. Procházková and colleagues followed 61 healthy adults in the PRIMA observational study. Daily diaries included Bristol stool form, stool frequency and constipation visual analogue ratings. On the test visit, every participant received a mixed meal including blueberries, yogurt, nuts, bread and egg. The Methods do not specify fresh versus frozen berries. All participants shared the multi-ingredient meal, so the collected bowel measures supply no blueberry-specific estimate. [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-024-01856-x)
