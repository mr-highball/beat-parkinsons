# Ginger preparations and measured anxiety outside Parkinson disease

## Ginger extract and anxiety in an Iranian migraine trial

Helli and colleagues first published April 18, 2022. The double-blind study randomized 110 people with migraine. Both groups received propranolol. The additional comparison used dried ginger rhizome extract tablets or starch placebo. At day 90, 51 ginger and 52 placebo participants remained. Table 2 gives mean Beck Anxiety Inventory scores of 17.2 versus 17.1 (p = .84). The study measured anxiety in its migraine cohort and cannot estimate a Parkinson-specific effect. [Local outcome extract](Source-Text/2022-Helli-Ginger-Migraine-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Original trial, Methods and Table 2](https://brieflands.com/journals/jjnpp/articles/120449)

## Ginger root powder and anxiety during chemotherapy

Crichton and colleagues published online September 10, 2023. They compared standardized, oral ginger root powder capsules with placebo in 103 adults receiving chemotherapy. On the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale, Table 6 reports between-arm differences of −0.84 points (95% CI −2.05 to 0.37), −0.28 (−1.59 to 1.03) and −0.63 (−2.15 to 0.89) at the three assessed cycles. The time-by-group comparison was inconclusive (p = .352). This was an acute post-chemotherapy measure in a standardized powder study. ## Ginger rhizome powder adjunct for generalized anxiety

Ataei and colleagues first published the article November 2, 2023. The study randomized 80 patients with generalized anxiety disorder to ginger capsules or starch capsules with an SSRI in both arms. The publisher abstract says the groups differed after treatment and the ginger arm's anxiety score increased from baseline, while its conclusion describes anxiety reduction. The original Hamilton scores and between-arm direction are inaccessible through the public publisher view. The publisher abstract explicitly identifies the registration as IRCT20201108049306N1. The registry specifies ginger rhizome powder, with sertraline or citalopram as background treatment. Fresh grated ginger is unverified. [Local abstract extract](Source-Text/2023-Ataei-Ginger-Anxiety-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Publisher original abstract](https://www.benthamscience.com/article/135835) · [Official registration](https://en.irct.ir/trial/53072)

## Generalized anxiety trial registration and measurement

IRCT20201108049306N1 was first registered January 29, 2021. The protocol scheduled Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale assessments at baseline and four follow-up visits over 12 weeks in adults receiving an SSRI. The protocol confirms formulation and intended endpoints. It gives no original study outcomes and cannot resolve the publication's conflicting language. [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/IRCT20201108049306N1.txt) · [Official registration](https://en.irct.ir/trial/53072)

## Injected isolated 6-shogaol in a head trauma model

Afzal and colleagues first published December 28, 2021. They administered isolated 6-shogaol intraperitoneally to groups of six mice after experimental traumatic brain injury. Table 1 shows more open-arm entries, with 2.10 in injured controls versus 6.00 to 7.70 across the three treated groups. The lowest-dose significance marking conflicts with the Results prose. Table 2 gives lower ambulation counts in treated groups, while the prose describes an increase. The change in ambulation complicates interpretation of the exploration-based proxy. This is an isolated compound, injection route and head-trauma model. [Local outcome extract](Source-Text/2021-Afzal-Shogaol-Anxiety-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Original article, Tables 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8756802/)

## Iranian migraine registration and preparation wording

IRCT20200126046263N1 was registered February 18, 2020 while recruiting. Its protocol says ginger powder tablets and lists migraine impact, frequency and intensity. The linked 2022 Helli paper describes a dried extract and coadministration of propranolol. It reports anxiety findings that the public registry's primary and secondary outcomes do not enumerate. [Local official fields](Source-Text/Trials/IRCT20200126046263N1.txt) · [Public registration](https://en.irct.ir/trial/45399)

## Ginger capsules and anxiety before burn dressing

Noormohammadi and colleagues randomized 120 burn inpatients across ginger capsules, starch placebo and a prayer group. The publisher records February 15, 2022 for its online publication and 2021 for the issue. Table V's ginger-minus-placebo differences on the Spielberger anxiety measure were +1.22 at admission, −0.89 on day 3, and +1.06 on day 6 (p = .761, .933, and .910). These visit-level contrasts are inconclusive, and the paper gives no group-by-time estimate. The article's broad conclusion about anxiety exceeds the evidence from its reported placebo contrasts. It does not establish the ginger species or whether the capsule contained ground rhizome or an extract. [Local outcome extract](Source-Text/2022-Noormohammadi-Ginger-Burn-Anxiety-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Original report, Tables IV–V](https://www.przeglepidemiol.pzh.gov.pl/pdf-181025-101586?filename=Comparison+of+the+effect.pdf)

## Bitter ginger extract and anxiety in advanced cancer

The de Queiroz study enrolled 35 people with advanced solid tumors, of whom 16 completed eight weeks. The 2024 article abstract reports improved HADS anxiety scores (p = .032) for this single cohort and does not provide the score change. The investigators' poster describes oral capsules of *Zingiber zerumbet* rhizome extract. The article abstract uses wording that may refer to isolated zerumbone. The capsule composition and actual amount of its constituent remain unclear. An earlier 2023 oncology conference abstract matches the sample and outcomes, with an unverified exact online posting date. These sources describe one uncontrolled study of a different ginger species. [Local outcome extract](Source-Text/2024-deQueiroz-Bitter-Ginger-Anxiety-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Primary publication abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38199583/) · [Investigators' formulation poster](https://simul-europe.com/2024/mascc/Files/1542.pdf)

## Ginger drink and categorical postoperative anxiety

A single-blind Indonesian trial randomized 40 surgical patients to a high-calorie drink with or without ginger extract. In original Table 1 the no-anxiety HARS category was 18/20 versus 14/20 before surgery and 20/20 versus 14/20 after surgery. The postoperative categorical comparison had p = .0293 with baseline imbalance and no reported adjusted change. Postoperative anxiety, oral extract and severity categories do not directly test grated ginger in Parkinson disease. [Local source text](Source-Text/PMC9758370.txt) · [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9758370/)

## Healthy adult powder trial and secondary anxiety outcome

Crichton and colleagues studied 51 adults without a medical condition in a placebo-controlled trial of standardized ginger root powder for two weeks. Anxiety was preidentified as a secondary outcome. The author manuscript in the Bond thesis gives DASS-21 anxiety change medians of −1 with ginger and 0 with placebo, with Mann–Whitney U = 286 and p = .511. The short comparison enrolled healthy adults with generally low baseline anxiety. These data leave the change comparison inconclusive. Indexed journal Table 3 confirms the values and rounds p to .51. This trial differs from the separate Crichton chemotherapy trial and enrolled no Parkinson patients. The paper cites ACTRN12620000302954p. [Local source text](Source-Text/2023-Crichton-Ginger-Healthy-Anxiety.txt) · [Original manuscript in the Bond thesis, Table 2](https://pure.bond.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/223955016/Megan_Crichton_Thesis.pdf) · [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tjnut.2023.09.002)

## Ginger-containing blend and anxiety in opioid maintenance

The trial randomized 81 people after assisted opioid detoxification to Hab-o Shefa or placebo. The abstract reports 41 completers and a favorable treatment-by-time Hamilton anxiety contrast (reported F = 2.58, p = .035). The product contains four herbs including ginger. The comparison is for the mixture. It yields no separately attributable ginger or Parkinson effect. [Local source text](Source-Text/2020-Moosavyzadeh-Ginger-Blend-Opioid-Anxiety.txt) · [Original source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32109133/)

## Aqueous ginger rhizome extract and rat open-arm time

Bano, Sharif and Badawy published June 1, 2021. The authors prepared a filtered aqueous extract from peeled supermarket ginger rhizomes without formal botanical authentication or constituent quantification. Six male rats per arm received oral extract equivalent to 500 mg/kg of ginger material daily or saline for three weeks. Table 3 open-arm time was 30 ± 0.6 versus 20 ± 0.5 seconds (mean ± SEM). The authors report a significant increase and label the table comparison p < .001. Open-field crossings were 117 ± 15 versus 120 ± 20 and were not significantly altered. The preparation was filtered extract and the outcome an animal behavioral proxy. [Original source](https://www.nepjol.info/index.php/AJMS/article/view/34480/28861) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2021-Bano-Ginger-Aqueous-Open-Arm.txt)

## Topical fresh ginger juice in a mixed irritable bowel syndrome intervention

Shi and colleagues published September 12, 2021. The author abstract describes 150 adults randomized equally to fire needling plus topical acupoint therapy, topical acupoint therapy alone or oral pinaverium bromide. It reports 14, 16 and 13 dropouts, respectively. The topical plaster mixed fresh ginger juice with a multicomponent herbal powder. The abstract reports higher IBS quality-of-life anxiety-related item scores with topical treatment than oral medication at days 7 and 28 (p<.05) without numerical item values. This is a shared or combined topical exposure in IBS, with no isolated fresh-ginger group or Parkinson anxiety scale. The original article body remains open. [Original source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34491647/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2021-Shi-Topical-Ginger-IBS-Anxiety.txt)

## Dietary gingerol extract and open-field behavior in diabetic neuropathy

In a male rat model of diabetic neuropathy, dietary gingerol-enriched extract was associated with more open-field center time and entries than the neuropathy control. Figure 1 reports n = 6–8 per group. The Methods describe eight weeks of supplementation, while the Results reference five weeks. The outcome is a behavioral proxy in a neuropathy model and the preparation is a concentrated extract.
