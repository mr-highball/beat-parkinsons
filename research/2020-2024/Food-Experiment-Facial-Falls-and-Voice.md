# Facial expression, falls, voice and related food outcomes

## Cold-pressed olive oil and anxiety in the DieTBra trial

Canheta et al. followed adults with severe obesity in three active arms for 12 weeks. The oil-only arm had a reported 38.23% within-arm decrease in HADS anxiety prevalence (p=.019), with decreases of 45.83% and 33.33% in the traditional-diet and combined arms. The primary companion report verifies the parent's 52 mL/day cold-pressed extra-virgin oil supplied unheated. The exact anxiety table was unavailable. An original published letter reports post-treatment prevalence favored the active diet alone. It questions using within-arm tests as an oil effect, and it gives no verified baseline-adjusted anxiety estimate. An author reply exists without a body recovered. The cohort has no matched Parkinson comparison. [Original source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32675019/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2020-Canheta-Olive-Anxiety-Original-Extract.txt)

## Original DieTBra companion confirms the oil preparation

Rodrigues et al. identify the cold-pressed, unheated extra-virgin olive oil, <0.2% acidity and packed in 13 mL sachets, and the 52 mL/day allocation. This original companion reports cardiometabolic outcomes. Its intervention verification belongs to the same parent study as the anxiety report. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7284483/) · [Local text](Source-Text/PMC7284483.txt)

## Black pepper extract and caffeine-challenge sleep

Yoon et al. used oral freeze-dried 70% ethanolic pepper extract in a mouse caffeine challenge. The EEG and EMG groups had 7–8 mice. Pepper extract plus caffeine gave more non-REM sleep and less wake time than caffeine alone. Vehicle contrasts did not clearly differ for sleep-onset in the combination. The EEG figure has no pepper-alone intervention, and the model used a concentrated fruit extract rather than ground pepper, without Parkinson disease or chronic insomnia. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9183155/) · [Local text](Source-Text/PMC9183155.txt)

## Baseline Parkinson olive oil anxiety and related item pairs

The investigator report's duplicate baseline PD-only heatmaps show a small inverse association between unspecified olive-oil frequency and self-reported anxiety. They also show a very small inverse coconut-oil and dyskinesia association, a near-zero positive turkey and broad speech association, and a near-zero inverse generic spice and insomnia association. The source gives no numerical result with pairwise uncertainty, adjustment, individual spice, cooking method, oil grade or an intelligibility-specific speech measure. The report's positive canned fruit and falls association applies to its own pooled exposure. [Original source](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/14919326-8cbc-4a3e-88d4-0f609019e178/MVP%20Study%2010-Year%20Summary-c634e33.pdf#page=64) · [Local text](Source-Text/2024-MVP-Oils-Voice-and-Sleep-Figure-Extract.txt)

## Turmeric use in a vocal hygiene questionnaire

Akgöl et al. included 406 professional or amateur voice users. Table 3 records turmeric products in 69 of 253 people who answered the herbal-use items. The authors compare use by occupation and describe hoarseness care. They did not measure a turmeric-specific acoustic change, identify a ground preparation or report a Parkinson cohort. First online November 10, 2022 even though the issue is dated 2025. [Original source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/365309111_Determining_Knowledge_Level_Attitude_Behaviors_Regarding_Vocal_Hygiene_and_Use_of_Medical_Drugs_and_Herbal_Medicinal_Products_for_Vocal_Health_in_Second_Level_Professional_Voice_Users) · [Local text](Source-Text/2022-Akgol-Turmeric-Voice-Extract.txt)

## DieTBra anxiety analysis critique

Eckert's original letter describes post-treatment anxiety prevalence favoring the diet-only active comparator and questions inference from separate within-arm tests. The original arm table remains inaccessible, so the between-arm effect size and how it accounts for baseline are unresolved. The authors' published reply was indexed and its body was not recovered. [Original source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/346132060_Letter_to_the_editor_Inadequate_statistical_inferences_in_the_randomized_clinical_trial_by_Canheta_et_al) · [Local text](Source-Text/2020-Eckert-DieTBra-Critique-Extract.txt)

## Sage oil aromatherapy with normal smell at enrollment

The randomized hospital study enrolled 174 adults with unimpaired smell and assigned common sage essential-oil inhalation or usual care. Ordinary evaluation measured inpatient satisfaction. The study gives no change in clinical olfactory function and no ground-sage ingestion.
