# Cumin formulations and swallowing-related outcomes

## Registered preliminary cumin and ginger oral spray

First posted August 10, 2021. The protocol describes a preliminary three-formulation saliva experiment in 21 healthy adults and a subsequent herbal versus commercial comparison targeting 112 adults. The 2024 report matches the first phase and the 2026 report names this registration for its healthy safety phase. The same registration does not establish shared participants. Cumin seed extract is combined with ginger in an artificial saliva product, with no toasted-cumin ingestion or swallowing endpoint. [Local factual extract](Source-Text/Trials/2021-NCT04997889-Cumin-Ginger-Saliva.txt) · [Official healthy-volunteer saliva registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04997889)

## Three herbal saliva formulations tested in healthy adults

The 2024 study followed laboratory and nonhuman preparation work with a small clinical study of three mixed cumin and ginger oral formulations in 21 healthy adults. It measured salivary stimulation and safety. This corresponds closely to the preliminary phase of NCT04997889. Clinical swallowing evaluation, culinary spice consumption and Parkinson disease were outside the accessible account. [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2024-Napavichayanun-Cumin-Ginger-Saliva-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Napavichayanun and colleagues, primary indexed report](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eujim.2024.102396)

## Registered crossover saliva study in people receiving hemodialysis

First posted June 26, 2024. The crossover design compares a cumin-ginger artificial saliva and placebo in people with hemodialysis-associated xerostomia. Protocol 0763/66 matches the later 2026 report's review-board identifier and design. The US registry showed Unknown recruitment status and no posted results. The primary report describes the clinical result separately. [Local factual extract](Source-Text/Trials/2024-NCT06476223-Hemodialysis-Saliva.txt) · [Official hemodialysis saliva registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06476223)
