# Canola and rapeseed oil in fatigue research

## Rapeseed oil in both arms of an ubiquinol fatigue comparison

First published June 2, 2020. Participants with mild fatigue received placebo or one of two ubiquinol doses. All formulations contained rapeseed oil, with additional oil used in placebo in place of ubiquinol. At week 12, reported mean change in post-task fatigue VAS was −2.1 for placebo, −10.9 for the lower dose and −3.6 for the higher dose. Neurologic disease was excluded. The protocol supplies no known refined grade or zero-oil comparison, and the measured effects cannot be assigned to oil alone. ## Registered canola comparator in a ketone ester frailty trial

First posted October 17, 2024. The registered placebo contains canola oil and is compared with a ketone ester in older adults with frailty. Protocol endpoints include a fatigue scale and leg-press fatigue testing. The record shows Recruiting and no posted results. It supplies no oil grade, zero-oil comparison or Parkinson enrollment. [Local factual extract](Source-Text/Trials/2024-NCT06645847-Canola-Fatigue.txt) · [Official frailty trial registration and arm description](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06645847)

## Registered ketone ester pilot with a matched comparison beverage

First posted October 19, 2022. The healthy older-adult trial lists a calorie-, appearance- and taste-matched nonketone beverage and registered tolerance, safety and ketone outcomes. The public arm description does not identify canola. Its published exploratory outcomes identify the comparison beverage as canola-containing and give physical fatigability outcomes. Those results appear in the 2025 bucket.
