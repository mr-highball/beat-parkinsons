# Botanical preparations in Parkinson’s models, 2020–2024

## Sunflower oil as a vehicle in a mushroom extract rat study

Aslam and colleagues published a rat study June 29, 2021. The study compared four groups of six rats, with injected sunflower oil alone as a vehicle control, rotenone dissolved in the oil as a lesion control, and two groups receiving a mushroom extract with rotenone. The authors report changes in catalepsy, rotarod performance, exploration and locomotion with aqueous *Agaricus blazei* extract. The sunflower oil was administered by injection and served as a vehicle, with no isolated comparison of sunflower oil against another vehicle or untreated control. The study gives no refining grade or clinical rigidity measurement. [Published study](https://journaljpri.com/index.php/JPRI/article/view/2633) · [Review manuscript, Methods 2.5–2.7](https://www.sdiarticle4.com/prh/doc/Ms_JPRI_69598.pdf)

## Nasco grape pomace nutriosomes and dopaminergic markers

Parekh and colleagues published the study on August 17, 2022. The formulation included extract from white Nasco grape pomace, soy phospholipid, olive oil and Nutriose. The investigators administered the extract intragastrically in nutriosomes or as a suspension. At 100 mg/kg extract, Figures 5–7 show that loaded nutriosomes attenuated the MPTP-associated reduction in striatal tyrosine hydroxylase and dopamine transporter staining and in nigral tyrosine hydroxylase-positive cell counts. The suspension attenuated the nigral cell-count reduction alone. At 50 mg/kg, the loaded formulation did not significantly attenuate any of the three changes relative to MPTP with empty nutriosomes. Table 1 gives striatal tyrosine hydroxylase staining of 83.4 ± 1.8% of the saline control at 50 mg/kg versus 81.7 ± 2.4% with empty nutriosomes. [Primary report, Methods, Figures 5–7 and Table 1](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2022.935784/full) · [Local article text](Source-Text/2022-Parekh-Nasco-Grape-Pomace-Nutriosomes.txt) · [HAL record](https://hal.science/hal-04099272v1) · [OpenAlex W4292295426](https://openalex.org/W4292295426)

The mouse outcomes were tissue markers. The experiment did not measure motor or nonmotor behavior. The preparation was a pomace extract with a carrier and differs from raw or frozen grape and from grapeseed oil. It leaves the frozen grape and grapeseed oil claims unresolved.

## Clove essential oil and motor tests in rats

A [March 2022 oral abstract](Food-Experiment-Thyme-Clove.md#earliest-recovered-presentation-of-the-clove-oil-rat-study) is the earliest recovered public report. Hamdi and colleagues posted a preprint on January 16, 2023 and published the journal article on July 11, 2023. These versions report the same cell and rat experiments. The rat study used an intraperitoneal clove bud essential oil formulation at 10 mg/kg before and after 6-OHDA lesioning, with six rats per group. Figure 4 and the Results describe more movement in actimetry and more time standing on both hind paws in a cylinder than with lesion alone. The Results give actimetry p = .032 and p = .04 at one and two weeks, respectively, and cylinder p < .0001 and p = .0002. The Figure 4 caption gives p = .032 for both actimetry time points. The two versions contain the same discrepancy. [Research Square preprint, Figure 4](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-2471354/v1) · [Local preprint text](Source-Text/2023-Hamdi-Clove-Essential-Oil-Preprint-v1.txt) · [Journal article, Figure 4](https://austinpublishinggroup.com/aapd/fulltext/aapd-v6-id1039.pdf) · [HAL record](https://hal.science/hal-04341963v1) · [OpenAlex W4392721572](https://openalex.org/W4392721572)

The motor results provide indirect context for `0778` (bradykinesia) and `0222` (rigidity). The preparation was injected essential oil. The study did not score either symptom, measure freezing of gait or falls, or test oral ground clove in humans.

## 2024 Clermont-Auvergne clove thesis

Dhouha Hamdi’s December 20, 2024 dissertation lists clove oil and three other plant oils. Its indexed abstract reports favorable motor performance and dopaminergic neuron measures with clove oil in the rat lesion model. Hamdi and both thesis supervisors authored the earlier clove report, and the study model and main findings match. The thesis also reports additional screening comparisons. The archived catalog marks the full thesis as unavailable online, so the original tables and animal-level overlap remain unresolved. Treat the shared clove results as one study family pending clarification. [National thesis record 2024UCFA0119](https://theses.fr/2024UCFA0119) · [Sudoc record 298746026](https://www.sudoc.fr/298746026.xml) · [2023 original report](#clove-essential-oil-and-motor-tests-in-rats)


## Early squalamine results in a paraquat and lectin rat model

The 2023 Society for Neuroscience report traces the rat motor work to an earlier 2022 presentation and reports histology in a 33-rat experiment. Swain and colleagues' November 22, 2024 university report describes 17 animals given squalamine after toxin exposure and 16 receiving no squalamine. Forelimb placement and stepping fell in the group without squalamine, while the squalamine group had no clear decrease from baseline. The reports use overlapping authors, group sizes and protocols and should not be counted as independent replications. A companion 2024 abstract reports maze outcomes with omnibus p = .02 for the count of spontaneous alternations and p = .090 for the percentage. Those tests do not isolate a human executive-function or food effect. [2023 conference abstracts](https://www.sfn.org/-/media/SfN/Documents/NEW-SfN/Meetings/Neuroscience-2023/Abstracts/Abstract-PDFs/SFN23_Abstracts-PDF-Posters_TUE_AM.pdf) · [2024 original motor report](https://doi.org/10.46570/utjms-2024-1161) · [2024 original cognition report](https://doi.org/10.46570/utjms-2024-1167) · [2026 report](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Botanical-Model-Followup.md#squalamine-in-the-expanded-rat-model)

The April 2024 ASNTR report identifies the 2022 virtual poster by title and authors. SfN’s meeting page says virtual access ended in December 2022. A 2023 University of Toledo program lists a poster with the same title and a partly overlapping byline. Its public listing supplies no outcomes. The 2024 ASNTR abstract itself describes 10 model rats, nine model rats given squalamine, and nine controls. The maze result differed from control for the untreated model group and was inconclusive for the treated model group. The abstract gives no direct treated-versus-untreated maze contrast. It also reports sleep abnormalities in the untreated model compared with controls. Individual animal overlap with the other reports is unresolved.
