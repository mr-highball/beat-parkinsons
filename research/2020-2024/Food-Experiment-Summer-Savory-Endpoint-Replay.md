# Summer savory preparation and endpoint search

## IRCT registration of a multi ingredient syrup in COVID-19

The Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials posted IRCT20200411047016N1 on May 31, 2020, while recruiting. It planned standard care with or without Stopcivir, a mixed syrup containing Satureja hortensis and several other ingredients. Registered outcomes were hospital stay, dyspnea and oxygen saturation. The record displays an empty secondary-outcomes section and lists no smell or taste outcome. It has no separate summer-savory arm. The publicly identified record supplies no trial estimate for olfaction. ## Odor quality ratings for sausages with lyophilized summer savory extract

Macari and colleagues published this food sensory experiment on November 4, 2021. Eleven trained technologists evaluated cooked sausage products with and without lyophilized Satureja hortensis extract. Table 2 gives the odor rating as 5.00/5 for the control and for the 0.05% summer-savory extract product, and 4.37 and 4.00 for the 0.10% and 0.20% products, respectively. Separate Tukey letters indicate a difference from the control at the two higher concentrations. The abstract says that sensory acceptability did not change and names thyme, while the full Methods and Table 2 describe the summer savory arm and give these odor-quality differences. Assessments reflect odor quality of a product. They do not measure recovery of a person’s ability to detect, distinguish or identify smells.
