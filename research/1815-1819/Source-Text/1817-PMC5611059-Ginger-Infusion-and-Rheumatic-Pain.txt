Published: 1817-04
Archive scope: Complete fifteen-page collection, volume 37, pages 337–351. Inspected the separate short note beginning on p. 342 and ending on p. 343.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

The editors attribute the ginger-infusion practice for rheumatism and rheumatic gout to French surgeons. They say pain initially intensified and later eased, with sweating in between. No named patient, denominator, comparison, root processing or defined assessment appears in the item. The following report on animal diets and olive oil is a distinct item. This is a reported clinical practice and course without an independent estimate for grated ginger, constipation, or Parkinson pain.
