# Risk and prevention

### 1967 — Population survey in Iceland

Gudmundsson published a population-based clinical survey of parkinsonism in Iceland. Community ascertainment complemented clinic cohorts and broadened the geographic evidence. Historical case definitions and selection should be checked before combining its estimates with modern Parkinson’s disease cohorts. [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5595209/)

### 1967 — Motor findings and manganese turnover in chronic poisoning

Mena and colleagues studied the clinical picture and manganese turnover in chronic manganese poisoning, an occupationally relevant cause of parkinsonian features. The study links an exposure syndrome with movement findings and provides a basis for later work on host susceptibility. Evidence from chronic poisoning does not estimate the risk of idiopathic Parkinson’s disease at ordinary dietary exposure. [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6066873/)

### 1968 — Retrospective tobacco exposure comparison

Nefzger, Quadfasel and Karl compared smoking histories in 198 Veterans Administration hospital patients with Parkinson’s disease and comparison patients. The reported inverse association supported further epidemiological work. Retrospective selection, recall and reverse causation limit interpretation, and the study does not establish a protective intervention. [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5673487/)

### 1969 — Iron absorption and susceptibility to chronic manganese poisoning

Mena and colleagues investigated variation in susceptibility to chronic manganese poisoning and its relation to iron absorption. This is adjacent evidence for interactions between trace metals and a toxic cause of parkinsonism. The indexed record establishes the question, and full-text extraction is needed for the magnitude and direction of findings. [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5387706/)
