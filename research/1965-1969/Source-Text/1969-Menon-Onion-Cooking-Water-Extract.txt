Title: Fresh onions and blood fibrinolysis
Author: I Sudhakaran Menon
Primary original PDF: https://www.bmj.com/content/bmj/1/5647/845.2.full.pdf
Date: 1969-03-29, BMJ correspondence, printed page 845
Coverage: factual extract of the primary study correspondence

Four volunteers ate a control breakfast, then on three consecutive days ate the same breakfast supplemented with liquidized fresh onion, liquidized and strained fresh onion, or the water in which onion had been boiled. Fresh onion in either form increased reported fibrinolytic activity, whereas the cooking water did not. The letter tests a blood assay and distinguishes the cooked bulb comparison in the earlier study from a cooking-water exposure. It measured neither speech nor Parkinson outcomes.
