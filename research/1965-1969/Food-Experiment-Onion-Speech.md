# Onion exposure and speech intelligibility

## Fried and boiled onion in the same breakfast design

Menon and colleagues reported a two-day meal comparison in 22 patients convalescing from bleeding peptic ulcers on 10 August 1968. Fourteen received a breakfast with and without 60 g of fried onion, and eight used boiled onion instead. Half of each preparation group received the onion meal on the first day and half on the second. Blood fibrinolytic activity rose with both prepared foods relative to the fat-enriched breakfast alone. No speech assessment, Parkinson cohort or isolated laryngeal outcome was reported. ## Onion cooking water and uncooked tissue in follow up

Menon reported four volunteers on 29 March 1969. The four consecutive conditions comprised breakfast alone, breakfast with liquidized raw onion, breakfast with liquidized and strained raw onion, and breakfast with water in which onions had been boiled. Raw onion preparations increased fibrinolytic activity, whereas the cooking water did not. This liquid comparison helps distinguish the bulb from water used to cook it. It measured blood clot lysis, without a speech outcome.
