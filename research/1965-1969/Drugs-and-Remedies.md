# Drugs and remedies

### 1967 — Chronic oral DOPA treatment

Cotzias, Van Woert and Schiffer reported improvement with gradually increased oral D,L-DOPA in an early clinical series. This established evidence that oral treatment could provide sustained symptomatic benefit in some patients. The regimen and study design differ from later levodopa-only trials. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM196702162760703)

### 1969 — Chronic treatment with L-DOPA

Cotzias, Papavasiliou and Gellene reported at least partial symptomatic improvement in a series of 28 patients treated with oral L-DOPA. The report discusses adverse effects and limitations of response. The uncontrolled series supports symptomatic benefit and leaves progression effects unresolved. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM196902132800701)

### 1969 — Blinded clinical comparison of levodopa

Yahr, Duvoisin, Schear, Barrett and Hoehn evaluated levodopa in parkinsonism, including a double-blind component, and reported clinical improvement with levodopa. Blinded assessment strengthened the symptomatic finding. The original cohort included more than one parkinsonian diagnosis. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1001/archneur.1969.00480160015001)

### 1969 — Amantadine evaluated in 163 cases

Schwab and colleagues reported subjective or objective improvement in 66% of their 163-person group, with some improvement persisting for several months. Reported adverse effects included insomnia, dizziness, confusion and, in a few cases, hallucinations. The uncontrolled design limits the size of the treatment effect that can be inferred. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.1969.03160070046011)
