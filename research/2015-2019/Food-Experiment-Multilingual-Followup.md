# Food experiment evidence traced through Spanish-language records

## Nutrient intake and Parkinson stage in northeastern Mexico

López-Botello and colleagues reported dietary and clinical measures in 15 outpatients with Parkinson disease aged 47–80 in a journal article available online August 10, 2017. The group completed one 24-hour dietary recall. Twelve listed tremor as their first symptom, two stiffness, and one bradykinesia. All reported vitamin D intake below the study’s adequacy threshold of 67% of the dietary reference intake. Table 2 reports nutrients by age group, and the paper provides neither a food or nutrient comparison by symptom nor an estimate of symptom change. The publisher specifies CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. [Publisher full text, DOI 10.1016/j.rmu.2017.03.002](https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-medicina-universitaria-304-pdf-download-S166557961730025X).

In a separate analysis reported in the Mexican Academy of Neurology’s November 2019 proceedings, López-Botello and colleagues collected one 24-hour recall and Hoehn and Yahr stage from 74 patients aged 30–88. Inadequate zinc intake was associated with moderate-stage disease (p = 0.003). The November 2019 author poster and February 2020 Pan American MDS abstract report the same sample size, setting and zinc result, so they count as a single source family. The reported association concerns a nutrient and aggregate disease stage. The proceedings and poster provide no individual food or symptom estimate. An October 2020 publisher abstract by the same group asks a similar question about vitamin and mineral intake across Parkinson stages. Its accessible preview does not establish the sample and outcome overlap. [2019 official proceedings, printed page S5](https://www.revmexneurociencia.com/portadas/5341ax191-rmn_suplem-trabajos-libres-2019.pdf), [author poster, DOI 10.13140/RG.2.2.31243.23846](https://doi.org/10.13140/RG.2.2.31243.23846), [February 2020 proceedings, abstract 162](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7017830/), [October 2020 publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.parkreldis.2020.06.167).

The June 2019 World Parkinson Congress poster from the same hospital reports 69 patients and uses anthropometry to measure malnutrition risk in relation to Hoehn and Yahr stage. It reports no food or nutrient intake. Participant overlap with the 74-person report is unverified. [Original author poster, DOI 10.13140/RG.2.2.33615.64161](https://doi.org/10.13140/RG.2.2.33615.64161).

## 2015 expanded Argentine yerba mate analysis

Gatto and colleagues reported 223 Parkinson’s cases and 406 controls in June 2015. In the model adjusting for sex, alcohol and smoking, the case-status odds ratios were 0.63 (95% CI 0.53–0.76) for yerba mate, 0.60 (0.42–0.86) for tea and 0.51 (0.35–0.73) for coffee. The minimum one-year disease duration and investigator overlap link this Argentine analysis to the earlier reports. Exact participant overlap remains unresolved. The 128-case and 256-control study in Montevideo is a separate cohort. These comparisons concern diagnosis and do not estimate effects on fatigue or other symptoms. The journal page lists June 23 and PubMed lists June 24 for online publication. [Primary publisher abstract](https://www.jns-journal.com/article/S0022-510X%2815%2900384-6/abstract) · [Primary abstract, PMID 26148934](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26148934/) · [Earlier Argentine reports](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Multilingual-Followup.md#2013-and-2014-argentine-yerba-mate-case-control-reports) · [Uruguay case-control study](Food-Experiment-Coffee-Followup.md#coffee-and-yerba-mate-history-in-montevideo)

## 2016 swallowing assessment with water and thickener in Asturias

Mamolar Andrés and colleagues screened 52 people with Parkinson’s and selected 19 with reported swallowing difficulties for clinical and endoscopic assessment. The methods describe plain water and water thickened to nectar or pudding consistency. The word café in the test instructions describes the size of a spoon. It does not identify a test beverage. The protocol varied bolus volume according to tolerance and administered the pudding consistency to all 19. Table 2 lists cough in nine people, penetration in six and aspiration in one. Pharyngeal residue was recorded in ten. The study reports signs under a diagnostic procedure and includes no baked-potato exposure for `0290`. It first appeared online June 3, 2016 and was assigned to a 2017 print issue. [Primary report abstract and online date, PMID 27269197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27269197/) · [Full article copy, pp. 17–19 and Table 2](https://es.scribd.com/document/716486719/Disfagia-y-parkinson)

## 2019 coffee and tea case-control table in Mexico

Ventura-Chávez and colleagues published a case-control comparison from Comarca Lagunera on August 9, 2019. Its Table 2 lists coffee use in 33 of 60 cases and 52 of 144 controls, and tea use in 26 of 60 cases and 26 of 144 controls. From those counts, the crude case-status odds ratios for reported use are 2.16 for coffee and 3.47 for tea. The same table gives 0.70 for coffee and 0.64 for tea, and the discussion gives 0.64 for coffee. The methods describe descriptive statistics and odds ratios without specifying an adjusted beverage model that explains the discrepancy. Table 3 also lists greater average tea intake among cases (0.58 vs 0.24 cups, p = .001) and an inconclusive coffee quantity comparison (0.63 vs 0.53 cups, p = .39). Controls were younger than cases. The report says Parkinson’s cases were stratified using the UPDRS, but its beverage results concern diagnosis and give no symptom estimates by beverage. The discrepancies leave the direction of the authors’ intended estimate unresolved. [Primary report, Tables 1–3 and publication date](https://www.scielo.org.mx/pdf/rmn/v20n4/1665-5044-rmn-20-4-174.pdf)

## 2019 nutritional assessment in Guayaquil

The indexed abstract of the Universidad Católica de Santiago de Guayaquil thesis by Reyes Guerrero and Romero Baidal describes 65 people with confirmed Parkinson’s at Hospital Teodoro Maldonado Carbo. The Mini Nutritional Assessment identified 21.5% as malnourished and 66.2% as at risk. The abstract also reports 66.2% in the normal BMI category. The study reports aggregate nutrition status. The institutional repository item and its PDF failed to load in this pass, so item-level food intake and associations with motor or swallowing outcomes remain unverified. The record and a later thesis bibliography place this work in September 2019. The later thesis cites the study for an activity-related finding, which requires verification against the Guayaquil source.
