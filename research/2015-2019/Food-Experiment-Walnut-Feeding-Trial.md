# Whole walnuts and vegetable-oil diets in a feeding trial

## Whole walnuts versus fatty-acid-matched diets

Tindall and colleagues published a randomized, three-period crossover feeding study on May 1, 2019. Forty-five adults at cardiovascular risk entered the study and 36 completed it. After a two-week run-in, each received three six-week diets: whole walnuts, a walnut-free fatty-acid-matched diet, and a walnut-free diet replacing much of the alpha-linolenic acid with oleic acid. A sample menu lists flaxseed and high-linoleic safflower oils in the matched diet and high-oleic and high-linoleic safflower oils in the replacement diet; it does not identify a walnut-oil arm. Central systolic blood pressure did not differ significantly. Central diastolic pressure changed −1.78 ± 1.0 mm Hg after the walnut diet versus 0.15 ± 0.7 after the replacement diet (p = .04); the walnut versus matched-diet difference was not significant (p = .20). These mixed diets did not measure Parkinson dystonia or muscle spasms. The conflicting identifier should not be used to merge the registrations.
