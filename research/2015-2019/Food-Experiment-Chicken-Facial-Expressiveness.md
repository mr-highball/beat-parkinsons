# Chicken and facial expressiveness

## 2018 Simmered chicken in a mixed Parkinson diet trial

Phillips et al., 2018, PMID 30098269, DOI 10.1002/mds.27390, randomized 47 people with Parkinson disease to low-fat and ketogenic plans for eight weeks. The low-fat plan supplement, recipe p.86, instructs simmering four skinless chicken drumsticks in eight cups of water with herbs and spices for 50–60 minutes, then vegetables and rice for 30–50 minutes and serving the meat in soup. This is moist-heat cooking close to poaching, but authors describe chicken and vegetable soup, not a standalone poached chicken intervention. The plans include other chicken preparations; individual recorded consumption and any chicken-specific comparison are not reported in the article. The low-fat group was not compared with chicken-free controls.

The outcome was MDS-UPDRS Part III total: mean change -8.58 (SD 5.50) low-fat versus -6.27 (4.07) ketogenic, between-group p=0.055. Part III contains facial expression item 3.2, but this publication does not separately report its score, so total change cannot be attributed to facial expressiveness. Medication timing was held consistent for assessments; this is not a timed poached-chicken challenge or a test of food-drug interaction. Authors monitored daily ticked completion of set meals rather than individual food diaries.

Primary article: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6175383/ . Low-fat recipe source: Phillips supplement S2, printed page 86, https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6175383.1/MDS-33-1306-s002.pdf. Findings do not establish whether poached chicken worsens or improves facial expressiveness.
