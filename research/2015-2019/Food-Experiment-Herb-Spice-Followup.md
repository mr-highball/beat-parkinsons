# Herb and spice evidence follow-up, 2015–2019

## Cinnamon powder and honey in a Parkinson’s case

Kalia, Devgan, and Mani describe a 71-year-old with Parkinson’s who took ¼ teaspoon of powdered cinnamon with ½ teaspoon of honey twice daily for eight weeks alongside antiparkinsonian medication and home physiotherapy. The illustrative symptom diaries show ON periods increasing from 2 hours before the combination to 3.5 hours after it. Each diary marks 15 minutes of dyskinesia. They give no dyskinesia severity rating or repeated episode count. The baseline diary marks falls and the follow-up diary does not, with no systematic fall counts. The history describes a prior carbidopa increase to 35 mg, while both diagrams show 25 mg. The report does not isolate cinnamon or supply a separate rigidity or walking-speed outcome. The diary is indirect evidence for ground cinnamon and dyskinesia severity (`0962`). [Primary report, November 2019 issue, pp. 652–653, Figures 1 and 2](https://zenodo.org/records/4051263) · [Local text and figure transcriptions](Source-Text/2019-Kalia-Cinnamon-Honey-Case.txt) · [Publisher record dated December 8, 2019](https://www.gujaratresearchsociety.in/index.php/JGRS/article/view/1479)

## Cinnamon powder and astrocytic GDNF in mice

Patel and colleagues reported in 2019 that oral cinnamon powder and sodium benzoate each improved movement measures in MPTP-exposed mice. The benefit was attenuated in mice with astrocytic GDNF deleted. The experiments measured open-field activity and rotarod performance, with no clinical walking speed or rigidity estimate. [Primary paper, PMID 31119595](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6708487/) · [2014 mouse study](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#oral-cinnamon-powder-in-an-mptp-mouse-model)

## Clove flower-bud extract in an eight-herb mouse intervention

Ahn and colleagues’ 2017 KD5040 study added clove flower-bud extract and patchouli extract to six-herb Cheong-Gan-Tang. In MPTP-exposed mice, the full formula improved motor performance with low-dose levodopa and lowered abnormal involuntary movement scores with high-dose levodopa. A separate 2019 mouse study combined the formula with acupuncture and reported improved motor performance in the combined group. Clove was one constituent of the formula, which leaves ingredient-specific effects and the clinical claims for ground clove (`0209`, `0222`, `0778`) untested. [2017 primary report, Methods and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5395961/) · [2019 primary report](https://doi.org/10.1155/2019/3612587)

## Swallowing reflex after red-pepper-seasoned cabbage

Shin and colleagues’ 2015 reports analyzed 16 older and 10 younger adults without a history of dysphagia after 20 days of eating pickled Napa cabbage before meals. In older participants, mean swallowing reflex latency changed from 2.04 s to 1.47 s after the intervention (p = .044) and was 1.99 s a week after stopping (p = .502 versus baseline). The study had no unexposed control group. Sliced red pepper seasoned the cabbage, and the slices and pickling liquid were discarded before serving. The study provides indirect evidence for [0724, ground cayenne and swallowing difficulty](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0724.txt) through the food preparation and laboratory measure of swallowing latency. The study described no Parkinson-specific analysis. [May 2015 funder report](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/report/KAKENHI-PROJECT-23792740/237927402013jisseki/) · [November 2015 online primary article, Methods and Table 2](https://d-nb.info/1095930257/34) · [July 2014 status report with different analysis groups](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#red-pepper-seasoned-cabbage-and-swallowing-reflex-timing)

## Clove oil and echinacoside absorption

Shen and colleagues tested clove oil alongside oral echinacoside in rats. The study reported 2.36-fold higher relative echinacoside bioavailability with the oil than with echinacoside alone. A separate Parkinsonian-model paper citing this work investigated echinacoside without a clove intervention. The rat absorption study measured drug exposure and provides no ground-clove result for rigidity (`0222`) or bradykinesia (`0778`). [Primary pharmacokinetic study, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4544722/) · [Separate echinacoside model study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5706584/)

## Ginger and turmeric in the Herbagut gastrointestinal trial

Lopresti and colleagues randomized 50 adults with unsatisfactory bowel habits to a proprietary blend of 14 herbal extracts or a roasted-rice placebo for 28 days. The blend includes extracts of *Zingiber officinale* ginger, *Alpinia galanga* galangal and *Curcuma longa* turmeric. At day 28, 72% of the blend group and 4% of the placebo group reported at least three bowel movements per week in Table 2. The day 28 between-group tests for abdominal pain and constipation domains each had p < .001. The discussion reports 76% for bowel frequency, which conflicts with Table 2 and the main Results text. The study used quality-of-life domains, without an anxiety-specific symptom scale. It evaluated the mixture in people with bowel complaints and did not isolate culinary ginger or turmeric or enroll a Parkinson’s cohort. It is indirect evidence for `0581`, `0725` and `0812`. A later Australian placebo-controlled Herbagut trial registered in 2019 reports 58 enrolled and completed data collection in 2020. No results were located for that trial in the registry, exact-title and registration-ID searches. [2018 primary report, Tables 2 and 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5859649/) · [Later registry ACTRN12619001514190](https://anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=378642)

## Ginger, temulawak, soy and shrimp shell versus meloxicam

Bertorio and colleagues compared 22 adults with knee osteoarthritis receiving a combination of extracts of temulawak, ginger, soybean and shrimp shell with 24 receiving meloxicam. In the 14-day quasi-experiment, the combination group improved on the total WOMAC scale covering joint pain, stiffness and function. The between-group test had p = .930 and the public abstract gives no separate pain contrast. This is indirect context for ginger and pain (`0725`). Joint stiffness differs from Parkinsonian rigidity (`0626`), and the shrimp-shell extract and osteoarthritis outcomes differ from boiled shrimp and falls (`0322`). [2017 institutional thesis abstract](https://etd.repository.ugm.ac.id/penelitian/detail/131872) · [Related randomized comparison with diclofenac](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#ginger-temulawak-soy-and-shrimp-shell-versus-diclofenac)

## Ginger preparations in migraine pain studies

Nicolodi and colleagues’ 2017 conference abstract P160 reported two comparisons of intranasal ginger essential oil and a capsaicin emulsion in chronic migraine. It described a smaller acute pain decrease for ginger oil than capsaicin and no reduction in hours with pain after the 30-day ginger course. The group counts for the longer comparison do not sum to the reported enrollment, and the p-value inequalities do not establish the stated statistical significance. This was an intranasal formulation in migraine, while `0725` asks about eating grated ginger and pain in Parkinson’s. [Primary conference abstract P160](Source-Text/PMC5709272.txt) · [Published proceedings](https://doi.org/10.1186/s10194-017-0817-z)

In separate trials first published in 2018 and 2019, Martins and colleagues found greater short-term headache relief after oral ginger extract added to intravenous ketoprofen in 60 participants, and no clear difference in days with severe pain after three months of ginger extract versus placebo in a 107-person prevention trial. The trials address different uses of an extract and report no results for Parkinson’s or for grated ginger. [Acute primary report, PMID 29768938](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29768938/) · [Prevention primary report, PMID 31398997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31398997/)

## Cinnamon and sesame oil in cross-cultural smell testing

Pinkhardt and colleagues studied 80 participants with Parkinson’s disease and 170 controls across China and Germany. In a culturally adapted smell test, sesame oil, soy sauce, chocolate, and garlic replaced cinnamon, liquorice, coffee, and cloves. All cohorts took both versions. The study compared how well people identified odors and how well the batteries distinguished patients from controls. It supplied no estimate from consuming cold-pressed sesame oil or ground cinnamon for `0701` or `0979`. [Primary report, PMID 31703081, Methods and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6839844/) · [Local full text](Source-Text/PMC6839844.txt)

## Mint as an odor-identification item

Millar Vernetti and colleagues compared Sniffin’ Sticks identification scores for 112 people with Parkinson’s in Argentina with previously published data from five other countries. Mint was one of six odors with a similar pattern across all countries in one component of the analysis. The test measures recognition of a presented scent and supplies no effect estimate for [0645, brewed spearmint and olfactory impairment](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0645.txt). [Primary report, PMID 26512070](https://doi.org/10.1093/chemse/bjv062) · [Public research institute copy](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/handle/11336/38811)

## Sage and Vitex menopause trial registration

The Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials records April 26, 2016 as the registration date for IRCT2015111713940N2. The protocol targeted 100 postmenopausal women and lists three groups receiving dry common sage extract, *Vitex agnus-castus* extract, or placebo. It lists bone-related blood measures as primary and physical and mental symptoms on the Menopause Rating Scale as secondary. The record is labelled registered while recruiting and gives projected recruitment in April–July 2016 with the actual dates blank. The symptom report gives July–November 2015 as the conduct period. The registration therefore does not verify prespecification before enrollment. The 2020 symptom report presents the sage and placebo groups and the 2021 laboratory report describes all three groups. [English official registration](https://irct.ir/trial/13656) · [Persian official registration](https://fa.irct.ir/trial/13656) · [2020 symptom report and companion record](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#sage-extract-and-exhaustion-in-a-registered-menopause-trial)

## Sage cognitive trial results attachment

The EU Clinical Trials Register first posted the grant report for EudraCT 2007-002899-34 on March 24, 2019. The recovered five-page report identifies Parkinson’s Disease Society grant K-0613 and gives March to August 2008 as the conduct dates. It describes a single-blind randomized six-week comparison of encapsulated dried sage leaves and vitamin E in people with Parkinson’s and mild cognitive impairment. Vitamin E replaced the planned placebo after the regulator rejected the available placebo. Computerized cognition testing took place at baseline, four hours after the first dose, six weeks, and following a two-week washout. Memory speed improved from baseline in the sage group, with no clear advantage over vitamin E. The report describes a between-group working-memory composite and a six-week salivation result favoring vitamin E. It gives no arm-level estimates for those contrasts.

The methods refer to a timetable of secondary measures that is absent from the posted attachment. The report gives no fatigue instrument, individual adverse-event table or arm-level fatigue result for [0883, fresh sage and fatigue](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0883.txt). The grant report mentions poster preparation in October 2008 and lists a manuscript by Leung, Burn, Middleton, Wesnes and Perry for planned submission in March 2009. The lead investigator’s 2010 account identifies 25 participants and still cites the manuscript as in preparation. [Official results listing](https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/trial/2007-002899-34/results) · [Primary grant report, pages 1–5](https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/rest/download/result/attachment/2007-002899-34/1/26590) · [2007 registration note](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#sage-cognitive-trial-registration) · [2010 investigator account](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#investigator-account-of-the-newcastle-sage-pilot)

## Sage extract and fatigue in an uncontrolled menopause study

The June 13, 2019 report by Dadfar and Bamdad examined 30 postmenopausal participants before and after four weeks of dry sage extract tablets. The Methods describe a single group. In Table 1, mean fatigue on the four-level symptom scale fell from 3.07 to 2.23 (p = .02). The group had no concurrent comparison, and the measure concerns menopausal symptoms. The dried extract and population differ from [0883, fresh sage and Parkinson’s fatigue](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0883.txt). [Primary report and date, PMID 31435607](https://doi.org/10.18502/ijrm.v17i4.4555) · [Local Table 1](Source-Text/PMC6686654.txt#L111)

The paper PDF prints registration IRCT20180130038557N1. The originating registry dates registration to October 17, 2018 and labels it retrospective. It describes a single, unblinded group taking sage extract without placebo. Its outcome is a menopause symptom questionnaire at baseline and one month, with no separate fatigue endpoint or posted results. It lists an expected recruitment period of June 20 to July 21, 2018, leaving actual dates blank. The paper describes study conduct in 2017 and records manuscript receipt on January 27, 2018. The difference in dates remains unexplained, and a separate first-public date for the registration is unavailable. [Official IRCT record](https://en.irct.ir/trial/29262) · [WHO ICTRP record](https://trialsearch.who.int/Trial2.aspx?TrialID=IRCT20180130038557N1) · [Paper PDF, p. 287](https://media.neliti.com/media/publications/411663-the-effect-of-saliva-officinalis-extract-f7751338.pdf)

## Cinnamon bark extract in a patent disclosure

Yichun University’s 2017 patent application CN106474191A describes a clinical observation comparing 41 people receiving an aqueous cinnamon bark extract with 38 receiving trihexyphenidyl. It reports global counts of 25 resolved, 15 improved, and 1 unchanged or worsened in the extract group, and 15, 20, and 3 in those categories with trihexyphenidyl. The disclosure gives no method of group assignment or blinding, comparable follow-up times, symptom-specific outcome scores, or way to distinguish unchanged from worsened. It lists rigidity and bradykinesia among the invention’s proposed applications. Exact patent-number and title searches located the disclosure without an independently identifiable clinical report. The different preparation and global categories leave [0146, ground cinnamon and rigidity](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0146.txt) and [0889, ground cinnamon and walking slowness](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0889.txt) unresolved. [Original patent disclosure, clinical efficacy summary](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN106474191A/zh)

## Cinnamon twig decoction in a patent disclosure

The 2017 patent application CN106728606A reports that 152 people with Parkinson’s were randomly assigned to a nine-ingredient decoction containing cinnamon twig or to levodopa/benserazide. The disclosure describes improvements in several motor items and reports lower Epworth Sleepiness Scale scores from baseline in the decoction group. It counts constipation in 5 of 76 participants receiving the decoction and 12 of 76 receiving levodopa/benserazide, and drooling in 2 of 76 and 5 of 76, respectively. These are reported adverse-event counts in an active-treatment comparison. The disclosure gives limited information on allocation and blinding, and the methods describe cinnamon twig in a mixture. This is indirect for [0045, ground cinnamon and daytime sleepiness](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0045.txt), [0371, ground cinnamon and drooling](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0371.txt), and [0737, ground cinnamon and constipation](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0737.txt). Exact-identifier and outcome searches did not locate an independently identifiable clinical paper. [Original patent, clinical methods and Tables 4–5](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN106728606A/zh)

## Culinary seasonings in a multimodal constipation case

Neiworth-Petshow and Baldwin-Sayre described a 73-year-old woman with Parkinson’s, three weeks of constipation, and reflux. The treatment plan recommended vegetables, more fiber and water, magnesium citrate, a probiotic, and other measures. Table 4 recommended ginger root, basil, oregano, black pepper, turmeric, and curry as seasonings. At five days, the patient reported cooking with more herbs and spices and passing one formed stool daily without straining. The report gives no record of which seasonings she ate or in what amounts or preparations. This is related to [0227, cracked black pepper and constipation](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0227.txt), [0581, grated ginger and constipation](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0581.txt), and [0662, dried basil and constipation](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0662.txt). The response to several concurrent changes leaves ingredient-specific effects unresolved. [Primary case, Table 4 and Follow-up, PMID 31043910](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6469461/)

## Butylphthalide combination and the MoCA executive subscore

Zhang et al. assigned 60 people with Parkinson’s disease dementia to donepezil or donepezil plus pharmaceutical DL-3-n-butylphthalide, oxiracetam, and Ginkgo biloba extract. Both groups received usual care. Table 3 shows that mean MoCA visuospatial/executive subscores changed from 1.03 to 1.34 with the combination and from 1.04 to 1.15 with donepezil by six months. The authors also reported a favorable difference in total MoCA. Abnormal liver function was reported in 5 of 30 and 4 of 30 participants, respectively. This is an endpoint-specific lead for [0164, dried celery leaf and executive function](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0164.txt). The MoCA item combines visuospatial and executive tasks, the intervention contains several active drugs, and the tested butylphthalide was a pharmaceutical capsule. [Primary report, Tables 2, 3 and 6, PMID 31296990](https://www.dovepress.com/article/download/46607)

## Pharmaceutical butylphthalide and motor symptoms

Zhou et al. randomly assigned 131 people with Parkinson’s to usual care with or without DL-3-n-butylphthalide. The study followed 103 through its final visit, with 43 in the treatment group and 60 in the control group. Assessors were blinded and participants knew their assignment. At 24 weeks, the authors reported an improvement in their off-medication non-tremor composite of 7.14 points with the drug and a 0.50-point worsening with control. Table II gives mean change -7.14 (SD 5.81) versus +0.50 (SD 9.48), p < .001. UPDRS III items 18-19 and 22-31 comprise this non-tremor subtotal. They include speech, facial expression, gait, rigidity and bradykinesia. An individual bradykinesia result was not published. The authors also reported improved sleep quality and mild gastrointestinal reactions in two patients. The published Table II prints identical numbers under tremor and quality-of-life scores, which limits confidence in those columns. [Primary report, Methods and Table II, PMID 30988766](https://doi.org/10.3892/etm.2019.7397)

In separate Parkinson’s dementia studies, Dai et al. randomized 90 and Liu et al. randomized 84 participants to usual care and donepezil with or without pharmaceutical butylphthalide. Their public abstracts report lower total UPDRS after adding the drug, 33.28 versus 41.26 and 20.32 versus 35.12, respectively. Liu et al. also reported higher global MoCA scores. Wang et al. compared butylphthalide with donepezil for 12 weeks in 76 people, alongside usual treatment. Their full report gives UPDRS, MoCA, MMSE and ADL totals in Table 1. These outcomes leave the individual symptoms in [0275, fresh celery leaf and bradykinesia](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0275.txt) and [0164, dried celery leaf and executive function](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0164.txt) unresolved. [Dai et al. primary abstract](https://rs.yiigle.com/cmaid/1021660) · [Liu et al. primary abstract](https://opaj.napstic.cn/periodicalArticle/0120160600402686) · [Wang et al. full report](https://js.dnzh.org/FileUpload/PdfFile/20171228094427.pdf) · [Eight-reference audit](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/followup/butylphthalide-chinese-primary-trail.md)

The drug is a synthetic compound historically isolated from celery seed. Its motor outcomes are near [0275, fresh celery leaf and bradykinesia](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0275.txt) and [0941, fresh celery leaf and stride length](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0941.txt). The comparison provides no separate bradykinesia or stride length effect estimate, and its sleep-quality comparison does not resolve [0389, fresh celery leaf and daytime sleepiness](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0389.txt).

## Aural capsaicin in mixed older dysphagia

Kondo et al. randomized 20 older patients with dysphagia after cerebrovascular disease or in Parkinson’s to aural capsaicin ointment or placebo. The groups included one and two people with Parkinson’s, respectively. The pooled capsaicin group showed short-term improvement in endoscopic swallowing and reflex scores. The study supplies no Parkinson-specific estimate. It is a topical-ear route lead for [0724, ground cayenne and swallowing difficulty](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0724.txt). The separate local entry for the São Paulo dissertation reports a direct Parkinson cohort receiving oral capsaicin extract. [Primary report, Table 1, PMID 29180855](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5691921/) · [Local oral extract study summary](Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#capsaicin-and-swallowing-in-parkinsons-disease)

## Fresh herbs and spices in a dietary survey

Mischley et al. analyzed cross-sectional survey data from 1,053 people with Parkinson’s. Higher intake frequency of grouped spices was associated with a 13.4-point lower patient-reported composite severity score per frequency step in the income-adjusted model (95% CI, 0.8 to 26.0 points lower). The same model’s fresh-herb estimate was 8.9 points lower, with a confidence interval from 21.7 points lower to 3.8 points higher. The authors made no adjustment for multiple comparisons. The exposure groups and composite outcome leave individual culinary herbs, spices and symptoms unresolved. [Primary report, Table 2 and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1155/2017/6405278)

## Rikkunshito and gastrointestinal symptoms

Yakabi et al. randomly assigned 16 people with Parkinson’s and upper gastrointestinal symptoms to receive an eight-ingredient rikkunshito extract with ginger before or after a four-week period without the extract. Fourteen participants contributed to the primary analysis. The open-label crossover found no clear difference on the constipation subscale (p = 0.798) or gastric emptying. The authors reported more favorable appetite, abdominal pain, and self-rated depression measures during treatment. The design and formulation leave the effect of grated ginger alone unresolved for [0581, constipation](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0581.txt) and [0725, pain severity](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0725.txt). The pain measure focused on gastrointestinal symptoms. [Primary report, Figure 1 and Table III, PMID 28912900](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5583142/)

The product uses a hot-water extract of eight crude herbs, including ginger. Overall motor UPDRS change during treatment and off treatment was 1.00 points in both crossover summaries, with five and six observations respectively. The favorable mood comparison used a depression scale. The paper gives overall motor and depression findings, leaving separate rigidity and clinical anxiety effects of grated ginger unresolved. [Primary report, composition and Tables I and III](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5583142/)

In a separate 2015 report from Asahikawa Medical Center, Suzuki followed eight patients with idiopathic Parkinson’s before and after four weeks of the eight-herb TJ-43 rikkunshito extract, which includes ginger, added to their usual treatment. Six of eight improved on the combined lower gastrointestinal GSRS score (Figure 2D, p = .044). The upper gastrointestinal score and group levodopa exposure results were inconclusive. Figure 2D gives no item-specific constipation estimate. Some patients also took constipation medicines. The lack of a parallel control and the combined herbal exposure leave the effect of grated ginger unresolved. [Primary institutional report, Methods and Figure 2D](https://asahikawa.hosp.go.jp/journal/pdf/igaku_vol1_03_09.pdf)

## Fennel oil in laboratory and animal models

Morshedi and Nasouti compared 15 essential oils in a cell-free alpha-synuclein fibrillation assay. Fennel, rosemary, and myrtle were reported to promote fibrillation under the assay conditions. The experiment also included essential oils of spearmint, summer savory, and tarragon. A separate 2018 animal study by Nemati et al. reported improved locomotor test outcomes with fennel essential oil in a reserpine model. The two studies measured different endpoints and used concentrated oil. Neither tested toasted fennel seed or the clinical outcomes in [0214, rest tremor](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0214.txt) and [0757, postural instability](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0757.txt). [2016 laboratory report](https://doi.org/10.1155/2016/6219249) · [2018 animal report](https://doi.org/10.5812/jjnpp.67391)

## A compound isolated from green shiso leaves in a mouse model

Izumi et al. reported that an isolated chalcone from green perilla leaves reduced neuronal loss and behavioral dysfunction after direct administration into the brain in a toxin-induced mouse model. This is preparation- and population-adjacent to [0398, fresh shiso and bradykinesia](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0398.txt) and [0831, fresh shiso and rigidity](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0831.txt). The study did not evaluate culinary shiso or symptom change in people. [Primary report, PMID 28167258](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejphar.2017.02.005)

## Oral rosemary and depressive symptoms in university students

Nematolahi et al. randomized 68 university students to 500 mg oral rosemary or placebo twice daily for one month. The 2017 online report describes lower Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale scores with rosemary compared with placebo. The abstract gives no arm-level depression scores. A later trial describes the preparation as powdered dried aerial parts. The reported direction is favorable in university students, with a different population and preparation from [0128, fresh rosemary and depressive symptoms in Parkinson’s](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0128.txt). [Primary trial abstract, PMID 29389474](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29389474/) · [Preparation citation in the later trial](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/12/11/3551#sec1-nutrients-12-03551) · [2020 rosemary follow-up](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#rosemary-extract-and-depression-in-japanese-working-adults)

The publisher’s indexed methods identify registration IRCT201703136026N6; a later antioxidant and anticholinesterase report cites the same registration and should not be counted as an independent trial. A 2026 conference review reports depression scores of 2.94 ± 2.97 with rosemary and 3.90 ± 3.36 with placebo, 34 per group, and a standardized mean difference of −0.30 (95% CI −0.78 to 0.18). These are secondary extractions: the original result table and timing have not been verified, and the estimate should not be substituted for the trial’s own analysis. The same review’s 4.63 versus 6.52 figures concern anxiety. [Publisher-indexed trial methods](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1744388117304723) · [Later report citing the registration](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212958821000215) · [Conference review, Figures 1–2](https://urncst.com/index.php/urncst/article/download/1069/572/15832)

## Isolated rosmarinic acid and mouse motor tests

Lv et al. reported improved motor performance with isolated rosmarinic acid in MPTP-exposed mice in a 2019 study using pole, traction, beam-crossing and cylinder tasks. The authors also studied inflammatory signaling. Their abstract describes the motor results collectively, with no separate clinical rigidity or postural instability measure. ## Thyme extract and behavioral tests in 6-OHDA rats

Mahmoodi et al. reported shorter bar-test catalepsy at the highest level of oral hydroalcoholic thyme extract in 6-OHDA-lesioned rats. The Results also describe longer rotarod balance time at that level, although the lesion group itself showed no significant rotarod deficit compared with the untreated control. Open-field center and perimeter measures and forced-swim immobility showed no clear improvement. The paper reports improved thermal pain tolerance, with inconsistent descriptions of the assay in the methods and figure caption. This is an extract and animal outcome lead for [0421, dried thyme and postural instability](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0421.txt) and [0362, dried thyme and anxiety](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0362.txt). The behavioral tests did not assess Parkinson’s dyskinesia (`0611`). [Primary report, Results and Figures 1–5](https://jbums.org/article-1-6447-en.pdf)

## Ginger-containing decoctions in a Korean gait case series

Koh and colleagues’ June 30, 2017 report gives individual measurements from six people treated with herbal formulas, acupuncture and other care. Case 3 had suspected Parkinson’s disease and sequelae of a right basal ganglia infarct. The first decoction listed both dried and fresh ginger, as did the later formula. During the first formula, measured stride length increased from 11 ± 1 to 13 ± 2 cm from March 27 to 30 while treadmill speed remained 0.3 km/h. Acupuncture, pharmacopuncture, and changes to other medications occurred in the same interval. UPDRS Part IV went from 2 to 0 between admission and discharge, with no separate dyskinesia item result. [Publisher record and date](https://journal.kci.go.kr/JSCIM/archive/articleView?artiId=ART002241182) · [Primary report, pp. 188–191](https://journal.kci.go.kr/JSCIM/archive/articlePdf?artiId=ART002241182#page=7)

Case 5 had a prior Parkinson’s diagnosis and continued carbidopa–levodopa. Stride length was 41 ± 3 and 42 ± 3 cm on March 2 and 16, respectively. Both measurements preceded the fresh-ginger formula introduced March 25. UPDRS Part IV remained 8 at admission and discharge and the paper gives no dyskinesia item score. The series is related to [0566, ground ginger and shortened stride](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0566.txt) and [0271, ground ginger and dyskinesia](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0271.txt). It gives no estimate specific to ground ginger. [Primary report, pp. 194–196](https://journal.kci.go.kr/JSCIM/archive/articlePdf?artiId=ART002241182#page=13)

## Brewed lemon balm and depression after burn injury

Chehroudi and colleagues published this randomized comparison online July 24, 2016. Thirty-six hospitalized people with second- or third-degree burns received lemon balm tea or black tea twice daily for 20 days. The lemon balm bags contained 2.5 g of leaves. Table 3 reports that the proportion in the no-depression category rose from 55.6% to 72.2% in the lemon balm arm and was roughly 67% at both assessments in the black tea arm. Both arms retained 11.1% in the severe category. Table 5 reports a between-arm p value of .014 and lists differences of 2.7 for black tea and 1.66 for lemon balm without enough information to reconstruct the contrast and its direction from that table. The authors report that the beverages differed in taste and smell and could not be double-blinded. They excluded participants with prior depression and say they would replace participants discharged before completing the trial. The Methods identify retrospective registration IRCT201205198177N4 and its Beck endpoint. ## Butylphthalide gait and tremor composite table check

Zhou and colleagues randomized 131 people with Parkinson disease to usual care with or without oral pharmaceutical DL-3-n-butylphthalide. Final follow-up included 43 for drug plus care and 60 for care only. The off-medication non-tremor UPDRS III subtotal includes gait, with no timed walk or separate gait item. The stated subtotal adds UPDRS III rest and action or postural items. Its two published baseline means, 40.49 and 48.10, exceed the 28-point maximum of the stated items when scored as described. Original Table II also repeats the PDQ-39 scores and between-group tests nearly verbatim, with a control change sign difference at 24 weeks. The report does not explain a score transformation. These inconsistencies prevent assigning a reliable tremor estimate. The trial used a synthetic product with historical seed discovery, without an oral celery-stalk exposure. The official WHO record ChiCTR1800018892 matches the paper. It was registered October 15, 2018, after the recorded 2014 start and article submission. It lists group sizes of 43 and 60, matching final follow-up, while the paper reports 131 randomized. It lists aggregate UPDRS and gives no separate resting-tremor item. ### November 26 Newcastle secondary-assessment follow-through

The [official Newcastle protocol](https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/trial/2007-002899-34/GB) lists cognitive testing as primary and leaves its secondary-objective field blank. It records dried sage-leaf capsules compared with vitamin E. The [only posted result version](https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/trial/2007-002899-34/results) is dated March 24, 2019, for a trial completed in 2008. Targeted searches by trial identifier and the planned investigator poster and manuscript did not recover the referenced secondary schedule or a facial-expression outcome. Whether facial expression was measured remains open. SAGE-217 results concern zuranolone and supply no evidence on sage leaf.
