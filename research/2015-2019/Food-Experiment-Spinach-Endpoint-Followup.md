# Spinach and Parkinson dietary trial follow-up

## Raw spinach options and dyskinesia in a mixed-diet trial

Phillips and colleagues published an eight-week randomized Parkinson pilot on August 11, 2018, assigning 23 people to a low-fat plan and 24 to a ketogenic plan. The ketogenic menu included a spinach salad containing 60 g spinach or kale leaves, mayonnaise and lemon juice, with no cooking step (printed p. 80); the low-fat menu included a 60 g spinach-or-kale green smoothie (printed p. 55). Participants checked off meal completion rather than keeping ingredient-specific food diaries. These alternative recipes do not establish which leaf anyone ate. One ketogenic participant required reductions in levodopa, ropinirole and apomorphine attributed to exacerbated dyskinesias, which partially resolved. The report does not connect that person's outcome to spinach, establish a spinach dose, or separate a food effect from other concurrent diet and medication changes.

Table 2 reports the composite MDS-UPDRS Part IV change as −0.79 ± 2.71 low-fat and −1.56 ± 2.45 ketogenic, between-group p = 0.32, with regression imputation. Part IV contains motor complications beyond dyskinesia; these are not dyskinesia-specific severity scores. The study does not report listener-rated intelligibility. It is related context for 0622, not a directionally informative spinach comparison.
