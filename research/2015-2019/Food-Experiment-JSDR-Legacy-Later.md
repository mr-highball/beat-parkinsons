# Capsinoid seasoning and black pepper aroma in swallowing studies

## 2015 CH-19 Sweet seasoning on a videofluoroscopy jelly

The KAKEN project team tested 17 people with dysphagia (15 men and two women, mean age 76.2 years) using a 4 g videofluoroscopy jelly with or without 0.5 g seasoning containing capsinoids from the nonpungent CH-19 Sweet pepper. The seasoning was a formulated extract product. The investigators report differences in tongue–palate contact, laryngeal vestibule closure and upper esophageal sphincter opening timing or movement (P < .01). The reports give no Parkinson-specific data or group-level aspiration or pneumonia effect. The funder first released this results report on May 28, 2015 and released the final report on June 25, 2015. The final report is internally dated June 13, 2014. This project concerns a different preparation and clinical population from [claim 0724, ground cayenne and Parkinson swallowing difficulty](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0724.txt). ## 2016 inhaled black pepper oil and swallowing in Parkinson with Lewy body dementia

Tanaka and colleagues studied five patients they described as having Parkinson’s with Lewy body dementia. The participants smelled a drop of black pepper aromatherapy oil on a tissue for one minute. Staff assessed repeated saliva swallowing and time to swallow at baseline, day 15 and day 30. Two patients improved on these measures, one stayed the same and two stopped the study early. The authors report stable clinical status, medication and rehabilitation for the two who improved and raise the possibility of a placebo effect. There was no comparator or quantitative group estimate. The preparation and route differ from [claim 0124, eating cracked black pepper and swallowing difficulty](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0124.txt). [Primary abstract P2-33-4, printed p. 1228](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100774.pdf#page=1229) · [Official November 11–12, 2016 meeting dates](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/cnt1-1_000087.html)

In the preceding abstract, Hongo and colleagues tested black pepper aroma patches before lunch for four weeks in three people with neurological disease who could eat orally. One improved on a modified water-swallow screen. Repeated saliva-swallow scores stayed the same or decreased in all three. Participants’ reports included easier swallowing, and mealtime observation showed little overall change. Individual diagnoses are absent from the abstract. The investigator group, hospital, delivery method and follow-up distinguish it from Tanaka’s five-patient report. [Primary abstract P2-33-3, printed p. 1227](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100774.pdf#page=1228)

## 2018 mealtime fatigue during jelly and thickened water rehabilitation

Yuri and colleagues report a man in his 60s with Parkinson’s, pneumonia, dysphagia and marked weight loss. Early in supervised rehabilitation, a sip of thickened water required seven to eight extra swallows. He managed 10–20% of the provided intake in 30 minutes and reported fatigue. After about a month, he could take jelly and thickened water within 30 minutes without reporting fatigue. Tube nutrition and multidisciplinary rehabilitation accompanied the feeding changes. The report describes one case without an ingredient-specific comparison or a standardized fatigue score. [Original NHO abstract P2-4A-373](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100779.pdf#page=447)
