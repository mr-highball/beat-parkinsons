# Optional coconut oil in a healthy-adult ketogenic crossover

## Registration and protocol

Iacovides and Meiring's January 2018 protocol (PMID 29361967) identifies PACTR201707002406306 and gives July 20, 2017 as the registration date. It planned a randomized crossover between two three-week diets in healthy adults. Coconut oil was listed with olive oil and butter as an example of unsaturated or saturated cooking fats participants could use. The diet plan does not assign an individual coconut-oil amount or grade. The old registry link returned 404, and the current registry and WHO records were not retrieved. The published protocol supplies the registration information above. [Published protocol](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-018-2462-5)

## Mood, freshness, sleep and executive performance

The 2019 results (PMID 31098615, DOI 10.1093/ajcn/nqz073) report 11 healthy adults who completed both diets. Participants received diet plans and kept daily food records. The original main article provides no individual intake or grade for coconut oil, and the intervention compared whole diets. Evening mood and morning freshness or alertness were single-item visual analogue scales. The three-condition tests covering the ketogenic diet, high-carbohydrate diet and washout reported p=.18 for mood and p=.29 for morning alertness. The executive task reported p=.60 for speed (Cohen's d=.31) and p=.90 for accuracy (d=.19) between diets. No separate fatigue outcome was identified in the original main text. The study cannot estimate an isolated virgin or refined coconut-oil effect. The separately listed 2019 supplement remains unverified.
