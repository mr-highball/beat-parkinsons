# Lemongrass preparation and symptom evidence

## Seated blood pressure after leaf tea in normotensive adults

The authors used the same 105-person three-dose and day 0, 10 and 30 design reported for the diuresis study. Blood pressure was taken after five minutes of seated rest. Several blood pressure indices decreased relative to baseline. The report did not measure standing balance, postural instability severity or incident falls, and participants did not have Parkinson disease. The indexed journal rendering provided Methods and Results; direct PDF retrieval returned HTTP 410. ## Urination frequency in the acid base report

The 105 healthy adults drank doses of 2, 4 or 8 g of powdered Cymbopogon citratus leaves infused in 150 mL hot water, with day 0, 10 and 30 assessments. Table 2 lists mean 24-hour void counts for the 2 g group of 6.42, 7.08 and 6.56; the 4 g group of 5.06, 7.04 and 6.74; and the 8 g group of 6.02, 7.02 and 8.00. The baseline label 'Control' in the table denotes pretreatment measurements of each dose group. Per the table and text, all doses had higher frequency on day 10 versus baseline and the 4 and 8 g groups remained higher on day 30. This fits the previous report's identical design and frequency pattern; treat the cohort overlap as likely. The authors measured void frequency rather than urgent desire to urinate. No matched Parkinson population was included. ## Leaf polyphenol extract in rodent movement models

Rats received an ethanolic leaf extract orally at 100, 200 or 400 mg/kg in haloperidol and reserpine movement models. The authors reported shorter haloperidol-related catalepsy with extract and altered locomotor measures in the reserpine condition. Endpoints concern induced pharmacological motor deficits. The source does not give clinical falls incidence or a clinical postural instability outcome. The concentrate cannot be treated as dietary minced plant tissue.
