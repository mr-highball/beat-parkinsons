# Thyme in two Parkinson diet plans

Phillips et al. published an eight-week randomized Parkinson trial online August 11, 2018. Both meal-plan supplements list a small vial of thyme. The low-fat plan's vermicelli sauce uses a teaspoon of thyme and is simmered (printed p. 81); its tuna casserole contains thyme and is baked (p. 82). The ketogenic plan includes thyme to taste in a cooked chicken casserole (p. 85). The plan does not specify botanical identity or establish that the seasoning was fresh. Patients marked completed meals rather than keeping ingredient-level diaries, and no actual thyme consumption or isolated thyme contrast was reported. [Primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6175383/) · [Low-fat plan](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6175383.1/MDS-33-1306-s002.pdf#page=81) · [Ketogenic plan](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6175383.1/MDS-33-1306-s003.pdf#page=85)

The trial randomized 47 patients; 44 began a diet and 38 completed. Missing outcomes were imputed in primary analysis. At week eight, changes in total MDS-UPDRS Part I averaged −0.99 ± 3.63 low-fat versus −4.58 ± 2.17 ketogenic (between-group p < .001); Part II −1.33 ± 3.28 versus −3.13 ± 4.01 (p = .11); and Part III −8.58 ± 5.50 versus −6.27 ± 4.07 (p = .055). The scale includes apathy and hallucinations/psychosis in Part I, handwriting in II, and facial expression in III. The paper does not report separate estimates for those four items. Hallucinations/psychosis is broader than visual hallucination frequency, and handwriting is not a direct measure of micrographia severity. These are whole-diet total scores, not effects of fresh thyme.
