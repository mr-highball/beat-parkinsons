# Squash, mushroom, eggplant, and zucchini evidence

## 2015 Freeze-dried white button mushroom and working memory in aged rats

Thangthaeng and colleagues randomized aged Fischer 344 rats to diets containing 0%, 0.5%, 1%, 2%, or 5% lyophilized white button mushroom. At eight weeks, the 0.5% group improved on a working-memory water-maze variant. The abstract reports no such improvement at the other concentrations. The 0.5% and 1% groups also remained on a narrow beam longer. This uses a prepared whole mushroom in non-Parkinson aging and provides no roasted exposure or clinical executive assessment. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26475179/)

## 2016 Protocol for button mushroom powder and cognition

Zajac and colleagues specified a six-month randomized comparison of vitamin D2-enriched mushroom powder, standard mushroom powder, vitamin D3, and placebo in healthy adults aged 60–90. The protocol planned repeated cognitive and mood testing and linked the design to ACTRN12613000891729. It reports the proposed methods. [Primary published protocol](https://researchmgt.monash.edu/ws/portalfiles/portal/262803910/262803580_oa.pdf) · [Later trial results](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Squash-Mushroom-Eggplant.md#2020-white-button-mushroom-capsules-and-cognition)

## 2017 Anaphylaxis after a dish containing baked eggplant

Gawrońska-Ukleja and colleagues reported immediate respiratory symptoms, face and tongue swelling, hypotension, and loss of consciousness after a dish containing baked eggplant in a 27-year-old man with recurrent anaphylaxis. Prick-by-prick testing reacted to fresh and cooked eggplant, and the authors suspected lipid transfer protein syndrome. The case documents an acute reaction in one susceptible person. It contains no smell test or dystonia examination and does not isolate roasted eggplant. The report was available online in 2017 and appeared in the June 2018 issue. [Primary case report](https://apjai-journal.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/7-AP0846.pdf) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29161052/)

## 2018 French poison-centre series for non-edible squash

Le Roux and colleagues included 353 people reported to French poison centres. Most recorded exposures were oral and 204 people were symptomatic, mainly with gastrointestinal complaints. Headache or vertigo occurred in some cases. The cases grouped non-edible squash and do not isolate ordinary zucchini or a particular preparation. The abstract's discussion of confusion concerns distinguishing edible from non-edible varieties. The study reports no cognitive testing or dream-enactment endpoint. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29323540/)

## 2019 Eggplant extract in a rotenone cell model

Youn and colleagues exposed PC-12 cells to rotenone and studied a chlorogenic-acid-rich extract of Solanum melongena. The abstract reports reduced oxidative stress and apoptosis-related changes after extract treatment. The study measured cell outcomes without any prepared food exposure or sensory and motor symptom assessment. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31368148/)
