# Chive anxiety evidence in 2015–2019

## Purified oral ferulic acid and open-arm time

The 6 December 2017 study of seven simple phenols included commercial ferulic acid of 99.6% purity, delivered orally to male CD-1 mice. Groups used ten mice. Table 1 reports higher elevated-plus-maze open-arm time with ferulic acid at 5, 10 and 20 mg/kg than with the shared vehicle. Open-arm entry counts for these doses have no significance marking and the corresponding omnibus test gives p=.362. The experiments also included a diazepam positive control. This separates two measures of behavior for the oral compound in healthy mice. Dried chives and Parkinson's anxiety remain outside this comparison.

## Powdered onion and stress-related behavior

Samad and Saleem published online on 25 November 2017. Male mice received drinking water or onion bulb powder (Allium cepa) dissolved in water for 14 days. Each starting group was then divided into unstressed and immobilization-stressed groups. The abstract reports that onion pretreatment attenuated stress-associated changes measured with the elevated plus maze and light-dark test. There are no arm-level estimates in the public abstract. Onion bulb and common-chive leaves (Allium schoenoprasum) are separate plants and preparations.

[Primary abstract, PMID 29178012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29178012/)

## Common chive leaf extract in cerebral ischemia mice

Singh and colleagues studied orally administered Allium schoenoprasum leaf extract following experimental cerebral ischemia reperfusion in mice. The abstract describes neurological and Morris water maze outcomes. The primary abstract gives no anxiety outcome. The exposure was a leaf extract within a stroke model.

[Primary abstract, PMID 29933243](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29933243/)
