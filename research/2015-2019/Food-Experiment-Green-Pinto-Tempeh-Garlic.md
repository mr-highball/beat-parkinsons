# Food experiment evidence for green beans, pinto beans, tempeh, and garlic

## Cooked soy tempeh and six-minute walking distance

Setiawan’s 2016 doctoral thesis reports a randomized comparison in 129 adults analyzed during standard pulmonary tuberculosis treatment. The food arm received boiled soy tempeh and the control arm received standard treatment alone. The six-minute walk distance increased by 49.67 m in the tempeh arm and 25.75 m in controls (p = .02 for change). The endpoint means at month two were 392.67 and 382.37 m (p = .44). Handgrip gain favored tempeh. The measure was walking endurance during recovery from pulmonary disease and the thesis reports no stride length or Parkinson sample. [Institutional source and rights statement](https://doi.org/10.22029/jlupub-16669) · [Thesis, Tables 6 and 7](https://jlupub.ub.uni-giessen.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/f1bbbf8a-b704-47d1-a4a5-a75dc4ec4f64/content)

## Fermented velvet bean extracts and forestride in rats

Suaidah’s 2018 Universitas Gadjah Mada thesis compared extracts of raw, boiled and tempe-form fermented *Mucuna pruriens* in paraquat-exposed rats. The institutional abstract reports no clear forestride difference at one, two or three weeks. Paraquat itself did not clearly shorten forestride. Hanging time was lower in a one-week boiled or fermented ethanol-extract comparison and there was no clear increase in hanging time at two or three weeks. The experiment uses velvet bean extracts and animal tests, with no cooked soy tempeh or clinical Parkinson stride comparison. [Institutional thesis record and bilingual results](https://etd.repository.ugm.ac.id/penelitian/detail/159054)

## Kidney bean seed extract and drug-induced catalepsy

The 2015 Anandpara article tested a methanol extract from dry, kidney-shaped red rajma seeds and reports reduced catalepsy after chlorpromazine or haloperidol in rats. The paper specifies kidney beans and describes its crude chemical screen. This is a separate preparation from cooked green pods or pinto seeds. The same species includes different cultivars and harvested parts. A separate 2016 worm study used purified common-bean erythroagglutinin and reported reduced dopaminergic GFP signals, without feeding cooked whole beans or measuring human motor symptoms. [Author-deposited 2015 primary article](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/280008665_Evaluation_and_Comparison_of_Anti_Parkinson_Activity_of_Methanolic_Extract_of_Phaseolus_vulgaris_With_L-DOPA) · [2016 primary worm study](Food-Experiment-Lentil-Kidney-Bean.md#purified-kidney-bean-lectin-in-nematodes)

## Boiled and roasted garlic and systemic metabolite exposure

Lawson and Hunsaker published a human breath-metabolite comparison in June 2018. Relative to raw garlic, cooked preparations showed 14–34% allicin bioequivalence using allyl methyl sulfide in exhaled breath. Roasted garlic gave a higher result than boiled garlic. This measures metabolite exposure after preparation and supplies no urinary urgency or other clinical Parkinson endpoint. [Primary article text](Source-Text/PMC6073756.txt)

## Registered tempeh trial and walking outcomes

NCT02554318 first posted September 18, 2015 and corresponds to the 2016 Setiawan dissertation. The registry gives a parallel comparison of standard tuberculosis therapy with or without cooked soy tempeh and names six-minute walk change as a secondary measure. The walking estimates were added to the registry in 2022. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02554318) · [Local registration fields](Source-Text/Trials/NCT02554318.txt) · [Results first posted in 2022](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Green-Pinto-Tempeh-Garlic.md#registered-tempeh-results)


## Registered pinto and green bean crossover

NCT04003194 first posted July 1, 2019 and records a randomized, open-label crossover in 13 adults with type 2 diabetes. Participants received pinto or green beans over 12-week phases separated by a four-week washout. Registry outcomes concern glucose and hemoglobin A1c. The 2020 article reports additional weekly gastrointestinal questionnaires and identifies both bean forms as canned. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04003194) · [Local registry snapshot](Source-Text/Trials/NCT04003194.txt) · [Primary trial outcomes](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Green-Pinto-Tempeh-Garlic.md#canned-pinto-and-green-beans-in-a-crossover)


## Common bean levodopa claims in a review

The 2019 Rijntjes review gives 0.25% levodopa for *Phaseolus vulgaris* and cites Ramya and Thaakur 2007. Their full review and its underlying Ingle 2003 plant survey do not supply that value for the species. A 2026 review repeats the claim by citing Rijntjes. The 2023 primary mass-spectrometry results distinguish pod tissue from fresh and dried seeds in four Italian ecotypes and report a much lower dry-weight range. Differences in cultivars, sampled tissues and analytical methods matter for comparing values. [Local 2019 article text](Source-Text/PMC6875167.txt#L93) · [Cited 2007 source](https://www.wisdomlib.org/uploads/journals/ansci/v-27-1-1-55-jul-sep-2007/1388728.pdf) · [Dated primary assay](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Green-Pinto-Tempeh-Garlic.md#levodopa-measured-in-common-bean-pods-and-seeds)


## Levodopa estimates in Iranian dried common bean seeds

Rahmani-Nezhad and colleagues first published February 1, 2018. Their Table 4 gives UV color assay estimates for 33 Iranian dried *Phaseolus vulgaris* seed accessions, most of them black. Reported means span 0.05% to 0.48%, with an approximate average of 0.30%. The Ks10005 accession gave 0.25 ± 0.09%. Seven other accessions were compared with an HPLC reference in Table 6. Dark-germinated seeds and cultured tissues were separate experiments. Neither the food matrix nor processing corresponds to cooked snap pods. The 2023 Italian mass-spectrometry study used a more molecule-specific approach in different samples and detected lower values. This does not establish whether assay, cultivar or both account for the discrepancy.
