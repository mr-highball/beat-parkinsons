# Dill and black sticky rice in animal stroke experiments

## Open-field activity after the dill and rice extract

On February 26, 2019, Ohnon and colleagues reported seven groups of six male Wistar rats. After 16 weeks of diet induction, a subset underwent temporary right middle cerebral artery occlusion. The treatment was an oral mixture of aqueous black sticky rice grain extract and 95% hydroalcoholic extract of dill aerial parts, with a rice-to-dill extract ratio of 1:6. The starting dill aerial material was oven-dried and the extract was freeze-dried. The report does not resolve leaves from stems and gives no dill-only or rice-only animal arm. The three combined-extract doses were 0.5, 5 and 50 mg/kg per day for 21 days.

With stroke and vehicle, center-square entries were lower than in intact controls at days 7 and 14 (p < .05). Compared with stroke and vehicle, center entries favored the low-dose mixture on day 7 (p < .01) and the middle dose on day 14 (p < .001). The high-dose arm showed no significant center-entry difference. Figures 5 and 6 report no clear group differences in total crossings, licking, rearing or grooming. The authors used the open field to assess locomotion alongside the water-maze results. Center entries can reflect avoidance, exploration and motor performance. There was no separate anxiety severity or bradykinesia measure. ## Composite neurological score in the related dill and rice experiment

On November 11, 2019, the same six investigators reported an 18-point stroke severity score with motor, sensory, reflex and balance components. All three oral dill-and-rice extract doses lowered the reported composite versus stroke and vehicle over follow-up (p < .001, Figure 2). No motor item, movement speed or bradykinesia-specific estimate is reported. The paper describes scoring at 24 hours in §2.8 and every seven days in §2.6. The outcomes cover stroke and a combined extract with no dill-only arm.

The papers share ethics approval AEKKU 30/2558, two voucher numbers, starting rat ages and weights, the planned arm sizes and assignments, the 16-week diet, lesion side and period, and extract doses. The November paper cites the February paper for the blend and procedure. The February surgical subsection explicitly puts the start of treatment one week after surgery, while the November paper describes post-operative administration without specifying that delay. The reports also describe different tissue endpoints and preservation workflows. The November report includes fresh brain sections for infarct staining as well as perfusion-fixed tissue for histology. Neither gives animal IDs or paper-by-paper allocation of specimens. The common research program is documented and overlap of animals is possible. These results are treated as one potentially overlapping preclinical lineage for the experiment.
