# Anise seed symptom and safety evidence, 2015–2019

## Oral aqueous anise seed extract and systolic pressure in salt-fed rats

The Portuguese abstract explicitly reports daily oral aqueous extract and tail systolic pressure in salt-fed rats. Pressure fell toward normotensive values and urine volume did not change. The experiment provides model and route context for the related 2019 report. It did not test standing symptoms or daily falls. [Original FCMSCSP proceedings abstract 33](https://arquivosmedicos.fcmsantacasasp.edu.br/index.php/AMSCSP/article/download/169/179/290) · [Local source text](Source-Text/2015-Bottino-Pontes-Anise-Salt-Fed-Rats.txt)

## Star anise and green anise infusion in infant poisoning

The case describes liver failure and seizures or nystagmus after a homemade infusion containing both star anise and green anise in an infant. The two botanicals and systemic metabolic illness prevent ingredient-specific interpretation. The report does not provide a visual hallucination measure. [Primary Spanish full text](https://scielo.isciii.es/scielo.php?pid=S1130-01082016001200015&script=sci_arttext) · [Local source text](Source-Text/2016-Obando-Pacheco-Mixed-Anise-Infant-Poisoning.txt)

## Aqueous anise seed extract and arterial pressure across rat models

The primary report found lower blood pressure and bradycardia with a freeze-dried aqueous seed extract across rat experiments, with isolated-vessel findings consistent with interference with calcium influx. The related 2015 source confirms an oral salt-loaded rat protocol. Clinical orthostatic symptoms, falls, and dietary ground seed remain unmeasured.
