# Recovered Parkinson clinical reports

## Hallucinations and rest tremor with concomitant muscular dystrophy

Paucar and colleagues describe a woman with genetically supported facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy and levodopa-responsive parkinsonism. Her clinical history included back pain, resting tremor and visual hallucinations. The report describes a qualitative reduction in hallucination frequency after introducing quetiapine and a tremor response to levodopa. The authors discuss idiopathic Parkinson disease as compatible with the presentation. The case has no symptom episode counts or assigned culinary exposure. [Original journal article](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mdc3.12247) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2015-Paucar-Dystrophy-Parkinsonism-Symptom-Extract.txt)

## Fatigue and first visual hallucinations in a prospective cohort

Ikeda and colleagues analyzed 63 people with Parkinson’s disease over three years. Thirty-one had first-onset visual hallucinations, including six with both visual and auditory hallucinations. Table 2 associates baseline Brief Fatigue Inventory score with incident hallucinations (adjusted OR 1.027 per score point, 95% CI 1.001–1.053). The first-event count differs from episodes per patient, and the paper did not test food. The text describes several fatigue subscores as lower among those with incident hallucinations, while Table 3 prints higher means for general fatigue, mood and relations in that group. [Original full paper, Tables 1–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5803089/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2016-Ikeda-Fatigue-and-Incident-Hallucinations.txt)

## CHAPS care coordination protocol and later outcomes

The CHAPS protocol names pain as a clinical problem routed to a care plan and adapts an MDS-UPDRS apathy item within its intervention assessment. The trial’s 2019 report compares nurse-led care coordination with usual care. The author abstract reports guideline adherence of 0.77 versus 0.58, with a reported mean difference of 0.19 (95% CI 0.16–0.22). Among eight secondary outcomes the one reported clear difference was a depression screen. The protocol assessment list describes clinical problems and does not provide a pain or apathy treatment estimate. The sources give no dried date comparison. [2015 original Table 2](https://bmcneurol.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12883-015-0506-y#page=6) · [2019 author results](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000007324) · [Local 2012 registration](../2010-2014/Source-Text/Trials/NCT01532986.txt) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2019-Connor-CHAPS-Care-and-Outcomes.txt)

## Camptocormia, postoperative back pain and rest tremor

In the 2016 case report, a patient with Parkinson’s disease had slight resting tremor and low-back pain described as moderate and at times severe. Pain varied with posture, previously improved temporarily after lumbar surgery and later improved after subthalamic stimulation. The reported pain-scale wording leaves the exact change unclear, and the rest tremor is described qualitatively. This source concerns clinical interventions and the patient's coexisting spine disease. It supplies no food exposure comparison. [Original case report and discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4765245/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2016-Camptocormia-Case-Pain-and-Rest-Tremor.txt)

## Apathy and hallucination ascertainment across imaging clusters

Segura and colleagues report Starkstein Apathy Scale means of 15.11, 11.60 and 11.29 in three cortical-thickness clusters of nondemented Parkinson participants and 8.38 in healthy controls (four-group p = .003). Table 1 classifies visual hallucination presence, with four-group p = .245. The second cluster is labeled n = 29 but gives the count and percentage as 6 (22.2%) without a reported item-specific denominator. This comparison assesses clinical characteristics across imaging-defined groups and has no report of fig or date intake. [Archived full original, Table 1](Source-Text/PMC5061099.txt#L140)

## Coffee recall and mental fatigue in two related Parkinson cases

Hertz and colleagues’ Table 1 reports a premorbid eight cups of coffee per day for case 1 and two to three cups per day in case 2. Mental-fatigue scores were 10.5 and 10 out of 44. The scores are contemporaneous clinical descriptions and the exposure history does not give brewing, dose by weight or timing relative to symptoms. The patients differ in disease stage and other clinical circumstances. Table 1 labels the first patient’s presenting tremor as resting, while the narrative states that resting tremor was not documented during the initial investigation. [Original Table 1 and case descriptions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6417758/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2019-Hertz-Coffee-and-Mental-Fatigue-Cases.txt)
