# Capsaicin and Capsicum preparation comparisons in mice and rats

## Oral capsaicin after sensory nerve transection

In a separate experiment Choi and colleagues gave male Sprague-Dawley rats oral isolated capsaicin or water after bilateral transection of the lingual and chorda tympani nerves. The 0.02% capsaicin solution was delivered in the oral cavity. Results after repeated exposure show increased center-zone activity, general ambulation, distance and rearing. The elevated plus-maze scores did not clearly differ. Forced-swim immobility was lower descriptively at p=.0547. Basal and stress-associated corticosterone were higher. The center-zone result occurred alongside greater activity, so its interpretation as a distinct anxiety effect is uncertain. The earlier intact-rat study measured reduced open-arm activity. The animal models, behavioral tasks and exposure periods differed, and the comparison does not test Parkinson anxiety after smoked paprika. ## Capsicum fruit extracts in mouse anxiety and sedation assays

Jawad and colleagues tested sun-dried Capsicum annuum fruit as a methanolic crude extract or n-hexane fraction. Their fraction increased elevated plus-maze open-arm entries and time for the tested doses, with reported p<.001. Light–dark transitions and time varied with preparation and dose. The study also measured muscle relaxation and barbiturate potentiation. The sleep arm used injection and defined duration as loss to return of righting after thiopental. An extract dose and the higher tested fraction dose prolonged that interval. Differences in locomotion, relaxation and sedation complicate attribution of maze behavior to anxiety. The tested fractions, administration, and induced responses leave the smoked-paprika anxiety and ground-paprika insomnia claims unresolved.
