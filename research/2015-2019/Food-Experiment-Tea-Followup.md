# Green tea, mucuna, and levodopa/benserazide in a Parkinson’s case

## Published case and outcome table

González-Maldonado and colleagues’ June 1, 2016 letter describes a person with Parkinson’s and substantial motor and nonmotor fluctuations. During monitored changes to the amount and timing of mucuna seed powder and extended-release levodopa/benserazide, the patient drank a cup of green tea with most administrations. The published table gives total UPDRS scores of 139 before and 99 after the regimen change during OFF periods, and 53 before and 33 after during ON periods. Part IV changed from 14 to 9 during OFF and 11 to 3 during ON, with no separate dyskinesia or OFF-time result. The authors also describe improvement in gait, fluctuations, anxiety and apathy at three months. [Publication date and citation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27222087/) · [Published letter and table, p. 524](https://storage.imrpress.com/journal/RN/62/11/10.33588/rn.6211.2016033/pdf/7c5432900ba932cf0e9e4c8eba052913.pdf)

An earlier author draft available online gives different totals and a two-month follow-up. The published journal table supplies the figures used here. The authors describe uncertainty about the contribution of green tea. The report gives neither brewing parameters nor separate results for [olfactory impairment, 0030](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0030.txt), [small handwriting, 0126](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0126.txt), or [fatigue, 0948](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0948.txt).
