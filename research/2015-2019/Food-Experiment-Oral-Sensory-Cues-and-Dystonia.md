# Oral sensory cues and dystonia

## Chewing gum as a sensory cue in a Parkinson case

The primary case report title identifies a chewing-gum sensory trick in a patient whose orolingual dystonia was a presenting feature of idiopathic Parkinson disease. The indexed first publication date is September 10, 2015. The publisher body could not be retrieved. No brand, flavor, ingredient, scoring method or measured effect size is established from the indexed record.

## An oral object and peak dose orolingual dystonia

Georgiev and colleagues first published the primary case on June 10, 2016. In one patient with advanced Parkinson disease and peak-dose orolingual dystonia, the authors observed a reduction in tongue and mouth movements during a maneuver with a straw or stick. The recorded change began just before the object was introduced. The case also separately describes diminished dyskinesia after adjustment of levodopa. The cue result concerns a mechanical or anticipatory maneuver. It does not test a food or flavor.

[Primary record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1002/mdc3.12362) · [Local text](Source-Text/2016-Georgiev-Orolingual-Dystonia-Sensory-Cue-Extract.txt)

## Chewing gum and candy in a lingual dystonia series

Yoshida's 2017 series reported sensory tricks in 57 of 95 patients with task-specific idiopathic or tardive lingual dystonia. Table 2 lists chewing gum in 33 and candy in seven. Touch and other objects are also reported. These are observed or reported sensory maneuvers in a selected clinical series. Assignments, ingredient composition and spice-specific Parkinson outcomes were outside the study.
