# Almond and avocado oil studies, 2015–2019

## Sweet almond massage and depressive symptoms after menopause

Lotfipur-Rafsanjani and colleagues assigned 120 participants to massage with geranium and sweet almond oil, massage with sweet almond oil alone, or no massage. Of 118 analyzed, 38 were in the almond massage arm. The table’s depression score changed from 1.01 to 0.81 with almond oil massage and stayed at 1.00 in the no-massage arm. The paper describes the Beck Depression Inventory but does not clearly label the scale transformation behind these table values. There was no otherwise identical oil-free massage arm. [Primary report, Tables 2 and 3](https://brieflands.com/journals/zjrms/articles/970)

## Almond oil as a Parkinson smell-test stimulus

Kadirova and colleagues presented a six-odor test in 66 people with Parkinson’s and 66 healthy volunteers at the June 2016 MDS Congress. Their abstract reports a lower combined recognition rate in Parkinson’s and identifies almond oil among the three odors with the largest differences. It gives no almond-specific rate and uses the oil as a diagnostic stimulus. [Primary congress abstract 298](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/olfactory-loss-early-non-motor-symptom-in-parkinsons-disease/)

## Oral and injected almond oil in a forced-swim test

Al-Sayari and colleagues compared oral and intraperitoneal almond oil in small groups of male Wistar rats. Figure 1C reports more climbing after oral oil and no clear difference in swimming or immobility. The high injected dose lowered immobility in a separate comparison. The figure’s overall caption calls it inhalation although the methods, subpanel labels and results distinguish oral and injected oil. The report names the manufacturer and leaves oil processing unspecified. Forced-swim behavior is an animal proxy for depressive-like behavior. [Primary article, Figure 1](https://www.alliedacademies.org/articles/the-antidepressantlike-effect-of-almond-oil-an-additive-effect-with-lavender-oil-10857.html)

## Maternal avocado oil and offspring memory

De Melo and colleagues fed maternal rats avocado oil, pulp or a water control during gestation and lactation. The 2019 article reported earlier attainment of some developmental reflex measures and higher novel-object recognition in offspring of supplemented dams. The university dissertation describes the same study and clarifies the Hass oil extraction and developmental timetable. Open-field habituation, reflex maturation and recognition memory leave clinical walking speed, hand dexterity and executive function unresolved. [Local primary full text](Source-Text/PMC6351466.txt) · [Dissertation record](https://repositorio.ufpb.br/jspui/handle/123456789/20020)

## Avocado oil and blood pressure in hypertensive rats

Márquez-Ramírez and colleagues reported 15.5% lower systolic and 21.2% lower diastolic pressure with avocado oil in chemically hypertensive rats. The study measured blood pressure and renal vascular responses, without a postural challenge or clinical lightheadedness outcome. A later report from the research group included normotensive oil-fed rats. [2018 primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29753173/) · [Later blood-pressure study](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Almond-Avocado-Oils.md#avocado-oil-and-resting-pressure-across-animal-and-human-comparisons)

## Lipoid pneumonia with avocado and soybean unsaponifiables

A 2019 case report documented lipoid pneumonia in an older adult with hiatal hernia and severe reflux who took oral avocado and soybean unsaponifiables for osteoarthritis. Bronchoalveolar lavage showed lipid-laden macrophages and lipid droplets. Cough regressed and lung function normalized a year after discontinuation. The authors attributed the case to probable aspiration of the preparation in the setting of reflux. The report gives no Parkinson diagnosis, objective dysphagia score, or exposure to refined culinary avocado oil. [Local primary case and follow-up](Source-Text/PMC6892022.txt#L94)
