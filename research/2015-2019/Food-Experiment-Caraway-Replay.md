# Carvone locomotion and an allergy case finding

## Distinct carvone enantiomers in mouse hyperlocomotion assays

Nogoceke’s 2015 university dissertation and the group’s 2016 journal article have closely matching investigators, dose ranges and methylphenidate and sleep-deprivation comparisons. Treat them as one study family. In the journal abstract, both isolated carvone enantiomers attenuated induced hyperlocomotion. The caraway-associated S-(+) enantiomer also reduced spontaneous activity in the sleep-deprivation experiment. The dissertation abstract additionally describes a spontaneous-activity decrease under prolonged S-(+) treatment. These are locomotor activity data, not an apathy or motivation instrument. ## Hypophonesis in the caraway and coriander allergy case

A 2016 case describes hypotension, tachycardia and *hypophonesis* on emergency examination after a mixed meal. Clinical use of hypophonesis concerns a diminished sound on auscultation or percussion. The report gives neither a spoken outcome nor an examination of vocal folds. Rereview for the voice claims leaves the existing food-allergy assessment’s scope intact.
