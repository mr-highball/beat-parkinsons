# Potato-containing meals and daytime sleepiness

## Boiled potatoes on a Parkinson trial menu

Phillips et al. randomized 47 Parkinson patients to low-fat or ketogenic diets for eight weeks; 44 started and 38 completed. The low-fat plan includes chicken and vegetable soup: four potatoes are cubed and simmered 30–50 minutes with chicken, celery, peas and rice; it also schedules a mixed cannellini-bean hotpot with two potatoes, brought to a boil and simmered 40 minutes. The soup recipe heading says four servings, but its nutrition line calculates per three servings. These are multi-food meals, and the report does not say which individual participants ate the potato-containing recipes. [Original plan, printed pp. 79 and 86](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6175383.1/MDS-33-1306-s002.pdf).

Daytime sleepiness was a reported MDS-UPDRS Part I item. The authors report the change in the ketogenic group was 0.45 points lower than the change in the low-fat group; the comparison is between whole dietary patterns and is does not estimate an effect of potatoes. Participants were instructed to take levodopa at least one hour before or after meals. [Primary trial](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002%2Fmds.27390).

## Registered pediatric potato meals and acute sleepiness

The official registry first posted July 1, 2016. Its 22-child crossover lists six morning conditions: mashed potatoes, fried potato strips, hash browns, white rice, white beans and no meal. Each food served 50 g available carbohydrate. Secondary mood ratings include acute sleepiness at baseline and 10, 30, 60, 120 and 180 minutes. The record does not identify boil preparation or supply sleepiness results. The later Lee primary abstract is a probable match based on the institution, actual sample, six conditions, dose and timing. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02820805) · [Local text](Source-Text/2016-NCT02820805-Potato-Sleepiness.txt)

## Published pediatric potato meal trial and missing sleepiness outcome

First published March 7, 2019. The primary abstract reports 22 children and the same six conditions as NCT02820805. It describes fresh potatoes processed into mash, frozen and microwaved, with no documented boiling step. Word recall was higher after French fries than after mash, 9.1 versus 8.2 words (p=.001). The abstract does not give acute sleepiness data. The full Methods and item-level result remain unverified.
