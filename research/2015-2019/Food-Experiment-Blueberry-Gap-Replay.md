# Blueberry preparations in two Parkinson diet plans

Phillips and colleagues' eight-week randomized Parkinson trial compared whole low-fat and ketogenic dietary patterns. The low-fat plan offered blueberries in uncooked fruit salad, with fresh or frozen berry substitutions permitted (printed p. 89), as well as berries explicitly marked frozen acceptable in shopping lists. The ketogenic plan offered raspberries or blueberries, likewise permitting frozen fruit. The protocol tracked completion of meals rather than maintaining ingredient-level food diaries, so neither actual blueberry selection nor the raw or frozen portion eaten by any participant is established. [Primary low-fat plan](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6175383.1/MDS-33-1306-s002.pdf#page=89) · [Ketogenic plan](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6175383.1/MDS-33-1306-s003.pdf) · [Primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6175383/)

Of 47 randomized patients, 44 commenced and 38 completed; missing primary outcomes were imputed. The trial reported total MDS-UPDRS Part III change −8.58 ± 5.50 low-fat and −6.27 ± 4.07 ketogenic, between-group p = .055. Part III includes clinical ratings of facial expression and speech; its speech rating combines volume, modulation and clarity. The primary report and supplementary sensitivity table do not give separate facial expression or speech-item results, acoustic loudness or listener-scored intelligibility. Total motor score differences cannot be assigned to blueberry or to these specific outcomes. ## Atypical parkinsonism case with blueberry in a fixed diet

Tetzloff et al. reported one person followed over ten years for progressive apraxia of speech, ultimately with autopsy-confirmed corticobasal degeneration. At a late visit the account describes a fixed diet including blueberries and examination found the person essentially mute with parkinsonism. Berry species, preparation, amount and duration are not stated; there is no exposure comparison and the clinical disease is not established idiopathic Parkinson's disease. The case cannot establish improvement or worsening from blueberry.
