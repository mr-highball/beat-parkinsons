# Chervil and lovage leaf outcomes

## 2015 Iranian registration of a lovage syrup study

IRCT2013061213655N1 was registered retrospectively on June 23, 2015. It specifies a single-arm, unblinded study in 18 people with cystinuria. The Persian record describes *Levisticum officinale* syrup over two weeks, with 24-hour urinary cystine as the primary outcome. The accessible protocol does not identify the plant organ or include a Parkinson symptom measure. [English registry](https://irct.ir/trial/13449) · [Persian registry](https://fa.irct.ir/trial/13449)

## 2016 Urinary markers after lovage extract in cystinuria

Mohammadi and colleagues reported before and after results from 18 patients with cystinuria. Urinary cystine and urine volume decreased, while the change in creatinine was inconclusive (p = .09). The paper does not identify a separate pain or neuropsychiatric outcome. The syrup/extract, comparator-free design and cystinuria population leave the ordinary culinary leaf claims unresolved. This paper is the apparent result of the 2015 Iranian registration based on matching institution, enrollment, intervention and endpoints. [Primary article](https://www.jscimedcentral.com/public/assets/articles/urology-3-1071.pdf) · [Registered study](#2015-iranian-registration-of-a-lovage-syrup-study)

## 2018 Registration for an acute lovage soup crossover

NCT03577145 first posted July 5, 2018. The randomized crossover included tomato, onion and lovage soup, the same soup with inulin, and inulin alone. The protocol lists lovage in a mixed meal and includes exploratory gastric emptying and orocecal transit measures. The registered primary outcome was urinary phenolic acid excretion in healthy adults. Neither an isolated leaf comparison nor a Parkinson symptom was specified. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03577145) · [Official protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/45/NCT03577145/Prot_SAP_000.pdf)

## 2019 Lovage soup crossover results

Cong and colleagues reported NCT03577145 at the April 2019 Nutrition Society conference, with a July 24 online abstract. Fifteen healthy adults completed randomized crossover exposures. Total urinary phenolic acid excretion excluding hippuric acid did not differ detectably between soup with and without inulin. In one participant, urinary metabolites incorporated a stable-isotope tracer from lovage. The report did not give the planned transit or gastric-emptying results or measure the frozen Parkinson outcomes. [Primary abstract](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0029665119000818) · [University record of April presentation](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/impact-of-inulin-on-phenolic-acid-bioavailability-of-tomato-onion)

## 2019 Registration for a six-week lovage soup trial

NCT03840746 first posted February 15, 2019. BLEND2 compared a cooked tomato, onion and lovage soup with or without added inulin and habitual diet in generally healthy adults. The official protocol specifies one portion per day over six weeks and scheduled blood, urine and stool assessments. The protocol provides no leaf-only exposure or Parkinson symptom measures. The registry indicated completed with no posted results when retrieved. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03840746) · [Official protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/46/NCT03840746/Prot_SAP_000.pdf) · [Later published feasibility abstract](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Chervil-Lovage-Outcomes.md#2020-feasibility-and-adverse-events-in-the-six-week-lovage-soup-trial)

## 2019 Lovage root and pain-like behavior in rat cystitis

Nausch and colleagues compared BNO 2103, its three individual herbal components and controls in a rat cystitis model. In Figure 1C–D, the pulverized lovage root arm significantly reduced the area under the mechanical nociception response curve compared with vehicle. The root gave the largest reduction among the individual components, with a smaller numerical reduction than the blend. The centaury arm did not reach statistical significance. This isolated-root comparison establishes a pain-like behavioral result in experimental cystitis, with a different plant part and condition from dried leaf in Parkinson disease. [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phymed.2019.152987) · [Accepted manuscript, Methods and Figure 1](https://www.academia.edu/165398003/The_standardized_herbal_combination_BNO_2103_contained_in_Canephron_N_alleviates_inflammatory_pain_in_experimental_cystitis_and_prostatitis)

## 2019 Lovage leaf phenolics and inflammatory enzyme activity

Złotek and colleagues studied phenolic fractions from *Levisticum officinale* leaves grown with or without jasmonic acid or yeast extract treatment. Figure 2 shows that some treatments altered lipoxygenase and cyclooxygenase-2 inhibition relative to untreated plant controls. No participant or animal pain was assessed. This work preceded the group's simulated digestion and drying comparisons. [Local primary text, Figure 2](Source-Text/PMC6480578.txt)

## Lovage root and leaf uses in Lithuanian interviews

2018-11-20. Pranskuniene and colleagues interviewed 28 people in Samogitia. Table 1 records two reports of internal root decoction for joint pain and two reports of internal leaf tea for inflammatory diseases. The preparations and indications are separate. It does not test pain intensity after dried lovage leaf or make a clinical Parkinson comparison.
