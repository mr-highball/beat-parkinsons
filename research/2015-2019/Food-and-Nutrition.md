# Food and Nutrition

### 2016 — Glucose challenges and post-meal blood pressure

Trahair and colleagues studied 21 people with mild-to-moderate Parkinson’s disease after a standardized glucose drink. Eight met the study’s postprandial hypotension definition. The blood pressure response had no clear association with the measured gastric emptying rate. In a separate report on untreated Parkinson’s disease, Umehara and colleagues found that blood pressure drops after glucose were associated with pre-existing orthostatic hypotension and other clinical findings. Both reports give physiological context for meal-associated hypotension. They provide no estimate for steamed brown rice or orthostatic lightheadedness severity. [Trahair primary full text, May 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4873878/) · [Umehara primary report, 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27393383/)

### 2016 — Fermented milk with probiotics and prebiotic fiber

A four-week randomized trial found about one additional complete bowel movement per week with the combined fermented milk formulation compared with placebo. The study measured constipation and tested the whole preparation, leaving the separate effects of the milk, fiber and probiotic strains unresolved. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27543643/)

### 2017 — Dairy categories in two prospective cohorts

Lower-fat dairy, especially skim or low-fat milk, was associated with higher incidence, while total dairy intake did not show a significant association. Results concern future diagnosis in observational cohorts and could reflect confounding. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28596209/)

### 2017 — Caffeine retested for sustained motor benefit

The multicenter Café-PD randomized trial found no motor-score advantage at six months. This longer study revisited the motor signal in the smaller 2012 trial. Incidence associations address a separate question. [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5664303/)

### 2017 — Mucuna pruriens in a single-dose crossover trial

In a double-blind crossover study of 18 participants with advanced disease, preparations from levodopa-containing velvet bean showed an acute motor response. Single-dose effects leave long-term tolerability, product consistency and clinical equivalence open. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28679598/)

### 2017 — Reported food intake and symptom burden

In a cross-sectional baseline analysis of 1,053 people with self-reported Parkinson’s, fresh produce, nuts and nonfried fish were among foods associated with lower patient-reported burden after adjustment. Fried foods, soda and several dairy foods were among those associated with higher burden. A single baseline observation and self-reported diet and diagnosis cannot establish that specific foods changed progression. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29081890/)

### 2018 — Ketogenic versus low-fat diet

In an eight-week pilot, the ketogenic group showed greater improvement on the MDS-UPDRS nonmotor experiences scale, with no significant between-group advantage on other parts. Intermittent tremor or rigidity worsening was reported in the ketogenic arm and hunger in the low-fat arm. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30098269/)

### 2019 — Patient account of banana intake and levodopa response

Shuji Kitagawa’s memoir was published in February 2019. A later publisher excerpt recounts his observation that mornings with weaker perceived levodopa effect and difficulty walking sometimes followed higher banana intake. The retrospective account contains no standardized symptom ratings or drug concentrations. The accessible sources give no link between Kitagawa and the patient in Hotozuka and colleagues’ later case. [National Diet Library book record](https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000002-I030477904) · [Publisher excerpt and clinical follow-up](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#japanese-banana-and-levodopa-follow-up)

### 2019 — Nutritional status and swallowing scores in the Befelatanana clinic

Rasaholiarison and colleagues reported 38 patients with Parkinson’s in Antananarivo. The Mini Nutritional Assessment classified 5 as malnourished, 18 at risk and 15 in the normal range. The group with nutritional problems averaged 8 on the Swallowing Disturbance Questionnaire versus 3 in the group without, a statistically inconclusive comparison. The abstract links nutritional status and swallowing measurements and gives no exposure or outcome comparison for an individual food. The publisher dates the abstract March 27, 2019, ahead of the April supplement. [Primary congress abstract](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1283790/evaluation-de-l-etat-nutritionnel-des-patients-aya)

### 2016 — Protein intake and levodopa timing in Macaé

Carmo and Ferreira measured three days of dietary intake and usual levodopa timing in 40 patients. Table 1 classified 9 as malnourished and 16 as at risk. Mean protein intake was 1.4 g/kg/day, and Table 4 classified 30 of 40 as outside the study’s timing rule. The design measured habits and nutrition at one point. It provides no symptom change, measured levodopa response, or individual-food estimate. [Primary paper, Tables 1–4](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbgg/a/JMqZFbyfncfB6By6xGxDjcr/?lang=pt) · [Local article text](Source-Text/2016-Carmo-Ferreira-Macae-Protein-Levodopa.txt)

### 2019 — Swallowing questionnaire in a Lima Parkinson’s clinic

Torres Ramírez and colleagues administered the Spanish Swallowing Disturbances Questionnaire to 200 patients able to eat orally. Thirty-five (17.5%) scored above 11. The questions include examples of apples, cookies, and purées alongside broader solid and liquid categories. Responses are grouped by question, so they do not identify the individual food that led to difficulty. No baked potato was administered or analyzed. The issue bears an October–December 2018 cover date, with April 17, 2019 reported for the online article. Both dates fall in this period. [Primary report, questionnaire and results](https://revistadiagnostico.fihu.org.pe/index.php/diagnostico/article/download/48/58/52) · [Online record](https://doi.org/10.33734/diagnostico.v57i4.48)

### 2018 — Red quinoa extract in a fly-model conference report

Espinosa and colleagues’ report from the November 6–8, 2018 SIEPE meeting describes three groups of flies studied for seven days. It reported that red quinoa extract attenuated rotenone-associated climbing and acetylcholinesterase changes. The overlapping team later documented a four-arm comparison and more endpoints. The reports do not identify shared flies, so the early comparison supplies related evidence with possible overlap. Neither report tested a culinary quinoa preparation or measured a symptom in people with Parkinson’s. [Primary meeting paper](https://guri.unipampa.edu.br/uploads/evt/arq_trabalhos/17363/seer_17363.pdf) · [University confirmation of the 2018 meeting](https://unipampa.edu.br/portal/es/node/4514) · [Later dissertation](../2020-2024/Food-and-Nutrition.md#2022--red-quinoa-extract-in-a-fly-model-dissertation)

### 2016 — Prediagnosis dietary B vitamins and olfactory scores

Håglin and colleagues' October 5 report linked the Umeå incident parkinsonism study to an earlier diet questionnaire. The analysis identified 96 cases with Parkinson’s or atypical parkinsonism and 375 controls. Lower dietary thiamin and folate density two to eight years before diagnosis was reported in patients with lower smell identification scores at diagnosis. The adjusted association was clearer for thiamin. The published abstract reports nutrient density with no individual food or preparation estimate. The 2011 Umeå report used diet records and recalls near diagnosis. Participant overlap and the availability of item-level foods remain unresolved. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27703161/) · [University bibliographic record](https://umu.diva-portal.org/smash/record.jsf?pid=diva2%3A1058478) · [Earlier Umeå study](../2010-2014/Food-and-Nutrition.md#2011--nutrient-intake-and-smell-identification-at-diagnosis)
