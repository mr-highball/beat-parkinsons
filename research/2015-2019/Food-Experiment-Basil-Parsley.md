# Basil and parsley evidence

## Holy basil mixture and registered fall frequency

NCT03365310 first posted December 7, 2017. The open-label plan lists three-month fall frequency as a secondary measure in older adults with frailty. The supplement uses holy basil (*Ocimum sanctum*) in a mixture with turmeric, ginger and milk. The April 2018 update was the latest available, status was unknown and no results were posted at review. The species, combination and population do not establish a dried sweet basil comparison in Parkinson disease. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03365310)

## Fresh parsley and stress related gastric injury

Balkan Medical Journal published this rat study on January 5, 2017. Fresh parsley incorporated into the diet reduced gastric mucosal injury and oxidative stress measures following experimental stress. The discussion also describes an author’s self-observation of less gastric burning and pain after fresh parsley. The animal study measured gastric injury and the self-observation did not quantify pain or compare with a control. Claim 0203 concerns dried parsley and Parkinson’s pain. [Local primary text](Source-Text/PMC5322505.txt) · [Published article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5322505/)

## Parsley allergy and preparation tolerance

Ventilacion’s 2017 case abstract reports a teenager with anaphylaxis after fresh parsley, a milder reaction after cooked parsley, and subsequent tolerance of dried parsley. This documents preparation-dependent allergy history in one patient. Speech intelligibility and swallowing function were not measured. [Primary abstract P.087](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/cea.13051)

## Dried parsley and blood pressure registration

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT03468361 on March 16, 2018. The study planned to give adults with hypertension capsules containing market-fresh parsley that was subsequently shade-dried and powdered. It planned repeated blood pressure and urine output measurements. The March 25, 2020 update reports zero participants and withdrawal because sites could not enroll. The planned outcomes did not specify standing blood pressure or lightheadedness. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03468361) · [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT03468361.txt)

## Parsley aerial extract and blood pressure in rats

Ajebli and Eddouks published in 2019. Oral aqueous extract from aerial parsley parts reduced systolic, diastolic and mean arterial pressure in normotensive and L-NAME hypertensive rats. The paper also reports reduced contraction in isolated aortic rings. This gives a blood-pressure-lowering lead for an extract, without a standing symptom measure or a Parkinson cohort. [Primary article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378874119300340)
