# Black tea, walking and tremor

## Randomized caffeine capsule study

The 2017 Café-PD trial randomized 121 patients to caffeine 200 mg twice daily or matched placebo capsules. The six-month motor examination score difference was −0.48 points (95% CI −3.21 to 2.25). Results describe inconclusive MDS-UPDRS II total and individual-item comparisons. That daily-living scale includes tremor and walking or balance items. No isolated Part III rest-tremor or timed walking comparison was found in the public main result. ## Caffeinated tea and clinical motor-stage transition

Paul and colleagues followed 360 Parkinson patients for mortality and actively reassessed 244. Their lifetime exposure grouped caffeinated tea without distinguishing black or green. Among 212 people modeled for Hoehn and Yahr stage ≥3, ever versus never caffeinated tea gave HR 0.93 (95% CI 0.51–1.71, p=.82). At or above median versus below median intake among drinkers gave HR 0.64 (95% CI 0.36–1.14, p=.13). A survival association is a separate endpoint. The motor staging models do not estimate walked speed or rest tremor severity. ## Coffee intake and separately scored resting tremor

In a separate cross-sectional study of 284 patients with newly diagnosed Parkinson disease, ever coffee drinkers had lower raw mean UPDRS rest-tremor scores than nondrinkers (1.49 ± 1.67 versus 2.41 ± 2.23). The adjusted comparison had p=.001. The adjusted intake-dose gradient was clear in men and inconclusive in women. Action tremor, gait and posture were inconclusive after adjustment. The study assessed coffee and does not estimate black tea effects.
