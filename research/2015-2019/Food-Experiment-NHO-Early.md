# Food, nutrition and swallowing in early National Hospital Organization proceedings

## Videofluoroscopy in the Omuta hospital feeding service

In 2015 Sakai and colleagues reported videofluoroscopy before and after three months of team swallowing therapy in 33 patients, including 19 with neurodegenerative conditions. Therapy included direct swallowing practice, exercise and oral ice massage. Only laryngeal penetration improved among 14 pooled outcomes. Absent, mild and moderate/severe counts changed from 14, 6 and 13 to 26, 2 and 5 (p = .01). None of the reported disease categories showed a significant change. The abstract names neither a Parkinson subgroup nor the food or bolus composition. The dates overlap the same hospital's earlier questionnaire and nutrition reports. [2015 NHO O-80-3, PDF page 716](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100775.pdf#page=716) · [Earlier series](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-NHO-Early.md#mixed-diagnosis-hospital-swallowing-service)

## Hospital mortality records for food choking and aspiration pneumonia

Koike and colleagues traced causes of death in 46 people with Parkinson disease admitted to NHO Nishiniigata Chuo Hospital between April 2005 and April 2015. Twenty-four deaths fell into the combined category of aspiration pneumonia or asphyxia caused by food. The abstract gives neither separate counts for the two causes nor the foods and preparations implicated. This is a selected hospital mortality series, without a measured association between a particular food and swallowing severity. [2015 NHO P2-1A-679, PDF page 1511](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100775.pdf#page=1511)

## Tongue pressure across five diet texture categories

Takeuchi and colleagues surveyed 46 Parkinson inpatients and measured tongue pressure. Regular-diet patients numbered five and averaged 25 kPa. The soft-side-dish group numbered ten (22 kPa), mashed and thickened group 21 (20 kPa), blended group three (10 kPa), and easy-swallow group seven (5 kPa). Patients already ate these diets when assessed. The abstract gives no individual ingredient, EAT-10 result or swallowing change after assignment to a different texture. [2015 NHO P2-1P-770, PDF page 1602](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100775.pdf#page=1602)

## Protein redistribution offered to Parkinson inpatients

Aita and colleagues reported a hospital program that allocated daily protein across breakfast, lunch and dinner at 25%, 25% and 50%. Twelve of 13 consenting Parkinson inpatients continued through discharge, with mean duration 13.6 days. One stopped because the program and snacking could not be reconciled. The abstract reports no worsening in measured rehabilitation indicators among completers, including simple upper-limb function testing. It gives no ingredient list, comparison group, measured levodopa concentrations or daily OFF-time outcome. [2015 NHO P2-2P-834, PDF page 2666](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100775.pdf#page=2666)

## Food texture adjustment with denture changes

Okumura and Katayama described an older Parkinson patient with vertebral compression fractures, severe kyphosis and difficulty chewing with dentures. Videofluoroscopy showed poor bolus formation, with no pharyngeal problem reported. The care team changed from Japanese dysphagia diet level 4 to level 2-1 and limited denture use. The authors reported less time to form a bolus and less interference with breathing. Several changes occurred together, and food ingredients and quantitative outcomes were unspecified. [2015 NHO P2-2P-830, PDF page 2662](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100775.pdf#page=2662)
