# Parkinson nutrition and swallowing in the 2016–2018 JSPEN meetings

## Black pepper aroma and swallowing reports in neighboring meetings

Fukushima and colleagues reported six psychiatric inpatients on long-term antipsychotics who used a black pepper essential-oil sheet for three months alongside swallowing exercises and oral care. Mean repeated saliva swallowing test counts were 3.0, 3.0 and 3.5 at months one, two and three. The mean number of self-reported symptoms increased from 3.17 to 4.67 of 13 over those months, while counts for swallowing difficulty and choking at meals were unchanged. No idiopathic Parkinson subgroup, ingestion, or parallel comparator was reported. [2015 JSNR P-7-4, printed p64](https://www.jsnr.or.jp/district/chugoku-shikoku/archive/028.pdf)

A 2018 black-pepper aroma presentation by Yūki Ikeda at Ryokuseikai Hospital appears under the same title and author order in two meetings. Its bodies remain unavailable. The 2019 JSPEN aroma-patch study is by Masaki Ikeda at a different institution with different coauthors. Two 2016 Kojima presentations likewise share a title and most authors, and their full abstract bodies remain gated. No shared cohort with the Tanaka Parkinson report has been established. ## Synbiotic tube feeding and constipation in 2016

Kazuyuki Yamaoka reported an 80-year-old man with Parkinson disease and constipation in P-0641. The team switched the gastrostomy feed from Hine E-Gel to YH Flore after one stool in six days. Constipation persisted despite a medication attempt. On day 28, they added 10 g/day of the named synbiotic electrolyte drink シンバイオの力イオンドリンク through the gastrostomy. The average went from 0.43 to 0.86 bowel movements per day across the respective 21-day periods, and Bristol stool form went from type 1 to type 4. This is a single pre/post course with a combined product and background enteral feeding. The abstract does not list its individual ingredients or a concurrent comparison. A contemporaneous corporate filing confirms the product family but does not give its composition. [Primary abstract P-0641, printed p. 514](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/31/1/31_454/_pdf/-char/ja#page=61) · [2016 company filing](https://kitaishihon.s3.isk01.sakurastorage.jp/IrLibrary/2207_securities_2015_t0xl.pdf)

## Stool changes with tube formula and fiber in 2016

Ōnaka described a Parkinson patient admitted with aspiration pneumonia and no visible swallowing reflex on endoscopy. A formula designed to gel in the stomach was followed two days later by abundant watery stool. Stool softened after adding glutamine, fiber and oligosaccharide, and the clinician reported better form after switching to a formula containing insoluble fiber. The report also describes salt supplementation during hyponatremia and continued tube feeding after a later oral intake attempt failed. It provides no stool counts, formula brand or individual ingredient effects. [Primary abstract P-0842, printed p. 564](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/31/1/31_554/_pdf/-char/ja#page=11)

## Aspiration, fatigue and gelled food in 2017

Nakatsuji and Urata reported a 73-year-old man with Parkinson disease who had previously been admitted with aspiration pneumonia. He developed fever the day after starting a jelly diet on day 2 and again after oral intake restarted on day 12. Oral intake later resumed, and the nasogastric tube came out on day 75. Around day 143, mealtime fatigue and reduced intake preceded another fever and suspected aspiration pneumonia. The patient returned to exclusive tube feeding on day 146. The abstract does not identify the jelly ingredients, confirm that they caused fever, or describe food blocking the airway. [Primary abstract P-0650, printed p. 657](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/32/supplement/32_595/_pdf/-char/ja#page=63)

Ryūji Yajima reported a woman in her 70s with a prior Parkinson diagnosis whose eating and speech deteriorated with opening jaw dystonia. Symptoms varied independently of her Parkinson motor signs. Denture adjustment reduced the jaw opening, and the patient progressed to eating gelled rice porridge before discharge. Speech became easier to understand. The rice porridge was a recovered eating ability, with no recipe or comparison of food effects. [Primary abstract P-0775, printed p. 688](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/32/supplement/32_595/_pdf/-char/ja#page=94)

## Miso and carrot formula in a stroke case with parkinsonism in 2017

In P-0171 a patient with cerebral and cerebellar infarctions, dementia and parkinsonism developed pseudomembranous colitis. The team gave tube-administered miso soup and a carrot-containing nutrition product alongside vancomycin. Diarrhea was described as soft stool by the second day of tube administration and C. difficile toxin later became negative. Vomiting attributed to the gastric tube occurred on day 13. This was infection-related diarrhea amid combined treatment. The report does not establish idiopathic Parkinson disease or the preparation and isolated effect of the carrot. [Primary abstract P-0171, printed p. 537](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/32/supplement/32_495/_pdf/-char/ja#page=43) · [Carrot formula description in 2016 P-0911](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/31/1/31_554/_pdf/-char/ja#page=28)

## Home sodium plan and dysphagia in 2018

Kozuka and colleagues reported a 69-year-old man with stage V Parkinson disease, opening apraxia, dehydration and hyponatremia. The home team planned sodium additions to staple food and tea, hydration support, a commercial nutrition supplement, and pudding or a similar food as a possible medication carrier. Weight increased from 46.1 kg at discharge to 52.3 kg after four months, and albumin rose from 2.8 to 3.3 g/dL. The report gives no tea type, confirmed pudding intake, or food-specific swallowing measure. [Primary abstract O-218, printed p. 331](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/33/supplement/33_231/_pdf/-char/ja#page=101) · [Presenting hospital annual report](https://lib2.naramed-u.ac.jp/lmt/24240419/v25n1Suita.pdf)

## Cocoa and postoperative copper deficiency in 2018

P-884 reports one postoperative patient with dysphagia, neuropathic symptoms and copper deficiency. The initial differential had included Parkinson disease. After 12 g/day cocoa containing 0.5 mg copper was added to gastrostomy feeding, copper increased from 21 to 88 µg/dL at three months. Numbness subsided and walking returned, so enteral nutrition was increased. The cocoa stopped at eight months. At 12 months, videofluoroscopy showed better clearance and a pharyngeal constriction ratio of 0.11 versus 0.27 initially, and regular food became possible. The authors attributed the course to correcting copper deficiency and increasing nutrition in postoperative sarcopenia. The abstract does not document a confirmed Parkinson diagnosis or cocoa consumed by mouth. [Primary abstract P-884, printed p. 578](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/33/supplement/33_483/_pdf/-char/ja#page=96)
