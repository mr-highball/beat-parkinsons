# Shiso and perilla follow-up

## Perilla leaf extract in an induced constipation model

The 2017 *Biomedical Research* paper tested *Perilla frutescens* var. *japonica* leaf extract separately from perilla fruit and mallow in mice with activated-carbon-induced constipation. Figure 6 reports intestinal transit of 64.2% and 79.3% at the lower and higher leaf-extract levels, respectively, compared with 28.9% in induced controls. The study tests an extract in an induced gastrointestinal model. The 2021 Book Publisher International chapter uses the same protocol and is not counted as a separate experiment. The earlier placebo-controlled human trial found no clear constipation difference for another leaf extract. [Primary paper, Figure 6](https://www.alliedacademies.org/download.php?download=articles%2Fpreventive-effects-of-malvae-verticilate-and-perilla-frutescens-var-japonica-leaf-on-activated-carbon-induced-constipation-in-icr-.pdf) · [Later chapter](https://stm.bookpi.org/SBFDHP/article/view/2711) · [Earlier human comparison](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Shiso-Followup.md#dried-leaf-extract-and-constipation-measures-in-adults)

## Perilla extract and micturition in hypertensive rats

Kitamura and colleagues first published on December 21, 2017. After two weeks, female spontaneously hypertensive rats given perilla extract had a 27% longer micturition interval than controls, with no significant difference in maximum bladder pressure. Human urothelial cells were used separately to examine inflammatory signaling. This result concerns an extract, animal urinary frequency and cell assays. Neither a perceived urge to urinate nor a fresh-leaf exposure in people with Parkinson’s was measured. [Primary report, PMID 29268303](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29268303/)

## Leaf extract and sleep-stage measures in rodents

Kwon’s 2015 Chungbuk National University thesis and the matching 2017 report describe ethanol extract of *Perillae Herba*. The journal study reports shorter pentobarbital-induced sleep latency and longer sleep duration in mice. In rats, Figure 4 shows more total sleep and less wakefulness, and the Results report no significant changes in REM or NREM duration separately. The closely related laboratory report on purified rosmarinic acid appeared online in 2016 and reports less time in REM sleep and more time in NREM sleep in rats. Time spent in a sleep stage does not measure movements or dream enactment. Both studies used concentrated preparations in rodents outside Parkinson models. [2015 thesis record](https://www.dbpia.co.kr/journal/detail?nodeId=T13869133) · [2017 extract paper, Figure 4](https://synapse.koreamed.org/upload/synapsedata/pdfdata/0228nps/nps-23-53.pdf) · [Purified constituent, PMID 27469144](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27469144/)

## Green leaf compound and motor behavior in a Parkinson model

Izumi and colleagues isolated the chalcone 2′,3′-dihydroxy-4′,6′-dimethoxychalcone (DDC) from green perilla leaves. Direct intracerebral DDC suppressed behavioral dysfunction and dopaminergic neuronal loss in a 6-OHDA mouse model. The accessible report describes aggregate behavioral dysfunction and supplies no clinical bradykinesia or rigidity result for consumed fresh leaves. This is the same study already linked from the period’s herb notes. [Primary report, PMID 28167258](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28167258/) · [Existing period note](Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#a-compound-isolated-from-green-shiso-leaves-in-a-mouse-model)

## Early fermented perilla patent examples

First published June 6, 2019. Ajou University's application describes a hot-water perilla leaf extract and extracts fermented with different organisms in a mouse pentobarbital sleep test. One example gives onset latencies of 325 s with saline, 261 s with unfermented extract and 180 s with Bacillus-fermented extract. The patent is an early development record and shares institution and close assay features with later reports. Independent cohorts are unverified. It provides no culinary dried-leaf or human comparison. ## Perilla leaf extract and screen-induced ocular fatigue

Kim and colleagues first published July 26, 2017. The placebo comparison used a prepared aqueous extract of dried Perilla frutescens var. acuta leaves. In Table 1 the extract group's binocular near-point distance was 7.96 ± 2.97 cm before and 7.27 ± 3.25 after two hours of screen use. The placebo group was 9.00 ± 2.45 and 9.67 ± 2.48 cm. The results text gives p < .001 within each arm, without a clear between-group change estimate in the table. The abstract and discussion say 30 participants, while Methods report 30 per group. This trial measured eye accommodation after screen use. It gives no Parkinson fatigue or culinary dried-leaf estimate.
NCT02641470 describes Park and colleagues' separate bog-bilberry extract study, which links to PMID 27538497. It does not identify this perilla study. No matching official registration was verified for Kim. [Original paper and Table 1](https://pubs.sciepub.com/jfnr/5/8/4/index.html) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2017-Kim-Perilla-Ocular-Accommodation.txt) · [Separate bog-bilberry registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02641470)

## Registered mixed formula and fatigue after gastrectomy

First posted January 19, 2017. The current record lists post-gastrectomy cancer patients, 130 enrolled, termination after 37.7% attrition at one year, and no posted results. Secondary symptom items include physical fatigue and weakness and mental fatigue and taciturnity. The registry describes a formula with added perilla leaf and no Platycodon, at 10 g/day. The linked journal protocol conflicts on recipe, dose and placebo flavor. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03025152) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2017-NCT03025152-Perilla-Formulation-Conflict.txt)

## Linked gastrectomy protocol gives a different formula

Published June 10, 2019. The journal protocol explicitly identifies NCT03025152. Table 1 lists ten herbs including Platycodon and no perilla. It gives 30 g/day of diet formula, including 10.1 g of herbal materials. The placebo differs slightly in taste and smell. The protocol contains planned fatigue items without results, and does not resolve the administered formula or establish a separate perilla effect.
