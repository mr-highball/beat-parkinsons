# Oral cinnamon and pain in clinical trials

## Registered cinnamon trials and pain instruments

The multiple-sclerosis trial registered pain using PQAS in August 2016. The migraine trial registered VAS severity in January 2019, after the listed end of participation. These registrations belong to their later publications and do not add independent study samples. [Multiple sclerosis registry](https://irct.ir/trial/23434) · [Migraine registry](https://irct.ir/trial/35715) · [Later publications](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Cinnamon-Pain.md)

## Cinnamon powder and primary menstrual pain

In 2015 Jaafarpour and colleagues randomized 114 young adults with primary dysmenorrhea to cinnamon powder capsules, starch placebo, or ibuprofen. The Results give mean pain on a 0–10 visual analogue scale of 4.1 versus 6.1 for cinnamon versus placebo at 24 hours, 3.2 versus 5.3 at 48 hours, and 1.8 versus 4.0 at 72 hours. The four-hour comparison was inconclusive, and ibuprofen had lower pain scores than cinnamon. The paper identifies the powder supplier and reports registration IRCT2013122114668N2. A second paper from the same site and team has matched arm sizes and identical means at 24 and 72 hours, although the 48-hour placebo value differs. The two papers are treated as one likely overlapping study family. [Original three-arm study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4437117/) · [Apparently overlapping report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4443385/)

## Cinnamon powder and rheumatoid arthritis pain

In 2018 Shishehbor and colleagues reported a randomized comparison of cinnamon powder or placebo capsules for eight weeks in 36 women with rheumatoid arthritis. The primary publication's abstract reports lower visual analogue pain scores with cinnamon (p < .001). The numerical pain table and participant flow were unavailable. This was a separate sample with inflammatory joint pain. [Original indexed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29722610/)

## Cinnamon capsules and menstrual pain over two cycles

A separate group led by Jahangirifar reported a cinnamon capsule versus starch trial in 2018. The abstract gives a time-by-treatment interaction of p = .02, with conflicting wording about which group had the larger pain reduction. The conclusion favors cinnamon. Group values and the form of the cinnamon inside the capsules remain unverified from the accessible abstract. [Original indexed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30396627/)
