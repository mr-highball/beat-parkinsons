# Laval dietary models, 2015–2019

## 2015 corn, safflower and soybean oils in post-lesion mouse feed

Coulombe and colleagues first published online on December 11, 2015, before the April 2016 issue. Eighty male mice were assigned to sham or 6-OHDA groups and received control or high-omega-3 chow for six weeks beginning three weeks after surgery. Table 1 gives control versus high-omega-3 amounts of 10 versus 4 g/kg corn oil, 10 versus 17 g/kg safflower oil, and 30 versus 0 g/kg soybean oil. The enriched feed added 45 g/kg of a gelatin-coated DHA preparation and contained 7.2 g/kg measured DHA. The Methods describes the DHA as algae-derived. The study reports partial recovery of striatal dopamine and tyrosine hydroxylase, with no clear dietary effect on apomorphine rotations, forelimb contacts in the cylinder, adjusting steps, or open-field distance. The diets changed several ingredients together and give no refining grades. The 2017 thesis repeats this cohort in chapter 2 and Table 2.1. [Primary article, Methods, Table 1 and Figure 1](https://www.optinutribrain.ulaval.ca/fileadmin/Fichiers/centre_recherche/Coulombe_2016.pdf) · [PubMed publication history](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27012630/) · [Thesis](https://hdl.handle.net/20.500.11794/28207)

## 2017 four dietary oils and motor testing in Thy1 mice

Chapter 3 of Coulombe’s 2017 thesis and the journal article first published on December 8, 2017 describe the same male Thy1-αSyn mouse study, distinct from the 6-OHDA cohort. For control, low-omega-3, and high-DHA chow, Table 1 gives corn oil at 30, 25, and 10 g/kg, safflower oil at 0, 25, and 19 g/kg, and soybean and canola oil each at 10, 0, and 0 g/kg. The high-DHA diet added 50 g/kg of DHA powder and contained 7.2 g/kg measured DHA. [Thesis, chapter 3 and Table 3.1](https://corpus.ulaval.ca/bitstreams/7417bed2-1234-4720-9416-d8561c3d2b02/download) · [Primary author manuscript, Table 1](https://savoirs.usherbrooke.ca/bitstream/11143/15866/4/Plourde_ExpNeurol_Vol301PartA_p39-49_2018.pdf)

Motor tests at four, eight and 12 months included the beam, pole, cylinder, hanging wire, and open field. The high-DHA group performed worse on the pole test, especially at four months. It retained higher open-field activity at 12 months when activity had declined on control feed. The low-omega-3 group showed a similar open-field trend without statistical significance. The authors describe the motor findings as inconsistent and report longer survival in the high-DHA group. These are results for whole feed formulas in mice. No independent oil or oil-grade effect was estimated. A later paper reused tissues from these animals.
