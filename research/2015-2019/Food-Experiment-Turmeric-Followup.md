# Turmeric preparation and symptom outcomes

## Physical function and curcumin trial registration

NCT03085680 first posted March 21, 2017. The older-adult trial compared oral curcumin capsules with placebo and listed 400-m walking speed and handgrip strength as primary outcomes. It also registered cognition using the MMSE. It did not register a Parkinson cohort or specify a falls or freezing frequency endpoint. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03085680) · [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT03085680.txt) · [2022 results](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Turmeric-Followup.md#posted-curcumin-walking-and-cognitive-outcomes)

## Solid lipid curcumin and pegboard or balance outcomes

Santos-Parker and colleagues randomized 39 healthy adults to a solid lipid curcumin supplement or placebo for 12 weeks. Nine-hole pegboard, rapid step test, and four-meter gait speed had no clear between-arm changes. Fluid cognition measures were also inconclusive. These are standardized physical and cognitive tasks outside Parkinson’s. The trial gives no fall frequency, freezing episodes or handwriting-size result. [2018 primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6004902/) · [Companion registry NCT01968564](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01968564)
## Intragastric curcumin pretreatment and rat catalepsy

Cui, Li and Zhu published online December 8, 2015. Figure 1C reports shorter bar and grid latency after intragastric isolated curcumin pretreatment than after rotenone alone (both p<.05, six rats per group in the figure). Sunflower seed oil carried the injection or was the vehicle control. The paper reports 50 rats in animal care without a complete flow to the behavioral groups. Bar and grid catalepsy are indirect motor outcomes. The study did not report a grated turmeric or clinical rigidity comparison. [Publisher original and Figure 1](https://www.spandidos-publications.com/10.3892/mmr.2015.4657) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2015-Cui-Curcumin-Catalepsy-Extract.txt)


## Posted curcumin composite scores and unreported attention domain

Official results first posted May 15, 2019. For 17 Theracurmin and 19 placebo participants in the MCCB table, mean composite T-scores were 36.6 and 32.9 at baseline, 36.3 and 33.8 at week 4, and 35.8 and 33.9 at week 8. The posted table does not give an attention/vigilance subscore or an adjusted between-arm change test. The registry links Wynn PMID 28965778 to the same study. These values describe a global score in schizophrenia. [Original record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02104752?tab=results) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2019-NCT02104752-Curcumin-Cognition-Results.txt)

## Terminated curcumin pilot with registered attention testing

First posted June 19, 2015. The Yale trial stopped for insufficient recruitment with 12 enrolled. The protocol lists Continuous Performance Test–Identical Pairs as a secondary attention/vigilance measure and reports no structured results. The registry lists a 600-mg capsule three times daily versus placebo. The 2019 pilot publication names this registry. See the reported attention results and formulation discrepancy below. [Original record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02476708) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2015-NCT02476708-Curcumin-Vigilance-Protocol.txt)

## Published curcumin pilot attention and working memory estimates

The 2019 article expressly identifies NCT02476708. Twelve schizophrenia outpatients were randomized. Each analyzed group in Table 1 contains five. Eight-week change in attention/vigilance was +9.20 ± 18.18 for the active arm and −6.80 ± 12.79 for placebo (Mann–Whitney Z=1.257, p=.209). Working memory had a different estimate (+10.60 ± 15.17 versus −4.00 ± 5.52, p=.028). The authors describe 180 mg/day of active curcumin and capsules made from 300 mg of a 30% formulation. The official registry describes 600 mg capsules three times daily. The publication leaves that difference unresolved. This study measured attention outside Parkinson disease and used Theracurmin capsules rather than culinary grated rhizome. [Original source](https://doi.org/10.1097/JCP.0000000000001012) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2019-Kucukgoncu-Curcumin-Pilot-Attention.txt)

## Bioavailable curcumin and Trail Making A in nondemented adults

Published online October 27, 2017. Small and colleagues randomized 40 nondemented adults to oral Theracurmin or placebo for 18 months. Table 2 gives Trail Making A time from 32.6 to 24.9 seconds with curcumin versus 30.5 to 28.4 seconds on placebo. The reported group-by-time result was p=.04. This was a secondary timed attention and motor-speed task. The trial did not test culinary turmeric, clinical Parkinson attention impairment or an attention-specific primary endpoint. [Original report](https://www.ajgponline.org/article/S1064748117305110/pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2017-Small-Theracurmin-Trail-Making-A.txt)

## Lipid curcumin and the Flanker attention task

Published online March 25, 2018. Thirty-nine adults aged 45–74 were assigned to Longvida 2000 mg/day or placebo for 12 weeks. Table 3 gives identical rounded flanker inhibitory control and attention means for curcumin and placebo, 8.3 to 8.5, arm-by-time p=.97. Improved scores over time in both groups do not support an added effect in this comparison. This course used a formulated supplement in healthy aging. [Original report](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/NHA-170029) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2018-Santos-Parker-Curcumin-Flanker.txt)

## Turmeric curcuminoid formulation and repeated cognitive testing

Published online in 2016. Rainey-Smith and colleagues enrolled 160 community-dwelling older adults and analyzed 96 who satisfied eligibility and compliance requirements. The BCM-95 product combined curcuminoids and volatile turmeric oils. The attention-containing cognitive battery showed no clear differential change. The MoCA intermediate visit drove the stated three-visit interaction, while the baseline to 12-month test gave p=.25. Attrition and baseline differences matter. The study did not test ordinary grated turmeric or Parkinson attention. [Original report](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/curcumin-and-cognition-a-randomised-placebocontrolled-doubleblind-study-of-communitydwelling-older-adults/83419D0C8B37ACD8BBC7C27B91FBAF58) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2016-Rainey-Smith-Curcumin-Cognition.txt)

## Prospective Longvida registration for sleep and attention

Registered April 13, 2016. The protocol assigned Longvida capsules or matched placebo for 12 weeks in healthy older adults and explicitly excluded a Parkinson history. Divided attention was among the primary cognitive outcomes. PSQI at four and 12 weeks was secondary, with no separate Epworth or standalone sleepiness outcome specified. The registry fields show a target of 80 and final accrual of 101. The 2020 publication describes 80 randomized. These numbers may reflect different participant-flow boundaries. The registration identifies a distinct longer trial from the earlier Cox acute and four-week experiment. [Original source](https://anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=370499) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2016-ACTRN12616000484448-Longvida-Sleep-Attention.txt)
