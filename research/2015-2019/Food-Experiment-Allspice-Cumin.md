# Allspice and cumin evidence

## Cumin intake in a UK Parkinson dietary survey

The multicentre survey included 139 people with Parkinson's. Table 4 reports cumin intake for 25/34 Asian participants, 11/66 White participants and 8/39 Black African and Caribbean participants. The authors' exploratory comparison covered dietary items and NMSS total, nine nonmotor domains and HADS total. No exploratory association survived correction. The article supplies no cumin-specific effect size, preparation, or ingredient-linked individual symptom item. NMSS gastrointestinal, cardiovascular, sleep/fatigue, mood/apathy, attention/memory and urinary domains combine symptoms. The dietary survey does not test a therapeutic direction. [Primary local article, Section 3.5 and Table 4](Source-Text/PMC6312592.txt#L139)

## Cumin fruit aqueous extract in an MPTP mouse experiment

Kim, Kopalli and Koppula administered a concentrated water extract of authenticated *Cuminum cyminum* fruit by gavage, beginning before MPTP exposure and continuing for 21 days. Figure 1 shows no rotarod contrast on day 5 and longer retention on day 20 compared with MPTP alone. Approximate Figure 1 day-20 values are 27 seconds for MPTP, 37–47 seconds across extract doses, and 56 seconds for vehicle. Figure 2 includes passive avoidance retention, and Figure 3 shows open-field measures. The Methods describe 15 animals per group, while behavioral figure captions give n = 5. The paper does not explain this discrepancy in the relevant passages. Retention on a rotating rod measures model motor coordination and balance. The study did not give toasted cumin, assess clinical muscle tone, or record dyskinesia or medication off time. [Local licensed publisher text](Source-Text/2016-Kim-Cumin-MPTP.txt) · [Primary PDF and Figures 1–3](https://www.ajol.info/index.php/tjpr/article/download/135164/124657)

## Cuminaldehyde and alpha-synuclein assays

Morshedi and colleagues screened *Cuminum cyminum* essential oil fractions and identified cuminaldehyde as an active compound in protein fibrillation assays. It inhibited assembly in the presence of fibril seeds under the tested conditions and showed no disaggregation of existing fibrils. The researchers also used a PC12 viability assay. The work gives biochemical outcomes without a symptom result from consuming cumin. [Primary abstract, PMID 26351865](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26351865/)

## Cumin extract and rat anxiety-like behavior

The 2016 study tested an ethanolic seed extract and diazepam in Wistar rats using elevated plus maze and other behavioral tasks. The report describes greater open-arm exploration at some doses. Its animal tasks and extract are indirect for the toasted cumin and anxiety hypothesis. [Primary report and Table 1](https://impactfactor.org/PDF/IJTPR/8/IJTPR%2CVol8%2CIssue4%2CArticle4.pdf)

## Resting blood pressure after cumin essential oil in metabolic syndrome

In a 56-person randomized triple-blind study, Morovati and colleagues compared *Cuminum cyminum* essential oil softgels with placebo for eight weeks. The adjusted endpoint difference for diastolic blood pressure was −3.98 mm Hg (95% CI −7.60 to −0.35), while within-group changes and other measured variables, including systolic pressure, were inconclusive. This is resting blood pressure in metabolic syndrome. Standing measurements, lightheadedness, Parkinson's disease and culinary ground cumin were outside the comparison. [Primary abstract](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.6500)

## Registration for an allspice-containing pumpkin spice meal

NCT03926442 was first posted April 24, 2019 and lists a crossover comparison with pumpkin spice mix as one meal arm. The record lists endothelial, glucose, insulin and inflammatory outcomes and no posted results. Later trial publications identify allspice as a component without giving its proportion.
