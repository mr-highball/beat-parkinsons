# Berry trial follow-up, 2015–2019

## Fresh and processed blueberry crossover registration

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT04175106 on November 22, 2019. The randomized four-way crossover assigned healthy adults to a single serving of each of two whole blueberry cultivars, a blueberry protein bar, and a matched beverage. The two primary outcomes measure urinary and serum polyphenol metabolites. The study has no registered constipation severity endpoint. The 2025 paper reports metabolite results, and the 2026 registry results include adverse-event counts. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04175106) · [Dated paper and results note](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#whole-blueberries-and-adverse-events-in-a-pharmacokinetics-crossover)

## Blueberry and grape seed extract during Helicobacter pylori treatment

Chua et al. first published a nine-week randomized add-on trial on February 16, 2016. The abstract reports increased constipation, diarrhea and epigastric pain in some recipients of a blueberry and grape seed extract mixture given alongside conventional triple therapy. The accessible abstract provides no arm-specific constipation counts or estimates. The trial used a mixture and treated *H. pylori*. The probable 2010 registry counterpart has no public result table. The original adverse-event table was unavailable through the publisher and institution routes checked. [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.3109/09637486.2016.1144716) · [Primary report, PMID 26883189](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26883189/) · [Investigator institution record](https://hub.tmu.edu.tw/en/publications/the-efficacy-of-blueberry-and-grape-seed-extract-combination-on-t/) · [2010 registration note](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#berry-extract-registration-during-helicobacter-pylori-treatment)

## Blackcurrant anthocyanin capsules in Parkinson disease

Alamri and colleagues reported an open-label study of blackcurrant antioxidant supplementation on August 25, 2016. Ten people with Parkinson disease took the supplement for 28 days between two research lumbar punctures. Before the second procedure, mean HADS anxiety was 2.5 versus 5.4 before the first (p = .025), and depression was 2.9 versus 4.1 (p = .074). The authors reported similar UPDRS, MMSE and MoCA scores at the two visits and interpreted the anxiety finding in the context of familiarity with the procedure. No placebo group or separate facial-expression, speech or constipation outcomes were reported. [2016 primary letter, PMID 27560949](https://doi.org/10.1159/000449056)

The September 2017 AWCBR poster 14.2 reported assessments in 11 people with Parkinson disease and a lower total HADS score after 28 days (p = .004), alongside higher cerebrospinal-fluid cyclic glycine-proline. Fan and colleagues' June 2, 2018 report specifies 300 mg of blackcurrant concentrate with 35% anthocyanins twice daily. Among six paired samples retained after excluding an outlier, cerebrospinal-fluid cyclic glycine-proline rose from 7.27 to 12.12 ng/mL (p < .01). Plasma levels and cerebrospinal-fluid IGF-1 had no significant change, and the biomarker change did not clearly correlate with HADS (r = .39, p = .18). The paper explicitly links its sampling to the 2016 study. [2017 meeting proceedings, printed p. 47](https://www.awcbr.org/_files/ugd/35f226_221169d9b1584e9eb0355deb94df1705.pdf#page=48) [2018 primary report, PMID 29865234](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6024688/) [Local CC BY text](Source-Text/PMC6024688.txt)

Alamri's June 27, 2018 report gives improved PDQ-39 emotional well-being in ten completers (paired t = −3.97, p = .008). The ethics reference, location, exposure and duration link the reports to the same cohort. There was no randomized group comparison. The reports concern a concentrate of blackcurrant, a different berry and preparation from the frozen blueberry claims. [2018 clinical report, PMID 29958758](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29958758/)
