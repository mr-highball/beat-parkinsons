# Coffee and tea in a UK Parkinson cohort

## Coffee and tea compared on nonmotor domains

In a multicentre UK study published December 18, 2018, Sauerbier et al. compared self-reported consumers of coffee, green tea, and black tea with participants who consumed the respective drink once a month or less. Table 4 lists 79, 31, and 61 consumers, respectively, out of 139 people with Parkinson’s. Section 3.5 reports comparisons of each dietary item on the total Non-Motor Symptoms Scale, all nine domains including the combined sleep and fatigue domain, and total Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale. The authors report no significant differences after correcting for multiple comparisons. The paper omits beverage-specific symptom estimates, separate fatigue-item results, serving quantities, and brewing details. This is a Parkinson-specific exploratory comparison relevant to `0121` and `0948`. Its results leave the direction and size of any fatigue-specific association unresolved. [Primary report, Table 4 and Sections 2.4–2.5 and 3.5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6312592/) · [Local CC BY source text](Source-Text/PMC6312592.txt)

The authors report data availability by request. Sauerbier’s related doctoral thesis was awarded June 1, 2019. The public King’s College London record describes ethnicity-related sleep and fatigue differences and a dietary analysis, with no public full-text link or beverage-by-symptom results. The study article is the earliest verified public result for the dietary comparison. [University thesis record](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/studentTheses/range-and-nature-of-non-motor-symptoms-in-people-with-parkinsons-/)

## Cayenne frequency and nonmotor domains

Table 4 of the same study lists cayenne separately from fresh chili. Twenty-three of 139 participants reported cayenne use above the study threshold of once monthly. Section 3.5 describes exploratory comparisons for the Table 4 items on the nine NMSS domains and total HADS. No clear differences remained after multiple-comparison correction. Cayenne preparation, an anxiety subscore, a stand-alone apathy score and cayenne-specific effect estimates are unavailable in the report. This is related evidence for [0096](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0096.txt) and [0669](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0669.txt). [Local primary article, section 3.5 and Table 4](Source-Text/PMC6312592.txt#L137)

## Italian caffeine cohort conference results

Moccia et al.’s August 2016 article and October 2016 Society of Italian Neurology congress abstract follow the same 79 newly diagnosed, untreated Parkinson’s participants at baseline, two years, and four years. The congress abstract reports an association between higher total caffeine and a lower NMSQuest total score (p = .008), with intake increments expressed as 50 mg caffeine, the equivalent of a cup of espresso. It gives no espresso-specific coefficient or fatigue item result. The conference levodopa initiation hazard ratio of 0.689 (95% CI 0.474–0.994) differs from the article’s 0.630 (0.382–0.996). The estimates are from two reports of the same cohort. The University of Verona record lists no associated file, and the University of Naples accepted manuscript is restricted. [October 22–25 congress booklet, pp. 35–36, archived transcript](https://slideum.com/doc/9727753/file-unico-viii---societ%C3%A0-italiana-di-neurologia) · [August 4 article](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27622969/) · [University of Verona record](https://iris.univr.it/handle/11562/951852) · [University of Naples record](https://www.iris.unina.it/handle/11588/758381)

## Caffeinated coffee and tea in the California progression cohort

Paul et al. published this California Parkinson’s Environment and Gene cohort on January 17, 2019. All 360 Parkinson’s participants were followed for mortality, and 244 had an active follow-up assessment. Interviewers collected histories of caffeinated coffee and caffeinated tea before diagnosis. In Table 3, ever coffee use was associated with a lower hazard of a four-point MMSE decline (HR 0.23, 95% CI 0.11–0.48). Progression to Hoehn and Yahr stage 3 was inconclusive (HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.28–1.01). The corresponding estimates for caffeinated tea were 0.56 (0.30–1.03) and 0.93 (0.51–1.71). Table 2 gives lower mortality hazards in both exposure groups, coffee HR 0.47 (0.32–0.69) and tea HR 0.67 (0.48–0.93). These comparisons are observational, concern prediagnosis histories, and give no fatigue rating or brewing-method analysis. [Primary report, Tables 2–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6544143/) · [Publication date](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1002/mds.27577)

## Coffee history and fatigue measurement in the Chinese Parkinson’s Disease Registry

The Xiangya Hospital registry NCT03887663 first posted on March 25, 2019. Its public protocol records coffee and tea drinking among baseline lifestyle habits and specifies the Parkinson Fatigue Scale. The public entry gives no coffee preparation or joint analysis. As of November 12, 2026, the registry showed no posted results, with December 13, 2019 as its latest public update. The 2026 paper and poster link the measurements to separate mortality models. ## First-year fatigue measures in ParkWest

Ongre et al.’s September 26, 2016 report describes Fatigue Severity Scale assessments at enrollment and one year in 181 newly diagnosed, untreated people with Parkinson’s and 162 controls. Mean fatigue scores in Parkinson’s changed from 4.4 (SD 1.6) to 4.0 (SD 1.6). The accessible report relates fatigue change to clinical features and dopaminergic treatment. It gives no result by coffee history, which was recorded at baseline and reported in an earlier publication. The Norwegian National Research Archive supplies external links to PubMed and Wiley. [Primary 2016 report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27670392/) · [Norwegian National Research Archive](https://nva.sikt.no/registration/0198cc7df00a-33e78fd0-183f-4186-a4c9-2b67de75cc9c) · [2010 coffee exposure report](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Coffee-Followup.md#coffee-history-and-fatigue-measures-in-parkwest) · [Nine-year follow-up](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Beverage-Fatigue-Followup.md#longitudinal-fatigue-in-parkwest)

## Paper-filtered coffee and acute mental fatigue in healthy adults

Haskell-Ramsay and colleagues reported a randomized three-period crossover on September 30, 2018. The analysis included 59 healthy habitual caffeine consumers, 30 older and 29 younger. At visits separated by at least seven days, participants drank 220 mL of paper-filtered black coffee (100 mg caffeine), paper-filtered black decaf (about 5 mg caffeine), or coffee-flavored hot water. Participants avoided caffeine from noon the previous day. At the testing session beginning 30 minutes after the drink, regular coffee lowered the visual analogue mental-fatigue rating versus flavored water (adjusted pairwise p = .01). The separate tiredness item was lower versus flavored water (p < .0001) and decaf (p = .003). The study population and short-term ratings limit application to Parkinson’s fatigue. [Primary report, §§2.3, 2.10–2.11, 3.1.6 and 3.1.9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6213082/) · [Local CC BY text](Source-Text/PMC6213082.txt)

## Nordic walking and separate apathy and fatigue scales

Cugusi and colleagues’ October 15, 2015 first online report randomized 20 people with Parkinson’s to Nordic walking or usual care. Apathy and fatigue were separately measured in the small 12-week exercise trial. Table 4 gives lower final apathy and fatigue means in the walking arm and reports across-group p < .0005 and p < .05, respectively. Its methods describe independent group tests without clearly identifying these as tests of change. A later Tai Chi paper cites these results from the separate Nordic walking cohort.
