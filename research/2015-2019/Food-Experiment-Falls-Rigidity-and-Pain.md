# Cod, corn oil and a registered coconut oil balance comparison

## Cod in a pooled seafood questionnaire

Kothari et al. assessed fatigue in 118 mixed-specialty clinic patients using the Fatigue Severity Scale. Figure 1 pools cod with other seafood. The fatigue contrast applies to tuna steak, swordfish, shark or marlin eaten at least weekly. Its adjusted odds ratio was 7.89 (95% CI 1.63–38.15). The result has no cod-specific estimate, cooking comparison or Parkinson subgroup. [Original Figure 1 and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4721310/) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC4721310.txt)

## Corn oil comparator in an osteoarthritis pain trial

Stebbings et al. randomized 80 patients to 600 mg/day of corn oil or green-lipped mussel extract in four capsules for 12 weeks. Table 3 gives pain VAS means of 4.5 ± 2.1 to 4.6 ± 2.3 for corn oil and 5.3 ± 2.1 to 5.0 ± 2.8 for mussel extract. The model p value was .110. WOMAC pain fell numerically in both groups, with p = .955 for the modeled comparison. Neither endpoint isolates an effect of corn oil because the comparison uses another intervention without an oil-free arm. Grade is unspecified. [Original Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5568208/) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC5568208.txt)

## Trial registration for coconut oil and epigallocatechin gallate

NCT03740295 was first posted on November 14, 2018. The multiple sclerosis registration specifies 60 mL/day coconut oil and 600 mg/day EGCG in the active intervention and lists Berg balance among its outcomes. The native official registry replay found no posted results. The balance paper from the same trial states 800 mg/day EGCG. Actual dosing cannot be reconciled from the available reports. ## Curcumin in a laryngeal amyloidosis case

Golombick et al. describe oral curcumin in a laryngeal amyloidosis case. The indexed fall term describes a spectrum of diseases. The clinical report has no physical falls measure or Parkinson intervention and was excluded for claim 0251.
