# Ginger extract and anxiety in a controlled migraine trial

## Brazilian migraine trial and additional anxiety outcomes

Martins and colleagues published their trial online August 9, 2019 before the January 2020 issue. They randomized 107 people with episodic migraine to ginger extract or placebo. At day 90, Table 2 reports median Beck Anxiety Inventory scores of 5 with ginger and 3 with placebo (p = .46). Reports of improvement within arms do not resolve a between-arm anxiety difference. One ginger participant was listed as having constipation, with none listed among placebo participants. This was an adverse-event report without a constipation severity estimate. The extract was encapsulated and the participants had migraine. [Local outcome extract](Source-Text/2019-Martins-Ginger-Migraine-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Original article, Table 2 and adverse events](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0333102419869319)

## Brazilian migraine registration and outcome disclosure

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT02570633 on October 7, 2015. The completed registration describes a 12-week ginger extract capsule comparison with cellulose. It lists migraine, biomarkers and energy expenditure outcomes. It does not list an anxiety score separately. The article reports Beck Anxiety Inventory outcomes and cites this registration. The public registry has no posted results table. [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT02570633.txt) · [Official trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02570633)

## Published chemotherapy trial protocol

The SPICE investigators first published a protocol July 23, 2018. It prespecified secondary anxiety and depression outcomes for the planned randomized ginger-powder comparison in people receiving chemotherapy. The registry identifier appears in the original protocol and later article. The direct ANZCTR record was unavailable, so its exact first public date and its full outcome fields remain unverified. The protocol and results belong to one study. ## Oral ethanolic ginger extract and mouse open-arm behavior

Sharma and colleagues published in October 2016. The study administered a single oral dose of ethanolic rhizome extract, 50 or 200 mg/kg, or vehicle to groups of eight mice and tested the elevated plus maze after 60 minutes. The authors report increased time in open arms with the extract (p < .01), with a stronger response at 200 mg/kg. The separately measured 60-minute locomotor activity did not differ significantly according to the text, and its numerical results were not shown. This is an extract and behavioral assay in mice outside a Parkinson model. [Original source](https://nopr.niscpr.res.in/bitstream/123456789/35694/1/IJEB%2054%2810%29%20664-669.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2016-Sharma-Ginger-Open-Arm.txt)

## Ginger extract and open-arm time after dark-box exposure

Fadaki, Modaresi and Sajjadian reported six groups of ten female mice in the July–September 2017 issue. Table 1 gives open-arm time means of 21.43 in the anxiety group and 58.65, 65.53 and 67.19 after 50, 100 and 200 mg/kg extract. The table does not label those units. Reported locomotor activity differed across groups. The Results describe movement at the highest dose inconsistently relative to diazepam. The Methods omit extract process, route and schedule details. This experiment gives anxiety-like behavior data in mice without a Parkinson model. [Original source](https://www.ijper.org/article/51/3s1/s159.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2017-Fadaki-Ginger-Dark-Box-Open-Arms.txt)

## Contemporaneous conference report on isolated ginger compounds

Alexandra Simon's 2019 presentation under Eszter Riethmüller reports isolated compounds from ginger-family plants and an artificial membrane permeability assay. It includes no behavioral anxiety or Parkinson comparison. The same investigator group and overlapping assay support a link to the later ginger-compound article first published in August 2019. The conference report covers a broader plant scope and does not establish that all results were reused in the journal paper. The institutional repository identifies the exact journal version and restricts its body.
