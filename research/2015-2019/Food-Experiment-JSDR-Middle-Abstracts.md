# Swallowing and food in the 2018 and 2019 Japanese proceedings

## 2018 Mont Blanc cake after swallowing rehabilitation

Okano and colleagues described a woman in her seventies with Parkinson’s, a gastrostomy and severe swallowing impairment after pneumonia. In hospital she progressed to supervised jelly training in a fully side-lying position. Four months after transfer to a care facility, videofluoroscopy supported rice porridge and thickened minced dishes in a 30-degree reclining position. She subsequently ate a Mont Blanc cake, her stated rehabilitation goal. The cake recipe, serving size, and cake-specific videofluoroscopy results are unavailable. The hospital’s copy describes the same patient and course. [2018 primary abstract O13-1, p. S101](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no24_abstracts.pdf#page=102) · [Hospital copy](https://minasehp.jp/minase-hospital/pdf/ronbun/2018-9.pdf)

## 2018 silent aspiration across changing meal textures

Nishimura and colleagues described a man in his late seventies with Parkinson’s and deconditioning after aspiration pneumonia. Videofluoroscopy showed marked impairment of upper esophageal opening on day 25. It showed no aspiration with fluid and a blended diet on day 60, then a small amount of silent aspiration with full rice porridge and thickened minced dishes on day 74. He could cough out the aspirated material when prompted. The clinical course, posture, swallowing instructions, and test meals changed over time. He did not resume three daily oral meals by discharge. The authors supplied no separate effect for rice or another food ingredient. A 12-person tube-feeding cohort presented at the same meeting shares the hospital and authors. The proceedings provide insufficient identifiers to rule out overlap with this case. [2018 primary abstract O38-3, p. S240](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no24_abstracts.pdf#page=241) · [Cohort abstract O2-3, p. S76](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no24_abstracts.pdf#page=77)

## 2018 red-chili gargle and saliva swallowing

Sakaguchi tested a gargle made by filtering a mixture of commercial ground red chili (*ichimi togarashi*) and warm water in ten healthy adults. The mean count of saliva swallows was 13.6 before the chili gargle, 13.5 immediately after, and 14.9 twenty minutes after. The twenty-minute comparison with baseline reached the stated significance threshold. In the warm-water control condition the corresponding counts were 13.5, 12.1, and 12.9. The abstract reports comparisons against each condition’s own baseline and gives no between-condition effect estimate. Gargling filtered chili and counting saliva swallows offer indirect context for ground cayenne and dysphagia (`0724`). The chili cultivar and clinical effects in Parkinson’s were untested. [2018 primary abstract P20-5, p. S315](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no24_abstracts.pdf#page=316)

## 2019 rice crackers and ice cream during therapy

Nagoshi and colleagues described a man in his seventies with stage 5 Parkinson’s and a gastrostomy after aspiration pneumonia. An interdisciplinary home team performed eight endoscopic swallowing evaluations over the course of care. Direct practice progressed to *okaki* rice crackers and ice cream. The abstract supplies no serving size, ingredient list, repeat comparison by food, or item-specific endoscopic or aspiration result. The report describes different authors and patient history from the 2017 Hori *senbei* case. [2019 primary abstract P15-8, p. S220](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no25_abstracts.pdf?20211220=#page=221) · [2017 Hori case](Food-Experiment-Japanese-Swallowing-Followup.md#rice-cracker-puree-and-porridge-in-the-same-parkinson-case)

## 2019 assisted gyoza, ramen, and almond jelly

Fujiwara and colleagues reported a woman in her seventies with advanced Parkinson’s and aspiration documented on videofluoroscopy during hospitalization for pneumonia. After rehabilitation and discharge, she attended a supported restaurant visit. The group arranged reclined positioning, assistance and supervision. A thickened Chinese-style sauce was prepared for her gyoza. The abstract also describes ramen and *annin tofu*, an almond-style jelly whose ingredients were unspecified. She ate with her husband’s assistance. The report gives no food-specific aspiration measurement or evidence of an isolated effect of any ingredient. [2019 primary abstract P43-7, p. S381](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no25_abstracts.pdf?20211220=#page=382)

## 2019 water challenge and recovery of oral intake

Chou and colleagues described an 82-year-old woman with Parkinson’s and nasogastric feeding after an acute hospitalization in Taipei. She could eat purée at baseline but showed a choking reaction with more than 3 mL of water. After swallowing practice, gradual diet advancement, and seated feeding, she could drink more than 15 mL of water without observed choking and take medication orally. The tube was subsequently removed. An emergency visit for constipation prompted advice to add vegetable juice or probiotics. The report identifies neither the juice ingredient nor a bowel response to it. [2019 primary abstract P31-2, p. S336](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no25_abstracts.pdf?20211220=#page=337) · [Official poster program](https://www.kokuhoken.jp/jsdr25/file/jsdr25_program_general_poster_day02.pdf)

Yamaguchi and colleagues reported a separate Parkinson’s case in which a bedside water drinking test stayed normal while videofluoroscopy showed silent aspiration at nine months and greater delay in oral transfer. The test bolus used during fluoroscopy was unnamed. The finding distinguishes bedside swallowing performance from instrumental aspiration. [2019 primary abstract P12-2, p. S205](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no25_abstracts.pdf?20211220=#page=206) · [Official poster program](https://www.kokuhoken.jp/jsdr25/file/jsdr25_program_general_poster_day01.pdf)
