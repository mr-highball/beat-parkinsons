# Facial signs in corticobasal degeneration and a DHA dyskinesia pilot

## Chicken and milk in a corticobasal case history

Tetzloff and colleagues followed a person with progressive apraxia of speech and autopsy-confirmed corticobasal degeneration. Clinically described hypomimia was recorded by 2013. In 2015, the family reported a fixed diet that included milk and occasional chicken, with an intermittent unusual facial appearance. These are separate clinical and diet observations in the same progressive case. Processing or pasteurization of the milk, cooking method of the chicken, earlier dietary history, portion and meal timing were unreported. There is no food-specific facial response, standardized change after eating, or idiopathic Parkinson cohort. The same case has already been indexed locally for blueberries. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6095655/)

## DHA pilot conference results and registry denominators

Chung and Lobb, 2017 International Congress abstract 1347. Thirty-three participants were randomized before starting levodopa. The poster reported dyskinesia by 12 months in 3/15 on DHA and 7/15 on placebo, with no difference in peak severity stated. The later clinical registry reports 3/9 versus 6/15 for its forceplate-defined outcome among available measurements. The records share investigators, 33 randomized, the specific intervention and visiting schedule. The denominators differ and the reports should not be pooled as independent trials or assumed to report the same endpoint set. Neither tested walnut oil or alpha-linolenic acid as the active ingredient. [Original source](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/preventing-levodopa-induced-dyskinesia-with-docosohexanoid-acid-in-parkinson-disease/)
