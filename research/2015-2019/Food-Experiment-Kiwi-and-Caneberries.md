# Kiwifruit, blackberry and raspberry studies

## Kiwifruit pulp at breakfast and attention tasks

Haley Edwards’ 2015 Massey University thesis compared four breakfasts in 20 healthy adults. The kiwifruit was peeled, frozen, processed into pulp and mixed into cereal at 200 g per breakfast. Matched breakfasts used an equivalent mix of sugars in water, with or without guar gum. The choice reaction-time appendix gives p = .66 for breakfast and p = .12 for breakfast by testing time. The overall Stroop interaction had p = .35 and incongruent Stroop showed no clear breakfast or interaction effect. Mean incongruent times for cereal plus kiwi versus cereal alone were 575.65 versus 565.30 ms at 90 minutes and 568.55 versus 568.80 ms at 180 minutes, with differing baseline means. No Parkinson participants were enrolled. [University record](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/items/1149ee8a-a5bf-4f6f-8d27-c0018e6c290a) · [Full dissertation, pp. 49–53, 57–66 and Appendix G](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/server/api/core/bitstreams/4cde9102-af0b-4ced-a3c2-ef72188eea10/content)

## Raspberry diets and contrasting motor and cognitive outcomes in aged rats

Galli and colleagues (2016) fed aged rats a diet with 2% extract prepared from fresh red raspberries. The paper reports improvement on selected motor outcomes and no clear Morris water maze benefit. A separate study from the same lab, published online in 2017, used 1% and 2% raspberry diets and separated rats by baseline motor ability. The motor composite rose relative to control in poor performers receiving either amount and fell in good performers receiving 2% (p < .05 in both comparisons). Selected plank and grip outcomes improved. These aging-model outcomes do not measure human anxiety, orthostatic symptoms or executive function. [2016 primary paper](https://doi.org/10.3233/JBR-160119) · [2017 primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29168860/)

## Frozen raspberry drink and acute blood pressure

NCT02734901 first posted April 12, 2016. In Istas and colleagues’ May 24, 2018 report, ten healthy men drank beverages prepared with 200 or 400 g of frozen red raspberries or a matched control. Flow-mediated dilation improved at two and 24 hours. Table 3 shows no clear effect on peripheral or central blood pressure. For example, the 200 g minus control change in central systolic pressure at two hours was 0.2 mmHg (95% CI −4.7 to 5.1). The trial did not measure postural blood pressure changes or dizziness. Istas’s 2019 dissertation includes the same experiment and is a companion source. ## Frozen whole blackberries in a metabolic crossover

Solverson and colleagues (2018) compared 600 g/day of whole frozen blackberries, removed from the freezer within 12 hours of consumption, with matched gelatin on a controlled high-fat diet. The methods include a prescribed walking bout inside a calorimeter. Energy substrate use during that walk differed but participants did not perform a gait speed, executive function, voice, sleep or pain test. The protocol and report are the same study. [Local primary text](Source-Text/PMC6115824.txt) · [Registration](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Kiwi-and-Caneberries.md#blackberry-controlled-feeding-registration)

## Raspberry snacks in type 2 diabetes

Schell and colleagues (2019) compared a raspberry meal and control and a separate four-week raspberry snack period in adults with type 2 diabetes. The primary abstract reports a tendency toward lower resting systolic pressure during raspberry supplementation. The publication does not report Parkinson symptoms, standing blood pressure or orthostatic lightheadedness. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30763939/) · [Institutional record](https://oasis.library.unlv.edu/kns_fac_articles/242/)

## Thawed berry mixture and executive tasks

Whyte and colleagues first published November 6, 2019. Forty healthy adults received a smoothie containing 75 g each of blackberry, raspberry, blueberry and strawberry, frozen and then thawed before blending, or a carbohydrate- and vitamin C-matched drink. Modified Attention Network responses were faster after the mixture at two and four hours, and accuracy at six hours favored the mixture. On task switching, accuracy and response time had no clear intervention main effect. The switching-response-time treatment-by-session interaction had p = .001, with faster six-hour responses within the mixture arm than at two and four hours. Negative affect showed no clear effect. The study supplies no estimates for a single berry or a clinical anxiety measure. [Local primary text](Source-Text/PMC6893475.txt) · [Primary report, Sections 2.2 and 3.1–3.6](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/11/11/2685)

## Red raspberry meal and cognition registration

NCT03879213 first posted March 18, 2019. The completed controlled crossover lists Profile of Mood States, Digit Symbol Coding, verbal learning and CANTAB paired associates, rapid visual information processing and spatial working memory, plus secondary psychomotor and endothelial function. The registry lists 30 participants and no results. The 2026 report describes 36 completers across two sites and selected outcome contrasts. [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT03879213.txt) · [2026 report](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Kiwi-and-Caneberries.md#red-raspberry-powder-and-post-meal-spatial-working-memory)

## Kiwifruit extract and spatial learning after lead exposure

Xue and colleagues (March 12, 2017) made a lyophilized extract from peeled and squeezed kiwifruit using ethanol and administered it by gavage in a rat lead exposure model. The authors report a benefit in the Morris water maze. The endpoint measures spatial learning and memory in a toxic exposure model. [Local primary text](Source-Text/PMC5366204.txt) · [Primary article](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28386309/)

## Black mulberry juice and dyskinesia in mice

The 2018 study that uses “blackberry” in its title identifies the fruit as Morus nigra. The authors tested juice from black mulberry in a levodopa-induced dyskinesia mouse model. Morus and Rubus are different genera. The motor result has no direct bearing on raw Rubus blackberry or on clinical walking speed. [Primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6037159/)

## Red raspberry leaf extract and trial adverse events

Velliquette and colleagues tested multiple extract capsules against placebo for seven days in overweight or obese adults. The 90 participants were randomized across five groups. The main outcome was postprandial triglycerides. The red raspberry leaf arm listed mild appetite loss, lower-leg aching, nausea and heartburn in the supplement. It supplies no posture-specific pressure or dizziness measurement. The authors report no product-related change in vital signs and do not present those data. Raspberry leaf extract is a distinct exposure from frozen berries. [Original source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12986-015-0025-2) · [Original adverse-event supplement](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art:10.1186%2Fs12986-015-0025-2/MediaObjects/12986_2015_25_MOESM2_ESM.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2015-Velliquette-Raspberry-Leaf-Adverse-Events-Extract.txt)

## Blackberry juice and locomotor tests in Wistar rats

Fernández-Demeneghi and colleagues’ 2019 abstract describes 21 days of blackberry juice in Wistar rats with maze, locomotor and forced-swim assessments. The intermediate exposure gave an anxiolytic-like result and an inconclusive locomotor comparison. The low and high exposures gave inconclusive maze contrasts. The preparation was juice, and human walking symptoms and full arm-level tables remain outside the verified record. [Original source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30892114/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2019-Fernandez-Demeneghi-Blackberry-Juice-Rat-Behavior-Abstract-Extract.txt)

## Posted black raspberry crossover adverse events

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted results March 15, 2016 for NCT01568827. The two randomized sequences each contain five participants. The record lists zero serious and zero other adverse events in each sequence over one month. Each sequence includes berry slurry and water, so the count does not isolate an exposure. A valid estimate for orthostatic lightheadedness was not reported. [Source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01568827?tab=results) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2016-NCT01568827-Black-Raspberry-Adverse-Events-Extract.txt)

## Kanon conference design for kiwi, sleep and cognition

Kanon and colleagues’ 2019 conference abstract describes a proposed crossover with measurements after the first and fourteenth nights, plus a washout. It lists polysomnography and mood and cognitive testing. The plan gives no results. The separate acute kiwi and later planned freeze-dried COMPASS registrations identify different measurements or time frames. The 2019 document supplies no shared registration to establish study identity. [Original source](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jsr.52_12913) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2019-Kanon-P053-Kiwi-Sleep-Cognition-Design-Extract.txt)

## Boston registration for a red raspberry breakfast

NCT03743792 first posted November 16, 2018 and lists six participants in a Boston randomized crossover. The active breakfast included 25 g of freeze-dried red raspberry powder. The official record names Grooved Pegboard psychomotor function and a secondary endothelial measure. It has no results and no listed anxiety outcome. The 2019 Chicago registry lists mood testing separately. Shared meal composition and related investigators do not establish how the individual Boston participants were analyzed in a publication.
