# Bean Sleep Followup

## Kidney bean yogurt and sleep in a 2018 conference report

The August 2018 meeting abstract reports a before-and-after study of fermented kidney bean yogurt in 17 older adults. Its reported sleep-quality scores match the later journal report, along with investigators, study population and intervention period. The abstract provides no control group or separate kidney bean effect. It represents the same likely cohort as the 2019 article. ## Kidney bean yogurt formulation and measured sleep quality

First published November 26, 2019. Seventeen older adults living in a care institution received kidney bean yogurt for two weeks. Mean Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index was 8.06 (SD 3.09) initially and 4.88 (SD 1.83) afterward (paired p=.001). The preparation combined kidney bean porridge with skim milk, sugar and cultures. The study provides no untreated comparison, pinto cultivar or Parkinson group. Its outcome measures sleep quality rather than an ingredient-specific clinical insomnia effect. ## Registered pinto crossover outcomes and absence of sleep measure

The registry gives January 19, 2015 as its first public posting and May 13, 2016 as its last posted update, both labeled estimated. The completed study lists eight participants and no posted results. The planned primary outcome covers phytochemical absorption, metabolism and excretion. Secondary measures include pulse-wave arterial stiffness and satiety. Sleep and insomnia are absent from the archived array of registered outcomes. The institutional thesis describes actual boiled pinto consumption during the crossover. That preparation does not link to an insomnia outcome or a Parkinson cohort. [Original registry export](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT02342340) · [Institutional thesis](https://mspace.lib.umanitoba.ca/bitstream/handle/1993/34298/clark_jaime.pdf). ## Boiled black common beans in the Clark dissertation

The University of Manitoba dates Jaime Clark's dissertation to 2019. It describes the registered crossover in which eight healthy adults ate black, navy, pinto and red kidney common beans and white rice on separate visits. The bean portions were soaked and boiled in broth, then frozen and reheated. This documents consumption of boiled black common beans. The post-meal protocol measures vascular and metabolic responses and does not include daytime sleepiness. The later journal article reports the same registered study.
