# Canola and safflower oil evidence

## Canola oil in minced food and sensory ease of swallowing

Sano and colleagues’ April 2015 study used Nisshin Canola Oil as the liquid oil in minced test food. Seven healthy volunteers rated the food smoother with liquid oil than without added fat, with mean scores of 3.63 and 2.63 respectively. There was no clear liquid-oil versus no-fat difference for perceived softness, residue or stickiness. Gelatinous fat had a different composition and stronger results on several ratings. No swallowing physiology, aspiration, Parkinson dysphagia or cold-press grade was established. [Primary paper, Table 1 and Figure 3](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jos/64/6/64_ess14279/_pdf)

## Canola feeding study results in peripheral arterial disease

Enns’ 2015 University of Manitoba dissertation describes eight weeks of conventional canola oil in prepared foods versus a Western oil blend in 50 peripheral arterial disease participants. The intervention did not affect systolic blood pressure. The apparent end-period difference in diastolic pressure was explained by baseline differences in the change analysis. Chapter 7 describes planned treadmill and neuropsychological tests. Chapter 9 describes the collected mobility and cognitive datasets as available for further analyses. The thesis results chapter gives no quantitative walking or attention comparison. [Primary dissertation, Chapters 7–9 and Table 8.2](https://mspace.lib.umanitoba.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/9b17969b-6a12-4683-a744-66ea412be4ae/content) · [2010 trial registration note](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Canola-Safflower.md#registered-canola-feeding-study-in-peripheral-arterial-disease)

## Canola oil and working memory in an Alzheimer mouse model

Lauretti and Praticò’s 2017 experiment found fewer spontaneous alternations in the Y-maze in canola-oil-fed triple-transgenic mice. Total arm entries did not show a clear between-group difference. The endpoint was working memory, and the model and exposure grade differ from the frozen Parkinson attention claim. [Primary full text](Source-Text/PMC5719422.txt)

## Registered canola diet in shift workers

NCT03977558 first posted on June 6, 2019. It compares a canola-containing isocaloric diet with the usual diet in centrally obese shift workers. The listed outcomes concern body composition, cardiometabolic biomarkers and melatonin. Shift-work sleep disturbance appears among the conditions. No daytime sleepiness or dream enactment outcome is specified. Status is unknown with no posted results. [Official API record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT03977558) · [Selected registry fields](Source-Text/Trials/NCT03977558.txt)

## Registered safflower comparator in a Glut1 movement disorder trial

NCT02960217 first posted November 9, 2016. The trial compared triheptanoin with safflower oil in a crossover for paroxysmal movement disorders associated with Glut1 deficiency. The registration calls the oil a placebo comparator. Results were first posted April 24, 2020 and published in a journal article in 2024. [Official study](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02960217) · [Dated results note](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Canola-Safflower.md#safflower-comparator-in-a-glut1-movement-disorder-trial)
