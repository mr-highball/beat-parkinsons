# Thyme and clove evidence, 2015–2019

## Thyme drops after an induced vocal fatigue task

Barsties von Latoszek and colleagues included 48 of 56 professional voice users who reported vocal fatigue after a 20-minute loading task. They randomized participants to a proprietary thyme drop or a placebo drop. Half of the thyme group reported increased comfort when talking compared with 16.7% of the placebo group. The primary paper provides subjective voice and throat ratings. It gives no listener-rated speech intelligibility, and the drop is a formulated product in healthy voice users. See the subsequent small study with acoustic outcomes. ## Injected carvacrol and oral movements in rats

Lins’s 2017 dissertation and the 2018 article describe the same reserpine experiment. Repeated injections of isolated carvacrol prevented an increase in vacuous chewing and catalepsy relative to the reserpine group. They did not reverse the lower open-field activity. Vacuous chewing is an experimentally elicited oral movement, whereas claim 0611 concerns dyskinesia in people with Parkinson’s who eat fresh thyme. [University dissertation, experiment I](https://ri.ufs.br/bitstream/riufs/3648/1/LIVIA_CRISTINA_R_F_LINS.pdf) · [Primary journal report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29378222/)

## Oral clove polyphenols and composite hangover severity

2018-09-20. Mammen and colleagues reported a randomized, placebo-controlled crossover of standardized oral clove polyphenols in 16 men following a controlled alcohol challenge. The report gives an overall 55.34% reduction in composite hangover severity. No separate pain, ache, or headache contrast could be verified from the accessible report. The code printed with the study belongs to the ethics committee rather than an identified trial registry. Article access and the later reanalysis are documented separately. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30234415/)

## Thyme in an eight-herb urinary urgency study

Risi and colleagues reported 42 women with mild stress or mixed incontinence taking an eight-herb product containing thyme. Table 1 gives urgency-intensity means of 1.357 ± 1.509 before and 1.26 ± 1.481 after two months (p = .705). All eight participants with the maximum baseline urgency score remained at that score. Five of 17 with urgency improved and one worsened. The uncontrolled product study does not identify the species, part and preparation of thyme. It does not measure changes in Parkinson urgency frequency after fresh thyme. ## Thyme oil constituents and experimental cholinergic outcomes

Sammi et al. first published online September 6, 2016. The author abstract reports that thyme oil and para-cymene altered cholinergic signaling in experimental models including C. elegans. Thymol and gamma-terpinene were inactive individually in the described comparison, with changes reported when combined. The abstract emphasizes synaptic and receptor endpoints. Fresh thyme consumption, human attention and Parkinson clinical effects remain untested in this record.
