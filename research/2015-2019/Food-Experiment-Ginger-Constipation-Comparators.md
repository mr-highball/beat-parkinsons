# Ginger constipation comparators, 2015-2019

## Ginger capsule trial and constipation domain in adults with IBS

Rostamkhani and colleagues published their placebo-controlled trial on December 10, 2018. They report 64 adults with irritable bowel syndrome, 32 per arm, assigned to ginger capsules at 1 g/day or placebo for four weeks. Table 4 gives mean ± SD scores for the constipation domain of the Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale. The ginger arm moved from 2.85 ± 1.11 to 1.09 ± 0.94 and placebo from 2.35 ± 1.35 to 1.80 ± 1.24. Between-arm Mann–Whitney tests gave p = .271 at baseline and p = .016 at follow-up. The table gives separate within-arm tests and no baseline-adjusted between-arm change estimate. The higher starting ginger score, small sample, capsule preparation and IBS population limit transfer to fresh grated ginger and Parkinson constipation. [Original article, Table 4 on page 117](https://jmp.ir/article-1-2297-en.pdf)

The article cites IRCT2017041633467N1. The WHO public fields give first registration on June 29, 2017, after the listed first enrollment date of May 22. The target sample was 64. The 2025 three-arm study by the same lead author cites a distinct registration, sponsor and enrollment period. [WHO ICTRP registration](https://trialsearch.who.int/Trial2.aspx?TrialID=IRCT2017041633467N1) · [Later study](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Ginger-Constipation-Comparators.md#ginger-and-zataria-ibs-trial-and-registration)

## Ginger powder arm in constipation-predominant irritable bowel syndrome

Al-Jassim assigned 45 adults with IBS-C, 15 per arm, to 1 g/day of encapsulated dry ginger root powder, brewer's yeast or brown-sugar placebo for 20 days. The ginger and yeast arms were separate. The protocol excluded Parkinson disease. Mean 0–100 constipation severity was 70.66, 55.26 and 49.13 in the ginger arm at baseline, day 10 and day 20, versus 75.60, 66.93 and 65.86 for placebo. Table 1 gives p<.05 for the between-arm differences at both follow-ups. It reports no baseline-adjusted change estimate. The prose reverses yeast time points, so the numbers here come from the table. The small trial used dry powder in capsules and measured self-rated severity rather than observed stool counts. A public registration was not located. ## Ginger in both arms of a senna-containing constipation trial

Somi and colleagues randomized 48 adults with chronic constipation and analyzed 40 completers. Both groups received dried ginger, peppermint and licorice. Only the Lax-Asab product also contained senna. Mean weekly defecations changed from 1.8 to 4.8 with Lax-Asab and 1.7 to 2.2 with the comparator over four weeks (time-by-treatment p<.0001). The original Methods identify ginger in the product and its comparison mixture. The group contrast cannot establish a ginger effect, and the study did not test fresh grated ginger or enroll a Parkinson cohort.
