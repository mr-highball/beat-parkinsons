# Banana, eggs, fatigue and related symptom evidence

## Whole banana and adolescent anxiety

Presented April 18–19, 2018. The original abstract describes randomization of 62 girls aged 15–17 to banana, walking, both or control. The banana-only arm received two fruits daily for 14 days. The reported Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale regression coefficient was −7.84 (p < .001). Allocation method, blinding, fruit preparation, arm-level data and full model are unavailable in the one-page abstract. The participants did not have Parkinson disease. [Original conference abstract](https://media.neliti.com/media/publications/256608-effect-of-banana-consumption-and-walking-a48d4f39.pdf) · [Local text](Source-Text/2018-Putra-Banana-Anxiety.txt)

## Fish and shrimp in a dietary pattern and constipation

Published December 27, 2019. In 2,267 Chinese adults aged 45–59, the adjusted constipation odds ratio for the highest versus lowest southern Chinese dietary pattern quartile was 0.79 (95% CI 0.626–0.981). Fish and shrimp together were one food-frequency item with a loading of .486. The pattern contains many foods. The cross-sectional effect estimate applies to the pattern. Shrimp-specific intake and boiling were unavailable. [Nutrition Journal original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6933695/) · [Local text](Source-Text/PMC6933695.txt)

## Salmon meals and timed hand dexterity in children

First published online December 22, 2018. The indexed abstract describes 205 preschool children randomized to farmed Atlantic salmon or meat meals three times weekly for 16 weeks. A nine-hole peg test measured dexterity. Full primary outcome tables remain open. Grilling and Parkinson bradykinesia were not verified. [Clinical Nutrition original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30598384/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2018-Demmelmair-Salmon-Dexterity.txt)
