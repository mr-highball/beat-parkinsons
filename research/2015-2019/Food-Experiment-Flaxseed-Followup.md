# Flaxseed oil in a rotenone mouse model, 2017

## Flaxseed oil and anxiety-like behavior in mice

Shallie and colleagues published this study on July 25, 2017. Fifty-six adult mice were assigned to control, rotenone, flaxseed oil before rotenone, or flaxseed oil after rotenone, with 14 animals per group and equal numbers of males and females. The investigators used 0.3 mL of oil orally for 14 days. The Methods identify Organo Shoppe in Lagos as the supplier and give no extraction method or oil composition. The abstract calls the rotenone route intraperitoneal and the Methods call it subcutaneous. [Journal record and publication date](https://journalindj.com/index.php/INDJ/article/view/47) · [Original article, Methods and Figures 1–4](https://dspace.ughe.org/bitstreams/bf8d1c0e-f47b-4e5c-aefc-1287c5f33cce/download) · [HAL record](https://hal.science/hal-05392352v1) · [OpenAlex W2741131281](https://openalex.org/W2741131281)

In the elevated plus maze, rotenone increased closed-arm entries and time and reduced open-arm entries and time. The authors describe attenuation with flaxseed oil before or after rotenone. The figures give omnibus treatment p < .0001 for closed-arm time and p = .0046 for open-arm entries. Open-arm time also had a sex-by-treatment interaction (reported p < .0018). These are measures of exploration and avoidance. The figures do not establish a clinical anxiety effect or verify the cold-pressed preparation in `0141`. Hippocampal histology also showed differences between the groups. [Original article, Figures 1–4 and histology](https://dspace.ughe.org/bitstreams/bf8d1c0e-f47b-4e5c-aefc-1287c5f33cce/download#page=5)

## Flaxseed oil and striatal histology in mice

Shallie and colleagues published the striatum study on June 8, 2017. Figure 3 gives mean cell counts per 249.330 µm² of 60 ± 3.78 in controls, 27 ± 2.52 with rotenone, 50 ± 3.24 with oil after rotenone and 40 ± 3.67 with oil before rotenone (mean ± SEM). The omnibus comparison had p = .0001. The caption reports higher cell counts for both oil groups versus rotenone. It describes the control versus post-treatment contrast as significant, while the plot marks that contrast nonsignificant. The outcomes were histology and body or brain weight. [Journal record and publication date](https://journaljammr.com/index.php/JAMMR/article/view/3093) · [Original article, Figure 3](https://journaljammr.com/index.php/JAMMR/article/download/3093/6207#page=5) · [HAL record](https://hal.science/hal-05388772v1) · [OpenAlex W2625856096](https://openalex.org/W2625856096)

The striatal and anxiety papers share seven authors, the Olabisi Onabanjo University site, the May–September 2016 study period, 56 mice weighing 23.9–26.3 g, the four treatment groups, the oil supplier and the Methods dosing schedule. Both were received May 18, 2017. Treat them as likely overlapping reports. Animal identifiers are unavailable. The 2025 HAL deposits and the January 10, 2017 index dates do not establish an earlier publication than the dates printed in the journal articles. ## Flaxseed oil and open-field movement in mice

A third Shallie paper in the 2017 *International Journal of Brain and Cognitive Sciences* reports open-field exploration and cerebellar histology. Its Methods describe 56 mice and the same treatment groups. Its abstract gives seven mice per group, and its Methods add a subcutaneous pump description and an unclear open-field timeline. The authors describe higher line crossing and rearing with oil than with rotenone alone. Figure 1 gives an omnibus treatment p < .0001 and a sex-by-treatment interaction p < .0001. The activity score combined crossings and rearing. These measures leave the bradykinesia, rigidity and rest tremor claims unresolved. Treat this as a potentially overlapping report given the matching animals and design. The authors’ university bibliography confirms the publication year.
