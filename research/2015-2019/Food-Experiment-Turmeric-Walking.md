# Turmeric formulations and walking, 2015–2019

## Lipid curcumin and four-meter gait in healthy aging

Santos-Parker and colleagues randomized 39 adults to formulated curcumin or placebo for 12 weeks. The four-meter walk speed was 1.2 m/s in both arms at both time points at the reported precision (group-by-time p=.9). The six-minute distance interaction was also inconclusive (p=.6). The lipid-curcumin preparation and high-functioning cohort leave Parkinson walking with grated turmeric untested. ## Turmeric rhizome extract with volatile oil in knee osteoarthritis

Haroyan and colleagues randomized 201 patients to a curcuminoid and turmeric volatile oil preparation, a second preparation also containing boswellia, or placebo for 12 weeks. Table 3 gives a mean change in 40-meter fast walking speed of +0.08 m/s for turmeric without boswellia and +0.01 for placebo, marked p<.05. A separate printed difference of 0.10 does not match the displayed rounded change means. Text about the contrast repeats the name of the combination arm. The result pertains to a manufactured extract and symptomatic knee osteoarthritis. [Original 2018 report, Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5761198/) · [Licensed paper text](Source-Text/PMC5761198.txt)

## Tamarind and turmeric extracts in nonarthritic knee discomfort

Rao and colleagues randomized 90 adults to two doses of a combined tamarind and turmeric extract or placebo for 90 days. The six-minute walking-speed changes were +0.08 and +0.11 m/s with the mixtures versus +0.02 with placebo, with adjusted p=.001 and p<.0001. Turmeric was not tested separately. Pain-related knee limitations differ from Parkinson walking slowness.
