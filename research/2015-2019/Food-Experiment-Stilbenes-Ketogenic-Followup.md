# Grapevine stilbenes and ketogenic studies traced through HAL

## 2016 public release of the Bordeaux grapevine stilbene dissertation

Hamza Temsamani defended the Université de Bordeaux dissertation on December 18, 2015. HAL records its release on March 18, 2016. The dissertation tests isolated stilbenes, including resveratrol, piceatannol and ε-viniferin, in protein aggregation, model membrane and rat PC12 cell assays. It also reports structural analyses of the protein–compound interactions. The conclusion identifies bioavailability and in vivo effects as future work. The experiments administered no whole grape, wine or grapeseed oil and produced no animal motor, cognitive or human symptom outcomes. The PDF displays HAL Authorization and contains previously published material with separate reuse restrictions. ## 2016 vine-stalk piceatannol and alpha-synuclein toxicity

The June 15, 2016 article by Temsamani and colleagues corresponds to the companion paper listed as forthcoming in the thesis. It tested piceatannol, ampelopsin A and isohopeaphenol isolated from *Vitis vinifera* stalks. Piceatannol reduced the fibril-associated signal to 29% of the untreated preparation at the reported screening concentration. Aggregated alpha-synuclein reduced PC12 viability to 65% of control. Piceatannol restored viability at the highest tested concentration, ampelopsin A gave no protection, and isohopeaphenol reduced viability at its highest tested concentration. These molecular and cell endpoints do not measure a motor or cognitive response. ## 2016 oral medium-chain triglycerides in a rat Parkinson model

Shaafi and colleagues randomized 56 male Wistar rats across seven groups in a 6-OHDA model. The ketogenic group received medium-chain triglyceride oil by oral gavage for half of required energy, with the remaining energy from regular feed. The report gives no oil source or processing grade. The modeled Parkinson group receiving the ketogenic diet crossed a beam in 5.41 seconds on average versus 6.25 seconds with the regular diet (p = .040). The cylinder forelimb-use result also differed (64.13% versus 71.63%, p = .020). The manuscript contains an inconsistent direction in its prose describing the bar test, whose reported group means were 80.63 and 103.13 seconds respectively. These comparisons measure motor behavior with an MCT-rich diet in rats. [Primary report, Methods and Figures 4–6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4912670/) · [Local CC BY article text](Source-Text/PMC4912670.txt)

## 2017 epsilon-viniferin conference report

Richard, Da Costa, Temsamani and colleagues presented a poster on isolated ε-viniferin at Wine Active Compounds, held March 29–31, 2017. They report alpha-synuclein aggregation, lipid vesicle permeabilization, cell viability and NMR assays. The poster cites the 2016 piceatannol article as earlier work. Its ε-viniferin experiments overlap with the methods and compound described in the thesis, and the abstract gives insufficient detail to establish which experiments were repeated. It reports no behavioral assays or oral food exposure. [Official conference abstract, printed p. 94](https://chaireunesco-vinetculture.u-bourgogne.fr/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Book_of_abstract_WAC2017.pdf#page=96)

## 2018 ketogenic diet review and its Parkinson citations

Veyrat-Durebex and colleagues published the review January 26, 2018. HAL released a copy July 23, 2024. Table 1 cites the [2005 VanItallie feasibility study](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Stilbenes-Ketogenic-Followup.md#2005-hyperketogenic-diet-feasibility-study) as its clinical Parkinson evidence. Its cited Parkinson models used whole diets, beta-hydroxybutyrate or unspecified MCT oil. Its separate MCT examples include Ohnuma’s clinical study in Alzheimer’s disease and animal models of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis and Rett syndrome. The review and its primary Parkinson citations give no isolated culinary coconut oil symptom estimate.
