# Fennel seed clinical endpoint follow-up

## Fennel fruit tea in postoperative bowel recovery

Ma, Zhao and Zhao randomized 159 patients after open surgery for gynecological malignancies, 78 to steeped dried fennel fruit and 81 to warm water. Mean time to first flatus was 53.1 versus 64.2 hours and time to first defecation was 4.3 versus 5.4 days (both p < .001). The control group had a reported ileus relative risk of 2.6 (95% CI 1.5 to 4.5). Those are postoperative bowel recovery measures. The report did not measure gastric emptying, levodopa exposure or Parkinson OFF time.

## Fennel oil and anethole in drug delayed gastric emptying

Asano and colleagues’ primary abstract reports that separately administered fennel oil, anethole and a multicomponent Anchu-san preparation improved clonidine-induced delayed gastric emptying in mice. Anethole did not accelerate normal gastric emptying in the reported mouse comparison. It also improved impaired gastric accommodation in rats. The oil, compound and formula are distinct exposures. The original abstract verifies the reported directions. Arm-level estimates from the full Methods and figures remain an access gap.

[Original 2016 record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0006291X16302674) · [Local text](Source-Text/2016-26915803-Fennel-Extract.txt)

## Roasting changed the fennel fruit volatile profile

Farag and colleagues compared roasted and raw fruits of fennel, anise, caraway, coriander and cumin using headspace gas chromatography–mass spectrometry and peroxide measurements. They reported estragole as the dominant volatile of their raw fennel sample and 6.1 mEq O₂/kg for the roasted fennel sample’s peroxide value. The authors identified relative volatile-profile changes after roasting across the study. Results describe the tested samples and processing conditions. No animals or patients consumed the study samples.

## Registered topical fennel intervention after colorectal resection

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted this registration on October 18, 2018. It compares an externally applied fennel intervention (“ironing”) with controls after colorectal resection and plans postoperative ileus outcomes. The current official record is completed and shows no posted results. The intervention and clinical context differ from oral toasted seeds with levodopa.

## Fennel essential oil and mouse gastric emptying versus intestinal propulsion

Zhang and colleagues measured stomach retention and small-intestinal progression separately after oral fennel essential oil. In normal mice, the middle oil dose reduced intestinal propulsion (p < .05), while the gastric emptying differences were nonsignificant. With atropine, neither outcome differed significantly from the drug-only group. With neostigmine, the high oil dose reduced intestinal propulsion (p < .01), while gastric emptying fell nonsignificantly. The Chinese Results and Tables 1–3 provide the endpoint-specific reading. Slower intestinal progression alone cannot establish delayed stomach emptying or longer Parkinson OFF time.
