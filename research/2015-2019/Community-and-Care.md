# Community and care

### 2016 — Racial differences in Parkinson’s medication use under Medicare Part D

Dahodwala and colleagues linked Medicare diagnostic claims with prescription files from 2007–2010. More than 81% of beneficiaries identified with Parkinson’s filled Parkinson’s medications, and Black beneficiaries had lower adjusted odds of doing so than White beneficiaries. Prescription claims capture dispensing and cannot resolve differences in prescribing, preferences, access or adherence. The journal issue appeared in 2017. [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6174430/)

### 2017 — Video specialist visits saved time and travel

Beck and colleagues randomized 195 participants to usual care or usual care supplemented with remote specialist visits. Participants completed most planned video visits, saving a median 88 minutes and 38 miles per visit. Quality of life and caregiver burden did not differ significantly. Most participants had already seen a specialist, and 96% were White, limiting what the trial shows about people with the least access. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28814455/)

### 2017 — ParkinsonNet physiotherapy delivery and costs

A retrospective analysis of Dutch claims found fewer Parkinson-related complications and lower costs among patients who saw ParkinsonNet-specialized physiotherapists than among those who received usual physiotherapy. Referral and treatment selection may have influenced the comparisons. First published online in 2017. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29246470/)
