# Pumpkin seed oil and motor outcome follow-up

## 2015 Mixed-oil comparator in older men with resistance exercise

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT02617511 on December 1, 2015. The record assigns older men to omega-3 supplements or a 3 g/day omega-3/6/9 comparator, with both groups performing resistance training for 12 weeks. The comparator intervention lists pumpkin seed oil alongside four other vegetable oils in its other-names field; this listing does not specify the product composition or confirm the amount of pumpkin oil actually consumed. The registry results posted in 2023 include 12 comparator participants and chest-press means of 49.6 kg at baseline and 65.0 kg after 12 weeks. They contain no handwriting or manual dexterity measure. Processing, including cold pressing, is not identified, and exercise and multiple oils prevent attributing a strength change to pumpkin oil. [Official trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02617511)

Cornish et al. first published a report on September 19, 2018 (PMID 30527263, DOI 10.1016/j.nutres.2018.09.005) with the same investigator, 23 older men, 11/12 group sizes, omega-3/placebo assignment and 12-week exercise protocol. These features make the trial match highly plausible, but the accessible abstract does not explicitly identify the NCT number or the placebo oils. It reports aggregate chest-press change over time and no hand or handwriting measure. [Original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30527263/)

## 2018 original methods and a matching 2015 comparator label

The accepted manuscript's section 2.1 identifies the comparator as a NOW Foods omega 3-6-9 blend, 3 g/day, in three breakfast capsules. Its stated fatty-acid amounts were 1,350 mg alpha-linolenic, 795 mg combined linoleic and gamma-linolenic, 525 mg oleic, and 330 mg other components. The author, site, 24 recruits and 23 completers, group sizes, arms, exercise regimen and functional outcomes align with NCT02617511. The manuscript copy and current trial registry lack an explicit PMID to NCT reference. [Primary accepted manuscript, sections 2.1–2.2](https://www.academia.edu/129051439/Omega_3_supplementation_with_resistance_training_does_not_improve_body_composition_or_lower_biomarkers_of_inflammation_more_so_than_resistance_training_alone_in_older_men) · [Published abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30527263/) · [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02617511)

A 2015 NIH Dietary Supplement Label Database record for NOW 1000 mg softgels lists exactly the same four fatty-acid percentages and the five botanical oils named in the registry. It lists 20 mg cold-pressed pumpkin seed oil per two softgels. If the matching formulation was used, the manuscript's three-softgel instruction implies 30 mg/day of pumpkin oil within the blend. The paper gives neither a lot identifier nor a purchase receipt. Participant intake of that amount therefore remains inferred. The older-men trial measured resistance strength and mobility. It did not measure handwriting or include a Parkinson cohort.
