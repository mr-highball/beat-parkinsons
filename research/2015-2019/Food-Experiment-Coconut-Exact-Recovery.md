# Coconut oil in a Parkinson symptom survey

## Earlier coconut food-frequency estimate and later MVP analyses

[Mischley et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5610862/), first published 10 September 2017, PMID 29081890, DOI 10.1155/2017/6405278, analyzed baseline responses from 1,053 people with self-reported idiopathic Parkinson disease. The food-frequency item named coconut oil with a one-teaspoon reference serving and ten frequency categories, from never to 5–6 times daily. The fully adjusted cross-sectional association per frequency-category step was −20.2 total PRO-PD points (SE 5.5, 95% CI −31.0 to −9.4), adjusted for age, gender, income and years since diagnosis. Table 2 prints an invalid p threshold of less than 0.000. This is a self-reported population association, not proof that each person consumed precisely a teaspoon or that oil caused the difference. The oil grade was not specified. The paper does not give separate estimates for dyskinesia, urinary urgency, dream enactment or listener-rated intelligibility.

The same paper also analyzed coconut oil as a binary supplement, a different exposure question; its fully adjusted estimate was −52.7 total points (95% CI −124.1 to 18.6, p=0.147), which crosses zero. Neither exposure defines a virgin or refined product. A later [2023 MVP supplement analysis](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Oil-Followup.md#coconut-oil-and-composite-parkinsons-symptom-scores) used a different supplement definition, while the [2026 unreviewed MVP preprint](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Coconut-Remaining.md#longitudinal-coconut-oil-association-in-mvp) used baseline consistent therapy use and several baseline and mixed-effects longitudinal analyses. These overlapping-project analyses are not independent trials; the 2017 cross-sectional frequency coefficient must not be presented as a longitudinal slope or a symptom-specific effect.
