## Date syrup and labor pain registration

IRCT registered IRCT201405182172N16 on March 15, 2015. The record describes date syrup, honey syrup, and a control, with pain measured every 30 minutes by visual analogue scale. The registry describes an unblinded design. The later reports differ in sample count and supply outcomes for acute labor pain outside Parkinson’s. [Primary registration](https://irct.ir/trial/1798) · [Outcome reports](Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#date-syrup-and-labor-pain)

## Topical date palm preparation and knee osteoarthritis registration

IRCT prospectively registered Bagherzadeh-Karimi’s IRCT20180126038510N1 on August 25, 2018. The registry specifies a planned 94 participants with mild to moderate knee osteoarthritis, unblinded block randomization, and four weeks of a topical date-palm preparation or diclofenac gel. It lists visual analogue and WOMAC pain measures at baseline and days 14 and 28, plus WOMAC stiffness and daily function. It marks recruitment complete and gives no results, actual enrollment, or botanical part. The Tabriz institution and ethics identifier IR.TBZMED.REC.1397.227 provide routes to the dissertation. The topical comparison addresses joint pain in another population. [English registration](https://irct.ir/trial/29648) · [Persian registration](https://fa.irct.ir/trial/29648) · [2020 dissertation trail](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#date-palm-preparation-and-knee-osteoarthritis-thesis)

## Whole Barhi dates and menstrual characteristics conference report

At the March 2018 International Date Palm Conference, Al-Sayyed and colleagues described a feeding comparison in 36 volunteers recruited by convenience. The intervention used Barhi dates over four menstrual cycles. The proceedings report hormone measures and menstrual characteristics, with no separate pain severity or apathy estimate. The later nutrient-intake and premenstrual-symptom papers cite this work. [Investigator’s proceedings copy, pp. 138–144](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334400446_Effect_of_feeding_date_palm_fruit_Phoenix_dactylifera_L_on_menstrual_health_in_a_convenient_sample_of_females) · [2020 nutrient-intake report](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Date-Pain-Followup.md#barhi-date-feeding-and-nutrient-intake) · [2025 symptom report](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Date-Pain-Followup.md#whole-date-feeding-and-premenstrual-symptoms)

## Aqueous date fruit extract and tail-flick latency

Maryam and colleagues assigned 24 Sprague–Dawley rats by convenience to four groups. After three weeks of oral exposure, the higher-dose aqueous fruit extract group had a median thermal tail-flick latency of 6.5 seconds versus 2.0 seconds with saline (reported pairwise p = 0.001). The low-dose group had a median of 3.5 seconds, and the report does not identify a statistically clear comparison with saline for that dose. The paper separately measured inflammatory ear edema. The extract, convenience assignment, and evoked animal response limit its relevance to [0502, dried dates and Parkinson’s pain severity](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0502.txt). [Primary report, Methods and Table 2](https://www.noveltyjournals.com/upload/paper/Anti-Inflammatory-438.pdf#page=4)

## Injected date palm heart extract and hot-plate latency

Sarhan and Ibrahim tested an injected ethanol extract of date palm heart in a rodent hot-plate experiment. Table 1 reports six animals per group, with mean response times of 14.45 seconds for the highest extract dose, 2.25 seconds for saline, and 17.36 seconds for meloxicam. The hot-plate methods introduce mice and then refer to rats, so the species assignment is inconsistently described. The November 27, 2019 report also tested edema in separate groups. The palm-heart extract, injection route, and evoked pain response limit applicability to eating dried date fruit. [Publisher record](https://indianjournals.com/article/ijfmt-13-4-242) · [Primary full text, Table 1](https://www.ijfmt.com/scripts/IJFMT_October%202019_Combined%20File.pdf)
