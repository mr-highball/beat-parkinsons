# Food experiment follow-up evidence

## Protein redistribution followed by a vegan diet in one parkinsonism case

Kurlan, Kumari and Ganihong published this case on April 5, 2016. A 64-year-old man with levodopa-responsive parkinsonism and prominent autonomic symptoms reported a more predictable levodopa response after avoiding protein at breakfast and lunch. Two months later he adopted an organic vegan diet excluding eggs, cheese, milk and other animal products. The authors report near resolution of gait freezing and start hesitation and a reduction in daily levodopa from 2,175 to 1,305 mg. Symptoms recurred with further levodopa reduction. Ropinirole was stopped and midodrine was also reduced during follow-up. [Publisher article](https://www.avensonline.org/fulltextarticles/JPA-2376-922X-03-0016.html) · [Local CC BY 3.0 article text](Source-Text/2016-Kurlan-Vegan-Parkinsonism.txt)

The authors considered Parkinson’s disease with dysautonomia and multiple system atrophy in the differential. They report frequent falls before the diet without a follow-up fall count. The case has no standardized motor scores, ingredient-specific exposure measurements, comparator, or return to a nonvegan diet. The report cannot isolate which parts of the dietary and medication changes contributed to the observed course. [Publisher PDF](https://www.avensonline.org/wp-content/uploads/JPA-2376-922X-03-0016.pdf)

## Vegetable intake and falls in older adults

Two August 2018 Sim reports analyze the same 1,429 older Australian women and the same 568 falls-related hospitalizations. Vegetable diversity was associated with fewer hospitalizations per additional vegetable per day (adjusted HR 0.92, 95% CI 0.86–0.99). In the intake analysis, the five-class model associated each 20 g/day of cruciferous vegetables with fewer falls-related hospitalizations (HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.82–0.97). The cruciferous group lists cabbage, Brussels sprouts, cauliflower and broccoli. The leafy group pools lettuce and other salad greens with celery, silverbeet and spinach. The fruit analysis groups oranges with other citrus, with an inconclusive association. The reports give no identifiable raw arugula intake and did not report a Parkinson subgroup or the frequency of noninjurious falls. [Primary vegetable-class report, methods and Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114518002155) · [Primary diversity report and local text](Source-Text/PMC6115713.txt)

In a separate August 2018 study, Agarwal and colleagues followed 706 older adults initially without parkinsonism. Higher MIND diet scores were associated with fewer incident parkinsonian signs (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.83–0.96) and a slower rise in the composite sign score (β −0.008, SE 0.0037). The intake measure combines leafy greens with other dietary groups. The study measured parkinsonian signs and did not analyze falls or raw arugula intake. [Primary Memory and Aging Project report, methods and Tables 2–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6436549/)

## Asparagus stem extract and fatigue after laboratory stress

Takanari and colleagues published a blinded, placebo-controlled crossover in 25 healthy volunteers in 2016. Participants received enzyme-treated asparagus stem extract or dextrin placebo for four weeks each. Figure 5 shows a significant reduction from day zero on the Profile of Mood States fatigue scale after the extract period. The significance marker compares days zero and 28 within that period, so it does not establish a difference in change between extract and placebo. The endpoint was recorded after an arithmetic stress task. The study tested an extract in healthy volunteers. [Primary report, Figure 5](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jnsv/62/3/62_198/_pdf/-char/ja)

## Asparagus extract preparation and fatigue in taxi drivers

Deguchi and colleagues enrolled 76 taxi drivers in an open-label randomized four-week comparison of a processed food containing asparagus extract with no test food. The per-protocol population was 25 intervention and 29 control participants. The report describes lower Chalder Fatigue Scale scores in the intervention group, alongside other sleep and fatigue measures. Knowledge of group assignment and the exclusions matter for the self-reported outcome. The journal and National Diet Library identify a 2019 issue. J-STAGE released the digital copy on September 30, 2020. [Primary report and fatigue scales](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jcts/19/1/19_33/_pdf/-char/ja) · [Journal publication record](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jcts/19/1/19_33/_article/-char/en) · [National Diet Library print citation](https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/en/books/R000000004-I030139971)

## 2017 journal report of sublingual palmitoylethanolamide

Brotini, Schievano, and Guidi published an observational study of 30 people with Parkinson’s disease receiving levodopa. The journal abstract reports reductions in MDS-UPDRS Parts I–IV and in most individual items after one year. Item-specific fatigue and rigidity estimates are unavailable in the public abstract. The reports share two authors, the sample size, treatment, and follow-up. They are treated as reports of one clinical series. The journal record entered PubMed on March 23, 2017, and the publisher deposited the DOI on April 17. Crossref gives August 23 for the issue publication, with no separate publisher-verified online publication day in the accessible metadata. A later review lists chicken and tomato as dietary sources of palmitoylethanolamide. The study tested a sublingual formulation and gives no estimate for poached chicken and fatigue `0348` or cooked tomato and rigidity `0544`. [2017 journal abstract, PMID 28325153](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28325153/) · [NLM publication history](https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=28325153&retmode=xml) · [Publisher DOI metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2174/1871527316666170321124949) · [2014 congress report](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#ultramicronized-palmitoylethanolamide-in-parkinsons-disease) · [Archived review](../2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC7765232.txt)

## Food tolerance in idiopathic and diabetic gastroparesis

Wytiaz and colleagues surveyed 45 people with gastroparesis, including 39 with idiopathic disease. Participants rated their usual experience with foods on a −3 to +3 scale. Oranges, orange juice, fried chicken, onions, tomato juice, coffee, and broccoli were among the foods reported to worsen upper gastrointestinal symptoms. Sweet potato, tea, salmon, and potato were among the foods generally tolerated. The study concerns gastroparesis symptoms and supplies no food-specific Parkinson falls or constipation estimate for `0259`, `0878`, or `1000`. [Primary report, PMID 25840923](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25840923/) · [Temple research-day abstract 64](https://medicine.temple.edu/sites/medicine/files/files/Sol%20Sherry%20FY2015.pdf) · [Archived Parkinson’s review](../2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC9403060.txt)

## Kefir powder and bone outcomes in osteoporosis

Tu and colleagues reported a double-blind parallel comparison of freeze-dried kefir-fermented milk plus calcium carbonate and a control with calcium carbonate in 40 people with osteoporosis over six months. They measured bone turnover and bone mineral density, reporting a more favorable hip bone-density change with kefir. They did not count incident falls. The powdered formulation and the population also differ from plain kefir in Parkinson’s in `0526`. [Primary trial, Methods and Results](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0144231) · [Trial record NCT02361372](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02361372) · [Archived source review](../2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC8999082.txt)

## Coffee, tea, and constipation in an Italian dietary survey

Barichella and colleagues compared 600 people with Parkinson’s disease with 600 controls. People with Parkinson’s reported less coffee (55 vs 70 g/day) and tea (87 vs 108 g/day), and constipation was more common (46.8% vs 6.8%). The within-Parkinson constipation comparison in Supplementary Table 2 concerned nutrient, fiber, and water intake and did not compare constipation by coffee, tea, or citrus intake. Protein redistribution was associated with a lower off-state score, with no clear difference in dyskinesia. A second paper used a 600-case, 600-control Italian questionnaire and reported dietary patterns and food frequencies. Neither analysis supplies a food-specific estimate for the frozen beverage and food claims. [2016 online primary paper and supplements, PMID 27406858](https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/e1dcb339-52d0-7715-e053-1705fe0a6cc9/BarichellaM_ClinNutr_2017_postprint.pdf) · [Second report, 2017](https://www.prd-journal.com/article/S1353-8020%2817%2930214-6/abstract)

## High-theanine green tea and fatigue registration

UMIN000034209 was first public on September 20, 2018. The randomized crossover protocol compares 150 mL each of 4% green tea, 2% green tea, water, and theanine in water in healthy male university students. The registry records 35 participants, and the secondary fatigue assessment at 60 minutes includes emotion and biomarker measures. All four public registry versions retain the four-drink crossover design and show unpublished results. A 2021 Kyoto Prefecture patent names a report with the same Japanese title and investigator, then describes a three-arm parallel experiment with 105 men and 200 mL drinks. The registry and patent do not establish how the cohorts relate. The registered crossover results remain unresolved, and its population differs from claim `0948`. [Primary UMIN record](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000039000) · [Registry history](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_his_list.cgi?recptno=R000039000) · [Japanese ministry record](https://rctportal.mhlw.go.jp/detail/um?trial_id=UMIN000034209) · [2021 patent experiment](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#tencha-and-task-related-fatigue-in-a-patent-disclosure) · [Earlier acute study](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#green-tea-after-a-laboratory-fatigue-task)

Watanabe’s separate registration UMIN000037886 first appeared September 2, 2019. It describes a four-condition crossover in healthy male students using 150 mL each of two grades of matcha, water and theanine water, with work-task, EEG and heart-rate-variability outcomes. Both registry versions leave the secondary outcome field blank, and the latest lists the study as completed with unpublished results. Watanabe’s faculty record lists a March 2020 presentation on concentration and relaxation after high-quality matcha. The listing supplies no cohort identifier, methods, or outcome values. [Separate matcha registration](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/icdr/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000043192) · [Registration history](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_his_list.cgi?recptno=R000043192) · [Investigator presentation list](https://www.fuksi-kagk-u.ac.jp/faculty/health/watanabe-eri.html)

Beauchamp and colleagues used orange essential oil in a mouse odor detection assay that compared tau and α-synuclein genotypes. They report no exposure to raw orange as food or constipation response for `0878`. ## Chicken essence in workers under stress

Chan and colleagues randomized 102 stressed workers to commercial essence of chicken or placebo for two weeks. The trial excluded Parkinson’s disease and assessed cognition, blood markers, and baseline mood. Fatigue is discussed through citations to earlier chicken extract trials. A 2018 Thai trial enrolled adults reporting fatigue but assessed cognition as its efficacy outcome. The extract and outcomes differ from poached chicken and Parkinson’s fatigue (`0348`). ## Virgin coconut oil in a scopolamine amnesia model

The 2019 conference abstract P2-H16 by Nilugal and colleagues reports longer time on a rotarod and faster access to food in a T-maze in rats given extra-virgin coconut oil during a scopolamine amnesia experiment. Its reported total of 20 rats conflicts with five groups of six, and the abstract does not report effect sizes or uncertainty intervals for the behavioral comparisons. The rotarod measure concerns motor coordination in a drug-induced amnesia model. It does not supply a clinical fall or dyskinesia estimate for `0151` or `0156`. Salomonsson and colleagues measured free and total BMAA in seafood from grocery stores in Uppsala. The compound was below the study’s detection limit in the shrimp samples. The study used different seafood samples and analytical methods from the earlier Florida and Swedish-market studies. It measured tissue composition and gives no human fall comparison for `0322`. ## Combined nutrition intervention and gastrointestinal symptoms

Flores Solís and colleagues’ July 10, 2017 report followed 54 patients with Parkinson’s disease in a Mexican clinic. The individualized program combined dietary guidance, supplements, soluble or insoluble fiber, and thickeners where indicated. Table 6 reports dysphagia prevalence of 66.7% at baseline and 21.7% at follow-up, constipation of 74.1% and 42.6%, and a combined tremor or gait category of 72.2% and 59.3%. The paper provides no concurrent control, paired change interval, or estimate for any named food or preparation. Its eligibility rules excluded patients who did not adhere to the program. The dysphagia figures are symptom prevalence and do not identify severity after a particular food. [Publisher date](https://respyn.uanl.mx/index.php/respyn/article/view/31) · [Primary full article, Table 6](https://respyn.uanl.mx/index.php/respyn/article/download/31/31/61#page=4)

## Whole date fruit and gastrointestinal symptoms outside Parkinson’s

Eid and colleagues compared whole Ajwa dates at the Tamr stage with a carbohydrate control in a randomized crossover in 21 healthy adults who completed the study. Stool frequency increased during the date intervention. The bowel diaries also tracked abdominal pain and subjective mood. The paper reports no other clear gastrointestinal symptom differences and gives no mood results or apathy instrument. The earlier conference abstract and registry also lack diary results. The study enrolled healthy participants and measured abdominal symptoms. It provides related evidence for `0502`, dried dates and pain severity, with no Parkinson-specific pain or apathy result. [Primary report, Table 2](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/impact-of-palm-date-consumption-on-microbiota-growth-and-large-intestinal-health-a-randomised-controlled-crossover-human-intervention-study/ED43BF5EF097AD6486B9BA81A9BCD2F2)

The publication describes single masking. The registry record posted in 2014 describes an open-label design, leaving the masking of diary assessments unclear. ## Date palm preparations in experimental inflammation and pain

Peyghambari and colleagues reported reduced nociceptive responses in mice given a hydroalcoholic extract of the date palm spathe, the sheath around the flower cluster. Kehili and colleagues reported lower paw edema and inflammatory biomarkers after a date fruit extract in a separate mouse model. Paw swelling and circulating biomarkers leave pain severity unmeasured in the fruit study. Both studies concern experimental preparations and models outside Parkinson’s disease. [2015 spathe study, PMID 26693469](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4685642/) · [2016 fruit study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09540105.2016.1183597)

Sheikh and colleagues gave mice ethanol extracts from dried Tamr-stage Ajwah, Safawy, and Sukkari dates. All three extracts reduced acetic-acid-induced writhing and prolonged hot-plate response latency at the measured peak one hour after administration. The same extracts reduced locomotion and exploration and prolonged drug-induced sleep. Reduced movement complicates interpretation of behavioral pain tests and provides no apathy measure for `0758`. The 2009 whole-fruit feeding study found no statistically clear effect on the hot-plate threshold. [2016 primary report, Methods and Tables 1–5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5040737/) · [2009 whole-fruit study](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#date-fig-and-olive-diets-in-a-mouse-pain-threshold-study)

Chenini-Bendiab and colleagues’ 2019 Algerian conference report describes dose- and time-dependent paw edema inhibition from aqueous and ethanolic Deglet Nour pulp extracts in mice. The investigators measured edema and histology. The abstract provides no behavioral pain result. [Primary French abstract CATIII 11, pp. 114–115](https://www.univ-chlef.dz/fsnv/wp-content/uploads/SNNS1-2019-Chlef.pdf)

## Date syrup and labor pain

The oral date syrup trial first appeared online December 26, 2018. It randomized 80 participants in active labor to syrup or a control that could drink unsweetened fluids. Pain ratings were lower in the syrup group at several time points after the first hour. The comparator did not control for the syrup’s sweetness or energy. A separate 2018 three-arm trial found no clear pain difference during labor between saffron with date syrup and saffron with artificial sweetener (reported mean difference −0.3 on a 0–10 scale, 95% CI −1.0 to 0.4). These trials concern acute obstetric pain and different preparations. [Oral date syrup trial, Table 2](https://brieflands.com/journals/semj/articles/69207) · [Saffron with or without date syrup trial, Table 2](https://doi.org/10.5812/ircmj.61289)

Fathi and Amraei’s Persian report describes 64 participants and also reports lower labor-pain ratings in the date-syrup group. Its Methods share registration IRCT201405182172N16, ethics code 132686-9111373029, and the Khorramabad site with Taavoni and colleagues. A 2017 conference abstract matches the 80-person Taavoni report. The 2018 Persian report by Fathi, Amraei, and Yari describes 80 women and reports labor duration. The later evidence synthesis matches it to the same registration. Treat these as related reports with a sample-count discrepancy. [Fathi and Amraei, DOI 10.29252/ijn.31.116.18, p. 21](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/412b/c556c1a5b922412beea58bb6fdbf1efc0a39.pdf#page=4) · [2017 conference abstract](https://hr2017.humanrepacademy.org/abstractbook/pdf/abs5543.pdf) · [2018 Persian labor-progress report](https://www.magiran.com/paper/2076914/the-effect-of-date-consumption-on-the-progress-of-labor-in-nulliparous-women?lang=en) · [Registration citation trail, references 62–64](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12884-023-06196-y)

Yousefy Jadidi and colleagues studied whole date fruit near term and reported earlier spontaneous labor. Kordi and colleagues assessed duration of labor stages and interventions after late-pregnancy date consumption. In these reports, the onset of labor pains indicates when labor began. Neither comparison estimates pain intensity after eating dates. [Yousefy Jadidi primary report abstract](https://doaj.org/article/6674d463492c4cf38e794fbdeb3db1c1) · [Kordi primary report, PMID 29033994](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5637148/)

## Whole Ajwa dates and mood after an acute stressor

Abdullah and colleagues measured mood and cognition in 20 healthy adults aged 18–30 after a laboratory stressor, before and after six weeks of eating seven Ajwa dates daily. The April 19, 2019 journal release reports one group. Figure 1 shows a small unmarked decrease in Profile of Mood States vigor, read visually at roughly 20 to 19, without an exact tabulated estimate or significance marker. Fatigue and depression also lack significance markers. The authors found lower tension and lower stress on the DASS-21 stress subscale. No apathy-specific measure or Parkinson’s cohort was used for claim `0758`. The report does not specify the drying process or maturity stage. ## Ground soybean crossover alongside levodopa and carbidopa

Nagashima and colleagues compared levodopa and carbidopa alone with the same drugs plus ground soybeans in a crossover with seven people with Parkinson’s and motor complications and five healthy volunteers. The Parkinson’s group had a longer on-period (p = 0.028) and lower modified Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale scores (p < 0.001) during the soybean condition. The abstract did not give confidence intervals for these effects. The observed effects concern acute drug coadministration and the ground-soybean preparation. Frozen claims `0462`, `0475`, `0123`, and `0693` name cooked or steamed edamame, cooked tempeh, or baked tofu, with rigidity, olfaction, stride, or drooling outcomes. [Primary crossover report, PMID 26810548](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26810548/)

## Fish, yogurt, and overall symptom scores in the MVP cohort

Mischley and colleagues analyzed baseline data for 1,053 participants who self-reported idiopathic Parkinson’s. After adjustment for age, sex, income, and time since diagnosis, the food-frequency regression associated each step of higher nonfried fish intake with a −29.5 point difference in the overall PRO-PD symptom score (95% CI −47.3 to −11.6) and yogurt with a +15.2 point difference (95% CI 0.2 to 30.1). Estimates for chicken and green tea crossed zero. These baseline associations cannot establish symptom progression or causal effects. The exposures were food groups and the reported outcome was a composite. Relevant frozen hypotheses include `0832` for strained yogurt and handwriting, `0247` for canned sardines and fatigue, `0583` for grilled salmon and bradykinesia, `0138` for grilled cod and fatigue, and `0396` for grilled trout and daytime sleepiness. [Primary paper, PMID 29081890](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5610862/) · [Local full text](Source-Text/PMC5610862.txt)

## Coffee intake, rest tremor, and nonmotor symptoms

Cho and colleagues studied 284 people with newly diagnosed, untreated Parkinson’s, of whom 204 reported coffee consumption. After adjustment, men with higher reported consumption had lower rest tremor scores. The dose relationship was inconclusive in women and for action tremor. The study did not isolate brewing method or test treatment. An earlier analysis by the same group in untreated early Parkinson’s reported a less severe combined mood and cognition domain among coffee drinkers. Cohort overlap is uncertain. These outcomes differ from the fatigue severity endpoint for brewed coffee in `0121`. The rest tremor association also cannot be carried across beverages to black tea claim `0904`. [Primary tremor paper, PMID 31412802](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6693140/) · [Local full text](Source-Text/PMC6693140.txt) · [Primary nonmotor paper, PMID 29449185](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29449185/)

## Orange pomace and stool frequency outside Parkinson’s disease

Dennis-Wall and colleagues reported higher mean weekly stool frequency in healthy adults assigned an orange-pomace beverage than in controls (p = 0.0281). The trial used an added fiber fraction and excluded whole oranges during the intervention. Frozen claim `0878` concerns worsening constipation after eating raw orange in Parkinson’s, so the preparation and population differ. A later crossover with a similar ingredient reported no clear change in healthy adults. [Primary 2019 report](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jff.2019.103438) · [Later crossover, PMID 36102329](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36102329/)

The December 1, 2016 registration NCT02979496 specifies a constipation symptom score in addition to stool frequency and form. The publication reports softer stool (p = .04) and more gas or bloating with pomace. The accessible primary text and public registry do not provide the separate constipation score result. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02979496) · [Local registry extract](Source-Text/Trials/NCT02979496.txt) · [Original publication](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1756464619303627)

## Protein-rich meals and reported levodopa fluctuations

Virmani and colleagues reviewed 1,037 Parkinson’s clinic records. Fifty-two of 877 people taking levodopa (5.9%) reported decreased or delayed benefit, earlier wearing off, or dose failure in connection with protein-rich meals. The proportion was 52 of 421 (12.4%) among those already experiencing motor fluctuations. The reported foods were predominantly meat, eggs and dairy, and the study relied on patients identifying protein-associated episodes. It tracked freezing of gait as a disease marker and supplied no comparison of fall frequency after shrimp. The study provides medication-interaction context for [0322, boiled shrimp and fall frequency](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0322.txt). [Primary chart review, PMID 27231577](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40734-016-0036-9)

## Cutting and eating apple during a motor assessment

Goubault and colleagues recorded people with Parkinson’s while they cut a standardized piece of apple and ate two pieces as part of a daily-activity test. The study used pooled rest, postural, and kinetic tremor as predictors of task performance. The final cutting-and-eating model included bradykinesia, age, and cognition, with no tremor estimate for that task. There was no before-and-after test of apple ingestion. This gives exposure context for [0833, raw apple and rest tremor](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0833.txt). ## Banana pulp and chemical compatibility with acetaminophen

Uesawa and Tsuji’s October 12, 2018 study measured acetaminophen concentration after direct mixing with banana pulp supernatant. The loss varied among banana samples and disappeared after autoclaving the supernatant. Chromatography and mass spectrometry detected oxidation products. The authors cite levodopa and methyldopa studies as background. Their reported bench comparisons tested acetaminophen, with no human data or new levodopa contrast. The preparation-dependent result provides context for the drug-compatibility hypothesis and leaves the raw banana Parkinson claims unresolved. ## Levodopa compatibility in a banana and milk mixture

Kyowa Kirin’s July 2019 EC-Doparl product monograph reproduces a bench compatibility test of dissolved levodopa/benserazide tablet in a banana and milk mixture. Relative to the amount immediately after mixing, 65.7% of the levodopa remained at 30 minutes and 41.7% at one hour. The document does not identify the product responsible for the discoloration. The earliest public version located is the 2019 revision. This mixture experiment used a different preparation from the 2005 granule and fresh-banana water test and gives no pharmacokinetic or symptom result in people. ## Acute purple grape juice and mood, full report

Haskell-Ramsay and colleagues published the full crossover trial on 20 April 2017. Twenty healthy young adults drank commercially available purple grape juice plus flavored cordial or a matched white-grape-juice control. At 20 minutes, adjusted calm ratings were 63.22 versus 57.61 (p = 0.046). A significant effect of administration order also influenced calm ratings. Attention responses were faster after purple juice in 16 evaluable participants. Alertness, contentment and memory did not show statistically significant treatment effects. The results concern juice and transient ratings outside Parkinson disease. They give indirect context for raw grapes and anxiety (`0860`) and no Parkinson daytime-sleepiness result (`0055`). [Primary report, Results and Table 3, PMID 28429081](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00394-017-1454-7) · [2013 proceedings report](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#acute-concord-grape-juice-crossover-2013-conference-report)

## Grape fruit extract and anxiety ratings in healthy older adults

Calapai and colleagues randomly assigned 111 healthy older adults to a 250 mg daily capsule containing *Vitis vinifera* fruit extract and maltodextrin or maltodextrin placebo for 12 weeks. The treated group’s Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale score fell 24.9% from baseline and was lower than placebo at 12 weeks (p < 0.05). The 24.9% is a within-group change. The overall baseline mean was 6.97 and participants did not have clinical anxiety. The report, published 31 October 2017, gives indirect context for `0860` because the test used an extract and people without Parkinson disease. [Primary report, Results and Figure 2, PMID 29163162](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2017.00776)

## Grape powder in aged rats

Patki and colleagues tested whole-grape powder in drinking water in 21-month-old and 3-month-old Fischer 344 rats. The aged rats given powder showed less anxiety-like behavior than aged controls, with no clear benefit for age-associated memory deficits. The non-Parkinson model and reconstituted powder give indirect context for raw grape and anxiety (`0860`). [Primary report, PMID 26022140](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nutres.2015.05.005)

## Whole dates versus exercise in older adults with depression

Abdelhamid and colleagues’ 2016 report assigned 100 adults aged 60–70 with mild depression to three dates daily or treadmill exercise for 24 weeks. Both groups continued their medical treatment. Table 3 gives median Geriatric Depression Scale scores of 17.5 to 3 in the date group and 17 to 2 in the exercise group, with p = 0.44 for the post-treatment comparison. The report gives no untreated comparison or separate apathy scores and does not specify the cultivar or processing state of the consumed fruit. The participants were recruited for depression, so the results leave Parkinson apathy (`0758`) unresolved. ## Isolated pea lectin in a rat exposure model

Anselmi and colleagues' September 27, 2018 paper identified the lectin as purified protein from *Pisum sativum*. In the rat model, animals given paraquat and the lectin had lower forelimb placement scores two weeks later (9.6 to 6.1 successful placements out of 10, p < .05) and lower stepping scores (76.4% of baseline, p < .05). The separate lectin-only and paraquat-only groups each had five rats and retained baseline performance by the authors' interpretation. The published sentence gives a comparison label that is hard to reconcile with the reported pattern, so it does not establish a precise between-group contrast. The study used the extracted protein, a toxicant challenge and forced motor tests. It gives no cooked-pea comparison or human walking-speed estimate. ## Uncontrolled trifoliate orange extract and neurogenic constipation

Kim, Lee and Joo’s September 25, 2016 report enrolled 31 adults with spinal cord injury and analyzed 25 after 14 days of aqueous immature *Poncirus trifoliata* fruit extract. Constipation score changed from 4.60 to 3.48 (p = .04) and mean total transit from 57.4 to 41.2 hours (p < .05). The study used a single-group before-and-after design. Seven participants had loose stool or diarrhea attributed to the product. The extract was from a different plant than sweet orange, and the population and preparation differ from raw orange in Parkinson disease. ## Pasteurized orange juice and bowel diary outcomes

Lima and colleagues first published online January 14, 2019. Ten apparently healthy women followed a 30-day baseline, 60 days of 300 mL/day commercial pasteurized 100% orange juice, and a 30-day washout. The same group went through all periods in that order. Table 1 gives mean (SD) ordinal bowel-frequency scores of 1.40 (0.69) at baseline, 1.30 (0.48) at day 30, 1.40 (0.69) at day 60, and 1.60 (0.69) at washout. The frequency scale used 1 for daily, 2 for every other day, and 3 for less than three times weekly. Baseline to day 60 stool consistency was 1.00 (0.94) to 1.20 (0.63), evacuation effort was 2.40 (0.69) to 2.20 (0.41), and painful evacuation was 3.50 (0.52) to 2.40 (0.51). The Results report no significant differences across periods for these diary outcomes. The study had no concurrent control group, enrolled healthy participants, and used pasteurized juice. ## Apple task in the earlier Parkinson motor study

The first report from this study series included cutting and eating a piece of apple in the daily-activity sequence. Rest tremor was assessed just before movement, with hands resting on the table. Measurable rest tremor coexisted with peak-dose dyskinesia in 12.7% of the 63-person measurable-dyskinesia group. This is a prevalence estimate within a subgroup. The protocol reports no raw-grade verification or apple-specific pre/post tremor contrast. The 2019 report used some data from this study.
