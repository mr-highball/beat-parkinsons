# Baked apple and dystonia in Parkinson disease

## Baked apple crisp in a randomized Parkinson diet trial

Phillips and colleagues randomized 47 people with Parkinson disease to one of two eight-week meal plans. Forty-four started and 38 completed. The low-fat plan lists apple crisp made with baked sliced apples in a mixed recipe on printed page 69. Menu pages 30, 34 and 35 give examples of planned servings. The assignment covered the entire diet. The authors discuss asking participants whether they completed meals and state that they did not use food logs. Individual apple intake is unavailable. [Trial, including supporting material](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6175383/) · [Low-fat meal plan](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6175383.1/MDS-33-1306-s002.pdf)

Table 2 reports mean changes in the MDS-UPDRS Part IV total of −0.79 (SD 2.71) in the low-fat arm and −1.56 (SD 2.45) in the ketogenic arm, with a between-arm p-value of 0.32. Part IV includes painful OFF-state dystonia as item 4.6. The trial published the Part IV total without separate item 4.6 results or a comparison by consumption of baked apple. The reported exacerbation of dyskinesia in the ketogenic arm concerns a different clinical endpoint. ## Related apple bolus evidence

Song and colleagues used an apple puree or applesauce bolus in assessments of 34 people with genetically confirmed X-linked dystonia-parkinsonism. Pooled swallowing outcomes and descriptions of baseline oral dystonia do not estimate a change in dystonia after a baked apple exposure. This is a distinct hereditary condition.
