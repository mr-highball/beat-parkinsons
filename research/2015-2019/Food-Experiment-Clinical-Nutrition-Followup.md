# Nutritional management and symptom outcomes

## Fever during overlapping levodopa and tube feeding

Whitman and colleagues published this case online January 21, 2016. A 72-year-old with longstanding Parkinson’s developed recurrent fever around the one daily carbidopa/levodopa dose that overlapped with overnight enteral feeding, after his pneumonia had resolved. No change in mental status was noted. Fever ceased after the coinciding dose was increased. The available abstract gives feeding rate and timing, with no formula identity or test of any single ingredient. [Primary abstract, PMID 26801658](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26801658/)

## Tremor and rigidity during nasogastric feeding

Shahbazi and colleagues’ issue PDF gives December 1, 2017 as publication date. A 67-year-old patient with Parkinson’s and pneumonia received a high-protein nasogastric regimen plus Healagen, a combined arginine and glutamine supplement. Neck rigidity emerged during critical care and tremor and rigidity worsened a week later. Increasing levodopa/benserazide dosing frequency did not fully resolve the symptoms. They improved over the following days after the medicine was separated from enteral feeding. The original report provides no verified feed brand for the base regimen, no levodopa concentration and no separate test of either amino acid. [Journal record](https://jpc.tums.ac.ir/index.php/jpc/article/view/178) · [Original report, pp. 79–80](https://jpc.tums.ac.ir/index.php/jpc/article/download/178/140)

Bernier and colleagues published a different case on May 5, 2017. Gastric feeding during jejunal levodopa/carbidopa gel delivery initially required no medication adjustment. Severe gastroparesis and further aspiration pneumonia prompted a switch to jejunal feeding months later. The report leaves food composition and effects of individual ingredients unresolved. Bove and colleagues' June 27 report describes two additional Parkinson cases in which the PEG tube was used for feeding while the jejunal tube delivered levodopa gel. Its public publisher preview does not expose the full cases. These routes and drug regimens differ from the oral levodopa cases. [Bernier full report, PMID 30838290](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6353387/) · [Bove publisher preview, PMID 28676196](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1353802017302225)

## Personalized nutritional support in Parkinson’s

Ongun’s October 2, 2018 report assessed 96 people with idiopathic Parkinson’s. Sixty-five with malnutrition or at risk of it received six weeks of individualized support. Advice covered small meals, water and fiber intake, protein distribution and levodopa timing. Malnourished patients who could not meet their needs with enriched meals were offered liquid oral nutrition formulas available in vanilla, banana or chocolate flavors. The mixtures contained omega-3 fatty acids, arginine, RNA nucleotides, calcium beta-hydroxy methylbutyrate and other nutrients. The paper gives no evidence that the banana or chocolate flavors contained the named foods or were compared with one another. Some patients also received indicated micronutrients. Tube-feeding patients were excluded. [Primary report, Methods](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0205100) · [Local full text](Source-Text/PMC6168151.txt)

In the 65-person before-and-after comparison, mean UPDRS Part III changed from 20.46 to 17.53 (p = .040), HADS anxiety from 14.88 to 9.07 and depression from 14.09 to 9.20 (both p < .001). The study gives no randomized or contemporaneous untreated comparison for this phase. It reports no outcome by specific formulation, flavor or food. The publisher records restricted access to participant-level data for confidentiality.
