# Pumpkin seed oil blood-pressure and sleep trial families

## Pumpkin seed oil compared with raw seeds in postmenopausal women

NCT02727036 first posted April 4, 2016 and records 27 enrollees in an open randomized comparison of 2 g per day of pumpkin seed oil and 4.1 g per day of seeds for 12 weeks. The registry excludes people with diagnosed depression treated with antidepressants. Its brief summary mentions menopausal symptoms, while the specified outcomes cover blood pressure, lipids, C-reactive protein and endothelial function. The registry has no posted results. It labels the 2008 animal study and 2011 clinical paper as study results, but their dates and designs identify separate background studies. The 2018 conference abstract P08-074 reports no significant blood-pressure change in the oil arm and a systolic decrease from 118 to 114 mmHg in the seed arm. It gives no orthostatic symptom or depression results. The page and repository API for the 2017 dissertation returned HTTP 403. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02727036) · [Primary conference abstract P08-074](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6265820/) · [Dissertation handle](https://hdl.handle.net/11274/9763)

## Pumpkin seed oil and arterial hemodynamics

NCT03716960 first posted October 23, 2018 after the recorded June 2018 completion. Wong and colleagues’ August 2019 report describes 23 postmenopausal women with elevated blood pressure randomized to pumpkin seed oil or placebo for six weeks. The abstract reports reductions in resting brachial and central systolic pressure and wave reflection in the oil arm, with no clear stiffness or heart-rate variability change. The available abstract does not provide a prespecified between-group effect estimate for standing blood pressure or record lightheadedness. This is distinct from the 27-person Maiya trial. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03716960) · [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31445363/)

## Intranasal whole pumpkin preparation and insomnia

IRCT20180424039397N1 first posted September 19, 2018, while recruiting. The 2019 Haghjoo paper describes an intranasal preparation of whole peeled pumpkin in sesame oil. Table 3 gives Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index daytime dysfunction means of 2.1 at baseline and 0.7 after treatment, compared with 1.9 at both times in the liquid paraffin arm. The full PSQI score differed at baseline (p = .011). The trial enrolled people with chronic insomnia, excluded Parkinson’s and other neurological conditions, and did not test toasted pumpkin seed oil or record dream enactment. [Official registry](https://irct.ir/trial/31300) · [Primary paper, Methods and Table 3](https://jppres.com/jppres/pdf/vol7/jppres19.654_7.6.471.pdf)

Figure 1 reports 100 randomized and 74 analyzed, with 35 preparation and 39 paraffin participants. The mean daytime-dysfunction improvement was 1.33 ± 0.75 points versus 0.00 ± 0.23 (p < .001). This PSQI component combines trouble staying awake with difficulty maintaining enthusiasm. The paper gives no separate sleepiness item. The reported April–August 2017 recruitment interval precedes the September 2018 first public registry posting. [Primary Figure 1 and Table 3](https://jppres.com/jppres/pdf/vol7/jppres19.654_7.6.471.pdf)

## Oral peanut oil comparator in the ENHANce trial registration

NCT03649698 first posted August 28, 2018. The trial registered peanut-oil softgels as the oral omega-3 placebo in older adults with sarcopenia. Registered secondary assessments include falls and attention. The 2026 feasibility report confirms the placebo in four arms and identifies Parkinson’s among the screening exclusions. Its principal results and endpoint distinctions are filed with the 2026 report. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03649698) · [2026 feasibility report note](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Pumpkin-Peanut-Oil.md#oral-peanut-oil-comparator-and-physical-function-in-older-adults)

## Pumpkin seed oil coupled to ultrasound and pelvic pain

Tantawy and colleagues randomized 60 men with chronic nonbacterial prostatitis among oil-coupled transperineal ultrasound, active ultrasound and sham. The September 18, 2018 report’s Table 2 gives mean NIH-CPSI pain changes from 17.20 to 3.71, 17.80 to 5.21 and 16.90 to 14.62, respectively. The simple additional reduction over active ultrasound was 0.90 points. The table does not give pain-subscore-specific contrast uncertainty. Pain improved with both active ultrasound procedures. The active gel description gives conflicting day and week units for treatment duration. The oil was pharmaceutical grade with no pressing method reported. This local intervention leaves oral exposure, pressing and Parkinson pain untested.
