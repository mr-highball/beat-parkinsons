# Mango fruit, sleep and fatigue

## Ataulfo pulp and peel beverages during exercise

ClinicalTrials.gov posted NCT03182543 on 9 June 2017, after study completion in April 2017. The record lists 19 healthy male university students in a randomized crossover comparing an Ataulfo mango beverage containing 200 g pulp, a second beverage with 160 g pulp and 40 g peel, and water. All conditions used water and an incremental cycle test two hours after ingestion. Registered outcomes include antioxidant capacity, oxidative markers, blood lactate, and postprandial glucose. Neither patient-reported nor measured fatigue severity appears among the registered outcomes. The registry has no posted results and provides no preparation details sufficient to establish consumption as raw fruit. ## Fatigue with the recalled use of boiled whole mango tea

Pinto and colleagues presented a 53-year-old woman with fatigue, anorexia, epigastric pain and abnormal liver tests in October 2017. She recalled drinking tea made by boiling the whole mango three to five times a week in the month before presentation. She also reported intermittent Excedrin. The authors assigned a RUCAM/CIOMS score of 7 for suspected liver injury related to the exposure and described improvement after cessation. Fatigue was a complaint during liver injury, with no severity scale, cultivar, Parkinson diagnosis, separate fruit arm or raw fruit exposure. [Pinto primary poster text](https://www.academia.edu/90688891/A_Rare_Case_of_Mango_Tea_associated_Hepatotoxicity) · [Conference program](https://acgcdn.gi.org/acgblog/uploads/2017/09/ACG2017_FinalProgram_9-13.pdf) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2017-Pinto-Boiled-Mango-Tea-Hepatotoxicity-Extract.txt)

## Mango servings and sleep duration in the UK cohort

Noorwali, Hardie and Cade published the prospective UK Women's Cohort Study on 20 November 2018. The full analysis covered 13,958 women with about four years of follow-up, with 6,930 included for the adjusted mango estimate. Baseline mango intake came from an FFQ. Sleep duration came from self-reported weekday and weekend minutes at follow-up. Table 2 estimated −6 minutes of sleep per additional 80 g/day of mangoes after adjustment for age, socioeconomic status, smoking, ethnicity and total energy intake (99% CI −17 to +4, reported p=.1). No cultivar, time of mango consumption, preparation, Parkinson cohort or scored insomnia endpoint is given.
