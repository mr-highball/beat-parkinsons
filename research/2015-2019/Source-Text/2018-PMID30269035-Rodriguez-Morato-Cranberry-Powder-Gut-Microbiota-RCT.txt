Title: Cranberries attenuate animal-based diet-induced changes in microbiota composition and functionality: a randomized crossover controlled feeding trial.
PMID: 30269035
DOI: 10.1016/j.jnutbio.2018.08.019
First published: 2018
Authors: Rodríguez-Morató J, Matthan NR, Liu J, de la Torre R, Chen CO
Journal: The Journal of nutritional biochemistry
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30269035/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled crossover feeding trial (Rodriguez-Morato et al., 2018, J Nutr Biochem) in 11 healthy adults consuming an animal-based diet for 5 days with 30 g/day freeze-dried whole cranberry powder vs placebo powder. Cranberry powder attenuated animal-diet declines in fecal short-chain fatty acids (acetate/butyrate), secondary bile acid increases, and microbiota shifts across 9 taxonomic clades, without measuring clinical constipation severity or stool frequency. Complemented by Straub et al. 2021 (PMID:33596852) and Nieman et al. 2024 (PMID:39408218) finding minimal global gut microbiome shifts.
