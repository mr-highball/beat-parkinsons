Publication: Martins et al., Cephalalgia, first published August 9, 2019. DOI 10.1177/0333102419869319. Registration NCT02570633.
Original: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0333102419869319
Coverage: Original Table 2, Methods, and adverse-event section, summarized in factual text.

The double-blind migraine trial randomized 53 to encapsulated Zingiber officinale extract and 54 to placebo. In Table 2, day-90 median Beck Anxiety Inventory score was 5.0 (range 0–27) with ginger and 3.0 (range 0–22) with placebo. The reported comparison gives p = .46. Fourteen ginger and eight placebo participants dropped out before completing treatment. The ginger group adverse-event list includes one constipation report, with no corresponding event in the placebo list. Neither constipation severity nor actual intake of grated ginger was measured. The clinical cohort had migraine, and an isolated treatment effect on Parkinson symptoms cannot be inferred.
