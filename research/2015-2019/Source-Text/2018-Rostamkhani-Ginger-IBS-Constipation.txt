Title: The Effect of Ginger on the Symptoms of Irritable Bowel Syndrome: A Randomized Clinical Trial
Registry: IRCT2017041633467N1
Source: https://jmp.ir/article-1-2297-en.pdf
Published: 2018-12-10, Journal of Medicinal Plants 17(68), 112–122.
Archive scope: Factual summary of original Persian Table 4 on page 117 and study methods.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Rostamkhani and colleagues randomized 64 adults with IBS, 32 per arm, to 1 g per day of ginger in capsules or placebo for four weeks. The constipation domain of the Gastrointestinal Symptom Rating Scale averaged 2.85 ± 1.11 before ginger and 1.09 ± 0.94 afterward. The corresponding placebo values were 2.35 ± 1.35 and 1.80 ± 1.24. Table 4 reports separate Mann–Whitney p-values of .271 at baseline and .016 at follow-up. The article's adjusted model applies to the overall gastrointestinal total. No comparable adjustment is supplied for this constipation domain.

The crude difference of mean changes is −1.21 scale points, with no derived confidence interval. Baseline scores differed by 0.50 points. The same-outcome result addresses ginger in adults with IBS. The dose form and cohort differ from grated ginger and Parkinson disease. The article quotes this registration and belongs to a separate family from the 2025 ginger and Zataria study.
