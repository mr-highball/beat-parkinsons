Title: Formulation and Evaluation of an Iranian Traditional Dosage Form Containing Linum and Ficus for Improvement of Functional Constipation
Source: https://brieflands.com/journals/jjnpp/articles/55321
DOI: 10.5812/jjnpp.40069
Earlier DOI: 10.17795/jjnpp-40069
Publication date: 2016-11-07, first online date. The journal issue is dated 2017.
Citation: Tofighi et al. Jundishapur Journal of Natural Pharmaceutical Products 12(4), e40069.
Text coverage: Selected paraphrased methods, comparison and findings from the complete publisher article and Table 2.

The paper describes an Iranian-tradition-inspired caplet combining flaxseed powder with freeze-dried extract prepared from Ficus carica fruit in hot water. This is an extracted two-ingredient product with no whole-dried-fig comparison. In the open two-week pilot, 15 adults aged 22–53 received the same caplet and completed bowel symptom questionnaires. There was no separate control group.

At two weeks, the reported defecation-frequency score increased from 0.0 to 3.0 under the table's scoring. Pain during evacuation decreased from 97.0 to 7.0 on its 0–100 scale. Both comparisons are reported as P<.01 against baseline. The report also describes lower stool hardness and less retentive posturing. These paired observations cannot distinguish a product effect from temporal changes or separate flaxseed's contribution from the extract. The study did not address a Parkinson visual symptom.

The full text names Kim et al.'s 2010 conference abstract. The related excerpt of that abstract reports within-arm improvements as well as no clear between-arm defecation differences. The two article DOIs here refer to early and issue versions of the same pilot.
