The authors identify Table 1 as measurements from their laboratory. The table summarizes locally obtained foods that were powdered after freeze drying. It reports mean concentrations from at least three samples per food using liquid chromatography and mass spectrometry. The reported ergothioneine values are 4.92 mg/kg dry weight for basil leaves and 2.60 mg/kg dry weight for cumin.

Table 1 establishes an analyte measurement in these samples. It provides no cultivar or species authentication for the named foods, no side-by-side test of culinary preparations, no intake estimate and no Parkinson symptom measures. Dry-weight sample composition alone supplies no exposure equivalence to purified ergothioneine in a clinical study. The separate mild cognitive impairment pilot reports a verbal-learning result after supplement use. The pilot uses neither basil nor cumin and does not establish an executive function effect in Parkinson disease.
