Title: Gut microbiota in Parkinson's disease: temporal stability and relations to disease progression
PMID: 31221587
PMCID: PMC6606744
DOI: 10.1016/j.ebiom.2019.05.064
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6606744/
Supplement: https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6606744.1/mmc1.pdf
Publication: 2019-06, EBioMedicine 44, pp. 691–707
Coverage: Original article, dietary results and Tables S1 and S3, with the associated analysis document checked for fig and sialorrhea variables.

Aho and colleagues compared 64 people with Parkinson disease and 64 matched controls in a microbiome follow-up. They used the Sialorrhea Clinical Scale for Parkinson's Disease and collected a 163-item food-frequency questionnaire at follow-up. The published supplement describes 171 analyzed food variables, including grouped variables. Tables S1 and S3 include broad fruit and fruit-juice measures. These results concern diagnosis or microbiome comparisons, and the sialorrhea score appears as a separate potential covariate.

The public analysis document imports a separate dietary input table and derives food labels from its columns. No standalone raw fig comparison or fig-by-sialorrhea estimate was found in the inspected article and supplement. The complete case-level clinical metadata is restricted. The published sequence accession supplies microbiome reads and does not establish food-item or outcome linkage.

The cited 1988 Pietinen validation used 44 questions. The 2019 methods describe their 163-item instrument as based on that earlier work. The earlier study does not establish the exact item list used in this cohort. See research/1985-1989/Source-Text/1988-PMID2843041-Pietinen-Finnish-Food-Questionnaire.txt.
