Title: The beneficial effects of berries on cognition, motor behaviour and neuronal function in ageing

Forty-two male 19-month-old Fischer 344 rats were randomized, 14 per group, to unsupplemented NIH-31-based feed, 2% freeze-dried strawberry powder or 2% freeze-dried blueberry powder for eight weeks. Before completion, two control, one blueberry and two strawberry animals were euthanized after weight loss. This left 12, 13 and 12, respectively. The authors used the time before the animal left an apparatus as a motor task result.

On the stationary rod the strawberry group fell sooner than the blueberry group (pairwise p<.05), with an overall group effect F(2,34)=4.30, p<.05. On the large plank the strawberry group stayed longer than the blueberry group (pairwise p<.05, overall F(2,34)=4.52, p<.05). Blueberry animals also turned around more frequently on the plank. On the rotarod the strawberry arm did not differ from either other arm. The authors reported a blueberry-control post hoc difference with an overall group test p=.10. Fig. 1 shows shared significance letters for strawberry and control in all three assays. Relative to control, the plotted strawberry mean is shorter on the stationary rod and longer on the rotarod and plank. Exact group means and intervals are unavailable in the text. The remaining motor comparisons were not different by group.

The discussion and USDA synopsis combine berry and task outcomes in a broad statement about motor performance. The separate strawberry results above preserve the actual exposure and comparator. The study uses freeze-dried strawberry powder in normally aging animals, and the apparatus measures latency to an induced fall rather than recurrence of spontaneous falls.
