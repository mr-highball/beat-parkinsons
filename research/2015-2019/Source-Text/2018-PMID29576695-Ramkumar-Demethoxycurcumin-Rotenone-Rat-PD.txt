Study design and exposure
- Animal model: Male Wistar rats randomized into 6 groups: control, rotenone (0.5 mg/kg/day i.p. in sunflower oil for 7 days), rotenone + oral demethoxycurcumin (DMC, a natural turmeric curcuminoid, at 5, 10, or 20 mg/kg body weight), and DMC 20 mg/kg alone, followed by chronic evaluation at the effective dose.
- Assessed behavioral motor outcomes alongside striatal dopamine concentration, oxidative stress indices, and neuroinflammatory protein expression.

Key findings
- Rotenone induced motor dysfunction, striatal dopamine depletion, oxidative stress, and elevated inflammatory marker expression.
- Oral administration of demethoxycurcumin attenuated rotenone-induced behavioral motor deficits, preserved striatal dopamine levels, and reduced oxidative and neuroinflammatory markers.

Relevance and limits for Parkinson's food experiments
- Provides preclinical oral curcuminoid motor evidence relevant to ground turmeric and bradykinesia (0507).
- Limits: Isolated single curcuminoid derivative (demethoxycurcumin) rather than culinary ground turmeric; rotenone rat model without human Parkinson clinical bradykinesia measurement.
