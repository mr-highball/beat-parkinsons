Title: Effects of High Consumption of Vegetables on Clinical, Immunological, and Antioxidant Markers in Subjects at Risk of Cardiovascular Diseases
PMID: 30402206
PMCID: PMC6196889
DOI: 10.1155/2018/5417165
First public publication: 2018-10-08
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6196889/
Format: Factual extraction from the original main text, Methods and food-allocation table.
Peluso and colleagues randomized 38 adults at cardiovascular risk after a run-in, with 19 per arm for four weeks. The higher-vegetable diet prescribed 4,200 g per week including 800 g of red cabbage. The lower-vegetable diet prescribed 800 g per week including 152 g of red cabbage. Both also supplied carrots, Jerusalem artichoke, tomatoes and peppers. The assigned comparison varied total vegetables and several foods at once. The published measures concern metabolic, immune and antioxidant markers and platelet activation. The main report gives no blood pressure or standing-symptom measurement and does not establish a specific isolated raw-cabbage effect.
