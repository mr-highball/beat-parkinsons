Title: Consumption of fig fruits grown in Oman can improve memory, anxiety, and learning skills in a transgenic mice model of Alzheimer's disease
PMID: 24938828
DOI: 10.1179/1476830514Y.0000000131
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24938828/
Publisher: https://doi.org/10.1179/1476830514Y.0000000131
Publication date: 2016 Dec (journal issue and NLM date; the institution's 2014 research report lists an earlier online date).
Coverage: Primary NLM abstract and bibliographic record. The exact preparation and numerical tables for this article remain open.

The original abstract describes 4% figs in the diets of transgenic Alzheimer-model mice, with memory, learning, motor coordination and anxiety-related behavior assessed using maze, rotarod and open-field tasks. It reports better performance in fig-supplemented mice on the named tasks relative to transgenic controls. The abstract does not describe ordinary dried figs or an assay of visual hallucinations. The separate 2015 biomarkers publication used frozen and ground fruit. Its preparation and animal lineage cannot be assigned to this behavioral publication without its own methods.
