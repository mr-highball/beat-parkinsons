Title: Metabolic recovery from heavy exertion following banana compared to sugar beverage or water only ingestion: A randomized, crossover trial.
PMID: 29566095
PMCID: PMC5864065
DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0194843
Registry ID: NCT02994628
First published: 2018
Authors: Nieman DC, Gillitt ND, Sha W, Esposito D, Ramamoorthy S
Journal: PloS one
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29566095/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

4-arm randomized crossover trial (PMC5864065, NCT02994628) in n=20 trained cyclists (14 men, 6 women; 2-week washouts) completing four 75-km cycling time trials while ingesting water only (WAT), raw Cavendish bananas (CAV), raw mini-yellow bananas (MIY, 63% higher phenolics), or a 6% sugar beverage (SUG) at 0.2 g/kg carbohydrate every 15 min. Performance time (178-184 min) and RPE (13.8 WAT, 13.8 CAV, 13.2 MIY, 13.6 SUG) did not differ. Cavendish banana yielded slightly higher watts than water (208+/-8.2 vs 195+/-9.3 W, p<0.05) and significantly attenuated the post-exercise Energy level drop on a 12-point Likert scale (-1.9+/-0.7 vs -3.6+/-0.7 for water, p<0.05; MIY -2.4+/-0.5 NS; SUG -2.4+/-0.4 NS). Post-exercise muscle cramping was significantly lower with CAV (1.8+/-0.5), MIY (2.0+/-0.4), and SUG (2.3+/-0.4) vs water (3.7+/-0.6, all p<0.05). Delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS, 1-10) was lower at 1.5 h post-exercise for MIY and SUG vs water (interaction p=0.014). Feeling full and bloating were higher with CAV and MIY vs water (p<0.05); diarrhea and nausea/vomiting did not differ. Ex vivo THP-1 monocyte COX-2 mRNA at 21 h post-exercise was significantly lower with both banana trials vs water/SUG. Limits: open-label acute exercise stress in trained non-PD athletes; benefits largely matched 6% sugar beverage.
