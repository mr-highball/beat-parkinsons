Title: Cooking affects glucosinolate concentration in mustard leaves and seeds
Source: https://doi.org/10.26534/kimika.v30i2.39-48
Journal reprint: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339293392_Cooking_Affects_Glucosinolate_Concentration_in_Mustard_Leaves_and_Seeds
First published online: 2019-12-01 (Crossref; publisher issue is 2019)
DOI: 10.26534/kimika.v30i2.39-48
Text coverage: original journal text, including the results and Table 2, accessed via the preserved reprint.

Malabed and colleagues measured total glucosinolates of 86.18 micromoles per gram in raw black mustard seed (Brassica nigra) and 33.70 micromoles per gram after roasting. Their separate unhydrolyzed total isothiocyanate comparison shows no significant change. The results differ by chemical class and assay treatment. Boiled Brassica juncea leaves in the same report are a different plant part and experiment. This preparation study supplies a reason to keep roasted and unheated seed exposure separate. There was no human administration or tremor measurement.
