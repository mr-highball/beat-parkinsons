Title: A review of herbal therapy in multiple sclerosis
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6311642/
PMID: 30607330
PMCID: PMC6311642
DOI: 10.15171/apb.2018.066
Publication: 2018-11-29
Coverage: Factual extract. Official PMC XML main article, use table and references.

The review is about multiple sclerosis. Its treatment-use table names Crocus sativus under antidepressant use, Piper methysticum and Valeriana officinalis under sleep problems, and Ginkgo biloba and Panax ginseng under fatigue. The saffron section pairs a Crocus–imipramine depression trial with rodent experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis papers on other outcomes. These references do not establish a controlled Parkinson depression, fatigue, rigidity or insomnia result. Traditional sedative descriptions supply no measured insomnia endpoint.

Ginger extract and shogaol appear under inflammation, including autoimmune encephalomyelitis. Turmeric and curcumin appear under immune signaling and model disease severity. Neither supplies the named grated preparation with a separate Parkinson rigidity, pain or anxiety endpoint. The grape section concerns resveratrol and other grape constituents, rather than raw or frozen fruit comparisons for anxiety or rigidity. Cranberry juice and capsules are discussed for urinary tract outcomes. Green tea and EGCG appear under inflammation and neurobiology. The cited multiple-sclerosis fatigue comparisons are for different herbs, and no separate brewed-tea fatigue result appears.
