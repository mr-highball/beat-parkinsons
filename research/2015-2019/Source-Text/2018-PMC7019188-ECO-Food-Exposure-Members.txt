Text coverage: Distilled member notes from the original PMC HTML body. The 944 presentations, posters and other member entries span the opening plenaries through T4P237, with one additional author-index record. All 17 late-breaking index IDs have a body heading, including the TIPLB2 spelling of T1PLB2. The author index ends on printed page 358. The article-level metadata lists 1–364, while the publisher PDF footer identifies 1–358. This record uses the authenticated HTML member boundaries.

T2P217. A beverage survey included black tea, coffee and kefir. The methods list 983 adults, whereas the table's sex counts total 1,000. The study compared beverage intake by sex and provided no walking, falls or fatigue outcomes. T2P75 described habits including sweetened Turkish or instant coffee. T4P192 presented coffee, tea or water with a cheese test meal, without a separate coffee effect. T4P95 included coffee as a covariate in a liver-fat model.

T1P147. In 90 adults with severe obesity, the conference dysbiosis analysis reported an association with coffee (p = 0.026), with no coffee effect estimate for fatigue. The subsequent article repeats the sample and evaluates the index in an additional group. Its separate comparison of non-nutritive sweeteners includes sweeteners used in tea or coffee, so those exposures must stay distinct. T2P126 describes the Danish MONICA coffee and adiposity analysis of up to 2,128 adults. The full report was published online in December 2017. Weak concurrent associations were not followed by consistent prospective associations.

T1P123. Ovariectomized rats on a metabolic-syndrome regimen received berry polyphenol extracts. The authors report lower triglycerides in the blueberry, cranberry and cowberry groups than in the saline group. Only blueberry is marked significant for LDL in the displayed table. The text lists 18 rats given cranberry but the table lists 17; the route wording also varies between catheter administration and injection. There is no raw blueberry, dried cranberry, constipation or depressive-symptom comparison.

T2P44. The students' dietary inventory lists broccoli, eggs, orange juice and zucchini among the foods considered for carotenoid exposure. The only parsley statement says no participant in the obesity subgroup consumed it. No separate parsley result is reported. The assessment was macular pigment optical density, with no confirmed association across the dietary sources. T2P112 lists broccoli, cauliflower and kale while estimating indole-3-carbinol intakes. Neither supplies a separate vegetable effect or a Parkinson endpoint. T4P235 used food-related IgG markers in a test of several foods, including blueberry, broccoli, orange and potato. It supplies no clinical food challenge or measured symptom response.

T2P4, T2P19 and T2P79. Poultry appears in a model menu-cost comparison, chicken in a label-use survey, and poultry within a combined food group in a dietary questionnaire. The label and diet surveys also include eggs. T2P36 includes eggs in a nutrition knowledge question, T2P53 records adolescent intake, and T2P74 incorporates eggs in a combined adult dietary pattern. These are not feeding studies of poached chicken or eggs and give no matching symptom results.

T1P44. Isolated resveratrol in male rats was tested with an energy-restriction comparator in a brown-adipose experiment. This is a grape-associated constituent with a separate experimental exposure and endpoint. T4P206 likens the appearance of açaí to a grape, which is not a grape exposure.

T4P189. A review of green tea or its extracts reports a pooled body-weight difference of −0.99 kg (95% CI −2.40 to 0.43) across 19 studies. The pooled body-composition interval also spans zero despite the favorable concluding language. The review gives no isolated brewed green-tea fatigue result.

T1P104. A structured lipid made from olive oil, soy oil and hydrogenated crambe oil was studied in mice. The modified fat is distinct from culinary olive oil. T1P127 compares soy-oil control, a normolipidic olive-oil diet and a high-fat olive-oil diet in mice. The high-fat contrast changes the fat level as well as the matrix. Reported outcomes are hepatic proteins, intake, body weight and metabolic measures. T4P152 concerns olive leaf and oleuropein, rather than a clinical olive-oil intervention.

T1P158. In a crossover study of 18 healthy men, potato-pulp fiber increased satiety ratings relative to control buns. The fiber preparations did not significantly change fecal fat or observed energy intake. No sleep outcome was measured. Other members record potatoes in shopping, diet and fried-snack categories, without a roasted-potato insomnia comparison. NCT02957318 and PMID 33203008 are the same crossover family.

T4P140. A randomized fish-oil versus safflower-oil crossover in adults with abdominal obesity reported different lipid outcomes for the two oils. The conference report describes eight-week periods and lists 38 participants, but its sex subgroup counts add to 34. Later linked publications and the registry clarify the planned versus reported interventions and the dose units. No falls, anxiety or swallowing endpoint was reported.

T4P199. A 20-person before-and-after study combined fish oil with dietary salmon over six months. Authors report lower triglycerides and interleukin-6 and higher adiponectin. Salmon was bundled with the supplement and the program did not measure anxiety.

T1P52 and T1P75. Mouse diets used high-oleic sunflower oil at 40% of the mixture, with palm oil or standard chow as comparators. T1P52 measures adipose gene expression. In T1P75 the palm-oil group showed poorer juvenile Y-maze alternation, but the sunflower group was not reported to differ from standard chow on that test. Later high-fat studies using sunflower oil are linked separately, including a radial-maze result in both sexes. There is no documented cold-pressed culinary preparation or Parkinson executive outcome.

T2P151. A 45-person PCOS study compared weight-loss diet with added curcumin or white bean extract and a diet-only group. The report describes within-group biochemical changes and no significant separation of weight loss among groups. It was an extract study without Parkinson outcomes. T3P37 tested preference for shapes and colours of bread in children. Turmeric provided the yellow colour, and the presentation reports less frequent selection of the yellow version. It is a sensory exposure without a swallowing or other symptom result.

The date/date-fruit search returned calendar and timing language. There was no member about an edible date preparation. Colour, acronym and lookalike matches remain distinct from the actual food observations.
