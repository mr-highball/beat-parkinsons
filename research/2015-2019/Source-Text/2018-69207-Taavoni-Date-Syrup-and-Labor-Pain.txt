Title: The effect of oral date syrup on severity of labor pain in nulliparous women
Source: https://brieflands.com/journals/semj/articles/69207
DOI: 10.5812/semj.69207
First online: 2018-12-26
Registration reported in the article: IRCT201405182172N16
Coverage: Paraphrase of original Methods and Table 2, matched to the conference abstract and related reports.

The Khorramabad comparison assigned 80 first-time mothers in active labor to date syrup or routine water. The report describes card-based, paired allocation and a masked pain recorder. It does not establish masking of participants to the obviously different drinks. Table 2 gives no clear difference at 30 minutes and lower syrup-group VAS means from 60 to 150 minutes. At 60 minutes, means were 7.77 and 9.05 on a 0–10 scale (p=.001). An uncertainty interval for the difference and per-timepoint analysis counts are unreported. Water does not control for taste, energy or expectation.

The original 64-person Persian report is distilled in research/2015-2019/Source-Text/2019-DOI3111618-Fathi-Date-Syrup-and-Labor-Pain.txt. The separate honey report in research/2015-2019/Source-Text/2019-PMID30858948-Honey-Syrup-and-Shared-Labor-Trial.txt shares the same registration. Five of the six control means and SDs match between the English date and honey reports. Preserve the discrepant 90-minute row and uncertainty about participant-level overlap.

The 2017 80-person conference abstract and the Persian labor report with the same registration belong in this trial family. The Persian report’s stated sample is 64. A separate honey comparison also cites the same registration and grant contract. A shared protocol or control group remains possible. Retain these companion reports together when judging independence.

Research gap. Match the original registry allocation, arm counts and any repeated-control use. The trial studied acute obstetric pain and a processed drink. A transferable whole dried-date and Parkinson pain comparison remains open.
