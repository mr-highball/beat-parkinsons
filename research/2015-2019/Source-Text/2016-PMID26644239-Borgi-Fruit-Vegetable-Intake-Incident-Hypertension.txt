Title: Fruit and Vegetable Consumption and the Incidence of Hypertension in Three Prospective Cohort Studies.
PMID: 26644239
PMCID: PMC5350612
DOI: 10.1161/HYPERTENSIONAHA.115.06497
First published: 2016
Authors: Borgi L, Muraki I, Satija A, Willett WC, Rimm EB, Forman JP
Journal: Hypertension
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26644239/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Prospective cohort analysis across NHS (n=62,175), NHSII (n=88,475), and HPFS (n=36,803; total n=187,453 adults) examining individual whole fruits and vegetables (>=4 servings/week vs <1/month) and incident hypertension. Higher cantaloupe intake was associated with slightly higher hypertension risk (pooled HR 1.07, 95% CI 1.01-1.13, p-trend=0.002). Higher carrot intake was associated with lower incident hypertension (HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.91-0.99, p-trend=0.003). Cauliflower showed a non-significant upward trend (HR 1.06, 95% CI 0.99-1.14). General-population hypertension incidence; no Parkinson orthostatic hypotension or blood-pressure symptom measurement.
