Ochiai et al. original article and Figure 1
Public source https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6261156/
PMID 30510754, PMC6261156, DOI 10.1002/fsn3.861

First published 2018-10-19. Effect of chlorogenic acids on fatigue and sleep in healthy males. Extraction and processing reduced the caffeine content. Figure 1 uses visual analogue ratings at specified weekday windows. The article reports a difference late in week two and supplies the distributions graphically. No exact numerical treatment estimate is transcribed. Waking fatigue and separately measured sleep outcomes belong to the same 15-person analysis.

Ochiai and colleagues randomized 16 healthy daytime-working men in a crossover, with 15 included in analysis. The active drink contained 300 mg of chlorogenic acids extracted from green Coffea canephora beans in 100 mL. Each period lasted 13 days and a one-week washout separated periods. Figure 1 reports no clear difference in fatigue on awakening early in week one and lower ratings with the active drink late in week two. The selected outcome concerns waking fatigue after an extract beverage in healthy workers. The 2026 Umeda trial used a distinct parallel-group cohort and dose.
