Registration: ACTRN12619000807156
Title: A Clinical Trial to Test a Modified Traditional Chinese Herbal Medicine for the Treatment of Endometriosis
First registered: 3 June 2019; current record last updated 9 October 2023
Official record: https://anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=377571
Linked protocol: Armour et al., Trials 22:299, 21 April 2021, PMID 33883001, DOI 10.1186/s13063-021-05265-x; https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-021-05265-x
Coverage: official registry current fields and published protocol, no outcome results.

The prospectively registered double-blind trial tests Gynoclear against matched placebo in women aged 18–45 with surgically confirmed endometriosis. The current registry states completed, final enrollment 93, first enrollment 11 June 2019, last data collection 28 August 2023. No BMI criterion was found in the official record. Six capsules daily for 12 weeks include 92 mg cinnamon per capsule alongside poria, safflower, tree peony, peony and red sage. The protocol identifies Cinnamomum cassia; the record does not identify culinary ground bark or isolate its effects.

Primary outcome is endometriosis-related pelvic pain from the Endometriosis Pain Daily Diary. Secondary fatigue is a composite of the diary and Fatigue Severity Scale (FSS) at baseline, three months and one month post-intervention. The protocol describes a 0–10 daily fatigue severity rating and FSS of nine seven-point items with a seven-day recall. Its abstract says two months of treatment, whereas the current registry specifies 12 weeks and later timepoints; preserve this discrepancy. Neither the official record nor the linked protocol supplies numeric fatigue results. The registry lists the protocol, not a separate clinical results publication; completion is not evidence of an effect. A targeted title and identifier web search did not locate a matched results paper, but this does not establish that none exists.
