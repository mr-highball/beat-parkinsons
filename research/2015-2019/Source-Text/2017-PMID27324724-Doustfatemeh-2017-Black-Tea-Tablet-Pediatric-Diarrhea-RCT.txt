Title: The Effect of Black Tea (Camellia sinensis (L) Kuntze) on Pediatrics With Acute Nonbacterial Diarrhea: A Randomized Controlled Trial.
PMID: 27324724
PMCID: PMC5871214
DOI: 10.1177/2156587216654600
First published: 2017
Authors: Doustfatemeh S, Imanieh MH, Mohagheghzade A, Zarshenas MM, Torkamani Z, Yousefi G, et al.
Journal: Journal of evidence-based complementary & alternative medicine
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5871214/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Single-blind randomized controlled trial in n=120 children (aged 2-12 y; 60 black tea tablet + standard care vs 60 standard care alone) with acute nonbacterial diarrhea. At 24 h, the black tea tablet group had higher formed-stool proportion (p<0.001) and lower defecation frequency (p<0.001). Limits: acute pediatric diarrhea (opposite pathophysiology to chronic adult PD constipation) and concentrated tablet rather than brewed black tea.
