Title: The feasibility and acceptability of using a novel wrist worn cueing device to self-manage drooling problems in people with Parkinson’s disease: A pilot study
Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2055668319852529
PMID: 31662884
PMCID: PMC6796203
DOI: 10.1177/2055668319852529
Publication date: 2019-10-15
Coverage: Paraphrased original methods, Results, Tables 1–3 and limitations.

Thirty-eight people consented, 28 contributed to the analyzed pilot and 24 remained at follow-up. The analyzed immediate and delayed groups numbered 17 and 11. The study describes a randomized delayed start, with two final participants assigned to the immediate group to finish recruitment. In the first comparison Table 2 reports a diary-severity difference (p=.010) and less conclusive estimates for duration (p=.150), frequency (p=.061), ROMP–Saliva (p=.619) and UPDRS item 2.2 (p=.225).

After both groups received wrist vibration prompts, the pooled within-person median diary severity changed from 3.14 to 1.18 (p=.031), with a median of 1.14 at follow-up. Pooled ROMP–Saliva comparisons remained inconclusive, and 22 people reported a perceived benefit in the exit interviews. Pooled measurements and interviews describe the same study participants and do not independently confirm the initial comparison. The exposure is an external reminder to swallow. The later CueBand protocol PMC9955579 cites this pilot as antecedent work under a separate study.
