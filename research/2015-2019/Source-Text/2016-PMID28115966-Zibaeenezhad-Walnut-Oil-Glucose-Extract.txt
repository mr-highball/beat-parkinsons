Title: The Effect of Walnut Oil Consumption on Blood Sugar in Patients With Diabetes Mellitus Type 2
PMID: 28115966
PMCID: PMC5219895
DOI: 10.5812/ijem.34889
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5219895/
First published: 2016-07-24.
Coverage: Factual extraction of complete original Methods/Results and Table 2.
Reviewed: 2026-11-17

The trial randomized 100 adults with type 2 diabetes; 45 per group were analyzed after first-cold-pressed walnut oil 15 g/day versus no intervention for three months. HbA1c changed from 7.00 (SD1.08) to 6.37 (1.29), versus 6.97 (1.21) to 6.98 (1.33); baseline-adjusted p=.005. Systolic/diastolic blood-pressure comparisons were inconclusive, p=.556/.788.

Two oil-arm participants withdrew for gastrointestinal intolerance; three more for dietary/noncompliance reasons. Table 2 and narrative interchange glucose/HbA1c percentage-change and within-group p-value labels. Registration IRCT2014022216682N1 and ethics CT-P-92-5670 match the later 2019 glycemic report. Paper recruitment May–June 2013 conflicts with the registry's expected March 2014 dates; actual registry dates are blank.

The 2017 lipid paper names another registration and ethics approval. Similar summaries do not prove shared participants or independence. No Parkinson symptom, pain or cognition outcome was measured; metabolic changes cannot be substituted for one.
