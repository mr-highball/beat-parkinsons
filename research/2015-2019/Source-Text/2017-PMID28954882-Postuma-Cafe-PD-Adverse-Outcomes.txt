Title: Caffeine as symptomatic treatment for Parkinson disease (Café-PD): A randomized trial
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5664303/

Date basis: First online date. The journal issue is dated October 24, 2017.
Coverage: Paraphrased primary article, including methods, tolerability and secondary outcomes. Supplemental adverse-event Table e-1 remains access-limited.

The double-blind capsule trial randomized 60 people with Parkinson disease to caffeine and 61 to placebo. The primary analysis included 57 versus 61. At six months the motor exam contrast was −0.48 points on MDS-UPDRS Part III (95% CI −3.21 to 2.25). This total does not report resting tremor separately.

Dyskinesia, a secondary measure combining MDS-UPDRS items 4.1 and 4.2, was 0.28 points higher with caffeine at three months (95% CI 0.01 to 0.55), 0.12 points higher at six months (−0.17 to 0.41), and 0.25 points higher in the model combining all time points (0.04 to 0.45). The same trial reported a six-month daytime somnolence item 0.49 points lower (−0.79 to −0.20). The nighttime sleep item moved 0.28 points higher (−0.06 to 0.62). These measures distinguish motor complications, daytime sleepiness and nighttime sleep.

Adverse events were reported by 29 caffeine and 31 placebo participants, and 7 versus 5 stopped the intervention because of side effects. The individual-event table is a separate supplement. The main article contains no standalone fatigue estimate. The randomized exposure was a capsule, with participants asked to maintain their background caffeine intake. These results supply no isolated brewed-coffee or brewed-tea estimate.
