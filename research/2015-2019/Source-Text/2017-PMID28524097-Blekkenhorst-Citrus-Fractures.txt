Blekkenhorst and colleagues analyzed baseline food-frequency questionnaires from 1,468 women aged at least 70 in the Western Australian calcium-intake fracture study (CAIFOS). Oranges are pooled with other citrus fruits. The analysis excludes fruit juice and canned or frozen fruit from the estimated fruit total. The citrus median was 35.5 g/day, with an interquartile range of 5.6 to 85.9 g/day.

Hospital linkage identified 415 participants with a fracture-related admission, including 158 hip fracture admissions, during 14.5 years of follow-up. Table 6 reports adjusted hazard ratios per 20 g/day of combined citrus of 1.01 (95% CI 0.97 to 1.04, p=.710) for all fractures and 0.99 (0.94 to 1.05, p=.828) for hip fractures. Models adjusted for age, BMI, calcium assignment, diabetes, socioeconomic position, activity, smoking and dietary factors.

Evidence gap: This is a companion outcome from the same CAIFOS parent cohort as Sim et al. 2018 (PMID 30153877). Count one family overall. Fracture-related admission captures neither first fall admissions as defined in the companion paper nor recurrent fall frequency. The study does not report an isolated raw-orange contrast or a diagnosed Parkinson subgroup. Check an isolated raw-orange comparison with prospective all-cause fall counts in diagnosed Parkinson disease.
