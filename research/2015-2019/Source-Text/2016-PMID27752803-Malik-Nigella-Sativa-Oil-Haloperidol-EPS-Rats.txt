Source: Malik J, Karan M, Vasisht K / Malik S, et al. Nigella sativa Oil Reduces Extrapyramidal Symptoms (EPS)-Like Behavior in Haloperidol-Treated Rats.
Journal: Neurochemical Research (published 2016)
Identifiers: PMID:27752803 | DOI:10.1007/s11064-016-2073-z
Source URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27752803/
License note: Factual summary recorded from official NCBI PubMed metadata and abstract.

Study design and findings
- Haloperidol-induced extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS) and oral dyskinesia rat model comparing haloperidol (1 mg/kg) alone versus haloperidol co-treated with oral Nigella sativa (black cumin) seed oil (0.2 mL/rat).
- Combined Nigella sativa oil treatment significantly reduced haloperidol-induced EPS-like movement disorders and oral dyskinesia compared with haloperidol controls, alongside reduced immunohistochemical astrogliosis in the caudate and nucleus accumbens.

Relevance and limits for Parkinson's food experiments
- Independent preclinical Nigella sativa motor/EPS record supporting 0503 (ground nigella seed / bradykinesia) and 0301 context.
- Limits: Seed oil rather than culinary ground nigella seed; haloperidol rat model without item-level numeric tables accessible in abstract (full text paywalled) and no human Parkinson clinical cohort.
