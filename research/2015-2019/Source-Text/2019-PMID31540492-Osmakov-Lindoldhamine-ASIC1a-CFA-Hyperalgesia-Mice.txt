Title: Alkaloid Lindoldhamine Inhibits Acid-Sensing Ion Channel 1a and Reveals Anti-Inflammatory Properties.
PMID: 31540492
PMCID: PMC6783924
DOI: 10.3390/toxins11090542
First published: 2019
Authors: Osmakov DI, Koshelev SG, Palikov VA, Palikova YA, Shaykhutdinova ER, Dyachenko IA, et al.
Journal: Toxins
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31540492/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Electrophysiological and mouse pain study (Osmakov et al., 2019, Toxins, PMC6783924) testing lindoldhamine, a bisbenzylisoquinoline alkaloid purified from Laurus nobilis leaves. Intravenous lindoldhamine (1 mg/kg i.v.) inhibited ASIC1a currents at pH 6.5-6.85 (IC50 9-150 uM) and reversed complete Freund's adjuvant (CFA) thermal hyperalgesia/inflammation in mice, but FAILED to reduce intraperitoneal acetic-acid writhing pain. Critically, the same team showed in PMID:29277899 that lindoldhamine activates pro-nociceptive ASIC3 channels at physiological pH. Limits: injected purified single alkaloid with discordant pain-assay and ASIC-subtype effects, remote from oral ground culinary bay leaf.
