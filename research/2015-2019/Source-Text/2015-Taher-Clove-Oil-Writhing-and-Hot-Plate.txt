Injected clove oil and two mouse nociception assays

Taher and colleagues published September 1, 2015. Injected clove oil reduced acetic-acid writhing from 94.8 ± 9.79 with saline to 12.3 ± 1.28 with oil (p < .01), versus 21.2 writhes with injected aspirin. In the hot-plate assay the authors report latencies of 1.1 ± 1.77 seconds with saline, 6.2 ± 1.39 with oil and 13.3 ± 2.53 with morphine. Oil versus saline p < .05. The authors note a sedative effect at higher doses, which may influence heat-evoked behavior. Both assays used intraperitoneal clove oil and animal responses.
