Title: Diet low in FODMAPs reduces symptoms of irritable bowel syndrome as well as traditional dietary advice: a randomized controlled trial

Inspected extent: Published article and appendices, including Table 2 bowel outcomes, dietary methods, and the separate fatigue questionnaire table.

Böhn and colleagues randomized 75 adults with IBS to low-FODMAP or traditional IBS dietary advice for four weeks. Sixty-seven participants completed the trial. The overall IBS symptom score improved in each arm. The between-arm comparison was inconclusive (p=.62). In Table 2, daily stool frequency decreased from 1.9 to 1.5 in the low-FODMAP arm and from 1.6 to 1.5 in the traditional advice arm. The between-arm p-value was .64. Stool frequency, bowel dissatisfaction and the composite IBS score are separate outcomes.

The Multidimensional Fatigue Inventory general fatigue mean changed from 15.3 to 15.1 in the low-FODMAP arm (within-arm p=.84) and from 12.9 to 13.6 in the traditional advice arm (within-arm p=.62). The intervention changed multiple food groups and meal practices. The traditional advice also discussed caffeine. No result isolates brewed coffee, raw orange or a Parkinson disease subgroup. The later adherence analysis reuses the same trial family.
