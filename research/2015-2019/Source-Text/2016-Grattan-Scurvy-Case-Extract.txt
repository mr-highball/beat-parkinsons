Ryan I. Grattan and Kevin Hauck. Abstract control 2469413, An Age-Old Diagnosis With a Twist, from the 2016 SGIM proceedings, PDF pages 461–462, printed pages S545–S546. Journal of General Internal Medicine 31 supplement 2, pages 85–922, May 2016 issue. Collection DOI 10.1007/s11606-016-3657-7, PMID 27114361, PMCID PMC4870402.

The 26-year-old patient described six weeks of fatigue and rash, with bruising, gum bleeding and knee pain. His diet had become nearly exclusive fried chicken and pasta over roughly three months, and he did not recall his most recent vegetables. The case reports undetectable vitamin C without a patient-specific numeric concentration or detection limit. Its cited 0.1–0.2 mg/dL threshold is general. Hemoglobin is printed with an atypical unit as 7 mg/dL, which the proceedings leave unresolved.

The authors report symptom resolution within two weeks of starting vitamin C and relate the symptoms to scurvy in the setting of nutritional restriction. The case does not give a separate fatigue score, test poached chicken or attribute a controlled effect to chicken. No Parkinson diagnosis is reported.
