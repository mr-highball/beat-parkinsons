Title: Effect of paprika xanthophyll supplementation on oxygen uptake in athletes: a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study
Source: https://doi.org/10.7600/jpfsm.7.247
DOI: 10.7600/jpfsm.7.247
Publication date: 2018-07-18
Date basis: First J-STAGE release July 18, 2018. Journal issue July 25, 2018.
Original: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpfsm/7/4/7_247/_pdf/-char/en
Coverage: Primary randomized study and original Table 2.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26

In 14 athletes, a four-week daily paprika-xanthophyll drink was compared with placebo. The endpoint was immediate post-treadmill fatigue using a 100-mm visual analogue scale. Table 2 gives active mean ± SD of 59.1 ± 13.1 at baseline and 43.9 ± 16.2 after four weeks, with mean change −15.3 ± 7.7. Placebo values were 51.9 ± 12.4 and 53.9 ± 9.5, with mean change +2.0 ± 14.9. The text reports p<.05 for the difference in change. These are immediate postexercise measurements and several investigators report employment with the manufacturer.

The trial evaluates an isolated pigment preparation and no raw pepper arm or Parkinson everyday fatigue scale. The 2025 PapriX registry UMIN000056794 is separate and remains without publicly available everyday-fatigue values. The product supplier describes a refined and deodorized paprika extract. Product-label reports should not be counted as independent outcome studies of the four-week comparison.
