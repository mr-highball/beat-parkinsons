Title: Consumption of whole eggs promotes greater stimulation of postexercise muscle protein synthesis than consumption of isonitrogenous amounts of egg whites in young men.
PMID: 28978542
DOI: 10.3945/ajcn.117.159855
First published: 2017
Authors: van Vliet S, Shy EL, Abou Sawan S, Beals JW, West DW, Skinner SK
Journal: The American journal of clinical nutrition
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28978542/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 4 review).

Randomized crossover human metabolic trial (NCT03117127) in 10 resistance-trained young men (21 +/- 1 y) using primed continuous L-[ring-2H5]phenylalanine and L-[1-13C]leucine infusions after ingesting intrinsically L-[5,5,5-2H3]leucine-labeled whole cooked eggs (18 g protein, 17 g fat) vs isonitrogenous egg whites (18 g protein, 0 g fat). Results: total 300-minute postprandial plasma availability of dietary egg-derived leucine was high and identical between whole eggs (68% +/- 1%) and egg whites (66% +/- 2%, p=0.75), though appearance rate peaked slightly faster for egg whites (p=0.01) and myofibrillar synthesis was greater for whole eggs (p=0.04). Directly demonstrates rapid, high systemic large neutral amino acid (leucine/LNAA) delivery from a typical 3-egg cooked meal (~18 g protein), bridging controlled PD protein-levodopa competition trials to cooked whole eggs.
