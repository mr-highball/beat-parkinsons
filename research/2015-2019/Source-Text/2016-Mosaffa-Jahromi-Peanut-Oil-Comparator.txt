Peanut oil in the anise trial depression comparator
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5871202/
PMID: 26873392
PMCID: PMC5871202
DOI: 10.1177/2156587216628374
Registration: NCT02364830
Coverage: full primary body; capsule methods and Beck Depression Inventory table

The February 11, 2016 irritable bowel syndrome report compared anise oil, peppermint oil and placebo. The oral placebo formulation used peanut oil with oleogel excipients. Its mean Beck Depression Inventory-II scores were 11.02 (SD 6.74, n=40) at baseline, 10.20 (SD 6.62, n=39) at week four and 10.11 (SD 6.79, n=37) at follow-up. These are descriptive control-arm changes. The relationship between the reported group p-values and the displayed dispersion remains unverified. The study provides no separate plain-peanut-oil arm, refined grade or Parkinson population. The related P248 conference abstract reports a different dosing frequency for a gastrointestinal outcome from the same registry, so report-specific descriptions are retained.
