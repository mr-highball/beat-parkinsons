Title: Antinociceptive and Neuropharmacological Activities of Methanol Extract of Phoenix sylvestris Fruit Pulp
PMID: 26483687
PMCID: PMC4591841
DOI: 10.3389/fphar.2015.00212
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4591841/
Published: 2015-10-02, Frontiers in Pharmacology 6, article 212.
Archive scope: Official NLM article, pain and behavioural Methods, Tables 2–3 and Results. Reviewed 2026-11-20.

The controlled mouse assays used a methanolic fruit-pulp extract from wild date palm, Phoenix sylvestris. This species differs from the common culinary date palm, Phoenix dactylifera. The study reports longer responses in thermal nociception tests and reductions in some chemically evoked behaviours. In Table 2 at 120 minutes, the highest tested extract arm averaged 8.88 seconds on the hot plate versus 3.65 seconds for vehicle, with five mice per reported group. The study also reports activity and sedative measures. Movement and sedation may affect behavioural response latencies, and the analysis does not establish the size of that influence.

The herbal use remarks refer to reported traditions and citations, without a comparative human pain outcome. The extract assays use acute laboratory stimuli, a different plant species and preparation, and a different population from chronic Parkinson pain.
