Title: Efficacy and safety of enteric coated capsules of anise oil to treat irritable bowel syndrome.
PMID: 27815079
DOI: 10.1016/j.jep.2016.10.083
Registry ID: NCT02364830
First published: 2016
Authors: Mosaffa-Jahromi M, Lankarani KB, Pasalar M, Afsharypuor S, Tamaddon AM
Journal: Journal of ethnopharmacology
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27815079/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Three-arm double-blind randomized controlled trial (NCT02364830; Shiraz University of Medical Sciences) in n=120 adults with Rome III irritable bowel syndrome (40/arm) comparing enteric-coated anise oil oleogel capsules (AnisEncap, 200 mg tid = 600 mg/day), peppermint oil (Colpermin 187 mg tid), and matched peanut-oil oleogel placebo for 4 weeks plus 2-week follow-up. Intention-to-treat IBS symptom freedom was 75% (AnisEncap) vs 35% (placebo) vs 52.5% (Colpermin), p < 0.001 (NNT = 4). Abstract specifically reports AnisEncap was significantly superior to both placebo and Colpermin at week 4 and week 6 for constipation severity, difficulty in defecation, abdominal discomfort/pain, bloating, diarrhea, gastroesophageal reflux, headache, tiredness, overall satisfaction, and IBS-QOL (p < 0.0001). Exposure is enteric-coated essential oil oleogel in IBS rather than ground anise seed in PD; full-text pairwise mean/SD constipation table is not open-access.
