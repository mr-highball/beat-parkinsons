Study: Randomized double-blind placebo-controlled crossover; ten healthy trained male cyclists. Acute Montmorency tart cherry concentrate vs matched fruit-flavoured low-fruit cordial plus whey protein and maltodextrin. Cycling began 1.5 hours after ingestion. Per manuscript, participants completed separate severe-intensity constant-work-rate test to task failure and 60-second all-out sprint after six minutes of severe-intensity cycling. The test to task failure ended when cadence fell more than 10 rpm below required cadence.

Dose conflict: Publisher's published abstract and accepted manuscript abstract say 30 mL. Accepted manuscript Methods explicitly state 60 mL CherryActive concentrate diluted in 100 mL water and justify the 60 mL choice; the discussion repeats 60 mL. No inference resolves the inconsistency. No correction found in public publisher page, targeted search, Crossref update-to metadata, or Europe PMC correction fields; this check cannot rule out an unindexed correction. No supporting-information link was found in public publisher page; full published article/supplement availability remains unverified.

Exercise tolerance: 772 ± 34 seconds cherry vs 733 ± 34 seconds placebo (means ± SEM), p=0.323; difference was not statistically significant. Sprint was a separate exercise visit: peak first-20-second power 363 ± 42 vs 330 ± 26 W, p=0.034, and total work over 60 seconds 21 ± 3 vs 19 ± 3 kJ, p=0.021. The manuscript figure labels its data as means ± SEM, while publisher abstract does not specify dispersion type for sprint outcomes. No chronic subjective fatigue questionnaire or separate between-arm perceived-exertion endpoint was found in manuscript text; exercise tolerance does not quantify lived fatigue.

Population and exposure fit: No Parkinson cohort; juice concentrate rather than dried whole cherry. Source cannot determine either direction of the frozen claim. Article cites Levers half-marathon study as previous work, but design and cohorts differ; Morgan 2019 cherry powder cycling and Horiuchi 2023 hypoxic cherry capsules are separate studies, not this trial. No claim of verified overlap with another cohort is made.
