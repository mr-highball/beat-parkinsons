Title: Watch it grow: Esophageal impaction with chia seeds
DOI: 10.5430/crim.v4n2p49
First published: 2017
Authors: Simmelink A, Rawl RE, Browne L, Scobey M
Journal: Case Reports in Internal Medicine 4(2):49-52
Source: https://www.sciedu.ca/journal/index.php/crim/article/view/11135
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Peer-reviewed case report (online published 2017-04-26; DOI 10.5430/crim.v4n2p49; Carolinas Medical Center, following the authors' earlier ACG 2014 case presentation).
Case summary: A 39-year-old man with asthma, seasonal allergies, and chronic intermittent solid-food dysphagia swallowed 1 tablespoon of DRY chia seeds followed by a glass of water 12 hours before presentation. He immediately developed epigastric discomfort and inability to manage oral secretions; IV glucagon failed.
Endoscopy: Emergent EGD revealed complete distal esophageal obstruction by a semi-solid hydrogel bolus of hydrated chia seeds plus mucosal linear furrows and white plaques consistent with suspected eosinophilic esophagitis (unconfirmed by biopsy due to loss to follow-up). Adult diagnostic endoscope, Roth net, and grasping forceps could not traverse or remove the gel; a neonatal gastroscope eventually pushed small portions into the stomach to clear the obstruction.
Key preparation & PD safety nuance: Dry chia seeds can absorb up to 27 times their weight in water and expand rapidly into a dense non-Newtonian hydrogel. Authors explicitly caution patients with any history of esophageal pathology against chia seeds, especially in DRY form. This contrasts directly with the frozen Round 3 preparation (soaked chia seed), where pre-hydration occurs prior to swallowing, though human PD videofluoroscopic swallowing safety of soaked whole chia seeds remains untested.
