Aqueous anise seed extract and arterial pressure across rat models
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30904702/
PMID: 30904702
Coverage: factual extract from primary abstract and published overview

Victória Caroline Bottino Pontes, Daniela Pereira Rodrigues, Ariadiny Caetano and Maria Thereza Gamberini. Journal of Ethnopharmacology 237:74–80. First online March 21, 2019. DOI 10.1016/j.jep.2019.03.050.
Water extraction, concentration and freeze drying yielded the intervention. The study evaluated tail artery blood pressure in several normotensive and hypertensive rat groups, plus direct blood pressure and heart rate in Wistar rats and in vitro portal-vein muscle effects. The paper reports lower arterial pressure, bradycardia and evidence consistent with inhibition of calcium influx. Sodium-loaded and spontaneous diuresis experiments recur in the authors’ 2015 proceedings, which independently verify daily oral exposure for the salt-fed experiment. The current abstract does not identify the route for each other experiment. Pressure and heart rate endpoints supply plausibility context only. The study did not identify orthostatic symptoms or everyday falls in Parkinson’s.
