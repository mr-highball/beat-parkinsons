Title: Zanthoxylum extract and drug-induced sleep in mice
Source: https://www.jetir.org/papers/JETIR1903823.pdf
Format: Factual extract prepared from the cited record.

Gurcharan Kour and colleagues, March 2019, JETIR volume 6 issue 3, pages 160–179. Methods and Table 10, page 170.
The investigators processed dried Zanthoxylum armatum fruit into a 1:1 ethanol–water extract and freeze-dried it. Oral doses of 125, 250 and 500 mg/kg were given to Swiss mice before pentobarbital (six mice per group).
At 500 mg/kg, mean loss-of-righting duration was 52.73 ± 3.85 minutes against 79.90 ± 2.65 in the vehicle group. The duration cell carries a significance mark. Onset was 5.80 ± 1.62 minutes versus 2.55 ± 1.14 and lacks a significance mark, despite stronger wording in the narrative.
This is a drug-induced anesthesia assay using an extract from a related Zanthoxylum taxon. Neither natural insomnia nor oral toasted Sichuan pepper in Parkinson disease was studied.
