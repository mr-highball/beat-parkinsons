Title: 帕金森病患者流涎与吞咽困难的关系研究
English title: Drooling and dysphagia in Parkinson disease
Source: https://weixin.incongress.cn/zhsjnotes.do?method=getNotesByIdLMV2&notesId=21409
DOI: 10.3760/cma.j.issn.1006-7876.2016.11.007
Publication date: 2016-11
Citation: Xie et al. (谢朝艳等), Chinese Journal of Neurology 49(11):856–863.
Date basis: The original issue date. The DOI and page range are corroborated in reference 24 at https://xb.njucm.edu.cn/article/doi/10.14148/j.issn.1672-0482.2021.0006.
Source depth: Original article body in Chinese, through the journal reproduction. English summary.

In this Zhejiang outpatient cohort, 69/116 people with idiopathic Parkinson disease had a positive UPDRS-II saliva item (59.5%, 95% CI 50.6–68.4). Six participants had item scores of 3–4. Videofluoroscopic testing across thin liquid, thick liquid, paste and solid boluses linked more saliva loss with oral food leakage, with correlations from .197 to .255. The grouped pharyngeal and esophageal dysphagia outcomes had no significant relationship to drooling in the article body. The component measures vary.

The authors report a pharyngeal dysfunction contrast across test textures and give different p-values in the abstract (.03) and body (.003). The texture findings describe swallowing and supply no named raw-fig drooling comparison. This study is separate from the 2017 Du quality-of-life sources.
