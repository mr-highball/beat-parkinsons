Factual source extract. Stojanovic et al. psychiatric outpatient surveys, 2017-02-10 and 2018-02-01.
Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5605899/ and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5856938/
Both full records were examined through Europe PMC XML. This extract summarizes selected findings.
The 2017 nonpsychotic survey records one Ocimum basilicum infusion user and labels the intended symptom anxiety.
The 2018 cross-sectional survey selected 106 patients with psychotic disorders in remission. Table 5 records one Ocimum basilicum user, classified as A for anxiety. Table 6 describes infusion use. Table 7 describes decreased self-reported symptoms without a reported adverse effect for one co-user of sweet basil with standard medication. The tables do not record a visual hallucination outcome for that herb. The results list other plant taxa for psychotic symptoms.
