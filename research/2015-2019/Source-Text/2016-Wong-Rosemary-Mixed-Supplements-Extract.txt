Wong MK et al., Five Supplements and Multiple Psychotic Symptoms: A Case Report, published 14 January 2016, PMID 27247830, PMCID PMC4874750, DOI 10.4088/PCC.15br01856. Primary case https://www.psychiatrist.com/pcc/five-supplements-and-multiple-psychotic-symptoms/ and table https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4874750/ . A 43-year-old man, no reported previous psychiatric history, medications or past medical history and no reported Parkinson diagnosis, had six months of auditory and visual hallucinations, including seeing random faces, and paranoid thoughts. At symptom onset he had started five supplements. One multi-ingredient product lists rosemary extract (3% rosmarinic acid) among numerous other ingredients, including holy basil, lemon balm, tea extract and acetyl-l-carnitine; four other supplement products were also used. Rosemary dose, fresh-leaf exposure and hallucination counts are not stated. The supplements were discontinued while risperidone, diphenhydramine and cyanocobalamin were started; symptoms improved, so simultaneous interventions and multiple exposures preclude isolating rosemary. The patient denied recreational drugs; drug screen was unremarkable. The same source and patient are already cataloged in Food-Experiment-Basil-Nonmotor-Context.md, and are not a new cohort.
