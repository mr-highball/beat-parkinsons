Title: Anti-inflammatory effect of resin fraction of cardamom in carrageenan-induced rat paw edema
Source: https://ir.cftri.res.in/19277/
DOI: 10.1016/j.phanu.2019.100165
Publisher: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213434419300982
Publication date: 2019-12
Date basis: Dated PharmaNutrition issue. Crossref record created in August 2019. The first exact day of public availability is unverified.
Coverage: Original author-repository abstract, journal bibliographic record and indexed scope. Full manuscript unavailable publicly at review.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26

Shantaram and colleagues report that ten days of dietary whole cardamom powder or cardamom oil did not effectively suppress experimentally induced rat paw edema. De-oiled powder reportedly inhibited edema by 9% at one hour. The brief abstract does not give the whole-powder group size, concentration, point estimate, uncertainty or statistical test. A separate acute comparison reports 31% and 30% inhibition for aqueous and acetone fractions versus 23% for ibuprofen. These acute extract estimates do not describe dietary powder.

Paw edema is swelling rather than a measured nociceptive response. The abstract provides neither a ground-cardamom Parkinson pain effect nor evidence of equivalence. The publisher response did not supply full Methods and tables, and the institutional copy is restricted. An earlier institutional annual report may describe the same project and supplies no independent whole-powder estimate.

Publisher preview follow-through, November 26, 2026. Indexed Methods describe male Wistar rats and powder made from ground, dehusked cardamom seeds. The abstract and introduction describe ten days of dietary exposure. The indexed conclusion describes a month of dietary intake, which does not agree with that duration. The whole-powder arm results and actual regimen remain unverified. Paw swelling is the measured outcome in the indexed comparison.

November 27 original scope follow-through
The institutional record authenticates the restricted six-page paper. The abstract reports no effective paw-swelling reduction with whole powder and gives 9% inhibition at one hour for a separate de-oiled fraction. Whole-powder group values and pain measurements remain unavailable. The publicly authenticated institutional entry identifies the restricted full printed text as six pages.
