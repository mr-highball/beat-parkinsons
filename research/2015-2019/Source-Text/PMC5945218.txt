Cohort and endpoint

The Johns Hopkins Udall longitudinal study began in 1998. The authors analyzed 165 of 182 recruited participants meeting the clinical criteria for idiopathic Parkinson disease. Study visits included psychiatric interviews, neurological assessment and medication lists. Of 165, 111 attended at least two evaluations. The median interval between serial evaluations was 2.1 years.

The primary dependent variable was presence or absence of a DSM-IV psychotic disorder judged directly related to Parkinson disease. Its definition allowed prominent hallucinations in any modality or delusions. Diagnoses attributed to medication or another disorder and phenomena occurring exclusively during delirium were outside the endpoint. The published comparison gives no separate count of visual episodes. See Patients and Methods, Measures, and Table 2.

Clinical course and medication observations

Thirty-two participants met the psychosis definition at baseline and another 30 first met it during follow-up. Thirteen of the 49 participants who had psychosis and multiple visits showed remission at a later evaluation. Two of the 13 later had a recorded relapse. Five had no evaluation after recorded remission. Four received antipsychotics both before and after remission. Visits about two years apart could miss intervening changes.

The multivariable model associated diagnosis with akinetic-rigid motor impairment, dementia, levodopa-carbidopa dose and use of anticholinergic medicine for overactive bladder. The 30 initial-onset cases showed a shift in relative levodopa and dopamine agonist doses between visits. The two-year visit spacing and observed medication changes leave the timing and causal role of treatment uncertain. See Tables 3 and 4.

Claim 0175

No dried fig intake was measured or assigned. The outcome is onset or remission of a psychiatric diagnosis per visit and does not estimate Parkinson visual hallucination episodes after a food exposure. The study cites the separate 2001 Rush hallucination cohort (PMID 11739829) and the Erskine postmortem Lewy body dementia study (PMID 28190674). Their endpoints and populations remain distinct.
