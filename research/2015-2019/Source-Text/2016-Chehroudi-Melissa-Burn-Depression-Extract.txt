Title: Effects of Melissa officinalis L. on Reducing Stress, Alleviating Anxiety Disorders, Depression, and Insomnia, and Increasing Total Antioxidants in Burn Patients

Reviewed: 2026-11-17

Thirty-six burn inpatients were assigned brewed lemon balm or black tea twice daily for 20 days. Each active bag contained 2.5 g leaves. Prior depression was excluded; early discharges would be replaced. The beverages differed in taste and smell and could not be double-blinded.

Table 3 no-depression proportions changed 55.6% to 72.2% under Melissa, approximately 67% to 67% under black tea; severe category remained 11.1% in both arms. These are group marginals, without individual transitions or continuous Beck changes. Table 3 p=.002 is a within-active comparison, although the abstract describes a between-arm result. Table 5 lists undefined differences 2.7 versus 1.66 and p=.014; the contrast remains ambiguous. IRCT201205198177N4 is retrospective. The findings give limited directional context outside Parkinson disease.
