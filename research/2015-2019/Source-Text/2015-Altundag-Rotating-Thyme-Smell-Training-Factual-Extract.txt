Title: Thyme in a rotating postinfectious smell training program
Source: https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/smell/human/2015-altundag.pdf
Format: Factual extract from the original article.

Altundag and colleagues, The Laryngoscope, first published June 2, 2015. Original Methods p. 1764 and Tables I and II pp. 1765–1766.
Eighty-five people with postinfectious olfactory impairment were allocated in inclusion order: rotating training 37, classical training 33 and no training 15. They were not randomized. Thyme was one of four odors in the rotating arm in weeks 13–24. The other three in that block were menthol, tangerine and jasmine. The program used different odors in other blocks. It trained odor perception by sniffing.
At week 36 the rotating-versus-classical comparison favored rotation for the combined smell threshold, discrimination and identification score (p = .034) and identification (p = .022). Twenty-one of 37 rotating and 15 of 33 classical participants improved at least six combined score points at week 24 and week 36. Neither arm added responders after week 24. The intervention differs in several odors and provides no effect isolated to thyme, fresh thyme ingestion or Parkinson disease.
