Title: Effects of Brazil nut consumption on selenium status and cognitive performance in older adults with mild cognitive impairment: a randomized controlled pilot trial.
PMID: 25567069
DOI: 10.1007/s00394-014-0829-2
First published: 2016
Authors: Rita Cardoso B, Apolinário D, da Silva Bandeira V, Busse AL, Magaldi RM, Jacob-Filho W, et al.
Journal: European journal of nutrition
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25567069/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

6-month randomized controlled pilot trial (NCT02121457; published erratum PMID33399977; companion SNP report PMID26661784) enrolling 31 older adults with mild cognitive impairment (MCI), with 20 completing analysis (11 Brazil nut, 9 usual-diet control; mean age 77.7+-5.3 y). Intervention: 1 Brazil nut/day (~5 g, delivering ~288.75 ug selenium/d) vs usual diet. Brazil nut significantly increased plasma/erythrocyte selenium and erythrocyte GPx activity (p=0.006) and yielded significantly greater improvements in CERAD verbal fluency (executive/semantic retrieval; p=0.007) and constructional praxis (visuoconstruction; p=0.031; score 7.7 to 9.2 in Pereira 2022 synthesis) versus control; MMSE and total CERAD did not show significant between-group separation. Limits: small sample, 35% attrition (31 to 20), unblinded usual-diet control, non-PD MCI cohort, erratum issued, daily Se dose (~289 ug) exceeded EFSA 2023 UL (255 ug/d).
