Title: Phytochemical analysis of Juglans regia oil and kernel exploring their antinociceptive and anti-inflammatory potentials utilizing combined bio-guided GC-FID, GC-MS and HPLC analyses
DOI: 10.1016/j.bjp.2018.03.007
Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1016/j.bjp.2018.03.007
Original PDF: https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1016/j.bjp.2018.03.007.pdf
First public date: 2018-05-15, original report.
Coverage: Factual extract of authenticated complete 11-page original, Methods, Figure 2 and Figures 10–12. Oil, ethyl-acetate extract and isolated linoleic acid remain distinct preparations.
Reviewed: 2026-11-17

Juglans regia kernel oil was obtained by hexane Soxhlet extraction with solvent removed. Mice received oral oil at 12.5, 25 or 50 mg/kg. The acute carrageenan experiment reports seven animals per group and paw-pressure threshold increases of 34.3%, 45.7% and 60.0% versus vehicle, reported p<.05. It tests evoked inflammatory pain.

In diabetic mice, thermal and mechanical withdrawal tests continued for eight weeks. The Figure 12 text gives oil mechanical-threshold changes of 6.5, 6.6 and 6.8-fold versus diabetic vehicle and 9.6-fold for tramadol. The abstract makes a broader high-dose superiority claim; its relation to these particular mechanical values remains unreconciled. Ambiguous thermal fold-change wording is not converted into absolute ratios.

These are genuine favorable animal nociception findings. The source does not test Parkinson disease, human clinical pain or cold-pressed culinary oil. The separate kernel extract and constituent findings cannot be assigned to the oil. No absence of clinical effect is established.
