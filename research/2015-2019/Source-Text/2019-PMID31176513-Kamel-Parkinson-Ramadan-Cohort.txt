Title: How do Parkinson's disease patients manage Ramadan fasting? An observational study
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31176513/
Author manuscript: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/am/pii/S0035378718308348?via%3Dihub=
Repository identifier: hal-03488512
PMID: 31176513
DOI: 10.1016/j.neurol.2018.12.008
Publication date: 2019-06-05
Citation: Kamel WA, Al Hashel JY, Damier P. Revue Neurologique 175:560–563.
Text coverage: Original summary of the accepted manuscript and its outcome and attrition sections.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

The 2016 Kuwait cohort enrolled 24 people with Parkinson disease who planned to fast. Three stopped fasting during the first week, including two because their Parkinson symptoms became bothersome and one for other medical problems. One person missed follow-up. Twenty completed the fast and entered the before-and-after analysis. Some completers reported more fatigue or worse symptoms during fasting. The group nonmotor total was 43.1 before and 43.1 after Ramadan, with no statistically clear change. The report gives no separate pain or apathy scores.

The abstract reports six who avoided daytime medication. The Methods and Results distinguish medication, food and fluid intake, with the Results describing five who met its strict daily fasting description. This difference should be retained with the original wording and timing. The 2017 Movement Disorder Society abstract 1106 describes the same site, Ramadan cycle and closely matching outcomes. Treat the two publications as one probable cohort.

Neither report measures food composition, acquisition, amount or preparation at iftar, including date fruit. Medication changes, selective continuation and the single-cohort design limit interpretation of the before-and-after results. Fasting outcomes are contextual evidence for exposures that may co-occur with iftar, without establishing a direction for dried dates.
