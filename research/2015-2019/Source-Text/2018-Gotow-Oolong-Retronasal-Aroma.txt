Title: Multi-Sip Time–Intensity Evaluation of Retronasal Aroma after Swallowing Oolong Tea Beverage
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6262627/
PMID: 30366388
PMCID: PMC6262627
DOI: 10.3390/foods7110177
First published: 2018-10-25
Text coverage: factual extraction from Methods, Results and Table 1.

Gotow and colleagues enrolled 25 volunteers aged 20–54. Participants swallowed ten successive servings of the same commercial bottled oolong beverage. After each sip they rated the strength of that drink's retronasal aroma for 60 seconds. All 25 contributed to time-curve comparisons. Twenty contributed to the derived-parameter analysis after five were excluded because the curve features could not be calculated.

In Table 1, peak aroma strength was 3.71 (SD 1.00) on the first trial and 4.13 (0.68) on the second. Peak intensity remained between 4.20 and 4.32 on trials three through ten. Time to peak was 14.88 seconds (SD 12.83) on the first trial and 7.92 (9.40) on the second. The repeated-measures analyses for peak intensity and time to peak were p<.001. The plateau duration and rate of decay gave no clear trial difference. This sensory panel measured immediate perception of the same beverage. Its design supplies no no-tea comparison, standardized clinical smell change, or result for brewed green tea or for Parkinson disease.
