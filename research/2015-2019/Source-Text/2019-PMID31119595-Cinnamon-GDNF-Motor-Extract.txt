Patel and colleagues tested ground original Ceylon cinnamon, identified as Cinnamomum verum, and separately administered sodium benzoate. Cinnamon 100 mg/kg/day in methylcellulose was orally gavaged for seven days, starting three hours after acute MPTP injections. This was early post-insult treatment, without a delayed established-disease comparison.

Figure 8 reports six mice per group. Relative to MPTP plus vehicle, cinnamon improved rotarod performance and open-field rearing/grooming in Gfap-cre littermates. Authors report loss of benefit after astrocyte-specific GDNF deletion. The rotarod interaction was p=.021; rearing/grooming interactions were inconclusive at .164/.322, limiting genotype-specific interpretation of those outcomes. The behavioral contrasts and genetic manipulation support a motor/mechanistic signal, not human walking velocity. Open-field exploration and rotarod performance also depend on coordination, motivation and balance.

The sodium-benzoate arm used 100 mg/kg/day and remains a separate exposure. Human astrocyte experiments are cell studies, not a patient trial. The report follows the same investigator program's 2014 MPTP work and should not be described as independent clinical replication. Its small animal groups and early treatment window limit transfer to culinary cinnamon and Parkinson walking slowness.
