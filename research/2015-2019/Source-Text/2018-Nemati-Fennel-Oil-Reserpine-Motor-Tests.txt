Title: Evaluation of the Effects of Foeniculum vulgare Essence on Behavioral-Motor Disorders of Parkinson's Disease induced by Reserpine in Ovariectomized and Non Ovariectomized Rats
DOI: 10.5812/jjnpp.67391
Publication date: 2018-02-18
Citation: Nemati and colleagues. Jundishapur Journal of Natural Pharmaceutical Products 13(1), e67391.
Source: https://brieflands.com/journals/jjnpp/articles/67391
Publisher PDF: https://brieflands.com/journals/jjnpp/articles/67391.pdf
Coverage: Primary article body, methods, Table 1 and figure comparisons reviewed.

The methods describe 80 female rats divided into ten groups, with oral essential oil tested in reserpine-exposed groups. During the bar test the experimenter placed the forepaws on an elevated bar and timed how long they remained. Among animals without ovariectomy, the oral-oil group averaged 17.67 ± 11.52 seconds and the matched reserpine-only group 146.75 ± 10.67 seconds. Table 1 also gives open-field values of 332.17 ± 5.56 and 4.25 ± 5.15 for those groups. Group IV received levodopa/carbidopa and gives a different pair of values. The figures compare groups to untreated group I. The oil-versus-reserpine contrasts are descriptive here. Serum estrogen was recorded separately. The methods and abstract describe subcutaneous reserpine, while the Results opening calls it intraperitoneal. The article mentions tremor as a disease feature in the introduction. The reported experiments contain no tremor score, and the oil is a concentrated product.
