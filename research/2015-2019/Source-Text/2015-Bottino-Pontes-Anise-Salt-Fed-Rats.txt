Oral aqueous anise seed extract and systolic pressure in salt-fed rats
Source: https://arquivosmedicos.fcmsantacasasp.edu.br/index.php/AMSCSP/article/download/169/179/290
Coverage: factual extract from original Portuguese proceedings abstract

Victória Caroline Bottino Pontes and Maria Thereza Gamberini, abstract 33, Arquivos Médicos dos Hospitais e da Faculdade de Ciências Médicas da Santa Casa de São Paulo 60(suppl 1), 2015. The printed volume identifies the conference abstracts as work presented by 2014 graduate cohorts.
The researchers fed Wistar rats an 8% sodium chloride diet after weaning and measured systolic blood pressure with an indirect tail method. Comparison rats received the 0.2% sodium chloride diet. After blood pressure stabilized, salt-loaded animals received an aqueous Pimpinella anisum extract by mouth daily. The authors report systolic pressure approaching the normotensive diet comparator and no difference in spontaneous urine volume versus water treatment. Exact dose and extract yield are not given in the selected abstract. Matching authors and salt-loading and diuresis experiments make this a likely precursor to the 2019 paper; cohort identity remains unresolved. The exposure was extract, and pressure was assessed without a standing challenge or behavioral fall count.
