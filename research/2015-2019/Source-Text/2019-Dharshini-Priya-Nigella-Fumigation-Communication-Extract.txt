2019 | Dharshini Priya, Arul Mozhi and Meenakshi Sundaram | Siddha combination and autism communication assessment

Source: published December 5, 2019, in the October 2019 journal issue. CTRI/2017/05/008698 is identified in the primary report and the related thesis study documents.

In the 30-child uncontrolled study, the oral decoction did not contain nigella. The study applied a topical treatment and a separate external fumigation blend which included ground Nigella seed. The registered preparation quotation in the thesis specifies Nigella sativa; the published formula lists Nigella sativum as a spelling variant.

Speech, language and communication contributed to a composite assessment. Its items concerned nonverbal communication, repetitive use of language, unusual noises, meaningless words and understanding communication. Table 2 reports a communication mean of 50.66 (SD 20.16) before and 98.00 (SD 25.37) after treatment. The emotional score occupies a different row. The printed t value is 10.36 with a probability greater than 1.00, which is invalid. The thesis gives individual category counts, including ratings for unusual noises. Those ratings do not measure intelligibility and some table percentages are internally inconsistent. The public comparison does not isolate one component or provide a listener intelligibility score. The 2023 Elangovan trial (CTRI/2019/12/022286) differs in registration and prescribed oral/topical formulations.
