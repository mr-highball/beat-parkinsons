Title: Whole-grain intake and prevalent versus incident insomnia
First published or posted: 2019-12-11
PMID: 31828298
PMCID: PMC6997082
DOI: 10.1093/ajcn/nqz275
Source URL: https://escholarship.org/uc/item/1q90v2rb
Text type: original-source evidence summary

Gangwisch and colleagues analyzed postmenopausal participants in the Women's Health Initiative observational cohort. Tables 3 and 4 compare whole-grain consumption quintiles. In the fully adjusted models, the highest versus lowest quintile had an odds ratio of 0.93 (95% CI 0.88 to 0.98) for prevalent insomnia among 77,860 participants and 1.00 (95% CI 0.93 to 1.07) for incident insomnia at three years among 53,069. Whole grains were grouped across foods. The cohort design, pooled exposure and population do not establish a causal rolled-oat effect or estimate its effect in Parkinson disease.
