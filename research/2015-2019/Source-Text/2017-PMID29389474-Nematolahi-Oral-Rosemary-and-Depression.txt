Title: Effects of Rosmarinus officinalis L. on memory performance, anxiety, depression, and sleep quality in university students: A randomized clinical trial
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29389474/

Checked: 2026-11-24
Review depth: Investigator abstract and limited publisher preview. Original numeric outcome tables pending.

The investigators enrolled 68 students aged 20–25 in a double-blind randomized trial. Oral rosemary and placebo were compared twice daily for one month. The assays were prospective and retrospective memory questionnaires, HADS anxiety and depression, and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index. The original abstract reports significantly lower depression and other scale scores with rosemary at one month, with exceptions for the PSQI sleep latency and duration components.

An endpoint standardized difference of -0.30 with 95% CI -0.78 to 0.18 is reported for this trial in the later Aucoin presentation. This is a secondary extraction and its interval crosses zero. Differences in analysis, baseline balance and missing data cannot be evaluated from the currently inspected original pages. The group lacked a known Parkinson cohort and the study compares capsules, described as dried powdered rosemary in the existing synthesis, with placebo. It provides no ordinary fresh rosemary contrast.
