Title: Drooling is no early sign of dysphagia in Parkinson's disease
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29178420/
PMID: 29178420
DOI: 10.1111/nmo.13259
Publication date: 2017-11-27
Date basis: Indexed online-first date. The journal issue followed in April 2018.
Source depth: Original author abstract. Original results tables remain open.

The controlled cross-sectional Hamburg study compared 119 Parkinson outpatients and 32 controls. Drooling was recorded in 50% and 9% respectively (p < .001). The authors report an association with dysphagia in Parkinson disease (p = .027). Among those with critical aspiration, 39% had no drooling. Of patients reported as having severe drooling, 41% had no clinically relevant dysphagia in the endoscopic examination. The subgroup denominators remain open.

The same institution, overlapping authors and identical 119/32 sample sizes make participant overlap with the Pflug study likely. No food exposure or raw-fig drooling comparison is reported in the abstract.
