Eid et al. Impact of palm date consumption on microbiota growth and large intestinal health: a randomised, controlled, cross-over, human intervention study. British Journal of Nutrition 114:1226–1236. Published online 2015-10-02.
PMID: 26428278
DOI: 10.1017/S0007114515002780
Source: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114515002780
Registration: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02288611
Coverage: factual digest of full journal Methods and results reporting.

Twenty-two healthy participants were enrolled and twenty-one completed. In the same NCT02288611 crossover, participants ate whole dry Tamr-stage Ajwa dates for 21 days and took a carbohydrate comparator for another 21-day period. The primary report refers to daily diary entries for subjective mood alongside abdominal and bowel outcomes. It does not publish mood values or identify an apathy scale. The original 2013 conference abstract and the 2014 registry record provide no mood result. The publication describes single masking, whereas the original registry says open label.

Table 2 reports post-period abdominal pain means of 0.22 (SD 0.29) with whole dates and 0.25 (SD 0.32) with carbohydrate control. The article reports no significant difference for this outcome and gives no paired confidence interval. The participants were healthy, with low diary scores. The values apply to abdominal symptom ascertainment in this trial.
