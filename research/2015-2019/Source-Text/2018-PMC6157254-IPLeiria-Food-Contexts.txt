Text coverage: Distilled member notes from the complete 183-page publisher PDF and official JATS XML. The original includes separate presentations and posters.

O79. A pharmacy survey of 381 older adults asked about sleep, medication and lifestyle. Coffee and tea consumption were reported by 68.8% and 73.2%, respectively. Fatigue was not measured as a separate outcome. The abstract reports no coffee-stratified sleep or daytime-sleepiness result and specifies no brewing method.

P21. In a within-patient comparison involving 60 users and 137 category II pressure wounds, topical ozonized extra-virgin olive oil was compared with topical cannabidiol oil. The authors report both groups improved and no significant difference in wound resolution, with a separate qualitative analgesia claim for cannabidiol. The oil was ozonized and applied to wounds. There was no Parkinson assessment.

P22 and P37. Separate rat skin-wound reports use topical extra-virgin olive oil as a comparator for cannabidiol and saline. P22 found no detected product-group difference in wound microbial colonization. P37 reports no significant between-product difference in thermal tail-withdrawal latency. Neither estimates an oral olive-oil effect. Animal or sample overlap across the two abstracts was not established.

P33 and P56. The original PDF and JATS both pair P33's rosemary-and-ginkgo title with a body describing 357 oral-antidiabetic users. P33 cannot supply an authenticated plant experiment result. Its reference 4 is a separate rosemary and common-sage extract paper (PMID 23017402). P56 is a coherent rosemary-leaf extraction study comparing three commercial sources, water versus 80% ethanol and Ginkgo biloba. In DPPH, rosemary IC50 ranged from 44.1 to 61.8 micrograms/mL in aqueous extracts and 20.8 to 23.3 in hydroalcoholic extracts. The assays were chemical, with no human mood or gait measurement.

P63. The 132 young volunteers completed questionnaires for Mediterranean diet adherence and mood. The authors report an inverse association between adherence and anxiety among the 80 women (p < 0.05). Olive oil is part of the described dietary pattern. The poster provides neither an oil-specific association nor Parkinson-specific results.

P71. "Single clove garlic" in the citations refers to the garlic bulb segment. The original member concerns Allium sativum and does not test the Syzygium aromaticum spice.

P179. The vegetable-liking program offered individual vegetables at kindergarten lunch in conjunction with education and a sticker reward. The list contains carrot, bell pepper, broccoli, tomato, cucumber, purple cabbage, spinach, arugula and beet. The abstract gives total n = 82 but prints arm counts of 68 and 16, which add to 84. Registration NCT03513081 gives total enrollment 82. The outcome was vegetable intake and liking in children, without adult motor, fatigue, mood or fall outcomes. Purple cabbage is the ordinary red-cabbage exposure class, with cultivar and cooking method unspecified.

The listed exposure, mixed-diet and comparator observations belong to their own members. References and signatures do not transfer outcomes between abstracts.
