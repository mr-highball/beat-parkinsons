Title: A Randomized Study of the Effect of Fish Oil on n-3 Fatty Acid Incorporation and Nutritional Status in Lung Cancer Patients
Authors: Andersen JR and colleagues
Publication: Austin Journal of Nutrition and Metabolism 2015;2(1):1011
Publication date: 2015-03-31, stated by publisher
Source: https://austinpublishinggroup.com/nutrition-metabolism/fulltext/ajnm-v2-id1011.php
Coverage: Publisher full methods and Tables 1 and 3. Inspected 2026-11-27.

This double-blind randomized comparison assigned 44 adults with advanced lung cancer receiving chemotherapy to 20 mL/day fish oil or rapeseed oil with meals. Two patients were excluded after randomization before baseline, leaving 20 and 22 respectively. The report does not specify rapeseed cultivar or refining. It records fatigue using EORTC quality-of-life instruments at three-week visits.

Table 3 gives median individual fatigue change from baseline and interquartile limits: fish oil 0.0 (0.0 to 11.1), n=19; rapeseed 0.0 (−11.1 to 0.0), n=22; between-group p=.461. Table 1 baseline medians were 33.3 (16.7–50.5), n=19, and 44.4 (22.2–66.7), n=22. The paper says only 25 patients were available after the intervention, while the Table 3 fatigue denominators total 41. The available table does not clearly explain how missing observations produced these denominators; do not treat its null result as equivalence. The comparison is between active oils in a chemotherapy cohort, with no no-oil control or Parkinson assessment.

Related earlier report: Scientific Posters Presented at Clinical Nutrition Week 2009, SP-1, first PDF page, https://s3.amazonaws.com/publicationslist.org/data/s.vanhoutvin/ref-15/ScientificPosters09.pdf. The poster has substantially overlapping investigators, 44 randomized participants, identical oils and dose, and the same quality-of-life instruments, strongly suggesting the same trial family; it reports 24 completing versus 25 available after intervention in the later article. Its conclusion suggests worse fatigue with fish oil, but no separate between-arm fatigue estimate is provided, and its reported significant finding is a biomarker correlation. Its event year is identified by the PDF heading; its separate publication date is unverified. Do not count as independent directional evidence.
