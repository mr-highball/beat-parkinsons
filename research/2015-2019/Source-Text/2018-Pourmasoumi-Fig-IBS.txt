Pourmasoumi et al., Explore. 2019;15(3):198–205. First published online in 2018.
Sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30318190/ and https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02559245
DOI: 10.1016/j.explore.2018.09.003
Coverage: Selected original abstract and native ClinicalTrials.gov public fields.

A single-blind randomized trial assigned 150 adults with constipation-predominant irritable bowel syndrome to fig, flixweed, or control for four months. It evaluated bowel symptoms, quality of life and serum C-reactive protein. Both active groups improved several gastrointestinal outcomes versus control. No difference in C-reactive protein or abdominal pain severity was reported. The clinical registry shows completion without posted tabular results. Parkinson diagnosis and visual hallucinations were outside the published comparison.
