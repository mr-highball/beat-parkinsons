Title: Comparison and Assessment of Flixweed and Fig Effects on Irritable Bowel Syndrome with Predominant Constipation: A Single-Blind Randomized Clinical Trial
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30318190/
Source alias: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.explore.2018.09.003
Publisher excerpt: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1550830718301873
PMID: 30318190
DOI: 10.1016/j.explore.2018.09.003
Trial registration: NCT02559245
Publication date: 2018-09-11
Date basis: NLM electronic publication date. May–June 2019 print issue, Explore 15(3), 198–205.
Text coverage: Author abstract, publisher excerpts from the setting, results and discussion, and independently retrieved registry. Original outcome tables remain open.

Pourmasoumi and colleagues enrolled 150 people with constipation-predominant irritable bowel syndrome into three randomized groups over four months. The publisher’s results excerpt reports 142 completers. It lists four withdrawals in the fig group and two each in the flixweed and usual-diet groups. The fig and flixweed groups had favorable changes against usual diet in reported pain frequency, abdominal distension, defecation frequency and hard stools. The abstract reports no clear change in pain severity or C-reactive protein. Outcome analysis denominators, effect sizes and item-level adverse events remain open.

The registry specifies investigator masking and a usual-diet control without a placebo food. The publisher’s setting explicitly cites NCT02559245. The discussion uses wording consistent with dried fruit, and separate reporting identifies it as dried. The original intervention methods still need verification. The study supplies bowel-symptom outcomes in a different condition and gives no Parkinson or visual hallucination estimate.
