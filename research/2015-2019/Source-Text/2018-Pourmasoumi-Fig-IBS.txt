Title: Comparison and Assessment of Flixweed and Fig Effects on Irritable Bowel Syndrome with Predominant Constipation: A Single-Blind Randomized Clinical Trial
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30318190/
Source alias: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.explore.2018.09.003
PMID: 30318190
DOI: 10.1016/j.explore.2018.09.003
Trial registration: NCT02559245
Publication date: 2018-09-11
Date basis: NLM electronic publication date. May–June 2019 print issue, Explore 15(3), 198–205.
Text coverage: Original author abstract and independently retrieved registry. Original outcome tables remain open.

Pourmasoumi and colleagues enrolled 150 people with constipation-predominant irritable bowel syndrome into three randomized groups over four months. The fig and flixweed groups had favorable changes against usual diet in reported pain frequency, abdominal distension, defecation frequency and hard stools. The abstract reports no clear change in pain severity or C-reactive protein. Effect sizes, analyzed denominators and item-level adverse events remain open.

The registry specifies investigator masking and a usual-diet control without a placebo food. A secondary reporting source identifies the fig as dried. The inspected original abstract and registry identify fig fruit without confirming that preparation. The study supplies bowel-symptom outcomes in a different condition and gives no Parkinson or visual hallucination estimate.
