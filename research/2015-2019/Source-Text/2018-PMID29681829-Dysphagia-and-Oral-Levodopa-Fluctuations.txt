Title: Dysphagia causes symptom fluctuations after oral L-DOPA treatment in a patient with Parkinson disease
PMID: 29681829
PMCID: PMC5903165
DOI: 10.1159/000488138
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5903165/
Publication: 2018
Coverage: Original clinical case, methods, results and figure captions.

The authors report choking during an oral levodopa/carbidopa test in one person with Parkinson disease and swallowing difficulty. They used jelly with the drug, leaving its ingredients unspecified. The reported maximum blood levodopa level was 0.78 nmol/mL at the first test. At the repeat test eight days into tongue training, the patient swallowed more quickly with jelly, and the reported level peaked at 9.99 nmol/mL after 30 minutes. The report describes motor and swallowing changes. Later care also included rotigotine.

The sequential case supplies clinical context for swallowing and oral drug transport. Jelly use occurred in both tests. Its composition, any independent jelly effect, a raw fig exposure and a separate drooling result were unverified.
