Calapai and colleagues randomized 111 healthy older adults in a double-blind grape fruit extract versus placebo comparison for 12 weeks. Figure 2 and the results report a 24.9% reduction from baseline in the extract group's Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale score and a between-group comparison favoring the extract (p < 0.05). The paper gives no readily usable interval for a clinically meaningful difference on that anxiety scale. People were enrolled as healthy and the report describes low scores. Separate cognitive and depression outcomes do not add independent anxiety observations.

The formulation was a standardized fruit extract with a carrier, rather than raw grapes. The trial population did not have Parkinson disease. The paper drops a leading zero in its ClinicalTrials.gov identifier. NCT03145987 agrees on design, enrollment and instruments. The registration was first posted after the listed primary completion date, so the public timing does not establish prospective outcome registration.
