Title: Lacto-fermented sauerkraut improves symptoms in IBS patients independent of product pasteurisation - a pilot study.
PMID: 30256365
DOI: 10.1039/c8fo00968f
First published: 2018
Authors: Nielsen ES, Garnås E, Jensen KJ, Hansen LH, Olsen PS, Ritz C
Journal: Food & function
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30256365/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 4 review).

Randomized double-blind pilot trial in 34 Norwegian irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) patients supplementing their daily diet with 75 g/day pasteurized (PS, n=15) vs unpasteurized (UPS, n=19) lacto-fermented white-cabbage sauerkraut for 6 weeks. Results: within-group IBS-Symptom Severity Score (IBS-SSS) improved significantly from baseline in both arms (-38.57 +/- 17.08 PS vs -56.99 +/- 16.92 UPS, both p < 0.04) with no significant between-group difference; fecal 16S rRNA composition shifted significantly (weighted & unweighted UniFrac p = 0.001) and Lactobacillus plantarum/brevis increased in UPS. CRITICAL LIMITATIONS: (1) NO non-sauerkraut placebo/control arm (cannot separate sauerkraut effect from regression to the mean or IBS placebo response), (2) IBS-SSS is a composite score (abdominal pain, distension, bowel dissatisfaction, life interference) rather than an isolated constipation severity or pain score, and (3) non-PD IBS sample.
