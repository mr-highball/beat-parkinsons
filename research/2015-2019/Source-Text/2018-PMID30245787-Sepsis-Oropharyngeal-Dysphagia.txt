Title: Examining the relationship between sepsis and oropharyngeal dysphagia in hospitalised elderly patients: a retrospective cohort study
PMID: 30245787
PMCID: PMC6145432
DOI: 10.1136/flgastro-2018-100994
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6145432/
Published: 2018-06-02 online, Frontline Gastroenterology 9(4), 256–261.
Archive scope: Factual text from the original Methods, recruitment flow, Table 1 and swallowing recovery results.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Sasegbon and colleagues reviewed March 2013 to 2014 dysphagia referrals for hospitalized adults aged 65 and older. The report describes 51 of 301 eligible referrals with sepsis and subsequent oropharyngeal dysphagia, after screening 1,761 referrals. The exact numerator and denominator convey the result more consistently than the 16% in the abstract and 17% in Results. Clinical swallowing assessment established 47 of the 51 cases, with videofluoroscopy in three and endoscopy in one.

Table 1 combines Parkinson disease and parkinsonism in seven of the 51 cases. It gives no diagnosis-specific denominator, isolated ingredient, or Parkinson-specific effect estimate. The 2025 PP0011 and 2026 clinical report instead study a later admissions cohort with a different denominator and explicit regression. The two periods should not be pooled as one sample.
