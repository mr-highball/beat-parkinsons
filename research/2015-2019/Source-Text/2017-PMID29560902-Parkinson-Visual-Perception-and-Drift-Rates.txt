Format: Factual digest of the primary report

The methods recruited 24 Parkinson participants with visual hallucinations and 26 without. Four from the first group and one from the second were left out of the analyses because of missed task responses. Table 1 includes the remaining 20 and 25, alongside 12 controls. The analysis uses reaction times and accuracy in a shortened attentional network task and a hierarchical drift diffusion model.

The analyzed visual hallucination group had slower modeled accumulation of sensory information than the other groups. This result compares a model parameter across groups defined by symptoms. The report used a limited hallucination assessment and the experimental task was completed during participants' usual medication on state. The task did not change food intake or track subsequent visual episodes.

BNA abstract P-W082 (PMC7545776) names the same original Parkinson enrollment cells and describes the same task and model. Its 13 controls differ from the journal report's 12. The reported exclusion counts explain the change in the Parkinson analysis denominators. The original article also says some participants overlapped with an earlier behavioral attention-task study. Keep the conference and journal comparison in a shared evidence family and preserve the unresolved control and earlier-cohort crosswalk.
