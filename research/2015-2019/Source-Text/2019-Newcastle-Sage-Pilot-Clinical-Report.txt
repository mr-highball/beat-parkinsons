The Parkinson pilot used dried common sage (Salvia officinalis) leaf capsules. The front page describes replacing the original placebo with vitamin E. The methods on page 2 describe six weeks of sage or vitamin E and computerized cognitive testing. The objective on the same page retains the earlier placebo wording. The 2010 investigator account is another report of this pilot and describes 25 participants. It should not be counted as another trial.

Page 3 reports a working-memory quality advantage and a lower salivation score with vitamin E relative to sage. Within-group sage cognitive-speed changes are not evidence of a between-group benefit. The attachment reports no arm-level facial expression, hypomimia or fatigue result. The secondary-outcome timetable referred to on page 2 and the proposed manuscript were not recovered. This leaves the actual facial assessment schedule uncertain.

Relevance to Round 1 claim 0125: same plant and Parkinson population, with a capsule preparation and a different reported outcome. Relevance to claim 0883: fresh culinary sage and measured fatigue are also distinct from the available result. The report does not supply a directional estimate for either claim.
