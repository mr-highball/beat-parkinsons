Text coverage: Distilled original paper, Methods, Results and visually checked Fig. 2D–E.

The authors assigned 48 young male mice to four conditions and report eight per group for the motor figure. The treatment was a purchased purified procyanidin B2 reagent, a compound found in several foods including strawberries. There was no strawberry diet, and the source plant of the reagent was unreported.

The rotarod test recorded the initial time to fall. Mice were placed back on the apparatus after a fall, and the researchers counted falls within the five-minute test. In Fig. 2E the high-dose group had 4.8 falls (reported error ±1.8), compared with 9.1 (±2.7) in the cuprizone group and 3.5 (±1.8) in the uninjured control. The figure marks high dose versus cuprizone at p<.01, and the low-dose falls-count bar lacks that mark. The caption describes its error bars as SEM.

Fig. 2D plots cuprizone-only latency near 60 seconds, while the prose assigns 123.3 seconds to that group. The control bar is near the latter value. Preserve the discrepancy and use Fig. 2E and the concordant count descriptions for the observed higher-dose direction. The specimen and phenotype concern an experimental demyelination model. Its provoking, replacing and counting protocol supplies no spontaneous clinical fall rate or raw strawberry contrast.
