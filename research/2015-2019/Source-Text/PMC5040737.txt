Title: Comparative study of neuropharmacological, analgesic properties and phenolic profile of Ajwah, Safawy and Sukkari cultivars of date palm
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5040737/
PMID: 27746708
PMCID: PMC5040737
DOI: 10.1007/s13596-016-0239-5
First published: 2016-08-16
Coverage: Distilled original methods and Tables 1–5.

Sheikh and colleagues used authenticated, dried ripe Ajwah, Safawy and Sukkari as the starting material for ethanol extracts. Each nociception table identifies five Swiss albino mice per group. The acetic-acid writhing means were 33.0 under vehicle, 9.4 under diclofenac and 21.0–25.0 across the six cultivar and amount groups. All extracts also prolonged responses to thermal stimulation. The same preparations reduced movement and exploration and prolonged barbiturate-induced sleep. Those results complicate whether slower responses reflect analgesia, reduced movement or both. The assigned preparations were extracts, the animals did not have Parkinson disease, and the endpoints were evoked behaviors. Background reports of date remedies for headache and sciatica derive from an earlier ethnobotanical citation.
