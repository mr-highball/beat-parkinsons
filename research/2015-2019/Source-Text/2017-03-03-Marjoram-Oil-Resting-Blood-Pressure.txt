Seated blood-pressure response to inhaled sweet marjoram oil
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5336204/
PMID: 28257441
PMCID: PMC5336204
DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0172841
Coverage: Primary full text, Methods, Results and Figures 2–3

Kawai and colleagues tested inhaled Origanum majorana essential oil in 18 healthy resting adults across two protocols. Nine seated participants inhaled oil fragrance and plain air through a mask for six minutes each in randomized order. Over the final two minutes, mean arterial pressure fell with fragrance versus plain air (F(1,8) = 5.769, p = .043). In a separate supine group of nine, mean pressure during the last five minutes of fragrance exposure fell by 3.0 ± 2.6 mmHg versus baseline (p = .004). The supine protocol used a within-person baseline comparison. The study measured resting physiology. Standing pressure, orthostatic symptoms, Parkinson disease and ingestion of fresh leaves were outside the study design.
