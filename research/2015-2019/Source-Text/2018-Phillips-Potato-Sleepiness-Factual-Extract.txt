Title: Boiled potatoes on a Parkinson trial menu
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6175383/

Phillips et al. randomized 47 Parkinson patients to low-fat or ketogenic diets for eight weeks; 44 started and 38 completed. The low-fat plan includes chicken and vegetable soup: four potatoes are cubed and simmered 30–50 minutes with chicken, celery, peas and rice; it also schedules a mixed cannellini-bean hotpot with two potatoes, brought to a boil and simmered 40 minutes. The soup recipe heading says four servings, but its nutrition line calculates per three servings. These are multi-food meals, and the report does not say which individual participants ate the potato-containing recipes. [Original plan, printed pp. 79 and 86](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6175383.1/MDS-33-1306-s002.pdf).

Daytime sleepiness was a reported MDS-UPDRS Part I item. The authors report the change in the ketogenic group was 0.45 points lower than the change in the low-fat group; the comparison is between whole dietary patterns and is does not estimate an effect of potatoes. Participants were instructed to take levodopa at least one hour before or after meals. [Primary trial](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002%2Fmds.27390).
