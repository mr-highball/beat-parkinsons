Title: Comparison of the Effect of Melissa officinalis Capsule and Care Educational Programs on the Intensity of Physical, Mental and Social Symptoms of Premenstrual Syndrome in High School Female Students
DOI: 10.15296/ijwhr.2018.05
Source: https://ijwhr.net/pdf/pdf_IJWHR_227.pdf
First public date: 2017-04-04 on original first page; January 2018 issue.
Coverage: Factual extract of original nine-page journal PDF, Methods, Tables 2 and 5 and trial acknowledgment.
Reviewed: 2026-11-17

Eight hundred students were screened and 200 assigned to four groups of 50: Melissa, education, placebo and control. Methods prescribe two 600 mg capsules daily during menstruation, while the abstract says 600 mg daily; powder and essence descriptions coexist. Table 5 lists 45 active and 46 placebo participants at each timepoint; Table 3 instead prints 50 per arm at baseline.

Printed Table 5, page 22, reports total PSST trajectories matching the 2015 report at all four timepoints. The narrative misnumbers the table. Emotional-domain Table 2 gives placebo baseline 11.8 (SD 4.42), versus 18.80 (SD 4.42) in the 2015 psychological table. Matching later domain values have different time labels. These discrepancies remain unresolved. No isolated depression outcome is established by the PSST totals.

Registry IRCT2014060717998N1 and thesis 92-6805 are shared with the 2015 and Heydari GHQ papers. Aggregate overlap is strong; participant-level mapping and GHQ analysis denominators remain unverified. Treat as one report family rather than independent replication.
