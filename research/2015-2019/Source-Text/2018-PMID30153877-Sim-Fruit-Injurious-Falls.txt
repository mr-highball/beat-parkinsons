Title: Vegetable and fruit intake and injurious falls risk in older women
PMID: 30153877
DOI: 10.1017/S0007114518002155
Authors: Sim et al.
First published: 2018-08-29
Source: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114518002155
Supplement: https://static.cambridge.org/content/id/urn:cambridge.org:id:article:S0007114518002155/resource/name/S0007114518002155sup001.docx
Coverage: primary article methods and results and original supplementary Table S4. Local factual extract.

The prospective analysis used baseline dietary questionnaires in 1,429 Western Australian women aged at least 70 from the CAIFOS/PLSAW study family. Over as many as 14.5 years, 568 participants (39.7%) experienced a fall-related hospital admission. Linked hospital records capture a severe subset of falls. The analysis estimated hazards for admission rather than counting all recurrent falls per person.

Strawberries were specifically queried, then pooled in the other-fruits group with melon, pineapple, apricots, peaches, mango and avocado. The fruit analysis excluded canned or frozen fruit and juice. Median other-fruit intake was 49.3 g/day (IQR 22.0–95.4). Strawberry preparation and amount were not analyzed separately.

Multivariable hazard ratios: total fruit per 150 g/day, 1.03 (95% CI 0.93–1.14), Table 2. Other fruits per 20 g/day, 1.00 (95% CI 0.98–1.03), p=.797, Supplementary Table S4. The category association across total fruit intake groups had log-rank p=.504. Total vegetables per 75 g/day had HR 0.90 (95% CI 0.82–0.99), a distinct exposure. The article's observational analysis cannot assign the pooled estimate to strawberries. It does not report a Parkinson disease stratum or individual strawberry estimates, despite having included the item in the food questionnaire.

Parent identifiers reported by the primary publication are ACTRN12615000750583 and ACTRN12617000640303. These identify related cohort records. The dietary comparison here was observational rather than a randomized strawberry assignment.
