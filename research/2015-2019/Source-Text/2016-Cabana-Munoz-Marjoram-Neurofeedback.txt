Marjoram aroma and a ten-session neurofeedback comparison
Source: https://clinicacirom.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/ART2016576.pdf
DOI: 10.21275/v5i7.ART2016576
Coverage: Complete original paper, Methods, repeated-measures results and Figures 1 and 3

Cabaña-Muñoz and colleagues compared 13 healthy dental-clinic volunteers inhaling marjoram oil via a nasal filter and 13 without the oil during ten sessions of proprietary neurofeedback. The repeated analysis of the device divergence outcome was inconclusive (F=3.19, p=.091). Selected session comparisons at sessions 4 and 8 gave p<.05. The report describes a 65% improvement in a visual analog rating without reporting an attention or reaction-time task. That rating and device electrophysiology do not estimate clinical attention impairment or the effects of eating marjoram.
