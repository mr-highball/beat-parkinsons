Title: Effect of Retort Processing and Storage on Imidazole Dipeptide Content of Chicken Meat
DOI: 10.3136/nskkk.66.210
Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/nskkk/66/6/66_210/_article/-char/en
Official PDF: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/nskkk/66/6/66_210/_pdf/-char/en
First published: 2019-06-15 issue date, released on J-STAGE 2019-07-10. Nippon Shokuhin Kagaku Kogaku Kaishi 66(6):210–214.
Archive scope: Original laboratory paper, formulation, chromatographic measurements and storage results.
Reviewed: 2026-11-19

Kouriki-Nagatomo and colleagues quantified imidazole dipeptides in different raw chicken cuts. The breast tender samples averaged approximately 752 mg anserine, 290 mg carnosine and 6 mg balenine per 100 g. The researchers heated tenders in sealed retort pouches with dilute saline under defined temperatures and found similar total dipeptide contents across the tested processing conditions. Their reported storage measurements showed no significant change under the selected conditions over one year. A sealed pouch and retained liquid differ from an open poaching process or a meal serving in which cooking liquid may be discarded.

The results show that whole chicken can supply the precursor materials studied as concentrated drinks. They do not measure absorption from the frozen preparation, fatigue, or a clinical effect. The article cites the 2009 Shimizu beverage study. Treat the two as separate experiments and keep food composition distinct from treatment efficacy.
