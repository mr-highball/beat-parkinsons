Title: High fish consumption decreased the likelihood of depressive symptoms in community-living older people: a randomized-controlled trial
Source: https://www.jgerontology-geriatrics.com/article/view/113
Full report: https://www.jgerontology-geriatrics.com/article/download/113/145
First published: December 15, 2017
Coverage: Factual extract from Methods and Tables II and III.

The study randomized 96 adults aged at least 60 from senior housing in Mashhad, Iran. The higher-fish group (54) was supplied 480 g of trout weekly, four meals for three months. The comparison group (42) was to eat fish two or fewer times weekly. The report does not specify trout species or cooking technique. Nurses who collected outcomes were masked to allocation.

On the 15-item Geriatric Depression Scale, the higher-fish arm changed from 3.67 (SD 2.08) to 2.83 (SD 1.99), and the comparison arm changed from 4.05 (SD 1.77) to 3.31 (SD 1.54). The adjusted contrast printed in Table II is −0.17 (95% CI −0.48 to 0.15), p = .295. The GHQ-12 contrast had p = .051 and concerns general mental health. Table III gives OR 0.73 (95% CI 0.28 to 1.87) on the dichotomized GDS; the table names GDS below seven and the narrative describes depression odds. The continuous result avoids relying on that coding ambiguity.

The report did not enroll a Parkinson cohort. This publication is separate from Sharifan et al., PMID 28124802, on Caspian white fish and perilla oil.
