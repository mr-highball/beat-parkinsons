Title: Effect of saffron with or without date sugar on pain and anxiety during labor
Source: https://doi.org/10.5812/ircmj.61289
DOI: 10.5812/ircmj.61289
Publication date: December 2018, journal issue
Coverage: Paraphrase of the original article copy, Methods and Table 2.
Original article copy: https://www.academia.edu/82495227/Effect_of_Saffron_with_or_Without_Date_Sugar_on_Intensity_of_Pain_and_Anxiety_During_Labor_in_Primiparous_Females_A_Randomized_Controlled_Trial

The trial compared three labor drinks. Two contained saffron with different sweeteners, date juice or artificial sweetener. The placebo had no saffron. Table 2 analyzed 88 participants across the three groups after emergency cesarean exclusions. For the date-juice versus artificial-sweetener contrast under saffron, the adjusted during-labor pain difference was −0.3 points on a 0–10 VAS (95% CI −1.0 to 0.4, p=.557). At one hour after delivery, the two active groups differed on a separate pain analysis (p=.015). Median pain at that point was zero in the date group and one in the artificial-sweetener group. Different stages and estimators should remain separate.

The active contrast estimates the extra contribution of sweetener within a saffron intervention. The result is inconclusive for the prespecified during-labor comparison. Postpartum pain, the co-exposure and the processed date preparation limit transfer to dried dates and Parkinson pain.
