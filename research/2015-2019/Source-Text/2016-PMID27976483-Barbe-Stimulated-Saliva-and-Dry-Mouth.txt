Title: Hyposalivation and xerostomia among Parkinson's disease patients and its impact on quality of life
Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/odi.12622
PMID: 27976483
DOI: 10.1111/odi.12622
Publication date: 2016-12-14
Source depth: Accepted article scan, methods and Table 2, and publisher publication history
Date basis: Wiley first public date in December 2016. Indexed version-of-record date is February 24, 2017. The final issue was published in May 2017.
Design: Matched observational oral-health study

The study included 30 people with Parkinson disease and 30 age- and sex-matched controls. The control recruitment criteria excluded people reporting dry mouth, so the observed dryness count of 15/30 versus 0/30 does not estimate an unbiased difference in symptom prevalence. Mean stimulated whole saliva was 0.4 versus 0.8 mL/min (reported p<.001). Table 2 and the body give low-flow classifications of 26/30 in Parkinson participants and 13/26 in controls (p=.001). The denominator of 26 belongs to that result even though 30 controls were enrolled, and its reason is not clear in the read results. Eleven of 15 Parkinson participants without reported dryness met the low-flow threshold.

The collection method used chewing stimulation under controlled sampling conditions. Dryness was elicited by a self-report question and graded in those endorsing it. Mouth-floor pooling and a general activities-of-daily-living scale were recorded, and Table 2 provides no isolated drooling result. The report describes possible care options for dry mouth without testing whether they alter drooling. There is no tested raw fig.
