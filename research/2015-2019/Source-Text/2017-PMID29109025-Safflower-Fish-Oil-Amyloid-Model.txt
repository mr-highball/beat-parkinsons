Title: Fish oil feeding attenuates neuroinflammatory gene expression without concomitant changes in brain eicosanoids and docosanoids in a mouse model of Alzheimer's disease
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29109025/
PMID: 29109025
DOI: 10.1016/j.bbi.2017.11.002
First public article date: 2017-11-03
Journal issue: 2018-03
Accessed: 2026-11-19
Text coverage: Distilled original indexed abstract and the later review's description of the dietary formulations. Full original figures remain a retrieval lead.

Hopperton and colleagues compared dietary fish oil with a safflower-rich low omega-3 diet in mice, also examining fat-1 mice. They infused amyloid-beta 1–40 or a control. The safflower-fed group showed an increase in inflammation-related gene-expression categories after amyloid infusion. This dietary comparison did not show corresponding differences in brain eicosanoids or docosanoids. The result concerns susceptibility to a brain amyloid challenge with different fatty-acid compositions. It supplies no independent clinical effect of cold-pressed safflower oil on swallowing or bradykinesia and no Parkinson participants.

The investigators reported related microglial and astrocyte work separately. Potential overlap in experiments requires methods-level lineage verification before treating them as independent.
