Title: A Rare Case of Mango Tea-associated Hepatotoxicity
Authors: Jonathan Pinto, Cesar Hidalgo, Jadesola Fapohunda, James Robilotti, Joan Culpepper-Morgan
Original poster text (author-associated copy): https://www.academia.edu/90688891/A_Rare_Case_of_Mango_Tea_associated_Hepatotoxicity
Publisher identifier: https://doi.org/10.14309/00000434-201710001-02198
Official meeting program: https://acgcdn.gi.org/acgblog/uploads/2017/09/ACG2017_FinalProgram_9-13.pdf
Date: October 2017 supplement, abstract 2197, printed page S1210
Coverage: factual extract of author abstract for a single case, prepared 2026-11-14

A 53-year-old patient presented with elevated liver tests, epigastric pain and a month of fatigue and anorexia. She recalled drinking a glass of homemade tea made by boiling whole mangoes three to five times a week in the prior month. She also used Excedrin intermittently. The authors attributed the suspected liver injury to the tea, gave it a RUCAM/CIOMS score of 7 and reported quick improvement after cessation. The case concerns an adverse event after a cooked preparation and contains no quantified fatigue severity, raw fruit contrast, cultivar or Parkinson diagnosis.
