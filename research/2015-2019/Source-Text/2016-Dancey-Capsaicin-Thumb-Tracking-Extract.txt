Title: Interactive effect of acute pain and motor learning acquisition on sensorimotor integration and motor learning outcomes
Authors: Erin Dancey and colleagues
Date: 2016-08-17 first online
Identifiers: PMID 27535371; PMCID PMC5102313; DOI 10.1152/jn.00337.2016
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5102313/
Coverage: Original factual extract of experimental Methods and behavioral Results.

The experimental task tracked visual targets with the thumb. The comparison was topical capsaicin cream versus control skin lotion. The capsaicin condition was more accurate after application but before task practice (p < .05) and after task acquisition (p < .05). The group comparison at retention was p=.06. Both groups improved with practice. The authors note larger relative gain in the control group, which started with more error. The experiment did not measure written letter length or Parkinson-related micrographia.
