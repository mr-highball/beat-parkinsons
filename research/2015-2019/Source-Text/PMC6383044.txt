Title: Increased Intraepidermal Nerve Fiber Degeneration and Impaired Regeneration Relate to Symptoms and Deficits in Parkinson's Disease
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6383044/
PMID: 30837937
PMCID: PMC6383044
DOI: 10.3389/fneur.2019.00111
Publication date: 2019-02-14
Coverage: Selected factual text distilled from the original PMC article XML and included tables; supplementary files are separate.

Twenty-three people diagnosed with Parkinson disease and 10 matched controls had skin biopsies and assessments for peripheral nerve dysfunction. The clinical instruments included King's Parkinson's Pain Scale. The Parkinson group mean was 23.05 with SD 15.4. The table does not report a control-group score for that Parkinson-specific scale. Heat and cold pain-threshold comparisons between groups were inconclusive.

The Parkinson group had lower skin nerve fiber density and shorter mean regenerating axonal length. These cross-sectional results suggest stratifying pain by established peripheral neuropathy, clinical phenotype and levodopa exposure. The study did not collect a whole-date exposure or test a fruit contrast.
