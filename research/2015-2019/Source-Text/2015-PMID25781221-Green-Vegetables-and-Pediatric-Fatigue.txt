Title: Steenbruggen and colleagues compared dietary advice for unexplained fatigue in children
Source: https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/7/3/1965
PMID: 25781221
PMCID: PMC4377893
DOI: 10.3390/nu7031965
Publication date: 2015-03-13
Reviewed: 2026-11-25
Evidence depth: Original methods, baseline and outcome tables, including the adjusted component analysis.

The intervention advised four dietary components, green vegetables, beef, whole milk and full-fat butter. The comparison used an earlier group from the same clinic. Ninety-eight children entered the analysis and 88 contributed to the Table 4 adjusted component analysis. The green-vegetable component analysis favored the intervention for cognitive fatigue by 16.45 points (95% CI 2.27–30.63, p=.024). The general-fatigue contrast was 8.88 points (95% CI −1.23 to 18.99). The displayed preplanned intervention description groups green vegetables and reports adherence to the category. It identifies no green-bean-specific contrast or intake by preparation. The population, allocation, and outcome differences limit use as direct evidence for the frozen Parkinson claim. The authors also report a different whole-diet result for need to sleep.
