Giacosa and colleagues randomized 126 adults with functional dyspepsia to a blend (65) or placebo (61) for 28 days. Each capsule contained 20 mg standardized ginger extract and 100 mg standardized artichoke leaf extract, taken before lunch and dinner in the study. Both arms received dietary advice. The supplier provided active and placebo capsules.

The primary outcome concerned global dyspepsia change. Epigastric pain was a secondary outcome. Table 4 reports a between-arm pain trajectory contrast of −0.173 units on its transformed ordinal scale (95% CI −0.281 to −0.065, p=.002). Table 3 shows an imbalance in starting severity categories, including five participants with severe pain in the blend group and sixteen in placebo. Table 4 distinguishes intercepts from slopes. The 14-day p=.017 quoted by the 2025 poster refers to the global response in the blend arm. It is distinct from the pain contrast.

There was no ginger-only or artichoke-only arm. The comparison cannot attribute the pain result to ginger, and it differs from eating grated ginger or measuring pain in Parkinson disease. A separate constipation effect is unreported. The 2003 Holtmann trial has an artichoke-only placebo comparison in a different cohort and cannot substitute for a factorial arm here.
