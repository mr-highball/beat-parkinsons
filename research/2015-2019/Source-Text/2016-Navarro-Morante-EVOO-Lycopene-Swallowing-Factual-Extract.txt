Title: Extra virgin olive oil and lycopene versus water for dry mouth
Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jop.12487
Format: Factual extract of original methods and Table 2.

Navarro Morante and colleagues, first published August 23, 2016. The randomized double-blind study allocated 30 adults per arm to oral extra-virgin olive oil plus lycopene or colored water. Participants swallowed the dose following local oral application. Forty-six completed, 25 in the active arm and 21 on placebo. The trial evaluated xerostomia, not Parkinson disease.
Table 2 item 2 gives dryness-related difficulty swallowing. The active arm changed from 7.4 to 5.6 cm, within-arm p = .36. Placebo changed from 5.0 to 4.4 cm, p = .61. The reported intergroup item comparison is p = .26. Baseline scores differ appreciably. The larger raw decline is inconclusive and the product combines ingredients. It gives no isolated extra-virgin olive oil effect.
Author-shared original article and Table 2: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/306415660_Natural_products_for_the_management_of_xerostomia_a_randomized_double-blinded_placebo-controlled_clinical_trial .
