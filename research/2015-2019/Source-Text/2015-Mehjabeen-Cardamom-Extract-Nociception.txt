Title: The role of Elettaria cardamomum in inflammatory, gastrointestinal and stress disorders

Publication date: 2015
Date basis: Journal volume 4, issue 6. Original accepted 2015-03-13.
Coverage: Original publisher article, materials and methods and Tables 1–5.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26

Mehjabeen and colleagues used a crude extract of purchased whole Elettaria cardamomum fruit with voucher 001116-03. The original delegates extraction detail to an earlier method and does not match the resulting extract to ground culinary spice.

For oral extract versus saline, Table 4 gives first- and second-phase mean acetic-acid writhes of 40 and 20 at 200 mg/kg, 31 and 16 at 300 mg/kg, and 27 and 15 at 500 mg/kg, versus 87 and 54 in controls. The table reports five animals per group. The first two hours of the tail-flick table favor longer response latencies after extract. Formalin paw licking has inconsistent apparent dose rank and is separately measured. The hot-plate narrative claims significance for extract, while the original table marks only a single aspirin result. Standard errors and timing differences are given in the original.

The same report describes sedation and separate open-field activity below saline. Fewer behavioral responses after the crude extract permit a motor or sedation explanation alongside altered nociception. No Parkinson model, food-powder comparison, clinical pain outcome, allocation concealment or blinded observation is verified. The original shows an experimental behavioral signal with a different preparation and substantial causal uncertainty for the frozen clinical hypothesis.
