Title: Abstracts from the 2019 Annual Meeting of the Society of General Internal Medicine
Publication date: 2019-05-14
PMID: 31090026
PMCID: PMC6582171
DOI: 10.1007/s11606-019-05007-5
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6582171/
Text coverage: Distilled clinical vignette and program notes from the complete printed S99–S867 supplement.

S517. A post-bypass patient with chronic kidney disease had increased intake of oxalate-rich vegetables including spinach and okra before biopsy-confirmed oxalate nephropathy. Dialysis and other care were required, with recovery to baseline over three months. Preparation was unspecified, and the case did not isolate a spinach effect or measure Parkinson dyskinesia or hallucinations.

S522–S523. Nausea, vomiting and abdominal symptoms began after a mixed chicken salad. The abstract reports later liver enzyme elevation while taking rivaroxaban, withdrawal of the drug, and resolution. The initial meal history and later drug interpretation both belong to this one case, with neither poached-chicken exposure verified nor fatigue assessed.

S551. In one patient with diabetes and end-stage kidney disease, two episodes of low glucose accompanied by symptoms improved after orange juice. The measurements reported were 37 and 67 mg/dL before the two administrations. This was a short-term response in one patient, without raw-orange exposure, an assigned comparator or a Parkinson falls or constipation outcome. The patient had recurrent hypoglycemia after the individual rescue episodes.

S605 and S687. One patient with a tick-borne illness reported needing more coffee during a drive after fatigue began. The sequence matters, and the abstract does not measure response to coffee. A separate scurvy vignette describes coffee in a restricted diet because of chronic diarrhea, with vitamin C replacement for the diagnosed deficiency. Neither is an assigned coffee comparison. The abstracts also contain a coffee-harvesting work history in a kidney disease vignette and a coffee-ground emesis descriptor. They do not report coffee consumption effects.

S695. A clinic garden distributed mixed produce, including green beans, kale, watermelon and chard, over three growing seasons. The reported measures concern produce weight, volunteers and opportunities for patient interactions. Clinical symptom changes and individual consumption were not measured. The member reports chard as a general produce category, with no further specification of variety.

Date fields, figure labels, Cape Cod, names matching ginger, Melissa and rosemary, and a salmon-colored rash account for other lexical matches. The orange-juice treatment is separate from references to places, colors and Agent Orange elsewhere in the proceedings.
