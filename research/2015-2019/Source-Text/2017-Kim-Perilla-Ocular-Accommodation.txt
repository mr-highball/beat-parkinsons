Perilla leaf extract and screen-induced ocular fatigue
Source: https://pubs.sciepub.com/jfnr/5/8/4/index.html
DOI: 10.12691/jfnr-5-8-4
Coverage: original factual methods and Table 1.

Kim and colleagues first published July 26, 2017. The placebo comparison used a prepared aqueous extract of dried Perilla frutescens var. acuta leaves. In Table 1 the extract group's binocular near-point distance was 7.96 ± 2.97 cm before and 7.27 ± 3.25 after two hours of screen use. The placebo group was 9.00 ± 2.45 and 9.67 ± 2.48 cm. The results text gives p < .001 within each arm, without a clear between-group change estimate in the table. The abstract and discussion say 30 participants, while Methods report 30 per group. This trial measured eye accommodation after screen use. It gives no Parkinson fatigue or culinary dried-leaf estimate.
NCT02641470 describes Park and colleagues' separate bog-bilberry extract study, which links to PMID 27538497. It does not identify this perilla study. No matching official registration was verified for Kim.
