Title: Separating “good” from “bad” faecal dysbiosis – evidence from two cross-sectional studies
Publication year: 2018
PMCID: PMC6276176
DOI: 10.1186/s40608-018-0207-3
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6276176/
Text coverage: Distilled original methods and results with comparison to ECO T1P147.

The test cohort has the same 90-person sample, sex counts and baseline characteristics as the congress poster. An additional validation group assessed a constructed dysbiosis index. The article centers on metformin and non-nutritive sweeteners, including sweeteners added to coffee or tea. The poster separately reports an association of coffee with dysbiosis (p = 0.026) without a coefficient. The full paper's mention of coffee as the carrier for some sweetener exposure does not confirm a fatigue response to coffee. The cross-sectional cohorts provide no Parkinson fatigue result.
