Title: Objective colonic dysfunction versus self-reported constipation in Parkinson disease
PMID: 28157109
DOI: 10.3233/JPD-161050
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28157109/
Publication date: 2017
Date basis: Journal of Parkinson's Disease 7(2), pp. 359–367.
Checked: 2026-11-25
Review depth: Original structured abstract.

Knudsen and colleagues assessed colonic transit time, CT colon volume, gastric emptying and patient symptom questionnaires in 32 people with Parkinson disease and 26 controls. The abstract reports prolonged colonic transit in 79% of the Parkinson group. Subjective constipation ranged from 3% to 38% depending on the questionnaire, and objective versus subjective measures agreed poorly.

The clinical report substantiates a specific plausible bridge from intestinal transit to one component of Parkinson bowel dysfunction. Colonic transit and the rat experiment's acute whole-intestine dye progression still differ. This study did not test basil or report a basil-specific effect.
