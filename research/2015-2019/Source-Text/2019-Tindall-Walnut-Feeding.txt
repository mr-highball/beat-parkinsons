Tindall AM, Petersen KS, Skulas-Ray AC, et al. Replacing Saturated Fat With Walnuts or Vegetable Oils Improves Central Blood Pressure and Serum Lipids in Adults at Risk for Cardiovascular Disease: A Randomized Controlled-Feeding Trial. J Am Heart Assoc. 2019 May 1;8:e011512.
PMID: 31039663; PMCID: PMC6512082; DOI: 10.1161/JAHA.118.011512; NCT: NCT02210767.
Original: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6512082/
Coverage: original Methods, sample menu, Results and supplementary table descriptions.

Forty-five adults at cardiovascular risk were enrolled and 36 completed the randomized three-period crossover. After a two-week run-in, participants received a whole-walnut diet, walnut-free fatty-acid-matched diet and walnut-free diet replacing most alpha-linolenic acid with oleic acid, each for six weeks. The sample menu lists flaxseed and high-linoleic safflower oils in the matched diet and high-oleic and high-linoleic safflower oils in the replacement diet. It does not identify a walnut-oil intervention. Primary central systolic blood pressure was unchanged; central diastolic pressure changed −1.78 ± 1.0 mm Hg after walnuts versus 0.15 ± 0.7 after the replacement diet, p=.04. The walnut versus matched comparison was not significant, p=.20. The study does not report a Parkinson dystonia or muscle-spasm outcome.

The original identifies registry NCT02210767; a later same-study secondary analysis instead cites NCT01235832, whose official record is an avocado study. The discrepancy remains unresolved and does not establish shared cohorts.
