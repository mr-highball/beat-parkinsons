Title: Effect of Hazelnut Oil on Muscle Cell Signalling and Differentiation
Authors: Ileana Terruzzi and colleagues
Year: 2018
PMID: 30210078
DOI: 10.5650/jos.ess18086
Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jos/67/10/67_ess18086/_pdf
Coverage: Original factual extract of the publisher's methods and cellular outcomes.

The two source materials were Ordu and Tonda Gentile. Methods 2.2 and 2.3 distinguish cuticle oil made with supercritical CO2 at 50 °C from kernel oil made after roasting and mechanical pressing. About 41 °C was the exiting temperature of the pressed oil. The manuscript does not give a roasting temperature. Each oil was added to cultured mouse skeletal muscle cells. Culture medium without oil and commercial olive oil supplied comparisons. The reported oil concentration was 10% in the medium.

For the proliferative phase, Section 3.1 reports higher p70 S6 kinase phosphorylation with Ordu cuticle oil and Tonda kernel oil at the 30-, 60- and 240-minute assessments. Ordu kernel oil, Tonda cuticle oil and the olive comparator did not show the same reported change in this phase. In differentiating cells, Figure 5 reports higher late myosin heavy chain with Tonda kernel oil. The late levels for Ordu kernel and both cuticle oils were similar to the untreated differentiation control. Section 3.3 and Figure 7 distinguish a larger myotube diameter after Ordu kernel oil from longer myotubes after the two cuticle oils. These are cellular development measures. No whole-animal muscle tone, passive limb resistance, handwriting or clinical Parkinson's outcome was measured.
