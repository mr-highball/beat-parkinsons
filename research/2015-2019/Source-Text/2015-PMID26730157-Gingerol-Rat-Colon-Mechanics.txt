Title: Effect of gingerol on colonic motility via inhibition of calcium channel currents in rats
PMID: 26730157
PMCID: PMC4690175
DOI: 10.3748/wjg.v21.i48.13466
Source: https://www.wjgnet.com/1007-9327/full/v21/i48/13466.htm
Published: 2015-12-28, World Journal of Gastroenterology 21, 13466–13472.
Archive scope: Factual summary of original methods and smooth-muscle and channel outcomes.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Cai and colleagues applied gingerol directly to isolated longitudinal muscle from rat distal colon. They report 13.3% inhibition of spontaneous contraction at 25 μmol/L and 80.5% at 100 μmol/L, with a separate L-type calcium-current comparison in dissociated cells. The muscle and cell endpoints are ex vivo. There was no ingested ginger, transit measurement in a whole animal, or constipation outcome in people. A later public thread cites the experiment as a hypothesis about human bowel movement. The original establishes a local effect under its measuring conditions, with transfer to that broader claim unresolved.
