Title: Effect of Experimental Hand Pain on Training-Induced Changes in Motor Performance and Corticospinal Excitability
Authors: Nicolas Mavromatis and colleagues
Date: 2017-02-04
Identifiers: PMID 28165363; PMCID PMC5332958; DOI 10.3390/brainsci7020015
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5332958/
Coverage: Original factual extract of primary Methods and Results, especially Figures 2 and 4.

Healthy participants controlled a cursor by pinching a force sensor. The topical capsaicin group had a higher overall performance index reflecting accuracy and speed (p=.03). Both pain and control groups adapted comparably with training. Movement time was not clearly affected by group. The intervention was local experimental pain. It did not assess handwriting, clinical Parkinson motor signs or smoked paprika.
