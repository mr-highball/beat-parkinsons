Title: Sub-chronic consumption of dark chocolate and cognitive testing

Sumiyoshi and colleagues randomized 20 healthy young adults to dark or white chocolate for 30 days. Eighteen contributed analyses, with ten in the dark chocolate group and eight in the white chocolate group. The dark chocolate arm improved from baseline on selected Stroop word and digital-cancellation measures. The cognitive tables report changes within groups. The study did not measure anxiety severity or enroll a Parkinson cohort.

The conference member 1P-115 matches the researchers, 18 analyzed participants and split, 30-day exposure, and cognitive assessments. Use one family for the two reports. The publication retains the differences in reporting timing and explicitly accounts for exclusions from analysis.
