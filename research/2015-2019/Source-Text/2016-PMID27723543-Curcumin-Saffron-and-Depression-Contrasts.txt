The 12-week randomized study of major depression analyzed 28 people on low-dose curcumin extract, 33 on high-dose curcumin extract, 26 on low-dose curcumin plus saffron and 36 on placebo. The analysis followed a placebo run-in with exclusions for large early symptom shifts. Analysis required a week-four observation and used last observation carried forward. A total of 111 participants met treatment-completion requirements.

The primary depression scale was the IDS-SR30. The published p=.031 applies to all active arms pooled versus placebo. The planned contrast of low-dose curcumin plus saffron against the same curcumin dose without saffron gives p=.766 for group-by-time, with no clear incremental effect. Table 2 reports mean scores of 34.65 to 24.38 for the combination and 35.29 to 23.04 for low-dose curcumin alone. The descriptive difference in change is +1.98 points for the add-on, with no corresponding interval in the article. The anxiety scale add-on interactions were also inconclusive. The 65% versus 35% atypical-depression subgroup response comparison pools active treatments.

The trial studies a supplement add-on in adults with major depression. There is no saffron-only arm, separately analyzed insomnia endpoint or Parkinson cohort. The isolated add-on comparison and pooled active comparison answer different questions.
