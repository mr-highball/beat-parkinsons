Title: Acute consumption of peppermint and chamomile teas produce contrasting effects on cognition and mood in healthy young adults.
DOI: 10.14719/pst.2016.3.3.246
First published: 2016
Authors: Moss M, Jones R, Moss L, Rippengale R, Wesnes K
Journal: Plant Science Today
Source: https://doi.org/10.14719/pst.2016.3.3.246
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 6 review).

Verified Round 6 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Three-arm randomized controlled trial (Plant Science Today 2016;3(3):327-336; Northumbria University, UK; n=180 healthy young adults) comparing acute consumption of brewed peppermint tea vs brewed chamomile tea vs hot water control. Peppermint tea significantly improved speed of memory/attention, working memory, long-term memory, and subjective alertness compared with chamomile tea and hot water (p<0.05), whereas chamomile slowed memory/attention speed. Limitations: acute single-session trial in healthy young adults (non-PD), non-blindable sensory taste/aroma of brewed herbal tea vs hot water, not indexed in PubMed (verified via Crossref DOI 10.14719/pst.2016.3.3.246).
