Title: The efficacy of traditional formulation on quality of life and fatigue in multiple sclerosis patients: a randomized double-blind placebo-control clinical trial
Authors: F. Namjooyan, R. Ghanavati, N. Majdinasab and H. Rezaei Zadeh
Journal: Journal of Contemporary Medical Sciences 5(2), 96–100
Publication: 26 April 2019, according to original article and journal page
DOI: 10.22317/jcms.v5i2.569
Original: https://www.jocms.org/index.php/jcms/article/download/569/305/1193
Journal identity: https://www.jocms.org/index.php/jcms/article/view/569
Coverage: Factual extract from publisher original, especially Methods, Results and Table 2, printed page 97.

At Khuzestan MS association, July–November 2018, 60 adults aged 20–50 years with multiple sclerosis and EDSS 2–5.5 were randomized 1:1 to a formulation or placebo. The Methods says two parts ajwain and one part Iranian borago were soaked in water for 24 hours, one part cinnamon for 72 hours, then distilled; the mixture or placebo was given at 15 cc/day for three months. The authors refer generally to Cinnamomum verum/zeylanicum bark in the introduction but do not clearly identify separately the exact source material or botanical authentication of the cinnamon in the formula. This mixture cannot isolate cinnamon. The text also says four capsules daily, while the relation of the 15 cc volume to capsules is not clarified.

The article reports 51 completed, with Table 1 indicating 26 in the formulation arm and 25 in placebo. Fatigue severity was assessed at baseline and monthly. Table 2 gives formulation means 3.729, 3.142, 2.853, 2.213 from baseline to months 1, 2 and 3; placebo means 3.520, 4.186, 4.652, 5.066. The reported overall between-group term was p=.353, F=1.0464; the group-by-time interaction was p<.001, F=126.393. The paper does not show an isolated cinnamon comparison or Parkinson cohort.

Important reporting uncertainties: Results say nine dropped out, but the listed reasons (three disease progression, one relocation, one sensitivity and five noncompliance) total ten. Table 2 labels dispersion as SD yet values such as baseline 0.027 are not consistent with the width of its printed 95% confidence interval 3.355–4.103 for 26 people; no correction was located in this source check. Its third-month formulation value is printed 2.213 ± 0.32, unlike other rows. Preserve table means as printed without treating its dispersion labels as verified precision. No trial registration number appears in the original paper.
