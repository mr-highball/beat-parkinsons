Review depth: Original Results, Figure 1 and motor assessment methods.

The study compared lifelong supplementary curcumin against a shared bioperine and vehicle background in a different Huntington mouse model from Hickey 2012. Figure 1 shows better rotarod performance in the exposed model group over the observed period. Its rotarod comparisons included eight model mice and five wild-type mice per condition. A second outcome, limb clasping, also favored the supplemented model group. The control contrast assigns curcumin while retaining both shared ingredients.

The 2012 experiment found shorter time on its rotating rod with dietary curcumin. The two experiments differ in model, formulation and observation setting. Neither trial compares culinary turmeric or reports spontaneous fall counts in people. Record the signs for the actual animal tasks and preserve uncertainty about clinical transfer.
