Title: Impact of Agaricus bisporus Mushroom Consumption on Gut Health Markers in Healthy Adults.
PMID: 30279332
PMCID: PMC6213353
DOI: 10.3390/nu10101402
First published: 2018
Authors: Hess J, Wang Q, Gould T, Slavin J
Journal: Nutrients
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6213353/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Randomized open-label 10-day crossover trial in n=32 healthy adults (companion to Hess 2017 PMID:28647383 satiety report) comparing 226 g/d sliced roasted Agaricus bisporus mushrooms vs protein-matched 28 g/d meat. Mushrooms significantly increased fecal wet weight (122.42 +/- 58.74 vs 94.62 +/- 56.58 g/stool, p=0.002) and altered fecal microbiota (Bacteroidetes p=0.0002, Firmicutes p=0.0009), but produced NO significant differences in stool frequency (4.25 vs 4.13/5d, p=0.35), Bristol stool consistency (3.12 vs 3.35, p=0.11), fecal pH (p=0.77), SCFAs, or day-1 constipation rating (0.36 vs 0.66, p=0.52). Total GI symptoms (gas p=0.0452, flatulence p=0.0002) were higher on days 1-2 and resolved by day 10; 31.25% had visible undigested mushroom fragments. Limits: healthy non-constipated adults, null frequency/consistency/constipation.
