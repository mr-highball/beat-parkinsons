Title: The effects of kiwi fruit consumption in students with chronic insomnia symptoms: a randomized controlled trial.
DOI: 10.1007/s41105-017-0095-9
First published: 2017
Authors: Nodtvedt OO, Hansen AL, Bjorvatn B, Pallesen S
Journal: Sleep and Biological Rhythms
Source: https://doi.org/10.1007/s41105-017-0095-9
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 5 review).

Verified Round 5 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Randomized controlled parallel trial (Sleep Biol Rhythms 2017;15:159-166; DOI: 10.1007/s41105-017-0095-9) evaluating 74 university students with chronic insomnia symptoms randomized after a 1-week baseline to eat either 130 g raw kiwifruit or 130 g raw pear (active fruit control) 1 hour before bedtime daily for 4 weeks. Outcomes spanned 12 variables across daily sleep diaries, wrist actigraphy, Bergen Insomnia Scale, and PSQI. Statistically significant group x time interactions favoring kiwifruit over pear emerged on 2 of 12 outcomes—subjective diary sleep quality and diary daytime functioning—whereas the remaining 10 variables, including ALL objective actigraphy parameters (sleep onset latency, sleep efficiency, total sleep time, WASO) and global insomnia scales, showed NO statistically significant between-group difference. Key strengths: direct whole raw kiwifruit RCT with an active fruit comparator in a chronic insomnia population. Key limitations: open-label (fruit identity cannot be blinded), young university student non-PD sample, null objective actigraphy and 10/12 endpoints, paywalled full text.
