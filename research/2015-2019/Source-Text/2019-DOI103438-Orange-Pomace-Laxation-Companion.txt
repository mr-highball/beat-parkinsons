The randomized, blinded study assigned 221 healthy adults to beverages with or without added citrus pomace for three weeks. The pomace group had greater weekly stool frequency than the control group (p = 0.0281) and slightly softer stools (p = 0.04). Gas and bloating scores also increased. The abstract discusses microbial changes, with no evidence there that bifidobacteria mediated the bowel result.

The journal report identifies the registration and agrees with the poster on sample size, intervention, follow-up, stool-frequency finding and the adverse gastrointestinal symptoms. The conference report, registration and journal article belong to one study. A separate juice–pomace study on post-meal glycemia in Nutrition member OR29-04 is a different experiment with a different outcome.

Fit to Round 1 claim 0878: the product supplies a processed fiber fraction. The trial enrolled healthy adults and reports bowel measures with no standalone Parkinson constipation-severity result or raw-orange treatment arm. It adds provenance to the existing minimal signal. The later null crossover (PMID 36102329) remains a separate study.
