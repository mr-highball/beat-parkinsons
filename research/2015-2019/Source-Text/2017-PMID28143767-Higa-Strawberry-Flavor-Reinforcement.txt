Title: Higa et al. — strawberry-flavored milk reward and separate learning and motivation outcomes in mice

DOI: 10.1016/j.bbr.2017.01.041

First published: 2017-01-29 online per the journal article embedded in the institutional dissertation, Behavioural Brain Research 323, 100–110.
Coverage: Embedded 2017 journal article, restricted to that article's methods, apparatus, behavioral findings and figure captions. Other dissertation experiments are separate.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

Methods describe strawberry-flavored Nesquik with nonfat milk as the shared reward in the probabilistic-learning and motivation tasks. The group comparison varied striatal dopamine D1 receptor suppression. The paper reports impaired reward-associated learning, with punishment-associated learning and the progressive ratio motivation measure relatively preserved. The report separates the task changes from broader spatial learning and response to a dopamine agonist.

The formulation and design are consistent with the 2012 Young et al. member M29. Treat the reports as likely related, without tallying a second cohort until animal overlap is verified. The milkshake flavor is a common reinforcement stimulus. The article has no raw strawberry comparison, human Parkinson cohort or clinical fall or attention endpoint.
