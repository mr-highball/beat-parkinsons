Title: Postural deformities in Parkinson's disease
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4731916/
PMID: 26835153
PMCID: PMC4731916
DOI: 10.1186/s40734-016-0029-8
Publication date: 2016-01-29
Date basis: First publication date in the article record.
Coverage: Paraphrased original methods, results, Tables 1–2 and discussion.

The investigators photographed 120 people with Parkinson disease who could stand unaided. They measured four postural angles and used the on period for patients with motor fluctuations. Neck flexion, forward trunk bending, knee flexion and lateral bending each correlated with disease stage in the univariate analysis. The simple levodopa-equivalent association concerned forward trunk bending. In the multiple regression, total levodopa-equivalent dose was the most explanatory included variable for lateral bending.

The discussion proposes difficulty swallowing and drooling as possible consequences of neck flexion. The study estimated associations for angles, with no rated drooling, saliva sampling or assigned food exposure. Its photographic measurements were adapted from the prior Oeda body-angle work.
