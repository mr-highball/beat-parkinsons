Cilantro leaf-and-stalk extract and motor behavior in rat offspring
First published online 2019-08-10. PMID 31408720. DOI 10.1016/j.fct.2019.110755.
Original institutional copy: https://repositorio.museu-goeldi.br/server/api/core/bitstreams/ee74e8d2-2c7c-449f-981e-1ff749e092cf/content
Coverage: Factual original Methods 2.1 and 2.5–2.6 and Results 3.4–3.5.
Rodrigues and colleagues prepared a water extract from freeze-dried cilantro leaves and stalks. Pregnant rats received extract or water during gestation and lactation with or without methylmercury exposure. One 30-day-old male from each of 14 litters per group contributed to the motor comparisons.
Figure 3A reports open-field distance of 20.47 ± 2.731 m for toxin plus extract and 14.35 ± 2.948 m for toxin alone, with p < .05 by the figure's comparison marker. Extract alone was 21.92 ± 3.680 m and control was 19.43 ± 4.349 m, with no significant difference reported. The authors also reported fewer beam slips in the toxin-plus-extract group. Distance covered during spontaneous exploration and limb slips do not quantify walking velocity. This developmental toxicant model does not measure Parkinson walking speed or direct ingestion of dried leaf.
