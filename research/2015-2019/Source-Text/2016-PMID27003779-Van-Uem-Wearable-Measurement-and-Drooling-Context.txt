Title: A Viewpoint on Wearable Technology-Enabled Measurement of Wellbeing and Health-Related Quality of Life in Parkinson's Disease
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4927928/
PMID: 27003779
PMCID: PMC4927928
DOI: 10.3233/JPD-150740
Publication date: 2016-03-10
Date basis: First online date on the journal site.
Coverage: Paraphrased original viewpoint, including Table 1 and the participant perspectives.

The article discusses wearables for home measurements of movement, sleep and selected cardiovascular or behavioral signals. Drooling is listed in the nonmotor context. Table 1 does not propose a dedicated saliva-loss signal or validate a device against rated drooling. First-person accounts describe unpredictable off periods and the usefulness of feedback. Those accounts concern experience with symptoms and measurement, without a fig exposure or oral outcome comparison.

The cited Ferreira SENSE-PARK feasibility study (PMID 26059091) enrolled 22 people, including 11 who used the system. Its primary outcome was dropout and its secondary outcomes included usability. The system was not evaluated as a treatment for drooling.
