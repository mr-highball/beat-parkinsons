Title: Mucuna pruriens in Parkinson disease: A double-blind, randomized, controlled, crossover study
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5539737/
PMID: 28679598
PMCID: PMC5539737
DOI: 10.1212/WNL.0000000000004175
First published online: 2017-07-05
Trial identifier: NCT02680977 (local registration: Trials/NCT02680977.txt)
Text coverage: factual extraction of the original acute motor comparisons.

Eighteen people with advanced Parkinson disease received randomized, single-dose challenges on six study days. Comparators included placebo, different Mucuna seed-powder preparations with or without benserazide and pharmaceutical levodopa with or without benserazide. The higher-dose Mucuna arm had an earlier ON response (p=.008) and mean ON duration of 221 versus 177 minutes (p<.001) compared with dispersible levodopa plus benserazide. At comparable levodopa doses and the same benserazide status, the Mucuna comparisons instead gave ON duration approximately 20% shorter than pharmaceutical levodopa (each p<.05). The comparisons change with dose and the presence of a decarboxylase inhibitor.

This trial measured the duration of motor response to a single dose, not hours of OFF over repeated home diary days. It supplies a plausible concurrent-treatment explanation for the motor observations in a later case that took both Mucuna and a cumin-containing constipation tablet. It gives no cumin comparison.

Supplement check on 2026-11-19. Table e-2 gives group UPDRS III motor totals, motor-state timing and dyskinesia scores. Table e-4 and Figure e-2 give pharmacokinetic and aggregate motor comparisons for a separate four-patient Italian substudy. The supplementary text discloses that one participant's plasma levodopa curves had already appeared in Cassani et al. 2016 (PMID 27206902, participant 4 in that publication's Table 2). Keep this curve in one family when reviewing the two reports. The official supplementary figures and tables give no separate numeric resting-tremor comparison. The chronic report from the same registration has a different follow-up and tolerability outcome.
