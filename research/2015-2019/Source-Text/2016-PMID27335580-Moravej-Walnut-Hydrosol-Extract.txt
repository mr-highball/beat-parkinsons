Title: Chemical Composition and the Effect of Walnut Hydrosol on Glycemic Control of Patients With Type 1 Diabetes
PMID: 27335580
PMCID: PMC4914821
DOI: 10.5812/ijem.34726
Source: https://brieflands.com/journals/ijem/articles/17732
First published: 2016-01-30 online.
Coverage: Factual complete original Methods and Results, including Table 2 and adverse-event narrative.
Reviewed: 2026-11-17

Eight insulin-treated participants aged 13–25 entered an uncontrolled pilot using commercially purchased walnut hydrosol. Methods do not establish the purchased product's plant part or processing, despite alternating walnut-leaf-extract terminology. A separately isolated volatile fraction was analyzed; it was not the administered cold-pressed kernel oil.

Two participants developed generalized itchy rash. One experienced hypoglycemic coma after three weeks. Authors judged the latter probably related, but concomitant insulin, dose adjustment and no comparator prevent causal attribution. Recovery was reported.

Table 2 shows lower average glucose in seven and lower insulin dose in five, whereas the abstract says both decreased in seven. Preserve this discrepancy. No pain outcome or Parkinson population was studied. Hydrosol, analyzed volatile fraction and culinary pressed oil must not be interchanged or pooled as the same preparation.
