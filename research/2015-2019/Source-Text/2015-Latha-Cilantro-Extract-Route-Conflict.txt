Extract made from dried cilantro leaves in mice
PMID 26109787. PMCID PMC4466768. DOI 10.4103/0974-8490.157996.
Original print article: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/279303841_Evaluation_of_anxiolytic_activity_of_aqueous_extract_of_Coriandrum_sativum_Linn_in_mice_A_preliminary_experimental_study
Coverage: factual reconciliation of abstract, Methods, Discussion and Table 1.

Latha and colleagues assigned six mice each to saline, diazepam, or three doses of an aqueous cilantro-leaf extract. The author abstract on S47 says oral feeding. The experimental procedure on S48 says the extract and controls were injected, without an injection site. S49 discusses an intraperitoneal route for a prior seed study. That passage does not resolve the current leaf experiment. The abstract describes boiling and evaporation, while the body describes maceration, warming and vacuum drying.

Table 1 gives 5.83 ± 0.54 open-arm entries for saline and 19.50 ± 0.62, 25.00 ± 0.37, and 29.17 ± 0.75 for the three extract groups. The lowest extract group is marked p < .01, while the other two are marked p < .001. The abstract describes all comparisons as p < .001. The tested material was an extract and the outcome was mouse maze behavior. Actual administration route, intact dried-leaf intake and clinical Parkinson anxiety remain unresolved.
