The Methods name Sattvic Foods cold-pressed flaxseed oil. Five groups of six healthy mice per behavioral test received oral saline, flaxseed oil at two levels, diazepam, or a combination. The product lot and analytical composition are unreported. The saline comparison does not match energy intake and the higher oil group received a different dosing volume.

Table 1 gives time in the lit compartment of 32.00 ± 5.55 seconds with saline and 65.33 ± 9.18 and 105.67 ± 9.07 seconds in the two oil-only groups. Light-compartment entries were 3.50 ± 1.05, 5.67 ± 1.37 and 6.17 ± 1.17 respectively. The table labels dispersion SD, while Results prose calls it SEM. The table supports a directional avoidance-behavior comparison. It does not establish that movement or exploratory activity remained unchanged.

Elevated-plus-maze Table 4 gives 36.33 ± 4.32 seconds in the open arms with saline and 53.67 ± 7.55 and 82.33 ± 19.08 seconds with oil. The omnibus entry statistic is listed as F=2.535 with p<.06. Table 5 does not show either oil-versus-saline posthoc time comparison used in the text's interpretation. The combination group's time is printed as 5.50 in Table 4 and 65.50 in Figure 4. These uncertainties are kept separately from the consistent direction of the oil-only light–dark comparisons.

The comparison measures anxiety-like avoidance in healthy mice without independent locomotor control or clinical Parkinson anxiety. The Results, tables and Discussion also disagree on dispersion labeling or dose units. Methods identify the oral preparation; the printed doses are animal-study conditions rather than a human regimen.
