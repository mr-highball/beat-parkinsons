Title: Whole eggs in a higher-protein diet and muscle composition in older adults
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6073477/
PMCID: PMC6073477
PMID: 30041437
DOI: 10.3390/nu10070946
Evidence date: 2018-07-23
Date basis: Journal first public publication.
Review basis: Full original structured text, including methods, Tables 2–6 and limitations.
Source type: Randomized human dietary trial.

Twenty-two older adults with overweight or obesity received a higher-protein diet including three whole eggs per day or an egg-free normal-protein diet for 12 weeks. MRI samples were usable for 17 participants at the thigh and 15 at the calf. MRI measures were largely inconclusive, with a smaller calf subcutaneous-fat-to-muscle ratio in the higher-protein arm over time (reported p=.031). DXA total and trunk lean mass had group-by-time p=.05 and p=.015. Both arms lost weight on average. The study jointly varied dietary protein and inclusion of eggs.

Round 1 claim 0329 asks about poached eggs and frequency of falls in Parkinson disease. The study did not compare fall frequency, gait or balance and did not isolate egg preparation or the egg contribution from higher protein.
