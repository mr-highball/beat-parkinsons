Title: Antidepressant Effect and Recognition Memory Improvement of Two Novel Plant Extract Combinations - Antistress I and Anti-stress II on Rats Subjected to a Model of Mild Chronic Stress

Coverage: Original article body, Tables 1–3 and statistics.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

Kandilarov and colleagues studied 72 male Wistar rats originally assigned across nine groups for eight weeks. The design included stressed and unstressed water groups, two combinations, and five separate dry extract groups. The Melissa-only comparison used an orally administered dry extract. Antistress I contained valerian, Melissa, hawthorn and Serratula, four to three to three to one. Antistress II contained valerian, Hypericum and Serratula, with no Melissa.

Table 2 forced-swim immobility was 199.0 seconds (SEM 21.7, n=6) for Melissa alone versus 225.5 seconds (SEM 15.9, n=8) for stressed water controls (p=.348). Antistress I was 227.5 seconds (SEM 10.4, n=6, p=.919 versus stressed control). Antistress II was 143.4 seconds (SEM 22.6, n=7, p=.015 versus stressed control). The favorable combination did not contain lemon balm. Table 3 recognition discrimination index for Melissa alone was .33 (SEM .04, n=6) versus .28 (SEM .03, n=8), p=.309.

The forced-swim table accounts for 64 of 72 assigned animals and the recognition table for 65. The reviewed Methods and Results do not reconcile the difference. Comparisons use individual t-tests and give no multiplicity adjustment in the statistics section. Small groups and the lack of a clear single-ingredient result limit inference. This model does not test brewed lemon balm, clinical depression or Parkinson disease.
