Title: Discordance Between Physician Assessment and Patient-Reported Depressive Symptoms in Parkinson Disease
PMID: 28535723
DOI: 10.1177/0891988717710335
Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0891988717710335
First published: 2017-05-24 online. Journal of Geriatric Psychiatry and Neurology 30(4).
Coverage: Factual extract of the publisher's author abstract. The journal body is restricted.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

Lachner and colleagues compared the patient-completed Brief Symptom Inventory-18 with the clinician-rated depression item of the original UPDRS in 1,076 Parkinson patients. The patient questionnaire identified 170 positive screens, or 16% of the sample. Among those 170, 104 (61%) did not meet the clinician item definition used in the comparison. The abstract gives a mean BSI-18 depression score of 51.4 (SD 9.7).

The comparison shows that ascertainment changes which people count as screen-positive. It does not establish which screen yields a confirmed diagnosis or report a change after treatment. No dietary intervention is described. The body is needed for exact cutoffs, exclusions and the full agreement table.
