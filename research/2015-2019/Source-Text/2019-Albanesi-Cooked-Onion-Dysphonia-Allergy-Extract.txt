Title: Immunological characterization of onion (Allium cepa) allergy
Authors: Marcello Albanesi and colleagues
Original publisher PDF: https://www.termedia.pl/doi_ft/10.5114/ada.2019.82829
PMC copy: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6409889/
Identifiers: PMID 30858787, PMCID PMC6409889, DOI 10.5114/ada.2019.82829
Date: February 2019, Adv Dermatol Allergol 36(1):98–103
Coverage: factual extract of participant history, clinical Methods, and quantitative skin tests in the original PDF

A 35-year-old man reported dysphonia, dyspnea, itching, urticaria and malaise several minutes after eating cooked onion and needed emergency treatment. The cooking procedure and dose for his meal are unreported. A separate skin test used raw bulb and a microwaved onion sample. The Skin Index is the wheal area relative to the histamine control. The reported values were 7.25 raw and 0.91 after microwave heating. The authors suggest that a heat-stable onion protein could explain residual sensitization but did not identify the eliciting protein clinically. The side-by-side comparison was a skin test and did not recreate the consumed meal or confirm the reaction by an oral rechallenge. No Parkinson cohort, acoustic record or speech intelligibility measurement is supplied.
