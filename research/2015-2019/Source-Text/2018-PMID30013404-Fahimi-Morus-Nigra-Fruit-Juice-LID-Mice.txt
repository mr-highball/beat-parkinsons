Botanical identity note (critical)
- Although the English title calls the fruit "blackberry," the explicit scientific species tested is Morus nigra (black mulberry, family Moraceae), NOT culinary Rubus fruticosus/Rubus subg. Rubus blackberry (family Rosaceae). Do not transfer this exposure directly to Rubus blackberry claims (e.g., 0167).

Study design and findings
- 42 male mice divided into 6 groups (7/group): control and 5 MPTP-lesioned groups. Four MPTP groups received levodopa 50 mg/kg i.p. for 2 weeks until onset of levodopa-induced dyskinesia (LID).
- Three LID groups received oral Morus nigra fruit juice (5, 10, or 15 mL/kg) alongside levodopa for 7 days; assessed via Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale (AIMS) and cylinder test (p < 0.05 significance threshold).
- Oral Morus nigra fruit juice at 10 and 15 mL/kg significantly attenuated AIMS dyskinesia scores relative to levodopa-only LID controls (p < 0.05), whereas the 5 mL/kg low dose did not show a significant effect.

Relevance and limits for Parkinson's food experiments
- Documented botanical homonymy boundary: resolves why PMC6037159 is excluded as an exposure match for Rubus blackberry claims while cataloging an authentic Morus nigra (black mulberry) MPTP+L-DOPA dyskinesia animal experiment.
- Limits: Non-Rubus species (Morus nigra), fruit juice rather than whole fruit, mouse MPTP/LID model, short 7-day duration, no human Parkinson clinical outcome.
