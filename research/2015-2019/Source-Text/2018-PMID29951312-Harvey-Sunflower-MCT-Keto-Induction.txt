Title: Harvey and colleagues' randomized comparison of MCT and sunflower oil during ketogenic diet induction

Reviewed: 2026-11-25
Evidence depth: Original report, methods, participant flow, Table 2, and discussion

Twenty-eight healthy adults entered the comparison and 23 completed it. Both groups followed a ketogenic diet. One arm received a medium-chain triglyceride supplement and the comparator arm received sunflower oil, called the long-chain triglyceride arm. The intervention duration is described as 20 days in the abstract and 19 days in the methods. The symptom questionnaire was analyzed as a sum. The mood analysis used the Profile of Mood States short form total disturbance score. The reported daily contrasts varied. The authors described unclear mood differences, and individual anxiety, depression and fatigue subscale estimates for sunflower oil are unavailable. The sunflower cultivar, high-oleic status and processing grade relevant to the frozen claims are unspecified.

The 2025 Parkinson ketogenic review mentions this trial alongside a distinct Parkinson feasibility study, Choi et al. 2024. Harvey is the source for the sunflower comparator. In Choi, the randomization compared two whole diets and yielded no isolated sunflower intervention. The review cites two separate study populations.
