Title: The effect of Atlantic salmon consumption on the cognitive performance of preschool children - A randomized controlled trial
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30598384/
PMID: 30598384
DOI: 10.1016/j.clnu.2018.11.031
Publication date: 2018-12-22
Coverage: Selected factual text from the original author abstract, linked registry and LMU record. Full results tables and cooking details remain open.

The paper describes 205 children randomized between meals containing farmed Atlantic salmon and meals containing meat three times weekly for 16 weeks. EPA and DHA biomarkers rose more in the salmon group. The authors report no clear between-group difference on the nine-hole peg test. The abstract provides no signed peg-test estimate or uncertainty interval. Positive findings in two raw cognition subtests concern separate outcomes.

The paper identifies NCT01951937. Its public registry gives actual enrollment as 200 and describes the comparison as a habitual diet. The origin of the enrollment discrepancy and the difference in comparator descriptions needs the full methods. The German cohort is separate from the Norwegian FINS-KIDS herring and mackerel intervention and the German PINGU infant trial.

The peg test assesses timed manual coordination in children. The report contains no verified grilling method, adult Parkinson population or bradykinesia measure.
