Title: Effects of resveratrol on cognition, mood and cerebrovascular function after menopause

Evans and colleagues randomized 80 postmenopausal women to 14 weeks of oral trans-resveratrol or placebo. The intervention used an isolated supplement at 75 mg twice daily. Table 4 includes 41 placebo and 38 resveratrol participants. The POMS anxiety score changed by −0.3 in placebo and −2.2 in the resveratrol arm, with a reported p value of 0.025 and Cohen's d of 0.50. The authors call it significant, while the printed adjusted cutoff is p < 0.025. The rounded result is at the reported boundary. POMS overall mood had p=0.085. The CES-D depression comparison had p=0.104.

This is a provisional constituent-level anxiety signal from a population that excluded neurological disorders. The trial used a purified supplement and provides no matched raw table-grape exposure or Parkinson anxiety estimate. The protocol registration ACTRN12615000291583 describes the same study family. Compare the distinct 2026 synthetic trans-resveratrol trial PMID 42459802 and the registered whole-grape powder trial NCT07214428. Round 1 context: claim 0860.
