Title: Using brewer’s yeast and ginger in the management of constipation-predominant irritable bowel syndrome
DOI: 10.22159/ajpcr.2019.v12i3.30716
Published: 2019. Publisher issue page lists March 7. Crossref deposits February 15 as the online publication date.
Source: https://innovareacademics.in/journals/index.php/ajpcr/article/download/30716/19480
Archive scope: Factual summary of the five-page publisher original, trial flow and Table 1.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Al-Jassim assigned 45 adults with IBS-C, 15 per arm, to 1 g/day of encapsulated dry ginger root powder, brewer's yeast or brown-sugar placebo for 20 days. The ginger and yeast arms were separate. The protocol excluded Parkinson disease. Table 1 gives mean 0–100 constipation severity of 70.66, 55.26 and 49.13 in the ginger arm at baseline, day 10 and day 20, versus 75.60, 66.93 and 65.86 for placebo. The table's lowercase group labels differ at both follow-ups at the reported p<.05 threshold.

Day-20 severity was 16.73 points lower with ginger. The crude difference of mean changes was −11.79 points after subtracting the starting separation. The publication gives no adjusted change or uncertainty interval for that derived contrast. The prose reverses yeast timepoints, so use Table 1. This measures self-rated severity in IBS-C with dried powder in capsules. A public trial-registration identity remains open.
