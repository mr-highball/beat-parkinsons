Ginger powder arm in constipation-predominant irritable bowel syndrome
2019. Publisher issue page lists March 7; Crossref deposits February 15 as the online publication date.
https://www.journals.innovareacademics.in/index.php/ajpcr/article/download/30716/19480

Al-Jassim assigned 45 adults with IBS-C, 15 per arm, to 1 g/day of encapsulated dry ginger root powder, brewer's yeast or brown-sugar placebo for 20 days. The ginger and yeast arms were separate. The protocol excluded Parkinson disease. Mean 0–100 constipation severity was 70.66, 55.26 and 49.13 in the ginger arm at baseline, day 10 and day 20, versus 75.60, 66.93 and 65.86 for placebo. Table 1 gives p<.05 for the between-arm differences at both follow-ups. It reports no baseline-adjusted change estimate. The prose reverses yeast time points, so the numbers here come from the table. The small trial used dry powder in capsules and measured self-rated severity rather than observed stool counts. A public registration was not located.
