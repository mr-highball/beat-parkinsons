Title: Saffron: an old medicinal plant and a potential novel functional food
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5943931/
PMID: 29295497
PMCID: PMC5943931
DOI: 10.3390/molecules23010030
Publication: 2017-12-23
Coverage: Factual extract. Official PMC XML main article and references.

This review identifies culinary saffron as dried Crocus sativus stigmas and distinguishes crocins, crocetin, picrocrocin and safranal. Section 4.1.9 summarizes clinical comparisons in depression involving saffron preparations and active comparators. The original plant parts, extraction methods and comparators matter. The review supplies no separate ground-saffron comparison for Parkinson depression.

Its crocetin discussion cites Mizuma et al., an exercise-fatigability comparison in healthy adults, with crocetin rather than whole dried stigma. The experiments, dosing descriptions and outcome in that original report take precedence over the review paraphrase. In the authentication section turmeric appears as a potential adulterant of ground saffron, not as an assigned treatment for pain.
