The authoritative food-composition record FDC 174665 (NDB 09094) identifies dried uncooked figs. The pantothenic acid result is 0.434 mg per 100 g, with eight analytical data points and a reported range of 0.350–0.531 mg per 100 g. This establishes that the ordinary dried fruit contains the nutrient under study. It provides no clinical exposure assessment, brain-tissue response or visual hallucination outcome.

Magnesium analysis of the same dried, uncooked fig entry gives 68 mg per 100 g, with eight analytical data points and a reported range of 63–73 mg per 100 g. This is a food composition measurement. It neither describes absorption nor predicts a clinical response to the food.

The same SR Legacy food-detail record measures iron 2.03 mg, zinc 0.55 mg, copper 0.287 mg and manganese 0.51 mg per 100 g of dried, uncooked figs. Each entry has eight analytical data points. Their reported ranges are iron 1.70–2.78 mg, zinc 0.44–0.80 mg, copper 0.238–0.320 mg and manganese 0.374–0.700 mg per 100 g. These food measurements are separate from the postmortem Parkinson brain tissue fractions in PMID 28944802. The data provide no measured intake-to-tissue or visual symptom relationship.
