Title: Dietary habits and neurological features of Parkinson's disease patients: Implications for practice
Authors: Michela Barichella and colleagues
Original university postprint: https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/handle/11585/585569/e1dcb339-52d0-7715-e053-1705fe0a6cc9/BarichellaM_ClinNutr_2017_postprint.pdf
Primary article abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27406858/
Identifiers: PMID 27406858, DOI 10.1016/j.clnu.2016.06.020
Date: 2016-07-05 online, August 2017 journal issue
Coverage: factual extract of diet and clinical Methods in the complete university postprint

The investigators assessed patients with diagnosed Parkinson disease and matched controls. They used a semi-quantitative 66-item food-frequency questionnaire with trained dietitian interviews. The 24-hour recall and food atlas helped check portions and adherence to protein redistribution. The study collected UPDRS Parts I–IV and used Part IV items to derive dyskinesia and OFF period measures. The accessible primary body and tables report food categories and nutrient intake rather than a defined cooked onion item. The published results do not estimate speech intelligibility or a speech item by onion exposure. The list of underlying diet entries is unavailable from the postprint.
