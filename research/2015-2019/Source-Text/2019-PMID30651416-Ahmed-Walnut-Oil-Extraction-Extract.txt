Title: Effects of Cold-Press and Soxhlet Extraction Systems on Antioxidant Activity, Total Phenol Contents, Fatty Acids, and Tocopherol Contents of Walnut Kernel Oils
PMID: 30651416
DOI: 10.5650/jos.ess18141
Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jos/68/2/68_ess18141/_pdf/-char/en
Publication: Journal of Oleo Science 68(2):167–173, 2019; exact first electronic day not independently established here.
Coverage: Factual extract of complete original seven-page article, Methods and Tables 2–3.
Reviewed: 2026-11-17

Three walnut cultivars were processed by cold pressing without heat treatment or petroleum-ether-assisted Soxhlet extraction. Table 2 linoleic acid ranges are 55.19–56.71% cold-pressed and 54.47–55.93% Soxhlet. Kaman-2 values are 56.71% (SD .89) versus 54.47% (SD .64). These are different analytical lots from Raafat's pain experiment.

The authors report cultivar and extraction differences in phenols, antioxidant assays and tocopherols. Table 3 gamma-tocopherol ranges are 9.41–10.83 versus 8.76–9.33 mg/100 g. Similar major fatty-acid proportions do not establish whole-oil equivalence. The table's Linolenic row is retained as printed rather than reassigned to a specific isomer.

The linoleic values are comparable in scale to the 55% in the favorable mouse oil report, supporting limited preparation relevance. The study measures chemistry, not pain, Parkinson disease or clinical efficacy.
