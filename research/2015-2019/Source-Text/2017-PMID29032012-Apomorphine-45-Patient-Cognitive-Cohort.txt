Title: Continuous subcutaneous apomorphine infusion in Parkinson's disease patients with cognitive dysfunction: A retrospective long-term follow-up study
Source: https://pure.rug.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/56051079/1_s2.0_S1353802017303577_main.pdf
PMID: 29032012
DOI: 10.1016/j.parkreldis.2017.09.025
Publication date: 2017-09-29
Source depth: complete university-hosted published article PDF; locally distilled

Forty-five people with advanced, fluctuating Parkinson disease and cognitive dysfunction started infusion. Table 1 reports active visual hallucinations in 32. The authors report no new or worsening visual hallucinations during inpatient titration. After discharge, nine with pre-existing visual hallucinations worsened and one discontinued because of refractory visual symptoms. Five of those nine later improved after treatment for an intercurrent infection. Medication changes included increases in clozapine and cholinesterase inhibitor use in relevant participants and reductions in other dopaminergic therapy.

The rehabilitation unit was part of the Groningen center, and its recruitment period was November 2004 through the end of 2016. The center and dates overlap the 125-participant chart study. The original does not identify an exact count of shared participants. The observations concern a changing treatment regimen and visual symptom trajectories, without a dried fig exposure. Published pages 34–36 were inspected for methods and individual outcome counts.
