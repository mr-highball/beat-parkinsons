Title: Ginger Extract and [6]-Gingerol Inhibit Contraction of Rat Entire Small Intestine
PMID: 29756476
PMCID: PMC5954582
DOI: 10.1177/2515690X18774273
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5954582/
Date basis: Original first online 2018-05-13, publisher.
Coverage: Factual extract of the full original methods, Figures 3–4 and Tables 1–2.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

The seven-day oral experiment randomly assigned rats to five groups of six. The researchers excised duodenum, jejunum and ileum and then stimulated the tissue with acetylcholine. Two higher ginger extract groups and the group given isolated 6-gingerol showed attenuated contractions. The low extract comparison showed no clear effect. The highest extract group had a mean maximal ileal response of 35.93% of the high-potassium reference contraction versus 56.29% for vehicle, with reported SEMs 1.91 and 4.05 respectively.

The separate direct organ-bath study also reported attenuated contractions after applying extract or 6-gingerol to the isolated tissue. Both parts measure responsiveness of excised small intestine. The measurements differ from live charcoal transport, distal-colon strips, stool frequency, and constipation severity. The ingested material was an extract or isolated constituent, with no culinary preparation or Parkinson cohort.
