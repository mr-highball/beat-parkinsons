Title: [Copper deficiency myelopathy probably caused by long-lasting daily excessive intake of zink].
PMID: 27645757
DOI: 10.5692/clinicalneurol.cn-000926
First published: 2016
Authors: Motooka R, Yamamoto S
Journal: Rinsho shinkeigaku = Clinical neurology
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27645757/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 5 review).

Primary Abstract:
We report a 39-year-old woman with slowly progressive spastic gait and paresthesia in the lower extremities. Cervical spinal MRI revealed high intensity in the dorsal column from vertebrae C2 to C7. Laboratory findings showed normal vitamin B12 and low serum copper level. By lifestyle history taking, we noticed her extreme unbalanced diet of having 15-20 oysters everyday over 5 years. Then we considered that zinc excess caused copper deficiency myelopathy. We needed to pay attention to copper and zinc level if the patient has a symptom resembling subacute combined degeneration of spinal cord with normal serum vitamin B12.

Verified Round 5 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Clinical neurological case report (Rinsho Shinkeigaku 2016) of a 39-y-old woman presenting with slowly progressive spastic gait, paresthesias, and C2-C7 dorsal-column MRI hyperintensity with normal vitamin B12 and low serum copper caused by eating 15-20 oysters daily for 5 years (chronic zinc excess blocking intestinal copper absorption). Critical extreme-intake neurological safety caveat.
