Title: Effectiveness of Anise Oil for Treatment of Mild to Moderate Depression in Patients With Irritable Bowel Syndrome: A Randomized Active and Placebo-Controlled Clinical Trial.
PMID: 26873392
PMCID: PMC5871202
DOI: 10.1177/2156587216628374
Registry ID: NCT02364830
First published: 2017
Authors: Mosaffa-Jahromi M, Tamaddon AM, Afsharypuor S, Salehi A, Seradj SH, Pasalar M, Jafari P, Lankarani KB
Journal: Journal of evidence-based complementary & alternative medicine
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5871202/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Full-text verified depression analysis (PMC5871202) of the three-arm double-blind RCT (NCT02364830) in n=120 adults (20-50 y) with Rome III IBS and mild-to-moderate depression (excluding severe depression) randomized equally (40/arm) to enteric-coated anise oil oleogel (AnisEncap 200 mg tid), Colpermin peppermint oil (187 mg tid), or matched peanut-oil/beeswax/Aerosil oleogel placebo for 4 weeks plus 2-week follow-up. Verified Table 2 Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II) scores (mean +/- SD): Baseline (p = 0.84) AnisEncap 11.55 +/- 10.07 (n=40), Colpermin 10.47 +/- 7.22 (n=40), Placebo 11.02 +/- 6.74 (n=40); Week 4 (p < 0.001) AnisEncap 8.54 +/- 9.47 (n=39), Colpermin 9.60 +/- 6.68 (n=38), Placebo 10.20 +/- 6.62 (n=39); Week 6 follow-up (p < 0.001) AnisEncap 7.34 +/- 9.94 (n=38), Colpermin 10.06 +/- 7.28 (n=33), Placebo 10.11 +/- 6.79 (n=37). No major adverse events reported. Quality/reliability caveats from full text: body weight in kg (71.9-73.0) is mislabeled as BMI kg/m2, and large SDs relative to modest absolute between-group mean differences without printed pairwise test statistics warrant partial reliability fit.
