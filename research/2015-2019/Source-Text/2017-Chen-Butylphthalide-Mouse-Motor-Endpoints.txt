Title: DL-3-n-butylphthalide reduces microglial activation in lipopolysaccharide-induced Parkinson's disease model mice
Authors: Yuhua Chen, Mujun Jiang, Li Li, Ming Ye, Meiling Yu, Lina Zhang, Bobo Ge, Wenfang Xu, and Daoxiang Wei
Citation: Molecular Medicine Reports. 2018;17:3884–3890.
First published online: 2017-12-20.
PMID: 29286148
DOI: 10.3892/mmr.2017.8332
Source: https://www.spandidos-publications.com/10.3892/mmr.2017.8332
Text coverage: selected facts paraphrased from the publisher's report, materials and methods, results, and Figures 1–2.

Thirty-six male C57BL/6 mice were randomized to saline, LPS, or LPS plus pharmaceutical DL-3-n-butylphthalide. The NBP group received 120 mg/kg/day intragastrically for 30 days. Six mice in each arm underwent behavioral testing at each of 4 and 28 weeks. The study recorded time remaining on a rotating bar and the frequency of open-field line crossing, rearing, and grooming. The rotarod comparison was not significant at 4 weeks. At 28 weeks, NBP attenuated the shorter rotarod dwell time in the LPS model, and open-field activity was greater in the NBP plus LPS group than in the LPS group. The experimental dosing did not include levodopa or a celery preparation, and the motor tests did not include an abnormal involuntary movement score. The abstract's use of the term dyskinesia therefore refers to the observed locomotor/coordination tasks. Tissue measures were distinct additional endpoints.
