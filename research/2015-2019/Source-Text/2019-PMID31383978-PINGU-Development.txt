Title: Effects of LC-PUFA supply via complementary food on infant development
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31383978/
PMID: 31383978
DOI: 10.1038/s41430-019-0491-0
Publication date: 2019-08-05
Coverage: Factual extract from the original author abstract and publisher metadata. The full methods and numeric outcome tables remain open.

The PINGU developmental report lists 54 term infants in the rapeseed-oil group, 48 in the oily-fish group and 58 in the corn-oil control group. The full PINGU registry gives 214 enrolled. The biomarker article verifies salmon jars for the oily-fish arm. At ten months the authors report no clear group differences in visual evoked potential latency, Bayley mental development or Bayley psychomotor development. The accessible report gives no signed pair-specific psychomotor estimate or precision interval.

Bayley development assesses age-appropriate movement and developmental skills. The tested infant preparation and psychomotor outcomes leave grilled salmon and bradykinesia in people with Parkinson disease open.
