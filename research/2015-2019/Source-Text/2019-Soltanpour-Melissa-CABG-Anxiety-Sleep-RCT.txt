Title: Effects of Melissa officinalis on anxiety and sleep quality in patients undergoing coronary artery bypass surgery: A double-blind randomized placebo controlled trial
DOI: 10.1016/j.eujim.2019.01.010
First published: 2019
Authors: Soltanpour A, Alijaniha F, Naseri M, Kazemnejad A, Heidari MR
Journal: European Journal of Integrative Medicine
Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eujim.2019.01.010
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 5 review).

Verified Round 5 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial (Eur J Integr Med 2019;28:27-32; verified via Crossref and Mathews 2024 Table 4, PMC11510126) in 80 hospitalized cardiac surgery inpatients aged 30-70 (mean age 58; 40 per arm, 0 dropouts) recovering from coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG). Participants received 500 mg dried Melissa officinalis leaf powder capsules three times daily (1.5 g/day; assayed 4.88 mg GA/g phenolics, 4.28 mg RU/g flavonoids) or matching wheat-starch placebo for 7 days. Outcomes: Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS) anxiety and St. Mary's Hospital Sleep Questionnaire (SMHSQ) sleep quality. Seven-day supplementation significantly reduced HADS anxiety (~49% reduction) and improved SMHSQ sleep quality (~54% improvement) versus placebo with no adverse events reported. Limitations: short 7-day postoperative cardiac setting, non-PD population, dried leaf powder capsules rather than culinary fresh leaves, and full numerical result tables paywalled.
