Original comparison
Bigdeli Shamloo and colleagues studied adults with traumatic limb pain. The intervention added topical sesame oil and massage to routine care. The comparator is described as routine care without a placebo oil. Of 150 randomized participants, 126 were analyzed after follow-up. The original narrative and reported arm totals disagree on which group lost more participants, which limits arm-specific attrition interpretation.

Table 2 gives mean pain on day 3 as 4.95 in the sesame-oil group and 5.83 in controls, and on day 10 as 1.14 and 2.83, respectively. Both reported between-group p values are .001. Table 3 reports lower analgesic-use frequency in the intervention group at the later assessments. These results are a related topical injury-pain observation with a routine-care comparison.

Continuing evidence gap
The source does not report oral culinary exposure, whether the oil came from toasted seeds, or a Parkinson population. Its comparator and attrition reporting limit transfer to a treatment effect for Round 1 claim 0144. Keep this trial separate from the 40-person 2017 paraffin-controlled trauma trial, PMID 28619303. Their route and injury outcome differ from the frozen oral exposure and Parkinson pain.
