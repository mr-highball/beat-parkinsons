Title: The Fennel, Foeniculum vulgare incorporated diet shows anxiolytic potential: A pre-clinical study
PMID: 31680077
DOI: 10.36721/pjps/32/4(SUP)/01.07.2019/1195/1813-1819
Publication date: 2019-07-04
Citation: Abbas and colleagues. Pakistan Journal of Pharmaceutical Sciences 32(4 supplementary), 1813–1819.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31680077/
Publisher: https://pjps.pk/article?id=5204
Coverage: Original author abstract and publisher metadata and introduction. Full results tables remain open.

The study fed ten mice per arm a usual diet or pellets incorporating 2% or 4% whole-crushed fennel seeds for two months. Measurements every 15 days included home activity, head dipping, light–dark behavior and open-field activity. The authors report fewer home-cage crossings but more central squares crossed in the open-field test in fennel-fed mice. They interpret the combination as an anxiolytic signal. Resting tremor and a Parkinson model were outside this experiment. Whole crushed seed makes this a closer food exposure than essential oil, with no reported toasting or analysis of post-processing compounds.
