Title: Circadian rhythms and influencing factors of xerostomia among Parkinson’s disease patients
Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/odi.12942
PMID: 30028552
DOI: 10.1111/odi.12942
Publication date: 2018-07-20
Source depth: Original author abstract, publisher publication history and linked primary study registration. Results tables and appendix files remain open.
Date basis: Wiley first published the accepted manuscript July 20, 2018. The version of record was published August 16, 2018. The issue is dated January 2019.
Design: Questionnaire and 24-hour diary in a single-center observational Parkinson study

Seventy-five people completed the study. The abstract reports 67% self-reported dysphagia, 76% drooling and 77% xerostomia. The mean VAS scores given for the symptoms are 4, 5 and 5 with SD 2 each. Diary entries separately covered motor experiences, saliva-related symptoms, and daily activities. Food intake is listed among associations with xerostomia (p=.005). The available results give no named food or paired effect on drooling. The reported peaks around 9 a.m. and 9 p.m. refer to xerostomia.

The registered protocol defines an hourly diary and names a separate correlation among the three symptoms as its primary question. The publication is linked directly in the register. This family is distinct in protocol and timing from the earlier matched clinic study. Whether any participants appeared in the earlier support-group sample is unknown.
