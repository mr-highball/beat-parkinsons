Title: Evolution of Orofacial Symptoms and Disease Progression in Idiopathic Parkinson's Disease: Longitudinal Data from the Jönköping Parkinson Registry
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5534316/
PMID: 28798882
PMCID: PMC5534316
DOI: 10.1155/2017/7802819
Publication date: 2017-07-16
Date basis: Electronic publication date in the author article.
Coverage: Paraphrased full original, including methods, Tables 1–3 and the time-to-event section.

The outpatient registry included 314 people with idiopathic Parkinson disease from 1998–2014, with mean follow-up of 4.2 years. The article reports drooling at baseline in 11.7% and during follow-up in 55.3%. Its multivariable analysis associated rated drooling with dominant rigidity (OR 5.2, 95% CI 1.4–19.1) and autonomic disturbance (OR 3.4, 95% CI 1.1–10.9). The authors discuss forward neck position as one possible contributor. No independently measured angle or fig exposure was part of the analysis.

The drooling group reached the Part II threshold in 5.0 versus 8.1 years (p=.018) and the Part III threshold in 4.9 versus 7.2 years (p=.030) in the reported comparison. Part II includes the saliva item used in classification. The overlap can contribute to the Part II association. The Part III threshold uses another scale component. These are observational comparisons, without an intervention or a dietary estimate.
