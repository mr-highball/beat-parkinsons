Title: Anti-Inflammatory and Analgesic Activities of Aqueous Extract Date Palm (Phoenix Dactylifera L) Fruit in Rats
Source: https://www.noveltyjournals.com/upload/paper/Anti-Inflammatory-438.pdf
Publication: 2015, volume 2 issue 3, pp. 166–172
Coverage: Original journal PDF and hot-tail-flick Table 2.

The experiment assigned four groups of six rats to saline, two oral levels of an aqueous fruit extract, or paracetamol. The article describes convenience allocation. Tail-flick latency medians were 2.0 (IQR 0.25) for saline, 3.5 (1.75) for the lower extract group and 6.5 (2.75) for the higher extract group. The higher group is the reported favorable comparison against saline. The table labels and abstract interchange the two omnibus statistics for the inflammation and analgesia outcomes. Their reported significance figures should remain qualified.

This result concerns heat-evoked withdrawal in rats given an extract. The separate ear-swelling assay measures inflammation. There is no human Parkinson pain comparison or ordinary dried-fruit exposure.
