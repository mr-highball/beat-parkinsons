Title: Corn oil placebo and attention testing in older adults
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12880479/
Format: Factual extract prepared from the cited record.

Carmichael and colleagues, 2018. Original Methods and Table 2. PMID 30080229, PMCID PMC12880479.
Ten cognitively healthy older adults completed blinded crossover phases of 26 days separated by a 30-day washout. The placebo was a corn oil emulsion matched to an active drink with fish oil, ginseng and green tea catechins. The corn oil amount and processing grade were not given.
Digit Symbol Substitution Test scores on corn oil went from 56.70 ± 10.17 to 59.30 ± 9.39, with change +2.60 ± 8.53. The active phase changed by +3.30 ± 2.75. The change on placebo and the difference between conditions were inconclusive. The placebo Stroop score changed by +4.80 ± 7.22 against +6.60 ± 8.71 on active, also without a clear between-condition difference.
Repeated testing and the absence of an oil-free comparison prevent attribution of a change to corn oil. No Parkinson cohort was enrolled.
