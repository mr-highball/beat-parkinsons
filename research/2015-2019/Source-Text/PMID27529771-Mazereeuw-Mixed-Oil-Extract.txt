Mazereeuw G, Herrmann N, Oh PI, et al. Omega-3 Fatty Acids, Depressive Symptoms, and Cognitive Performance in Patients With Coronary Artery Disease: Analyses From a Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial. Journal of Clinical Psychopharmacology 2016;36(5):436–444. PMID 27529771; PMC5017271; DOI 10.1097/JCP.0000000000000565. Original full-text JATS https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC5017271/fullTextXML ; trial NCT00981383.

Factual extract: 92 adults with stable coronary artery disease in cardiac rehabilitation were randomized (45 omega-3; 47 placebo); 82 completed. Neurological conditions were excluded. Participants with and without depressive episodes were eligible; mean baseline HAM-D 6.9 ± 5.9 and BDI-II 12.3 ± 10.9. A two-week placebo lead-in preceded 12-week double-blind treatment. Active arm received three capsules supplying 1.2 g EPA, 0.6 g DHA, 0.1 g other omega-3 in ethyl-ester form daily. Placebo was three 1-g capsules of a 50:50 soybean/corn oil blend, less than 0.1 g omega-3; food-oil refining and pressing were not identified. Both capsules were lemon-lime flavored. There was no no-oil arm.

Hamilton and BDI-II were measured at baseline and weeks 4, 8 and 12. Intention-to-treat repeated measures included multiple imputation for missing follow-up scores and covariates. HAM-D treatment-by-time F(3,91)=1.59, p=.20; BDI-II F(3,91)=0.46, p=.50. Mean change over both arms was reported as 1.1 ± 4.6 HAM-D points and −2.5 ± 8.0 BDI-II points; these are not arm-specific effects. Baseline depressive episode did not significantly modify HAM-D (p=.28) or BDI-II (p=.76) treatment response. Exclusion of antidepressant users and adjustment for compliance did not reveal significant depression treatment effects. Figure 1 uses covariate-adjusted HAM-D estimates; exact numeric arm values are not tabulated in the available full text. Results cannot isolate the effects of either oil or assess Parkinson symptoms.
