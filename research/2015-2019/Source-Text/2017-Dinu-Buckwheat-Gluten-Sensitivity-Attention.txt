Buckwheat product substitution and self-reported attention in gluten sensitivity
Source: https://apjcn.nhri.org.tw/server/APJCN/26/4/630.pdf
PMID: 28582812
DOI: 10.6133/apjcn.072016.07
Coverage: factual extract from intervention and symptom results

Monica Dinu, D Macchia, G Pagliai, A M Gori, F Cesari, R Marcucci, F Sofi, and A Casini. Asia Pacific Journal of Clinical Nutrition, 2017, 26(4):631–637.
Randomized crossover, 19 adults with non-celiac gluten sensitivity, 18 women and one man, and two six-week periods. The intervention supplied 100% buckwheat pasta, plus mixed-ingredient buckwheat hardtacks, biscuits and flakes, to replace cereal products. The control participants consumed their customary gluten-free diets. A weekly modified 7-point Global Assessment of Improvement item concerning attention was self-reported with higher categories denoting improvement. The authors report a nonsignificant improvement trend on the broader Global Assessment of Improvement questionnaire after the buckwheat products. The control-period attention item fell from 4.8 ± 1.5 to 3.9 ± 0.7, p < 0.05 within that condition. Three participants (16%) reported reduced lack of concentration on the buckwheat intervention, while one (5%) was reported to improve after control in the sentence coupling nausea and concentration. The paper does not report a dedicated between-period statistical contrast for the attention item. Direct inferential results on gastrointestinal symptoms are a separate endpoint.
Table 2 reports a +0.08 mg/dL within-buckwheat-period change in circulating magnesium (+4.7%, paired p < 0.05). This is neither brain magnesium nor a tested mediator of attention. The paper does not separate the four buckwheat products or classify the flakes as toasted groats. No Parkinson or formal attention-task cohort was studied.
