# Pumpkin seed oil coupled to ultrasound and pelvic pain

Original source https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6149936/
Source coverage original Methods and Table 2

Tantawy and colleagues randomized 60 men with chronic nonbacterial prostatitis among oil-coupled transperineal ultrasound, active ultrasound and sham. The September 18, 2018 report’s Table 2 gives mean NIH-CPSI pain changes from 17.20 to 3.71, 17.80 to 5.21 and 16.90 to 14.62, respectively. The simple additional reduction over active ultrasound was 0.90 points. The table does not give pain-subscore-specific contrast uncertainty. Pain improved with both active ultrasound procedures. The active gel description gives conflicting day and week units for treatment duration. The oil was pharmaceutical grade with no pressing method reported. This local intervention leaves oral exposure, pressing and Parkinson pain untested.
