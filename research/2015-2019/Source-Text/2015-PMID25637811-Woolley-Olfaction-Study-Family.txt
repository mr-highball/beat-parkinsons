Title: Woolley et al. — odor-specific results after intranasal oxytocin
PMID: 25637811
PMCID: PMC4503321
DOI: 10.1016/j.psyneuen.2014.12.018
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4503321/
First published: 2015-01-07 online, March 2015 issue.
Coverage: Posted original manuscript text and figures as described in the main article. Supplementary protocol details were not extracted.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

The crossover assigned intranasal oxytocin or placebo to 31 participants with schizophrenia-spectrum illness and 34 healthy controls. The original article's outcome methods specify Lyral and anise oil. The schizophrenia group detected Lyral at lower concentrations after oxytocin, while the anise result was inconclusive. The controls showed no clear effect on either odor.

The 2011 and 2012 conference reports describe a similar design and include clove oil. The journal manuscript supplies no clove estimate. Keep the preliminary clove result at conference depth and avoid treating the journal's other odor results as confirmation or independent clove evidence. The intervention and outcomes differ from ingesting ground clove for Parkinson symptoms.
