Title: Supplementation of the diet by exogenous myrosinase via mustard seeds to increase the bioavailability of sulforaphane in healthy human subjects after the consumption of cooked broccoli
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29806738/
Author manuscript: https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/77433/
First published online: 2018-07-26
PMID: 29806738
DOI: 10.1002/mnfr.201700980
Text coverage: original author abstract and the institutional accepted manuscript, Methods, Figure 2 and Table 1.

Twelve healthy adults completed a randomized crossover comparing cooked broccoli with and without powdered brown mustard seed (Brassica juncea). The mustard seeds were room-temperature dried and added after the broccoli was cooked. The 24-hour urinary sulforaphane metabolite normalized to creatinine was 44.7 ± 33.9 with mustard versus 9.8 ± 5.1 micromoles per gram without it. Individual ratios varied. The study measures a biotransformation marker in healthy adults. It gives no comparison of toasted seeds, Parkinson disease, tremor or clinical outcomes.
