Title: Effect of Salivation on Quality of Life in PD Patients
Source: https://agsjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jgs.15055
DOI: 10.1111/jgs.15055
Publication date: 2017-09
Source depth: Original paper-session abstract P41 on the publisher page and PubMed proceedings metadata
Date basis: September 2017 supplement of the Journal of the American Geriatrics Society. DOI and PMID 28866872 identify the proceedings, not a standalone paper for P41.
Design: Observational comparison in 186 people with Parkinson disease

The original conference abstract reports 133 participants with salivation and 53 without. It uses the Sialorrhea Clinical Scale for Parkinson's Disease and reports more salivation among men than women (78.8% versus 58.8%, p<.01). Disease duration and several clinical scores are associated with the sialorrhea scale. The abstract lists the sialorrhea score among correlates of quality of life, without an effect magnitude or a randomized exposure for that association. It supplies no test of a food, ingredient or raw fig.
