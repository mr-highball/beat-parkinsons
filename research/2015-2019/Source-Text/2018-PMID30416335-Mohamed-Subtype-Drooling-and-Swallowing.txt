Title: Evaluation of dysphagia in different phenotypes of early and idiopathic Parkinsonism
Source: https://doi.org/10.1186/s41983-018-0031-1
PMID: 30416335
PMCID: PMC6208737
DOI: 10.1186/s41983-018-0031-1
Publication date: 2018-10-24
Source depth: Original body, Tables 2–4 and Appendix 1.

Mohamed and colleagues assessed 54 early idiopathic Parkinson outpatients in Sohag. Table 4 records sialorrhea in 34/46 tremor-dominant patients and 3/8 postural-instability/gait-disorder patients (73.9% and 37.5%, reported p = .041). Instrumental dysphagia appeared in 16/46 and 6/8 (34.8% and 75.0%). Table 4 gives p = .033 for the phenotype/dysphagia comparison. Table 2 gives p = .022 for the same displayed subgroup counts.

Table 2 records sialorrhea in 23/32 without and 14/22 with endoscopically measured dysphagia (reported p = .5). Table 3 gives an adjusted dysphagia odds ratio of 1.530 (95% CI .417–5.619) for sialorrhea. Appendix 1 grades frequency and severity separately. The precise threshold behind the binary table row is unclear. The gait-dominant sample of eight and differing group definitions limit comparison with the larger Suzhou study. No culinary exposure or raw-fig drooling response was studied.
