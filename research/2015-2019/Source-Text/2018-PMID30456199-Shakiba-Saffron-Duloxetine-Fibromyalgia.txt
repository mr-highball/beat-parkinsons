Shakiba and colleagues randomized 54 adults with fibromyalgia equally to saffron extract or duloxetine for eight weeks in Tehran. Table 2 reports 23 participants per arm. The manuscript describes an intention-to-treat analysis with carried-forward outcomes for participants with at least one follow-up. The saffron product was an ethanolic extract of dried stigma, delivered orally. Each 15 mg capsule contained 1.65–1.75 mg crocin. This differs from ground stigma added to food.

At week eight, the saffron-minus-duloxetine difference in change was +0.16 (95% CI −2.39 to +2.71, p=.900) on the Hamilton depression scale and +0.68 (−6.52 to +7.87, p=.850) on the Global Fatigue Index. HADS depression differed by +1.74 (−0.85 to +4.34, p=.182) and Brief Pain Inventory by −0.36 (−1.41 to +0.69, p=.493). All four intervals span zero. Although scores improved within the arms, the trial had an active drug control and did not specify an equivalence or noninferiority margin. It therefore cannot establish benefit over no treatment or equivalence to duloxetine.

Continuing evidence gaps
The trial enrolled people with fibromyalgia. These comparisons give no directional input for ordinary dried or ground saffron in Parkinson disease. Separate insomnia severity and fall events were absent from the reported outcomes. The prospective registry lists fatigue as primary at repeated visits. The published objectives name Hamilton depression, fibromyalgia impact and pain as primary comparisons, while Table 2 also reports fatigue. The published randomized and analyzed denominators differ from the registered target and are retained separately. An independently replicated culinary-stigma Parkinson trial with prespecified symptom endpoints remains open.
