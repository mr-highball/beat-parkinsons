Title: Curcumin supplementation and motor-cognitive function in healthy middle-aged and older adults
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6004902/
PMCID: PMC6004902
PMID: 29951592
DOI: 10.3233/NHA-170029
Evidence date: 2018-06-15
Date basis: Journal publication.
Review basis: Full original structured text, including methods, Table 2 and discussion.
Source type: Randomized placebo-controlled human trial.

Thirty-nine healthy middle-aged or older adults received solid-lipid curcumin (Longvida, n=20) or placebo (n=19) for 12 weeks. Rapid-step time changed from 35.1 to 33.9 seconds in the curcumin group and from 33.7 to 32.8 with placebo. The group-by-time p value was .9. Mean errors declined from 2.4 to 1.4 and from 2.3 to 1.8, respectively (interaction p=.4). Four-meter gait speed had interaction p=.9. Fatigue Severity Scale scores declined in both groups (interaction p=.9). The paper reports means and standard errors.

Round 1 claim 0251 asks about ground turmeric and frequency of falls in Parkinson disease. This study measured step performance, gait and fatigue in people without Parkinson disease. No falls outcome or ordinary ground turmeric comparison was reported. The group comparisons are inconclusive and do not establish equivalence.
