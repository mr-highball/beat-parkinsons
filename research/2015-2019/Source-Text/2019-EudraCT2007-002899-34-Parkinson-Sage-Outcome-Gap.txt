Title: Pilot controlled clinical trial of common sage for cognitive impairment in Parkinson disease
Source: https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/trial/2007-002899-34/results
Trial registration: EudraCT 2007-002899-34
Protocol: https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/trial/2007-002899-34/GB
Original results attachment: https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/rest/download/result/attachment/2007-002899-34/1/26590
Publication date: 2019-03-24, first posting date of the registry results
Reviewed: 2026-11-22 UTC
Coverage: Original five-page grant report and registry links. The report is a summary of the study.

The 2008 Newcastle single-blind pilot compared capsules containing dried Salvia officinalis leaves with vitamin E. Participants had Parkinson disease and mild cognitive impairment. The report summarizes cognition and salivation. Vitamin E replaced the planned placebo. Memory speed improved within the sage group, with no clear between-group memory result reported. The reported endpoints do not establish effects on fatigue, apathy, dystonia, rigidity or falls.

Research gap. A secondary assessment timetable referenced in the report is absent from the public attachment. Find the full protocol and appendices, the planned 2008 poster and the 2009 manuscript named in the report through exact investigator, registry and grant trails. Seek any instrument, time point and arm-level result before interpreting missing outcomes as measured. Treat any recovered report as the same Newcastle trial unless it describes a separate cohort. Dried leaf in capsules differs from fresh and ground kitchen preparations.
