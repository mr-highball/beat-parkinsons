Title: Association between changes in visual evoked magnetic fields and non-motor features in Parkinson's disease
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5472540/
PMCID: PMC5472540
PMID: 28626250
DOI: 10.18999/nagjms.79.2.147
Publication date: 2017-05
Date basis: Nagoya journal volume 79 issue 2 and PMC structured issue; NLM journal issue field gives February
Source depth: Complete original PMC structured article body
Format: Factual digest of the inspected material

Ten Parkinson patients and sixteen controls underwent visual evoked magnetic-field testing. The Parkinson participants reported neither hallucinations nor visual impairment. The visual cortical response and nonmotor measures are pre-hallucination physiology rather than a test of food intake or visual episode frequency.

The researchers used checkerboard reversal and grating stimuli during magnetoencephalography. The authors interpreted longer first-component latency and clinical correlations as possible early visual pathway changes. None of the ten patients reported visual hallucinations at testing. A physiological precursor in this sample cannot estimate a clinical visual hallucination frequency or establish a food effect.
