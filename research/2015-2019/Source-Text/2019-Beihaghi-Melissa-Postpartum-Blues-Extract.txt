Title: The Effect of Melissa Officinalis on Postpartum Blues in Women Undergoing Cesarean Section
DOI: 10.22038/jmrh.2019.28685.1330
Source: https://jmrh.mums.ac.ir/article_12535.html
Original PDF: https://jmrh.mums.ac.ir/article_12535_ee88b3cea2051be97bcddf2e0d9a28f6.pdf
Reference: Beihaghi et al., Journal of Midwifery and Reproductive Health 7(2), April 2019, original printed pages 1656–1663.
Text coverage: Original Methods and Tables 3–4.

A placebo comparison after cesarean section used dried lemon-balm herb capsules for 10 days. The paper describes triple masking and its tables show 30 people per arm. Methods report placebo exclusions and discuss interruptions in both groups, without reconciling their handling with the table denominators.

Mean Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale scores rose from 1.8 to 3.7 under lemon balm and from 2.3 to 8.6 under placebo by day 14. Table 4 reports p=.001 for the day-14 arm comparison and p=.004 for the comparison of increases (1.9 vs 6.2). Table 3 reports 0/30 versus 10/30 postpartum blues cases at that time. The comparison addresses early postpartum prevention with low starting scores. It gives limited information on a brewed-leaf intervention or Parkinson depression. Several bibliographic copies give different page numbers. The study figures here use the printed journal PDF.

Printed page 1662 names university study code 950917 and gives IRCT1520200597 as the registration string. A matching public registry record was not independently verified. The publisher issue confirms April 2019 without a separate earlier online date.
