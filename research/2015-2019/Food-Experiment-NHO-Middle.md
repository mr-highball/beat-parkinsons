# Japanese National Hospital Organization meeting abstracts, 2016–2019

These summaries use the dates of the 70th through 73rd meetings as the proceedings dates. Page numbers in the links are PDF viewer pages. The meeting organizer publishes an abstract copyright policy. The original Japanese abstracts remain at the linked pages. [Official meeting chronology](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/research/research_gakkai.html), [abstract copyright policy](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000130903.pdf).

## 2016 ornithine formula in advanced Parkinson disease

Ōmura, Sakai and Saisho presented case P4-30-1 on November 12, 2016. A 64-year-old man with stage 4 Parkinson disease, diabetes, dysphagia and bilateral ischial pressure ulcers took about half his meals. Admission BMI was 18.1 and albumin 2.8 g/dL. The team targeted 1,700 kcal a day, adjusted snacks to match his preferences and offered an ornithine-containing, low-carbohydrate Ornut powder that could be thickened. Intake improved and the two ulcers healed on days 27 and 48 during nutrition and wound care. The report gives no separate formula effect, comparison or quantified change in swallowing. [Primary conference abstract, PDF p. 1851](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100774.pdf#page=1851).

## 2016 to 2018 intake and weight in the Utano cohort

Park and colleagues' 2016 P2-30-8 compared 91 patients with Parkinson disease and 56 controls enrolled June 2015 through April 2016. The food-frequency questionnaire estimates of total energy were higher in Parkinson disease for men (2,243 versus 2,023 kcal/day) and women (1,972 versus 1,775 kcal/day), p=.023 for each. Patients had lower body fat and BMI, with no clear resting metabolic rate difference. The 2017 P2-1A-714 extended the enrollment window to June 2016 with 98 patients and 57 controls at the same site. Energy per kilogram remained higher, leptin was lower in both sexes, and men with Parkinson disease had lower ghrelin (27.4 ± 24.7 versus 41.9 ± 31.2, p=.019, units unstated). The two reports likely share participants based on setting, authors and enrollment, though the exact overlap is unreported. Lower female lean mass was reported in 2016, while the later report found no clear lean mass difference in either sex. [2016 primary conference abstract, PDF p. 1216](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100774.pdf#page=1216), [2017 primary conference abstract, PDF p. 396](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100773.pdf#page=396).

The 2018 prospective report P2-4A-368 followed 22 Parkinson patients, 12 men. In men, mean two-year weight and fat changes were −2.7 ± 2.4 kg and −2.6 ± 3.7 kg. Worse SCOPA-AUT scores correlated with greater weight and fat loss (r=.714 and .632, p=.010 and .028). The male ghrelin–fat relationship was imprecise (p=.068) and clear analogous associations were absent in women. The report does not specify participant overlap with the earlier cross-sectional samples. These reports use aggregate dietary or metabolic measures and give no outcome by individual food. [2018 primary conference abstract, PDF p. 445](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100779.pdf#page=445).

## 2016 to 2019 aspiration prediction with citric acid and jelly

The Dryad companion to the 2018 Utano video-fluoroscopy article was checked against the repository file checksum. Its only populated worksheet contains 184 study rows and 53 fields. These include a binary feeding field, motor scores, aspiration-pneumonia indicators and swallowing measures. The article describes dietary intervention as ordinary or processed food. The workbook has no ingredient, menu, recipe, serving or food-specific outcome fields. The imaging analysis used one contrast jelly. [Local dataset text](Source-Text/2019-Tomita-PDVFS-Dryad.txt) · [Dryad CC0 dataset](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.g0v07) · [Version 13744 file inventory and MD5](https://datadryad.org/api/v2/versions/13744/files) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC5991364.txt)

The 2016 Utano report P2-30-7 and 2017 P2-1A-712 evaluated cough response to inhaled citric acid in saline. In the 2017 prospective analysis of 110 Parkinson patients without known prior aspiration pneumonia, 32 had a reduced response (C5 greater than 1.0%) and seven developed aspiration pneumonia over mean follow-up of 396 ± 173 days. Pneumonia developed sooner among participants with a reduced response (p=.009). Citric acid was a provocation test. The 2019 follow-up report P1-2-821 analyzed 44 patients without a prior episode. Five developed aspiration pneumonia in 1.5 years. Adjusted hazard ratios were 21.1 (95% CI 2.3–189.1) for the swallowing score and 6.3 (0.7–56.2) for reduced cough sensitivity. These estimates come from few events and the abstracts do not give exact cohort overlap. [2016 primary conference abstract, PDF p. 1215](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100774.pdf#page=1215), [2017 primary conference abstract, PDF p. 396](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100773.pdf#page=396), [2019 primary conference abstract, PDF p. 1489](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000130904.pdf#page=1489).

The 2019 report references Tomita et al.'s June 6, 2018 scale development article. In that separate retrospective cohort, 184 patients with Parkinson disease and suspected dysphagia swallowed 3 mL of dietitian-prepared jelly containing nonionic radiographic contrast during videofluoroscopy in the medication-on state. Twenty-five developed aspiration pneumonia within six months. A scale combining mastication, lingual motility before transfer, aspiration and total swallow time reached 92% sensitivity and 82% specificity at cutoff 3 in the development sample. The design evaluates prognostic test performance and contains one test consistency. The article mentions a prescribed jelly recipe without specifying ingredients in the public methods. The article is already archived locally. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0197608), [local full text](Source-Text/PMC5991364.txt).

## 2016 to 2019 oral intake rehabilitation and meal access

Kikuchi's 2016 P3-21-4 describes a Parkinson patient after aspiration pneumonia. Oral care, self-care support and speech therapy accompanied progression from one oral meal to three independent meals and removal of the nasogastric tube. Arai's 2017 P2-1P-754 describes an 83-year-old woman with Parkinson tremor and weakness who received nutrition by gastrostomy and ate lunch orally. A small support pillow, adapted spoon and dish reduced spills and shortened mealtimes according to the case team. Irie and colleagues' 2019 P2-1-1117 describes four weeks of occupational therapy before lunch for a stage 4 Parkinson patient with posture and wakefulness impairments. Tilt/reclining seating, positioning, stretches and adapted utensils accompanied improvement in FIM eating score from 1 to 5 and STEF hand function from 54 to 63. Daytime somnolence also decreased. Kuri and colleagues' 2019 P2-2-1554 used sitting posture training with a patient with stage 5 Parkinson disease, depression and a compression fracture. After two weeks, unsupported edge sitting rose from 18 to 90 seconds, meal-goal performance and satisfaction each rose from 4 to 5, and self-rated depression fell from 45 to 43. No named meal ingredients were reported in these cases. [2016 oral intake, PDF p. 1496](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100774.pdf#page=1496), [2017 adapted utensils, PDF p. 406](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100773.pdf#page=406), [2019 pre-lunch intervention, PDF p. 1785](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000130904.pdf#page=1785), [2019 sitting training, PDF p. 2222](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000130904.pdf#page=2222).

Shioji's 2018 symposium report SY11-2 includes a woman in her 80s with Parkinson disease and marked dyskinesia who could prepare rice at home. Delivered bento side dishes supplemented lunch and dinner. The author did not quantify a disease or nutritional outcome for her. Thickened water, paste-texture foods and a commercial supplement on the same page belong to two other cases. [Primary conference abstract, PDF p. 21](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100779.pdf#page=21).

## 2017 sweet potato menu and agar hydration report

The 2017 P2-1P-639 presents regional menus at Hansen disease institutions that included sweet-potato steamed bread, grilled rainbow trout and eel rice sushi. No Parkinson subgroup or item-level swallowing result appears. The distinct P2-1P-641 followed a dysphagic resident with congenital speech and hearing impairment. An oral rehydration solution was gelled with 4 g agar and offered with positioning at 30 degrees. Over four weeks intake rose from 460 to 1,100 mL per day alongside an increase in drinking opportunities. The report gives no Parkinson diagnosis. [Primary conference abstracts, PDF p. 378](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100773.pdf#page=378).

## 2018 meal aspiration and staged texture changes

Ando and colleagues' P1-2P-475 describes a 91-year-old woman with Parkinson disease and multifocal stroke whose swallowing deteriorated. Six months into admission she collapsed while eating independently. Food residue was recovered from her airway and she died. The abstract does not name the meal ingredient, and stroke was a concurrent cause of dysphagia. The adjacent P1-2P-474 report about suspected clumped thickener describes a separate hospital event. [Primary conference abstracts, PDF p. 277](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100779.pdf#page=277).

Kagaya and colleagues' P2-4A-381 describes a man in his 80s with advanced Parkinson disease who initially resumed part of a blenderized diet and later received gastrostomy because oral intake fell short of nutrition requirements. After gastrostomy he resumed a half portion of jelly at lunch and selected snacks, with medication timing, meal assistance and continued treatment adjusted. Frequency and volume increased and he reached full oral intake after a further four months. Aspiration pneumonia recurred, and he later received only pleasure feeds by mouth. Oike and colleagues' separate P2-4A-433 describes a patient in their 70s with Parkinson disease for 14 years and repeated aspiration. Tube nutrition, antiparkinsonian therapy, serial endoscopic swallowing exams and staged texture changes accompanied resumption of three mousse-texture meals per day and tube removal on day 105. Thickened soft side dishes followed before discharge on day 163. Both reports omit recipe composition and provide outcomes for combined care. [Kagaya primary conference abstract, PDF p. 449](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100779.pdf#page=449), [Oike primary conference abstract, PDF p. 462](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100779.pdf#page=462).

## 2018 and 2019 tube feeding after oral intake ceased

The 2018 Utano P2-4A-366 evaluated records from December 2006 to April 2017. Of 91 Parkinson patients, 41 received tube feeding and 36 received intravenous nutrition alone after oral feeding ceased. Median survival in those groups was 14 and 0.8 months, respectively. The same site, record dates and denominators appear in the May 26, 2018 Japanese Neurological Society Pj-088-6 report, which gave means of 809 ± 867 days and 40 ± 42 days (p<.001). PSP and MSA had separate denominators. The hospital's study notice specifies the retrospective selection of patients who died during the observation period. [2018 NHO abstract, PDF p. 445](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100779.pdf#page=445), [May proceedings, PDF p. 97, printed p. S346](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/clinicalneurol/58/Supplement/58_58_supplement_S250/_pdf/-char/ja#page=97), [official May program](https://www.neurology-jp.org/neuro2018/program/pdf/33.pdf), [hospital study notice](https://utano.hosp.go.jp/files/000139101.pdf).

On the NHO page, Motozaki's distinct P2-4A-367 followed ten neuromuscular patients after aspiration-prevention surgery. One Parkinson patient was among seven who resumed some oral intake. The report does not identify what that person ate. Kōno and colleagues' separate 2019 Omuta P2-2-1612 review compared deceased patients after loss of oral swallowing. Among patients with Parkinson disease, mean survival was 41.1 ± 33.4 days for the ten receiving IV fluids alone and 38.1 ± 40.6 months for the 34 receiving gastrostomy or nasal tube feeding. The retrospective route comparisons are subject to selection by disease severity and goals of care. The reports give no named formula. [2018 primary conference abstracts, PDF p. 445](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100779.pdf#page=445), [2019 primary conference abstract, PDF p. 2280](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000130904.pdf#page=2280).

## 2017 and 2018 food texture choices in Hokkaido Parkinson outpatients

Mori and colleagues' May 25, 2017 public research report follows three survey waves at three Hokkaido institutions and cites Matsushima's 2016 cross-sectional publication on the baseline cohort. Meal assistance was associated with less progression on the Manor dysphagia scale in Spearman analysis. Sixteen patients died between the second and third waves. The report gave a mortality HR of 3.04 (95% CI 1.08–8.53) for the specified Manor cut point of 11, and HR 5.55 (1.24–24.83) for Hoehn and Yahr stage 3 or above. The observational surveys did not establish an effect from a particular food. [Public release record](https://mhlw-grants.niph.go.jp/project/25959), [primary research report](https://mhlw-grants.niph.go.jp/system/files/2016/162051/201610043B_upload/201610043B0006.pdf), [2016 primary article, local full text](Source-Text/PMC4949767.txt).

Matsushima and colleagues' May 26, 2018 poster Pj-088-5 analyzed 237 patients interviewed in 2013 and 205 responding by mail in 2015. Standard meals alone fell from 149/237 (62.8%) to 96/205 (46.8%), while standard meals plus dysphagia-oriented commercial care foods increased from 4/237 (1.7%) to 43/205 (21.0%, p<.0001). In the 205-person paired dysphagia comparison, cases increased from 51 to 92 (p<.0001). BMI did not differ by meal format in the poster. The authors discuss advice given around the initial survey. Diet choices, dysphagia progression and changes in response mode may all affect the comparison. No ingredient, recipe or food-level outcome is given. The 2014 poster and 2016 article describe the overlapping baseline wave. [Primary abstract, PDF p. 96, printed p. S345](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/clinicalneurol/58/Supplement/58_58_supplement_S250/_pdf/-char/ja#page=96), [official May program](https://www.neurology-jp.org/neuro2018/program/pdf/33.pdf), [2014 baseline abstract](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-NHO-Middle.md#2014-diet-modifications-and-dysphagia-in-hokkaido-parkinson-outpatients).

## 2019 cooked rice and rice porridge swallowing in one patient

Ōtsuki and colleagues presented this case at the February 2, 2019 Sapporo City Hospital meeting and expanded it in the Hokkaido neurological disease center's November 2019 bulletin. A woman in her late 80s had Hoehn and Yahr stage 3 Parkinson disease, oral dyskinesia and cognitive impairment (MMSE 15). On endoscopy, cooked rice reached the pharynx with poor bolus formation and left small pharyngeal residue. Alternating with liquid cleared it. Full rice porridge left no pharyngeal residue on that exam. Rice variety, dose, repeated contrasts and numerical residue ratings were unreported. [Primary case report, viewer pp. 67–69](https://www.hokkaido-find.jp/sys/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/FIND_06_2019.pdf#page=67), [endoscopy findings, viewer p. 68](https://www.hokkaido-find.jp/sys/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/FIND_06_2019.pdf#page=68), [conference abstract, viewer p. 108](https://www.hokkaido-find.jp/sys/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/FIND_06_2019.pdf#page=108).

The patient chewed gummy candy as a favorite food to receive visual and tactile feedback, and also performed tongue-coordination practice with a swab. Illustrative chew counts were 35 at baseline, 40 at week one and 50 at week two. Staff reported better bolus formation and less pharyngeal residue during ordinary meals after combined training. Candy composition and an isolated candy effect were unreported. [Primary case report, viewer pp. 68–69](https://www.hokkaido-find.jp/sys/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/FIND_06_2019.pdf#page=68).

## 2018 green tea through gastrostomy

Ichihara's P2-4A-396 gave 200 mL green tea per day, reported as 232 mg tannin, through gastrostomy to three neurological inpatients with indwelling urinary catheters. Some urine pH, white cell or protein measures fell. The authors found no marked overall improvement and did not give catheter blockage counts. The case diagnoses and tea preparation are unreported. Cranberry juice appears as background. The adjacent report that lists Parkinson disease evaluates positioning in a separate group. Olfaction, handwriting and fatigue were outside the tea report's outcomes. [Primary conference abstract, PDF p. 452](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100779.pdf#page=452).

## 2019 coffee in drug-induced parkinsonism

Kamada's P2-1-1230 describes a woman in her 70s with drug-induced parkinsonism, disorientation and agitation. Her care team used familiar enka music, knitting and conversation and worked with speech therapy so she could drink her favorite coffee. The team described qualitative improvement in agitation and expression during this package. The report gives no coffee preparation or dose, no fatigue measure and no separate coffee comparison. [Primary conference abstract, PDF p. 1898](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000130904.pdf#page=1898).

## 2019 food odors as Parkinson smell-test stimuli

Ishigure and colleagues' WS13-4 tested 18 patients with Parkinson disease, including 13 women, using the Japanese odor stick identification test. Overall identification was poorer relative to test norms. Menthol and curry odors were comparatively preserved, and correct and incorrect responses were near evenly split for mandarin and condensed milk among other odorants. The authors reported no clear pre/post DBS change in olfaction and did not specify the paired sample size. The odorants were test stimuli. [Primary conference abstract, PDF p. 282](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000130904.pdf#page=282).

## 2016 topical sesame oil in neuromuscular oral care

The off-flag P2-32-4 reports three adults unable to eat orally while using continuous noninvasive positive-pressure ventilation, two with myotonic dystrophy and one with facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy. Sesame oil was applied to the mouth after cleaning three times a day for one week. The team reported a greater oral-moisture response in two patients with milder dryness than in the patient with severe dryness. The exposure was topical and the diagnoses were neuromuscular conditions outside Parkinson disease. [Primary conference abstract, PDF p. 1224](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100774.pdf#page=1224).
