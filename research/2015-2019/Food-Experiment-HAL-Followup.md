# French-language dietary research

## 2016 spectrophotometry of sprouted chickpeas and broad beans

Gouigah and colleagues’ French-language report appeared online August 3, 2016, ahead of its 2017 issue. The publisher abstract describes UV-visible spectrophotometry of levodopa in germinated and nongerminated legumes. The authors report higher estimated concentrations in germinated chickpeas than in the sampled broad bean tissues except the tissue connecting seed and pod. The abstract does not give a numerical estimate for chickpeas or enough assay detail to check compound specificity. A conference abstract by the same authors reports the same comparisons and has no confirmed date on the accessible page. It is treated as a duplicate report. [Journal abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10298-016-1037-4) · [Matching conference abstract](https://publications.waset.org/abstracts/42205/spectrophotometric-determination-of-l-dopa-in-germinated-and-non-germinated-broad-beans-vicia-faba-l-and-chickpea-cicer-aritinum-l)

The source concerns food composition. It supplies no estimates for roasted chickpeas or dyskinesia after eating them. A separate case from the preceding period describes dyskinesia after raw and stewed broad beans. Chickpeas were absent from that meal history. [2013 clinical report](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Broad-Bean-Followup.md#severe-dyskinesia-after-raw-and-stewed-broad-beans)

## 2016 coffee intake and REM sleep behavior disorder

Jacobs and colleagues compared 98 people with Parkinson’s and REM sleep behavior disorder with 91 with Parkinson’s and no REM sleep behavior disorder. In Table 1, 80.6% versus 81.3% reported regular coffee use, with an adjusted odds ratio of 0.78 (95% CI 0.36–1.70). The estimate per cup per week was 1.01 (0.98–1.05). The questionnaire asked about coffee, tea and colas, while the table supplies estimates for coffee and gives no preparation-specific results. The outcome was subtype status based on sleep assessment. The study measured neither fatigue severity after coffee nor a change in the frequency of dream enactment following intake. [Primary report and Table 1](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/JPD-150725) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26889635/)

## 2017 green tea polyphenols and visual-system tissue in mice

Shallie and colleagues reported superior colliculus histology on May 12, 2017 and retinal outcomes on June 20, 2017. Both reports describe 25 male mice in five groups with matching weights, treatment sequences and oral green tea exposure. They share the lead investigator and other authors. Cohort overlap is plausible and the reports do not provide individual identifiers. The colliculus abstract reports higher neuronal density with pretreatment than in the MPTP and tea-only groups. The retinal article reports changes in thickness and retinal biochemistry. The reports provide no visual behavior, olfactory test or fatigue measure. [Colliculus report and publisher date](https://journaljammr.com/index.php/JAMMR/article/view/3169) · [Retinal article and publication date](https://journals.indexcopernicus.com/api/file/viewByFileId/282956)
