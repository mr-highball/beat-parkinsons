# Posture, pain, urinary urgency and related outcomes

## Mixed fruit juice and exercise soreness ingredient correction

Lima et al. randomized 30 men to fruit juice or a maltodextrin comparator. The study reports different knee-soreness trajectories after downhill running. Section 2.3 names apple, plum, blueberry, maqui berry, raspberry and cranberry concentrates. The source does not contain blackberry. A later review misidentified the ingredients, so this comparison cannot independently support the blackberry soreness observation. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6835892/) · [Local source text](Source-Text/PMC6835892.txt)

## Registered fish oil and HMB comparison in COPD

NCT03796455 first posted January 8, 2019 and lists six-minute walk distance as a secondary functional measure. It describes placebo oil capsules with no ingredient breakdown in the intervention text. The official record shows completion with no posted results. The linked 2024 trial publication identifies a soybean-and-corn-oil capsule comparison with maltodextrin powder. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03796455) · [Local source text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT03796455.txt)

## Curcumin in a multiarm rotenone model

Motawi et al. first published online December 9, 2019. The primary abstract describes variable behavioral changes after interventions including isolated curcumin, niacin and an adenosine receptor antagonist. The publisher body and arm-specific rigidity estimates remain unverified. The abstract gives no grated turmeric or clinical outcome. [Original source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11010-019-03670-0) · [Local source text](Source-Text/2019-Motawi-Curcumin-Behavior-Extract.txt)

## Rigidity terminology in an herbal treatment review

The opened publisher PDF starts with rigidity as a feature of Parkinson disease. The turmeric citations concern cells and experimental mice. The review does not contain a separate rigidity result after grated turmeric.
