# Pomegranate and experimental parkinsonism or drowsiness

## Pomegranate juice and postural stability in Poznań

Kujawska and colleagues first published this report on December 27, 2019. In male Wistar rats with a lower-dose subcutaneous rotenone regimen, a commercial pomegranate concentrate given before and alongside toxin exposure reduced postural impairment by 20% relative to the rotenone arm. The study also reported less nigral cell injury and altered α-synuclein measures. The earlier Tapias study used Lewis rats, another juice and a higher-dose intraperitoneal rotenone regimen. It found worse rearing and neuronal loss after juice, with an inconclusive postural comparison. These differences permit several hypotheses about the divergent results. The two studies did not test those explanations directly or measure the four frozen aril symptoms. ## Pomegranate juice and levodopa induced dyskinesia in mice

Rezaee and Hadipour Jahromy’s 2018 MPTP study added oral pomegranate juice to levodopa after induced dyskinesia was established. The paper reports lower abnormal involuntary movement and catalepsy scores during the next 20 days than with continued levodopa alone. The exposure was juice and the outcomes were movement assays in mice. The report adds a distinct model to the preclinical record. [Primary report and figures](https://www.phcogj.com/sites/default/files/PharmacognJ-10-700.pdf)

## Pomegranate water extract and drowsiness after sleep deprivation

Na and colleagues’ November 30, 2016 abstract reports that a hot-water pomegranate extract improved drowsiness scores and other measures in sleep-deprived rats. The available abstract provides no numerical drowsiness comparison. Induced sleep deprivation and hot-water extract differ from daytime sleepiness in people with Parkinson’s eating raw fruit. [Original DOI](https://doi.org/10.3746/jkfn.2016.45.11.1533) · [Primary abstract indexed by the Korean Traditional Knowledge Portal](https://koreantk.com/ktkp2014/thesis/thesis-view.view?ctrlNo=SPOOBG_2016_v45n11_1533)

## Registered daytime sleepiness after a mixed polyphenol supplement

NCT03432104 first appeared February 14, 2018. The crossover study in healthy adults registered daytime sleepiness as a secondary outcome. The intervention combined water and ethanol extracts from pomegranate, olive leaf, grape seed, citrus, tea and blueberry. The later pharmacokinetic publication omits a separate sleepiness outcome. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03432104) · [Local 2022 primary text](../2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC9692523.txt)
