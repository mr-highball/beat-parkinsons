## Park-Vida diet and bowel symptoms in Lins

Thaís Assis Astorino and colleagues reported on eight people with Parkinson’s disease aged 61–80 at the November 29–30, 2019 CONIC-Semesp meeting. Their cross-sectional study paired 24-hour dietary recall with a questionnaire covering bowel habits, chewing, swallowing, appetite and medication timing. Four of eight reported constipation and seven of eight drank less than the study’s water benchmark. No participant reported difficulty chewing or swallowing. The authors report that participants separated levodopa from protein-containing meals by at least one hour. The report also tabulates nutrient intake, including iron. It provides no food-specific or symptom-stratified intake results. The same project’s 2018 proposal predates data collection and provides no results. [2019 proceedings report](https://conic-semesp.org.br/anais/files/2019/1000005036.pdf), [official 2019 meeting dates](https://www.semesp.org.br/mais-eventos/2019/11/27/conic-semesp-recebe-cerca-de-2-mil-trabalhos/), [institutional 2019 proceedings citation](https://www.unisalesiano.com.br/cpa/relatorios/Relatorio2019.pdf), [2018 proposal](https://conic-semesp.org.br/anais/files/2018/trabalho-1000001174.pdf)

## Nutritional counseling and constipation in Barra Mansa

The April 17, 2018 CONBRAN clinical nutrition proceedings include two reports from Jucimara Martins dos Santos and colleagues. The first followed 13 adults with Parkinson’s disease, ages 52–73, for four months, with nutrition consultations at least monthly and continued medical care. The abstract describes limited fruit and vegetable intake and low fluid intake before counseling. It reports constipation in 54% of participants and improvement in 23% after counseling. It also reports increased liquid intake and advice on separating dietary protein from levodopa/benserazide. There was no control group, named food comparison, symptom scale or analysis linking a particular intake change with improvement. The abstract is unclear about the denominator for the improvement percentage. [CONBRAN proceedings, PDF pp. 18–19](https://www.rasbran.com.br/rasbran/article/download/985/196/2739#page=18), [local abstract text](Source-Text/2018-Santos-Miranda-CONBRAN-Parkinson-Constipation.txt)

The second report describes a 59-year-old man with Parkinson’s disease and metabolic syndrome followed for 11 months. It records changes in dietary pattern, body weight and blood lipids. It gives no change in measured Parkinson’s symptoms. Both abstracts carry the same ethics identifier, CAAE 61623916.0.0000.5236, so the case may overlap the 13-person report. [CONBRAN proceedings, PDF pp. 325–326](https://www.rasbran.com.br/rasbran/article/download/985/196/2739#page=325), [local abstract text](Source-Text/2018-Santos-CONBRAN-Parkinson-Metabolic-Case.txt), [publisher license and publication date](https://www.rasbran.com.br/rasbran/article/view/985)
