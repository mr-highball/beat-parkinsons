# Portuguese-language Parkinson swallowing studies, 2015–2019

## Earlier Pará questionnaire and choking observations

Barbosa and colleagues reported eight men with Parkinson disease in poster P040 at the 49th Brazilian Congress of Otorhinolaryngology in 2019. Five reported choking, three reported coughing and three oral escape. Four had changed food consistency. Two people at Hoehn and Yahr stage 4 scored 16 and 18 on the EAT-10. The report gives no named food or instrumental potato comparison. It shares the first author, five other investigators and the same institution with the 24-person study published in 2020. The reports do not identify whether the eight participants were included in the later sample. Two examiners’ academic records list a 2019 residency monograph by the first author with a title corresponding to the later article. The monograph and worksheet have not been located publicly. ## French bread and water in a matched clinical swallowing assessment

Diogo Mello Rodrigues's 2015 UFRGS thesis examined 47 people with Parkinson disease at Hoehn and Yahr stages II–III and 47 comparison participants. The clinician offered approximately 50 g of pão francês for habitual eating, followed by approximately 50 mL of water in small sips. The clinical assessment observed choking in 13% of participants with Parkinson disease and 0% of controls. Table 1 aggregates clinical signs by group and gives no event-by-bolus counts. The report does not establish whether the observed choking occurred with bread or with water. It does not identify airway obstruction, meal emergencies or a baked-potato preparation. The title page gives February 20, 2015. ## Clinical and endoscopic dysphagia comparison across three diagnoses

The University of São Paulo released Luciana Avila dos Santos's thesis on January 8, 2016, after the June 3, 2015 defense. Its abstract reports 35 participants aged 52–82, of whom six were women, with stroke, Alzheimer's disease or Parkinson disease. The same people underwent a clinical assessment and nasoendoscopy on one day. The abstract reports aggregate agreement. The official thesis PDF front page was verified on an earlier pass. The deposited PDF routes timed out on November 12, 2026. The December 31, 2024 journal article has the same study title, investigator and advisor, and matching sample and protocol details. Its documented PDF download and viewer returned HTTP 403. The available records are consistent with one cohort. The size of the Parkinson subgroup, tested preparations and item-level outcomes remain unverified. ## Food examples in the Brazilian Parkinson swallowing questionnaire

Ayres and colleagues' July–August 2016 report tested the Brazilian Portuguese Swallowing Disturbance Questionnaire (SDQ-DP) twice in 23 people with Parkinson disease. Four scored at least 11. The test-retest intraclass correlation for the total was .912 (95% CI .792–.963). Annex 1 groups apple, water-and-salt cracker and filled cookie as examples in the solid-chewing item. The solid-swallowing item uses apple and crackers as examples of food sticking in the throat. Table 2 gives item-level test-retest comparisons. It does not separate responses by named food or report any administered bolus. This is a different sample from the original Israeli validation and later Pará survey. ## Standardized bread and Parkinson chewing kinematics

Albuquerque and colleagues' February 2017 report measured 20 seconds of chewing 15 g of bread from one baker and batch. The sample included 15 comparison participants, 14 people with rigidity-predominant Parkinson disease and 13 with tremor-predominant Parkinson disease. The rigidity group had fewer chewing cycles than controls (mean 18.21 vs. 23.13, p = .034) and a smaller maximum mouth opening (26.72 vs. 34.66 mm, p = .018). Maximum jaw speed did not differ significantly. Participants with Parkinson disease did not report dysphagia at enrollment. The study measured jaw motion and gives no swallowing, aspiration or choking outcome. ## Dysphagia and motor severity in a Belo Horizonte sample

Daianny de Souza Silva's 2018 UFMG study assessed 30 people with Parkinson disease. Nineteen screened positive for dysphagia on EAT-10. EAT-10 correlated with UPDRS III (Spearman r = .362, p = .049) and total UPDRS (r = .402, p = .028). The SCOPA-AUT analysis sums its first four swallowing items. The tables show associations for the combined symptom measures, and the study includes no food challenge or named food-level estimate.
