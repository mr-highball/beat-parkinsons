# Cumin and dyskinesia

## 2016 Cuminum extract in an MPTP mouse model

Kim et al., 2016-04-30, DOI 10.4314/tjpr.v15i4.14, authenticated dried Cuminum cyminum fruit, powdered it, extracted it using boiling water, then administered the aqueous extract by oral gavage to MPTP-treated mice at 100, 200 or 300 mg/kg for 21 days. Rotarod, open-field line crossings, rearing, grooming and immobility, and passive-avoidance tests were used. The authors report higher rotarod performance on day 20 with the extracts. These are movement and learning measures in a toxin model, not levodopa-induced dyskinesia. The methods contain an apparent group-name typo, MPTP + ITE 300 mg/kg, while the dosing paragraph and results call the group cumin extract. No toasted culinary seed or human exposure was tested.

## 2016 Nigella oil and haloperidol-related oral dyskinesia

Malik et al., PMID 27752803, reported that Nigella sativa seed oil 0.2 mL per rat with haloperidol 1 mg/kg reduced extrapyramidal-like behavior and oral dyskinesia in rats. The original abstract is accessible; full methods remain unverified. Nigella sativa, often called black cumin, is a different species from Cuminum cyminum; extracted oil, antipsychotic-induced movement and rats differ from toasted cumin and levodopa dyskinesia in people with Parkinson disease. A 2018 ASENT abstract (PMID 29987760, PMC6095775), titled The Key Roles of Nigella Sativa-Oil in Regulating Microglia and Mitochondrial Activity in Parkinsonism, has overlapping authors, identical drug and oil doses, and matching behavioral outcome; treat as potentially overlapping, not independent replication.

## 2018 Cumin intake in a Parkinson diet survey

Sauerbier et al., published 2018-12-18, PMID 30662706, PMCID PMC6312592, DOI 10.1155/2018/7274085, surveyed 139 Parkinson patients about spices and herbs used in the previous month. Cumin was checked by 11/66 White, 25/34 Asian, and 8/39 Black African/Caribbean participants. The questionnaire did not specify cumin botanical identity, toasting or dose. The study examined Hoehn–Yahr stage and nonmotor symptom scales, with no cumin-specific dyskinesia comparison. Reported individual use is not assigned treatment or evidence of change in dyskinesia.

## 2018 Heated cumin in a mixed Parkinson diet trial

Phillips et al., 2018, PMID 30098269, PMCID PMC6175383, DOI 10.1002/mds.27390, ketogenic recipe supplement S3 p.72 includes cumin added to a pan with oil and vegetables and then simmered in mixed chicken enchilada soup. It does not say seed was toasted; individual ingredient consumption is unverified. Motor complications were reported as total MDS-UPDRS IV, not cumin-specific dyskinesia severity. Diet group comparisons and individual medication changes cannot isolate the ingredient effect.
