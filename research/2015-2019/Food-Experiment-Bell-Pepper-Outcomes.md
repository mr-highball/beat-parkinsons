# Bell pepper preparation and symptom outcomes

## Red and green capsicum in two Parkinson diet menus

In the report published August 11, 2018, Phillips et al. randomized 47 people with Parkinson disease to a low-fat or ketogenic menu for eight weeks. Forty-four commenced the assigned diets and 38 completed the trial. Both original menus list red or green capsicum in shopping lists and recipes. The ketogenic menu separately marks optional green chile or sliced jalapeños in relevant recipes. Paprika spice is listed separately from whole capsicum. In the New Zealand menu, the red and green vegetable entries indicate the sweet capsicum food category. Cultivar and individual pepper intake were unavailable. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6175383/) · [Official registration](https://www.anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?ACTRN=12617000027314) · [Low-fat plan](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6175383.1/MDS-33-1306-s002.pdf) · [Ketogenic plan](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6175383.1/MDS-33-1306-s003.pdf)

- The ketogenic plan sautés capsicum before baking it in the frittata (printed p. 58), bakes stuffed Vine Sweet mini peppers at 200 °C for 18–22 minutes (p. 89), and bakes diced red or green capsicum in Tex Mex Casserole, with jalapeño a separate optional ingredient (p. 91). Other recipes simmer or pan-cook capsicum. The low-fat plan uses capsicum in a pan-cooked vegetable medley and baked mini pizza toppings. Its recipe titled Roast Vegetables (p. 83) does not include capsicum. The reviewed recipes do not establish a separately grilled or raw serving, and the baked peppers are the closest documented exposure to roasted. [Low-fat plan](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6175383.1/MDS-33-1306-s002.pdf) · [Ketogenic plan](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6175383.1/MDS-33-1306-s003.pdf)
- The daytime sleepiness item of MDS-UPDRS Part I decreased 0.45 points more for the ketogenic diet, a descriptive item difference under two pepper-containing diets. Part I includes apathy, and the paper supplies no apathy-item result. Part II includes a handwriting-legibility item and reported mean total changes of −1.33 ± 3.28 low-fat versus −3.13 ± 4.01 ketogenic (between p=.11). Part III includes gait and hand motor tasks and reported mean total changes of −8.58 ± 5.50 versus −6.27 ± 4.07 (p=.055). The report and appendix give no handwriting size, independent gait-speed or hand-dexterity change, or pepper-specific contrast. ## Sweet pepper intake and handgrip in Tianjin adults

Wu et al. analyzed 3,717 adults in Tianjin. The 88-item questionnaire asked separately about chili and sweet pepper consumption in the previous month. The sweet-pepper item used portions of 33 g for men and 29 g for women and combined intake into less than once weekly, once weekly and at least two to three times weekly. Preparation within the sweet-pepper item was unspecified. Grip was the best of one dynamometer effort per hand. The study applied separate sex-specific adjustments, including chili consumption when modeling sweet pepper. [Primary article, Methods and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12879805/) · [Readable primary paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/285782042_Consumption_of_chilies_but_not_sweet_peppers_is_positively_related_to_handgrip_strength_in_an_adult_population)

| Group | Less than weekly, kg (95% CI) | Once weekly, kg (95% CI) | At least two to three times weekly, kg (95% CI) | Trend p |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Men | 45.4 (42.8–48.0) | 45.4 (42.8–47.9) | 45.2 (42.6–47.7) | .55 |
| Women | 27.6 (23.6–31.6) | 27.7 (23.7–31.6) | 27.7 (23.7–31.6) | .77 |

For chili, the fully adjusted handgrip estimates in men were 44.7, 45.5 and 45.8 kg in the corresponding categories (p<.01). The women’s chili trend was p=.19. The reported chili association cannot be assigned to sweet pepper. This cross-sectional study gives grip strength, a related measure distinct from fine hand dexterity in Parkinson disease. ## Nonpungent Capsicum activity trial registration

UMIN000032451 gives December 31, 2018 as the disclosed public date and May 1, 2018 as the registration date. The anticipated start was February 5, 2018. Its intervention was 9 mg/day of Capsicum extract versus placebo for 12 weeks in healthy adults. Primary outcomes included step count, activity intensity and SF-36. Secondary outcomes included Japanese POMS2 ratings for vigor and fatigue. The public record marks main results published in its recruitment-status field and unpublished in its separate result-publication field. It contains no outcome table. The linked January 2020 article supplies outcomes and identifies the nonpungent cultivar. The paper says the study was preregistered, and the publicly labeled dates follow the planned start. No earlier public preregistration record was verified.
