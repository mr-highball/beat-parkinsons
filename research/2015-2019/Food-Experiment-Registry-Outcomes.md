# Capsaicin as a cough assessment stimulus

Hegland and colleagues compared nebulized capsaicin with aerosolized water in 21 participants with Parkinson’s and 49 healthy participants. Participants coughed more after capsaicin than after water fog (p < 0.001). In this sample, a cough response to water fog classified Parkinson’s participants by dysphagia status with reported sensitivity of 77.8% and specificity of 90.9%. The corresponding registry record enrolled 158 participants, and linkage of this 70-person report to that enrollment could not be confirmed. The study compared cough responses to diagnostic stimuli and assessed pre-existing swallowing dysfunction. [Primary paper, PMID 26497650](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26497650/) · [Related registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT02390089.txt)

The posted NCT02183519 results and a matching 2017 paper describe 16 participants with Parkinson’s and 28 healthy older adults before exclusions. The analyzed sample had 13 and 25, respectively. The paper reported higher expiratory airflow and expired volume under verbal and visual cueing for both capsaicin-induced reflex cough and voluntary cough. The registry’s displayed group means alone do not estimate that cueing effect. [Primary paper, PMID 28360120](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28360120/) · [Posted results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02183519?tab=results)

These studies are relevant to swallowing assessment and airway protection. They did not test whether consuming ground cayenne reduces swallowing difficulty in Parkinson’s disease, the outcome in frozen claim `0724`.
