# Canola and rapeseed oil outcome follow-up

## Symptom reporting in a Parkinson diet trial with canola on the menu

Phillips et al. published this randomized eight-week comparison in August 2018. Forty-seven people with Parkinson disease were randomized to low-fat or ketogenic diet, 44 commenced the diets and 38 finished. The low-fat meal plan lists canola among the menu ingredients without specifying pressing or refining. Its oil dose and intake were not reported separately. The whole-diet arms differed in macronutrients and numerous other foods. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6175383/) · [Low-fat menu](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6175383.1/MDS-33-1306-s002.pdf) · [Earlier local diet and menu review](Food-Experiment-MDS-Diet-Followup.md#low-fat-and-ketogenic-meal-plans-with-measured-symptom-outcomes)

- **Urinary problems and standing symptoms.** The article describes a between-arm difference of −0.70 points favoring ketogenic diet for the MDS-UPDRS Part I urinary problems item. This ordinal item combines manifestations such as urgency, frequency and leakage. No individual urgency-episode count, oil contrast or item-level uncertainty interval was published. In Table 3, lightheadedness was reported by 2/23 and 4/23 low-fat participants during weeks 1–4 and 5–8, respectively, versus 2/24 in each interval in the ketogenic arm. A low-fat participant with preexisting orthostatic hypotension and falls needed more midodrine. Standing-triggered lightheadedness was not measured systematically. [Primary results](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mds.27390)
- **Off time and dyskinesia.** The Part IV motor-complications aggregate changed by −0.79 ± 2.71 for low-fat and −1.56 ± 2.45 for ketogenic diet, between-arm p=.32. The report gives no component-level daily off time or dyskinesia-severity estimates. A ketogenic-arm participant reduced several dopaminergic drugs because of worsening dyskinesia, which partially resolved. The case combines a different diet with medication changes. [Primary results](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mds.27390)
- **Facial expression, posture, freezing, handwriting and speech.** Parts II and III had between-diet p=.11 and p=.055, respectively. Their totals combine different motor domains. Item-level facial expression, postural stability, freezing, handwriting and speech values were unavailable in the article and linked menus. It did not report frequency of freezing or an acoustic measure of voice volume. [Primary results](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/mds.27390) · [Movement Disorder Society scale documentation](https://www.movementdisorders.org/MDS/MDS-Rating-Scales/MDS-Unified-Parkinsons-Disease-Rating-Scale-MDS-UPDRS.htm)

## Happy Nurse protocol specifies the comparator oil mixture

Watanabe et al. published the randomized protocol January 31, 2015. It specifies a capsule with 47% rapeseed oil, 25% soybean oil, 25% olive oil and 3% fish oil. The fish oil was included to help preserve masking. No extraction method or cultivar for the rapeseed component was specified. Anxiety and depression scales were among the secondary outcomes. [Primary protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4326519/) · [Local licensed protocol text](Source-Text/2015-Watanabe-Happy-Nurse-Protocol.txt)

## Anxiety outcomes with a rapeseed oil blend in hospital nurses

The 2018 primary report randomized 80 hospital nurses. The primary 26-week HADS measure was the total score. The authors' table in the Japanese labor ministry report gives anxiety-specific values. At 26 weeks, the comparator blend moved from 3.95 to 3.75 on HADS anxiety. The treatment-by-time contrast was +0.13 points (95% CI −0.91 to +1.16, p=.812). On GAD-7 the corresponding contrast was −0.60 (95% CI −1.78 to +0.57, p=.313). At 52 weeks the HADS anxiety contrast was +1.04 (95% CI −0.01 to +2.08, p=.050 as printed) and GAD-7 −0.08 (p=.893). The comparison was fish oil against a blended control and enrolled hospital staff. It provides no isolated rapeseed, pressing-method or Parkinson anxiety estimate. [Primary article record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29627596/) · [Authors' results and table in the labor ministry report](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/seisakunitsuite/bunya/koyou_roudou/roudoukijun/rousai/hojokin/dl/29_160102-01.pdf)

## Insomnia scores with the rapeseed-oil blend

Table 2 of the same Happy Nurse trial reports a 13-week Insomnia Severity Index mean of 5.07 with fish oil and 6.62 with the mixed comparator containing 47% rapeseed oil. The group-by-time comparison was +2.05 (95% CI 0.34–3.76, p=.019). The 26- and 52-week comparisons were inconclusive. The authors caution about multiple secondary comparisons. The trial had no isolated rapeseed or oil-free arm, and it did not measure dream enactment. Pressing method and Parkinson-specific outcomes were unreported. ## Rapeseed versus palm diet in a developmental mouse anxiety experiment

Desor, Ould-Hamouda, Soulimani and Delplanque reported this work at the July 2015 International Rapeseed Congress on printed page 403. Swiss dams consumed a higher-alpha-linolenic rapeseed diet or a low-alpha-linolenic palm diet before and during gestation and lactation. Pups continued their assigned diets after weaning. Female offspring from the rapeseed group had lower anxiety-related behavior in the elevated plus maze than those in the palm group. Neonatal geotaxis and suspension showed no significant differences. The meeting abstract provides limited quantitative reporting and leaves preparation unverified. The developmental exposure and fatty-acid-poor palm comparator limit inference to adult Parkinson anxiety. [Congress abstract](https://www.gcirc.org/fileadmin/documents/Proceedings/IRC2015SaskatoonCanada/IRC2015-C_PostersAbstracts.pdf)

## Anxiety behavior after prenatal canola-rich and lard-rich diets

Dos Santos Souto et al. first published this work online October 30, 2019, with print publication in 2020. Dams were assigned standard chow or high-fat diets rich in canola oil or lard. The authors reported more anxiety-related behavior in offspring from the high-fat groups on the elevated plus maze. The comparison varied dietary fat and composition against standard chow and involved prenatal exposure. The abstract provides no cold-pressed canola or Parkinson-specific estimate. [Primary article record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31676261/)

## Anxiety behavior with fortified rapeseed oil in db db mice

Fourrier et al. first published online November 12, 2019. The fortified experimental diet used ALA-rich rapeseed oil with coenzyme Q10, tocopherol and canolol. The authors reported no improvement in anxiety-like behavior in db/db mice despite recovery of spatial memory performance in a water maze. The authors assessed metabolic syndrome and a fortified preparation. The experiment does not isolate cold-pressed oil or apply the Parkinson anxiety outcome. [Primary article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0889159119304635)

## Canola vehicle in a Parkinson model with motor assays

The December 2016 online publication used a unilateral 6-hydroxydopamine rat model. The control vehicle was canola oil, administered during the 30-day comparison. The experimental assignment tested diphenyl diselenide. The authors found improvements with the active treatment in stepping, cylinder and bridge tasks, with no improvement in rotarod performance. The abstract does not report an independent oil comparison, oil pressing method, a Parkinson clinical pull test or freezing-event counts. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27915043/)
