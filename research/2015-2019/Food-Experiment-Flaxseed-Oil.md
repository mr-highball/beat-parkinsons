# Flaxseed oil evidence

## Oil as a carrier for an intranasal drug

A 2018 delivery study tracked ropinirole in plasma and brain after administering an oil-based formulation by the nasal route in mice. Its results address drug distribution. A separate behavior study and an oral oil comparison are absent from the accessible report. [Primary source scope](Source-Text/2018-PMID30201564-Flaxseed-Oil-Nasal-Drug-Carrier-Scope.txt).

## Flaxseed-derived omega-3 plus vitamin E and UPDRS parts

Taghizadeh and colleagues published the 60-person Parkinson trial on March 22, 2017. The intervention combined flaxseed-derived omega-3 with vitamin E. The accepted manuscript describes assessment of four UPDRS parts but Table 2 publishes only the combined total, with a change of −3.3 versus +4.4 with placebo (p = .02). The report gives no individual symptom results or verified oil pressing method. The study identifier is IRCT201604035623N73. [Primary accepted manuscript, Methods and Table 2](https://magistralbr.caldic.com/storage/product-files/1634072890.pdf) · [Earlier archive entry](Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#flaxseed-derived-omega-3-plus-vitamin-e)

Fifty-four participants completed the trial and 60 were analyzed by intention to treat. Table 2 gives SD 10.0 and 14.9 for the two changes above. Table 3 reports adjusted changes of −3.4 and +4.7 (p=.005). Section 2.6 cites the 2008 revised instrument, while Tables 2–3 print a 0–195 total range that differs from the full revised-scale sum. The paper leaves exact implementation and item-level pain, rigidity and bradykinesia outcomes unverified. The sample-size calculation used hs-CRP. The three assessed symptom links identify this single cohort. [Primary manuscript, Tables 2–3](https://magistralbr.caldic.com/storage/product-files/1634072890.pdf) · [Revised scale](https://www.movementdisorders.org/MDS-Files1/Resources/PDFs/MDS-UPDRS.pdf)

Tamtaji and colleagues published the independent 40-person trial online December 8, 2018 under IRCT2017061234497N1. Total change was −3.4 versus +7.1 (p = .008). The combined supplement and placebo group changes in Part I were −0.1 and +1.8 (p = .01). Table 2 gives Part II motor daily living changes of −0.8 and +1.0 and Part III motor examination changes of −1.9 and +3.1. Both Part II and III comparisons were printed as p = .05 and the authors described those parts as having no clear treatment effect. The publication does not provide item-level estimates or an oil-only group. [Primary full report, Table 2](https://magistralbr.caldic.com/storage/product-files/1339864240.pdf) · [Publisher report](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0303846718304633)

## Oral flaxseed oil and open-field activity in rotenone mice

Shallie and colleagues reported that oil groups crossed more lines and reared more often than rotenone-only mice. This is the open-field report from the earlier catalogued, potentially overlapping mouse study family. The tests combine general exploration and movement and do not directly measure rest tremor, examiner-rated rigidity or clinical bradykinesia. The commercial oil’s pressing method was unreported. [Earlier source review and study identity](Food-Experiment-Flaxseed-Followup.md#flaxseed-oil-and-open-field-movement-in-mice) · [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.5923/j.ijbcs.20170603.01)

## Cold-pressed flaxseed oil with and without fish oil in therapeutic food

Jones and colleagues published April 23, 2015. The Kenyan trial randomized 60 young children with severe acute malnutrition to standard therapeutic food, the food with cold-pressed flaxseed oil, or the flaxseed-oil food plus fish oil. Erythrocyte DHA fell from baseline in the two arms without fish oil and rose in the arm with fish oil. The flaxseed-only enrichment had modest effects on EPA and the groups showed no clear differences in safety outcomes. These were nutritional rehabilitation and biomarker outcomes. The trial measured no Parkinson symptoms or motor and speech tasks. [Primary report, Methods and Figure 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4407555/) · [Local licensed text](Source-Text/PMC4407555.txt)

## Oral flaxseed oil and reported constipation in a lipid study

Vyas reported on January 30, 2018 a randomized lipid-treatment study with 120 allocated, 60 per group. Table 5 lists constipation in 2 of 57 flaxseed-oil participants and 5 of 55 liquid-paraffin participants. These are adverse-report counts in the analyzed groups, with no comparable symptom-severity measure. Liquid paraffin is an active laxative even though the paper calls it placebo. The report does not verify the oil's pressing grade. [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2018-Vyas-Flaxseed-Oil-Safety-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Original article, Methods and Table 5](https://saspublishers.com/media/articles/SJAMS_61D_223-228.pdf)

## Original flaxseed omega-3 and vitamin E registration and later UPDRS revision

The IRCT first public version dated April 7, 2016 listed total antioxidant capacity and malondialdehyde as primary outcomes, 1800 mg omega-3 plus 200 IU vitamin E versus placebo, and Naghavi clinic. It did not list UPDRS. The September 2019 public revision lists hs-CRP and neurologist-measured UPDRS, 1000 mg omega-3 plus 400 IU vitamin E, and Shahid Beheshti clinic. The 2017 accepted manuscript cites this registration and describes the later dose. The later public record cannot verify advance specification of the clinical outcome. Neither registry version supplies a separate rigidity outcome or a method of pressing the oil. [September 2019 revision](https://en.irct.ir/trial/6103?revision=103566) · [2017 accepted manuscript](https://magistralbr.caldic.com/storage/product-files/1634072890.pdf) [Original source](https://en.irct.ir/trial/6103?revision=6103) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2016-IRCT201604035623N73-Registration-History.txt)

## Official flax trial revision histories and rest-tremor reporting

The 2016 Taghizadeh registration added clinical UPDRS after publication, with changes in clinic and supplement quantities. The separate 2017 Tamtaji record changes the same kinds of fields between its first and 2019 versions, while both versions list molecular outcomes. The WHO label for Tamtaji's enrollment date differs from the source registry's expected-start label. The human papers report combined intervention scores and leave motor assessment medication state, cold pressing and isolated rest-tremor estimates unresolved.
