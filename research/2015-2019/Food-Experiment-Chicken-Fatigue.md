# Chicken essence and fatigue-related mood scales

## Chicken essence and routine energy or tiredness

Young, Benton and Carter reported 50 randomized and 46 completing ten days of chicken extract or placebo. POMS energetic/tired showed no treatment effect. The separate visual-analogue treatment main effect was inconclusive, and the reported treatment-by-time p value was below .07. A larger 2015 report from Benton and Young gives an energetic/tired numerical difference favoring essence, with reported p below .06 and an unclear printed F statistic. Cohort overlap remains unresolved. The soup study from the preceding period additionally found an inconclusive fatigue comparison. [Young et al. original](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/7/2/887) · [Larger study author copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/292375431_The_effect_of_chicken_essence_on_cognition_and_mood_A_randomized_controlled_trial) · [Earlier soup comparison](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Chicken-Fatigue.md)

## Registered chicken meat and exercise-fatigue study

UMIN disclosed trial UMIN000033042 on June 19, 2018. Its open randomized crossover planned 100 g of “salad chicken” before exercise versus no test food in 10 healthy adults aged 20–39. The primary outcome was change in serum creatine phosphokinase; secondary outcomes included a visual analogue scale, exercise duration and adverse events. The registry does not specify the scale's construct or the chicken's cooking method. It reports the study completed but results unpublished, with outcome and actual enrollment fields blank. It does not establish an effect or a Parkinson comparison. [Official registry](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/icdr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000037663) · [Institutional study list](https://www.miyazaki-u.ac.jp/kscrs/healthcare/achievement.html)

## Chicken intake and nonmotor symptoms in Parkinson disease

Sauerbier and colleagues' 2018 cross-sectional study recorded chicken intake in people with Parkinson disease using a 125 g serving. Weekly consumption was reported by 58 of 66 White, 23 of 33 Asian and 32 of 39 Black African or Caribbean participants. The study assessed a sleep/fatigue domain, but explicitly did not compare chicken or beef intake with clinical parameters because of sample size. It therefore provides no chicken-specific fatigue estimate or null comparison. [Original report, Table 5 and section 3.4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2018/7274085) · [Existing study note](Food-Experiment-Cumin-Dyskinesia.md)
