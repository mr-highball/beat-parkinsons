# Canola oil and pain, 2015–2019

## Dietary oil feasibility study registration

The WHO registry copy identifies ACTRN12619001270101, prospectively registered 12 September 2019. Adults with hand or knee osteoarthritis are assigned dietary olive oil, canola oil or fish oil. Pain VAS is secondary to feasibility, adherence and acceptability. The canola arm specifies a tablespoon daily. The 2021 P49 presentation identifies a closely matching design and describes 20 mL per day. The official record marks results available while its summary and link fields are empty. The presentation gives an inconclusive group comparison on secondary measures without providing arm-level pain estimates. Oil processing was not reported. ## Source identity of the Sunola comparator

Hill and colleagues' original knee osteoarthritis report, first available in 2015, names Sunola in the low-dose fish oil arm. The authors report a two-year WOMAC pain difference favoring that arm and compared different fish-oil doses. The lead investigator separately called the diluent canola in an interview, while Sunola can denote high-oleic sunflower oil. Oil identity cannot be resolved from those sources. The trial does not provide an isolated cold-pressed canola effect.
