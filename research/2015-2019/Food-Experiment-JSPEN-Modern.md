# Parkinson clinical nutrition in the Japanese proceedings

## Oral MCT oil in four malnourished older patients

Kitabayashi and colleagues described four older adults given one teaspoon of MCT oil per meal. One, an 89-year-old woman with Parkinson’s disease, heart failure and COPD, had MCT oil added to side dishes on day seven and moved to the staple food on day 29 amid further meal adjustments. The reported 25-day course includes intravenous nutrition ending on day 29. Across all four cases, oral energy fulfillment rose from 40% to 105% of calculated need, albumin from 2.6 to 3.0 g/dL, and prealbumin from 9.6 to 17.4 mg/dL. The report gives pooled values, no individual Parkinson symptom measure, and no oil source. [JSPEN 2019 P-11-6, printed p924](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/1/Supplement/1_802/_pdf/-char/ja#page=123)

## Black pepper and menthol patch after aspiration pneumonia

Ikeda and colleagues treated eight patients aged 74–90 with a black pepper essential-oil and menthol aroma patch for two weeks. Modified water swallowing improved in two of five patients with measurements, water swallowing in one of five and repetitive saliva swallowing in one of five. Oral intake rose in four of six taking food orally. One patient had recurrent pneumonia. The abstract does not identify a Parkinson subgroup, and the uncontrolled exposure was to a combination aroma delivered by a patch. The neighboring Parkinson case involved excess saliva and inhaled ipratropium. The program page containing both titles generated the lexical match for cracked black pepper and choking. [JSPEN 2019 P-23-10, printed p1070](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/1/Supplement/1_802/_pdf/-char/ja#page=269) · [Separate P-23-6](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/1/Supplement/1_802/_pdf/-char/ja#page=265) · [Organizer’s program](https://www.congre.co.jp/jspen2019/files/program.pdf#page=91)

## Oral intake after pneumonia in mixed parkinsonism

Tazumi and colleagues’ 2019 abstract and 2020 article describe the same 2015–2017 Okazaki cohort of 136 patients, including 83 whose admission record gave Parkinson’s disease. At discharge, 40 of those 83 were taking all three meals orally. Initial swallowing impairment and pre-admission independence predicted three-meal oral intake in the combined sample. The 2021 paper reports a separate retrospective change in referral workflow for neurologic care in 30 Parkinson patients with severe dysphagia. Three-meal oral intake at discharge was 4/19 before and 7/11 after the change (p = .027). Neurologist involvement and peak levodopa-equivalent doses also rose, and disease duration differed between groups. No ingredient or preparation was compared. The studies share a hospital and part of their observation windows, so individual overlap remains possible. The hospital separately lists a 2020 symposium abstract on clinical nutrition and medication management, page 162, whose body still needs retrieval. [JSPEN 2019 O-58-4](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/1/Supplement/1_347/_pdf/-char/ja#page=397) · [2020 cohort article](https://doi.org/10.11244/ejspen.2.1_16) · [2021 clinical workflow article](https://doi.org/10.11244/ejspen.3.5_291) · [2020 symposium record](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=202002234170788153)

## Tube formula and levodopa-carbidopa in advanced Parkinson

Takahashi and colleagues describe a woman in her seventies with advanced Parkinson’s disease, dysphagia, poor oral intake and bilateral pressure ulcers. The team used intravenous, gastric tube and oral nutrition as her condition changed, with a semisolid gastrostomy feed after starting levodopa-carbidopa intestinal gel. The 2020 article describes the same patient and attributes improved daily activities to the medication course. No independent formula effect or specific oral food was measured. [JSPEN 2019 O-38-4](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ejspen/1/Supplement/1_347/_pdf/-char/ja#page=256) · [2020 case article](https://doi.org/10.11244/ejspen.2.4_270)
