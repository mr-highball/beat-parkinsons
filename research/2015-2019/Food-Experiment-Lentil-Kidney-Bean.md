# Lentil and kidney bean evidence

## Red lentil extract in rat catalepsy

Houshmand and colleagues tested a hydroalcoholic extract of red lentil seeds in 48 male Sprague-Dawley rats randomly allocated to six groups. The extract was made from crushed red lentil seed using aqueous ethanol. Rats received one intraperitoneal pretreatment before intraperitoneal perphenazine, a dopamine-blocking drug used to provoke catalepsy. Table/Figure 1 reports lower Morpurgo posture scores with 400 mg/kg at 20 through 180 minutes and with 800 or 1,000 mg/kg at all recorded times through 240 minutes. The 200 mg/kg group did not separate from saline. At 120 minutes the mean scores for saline, 200, 400, 800 and 1,000 mg/kg were 3.50, 3.30, 2.70, 1.89 and 1.94, respectively. Bromocriptine gave 1.62. The authors describe the endpoint as catatonia or rigidity, although the bar test measures persistence of imposed posture. The experiment used injected extract in an acute drug-challenge model. It provides a preclinical lead for 0056 while leaving oral boiled lentils and clinical rigidity untested. [Primary paper, Methods and Table/Figure 1](https://doi.org/10.7860/JCDR/2016/17813.7977) · [Public full report, PMID 27504309](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4963669/)

## Purified kidney bean lectin in nematodes

Zheng and colleagues fed transgenic *Caenorhabditis elegans* bacteria with fluorescently tagged *Phaseolus vulgaris* erythroagglutinin. Table 1 and Figure 1 report reduced dopamine-transporter-linked fluorescence over the tested concentration range and fewer or smaller GFP-labeled dopaminergic profiles at the highest concentration, 0.002 mM (p < .01). These are reporter measurements from a nematode model. The discussion includes a conflicting unqualified sentence about the number and size outcomes, so the table and figure give the clearer concentration-specific record. The paper contains neither an ordinary whole-bean ingestion contrast nor a human symptom assessment. The later Anselmi rat study used an isolated lectin from *Pisum sativum*, a separate species. ## Kidney beans as objects in a Parkinson feeding simulation

The NCT02600858 registry was first posted November 9, 2015. Paul and colleagues' primary report first appeared online September 14, 2019. Twenty-three people with Parkinson's practiced a simulated feeding task on or off levodopa. They moved two raw kidney beans per spoonful from a home cup to target cups and were timed. The on-medication group retained more skill two days later. The beans were task objects. Participants did not eat them and the trial did not assess drooling or hallucinations. A 2021 report analyzes cognition and nine-day retention in the same trial. The 2024 Reed analysis linked by the registry concerns cognitive screening using the same task and contributes no culinary exposure. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02600858) · [Primary report, Methods, PMID 31526767](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7398159/) · [2021 secondary analysis](https://doi.org/10.3389/fresc.2021.754118) · [2024 secondary analysis, PMID 39044501](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39044501/)

## Legume intake by swallowing status in an Italian Parkinson cohort

Cassani and colleagues' 2017 analysis of 600 people with Parkinson's reported 48 ± 35 g/day of pooled legumes in 72 people with self-reported swallowing disturbance and 40 ± 27 g/day in 528 people without it (p = .024). Table 1 gave 41 ± 28 g/day in Parkinson participants and 43 ± 28 in 600 matched controls, marked nonsignificant. The food-frequency category pooled legume species and preparations. The study measured eating patterns in people who already reported swallowing symptoms, using a Part II UPDRS item. It cannot establish what changes swallowing or drooling after canned kidney beans.
