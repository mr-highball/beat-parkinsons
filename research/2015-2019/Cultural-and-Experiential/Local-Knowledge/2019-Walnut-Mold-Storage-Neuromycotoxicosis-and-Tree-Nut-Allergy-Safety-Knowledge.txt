Title: Practical safety and storage knowledge for walnuts: severe tree-nut anaphylaxis, walnut-pecan cross-reactivity, dysphagia texture caution, and moldy-kernel tremorgenic toxicosis
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30088215/
Source kind: Clinical toxicology and consumer safety knowledge record
Publication date: 2019
Date basis: Publication year of the human moldy-walnut neuromycotoxicosis case report (Botha et al., Mycotoxin Res 2019, PMID:30088215), cross-referenced with the NUT CRACKER clinical walnut-pecan allergy cohort (Elizur et al., 2020, PMID:31513892) and PD nut-coughing forum reports (2019, forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/20445).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Cautionary / adverse safety perspectives counterbalancing popular "brain food" promotion: documents IgE anaphylaxis, PD nut-coughing texture difficulty, and acute tremorgenic poisoning from spoiled moldy walnuts.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed clinical toxicology and allergy publications (PMID:30088215; PMID:31513892) plus archived PD community observations.
Efficacy evidence: Safety and food-handling context only; acute mycotoxin toxicity reflects fungal spoilage (Penicillium crustosum) rather than sound culinary walnuts, and allergy reflects IgE-sensitized individuals.

Community/context: Practical household storage, allergy-management, and clinical-safety knowledge surrounding culinary English walnuts (Juglans regia), balancing popular cultural claims of walnuts as a universal brain tonic.

Exposure and adverse observations: Three distinct real-world safety hazards are documented in traceable clinical and patient records: (1) Severe IgE-mediated tree-nut allergy with ~80% clinical co-allergy between walnut and pecan and markedly lower reaction thresholds (median 100 mg) in dual-allergic individuals (NUT CRACKER cohort, Elizur et al. 2020, PMID:31513892); (2) Rare acute human neuromycotoxicosis after ingesting visibly moldy, improperly stored walnuts contaminated by Penicillium crustosum producing roquefortine C (21.7 ug/g) and penitrem A (1.9 ng/g), causing acute tremors, generalized pain, incoordination, confusion, anxiety, and diaphoresis within 12 hours (Botha et al. 2019, PMID:30088215); and (3) Mechanical coughing and airway-clearance difficulty with hard, crumbly whole nuts reported by people with Parkinson disease experiencing dysphagia (archived locally in 2019-Parkinsons-UK-Nuts-Coughing-Swallowing.txt).

Evidence and limits: Emphasizes storing shelled walnuts cool/dry, discarding rancid or moldy kernels, adapting nut textures (e.g., finely ground/butter) when PD swallowing is impaired, and strictly avoiding walnuts in tree-nut-allergic individuals.
