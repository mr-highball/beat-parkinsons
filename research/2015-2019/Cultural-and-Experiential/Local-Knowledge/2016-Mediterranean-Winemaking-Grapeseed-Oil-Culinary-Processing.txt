Title: Culinary history and cold-pressed versus solvent-refined processing of winemaking-byproduct grapeseed oil

Reported direction: Contextual culinary record distinguishing high-linoleic-acid culinary grapeseed oil (cold-pressed vs solvent-refined) from aqueous grape-seed polyphenol extracts; makes no Parkinson symptom claim.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed food-science synthesis indexed in PubMed Central (Garavaglia et al. 2016, Nutr Metab Insights, PMC4988453; Di Pietro Fernandes et al. 2023, Molecules, PMC10708499).
Efficacy evidence: Culinary and food-processing context only; confirms absence of traditional neurological use or human Parkinson clinical efficacy.

Grapeseed oil is obtained from Vitis vinifera seeds left as a pomace by-product of Mediterranean and global winemaking (8-20% lipid by dry seed weight). Traditional mechanical cold-pressing yields a lower-volume culinary oil retaining lipophilic tocopherols (vitamin E), phytosterols, carotenoids, and trace phenolics without chemical solvents, whereas industrial hexane extraction followed by refining, bleaching, and deodorizing (RBD) produces a neutral high-smoke-point frying oil dominated by omega-6 linoleic acid (60-76%). Both cold-pressed and refined culinary grapeseed oils are chemically distinct from aqueous or hydroalcoholic grape-seed proanthocyanidin extracts (GSPE/GSE) sold as polyphenol capsules, and neither oil form has a traditional clinical indication for Parkinson motor or cognitive symptoms.
