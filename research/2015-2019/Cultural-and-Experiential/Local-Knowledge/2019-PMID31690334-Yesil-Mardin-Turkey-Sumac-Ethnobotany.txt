Date basis: Peer-reviewed journal publication year (Journal of Ethnobiology and Ethnomedicine, 2019; fieldwork March 2017 to March 2019).

Reported direction: High-consensus staple culinary spice and processed fruit; no Parkinson-specific therapeutic claim.
Authenticity: Field survey of 62 informants in southeastern Turkey (PMC6833206, DOI:10.1186/s13002-019-0327-y) with quantified Cultural Importance and Informant Consensus indices.
Efficacy evidence: Cultural dietary documentation only; establishes culinary familiarity rather than clinical symptom efficacy.

Yeşil et al. (Journal of Ethnobiology and Ethnomedicine, 2019; PMID:31690334; PMC6833206) interviewed 62 informants across multicultural communities in Yeşilli (Mardin, southeastern Turkey) between 2017 and 2019, documenting 74 wild edible taxa. Rhus coriaria ranked among the most culturally important edible species by Cultural Importance index, within the highest informant-consensus category for spices and processed fruits (FIC = 0.99). The survey confirms deep everyday culinary use of ground sumac in Anatolian/Mesopotamian diets while reporting no neurological or Parkinson-specific medical practice.
