Title: Monash University Low-FODMAP Dietetics Guidance on Culinary Chives (Allium schoenoprasum) as a Tolerated Onion Substitute
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://www.monashfodmap.com/blog/adding-flavour-without-adding-symptoms/
Source kind: Clinical dietetics and patient-guidance blog
Publication date: 2015-03-19
Date basis: Published blog date on Monash University FODMAP official blog (2015-03-19, verified HTTP 200), cross-checked with IrritableBowelSyndrome.net patient guidance (2022-12-21, https://irritablebowelsyndrome.net/food/onion-garlic-substitutes) distinguishing fresh/dried common chives (Allium schoenoprasum) from garlic chives (Allium tuberosum).

Monash University's FODMAP clinical dietetics team (19 March 2015) recommends culinary chives (Allium schoenoprasum) as a low-FODMAP flavor substitute for high-fructan onions and garlic so patients with irritable bowel syndrome or functional GI sensitivity can season meals without aggravating fructan-mediated abdominal pain or bloating. Complementary IBS patient guidance (IrritableBowelSyndrome.net, 2022-12-21) notes that both fresh and dried common onion chives are generally FODMAP-friendly in culinary garnish portions while explicitly distinguishing round-leaved common chives (Allium schoenoprasum) from flat-leaved Asian/garlic chives (Allium tuberosum) and emphasizing that individual tolerances vary. Public Parkinson's forums (HealthUnlocked Cure Parkinson's and Parkinson's UK) contain no standalone threads reporting Parkinson's motor or non-motor benefit or harm from fresh or dried chives.
