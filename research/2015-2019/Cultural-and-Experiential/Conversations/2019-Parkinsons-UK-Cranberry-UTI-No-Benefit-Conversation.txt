Title: Parkinson's UK Forum no-benefit report of cranberry juice and tablets for active urinary infection alongside constipation
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/tell-me-what-worked-for-you/20827
Source kind: Conversation
Publication date: 2019-09-15
Date basis: Public thread opening post dated 2019-09-15; follow-up replies inspected through 2019-09-17 (Posts #1-#7).

In a Parkinson's UK public forum thread opened September 15, 2019 (all participant handles omitted), a member with Parkinson's disease experiencing concurrent refractory constipation, severe left-sided abdominal cramping, and an active urinary tract infection explicitly reported that cranberry juice and cranberry tablets were not working ('have urinary infection, cranberry juice/tablets not working') despite hoping to self-manage without a clinic visit.
Follow-up (September 17, 2019, Posts #6-#7): Symptoms worsened sufficiently to interrupt work and require a GP appointment, prescription infection medication, urinary alkalinizer sachets, macrogol laxatives, and fresh citrus fruit before symptoms began improving.
Observation and limits: Important real-world no-benefit/failure account illustrating that cranberry products do not treat established active UTIs in Parkinson's disease and should not delay medical evaluation. Diagnosis and cranberry dose/formulation were self-reported without clinical verification.
