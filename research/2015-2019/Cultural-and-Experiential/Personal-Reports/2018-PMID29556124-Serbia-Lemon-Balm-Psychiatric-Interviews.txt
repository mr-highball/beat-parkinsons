The study interviewed 106 psychotic outpatients. Fifty-seven reported herbal product use. Table 5 records 15 lemon-balm users, including nine listing anxiety, three psychotic symptoms, two depression and one insomnia. Table 6 describes an infusion. Among the eight lemon-balm users who also reported prescribed treatment, Table 7 records five with no perceived change and three with decreased symptom intensity. It lists one nausea report. The outcomes are pooled across symptoms.

Reported direction: Mixed perceived change and one reported adverse experience during co-use.
Authenticity: Original article text and NLM full-text XML, Tables 1 and 5–7.
Efficacy evidence: Observational reports in a cohort recruited for psychotic disorders. The two reports of use for depression cannot be mapped to the pooled changes. Co-use does not establish which treatment caused a change or adverse experience.
Related research: The earlier non-psychotic outpatient cohort at the same center has distinct inclusion criteria and table counts. The cohorts should not be combined into a depression response estimate.
Open trail: No confirmed Parkinson subgroup or standardized severity trajectory is reported for lemon balm.
