Fifty-five of 136 interviewed adults reported herbal products for their symptoms. Table 6 identifies lemon balm among 26 users across anxiety, depression, combined anxiety and depression, and insomnia categories. Table 7 identifies the botanical as an infusion. Table 8 lists 11 of the 26 as co-users with prescribed treatment. Seven reported no change and three lower symptom intensity in the co-use group. The eleventh has no direction in that table. The lemon balm row lists one report each of dizziness and nausea.

Reported direction: Mixed self-reported relief and no change during co-use. The table does not link each reported change to a specific symptom.
Authenticity: Original publisher text and NLM full-text XML, Tables 1, 6, 7 and 8.
Efficacy evidence: Cross-sectional self-report with concurrent care, no assigned control group and no Parkinson-specific cohort. Neither the counts nor the reported adverse experiences establish attribution.
Related research: The later psychotic outpatient survey at the same center used a different inclusion group and different denominators. Retain its results separately.
Open trail: Ingredient-specific results for depression alone, verified timing and medication attribution are unavailable in these aggregate tables.
