Title: Iranian Traditional Medicine recommendation of hazelnut (Corylus avellana L.) for age-related memory impairment
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26808646/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2017
Date basis: Peer-reviewed Nutritional Neuroscience publication date (Bahaeddin et al., 2017); separates modern citation date from historical Iranian Traditional Medicine texts.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional recommendation for age-related forgetfulness/dementia-like memory decline; no clinical human efficacy trial in Parkinson disease.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed neuroscience paper documenting the traditional rationale (Bahaeddin et al., 2017, Nutritional Neuroscience, PMID:26808646, DOI:10.1080/1028415X.2015.1126954), cross-referenced with Talebi et al. (2024, PMID:38151890).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional medical heritage context only; historical Iranian recommendations for memory loss do not establish clinical efficacy for Parkinson disease attention, executive function, or mood.

Community and practice context: Iranian Traditional Medicine (ITM / Persian traditional medical heritage), where hazelnut kernels (Corylus avellana L., Fondoq) are categorized among brain-nourishing foods.

Exposure and reported symptom: Bahaeddin et al. (2017, Nutritional Neuroscience, PMID:26808646) explicitly note that their rodent neuroprotection investigation was initiated because Iranian traditional medicine recommends eating hazelnut kernels for older individuals suffering from a dementia-like condition characterized by memory loss (resembling modern Alzheimer-type forgetfulness). Olofinnade et al. (2021, PMID:33371863) similarly note traditional scripts attributing anti-aging brain benefits to nuts including hazelnut.

Evidence limits and distinction: Historical concepts of forgetfulness or brain weakness do not map directly to diagnosed Parkinson disease attention impairment, executive dysfunction, or motor signs. While rodent whole-kernel studies showed memory and EPM-anxiety gains in amyloid-beta and aging models, zero monotherapy human hazelnut cognitive trials exist, and human mixed-nut trials containing hazelnuts found null effects on attention and executive function.
