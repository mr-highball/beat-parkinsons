Title: Korean, Chinese, and Southern African traditional dietary and folk uses of Cucurbita moschata (butternut/winter squash)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26820672/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2016-03-01
Date basis: Publication date of Kim et al. (2016) Life Sciences documenting Korean traditional use of baked Cucurbita moschata for depression (PMID:26820672), paired with Chinese constipation formula (Li et al., 2021, PMID:32980482) and Eastern Cape ethnobotany (Maroyi, 2022, PMC8925170).
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional favorable uses for low mood (Korean baked C. moschata), constipation formulas (Chinese dried fruit blended with Aloe/herbs), and restorative nourishment, without human PD clinical confirmation.
Authenticity: Documented across three peer-reviewed ethnopharmacological and ethnobotanical publications (PMID:26820672, PMID:32980482, PMID:35292046).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional and ethnobotanical accounts only; does not establish clinical Parkinson efficacy.

Cucurbita moschata Duchesne — the botanical species encompassing butternut squash and traditional sweet winter pumpkins — is cultivated globally with multiple documented ethnomedicinal traditions:

1. Korean traditional practice: Kim et al. (2016, Life Sci, PMID:26820672) explicitly note that baked Cucurbita moschata (Sweetme Sweet Pumpkin) has been traditionally used in Korea for patients experiencing depressive symptoms, motivating their 28-day mouse forced-swim investigation.
2. Chinese functional-food tradition: Li et al. (2021, J Ethnopharmacol, PMID:32980482) document dried C. moschata fruit (25% by weight) as a traditional ingredient in the Chinese anti-constipation formula Tiantian Capsule alongside Aloe vera, xylooligosaccharides, Poria, and Tremella (making single-ingredient attribution impossible).
3. Southern African ethnobotany: Maroyi (2022, PMC8925170) documents C. moschata fruits, shoots, and seeds as traditional nourishing foods and household remedies in Eastern Cape, South Africa.

Separately, case reports document occasional irritant or allergic contact dermatitis to raw butternut squash pulp during kitchen peeling/cutting (e.g., Ravichandran et al., 2024, PMC11178144), while cooked oral ingestion is typically tolerated.
