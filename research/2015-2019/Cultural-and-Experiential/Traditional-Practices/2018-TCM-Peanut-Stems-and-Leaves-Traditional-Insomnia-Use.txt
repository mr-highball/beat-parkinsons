Title: Traditional Chinese and Asian herbal use of Arachis hypogaea stems and leaves for insomnia (plant-part distinction from seeds)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29572847/
Source kind: Traditional Practice
Publication date: 2018-09
Date basis: Peer-reviewed publication date of Deng, Shi & Wang (J Sci Food Agric, PMID:29572847; corroborated by Cossetin et al. 2020, PMID:32416109). Underlying classical TCM textual traditions are older and undated here.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Customary sedative/sleep-aid use specifically attributed to peanut stems and leaves (foliage) in classical Asian herbal medicine; does NOT concern roasted or boiled peanut kernels.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed ethnobotanical and pharmacological documentation in J Sci Food Agric (2018, PMID:29572847) and Regul Toxicol Pharmacol (2020, PMID:32416109).
Efficacy evidence: Historical herbal attribution and rodent pentobarbital/EEG sleep assays restricted to aerial stems and leaves; supplies zero human efficacy evidence for culinary roasted or boiled peanut seeds on insomnia or anxiety.

Classical Traditional Chinese Medicine and Asian ethnobotanical literature documented by Deng et al. (2018, PMID:29572847) and Cossetin et al. (2020, PMID:32416109) record aqueous decoctions and herbal preparations of Arachis hypogaea stems and leaves (peanut foliage, PSL) as customary remedies for insomnia, restlessness, and poor sleep. Phytochemical and rodent investigations attribute the preclinical sedative properties of foliage extracts to linalool and leaf-specific flavanones/chalcones (such as 5-hydroxy-4',7-dimethoxyflavanone and 2'-O-methylisoliquiritigenin) acting on GABAergic pathways. Critically for botanical boundary integrity, these traditional sleep uses apply strictly to aerial stems and leaves rather than boiled or roasted peanut seeds (kernels), and no human clinical trial demonstrates sleep improvement from peanut foliage or culinary peanut kernels (whereas the sole human peanut-butter sleep RCT, Oberther 2024, was null).
