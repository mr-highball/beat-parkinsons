Summary of Traditional & Culinary Practice:
Across Japan (shiso / oba / soyo), China (zisuye), Korea (kkaennip / so-yeop), and Vietnam (tia to), fresh and dried leaves of Perilla frutescens (Lamiaceae) have centuries of culinary and traditional medical use (Ahmed 2018, PMC6337106; Hou et al. 2022, PMC9182122). In Traditional Chinese Medicine and Japanese Kampo medicine, dried perilla leaf (Zisuye / Soyo) is classified as aromatic, pungent, and warming, traditionally used to regulate qi, ease epigastric fullness/nausea, relieve exterior cold symptoms, and soothe emotional constraint ('plum-pit qi' / throat tightness) — most notably as one of five herbs in Banxia Houpo Tang (Hange-koboku-to / Banhahubak-tang). Culinarily, fresh green and purple shiso leaves serve as sashimi garnishes, pickles (umeboshi coloring), wraps, and herbal infusions.

Boundary & Botanical Distinction:
Traditional herbalism sharply distinguishes Perilla frutescens leaf (Zisuye, rich in rosmarinic acid, luteolin, perillaldehyde, and anthocyanins) from perilla seed (Zisuzi) and pressed perilla seed oil (rich in alpha-linolenic acid omega-3 fatty acids). Modern seed-oil trials do not represent culinary shiso leaf.
