Title: Traditional Persian Medicine uses of Allium ampeloprasum (Tarreh / wild leek) and modern low-FODMAP leek-bulb vs green-leaf culinary guidance
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25608984/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2015-07-01
Date basis: Peer-reviewed publication date of Mosavat et al. (J Evid Based Complementary Altern Med 2015;20(3):191-195), supplemented by Movahedian 2006 (PMID:16579735), Zohalinezhad 2026 (PMC13620397), and Muir 2007 (PMID:17625872).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional Persian Medicine documents Allium ampeloprasum ('Tarreh') in topical antihemorrhoidal ointments ('Zemad') and dietary vascular/digestive remedies; modern Monash low-FODMAP dietetics flags white leek bulbs as high-fructan GI triggers while tolerating green leek leaves.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed traditional-medicine and food-chemistry sources indexed in PubMed/PMC (PMID:25608984, PMID:16579735, PMID:42812757, PMID:17625872).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional and dietetic-tolerance context only; topical hemorrhoid traditions do not transfer to oral culinary leek for PD pain, and FODMAP guidance addresses IBS bloating rather than PD efficacy.

Traditional Persian Medicine (TPM) texts—cited in clinical evaluations by Mosavat et al. 2015 (PMID:25608984), Zohalinezhad et al. 2026 (PMC13620397), and Movahedian et al. 2006 (PMID:16579735)—document Persian leek (Allium ampeloprasum / A. porrum, known as Tarreh) as both a culinary vegetable and an ingredient in topical antihemorrhoidal ointments ('Zemad') for anal bleeding and discomfort. Separately, modern Monash University low-FODMAP food-composition research (Muir 2007, PMID:17625872) separates high-fructan white leek bulbs (which can provoke gas or abdominal discomfort in IBS-prone individuals) from low-FODMAP green leek tops. Historical TPM descriptions and FODMAP tolerance rules provide cultural and GI-tolerance context without establishing clinical efficacy for Parkinson's motor or non-motor symptoms.
