Title: Traditional European and Mediterranean culinary cultivation of kale and contemporary adult consumer perceptions
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29557674/
Source kind: Traditional Practice
Publication date: 2019
Date basis: Publication year of Šamec et al. 2019 kale traditional-use review (PMID:29557674), supplemented by Alfawaz et al. 2022 adult consumer survey (PMC8782012).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Hardy traditional leafy brassica valued for nutrient density and dietary fiber; modern consumer surveys report mixed anecdotal digestive beliefs (constipation relief vs mild GI irritation in 17.8%).
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed food-science review (Šamec et al. 2019, Crit Rev Food Sci Nutr, PMID:29557674) and 1,200-adult cross-sectional survey (Alfawaz et al. 2022, PMC8782012).
Efficacy evidence: Cultural food-history and observational survey context only; does not establish clinical Parkinson's disease efficacy.

Non-heading kale (Brassica oleracea var. acephala / sabellica) has been cultivated for centuries across Mediterranean, Northern European, Scottish, Dutch, Portuguese (couve galega), and Balkan cuisines as a frost-tolerant winter staple prepared raw, boiled in soups, steamed, braised, or roasted (Šamec et al. 2019, PMID:29557674). Modern 'superfood' marketing often exceeds kale-specific clinical trials compared with other cruciferous vegetables. In a 2020 cross-sectional survey of 1,200 Saudi adults (Alfawaz et al. 2022, PMC8782012), awareness of kale was low overall; among respondents who had eaten kale, commonly perceived benefits included weight management and digestive/constipation support, while 17.8% self-reported mild gastrointestinal complaints such as irritation or constipation. Traditional culinary records note kale as a vitamin-K-rich leafy vegetable requiring consistent daily portions for patients taking warfarin.
