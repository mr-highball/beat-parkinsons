The European Medicines Agency Committee on Herbal Medicinal Products (EMA/HMPC/715092/2013, adopted 2015-07-07) recognizes Carum carvi L., fructus (whole/comminuted caraway fruit) solely as a traditional herbal medicinal product for the symptomatic relief of digestive disorders such as bloating and flatulence, explicitly noting that plausible traditional use rather than controlled clinical proof underpins the monograph. A 2026 Lithuanian ethnobotanical survey (Karpaviciene et al., PMID:42122882, PMC13164646) similarly recorded widespread Baltic folk use of caraway fruit infusions for mild abdominal cramps, flatulence, and digestion, while a 2025 Latvian food-supplement assessment (PMID:41373965, PMC12693992) highlighted that most digestive label claims for caraway supplements lack robust monotherapy clinical substantiation. Moroccan folk medicine also documents aqueous caraway fruit infusions as a traditional diuretic (investigated preclinically by Lahlou et al., 2007, PMID:17113735). Across these European and North African traditions, caraway is not used as a standalone remedy for Parkinson's motor symptoms, anxiety, depression, cognition, or sleep, and rare umbellifer allergy (celery-mugwort-spice syndrome, 26% caraway co-sensitization in Wuthrich & Hofer 1984) and levothyroxine-TSH interference (Naghibi 2015) are noted safety boundaries.
