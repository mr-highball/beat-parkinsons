Title: Colombian Caribbean (Hierbabuena), Iranian (Na'na'), and Anatolian Turkish Traditional Uses and Cautions for Spearmint (Mentha spicata)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6106973/
Source kind: Traditional-practice and ethnobotanical record
Publication date: 2018
Date basis: Publication year of ethnobotanical-pharmacological report (Caro et al. 2018, PMC6106973; corroborated by Mahboubi 2021, PMC7936090 and Akdogan 2007, PMID:17310494).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional accounts report favorable calming, sedative/anxiolytic, and carminative/digestive effects of brewed spearmint leaf infusions, alongside unfavorable Anatolian folk warnings of reduced male libido (anti-androgenic effect) and rare clinical contact stomatitis/allergy to carvone/spearmint flavorings.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed ethnobotanical and clinical sources (Caro et al. 2018, PMC6106973; Mahboubi 2021, PMC7936090; Akdogan et al. 2007, PMID:17310494) inspected during Round 7 review.
Efficacy evidence: Traditional calming and digestive folklore does not establish PD clinical efficacy; note that modern double-blind human RCTs of spearmint extract found NULL effects on human anxiety (Herrlinger 2018, Falcone 2019).

Cross-Cultural Traditional Perspectives:
1. Colombian Caribbean (Hierbabuena): Aqueous infusions of fresh or dried Mentha spicata leaves are widely used in folk medicine to ease nervousness, anxiety, insomnia, and stomach discomfort (ethnobotanical motivation documented in Caro et al. 2018, PMC6106973).
2. Iranian Traditional Medicine (Na'na'): Dried and fresh spearmint leaves and distillate (aragh-e-na'na) are classical carminative and antispasmodic remedies for flatulence, abdominal colic, and indigestion (Mahboubi 2021, PMC7936090).
3. Anatolian Turkey (Unfavorable/Endocrine Folk Observation): Men in southwestern Anatolia (Isparta region) traditionally reported diminished libido after frequent consumption of spearmint tea, prompting clinical endocrine investigations that confirmed significant reductions in free testosterone in women with hirsutism/PCOS (Akdogan et al. 2007, PMID:17310494; Grant 2010, PMID:19585478).

Safety & Balanced Appraisal:
While culinary spearmint tea is generally well tolerated, high-frequency brewed intake exhibits measurable anti-androgenic activity in clinical studies, and rare allergic oral contact lichenoid reactions to spearmint/carvone flavorings are documented. Traditional calming claims have not been replicated in controlled human anxiety trials (Herrlinger 2018 POMS Tension-Anxiety p=0.3411; Falcone 2019 all POMS domains null).
