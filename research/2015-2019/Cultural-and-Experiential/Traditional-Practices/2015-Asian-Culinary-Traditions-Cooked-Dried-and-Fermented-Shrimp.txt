Across East and Southeast Asian, Mediterranean, and coastal cuisines, penaeid and caridean shrimp (Litopenaeus vannamei, Penaeus monodon, Pandalus borealis) are consumed both as quick-cooked whole peeled tail meat (boiled, steamed, or grilled) and as traditional sun-dried or salt-fermented condiments rich in free glutamate and umami peptides (Hajeb & Jinap 2015, PMID:24915349; Yang et al. 2026, PMC13099502). Preparation markedly changes matrix composition: peeled cooked shrimp tail meat supplies dense, lean complete protein with minimal carotenoid content, whereas whole-shrimp pastes and industrial shell/head byproducts concentrate astaxanthin, chitin, and sodium. These culinary traditions document everyday nourishment rather than targeted treatment for Parkinson disease symptoms.
