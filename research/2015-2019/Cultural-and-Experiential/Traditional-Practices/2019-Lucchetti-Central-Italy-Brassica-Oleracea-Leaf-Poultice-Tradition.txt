Title: Central Italian rural ethnobotanical use of fresh Brassica oleracea leaves for rheumatic pain
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6364442/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2019
Date basis: Peer-reviewed Journal of Ethnobiology and Ethnomedicine publication year (PMID:30722788, PMC6364442).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional topical folk remedy (fresh cabbage leaf wraps for rheumatic pain and bruises); highlights route contrast with ingested cabbage.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed open-access ethnobotanical field survey of 120 rural informants in the Marche region of Central Italy (Lucchetti et al. 2019, PMC6364442) inspected via NCBI PMC XML during Round 4 review.
Efficacy evidence: Traditional topical poultice folk knowledge only; external leaf compresses cannot be extrapolated to ingested cooked, raw, or fermented green cabbage in Parkinson's disease.

In a field ethnobotanical survey of 120 rural informants across three coastal, hilly, and Apennine zones of the Ancona district (Marche region, Central Italy), Lucchetti et al. (2019, Journal of Ethnobiology and Ethnomedicine, PMC6364442) documented the traditional folk-medicine application of fresh Brassica oleracea L. (cavolo/verza) leaves as topical wraps to soothe rheumatic joint pain in humans and bruises in veterinary care. This traditional external poultice practice parallels modern topical knee-osteoarthritis cabbage-wrap trials (Lauche 2016) and underscores the critical route distinction from dietary ingestion of cooked, raw, or fermented green cabbage.
