Title: Sesame oil in a 2019 account of Ayurvedic oral holding for dry mouth
Collection: Cultural and experiential

Source kind: Traditional-practice review
Publication date: 2019-07
Reviewed: 2026-11-25
Date basis: Published AYUSH Cancer Conclave accepted oral papers. Abstract 5 appears on the second PDF page.
Community and context: An Ayurveda conference review discusses Gandusha, the practice of holding liquid in the mouth, as supportive care for treatment-associated dry mouth.
Exposure: Sesame oil or sesame preparations among several possible oral liquids. The review describes holding the material in the mouth. It does not establish ingestion of a cold-pressed or toasted culinary oil.
Reported symptom: Xerostomia during cancer treatment. The member does not report a separate Parkinson swallowing endpoint.
Reported direction: The review proposes possible supportive benefit based on its practice context. No oil-specific clinical improvement was measured in the conference abstract.
Authenticity: The original numbered AYUSH conference abstract documents the authors' description of the oral-holding practice.
Efficacy evidence: The conference review reports a proposed use. It contains no isolated sesame-oil effect on a Parkinson symptom.
Evidence limits: An identifiable conference record documents the practice. It gives no controlled Parkinson cohort, separately measured effect or comparative oil preparation.
Summary: The authors name sesame oil as one customary preparation for oral holding and discuss xerostomia in a cancer supportive-care setting. The original member provides cultural and clinical context. It leaves any effect of consuming culinary sesame oil on Parkinson symptoms open.
