Title: Valencian field records distinguish Spanish sage headache and fatigue preparations

Location inspected: Field methods, Table 2 records R51 and R53.83 and the species and route legend.

Exposure and reported symptom: The headache entry from Majorca describes rubbing fresh leaves of Salvia officinalis subsp. lavandulifolia on the forehead. The Albacete fatigue entry describes a topical alcoholic preparation of the aerial parts of that subspecies. The report distinguishes it from common garden sage, Salvia officinalis subsp. officinalis, and from other locally used Salvia taxa.
Reported direction: Intended relief of headache or fatigue in customary use.
Evidence and limits: The table assembles location-specific documented uses and cites its underlying sources. Its headache and fatigue entries describe application to the skin. It gives no reported individual symptom change, clinical severity comparison, Parkinson diagnosis or fall-frequency outcome. These entries supply no evidence for ingesting ordinary ground or fresh garden sage.
Summary: The two customary indications concern topical Spanish sage. The headache entry involves fresh leaves. The fatigue entry involves an alcoholic preparation. The report provides no fall-frequency comparison or observed change measured in Parkinson disease.
Authenticity: Complete original publication and labeled table checked. The two rows cite different underlying records.
Efficacy evidence: Use documentation with no measurable clinical effect.
