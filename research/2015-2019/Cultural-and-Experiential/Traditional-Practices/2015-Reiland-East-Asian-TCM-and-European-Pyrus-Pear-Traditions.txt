Title: East Asian TCM (Li) and European culinary-medicinal traditions for raw and poached/steamed Pyrus pears
Collection: Cultural and experiential

Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2015
Date basis: Peer-reviewed publication years (Reiland & Slavin, Nutr Today 2015, PMC4657810; Li et al., Food Chem Toxicol 2012, PMID:22824088); East Asian TCM culinary-medicinal pear traditions span over two millennia.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Favorable traditional respiratory-moistening, throat-soothing, cough, alcohol-recovery, and mild digestive uses; traditional caution that raw pears are cooling and may provoke loose stools or bloating in sensitive digestion, favoring poached or steamed pears.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed systematic and phytochemical literature inspected during Round 6 review (PMC4657810; PMID:22824088; PMID:23587660; PMC8073155).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional dietary and ethnobotanical context only; does not establish clinical Parkinson efficacy and strictly separates Pyrus pears from Opuntia prickly pear cactus.

Across East Asian Traditional Chinese Medicine (where Asian pears, Pyrus pyrifolia and Pyrus bretschneideri, are termed Li) and European dietary traditions (Pyrus communis), pears have served for centuries as seasonal functional fruits valued for moistening dryness, soothing dry coughs and hoarse throats, easing mild digestive sluggishness, and aiding recovery after alcohol consumption (Reiland & Slavin, 2015, PMC4657810; Li et al., 2012, PMID:22824088; Lee et al., 2013, PMID:23587660). Traditional Chinese dietary guidance classifies raw pears as cold in nature and cautions that excessive raw intake can aggravate loose stools, cramping, or bloating in people with weak digestion—a folk observation consonant with modern findings on pear excess fructose and sorbitol FODMAP malabsorption—and therefore often favors steaming or poaching pears (e.g., poached pear compotes or steamed pear broths). Cooking also denatures heat-labile Bet v 1 cross-reactive birch-pollen allergens present in raw Pyrus communis (de Jong et al., 2021, PMC8073155). Traditional practices supply no controlled Parkinson disease symptom trials and must be strictly separated from unrelated prickly pear cactus (Opuntia ficus-indica) and avocado pear.
