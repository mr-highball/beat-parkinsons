Title: Teimouri et al. — traditional practitioner reports of lemon balm use for anxiety and depression
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Ethnobotanical survey of traditional practitioners
DOI: 10.21608/ejvs.2019.12612.1077
Source: https://ejvs.journals.ekb.eg/article_35909_1aa5c47a80c5abdd7dd2c3e3a20e2bd2.pdf
Publication date: 2019
Date basis: Journal publication in 2019, with interviews conducted in 2017. The survey does not establish when the practice began.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC
Location inspected: Original journal pages 81–83, methods and Table 1.

Community/context: The Shahrekord survey interviewed 29 traditional therapists. The questionnaire elicited local plant names, parts used and indications.
Exposure and reported symptom: Table 1 lists the aerial parts of Melissa officinalis, locally named Badranjboyeh, under combined anxiety/depression use. The paper gives a 3% use frequency using its respondent-based measure. The preparation and dose are unspecified.
Reported direction: Use for relief was reported. The survey did not measure changes in anxiety or depressive symptoms.
Authenticity: The publisher's Table 1 provides the scientific name, local name and reported use together.
Efficacy evidence: The frequency counts practitioner reports rather than treated patients. No arm comparison, before-and-after score, Parkinson diagnosis or disease-specific efficacy result appears in the use record.
Related research: Later publications from a different study in Shahrekord report acute postoperative pain and anxiety after oral tea. Shared geography and species do not establish shared preparation or diagnosis.
Open trail: Establish the preparation, duration, and whether reports distinguish depressive symptoms from anxiety.
