Title: Date fruit and pain-related customary uses in a 2015 Unani review
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://applications.emro.who.int/imemrf/Hamdard_Med/Hamdard_Med_2015_58_2_71_83.pdf
Source kind: Traditional practice
Publication date: 2015
Date basis: Hamdard Medicus volume 58, issue 2, pages 71–83. Earlier Unani works are named inside the review. Their editions were outside this source check.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24, complete published article available from the WHO Eastern Mediterranean archive

The review identifies Khurma as Phoenix dactylifera. On pages 72–73 it summarizes traditional descriptions of an analgesic action, use for backache and sciatica, and a separate warning against use during headache. The section identifies the plant and fruit in its general introduction. It does not establish a particular drying state, the precise form for each indication or a person-level response. A dental account separately concerns leaf use and another concerns root use. Distinguish those exposures from eating fruit.

The descriptive review relays historical indications and cautions from classical texts. It gives no individual pain scale, comparator or diagnosis of Parkinson disease. Its later pharmacological section cites birth outcomes and distinct experimental preparations. These serve different questions from dried date fruit and Parkinson pain.

Reported direction: The review records customary analgesic indications for backache and sciatica and a separate caution during headache.
Authenticity: Complete published review in the WHO Eastern Mediterranean archive. The earlier editions are cited through that review.
Efficacy evidence: The historical uses provide preparation leads. The review does not establish an individual pain response to dried date fruit in Parkinson disease.
