Reviewed: 2026-11-20
Observed by: Selmani, Chabane and Bouguedoura collected local accounts through questionnaires and personal interviews in 17 Algerian oases.
Reported direction: Date syrup is listed among preparations used for sore pain.
Authenticity: Original field survey. Table 3 groups the syrup under other cultivars and gives no individual patient outcome, frequency or route for this indication.
Efficacy evidence: Described use. The field study supplies no controlled pain comparison, reliable diagnostic distinction, or participant-level benefit.

The study interviewed 316 people, including 131 women and 185 men. Its primary scope was to describe cultivar knowledge and uses of date palm pollen. Table 3 lists date syrup for several indications, including pain associated with a sore. That entry gives no specific cultivar, administration route, measured pain intensity or number of respondents reporting that use. The survey records differences in traditional usage across locations.

The paper also contains a laboratory study of date-palm pollen and rat reproductive endpoints. That study has a different preparation and no pain measure. Sore pain and syrup are distinct from the abdominal pain and unspecified fruit preparation mentioned by Ouafi and Bounaga, and from the frozen claim about dried dates and Parkinson pain severity.
