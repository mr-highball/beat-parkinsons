Title: Historical emergence of culinary zucchini (Cucurbita pepo) in 19th-century Milan and Mesoamerican cucurbit domestication
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4934399/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2016-06-24
Date basis: Online publication date of peer-reviewed botanical-culinary history review (Ann Bot 2016 Jun 24 / Jul 2016; PMC4934399; complemented by Mesoamerican cucurbit archaeobotany Sci Rep 2024-05-13, PMC11091142).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Historical culinary and plant-part boundary record: documents multi-millennia Mesoamerican domestication of Cucurbita pepo and the 19th-century Italian culinary selection of slender immature cylindrical zucchini (zucchino) harvested young as a tender vegetable, strictly distinguishing culinary immature fruit from mature roasted pumpkin seeds/oils used in European urological traditions and noting traditional vigilance against bitter cucurbitacin-bearing reversion fruits.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed open-access botanical-historical and archaeobotanical studies (Paris 2016, Ann Bot PMC4934399; Sci Rep 2024 PMC11091142) verified via NCBI PMC during Round 7 review.
Efficacy evidence: Culinary-history and botanical plant-part context only; prevents erroneous transfer of mature C. pepo pumpkin-seed traditions to immature culinary zucchini fruit and establishes no PD clinical efficacy.

Peer-reviewed horticultural and culinary-historical scholarship (Paris 2016, Ann Bot; PMC4934399) and Mesoamerican archaeobotanical research (Sci Rep 2024; PMC11091142) trace the domestication of Cucurbita pepo over thousands of years in the Americas, followed after the 16th-century Columbian Exchange by Italian breeding of slender, uniform cylindrical summer squashes harvested immature as zucchino/zucchini in 19th-century northern Italy (Milan). Crucially, although culinary zucchini belongs to the same botanical species (Cucurbita pepo) as oilseed pumpkin, traditional foodways and modern phytochemistry strictly separate tender immature zucchini fruit (high water, low fat, culinary vegetable) from mature lipid- and phytosterol-rich pumpkin seeds and seed oils traditionally used for urinary/prostate health in Central Europe. Traditional European and modern poison-control guidance (Le Roux 2018, PMID:29323540) also emphasizes tasting and immediately discarding any unusually bitter home-grown or cross-pollinated squash/zucchini due to heat-stable cucurbitacin GI toxins. No traditional lineage attributes Parkinson symptom modification to zucchini fruit.
