Title: Traditional Persian Medicine Nokhodab (chickpea broth) and Ayurvedic Chanaka dietary traditions for vitality and digestion
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31132760/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2019
Date basis: Peer-reviewed journal publication year of Heydarirad et al. (Complement Med Res 2019; PMID:31132760) and Nimrouzi & Zarshenas (Chin J Integr Med 2019; PMID:26597285); classical Persian (Avicenna/Makhzan-ol-advie) and Ayurvedic texts predate modern publication by centuries.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: Customary restorative food use for general debility/fatigue (Persian Nokhodab) and digestive regularity, balanced against traditional Ayurvedic cautions that improperly cooked chickpeas can feel heavy or induce flatulence.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed scholarly publications documenting Traditional Persian Medicine chickpea broth (PMID:31132760) and TPM functional constipation dietary principles (PMID:26597285), cross-linked with Persian tremor pharmacology review Iranshahy et al. 2022 (PMID:35244296).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional dietary-medicine heritage only; does not establish controlled clinical Parkinson efficacy for cooked, canned, or roasted chickpeas.

Community/context: Traditional Persian Medicine (TPM) texts including Avicenna's Canon and Makhzan-ol-advie classify slow-simmered chickpea broth (Nokhodab) as a fortifying, easily assimilated restorative dish for general weakness and fatigue (documented in Heydarirad et al., 2019, PMID:31132760) alongside TPM dietary regimens for sluggish bowel motility (Ea'teghal-e-batn; Nimrouzi & Zarshenas, 2019, PMID:26597285) and classical tremor/nervous-system materia medica listing Cicer arietinum (Iranshahy et al., 2022, PMID:35244296). In South Asian Ayurvedic dietary traditions, chickpeas (Chanaka) are similarly regarded as strength-building staples when thoroughly soaked, cooked, and spiced, while also carrying traditional cautions that poorly prepared or excessive whole chickpeas can provoke bloating and abdominal gas.
Evidence and limits: Separates publication date (2019-2022 scholarly articles) from centuries-old historical dietary traditions. Traditional restorative or digestive reputations do not substitute for randomized controlled trials in diagnosed Parkinson disease.
