Reported direction: Documents traditional tropical practices using banana peel and fruit for burn wound healing and preventing or overcoming depression, and discusses banana dopamine/L-dopa content in Parkinson's disease phytochemistry.
Authenticity: Verified via PubMed abstract (Pereira & Maraschin, Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2015; PMID 25449450).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional-practice and phytochemical review only; note that dietary dopamine from banana does not cross the blood-brain barrier and fresh banana polyphenol oxidase can degrade oral levodopa.

Pereira and Maraschin (2015, Journal of Ethnopharmacology, PMID 25449450) reviewed the botanical classification, local/traditional tropical uses, and phytochemistry of Musa spp. fruit pulp and peel. The authors note that throughout the tropics, banana peel and pulp carry local traditional uses for promoting burn wound healing and helping prevent or overcome illnesses including depression, alongside modern phytochemical interest in carotenoids, phenolics, and biogenic amines (dopamine and L-dopa). Traditional attribution and phytochemical presence do not establish clinical efficacy in Parkinson's disease; peripheral dietary dopamine does not cross the blood-brain barrier.
