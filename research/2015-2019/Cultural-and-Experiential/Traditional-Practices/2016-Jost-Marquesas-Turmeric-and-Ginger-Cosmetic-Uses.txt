Title: Marquesan turmeric and ginger in published accounts of cosmetic plant use
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13002-016-0128-5
Source kind: Customary cosmetic practices documented in an ethnobotanical interview study
Publication date: 2016-11-29
Date basis: First electronic publication of the survey. Interviews were conducted in 2014 and 2015.
Source locator: Jost and colleagues, Tables 3–4 and the discussion of plant parts and uses.
Community/context: Twenty-eight interviewed Marquesan informants described plants and cosmetic preparations. The authors compiled accounts from the islands and from Marquesans living in Tahiti.
Exposure and reported symptom: Curcuma longa appears in practices including skin hydration and perfume. Table 3 identifies cultivated Zingiber officinale separately from Zingiber zerumbet, the species named in the shampoo passage. The reports describe cosmetic uses and plant identities. The source supplies no turmeric- or culinary-ginger-specific Parkinson pain observation.
Reported direction: Unspecified for pain. Reported use terms describe cosmetic purposes.
Authenticity: Original peer-reviewed interview study with documented methods and plant tables. Recorded citations are mentions. Multiple mentions should not be counted as independent effects.
Efficacy evidence: Interview reports establish the documented practices and taxa. They provide neither measured symptom change for the two frozen preparations nor a controlled oral comparison.
Access and follow-up: The published survey is inspectable. Any oral practice specifically reporting symptoms would need its own source and preparation evidence.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24
