Bouyahya and colleagues interviewed 113 residents and herbalists across five districts of Ouezzane, northwestern Morocco. Separate laboratory screens in the paper do not test whether the customary fig preparation changes a clinical symptom.

The common fig entry identifies the local name Karmôs and describes leaf powder taken with milk. It attributes the cold and sexual-vitality uses to the survey and lists a digestive use separately from an earlier publication. The interviewed practice concerns fig leaves. The prior citation and laboratory context do not convert it into an observation on eating dried fig fruit.

Open trail: Trace the earlier digestive-use citation as its own report. This accession supplies no quantity, outcome follow-up or Parkinson visual measurement.
