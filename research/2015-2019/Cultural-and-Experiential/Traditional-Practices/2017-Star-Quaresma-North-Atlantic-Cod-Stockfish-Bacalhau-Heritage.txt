Title: North Atlantic Viking Age stockfish and Iberian bacalhau: millennium-scale dietary heritage of Atlantic cod (Gadus morhua)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5576834/

Reported direction: Traditional staple lean-protein foodway across Northern Europe and Iberia; no traditional Parkinson's disease treatment claim.

Atlantic cod (Gadus morhua) has anchored North Atlantic and Southern European Atlantic diets for over a millennium. Star et al. (PNAS 2017; PMID:28784790; PMC5576834) used ancient DNA from Viking Age cod bones excavated at Haithabu (Germany) to demonstrate long-distance transport of air-dried Arctic Norwegian cod (stockfish / torrfisk) from Lofoten across Northern Europe as early as the 9th-11th centuries, and Martinez-Garcia et al. (Proc Biol Sci 2024; PMID:39592000; PMC11597396) traced 600 years of medieval and post-medieval cod trade in Oslo.

In Portugal and Galicia, salted dried Atlantic cod (bacalhau) became a cornerstone of the Southern European Atlantic Diet, where Quaresma et al. (Front Nutr 2023; PMID:37125032; PMC10140297) verified its lean, high-protein essential amino acid signature. Historically and nutritionally, lean cod muscle fillet (baked, poached, or grilled) is a low-fat marine protein staple strictly distinct from liver-derived cod liver oil used as a folk vitamin A/D and joint supplement.
