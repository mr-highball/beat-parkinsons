Reviewed: 2026-11-21 UTC
Location inspected: PubMed bibliographic record and full structured abstract (PMID 28088492 / PMC6149223).

Community/context: Interviews with healers and local community members on the Blackfeet Indian Reservation in Western Montana concerning contemporary Pikuni-Blackfeet herbal practices for Parkinson-related and central nervous system symptoms.
Exposure and reported symptom: Field interviews documented 26 medicinal plants used for ailments including symptoms related to Parkinson disease. Aqueous extracts of 10 collected plants were assayed in vitro; water extracts of Allium sativum (garlic) cloves, Trifolium pratense flowers, and Amelanchier arborea berries protected against rotenone neurotoxicity, while Allium sativum and Amelanchier arborea extracts alleviated paraquat-induced dopaminergic cell death, and 7 of 10 tested extracts activated Nrf2 transcriptional responses in primary cortical astrocytes. Specific individual Parkinson symptoms (e.g., rest tremor vs bradykinesia) and culinary human doses were not clinically measured.
Reported direction: Traditional community use reported for Parkinson-related CNS symptoms, accompanied by in vitro cellular neuroprotection for garlic clove aqueous extract.
Evidence and limits: Combines qualitative community interviews with primary astrocyte/dopaminergic cell-culture assays. No human intervention arm, clinical Parkinson symptom rating, or in vivo behavioral trial is included.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed ethnobotanical field study with voucher plant collection and laboratory follow-up (DOI 10.1016/j.jep.2017.01.001).
Efficacy evidence: Neither traditional community attribution nor cell-culture Nrf2/toxin-protection assays establish human clinical efficacy of dietary garlic for Parkinson symptoms.
Related research: Cross-links with archived garlic/allicin preclinical Parkinson literature in research/ Source-Text/ without duplicating laboratory cohorts.
