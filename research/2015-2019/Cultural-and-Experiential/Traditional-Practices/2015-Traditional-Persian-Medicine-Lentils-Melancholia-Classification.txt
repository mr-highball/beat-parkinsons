Title: Traditional Persian Medicine humoral prohibition of lentils for melancholic depression and heavy digestion
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4587607/
Source kind: Traditional Practice
Publication date: 2015-05
Date basis: Peer-reviewed publication date of Tavakkoli-Kakhki et al. (Avicenna J Phytomed 2015, PMC4587607; companion paper 2014, PMC4110782). Underlying classical Persian medical canons (Avicenna/Rhazes/Jorjani) are medieval and undated here.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Classical unfavorable humoral framing: Traditional Persian Medicine texts classified lentils (Lens culinaris) as cold, dry, black-bile (sauda)-generating foods and prohibited them in diets for patients with melancholic depression (malikhuliya), contrasting sharply with contemporary prebiotic/gut-brain dietary models.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed synthesis of classical Iranian Traditional Medicine texts published in Avicenna J Phytomed (2015, PMID:26445711, PMC4587607).
Efficacy evidence: Historical humoral dietary doctrine without controlled clinical outcome data; does not establish worsening or easing of clinical PD depressive symptom severity.

In peer-reviewed evaluations of classical Iranian Traditional Medicine (TPM) texts (Tavakkoli-Kakhki et al., Avicenna J Phytomed 2014 PMC4110782 and 2015 PMC4587607), lentils (Lens culinaris) are listed among foodstuffs traditionally prohibited for patients with melancholic depression (malikhuliya), alongside broad beans, beef, cabbage, eggplant, onion, and garlic. Within classical humoral theory derived from Avicenna and Rhazes, lentils were categorized as cold and dry foods thought to thicken blood, slow sluggish digestion, and promote black bile (sauda), whereas warming or moistening foods (e.g., spinach, lettuce, almonds, pistachios, saffron) were prescribed. This traditional humoral prohibition stands in notable cultural contrast with modern prospective nutrition cohorts (such as the Brazilian CUME cohort linking pooled beans/lentils antioxidant capacity to lower depression incidence, PMID:36722158) and fiber-fermentation research, while supplying no modern clinical efficacy evidence.
