Title: European, North American Indigenous, and East Asian traditional separation of culinary raspberry fruit from astringent leaf and unripe berry remedies
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4717884/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2016
Date basis: Peer-reviewed open-access review publication year (Burton-Freeman et al., 2016, PMC4717884; supplemented by Arya et al., 2026, PMC12867104 and Jung et al., 2023, PMC10421075).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Contextual ethnobotanical and nutritional distinction separating ripe culinary raspberry fruit from astringent leaf infusions and Korean unripe black raspberry remedies; no traditional PD efficacy claim.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed open-access botanical and nutritional reviews inspected during Round 6 review (PMC4717884; PMC12867104; PMC10421075).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional foodway and herbalism context only; confirms botanical-part separation critical to avoiding conflation of leaf or synthetic ketone studies with whole culinary fruit.

Across European, Eastern European, and North American Indigenous ethnobotanical traditions, Rubus idaeus (European/red raspberry) and closely related Rubus strigosus and Rubus occidentalis (black raspberry) exhibit sharp functional separation by plant part and ripeness (Burton-Freeman et al., 2016, PMC4717884; Arya et al., 2026, PMC12867104). Ripe fresh or dried raspberry fruits have been valued primarily as seasonal nutrient- and fiber-dense foods (~6.5 g dietary fiber per 100 g raw berries, rich in cyanidin anthocyanins and ellagitannins) and gentle cooling syrups/cordials for fever and general vitality. By contrast, European and Indigenous herbalism brewed dried Rubus idaeus leaves and young shoots (far higher in astringent hydrolyzable tannins such as sanguiin H-6 and lambertianin C) specifically for diarrhea, sore throat/mouth ulcers, and pregnancy/labor uterine support; similarly, traditional Korean medicine used dried UNRIPE black raspberries (bokbunja) for urological and male climacteric complaints (Jung et al., 2023, PMC10421075). Separately, commercial "raspberry ketone" weight-loss supplements are overwhelmingly synthetic aromatic compounds unrelated to whole-fruit dietary exposure. No historical or traditional medical system documents ripe raw or frozen raspberries as a Parkinson disease therapy.
