Feitosa et al. (2018, Foods, PMID:30065167, PMC6111528) document traditional household preparation of common black beans (Phaseolus vulgaris L.) across Latin America, noting that Brazil is the world's largest consumer of common beans (up to 19 kg/year per capita, with black beans the second most consumed variety) and corroborating Mesoamerican/Mexican dietary heritage reviewed in Chavez-Mendoza & Sanchez (2017, Molecules, PMC6152262). Traditional household preparation involves 12-hour overnight soaking followed by boiling in a regular pot or pressure cooking (with or without discarding soaking water) to soften the matrix and reduce tannins and polyphenols by roughly 20-30%. These staple culinary practices provide nutritional context for cooked and boiled black beans—distinct from East Asian black soybeans (Glycine max) and L-DOPA-bearing Vicia faba/Mucuna pruriens—without claiming Parkinson's symptom treatment.
