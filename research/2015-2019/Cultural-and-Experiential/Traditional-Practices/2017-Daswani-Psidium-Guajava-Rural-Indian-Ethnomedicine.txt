Title: Rural Indian Traditional Medicine and Food-as-Medicine Uses of Psidium guajava (Guava) Leaves, Bark, and Fruit
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5628524/

Publication date: 2017
Date basis: Publication year of peer-reviewed Pharmacognosy Reviews synthesis (PMID:28989253, PMC5628524).

Reported direction: Traditional leaf/bark remedies for diarrhea, cough, mouth ulcers, rheumatism, and hypertension; ripe fruit valued nutritionally ('poor man's apple of the tropics') and fruit powder with buttermilk used traditionally for diabetes; no traditional Parkinson motor indication.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed open-access review (Daswani PG, Gholkar MS, Birdi TJ, Pharmacogn Rev 2017;11(22):167-174; PMID:28989253, PMC5628524) verified from full-text XML during Round 4 review.
Efficacy evidence: Ethnobotanical and traditional-use synthesis only; explicitly distinguishes leaf/bark decoctions from eating raw guava fruit and does not establish clinical efficacy for Parkinson's symptoms.

Peer-reviewed ethnomedical synthesis of Psidium guajava L. across rural India (Maharashtra, Assam, North Sikkim, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu) documents clear plant-part specialization. Leaves, young shoots, and stem bark—prepared mainly as oral decoctions, chewed tender shoots, or pounded poultices—are the primary traditional medicinal parts used for acute diarrhea and dysentery, sore throat and cough, mouth ulcers and toothache, rheumatism, and folk hypertension management. By contrast, raw ripe guava fruit is widely eaten as an affordable vitamin C-, fiber-, and mineral-rich staple ('poor man's apple of the tropics') for preventing nutritional deficiencies and anemia, while powdered fruit with buttermilk is cited in Andhra Pradesh for diabetes. No traditional Indian account documents raw guava fruit treating Parkinson's disease motor or non-motor symptoms, and traditional antidiarrheal leaf/unripe-fruit astringency differs from ripe-fruit culinary consumption.
