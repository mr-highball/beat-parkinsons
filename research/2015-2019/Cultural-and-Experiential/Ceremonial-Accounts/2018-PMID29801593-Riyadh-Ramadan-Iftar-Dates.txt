Title: Riyadh focus-group accounts of date fruit at Ramadan iftar
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Ceremonial account
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7386449/
PMCID: PMC7386449
PMID: 29801593
DOI: 10.1016/j.jegh.2018.04.004
Publication date: 2018-05-03
Date basis: First electronic publication of the journal article. The study reports discussions during Ramadan in 2015.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20
Observed by: Study participants described their practice in focus groups reported by the investigators.
Reported direction: Ceremonial use with variation in reported amounts. No date-specific symptom direction is recorded.
Authenticity: Original qualitative study with two focus groups and 15 participants with diabetes in Riyadh.
Efficacy evidence: Self-reported custom, without measured date-specific symptoms, an exposure comparison or a Parkinson cohort.

The authors report consensus among participants that breaking the fast with dates follows an Islamic tradition. Some participants described limiting their intake to up to three dates. Others said they did not count the fruit. These are descriptions of how these participants approached iftar, without a common assigned dose or verified cultivar, ripeness or preparation.

The paper also reports concerns about meal timing, glucose management and dietary variation. It does not connect those observations to date-attributable changes in pain, apathy or Parkinson symptoms. The accounts provide context for why a fasting study could contain dietary differences even when its clinical report records medication schedules rather than food-level exposures.
