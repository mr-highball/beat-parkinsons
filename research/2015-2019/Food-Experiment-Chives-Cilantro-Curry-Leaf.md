## Extract made from dried cilantro leaves in mice

Latha and colleagues assigned six mice each to saline, diazepam, or three doses of an aqueous cilantro-leaf extract. The author abstract on S47 says oral feeding. The experimental procedure on S48 says the extract and controls were injected, without an injection site. S49 discusses an intraperitoneal route for a prior seed study. That passage does not resolve the current leaf experiment. The abstract describes boiling and evaporation, while the body describes maceration, warming and vacuum drying. Table 1 gives 5.83 ± 0.54 open-arm entries for saline and 19.50 ± 0.62, 25.00 ± 0.37, and 29.17 ± 0.75 for the three extract groups. The lowest extract group is marked p < .01, while the other two are marked p < .001. The abstract describes all comparisons as p < .001. The tested material was an extract and the outcome was mouse maze behavior. Actual administration route, intact dried-leaf intake and clinical Parkinson anxiety remain unresolved. ## Chive leaf extract after cerebral ischemia in mice

Singh, Krishan and Shri reported oral *Allium schoenoprasum* leaf extract after cerebral ischemia and reperfusion. The authors reported improvement in Morris water maze and composite neurological severity scores after seven days. The abstract does not give a walking-speed, stride or executive function comparison. The model concerns cerebral ischemia and the exposure was an extract. [Primary report, first published June 22, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1515/jbcpp-2017-0070) · [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29933243/)

## Coriander seed extract and induced orofacial movements

The 2015 study gave an ethanolic coriander seed extract orally for 15 days in tacrine-treated rats, with five animals per group. It reports fewer induced vacuous chewing, tongue protrusions and orofacial bursts. These are excess movement outcomes after a seed extract. The study did not measure masked expression, dried cilantro leaves or patients with Parkinson disease. ## Cilantro leaf and stalk extract and motor behavior in rat offspring

The August 10, 2019 report measured spontaneous locomotion in rat offspring after maternal treatment. Figure 3A gives 20.47 ± 2.731 m traveled for methylmercury plus extract and 14.35 ± 2.948 m for methylmercury alone (p < .05). The extract-only group and control did not differ significantly. The extract came from freeze-dried leaves and stalks. The exposure was maternal, and the outcome was exploration over a fixed period. Clinical walking speed and intake of dried culinary leaves remain unmeasured.
