# Nigella seed and anise seed evidence

## Anise powder and epigastric pain in functional dyspepsia

The January 2015 report from the same 107-person Ghoshegir trial published symptom-level results. At week four, 34 of 47 patients assigned anise reported no or rare epigastric pain, compared with 10 of 60 assigned cornstarch (four-category comparison p = .0001). Table 1 reports week-four mean epigastric pain severity 0.38 ± 0.70 with anise and 2.05 ± 1.18 with cornstarch (p=.0001). The site and cause of the pain differ from the Parkinson hypothesis. The companion paper first appeared in December 2014 and measured SF-36 domains. [2015 report, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4354059/) · [2014 companion](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Nigella-Anise.md#anise-powder-and-vitality-in-functional-dyspepsia)

## Anise oil capsules in irritable bowel syndrome

The February 2015 registry records 120 adults assigned anise oil, peppermint oil or placebo. The 2016 gastrointestinal report lists a visual analog symptom scale and quality of life. The separate depression paper from the same investigators used the same sample size, site and treatment arms. That paper reports a lower total depression score at weeks four and six. These records describe a likely shared trial family. The relevant pain outcome was abdominal and the preparation was enteric-coated oil. [Registry snapshot](Source-Text/Trials/NCT02364830.txt) · [Gastrointestinal report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27815079/) · [Depression report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26873392/)

## Anise aqueous extract and habituation in healthy rats

Gamberini and colleagues tested a freeze-dried aqueous seed extract. A middle exposure delayed the start of exploration without changing overall activity, while the highest maintained peripheral movement and rearing in the open field. Elevated plus-maze findings were inconclusive. The work concerns rodent exploratory habituation and does not estimate executive function, attention, fatigue, sleep or falls after ground anise. [Original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25839118/)

## Nigella oil and drug-induced movement signs in rats

The 2016 study reported lower extrapyramidal behavior and oral dyskinesia during combined haloperidol and nigella oil exposure than with haloperidol alone. The full outcome tables remain an access lead. A 2015 Society for Neuroscience abstract lists the same study topic and may report the same cohort. This is drug-induced parkinsonism and oil rather than ground seed. [Original article abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27752803/) · [2015 society program, abstract 678.23](https://neuronline.sfn.org/-/media/SfN/Documents/Annual-Meeting/FinalProgram/NS2015/Daily-Books-2015/AM15Wednesday.pdf)

## Early reports on nigella oil in an MPTP mouse model

Folarin and colleagues’ 2019 preprint reports motor and histology measures in mice pretreated with nigella oil. Its narrative reports 32 mice and its four group table lists 12 in each group, leaving the sample accounting unclear. The contemporaneous meeting report concerns the same model and appears in the later preprint’s citations. The 2021 version retains the same ethics approval identifier. [2019 preprint](https://dspace.ughe.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/71ccf354-a73b-4b50-9731-d277bf45919a/content) · [2019 meeting report](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ibror.2019.09.025) · [Later version](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Nigella-Anise.md#nigella-oil-and-movement-in-an-mptp-preprint-family)

## Nigella oil mouth rinse and diet consistency during chemotherapy

Fifty-four patients with acute myeloid leukemia were randomized to nigella oil mouth rinse or the clinic’s comparison mouthwash. The report describes better tolerance of normal food in the oil arm during chemotherapy. Swallowing was scored by ability to consume food and liquid during mucositis, without an instrumental swallowing measure or separate drooling endpoint. This topical oral comparison does not establish an effect of ground seed on Parkinson dysphagia. [Licensed article text](Source-Text/PMC6875195.txt)

## Anise oil and reported tiredness in the IBS trial

Mosaffa-Jahromi and colleagues randomized 120 adults with IBS to enteric-coated anise oil, placebo or peppermint oil. The original Table 2 reports tiredness on a 0–10 scale (n = 39, 39, 38). Anise oil mean scores were 5.77 at baseline, 3.15 at four weeks and 2.74 at follow-up. Placebo was 5.25, 3.94 and 3.54. Peppermint oil was 5.50, 4.24 and 4.03. The trial table reports p < .001 for the three-group contrasts after baseline, with no independently verified adjusted pairwise contrast. The oil form and population differ from eating ground anise seed in Parkinson disease. A 2023 secondary review says tiredness did not improve. Its wording conflicts with the original symptom table and abstract. Conflicting review: https://doi.org/10.3390/nu15184070. [Local factual text](Source-Text/2016-Mosaffa-Jahromi-Anise-Oil-Tiredness.txt) · [Primary source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jep.2016.10.083)

## Nigella seed extract and exhaustion time in rats

The original Figure 1 reports a 24.6% longer time to exhaustion after oral concentrated Nigella seed extract versus water in the 2017 rat experiment, with ten animals per group. Its statistical description calls the test paired despite separate group labels. The extraction, animal population, experimental exercise exposure and time to exhaustion differ from ground seed and reported fatigue severity in Parkinson disease. The original result supports an extract performance lead with uncertain transfer to subjective fatigue. [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2017-Nigella-Swim-Exhaustion-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Original animal study and Figure 1](https://www.spandidos-publications.com/10.3892/br.2017.866)

## Isolated thymoquinone and chlorpromazine motor outcomes

The March 2016 original abstract describes four rat groups of eight and reports less catalepsy with oral isolated thymoquinone than with chlorpromazine alone. Coordination and spontaneous movement were also reported as improved. The accessible abstract gives no numerical trajectory for clinical bradykinesia or ground seed intake. Drug-induced motor responses and isolated compound remain an indirect comparator. [original author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27180446/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2016-Thymoquinone-Chlorpromazine-Motor-Abstract-Extract.txt)

## Nigella derived melanin and reflux registry outcomes

First posted October 27, 2015. The public registry identifies Melanole as a Nigella sativa extract. The planned capsules supplied a purified herbal-derived preparation during a reflux comparison against placebo. Outcomes include GERD-Q, pH-defined reflux episodes and treatment-emergent reports. The protocol supplies no Parkinson sample, assigned ground seed or measured dysphagia endpoint. Its official status is Unknown and no results are posted. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02587910) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2015-NCT02587910-Nigella-Melanole-Reflux.txt)

## Nigella mouth rinse and normal food counts

Hussain and colleagues randomized 54 patients receiving chemotherapy for acute myeloid leukemia. Two control-arm participants died in the first week and 52 were analyzed. Table 2 shows normal food in 27/27 nigella-oil versus 20/25 control at day 4, 27/27 versus 15/25 at day 12 and 27/27 versus 18/25 at day 18. The reported diet-category differences had p=.02, p<.001 and p=.004, respectively. At day 28 the counts were 26/27 versus 20/25, with one oil-arm participant on a liquid diet and p=.159 across categories. The table qualifies the broad statement in the abstract that all oil-arm participants could eat normal food throughout. The trial was open label and used a topical oil rinse during mucositis. Diet texture was self-reported. The tested route, mechanism and population differ from eating ground nigella seed for Parkinson dysphagia. [Original source](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2019/3619357) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2019-Hussain-Nigella-Mouth-Rinse-Table2.txt)

## Peanut oil in the anise trial depression comparator

The February 11, 2016 irritable bowel syndrome report compared anise oil, peppermint oil and placebo. The oral placebo formulation used peanut oil with oleogel excipients. Its mean Beck Depression Inventory-II scores were 11.02 (SD 6.74, n=40) at baseline, 10.20 (SD 6.62, n=39) at week four and 10.11 (SD 6.79, n=37) at follow-up. These are descriptive control-arm changes. The relationship between the reported group p-values and the displayed dispersion remains unverified. The study provides no separate plain-peanut-oil arm, refined grade or Parkinson population. The related P248 conference abstract reports a different dosing frequency for a gastrointestinal outcome from the same registry, so report-specific descriptions are retained. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5871202/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2016-Mosaffa-Jahromi-Peanut-Oil-Comparator.txt)
