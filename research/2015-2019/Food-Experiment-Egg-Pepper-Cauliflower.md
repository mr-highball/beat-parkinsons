# Egg, bell pepper and cauliflower studies, 2015–2019

## Egg recipe trial registration and early results

The Bournemouth trial NCT02777918 was first posted on May 19, 2016 according to the registry's estimated posting field. Results were first posted July 29, 2019. One hundred community-dwelling older adults were randomized to receive egg recipes plus shared nutrition advice or the shared advice alone. The registry's 12-week change in the Short Physical Performance Battery was 0.6 (SD 2.6) in the recipe group and −0.2 (SD 2.1) in the comparison group. It reports a composite rather than a separate walking-speed value. The adverse-event fields do not include falls or hallucinations. The later paper confirms that the intervention offered multiple recipes and that the food questionnaire pooled some preparations. ## Paprika xanthophyll and postexercise fatigue

Ichihara and colleagues randomized 14 athletes to a drink containing red paprika xanthophylls or a placebo drink for four weeks. Subjective fatigue immediately after treadmill exercise declined more in the paprika arm (between-group change p < .05 in Table 2). This is an extract drink and a short-term response to exercise in athletes. Raw sweet pepper and Parkinson fatigue were outside the trial. Several investigators disclosed employment with the manufacturer. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.7600/jpfsm.7.247) · [Original Table 2](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpfsm/7/4/7_247/_pdf/-char/en#page=4)

## Paprika xanthophyll study with preregistered exercise fatigue

UMIN000028271 was first publicly disclosed on July 28, 2017. It specifies fatigue VAS as secondary in an eight-week paprika carotenoid placebo comparison, with 21 participants. Its linked 2020 publication reports respiratory metabolism for 11 active and 10 placebo participants and gives no fatigue estimate in the accessible report. It is distinct from the shorter 2018 athlete intervention.
