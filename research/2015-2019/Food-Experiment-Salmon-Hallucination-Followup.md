# Salmon, protein exposure and hallucination reporting

## Salmon preparations in two Parkinson diet menus

The Phillips eight-week diet trial randomized 47 people with Parkinson disease, with 44 starting and 38 completing. The low-fat plan shows a canned-salmon salad on printed page 64. The ketogenic plan shows a salmon fillet cooked in a pan and served with tarragon-dill cream sauce on printed page 81. The recipes do not specify grilled salmon. The trial gives no consumed-food diary at participant level. One person assigned to the ketogenic arm developed visual hallucinations that resolved after pramipexole reduction. No event timing by meal, individual salmon intake or salmon-specific visual hallucination frequency is reported. ## Protein-related motor fluctuations and charted hallucinations

Virmani and colleagues reviewed records of 1,037 people with idiopathic Parkinson disease, of whom 877 took levodopa. Fifty-two met the study's criteria for protein-associated motor fluctuations based on reported responses to high-protein meals. The methods list meat, eggs and dairy as predominant food groups. Among 421 levodopa users with motor fluctuations, 52 had reported protein-associated fluctuations. In this subgroup, any hallucinations documented in the clinical history were less common among people reporting a protein interaction. It was a retrospective group association. The study did not link hallucinations to a specific meal, establish visual modality or count episodes. The study gives no exposure estimate for grilled salmon.
