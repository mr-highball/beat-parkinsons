# Chives, related Allium species and dystonia

## 2017 — Fresh and steamed Chinese chive extracts in cultured cells

Gao and colleagues compared raw and steamed Chinese chives (*Allium tuberosum*). Their reported endpoints include volatile chemistry and extract cytotoxicity in cultured rat liver and kidney cells. The public primary abstract does not explicitly identify the harvested organ. The outcome concerns cell viability without any motor measure. A 2018 conference abstract repeats the same species, cooking preparation and cytotoxicity work. The journal article first appeared online in February 2017. [Primary abstract and publication record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28216165/) · [Conference abstract 52](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5954276/)

## 2017 — Chinese chive seed extract and isolated smooth muscle

Tang and colleagues prepared solvent fractions from authenticated *Allium tuberosum* seeds. Figure 1 shows the butanol fraction at 200 micrograms per millilitre relaxed isolated rat corpus cavernosum smooth muscle by 68.9%. A separate repeated oral exposure assessed sexual behavior and reproductive variables in male Wistar rats. The fraction came from seeds and the contraction test was penile smooth muscle. Both features limit its relevance to dietary fresh shoots and dystonia. [Original local text, Methods and Figure 1](Source-Text/PMC5709831.txt) · [Original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5709831/)

## 2019 — Dried red onion extract and learning in toxin-lesioned rats

Salami and colleagues prepared a 70% ethanol extract of washed, cut, dried and ground red onion (*Allium cepa*). The article describes the material as red onion without explicitly naming the organ. Male rats received the extract orally at 50, 100 or 200 mg/kg/day for 14 days before and seven days after a 6-OHDA lesion. The 100 and 200 mg/kg groups performed better on the water-maze acquisition task than the lesioned group. The behavioral methods cover acquisition and memory, with biochemical and histological endpoints in addition. Dystonia was unmeasured. The article went online December 21, 2019, before its 2020 issue. [First publication record](https://medicaljournal.gazi.edu.tr/index.php/GMJ/article/view/1951) · [Original report, Methods and Results](https://gazimedj.com/pdf/32e1d65a-7ef3-4d28-858e-7da63d316173/articles/gmj.2020.07/gmj-31-25.pdf)
