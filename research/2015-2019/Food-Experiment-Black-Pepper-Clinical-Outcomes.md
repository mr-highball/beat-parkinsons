# Black pepper and clinical outcomes

Review completed on 2026-11-14 (US Eastern).

## Inhaled black pepper essential oil and immediate pain

Costa and colleagues reported this study in the 2016 volume 2 issue 2 of the journal. Fifty-four adults with heterogeneous pain were allocated by lot to inhale *Piper nigrum* essential oil (n=25) or sesame oil (n=29) for 15 minutes. Identical coded vials concealed group codes. The article provides no check that the scent was masked. Median 0–10 pain scores before inhalation were 6 and 7 (p=.930). Immediately after inhalation, medians were 3 and 6 (Mann–Whitney U=117.5, p<.001). These are between-group contrasts at each timepoint. The diagnosis and treatment setting did not identify a PD subgroup, and the essential oil was delivered by inhalation.
