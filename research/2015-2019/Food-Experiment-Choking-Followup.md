# Food experiment choking evidence

## Registration for a five-consistency swallowing study

The ClinicalTrials.gov record first posted June 20, 2017. The study compared bolus viscosity and swallowing in people with Parkinson’s or amyotrophic lateral sclerosis using videofluoroscopy. The registry lists potato-starch allergy among several exclusion criteria. The posted results identify xanthan gum as the thickener used for the tested liquids. [Primary registration and history](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03192358?tab=history) · [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT03192358.txt) · [2022 results](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Choking-Followup.md#xanthan-thickened-test-liquids-in-parkinson-videofluoroscopy)

## Water, buttered bread and wheat biscuit in the Hamburg endoscopy cohort

Pflug and colleagues’ journal report first appeared August 21, 2017 and examined 119 people with Parkinson’s and 32 controls using endoscopy. The public abstract describes aspiration in 30 of 119 patients and says it was always related to water. The same cohort appears in coauthor Moritz Bihler’s 2017 Hamburg dissertation. The original OPUS record was created July 20, 2017 and its former `/volltexte/2017/8612/` address redirects to the current repository. The current repository metadata records availability October 19, 2020. [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00455-017-9831-1) · [Existing journal discussion](Nonmotor-and-Daily-Life.md#2017--endoscopic-testing-detected-aspiration-in-early-parkinsons) · [Original repository route](https://ediss.sub.uni-hamburg.de/volltexte/2017/8612/) · [Repository date metadata](https://ediss.sub.uni-hamburg.de/handle/ediss/7275?mode=full)

The dissertation names half a slice of soft-crust mixed wheat bread with 5 g of butter and a 20 g sugar-topped wheat biscuit. Its Tables 20–22 give Penetration–Aspiration Scale scores of 6–8 in 30 of 119 Parkinson participants on water, 3 of 119 on the buttered bread and 3 of 117 on the biscuit. For each solid, all three ratings were PAS 8, silent aspiration. For water, 24 were PAS 8. No controls had aspiration on these tests. The dissertation reports that participants whose highest score was uniquely on a solid had a maximum of 2 or 4, which is consistent with water also contributing to the worst scores in those who aspirated. The per-bolus counts can overlap within participants and should not be added. The study did not test potatoes. [Dissertation, test materials, printed pp. 17–19](https://ediss.sub.uni-hamburg.de/bitstream/ediss/7275/1/Dissertation.pdf#page=22) · [Dissertation, §4.3.3 and Tables 20–22, printed pp. 31–33](https://ediss.sub.uni-hamburg.de/bitstream/ediss/7275/1/Dissertation.pdf#page=36)

## Baked potato flesh eaten in a North Carolina dysphagia inspection

A November 2017 state inspection describes a meal for a resident with dysphagia and previous aspiration. The menu for the ordered texture specified peeled baked potato with gravy. The surveyor observed a buttered baked potato served with its peel intact and without gravy. The resident ate about half the potato, ate none of its peel, and showed no difficulty during the observed mixed meal. The diagnoses listed for this resident do not include Parkinson’s. The report records a single observed meal without a comparative or instrumental swallowing assessment. [Primary North Carolina report WH5L11, D310, pp. 2–5](https://info.ncdhhs.gov/dhsr/acls/star/docs/2017/20171211-955352.pdf#page=2)

## Mashed potato and other soft foods in a Parkinson swallowing questionnaire

Noble, Jones and Miller’s December 2015 study compared questionnaire answers from 119 people with Parkinson’s with age- and sex-matched controls and also used a timed water swallow test. Table 2 asks about having to be careful when eating soft foods, giving mashed potato and scrambled egg as examples. Both groups had a median of 0 on the soft-food item. The Parkinson group’s total score range for that item was 0–2, compared with 0–1 in controls, and the paper reports p < 0.001 for the group comparison. The item covers soft foods collectively, and the timed test used water. The article gives no individual potato exposure or outcome. [Primary accepted manuscript, questionnaire and Table 2](https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/files/2566131/Patient_Reported_Changes_to_Swallowing_accepted.pdf) · [Published article](https://doi.org/10.12968/ijtr.2015.22.12.573)

## Food-related choking in US death certificates

Wu and colleagues examined US multiple-cause mortality data for people aged 65 and older in 2009–2013. Table 3 reports 273 certificates with both Parkinson’s disease and food-related choking compared with 97 expected under the study’s cause-of-death distribution, an observed-to-expected ratio of 2.82 (95% CI 2.49–3.17). The outcome code combines food-related obstructions, without ingredient or preparation. A preceding Kramarow analysis drew on overlapping years and also found an association. Neither study estimates how baked potato affects dysphagia. [Wu et al., 2015, Table 3](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/bmjopen/5/11/e009464.full.pdf) · [Earlier Kramarow analysis](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Choking-Followup.md#food-suffocation-and-parkinsons-in-national-vital-statistics)

## Early suction-device case report with Parkinson’s disease

Saperstein and colleagues’ 2018 ten-case series describes a 70-year-old man with Parkinson’s disease in Wales whose obstruction was cleared and whose breathing resumed. Case 3 identifies the removed material only as a food bolus. A systematic review links this case to the same team’s 2017 preliminary report, so count the reports as one observation. The food preparation and a comparison of swallowing difficulty are missing. [Primary full report, case 3](https://www.ijocs.org/clinical-journal/successful-use-of-a-novel-device-called-the-lifevac-ton-resuscitate-choking-victims-worldwide-results-12594.html) · [2017 conference program, P1997](https://webfiles.gi.org/docs/about/ACG2017_Final_Program.pdf) · [Overlap assessment, 2020 systematic review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339541654_A_systematic_review_on_the_effectiveness_of_anti-choking_suction_devices_and_identification_of_research_gaps) · [Later LifeVac case series](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Choking-Followup.md#potato-and-parkinsons-disease-in-a-suction-device-series)

## Caregiver account of dry and moistened baked potato

In a May 2019 discussion, a caregiver described their father developing Parkinson’s and needing softer foods. They reported that he choked when baked potato was dry and had no difficulty with the potato moistened with butter and broth. The direction of the observation favors moistened over dry baked potato for this individual. The account gives no clinical diagnosis record, standardized swallowing outcome, number of exposures, or independent confirmation. Both preparations contain baked potato, and the account describes choking rather than a graded change in dysphagia. [Firsthand caregiver post, comment by TNtechie](https://www.agingcare.com/questions/doing-more-harm-than-good-food-related-449251.htm?orderby=recent#1028342) · [Later dietitian description of a moistened preparation](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Choking-Followup.md#baked-potato-preparation-in-parkinsons-recipe-guidance)

## Maria biscuit and juice boluses in a Brazilian Parkinson cohort

Cynthia Tagiane Costa Beserra’s UFPE thesis was approved August 30, 2016 and released in the institutional repository on July 20, 2018. This cross-sectional study assessed 42 people with Parkinson’s. It used existing swallowing examinations when available and videofluoroscopy for other participants. The fluoroscopy protocol used a Maria biscuit soaked in barium sulfate as the solid, boxed juice mixed with barium as the liquid, and the same juice–barium mixture with thickener for the thick bolus. Annex F lists 5 mL and 10 mL options for the liquid and thick textures, and fields for penetration and aspiration. [Institutional issue and availability dates](https://repositorio.ufpe.br/handle/123456789/25156?mode=full) · [Primary thesis, methods on printed p. 27 and Annex F on p. 57](https://repositorio.ufpe.br/bitstream/123456789/25156/1/DISSERTA%c3%87%c3%83O%20Cynthia%20Tagiane%20Beserra.pdf#page=28)

The Results chapter reports 25 participants with normal or functional swallowing and 17 with dysphagia. Mean SWAL-QOL was 73.88 and 65.89, respectively (p = 0.06). Table 3 reports p = 0.03 for symptom frequency and p = 0.06 for fatigue. The results compare dysphagia groups and give no penetration, aspiration or severity result for the biscuit or juice separately. The protocol names no potato bolus. [Primary thesis, Figure 1 and Table 3, printed pp. 29–30](https://repositorio.ufpe.br/bitstream/123456789/25156/1/DISSERTA%c3%87%c3%83O%20Cynthia%20Tagiane%20Beserra.pdf#page=30)

## Videofluoroscopy protocol in a Brazilian electrical-stimulation trial

Glaucia Santana Trindade’s FAMERP thesis dates its examination June 25, 2019. The institution’s Health Sciences program requires a public master’s defense. The thesis entered the public repository on July 22, 2021. Fifteen people with Parkinson’s and dysphagia were assigned to conventional swallowing therapy (four) or the same therapy plus submental neuromuscular electrical stimulation (11). The study used videofluoroscopy with barium-modified foods during medication ON periods. The assessment form in Annex 5 specifies paste, solid and liquid with separate fields for signs such as coughing, choking and residue. It gives no recipe, named ingredient or completed patient-level forms. [FAMERP issue and accession dates](https://bdtd.famerp.br/handle/tede/639?mode=full) · [Public defense rules, PDF p. 3](https://www.famerp.br/images/documentos/pos-graduacao/normas_julgamentos.pdf#page=3) · [Primary thesis, §2.2.2 and Annex 5](https://bdtd.famerp.br/bitstream/tede/639/2/GlauciaSantanaTrindade_Dissert.pdf#page=60)

Table 2 compares before and after within each arm. FOIS p values were 0.371 for conventional therapy and 0.022 for combined therapy. DOSS p values were 0.593 and 0.022, respectively. The author reports a one-point FOIS rise in most cases and a one-point DOSS drop, which signals more severe dysphagia under the scoring scale defined in Annex 6. Follow-up intervals varied. The table supplies no individual food outcomes or comparison of between-group changes. The journal report reproduces the same cohort and results. [Primary thesis, Table 2 on printed p. 22 and scale in Annex 6](https://bdtd.famerp.br/bitstream/tede/639/2/GlauciaSantanaTrindade_Dissert.pdf#page=37) · [Later journal report](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Choking-Followup.md#journal-report-of-the-brazilian-electrical-stimulation-trial)

## Applesauce and water in hereditary X-linked dystonia-parkinsonism

Song and colleagues published this study online March 19, 2019. They recruited 34 men with genetically confirmed X-linked dystonia-parkinsonism and communication or swallowing difficulties. In clinical testing and endoscopic swallowing assessment, they administered sips of water and puréed applesauce. The endoscopic procedure used blue dye and a water wash. Investigators omitted solid food to limit the risk of aspiration. Table II reports 22 of 32 with penetration and six of 32 with aspiration, with outcomes pooled across boluses. The cohort has a hereditary dystonia-parkinsonism diagnosis, and the report supplies no outcome specific to applesauce or baked potato. The issue was dated January 2020. [Publisher abstract and online date](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/lary.27897) · [Primary article copy, Methods and Table II](https://www.academia.edu/123522127/Voice_and_swallowing_dysfunction_in_X_linked_dystonia_parkinsonism)

## Timed water swallowing against endoscopy in Parkinson’s disease

Pflug and colleagues published a controlled cross-sectional study online August 29, 2019, in which 115 Parkinson outpatients and 32 controls drank 90 mL water. Mean swallowing speed was 6.5 mL/s in Parkinson’s and 8.5 mL/s in controls (p < .01). For endoscopically confirmed water aspiration, a cutoff below 10 mL/s had sensitivity of 88% and specificity of 19%. The sample-derived cutoff of 5.5 mL/s gave 69% sensitivity and 64% specificity (AUC .72). The authors judged timed swallowing speed unsuitable as a predictor of aspiration. This study tested swallowing speed against endoscopy. The later Tanzanian study used a combination of clinical signs and cough peak flow. [Primary article, PMID 31464354](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31464354/) · [Tanzanian pilot](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Choking-Followup.md#water-swallow-screening-in-northern-tanzania)

## Baked potato and mashed meals in UK caregiver accounts

On March 10, 2016, a caregiver described a baked potato with butter and salad among several meals eaten before outings by her husband, who had Parkinson’s and took Sinemet. The reported experience concerned medication response. The post gives no swallow assessment, dysphagia history or choking result for that meal. Parkinson’s UK reprinted the same account in its June 2016 booklet, and Parkinson’s Western Australia reproduced it in a 2021 adaptation. These versions trace to one account. A separate poster on March 12 described living on mashed potatoes and vegetable soup after developing swallowing difficulty. The UK booklet gives a different caregiver account of small portions of mashed salmon and potato and other foods eaten by her mother with Parkinson’s. Those reports supply no comparison of potato preparations or measured swallowing changes. ## Cold tap water and barium in early Parkinson in Lille

Moreau and colleagues published the ProdDiGYPark assessment on September 21, 2016. The study enrolled 66 people with early Parkinson’s. Participants drank 150 mL cold tap water for a timed test. Average speed in mL/s was 15.5 versus 21 in female participants and controls, and 20 versus 29 in male participants and controls, respectively. A subset of 31 underwent videofluoroscopy using two 10 mL low-density barium boluses. The report gives about 60 percent oral-phase and 21 percent pharyngeal-phase abnormalities, plus aspiration in two men and one woman. Its reported 5 percent overall is consistent with rounding 3 of 66 enrolled. The paper gives no separate aspiration percentage for the imaged subset. The water and barium tests provide context on liquid swallowing. No solid food or baked potato exposure was recorded in the reported protocol. The paper and the related Lille abstracts may share participants. [Primary report and Table 2](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0162904) · [Local licensed text](Source-Text/PMC5031440.txt#L178) · [Registration NCT02627664](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02627664) · [2014 interim conference abstract](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Choking-Followup.md#2014-early-parkinson-swallowing-imaging-in-lille)
