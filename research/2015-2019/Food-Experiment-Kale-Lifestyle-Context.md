# Kale juice in a combined lifestyle program

Null, Pennesi and Feldman first published online March 14, 2016, ahead of the 2017 issue of Journal of Evidence-Based Complementary & Alternative Medicine (22:68–74). Twenty-seven people started a group diet, juice, supplements, exercise and other lifestyle program. Final results came from 21 participants' testimonials. Kale was among the greens suggested for juice. There was no comparison arm. The two accounts below concern different people in the same article. [Original publisher](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2156587216637539) · [Full original text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5871207/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2016-Null-Kale-Lifestyle-Extract.txt)

## Kale juice and caregiver-reported speech

The daughter of a woman with dementia and two previous minor strokes reported that her mother drank green juices containing kale and began to speak more fluently. The account supplies no Parkinson diagnosis, roasted kale, assigned ingredient dose or test of speech intelligibility. It cannot isolate kale from the other program components.

## Parkinson testimonial and nighttime screaming

A different participant, diagnosed with Parkinson disease in 1999, described sleeping better and no longer screaming in the middle of the night after twelve weeks. Her own kale intake and its preparation are unreported. There were no episode counts, dream reports, sleep-stage measurements or diagnostic confirmation of REM sleep behavior disorder. The case cannot determine an ingredient-specific effect.

## Spearmint in the allowable diet list

The Protocol Diet section names spearmint among allowable herbs in the same combined lifestyle program. It gives no verified individual spearmint intake, fresh state, prescribed amount or timing. The two Parkinson testimonials concern memory, medication use, walking or sleep, with no timed daily OFF diary or ingredient-level control. The statement about medication use is a participant's report and supplies no OFF-time estimate. [Primary full Methods and Outcomes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5871207/)

## Celery juice instructions and a walking testimonial

Null and colleagues’ program suggested a 16-ounce juice with celery, cucumber and other produce as part of a multi-component plan. One video testimonial, from a participant identified as Monique with a Parkinson diagnosis, described improved walking after 12 weeks. The source does not establish her individual celery consumption, whether she ate raw stalk or how her walking speed changed. Exercise, dietary changes and supplements were part of the same uncontrolled program. [Primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5871207/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2016-Null-Kale-Lifestyle-Extract.txt)

## Permitted dill and spearmint and separate fatigue testimonials

Null and colleagues’ original Protocol section permits dill weed and spearmint as herbs in a multicomponent lifestyle intervention. Fatigue improvement appears in testimonials involving other conditions, including depression and multiple sclerosis. The Parkinson narratives are separate and the report does not identify individual dill or spearmint use, preparation, or a food-specific fatigue comparison. The Methods list and the testimonials should be recorded separately.
