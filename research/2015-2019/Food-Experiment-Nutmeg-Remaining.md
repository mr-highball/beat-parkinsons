# Nutmeg in a Parkinson diet plan and an isolated compound

Phillips et al.'s 2018 randomized Parkinson diet study assigned low-fat versus ketogenic meal patterns for eight weeks. The low-fat plan schedules apple crisp (printed p. 69): a four-serving baked recipe containing one teaspoon of nutmeg along with cinnamon and other ingredients. A shopping list calls for a vial of nutmeg; it does not establish whether it was freshly grated, its botanical identity, individual intake or dose actually eaten. The investigators tracked meal completion, not individual food consumption. The intervention varied the whole diet and had no isolated nutmeg contrast. [Primary low-fat plan](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6175383.1/MDS-33-1306-s002.pdf#page=69) · [Trial report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6175383/)

The paper reports Part III total MDS-UPDRS changes of −8.58 ± 5.50 low-fat and −6.27 ± 4.07 ketogenic (between-group p = .055). The exam includes a facial expression item and a speech item combining loudness, modulation and clarity, but the publication and sensitivity supplement do not give separate item estimates or a listener-rated intelligibility comparison. The aggregate is related to all three endpoints but cannot determine their individual direction or an effect of nutmeg. [Primary Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6175383/) · [Scale item descriptions](https://www.uwo.ca/fhs/csd/smdl/docs/MDS-UPDRS_English_FINAL_Updated_August2019.pdf)

An April 25, 2015 report tested macelignan, a compound derived from nutmeg, in inflammatory midbrain slice cultures. The author abstract reports preservation of dopaminergic neurons with compound exposure and dependence on arginase signaling. It did not test culinary nutmeg or patients' speech, voice volume or facial expression. The isolated compound is not interchangeable with whole grated spice. [Primary abstract, PMID 25917324](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25917324/)
