# Cayenne pepper motor and facial outcome follow-up

## Seated force plate balance after topical cayenne paste

Šarabon compared cayenne mixed with kaolin and water to kaolin and water alone in healthy adults. The topical procedure was applied over the back. The preliminary report describes 30-second seated centre-of-pressure testing on a wobble board and no detected change in balance or trunk strength. It supplies no standing postural responses or falls. The written 2015 article transcribes a presentation given in November 2014.

[Original 2015 record](https://pagepressjournals.org/index.php/bam/article/download/bam.2015.3.191/4810) · [Local text](Source-Text/2015-Sarabon-Munari-Seated-Balance-Extract.txt)

## Cayenne cataplasm and trunk sensorimotor measurements

The later topical cayenne–kaolin study in healthy volunteers varied cayenne concentration and measured acute sensory, range-of-motion and force outcomes. Results report increased pressure-pain threshold after the two higher concentrations in the concentration experiment. The source does not give a walking, fall-diary or Parkinson postural instability estimate. Its protocol is closely related to the preliminary report, and shared participants remain unverified.

[Original 2018 record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5895990/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2018-Sarabon-Cayenne-Cataplasm-Extract.txt)

## Registered knee injection study of capsaicin

The randomized trial first posted February 12, 2018. It compared intra-articular investigational capsaicin CNTX-4975-05 with a matching injection in adults with knee osteoarthritis. The primary measure was change in knee pain with walking. The registration therefore specifies pain experienced during walking, a distinct measure from gait speed.

[Original 2018 record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03429049) · [Local text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT03429049.txt)

## Registered ground cayenne meal and metabolic outcomes

First posted March 1, 2019. The trial planned a cayenne omelette, an otherwise similar control meal, and postprandial substrate oxidation and energy expenditure. The registry still has no posted results and lists unknown status. Rafia Virk's identifier-matched thesis is dated Summer 2019 but exact first public date remains unverified. Its ten adults all received control first and cayenne second, differing from registered random allocation. The measured metabolic endpoints do not address Parkinson dyskinesia or facial function.
