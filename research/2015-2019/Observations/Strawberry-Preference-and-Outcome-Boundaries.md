# Strawberry preference and outcome boundaries

The [2018 geriatric preference poster](../Source-Text/2018-PMID32218879-P426-Strawberry-Flavor-Preference.txt) reports interest in a strawberry-flavored supplement. The adjacent poster reports falls risk by vitamin D status in a separate study. The page layout places those results next to each other. The study identifiers and reported participant groups keep the outcomes distinct.

The earlier [dietary cohort](../../2010-2014/Source-Text/2012-PMID22535616-Devore-Strawberry-Intake-and-Cognitive-Change.txt) identifies strawberries separately and estimates change in cognitive test scores. The [older dermatology reference](../../2005-2009/Source-Text/2006-PMC7144037-Lexikon-Strawberry-Food-Reaction-Context.txt) identifies a possible tolerability concern in its food-reaction discussions. Those sources measure or discuss different endpoints. The later [grouped fruit report](../../2025-2029/Source-Text/2026-PMID42241377-LBP2-005-Grouped-Fruit-and-Overeating.txt) places strawberries, cherries and grapes together in an overeating table. Each source leaves repeated falls after raw strawberry consumption in Parkinson disease open.
