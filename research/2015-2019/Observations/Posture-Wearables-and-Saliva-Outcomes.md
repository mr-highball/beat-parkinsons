# Posture, wearables and saliva outcomes

The previous period's Oeda cohort measured trunk and neck angles and compared those angles with characteristics of disease. Storch and colleagues measured ten nonmotor symptoms during motor on and off periods, including dysphagia. The 2016 Yoshii study extended photographic measurement to four angles and discussed saliva loss as a possible effect of neck flexion. Neither of these angle measurements quantified drooling, and the Storch study did not include it among the ten outcomes. [Oeda](../../2010-2014/Source-Text/PMC3777935.txt) · [Storch](../../2010-2014/Source-Text/2013-PMID23365054-Storch-On-Off-Nonmotor-Outcomes.txt) · [Yoshii](../Source-Text/2016-PMID26835153-Yoshii-Posture-Angles-and-Drooling-Context.txt)

Earlier medication-associated neck-flexion reports and the later Iijima compilation measure a different outcome. The compilation reproduces the Suzuki case and two Uzawa cases. The wearable viewpoint discusses drooling in its clinical background, and the SENSE-PARK feasibility study tracked dropout and usability. The 2017 Swedish registry did measure drooling, as a UPDRS item. Its Part II progression threshold includes that item, while Part III provides a distinct motor assessment. [Suzuki](../../2005-2009/Source-Text/2007-PMID17826796-Suzuki-Pramipexole-Antecollis.txt) · [Uzawa](../../2005-2009/Source-Text/2009-PMID19890970-Uzawa-Dopamine-Agonist-Antecollis.txt) · [Iijima](../Source-Text/2015-PMID29468202-Iijima-Antecollis-Case-Lineage.txt) · [Wearable viewpoint](../Source-Text/2016-PMID27003779-Van-Uem-Wearable-Measurement-and-Drooling-Context.txt) · [Swedish registry](../Source-Text/2017-PMID28798882-Fereshtehnejad-Registry-Drooling.txt)

The later Portuguese cohort measured patient or caregiver-rated drooling and clinician-rated posture at both visits. Posture worsened, whereas the drooling change was inconclusive. No progression model estimated an association between the two. The supplemental subgroups reuse the same cohort and preserve distinct saliva, speech and swallowing rows. No food was tested for saliva loss in these sources.
