# Apomorphine treatment changes and visual events

The 1997 and 2015 records describe reports of improvement after changes to subcutaneous and oral treatment. The Groningen 125-person cohort also reports three people with worsening and twelve missing follow-ups among 38 with baseline visual hallucinations. These were clinician-rated trajectories, with changes to concurrent medication. They extend the preceding periods' clinical observations without establishing an isolated drug or dietary effect. [1997 original](../../1995-1999/Source-Text/1997-PMID18591063-Apomorphine-and-Neuropsychiatric-Complications.txt) · [125-person cohort](../Source-Text/2015-PMID26709292-Apomorphine-125-Patient-Cohort.txt) · [Earlier letter access](../../2005-2009/Source-Text/2009-PMID19524477-Apomorphine-Visual-Hallucination-Letter-Access.txt).

The later 45-person cognitive cohort distinguishes inpatient titration and longer-term worsening after discharge. It overlaps the earlier cohort's institution and recruitment dates, with no participant crosswalk. The 2016 review cites earlier reports and supplies no additional patients. The later apomorphine protocol's citations predate its first registration and cannot stand in for its randomized outcomes. [Cognitive cohort](../Source-Text/2017-PMID29032012-Apomorphine-45-Patient-Cognitive-Cohort.txt) · [2016 review](../Source-Text/2016-PMID27132157-Apomorphine-Visual-Hallucination-Review.txt) · [Registered trial](../Source-Text/Trials/NCT02702076.txt).

The 2018 original sources also distinguish visual symptom presence in nursing-home and perfusion cohorts from visual cortex chemistry, measured eye movement and selected apathy or prognosis measures. The Acacia review concerns a different botanical. The literature supplies no measured dried fig and Parkinson visual episode-frequency comparison.
