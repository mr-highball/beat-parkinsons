# Visual hallucination measurements and fig exposure context

The 2015–2019 original texts distinguish baseline hallucination status, first onset, adverse-event participants, perceptual illusions, and perceived frequency. In the two-year follow-up of 78 people, 26 first reported visual hallucinations. The pooled extended-release amantadine trials report the number of participants with a visual hallucination event and time to first report. The visual flicker experiment did not reliably elicit people's everyday visual hallucinations. These measures cannot be combined as a count of spontaneous visual episodes after eating dried figs. [First-onset cohort](../Source-Text/2015-PMID30363508-Feeling-of-Presence-and-New-Hallucinations.txt) · [Amantadine comparison](../Source-Text/2018-PMID29532440-Amantadine-Visual-Hallucination-Adverse-Events.txt) · [Flicker experiment](../Source-Text/PMC6700621.txt).

The previous period's fruit extract experiment used a rat drug challenge and maze timings. The 2018 stem bark study evaluates convulsions and righting reflex. The historical harvest accounts describe excitement in nonclinical terms, and the 1982 self-experiment's dried-fruit category lists cognitive complaints without attributing hallucinations to figs. Each preparation, observation and outcome needs its own evidence.
