# Salmon trial families and motor measures

The 2010–2014 registry records establish two different German interventions. PINGU enrolled infants starting complementary foods. FINS-547 randomized preschool children to salmon meals or a comparison diet. The later biomarker papers verify changes in fatty acid status under their assigned feeding programs. These exposure measurements and later studies of child development answer separate questions. [PINGU registry](../../2010-2014/Source-Text/Trials/NCT01487889.txt) · [FINS-547 registry](../../2010-2014/Source-Text/Trials/NCT01951937.txt) · [PINGU biomarker paper](../Source-Text/2015-PMID26169870-PINGU-Fatty-Acids.txt)

The 2018-online preschool trial outcome report gives an inconclusive peg-test comparison. The 2019-online infant report gives an inconclusive comparison on the Bayley psychomotor index. The preschool report and its registry use different enrollment counts and descriptions of the comparison meals. The original numerical tables and cooking methods are needed to judge transfer to the frozen exposure. The two child studies address different tasks and ages, and the Norwegian herring and mackerel study is a separate trial. [Preschool study](../Source-Text/2018-Demmelmair-Salmon-Dexterity.txt) · [Infant outcome study](../Source-Text/2019-PMID31383978-PINGU-Development.txt)

The later Parkinson baseline report shows a small inverse graphical sign for a pooled non-fried fish variable and subjective slow movement. Clinical bradykinesia, the tested fish species and the preparation remain important for interpreting that sign. [Parkinson baseline extract](../../2020-2024/Source-Text/2024-MVP-Eggs-Fish-Oils-Spices.txt)
