# Fig paste, clinical outcomes and preparation

The eight-week supplied fig-paste trial reported greater shortening of colon transit time than placebo in young adults with functional constipation. The trial also favored paste for stool form and abdominal discomfort. Weekly bowel movement frequency and abdominal pain remained inconclusive in the between-group comparison. Neurological disorders were exclusionary, and the study assessed bowel outcomes. [2016 printed trial and tables](../Source-Text/2016-PMID27440682-Fig-Paste-and-Functional-Constipation.txt).

The prior period contains two registrations with similar titles. The 2016 printed methods identify the 80-person protocol explicitly. The earlier 40-person registration concerns women and lists a longer outcome time frame. Both are useful protocol sources, and the only verified outcome family here is the 80-person registration and its published report. [2014 registration](../../2010-2014/Source-Text/Trials/NCT02138851.txt) · [2010 registration](../../2010-2014/Source-Text/Trials/NCT01185431.txt).

The preceding period's Parkinson literature distinguishes patients reporting visual hallucinations during an interview window from counts of repeated events. The fig-paste study adds a verified oral exposure comparison with a different preparation, population and endpoint. The combination leaves the dried-fig visual-frequency question open.
