# Psychosis remission and visual symptom status

The 2017 Johns Hopkins cohort reports broader Parkinson psychosis at scheduled visits. Its diagnosis included prominent hallucinations or delusions and excluded episodes attributed to treatment or occurring solely during delirium. Thirteen of 49 participants with psychosis and repeated visits had a recorded remission. The long gaps between visits and incomplete follow-up after remission limit what the paper can say about day-to-day visual episodes. The earlier Rush cohort used a different instrument with closer scheduled interviews in the first 18 months. Its main article remains open. [Johns Hopkins original](../Source-Text/PMC5945218.txt) · [Rush abstract record](../../2000-2004/Source-Text/2001-PMID11739829-Parkinson-Hallucination-Followup-Abstract.txt).

The 2017 European Association of Nuclear Medicine abstract OP-367 uses visual hallucination status in dementia with Lewy bodies as an imaging subgroup variable. The 2019 Morbelli report draws on the same European consortium's imaging repository and includes more participants and a different adjustment strategy. Shared sites, investigators and data source make participant reuse plausible. The 2019 original and its supplemental table still need body-level comparison before counting these as independent cohorts. Neither paper currently supplies an analysis of dried fig intake. [2017 abstract scope](../Source-Text/2017-PMC7103094-Conference-Scope.txt) · [2019 publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.25453).

The [2018 provider-date apomorphine checkpoint](Apomorphine-Treatment-Changes-and-Visual-Events.md) examines actual changes in reported visual symptoms during treatment. Those observations address different questions from a DSM-IV diagnosis per visit or visual hallucination status at a PET scan. The 2017 studies provide clinical context for the dried fig search through their endpoint definitions.
