# Exposure, source membership and traditional guidance

[2010–2014 observations](../../2010-2014/Observations/README.md)

The preceding period supplies several studies of processed constituents. The tea catechin study reports aggregate motor changes in a nonhuman model. The current period adds a small human feasibility study that actually used black and green tea and measured EEG. Across those two records, matching the exposure or being closer to the disease model gives a different kind of evidence. The paired records supply no brewed black tea comparison measuring bradykinesia. The practitioner account lists chicken, fresh ginger and cinnamon with other foods, while the Lumbee manuscript describes an oral ginger and sage combination for respiratory complaints. Both preserve preparations and context. The traditional labels and use of several ingredients in each pathway limit attribution to a named Parkinson symptom.
