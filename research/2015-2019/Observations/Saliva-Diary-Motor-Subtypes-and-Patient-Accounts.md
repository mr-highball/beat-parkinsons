# Saliva diaries, motor subtypes and patient accounts

The [Cologne clinic study](../Source-Text/2016-PMID27976483-Barbe-Stimulated-Saliva-and-Dry-Mouth.txt) measured stimulated saliva and subjective oral dryness. Its enrollment criteria excluded control participants with dry mouth. Its low-flow classification used 26 of the 30 control participants in the results table. The separate [support-group survey](../Source-Text/2016-PMID27231151-Barbe-Support-Group-Oral-Symptoms.txt) reports drooling, dryness and swallowing as distinct experiences.

The [Cologne diary registration](../Source-Text/Trials/DRKS00012458.txt) separates hourly oral symptom reports from daily activities. The [article abstract](../Source-Text/2018-PMID30028552-Barbe-Symptom-Diary-and-Meals.txt) reports an association between food intake and dry mouth. Its full results tables are needed to inspect ingredient granularity and any reported covariation with drooling.

The [2019 proceedings](../Source-Text/PMC6459563.txt) include genetic and motor subtype comparisons. The [Suzhou study](../Source-Text/2018-PMID29315470-Mao-Drooling-and-Motor-Subtypes.txt) also uses a salivary symptom item across motor subtypes. The [Wenzhou journal paper](../../2020-2024/Source-Text/2020-DOI10.3389-fneur.2020.577128-Wang-Autonomic-Subtype-Outcomes.txt) reports a gastrointestinal composite score, with unresolved overlap with the conference sample.

The [Angulo interview account](../Cultural-and-Experiential/Personal-Reports/2019-PMID31081792-Angulo-Drooling-and-Social-Participation.txt) illustrates the social impact of spilling food and drooling. Its reported experience concerns participation in public settings. No tested culinary intervention links the sources to raw fig.
