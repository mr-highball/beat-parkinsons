# Spanish evidence follow-up, 2015–2019

## Compulsive eating in the Vall d’Hebron cohort

Sáez-Francàs and colleagues first published June 19, 2015. Of 115 consecutive participants with idiopathic Parkinson disease, 27 met criteria for an impulse-control disorder and 12 were classified with compulsive eating. The article reports 10.1% for the latter, while 12 divided by 115 gives 10.4%. Trait anxiety and dopaminergic agonist treatment were associated with the combined impulse-control outcome. The study measures disordered eating as a behavior and supplies no food identity, preparation or diet-to-symptom comparison. [Primary article, PMID 26096669](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0213485315001139) · [Earlier congress report](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Spanish-Context-Followup.md#early-report-of-impulse-control-outcomes-in-parkinson-disease)

## Isolated olive phenol and movement in transgenic worms

Calahorra García-Moreno and colleagues first published July 11, 2019. The accepted manuscript reproduced in the Universidad de Jaén dissertation shows that 1 mM isolated tyrosol added to the culture medium increased adult day 9 movement counts from 7.7 to 20.9 per 30 minutes in NL5901 worms expressing human alpha-synuclein in body-wall muscle (p = .0364). Both groups approached complete paralysis by day 11. In a separate UA44 strain expressing alpha-synuclein in dopaminergic neurons, 80% of exposed worms versus 45.33% of controls had all six anterior dopaminergic neurons intact at day 14 (p = .0012). The dissertation and journal article report the same experiment. The results concern an isolated extra-virgin olive-oil phenol and nematode movement. They give no estimates of clinical walking speed, stride length or bradykinesia. [Primary article, PMID 31404721](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31404721/) · [Accepted manuscript in the thesis, Results 3.3–3.4 and Figures 3–4](https://ruja.ujaen.es/bitstreams/3a0f7b88-2061-49f1-afaa-10d417e9342f/download) · [University thesis record, November 14, 2019](https://hdl.handle.net/10953/2603)

## Intravenous hydroxytyrosol and movement in a rat model

Katia Dinorah Sánchez González’s January 2019 Universidad Autónoma del Estado de Morelos thesis assigned rats to five groups of nine and injected isolated hydroxytyrosol intravenously in an MPP+ lesion model. Graph 1 gives no clear difference in spontaneous locomotor counts. Graph 4 shows less forced-swim immobility in the hydroxytyrosol and MPP+ group than with MPP+ alone. Graph 5 gives 207 ± 18 versus 290 ± 23 apomorphine-induced rotations, with inconsistent significance notation between the results text and caption. The dopamine caption calls the difference significant and also calls it a trend. These tests supply no olive-oil feeding, resting tremor, or clinical bradykinesia result. ## Bread biscuits and yogurt as swallowing test foods

Ayres et al. published on January 19, 2017. Their Porto Alegre study of a chin-down swallowing maneuver used dyed salt biscuits in the endoscopic examination and bread in the clinical assessment. Table 2 reports improvement after the maneuver for clinical solid (p < .001) and liquid (p = .022) scores. The endoscopic observations did not show an improvement. All groups underwent the same food assessment. The comparison tests the maneuver and leaves individual food effects unresolved. The matching registration first posted on November 25, 2016. [Primary paper, PMID 28203475](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5288524/) · [Local methods](Source-Text/PMC5288524.txt#L88) · [Local registration](Source-Text/Trials/NCT02973698.txt)

Sundstedt et al. published on April 21, 2017. Their 14-person endoscopy analysis after caudal zona incerta deep brain stimulation expanded the earlier eight-person report. Participants swallowed a biscuit with green jelly and dyed water. Median penetration/aspiration score was zero for both preparations in each condition. Table 2 reports less premature biscuit spillage from baseline to follow-up with stimulation off (p = .01), with no clear change in biscuit residue or penetration/aspiration. The food was used to measure swallowing during a stimulation study. [Earlier report, PMID 22384826](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22384826/) · [Local later primary paper, test foods and Table 2](Source-Text/PMC5474712.txt#L156)

Mohamed et al. published on October 24, 2018. Their endoscopic protocol used bread soaked in yogurt, yogurt alone and dyed water in 54 people with early idiopathic Parkinson disease evaluated in the medication ON state. The report gives dysphagia in 40.7% by endoscopy and 35.1% by questionnaire. Its tables compare diagnostic phenotypes and participant characteristics and pool the three boluses for the dysphagia frequency. No grade or separate estimate for a frozen bread or yogurt claim is available.
