# Brown rice, postural instability and dream enactment

## Germinated brown rice in a rotenone rat model

Chompoopong and colleagues first published July 18, 2016. The original abstract distinguishes increased open-field ambulation after germinated brown rice pretreatment from an inconclusive difference in mobility duration. Rats receiving the rice preparation showed shorter mean times to turn and descend a pole than rotenone controls, but both pole measures were statistically significant only for the 4-phenylbutyric acid positive-control group. The abstract does not specify culinary cooking or provide exact pole estimates, and postural instability is part of the model description. Neither laboratory task gives a human clinical postural-instability score. This is a related preparation and animal motor comparison for 0471. ## Fermented rice-germ GABA and REM polysomnography

Byun and colleagues first published April 27, 2018. They assigned 40 adults with insomnia symptoms to 300 mg/day GABA extracted from fermented unpolished rice germ (n = 30) or placebo (n = 10) for four weeks. Table 2 reports active-arm REM sleep share of 16.3% to 18.3% versus 17.8% to 18.1% on placebo, with no clear group-by-time interaction for REM share or REM latency. Overall sleep latency shortened relative to placebo (interaction p = .009), while sleep efficacy improved only in the within-arm analysis. The study reported sleep stages and onset times. It did not assay dream enactment or quantify REM without atonia, and the supplement was distinct from steamed whole rice. ## Rice bran extract and REM polysomnography

Um and colleagues published August 26, 2019. They randomized 50 adults with sleep disturbance to 1,000 mg/day rice bran extract or matching placebo for two weeks. The protocol excluded neurological disorders. Table 3 shows REM share changes of +1.4 percentage points with extract and +0.5 with placebo (p = .611 unadjusted, p = .450 after caffeine adjustment). REM latency changes were −15.5 versus −10.4 minutes (p = .774 unadjusted, p = .983 adjusted). Actively treated participants saw longer stage 2 and total sleep relative to placebo. The report does not measure dream enactment or REM motor tone and the formulation was extract capsules. The paper identifies KCT0001893 and reports its registration date as April 21, 2016.
