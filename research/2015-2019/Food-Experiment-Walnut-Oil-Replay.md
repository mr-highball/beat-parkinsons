# Walnut-oil comparator and dream-related sleep disruption

McDonnell and Newcomb published their double-blind crossover on September 26, 2019. Forty-two cardiac rehabilitation patients supplied complete data after two five-night bedside exposures separated by a week. The control was grocery La Tourangelle 100% walnut oil, three drops on each of three cotton balls, with jojoba also on each ball. The comparator was one drop each of lavandin, bergamot and ylang-ylang on jojoba. This was inhalation exposure, not ingestion; walnut oil was the control, not a comparison with no oil. The paper does not specify the tested lot's extraction method. [Primary Methods and Table 2](https://scispace.com/pdf/trial-of-essential-oils-to-improve-sleep-for-patients-in-m1ml2xvff0.pdf#page=3) · [Earlier local factual extract](Source-Text/PMID31556690-walnut-comparator-extract.txt)

The modified Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index asked about sleep disruption over the past week rather than the usual month. Table 2's bad-dream sleep-disruption item averaged 0.42 (SD 0.917) under walnut versus 0.25 (SD 0.534) under the essential-oil blend; the paired comparison was labeled nonsignificant. This is neither a no-treatment contrast nor a measure of acting out dreams, and the population was not selected for Parkinson's. There was no polysomnography. The finding does not support or refute a direction for consumed cold-pressed walnut oil and dream enactment frequency.
