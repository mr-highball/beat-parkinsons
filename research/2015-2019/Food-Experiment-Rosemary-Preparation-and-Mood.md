# Rosemary drinks and acute subjective ratings

## Small rosemary-water pilot and affect ratings

The July 2018 pilot assigned ten healthy adults each to a carbonated rosemary-containing drink or carbonated water. Table 2 gives changes in PANAS and Bond–Lader ratings without significance tests. Its calmness changes are both 10.00 even though the narrative describes a greater rosemary change. The product combined an extract and hydrolat, and the text does not establish the maturity or freshness of the actual leaves used. This pilot is a distinct design from the larger 2018 study. ## Fresh-derived extract in a larger rosemary-water study

The October 2018 article reports 40 participants per arm comparing a commercially prepared fresh-rosemary extract and hydrolat with plain mineral water. It measured post-task alertness and mental fatigue on analog scales. The authors describe very small unfavorable estimates in the subjective measures and report that nominal individual outcome signals failed the multiple-comparison correction. The numeric Table 1 was missing from the readable original manuscript rendering, so precise subjective contrast estimates and uncertainty remain unverified. The trial used an extract drink and had no depression scale or Parkinson population. ## Dried rosemary extract in the Tsukuba dissertation

Kondo’s dissertation was awarded in July 2016 and the repository record was public on March 15, 2017. The repository metadata separately dates public availability of the full dissertation to June 3, 2020. Its introduction describes earlier work in the same laboratory. Figure 1-14 gives shorter immobility in the tail-suspension test for both orally administered doses of hydroethanolic dried rosemary leaf extract compared with water (p < .01). The thesis identifies the Tunisian leaf source and reports an extract composition measurement. The same-group Sasaki paper remains abstract-accessible. The thesis does not explicitly establish the exact animal groups or extract identity across papers.
