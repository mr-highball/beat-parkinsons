# Ground sumac and symptom outcomes

## 2016 Sumac fruit drink and exercise pain

Alghadir and Gabr studied 40 healthy volunteers ages 15–25 in an exercise program with a sumac fruit drink or placebo. Table IV reports mean pain scores after the four-week program of 24.1 with sumac and 56.3 with placebo (p < .001). The sumac arm's two-week mean was slightly below baseline despite the narrative description of increased pain in both groups at that visit. The paper measured exercise pain and biomarkers. It provides no clinical walking-speed, stride-length, dystonia or Parkinson outcome. [Primary report, PMID 28639865](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28639865/) · [University repository article, Table IV](https://real.mtak.hu/38085/1/036.103.2016.2.10.pdf)

## 2017 Sumac ingestion and allergy in a mixed salad

Che and colleagues described a 14-year-old with cashew allergy who developed abdominal discomfort after a Fattoush salad, followed by itch, coughing and eyelid swelling during basketball. Re-exposure was followed by abdominal discomfort and hives. The patient's skin tests reacted to sumac and hummus, and the meeting report says she later tolerated hummus. The case concerns a mixed meal and suspected allergy. The authors did not report visual hallucinations, formal speech intelligibility, olfactory impairment, or Parkinson disease. The same case was presented at the society's 2016 meeting and published in 2017. [Primary conference proceedings, abstract A26](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5390240/#Sec41) · [Journal case report](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaip.2016.12.024)

## 2017 and 2018 Injected sumac fruit extract and rat avoidance learning

Karimi Arab and colleagues reported two related experiments. The Shahid Sadoughi University journal describes 32 rats in four groups, including gonadectomized animals. It reports reduced passive avoidance after gonadectomy and increased avoidance-related latency after injected hydroalcoholic sumac extract. The Mazandaran paper describes 24 adult male rats in three groups without gonadectomy. Its abstract identifies significantly longer latency to enter a dark compartment at the lower tested dose versus solvent. The accessible results do not quantify an equivalent higher-dose effect. The studies used the same authors and related methods, with different group structures. Animal overlap remains unverified. The injection and avoidance-memory task leave attention after culinary sumac in Parkinson disease unresolved. [2017 primary abstract](https://www.magiran.com/paper/1748405) · [2018 issue with primary abstract and full-text link](https://www.magiran.com/volume/126825)

## 2017 Sumac fruit extract and muscle cell stress

Najjar and colleagues assayed a *Rhus coriaria* fruit extract and fractions in cultured human muscle progenitors and zebrafish embryos under hydrogen peroxide exposure. The ethyl acetate fraction reduced oxidative stress and restored adhesion in stressed cultured cells. The embryo assay compared survival after hydrogen peroxide. Neither experiment measured muscle function, walking, stride or dystonia. The October 31 preprint and December 12 article describe the same work. [Primary preprint](https://doi.org/10.7287/peerj.preprints.3383v1) · [Local licensed journal text](Source-Text/PMC5731335.txt)

## 2018 Sumac fruit volatile profiles by preparation

Farag and colleagues compared volatiles from three geographical sources of *Rhus coriaria*, roasted fruits and a cold tea preparation. The assays detected 74 components, with more furans and aldehydes after roasting and no distinct aroma components in the separated seeds under the study conditions. The measurements characterize the material being smelled. They give no clinical smell threshold or impairment change after eating ground sumac. [Local licensed full text](Source-Text/PMC6035716.txt) · [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.5121)

## 2019 Sumac fruit extracts and cholinesterase activity

Gezici tested *Rhus coriaria* fruit extracts prepared with four solvents. Table 1 reports that water extract inhibited acetylcholinesterase by 28.62% and butyrylcholinesterase by 41.08% at the reported comparison concentration. Methanol and hexane extracts were weaker, and the dichloromethane preparation showed no activity on either assay. The study tested isolated enzymes and had no behavioral attention assessment or Parkinson participants. [Primary report and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.15671/HJBC.621202) · [Publisher full text](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/809769)

## 2019 Fragrant sumach bark mixture and urinary symptoms

Gauruder-Burmester and colleagues followed 117 women in a noninterventional study of a mixture of *Rhus aromatica* bark extract, pumpkin seed oil and hop extract. The abstract reports improved daytime urination frequency in 77 of 99 participants with data at week 12. Urination frequency and urgency episodes are distinct indicators. The report concerns fragrant sumach bark from a different species, other active ingredients, and a population with overactive bladder. It does not isolate the effect of ground *Rhus coriaria* fruit. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31261419/)
