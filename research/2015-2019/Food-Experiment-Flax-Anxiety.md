# Flaxseed oil and clinical anxiety measures

## Combined flax-derived omega-3 and vitamin E in Parkinson trials

Taghizadeh and colleagues randomized 60 people with Parkinson disease to flaxseed-derived omega-3 plus vitamin E or placebo for 12 weeks. The paper reports change on total UPDRS of −3.3 versus +4.4 (p = .02). The registry and accepted manuscript do not report an anxiety estimate. A separate report by Tamtaji and colleagues found a combined score difference in Part I (−0.1 versus +1.8, p = .01). The anxiety item and oil-only attribution remain unavailable. [Original Taghizadeh report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28342967/) · [Registration IRCT201604035623N73](https://irct.ir/trial/6103) · [Original Tamtaji report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30554092/)

## Flaxseed oil and Beck anxiety scores

Prior and colleagues reported a 2015 placebo-controlled flaxseed-oil study in adults with binge eating. Twenty-five completed follow-up. Table 1 gives mean Beck Anxiety Inventory scores with standard errors of 7.5 ± 2.2 to 4.5 ± 1.4 in 11 flaxseed-oil completers and 6.8 ± 1.5 to 6.7 ± 1.7 in 14 placebo completers. The final comparison was inconclusive (reported p = .31), and no adjusted change contrast is provided. Moderate anxiety was an exclusion criterion. The investigators identified the finished product and supplier without specifying cold pressing. The reported values give a small favorable numerical direction for an oil and the named symptom, with limited clinical transfer.
