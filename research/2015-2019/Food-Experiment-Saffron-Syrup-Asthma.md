# Multi-extract honey syrup and asthma-related waking

## Compound syrup and asthma-related waking

The original article from Sadr, Choopani and colleagues was first published on 29 December 2017. The current Galen Medical Journal citation uses DOI 10.31661/gmj.v6i4.884. The original page header and abstract give the older DOI 10.22086/gmj.v6i3.884. The author names, title, issue and results identify one report. The publisher identifies the article as CC BY 4.0. It lists IRCT2015083123834N1 in the methods. The registry posting date and archived outcome record are unverified. [Publisher record](https://journals.salviapub.com/index.php/gmj/article/view/884) · [Original PDF](https://journals.salviapub.com/index.php/gmj/article/download/884/636).

In a study of 80 randomized children with mild-to-moderate asthma, 72 were analyzed. Both arms received usual asthma drugs. The intervention arm added syrup containing extracts of saffron stigma, cinnamon bark, cardamom fruit, ginger and galangal with honey. The Table 2 post-treatment ACQ night-symptom scores were 0.08 versus 0.50 (p=.002). The original ACQ defines this item as waking during the night because of asthma. The trial measures asthma-specific waking, with no insomnia scale or ground saffron isolated. Different treatments and an asthma-specific study population make this very low-fit for Parkinson insomnia, with no effect attributable to saffron.
