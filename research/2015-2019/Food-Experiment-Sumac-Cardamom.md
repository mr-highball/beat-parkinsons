# Food experiment evidence for sumac and cardamom

## 2016 Sumac powder, hypertension and sleep adverse events

Ardalani and colleagues reported that 80 of 95 enrolled adults with hypertension completed eight weeks of captopril with either 1 g/day *Rhus coriaria* powder capsules or placebo. Baseline systolic pressure was 145.34 and 143.64 mmHg and week-eight pressure was 115.21 and 131.39 mmHg, respectively. The study measured hypertension. Its data do not identify standing blood pressure or posture-linked lightheadedness for 0337.

For 0378 and 0781, the published narrative describes drowsiness in one sumac recipient. Table 3 lists no drowsiness in either arm and one weakness report in the sumac arm. Table 3 lists one insomnia and one headache in placebo and none in the sumac arm. The narrative groups those two placebo events together. The source does not resolve the drowsiness/weakness discrepancy and prints out-of-range values in the P-value column for weakness and insomnia. No sleep scale or Parkinson cohort was included. [Publisher, Tables 2–4](https://doi.org/10.1039/C5RA22840A) · [Production article, pages 11508–11509](https://www.academia.edu/30360107/Sumac_as_a_novel_adjunctive_treatment_in_hypertension_A_randomized_double_blind_placebo_controlled_clinical_trial)

## 2017 Green cardamom powder, blood pressure and digestive adverse events

Yaghooblou and colleagues randomized 80 overweight or obese women with prediabetes to 3 g/day green cardamom or rusk powder placebo for eight weeks. Between-arm blood pressure differences were not significant. The adverse-event paragraph reports two cases of early diarrhea, one mild skin inflammation, one glossitis and ten reports of reduced burning foot in the cardamom arm. One early report of constipation occurred in the placebo arm. The spontaneous event counts give leads for 0015 and 0363 without a constipation efficacy endpoint or a Parkinson pain assessment. The blood pressure comparison did not test 0453’s lightheadedness on standing. [Primary report, Results and Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5623966/) · [Local full text](Source-Text/PMC5623966.txt)

## 2018 Sumac capsules and blood pressure in a crossover trial

Asgary and colleagues randomized 30 adults with hyperlipidemia to two four-week sequences of 500 mg sumac twice daily and placebo, separated by a two-week washout. The authors reported that systolic and diastolic blood pressure were lower with sumac by approximately 1.87 and 1.32 mmHg. This is an indirect comparator for the blood-pressure finding in Ardalani’s hypertensive cohort. It did not measure Parkinson’s disease or orthostatic symptoms for 0337. Published online February 9, 2018. [Primary report, PMID 29425477](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29425477/)

## 2019 Cardamom oil in an aluminum neurotoxicity model

Auti and Kulkarni evaluated cardamom oil in rats exposed to aluminum chloride as an Alzheimer-like model. They reported improvements in water-maze escape latency, passive avoidance and locomotor activity. The tasks involved spatial or recognition memory and activity. This is adjacent evidence for 0574, which specifies attention impairment after ground cardamom in Parkinson’s. [Primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6502995/) · [Local full text](Source-Text/PMC6502995.txt)

## 2019 Registration for sumac powder and depression

IRCT20131228015968N6 registered 66 planned participants on May 10, 2019. It lists depression among the secondary outcomes and describes 3 g/day ground sumac in capsules or starch placebo, both with a restricted-calorie diet. The 2020 report gives data on 62 women and the between-arm Beck depression contrast. The two records concern the same trial family. [Official registration](https://irct.ir/trial/36888) · [2020 report assessment](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Sumac-Cardamom.md#2020-sumac-powder-and-beck-depression-scores)

## Registered ground sumac and Beck depression outcome

The Iranian registry first posted IRCT20131228015968N6 on May 10, 2019 while recruitment was listed as ongoing. The detailed arms specify 3 g/day of ground sumac or starch placebo with calorie restriction for 12 weeks. Depression was secondary outcome 15 and the record specifies baseline and week-12 Beck Depression Inventory assessments. The summary separately says six weeks. The reports published in 2020 and 2023 give the same registry ID and ethics approval. ## Cardamom essence in an endocrine smell identification test

The 2019 idiopathic hypogonadotropic hypogonadism study used cardamom among ten commercially supplied essences presented to each nostril. The study evaluated cross-sectional olfactory test scores and olfactory apparatus MRI against controls. This identifies a distinct clinical use of cardamom as an odor stimulus. It does not isolate ingestion or a treatment-related change in Parkinson olfaction.
