# Cinnamon and sesame oil evidence

## Sesame oil use in an Indian Parkinson survey

Pandit and colleagues first published August 20, 2015. Among 233 patients seen at an Indian movement disorders clinic, 106 reported complementary and alternative medicine use. The authors list til oil, which they identify as sesame seed oil, among named Ayurvedic products. They do not give a sesame-specific denominator, preparation, route, fall, dystonia or other symptom estimate. [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jtcme.2015.03.009)

## Cinnamon consumption and nonmotor measures in a Parkinson cohort

The 2018 UK multicentre report included 139 people with Parkinson disease. Table 4 lists cinnamon use for 11 of 66 White, 22 of 34 Asian and 14 of 39 Black African or Caribbean participants. Section 3.5 says the exploratory diet analyses found no differences after multiplicity correction in total or domain NMSS or total HADS scores. The paper gives no cinnamon-specific effect estimate for an individual urinary, mood or cardiovascular item. The exposure is reported consumption with no ground preparation coding. [Full local article, Table 4 and Section 3.5](Source-Text/PMC6312592.txt#L138)

## Registration of a patch trial for overactive bladder

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT03532789 on May 22, 2018. The record lists 66 actual participants, the herbal and placebo patch arms, and the Overactive Bladder Symptom Score and urgency severity scale. The official protocol contains no posted registry results. It describes a two-week assessment and identifies cinnamon in the study summary, while the intervention label says herbal patch. The 2020 publication cites this registration and reports a four-week follow-up after the two-week treatment assessment. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03532789) · [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT03532789.txt) · [Dated results](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Cinnamon-Sesame.md#cinnamon-patch-and-overactive-bladder)

## Isolated cinnamaldehyde and anxiety-like behavior in mice

Etaee and colleagues published February 26, 2019. They compared oral isolated cinnamaldehyde and oil vehicle in mice under acute, chronic or no experimental stress. Several elevated-plus-maze and open-field center outcomes favored the compound, while open-field distance was inconclusive (omnibus p > .05). The isolated compound, experimental stress and mouse behaviors differ from culinary cinnamon and clinical anxiety or gait in Parkinson disease. [Original methods and Figure 5](https://doi.org/10.1080/10253890.2019.1567710)

## Cinnamon-containing Kampo formula and apathy in an open pilot

Ohsawa and colleagues published December 9, 2017. They followed 20 people with Alzheimer or mixed dementia treated with TJ-108, a 12-component extract whose manufacturer lists cinnamon bark. Apathy scores declined from baseline from week four onward. The series did not have a parallel comparison group and did not isolate cinnamon. [Primary pilot](https://doi.org/10.3233/ADR-170026) · [Manufacturer formulation](https://www.tsumura.co.jp/english/products/pi/JPR_T108.pdf)

## Cinnamaldehyde preparation and rigidity in a Parkinson model abstract

Rajput and Sinha reported in 2019 that pretreatment with two levels of a cinnamaldehyde preparation reduced rigidity and catalepsy and increased locomotion in a 6-OHDA model. The conference abstract describes a methanolic preparation and switches between mouse and rat in its Methods. It does not give gait speed, sustained dystonic posturing or a clinical outcome. The methods and numerical tables remain to be located. [Original meeting abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jns.2019.10.1146)
