# Salmon and anxiety follow-up

## Mixed oily-fish lunches and emotional symptoms in FINS-TEENS

Skotheim and colleagues, October 12, 2017. This Norwegian randomized trial's mental-health report describes 425 adolescents. Lunches with mixed salmon, mackerel and herring were offered three days a week for 12 weeks and compared with meat meals and omega-3 capsules. The food arm changed the adjusted Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire emotional-problems score by +0.12 (95% CI −0.15 to .39), versus +0.02 (−0.22 to .26) with meat, p = .61. The smaller subgroup with high baseline emotional scores improved by −0.21 in the fish arm and −1.20 in meat controls, p = .03, with lower score denoting fewer difficulties. The subgroup was small and the fish arm had low meal adherence. The report does not provide a standalone anxiety measure, a salmon-specific effect or a preparation-specific effect. [Primary paper and local text, PMID 29056893](Source-Text/PMC5642191.txt)

## Poached salmon in a randomized glycemia crossover

Ballance and colleagues, published online September 14, 2018 and in a 2019 journal issue. In a controlled healthy-adult crossover, mixed meals paired cooked carbohydrate staples with poached salmon, boiled carrots and herb sauce. The comparator meals consisted of the carbohydrate staples alone. Outcomes were blood glucose and insulin after the meals. Anxiety outcomes were outside the design, and participants were healthy volunteers. [Primary report, PMID 30218140](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30218140/) · [Public copy of primary methods](https://potet.nlr.no/files/documents/Fagforum-Potet/Filer/Ballance_et_al-2018-European_Journal_of_Nutrition.pdf)

## FINS-KIDS fish meals and preschool behavior

Hysing and colleagues, 2018. The separate FINS-KIDS preschool sample received herring or mackerel three times a week for 16 weeks versus meat. The report found no significant differences in parent-rated Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire scales. The intervention's two fish species did not include salmon. [Primary paper and local text, PMID 30314281](Source-Text/PMC6213752.txt)

## Registered adolescent and childhood oily-fish trials

The FINS-TEENS record first posted January 29, 2015. It registers the Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire as a secondary mental-health outcome after 12 weeks of school meals with fatty fish or meat and cheese, or omega-3 capsules. The linked 2017 publication identifies the fish species. The FiSK Junior record first posted June 22, 2016. It names salmon, herring and mackerel in the same intervention arm and registers the parent-rated Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire among its other outcomes. The 2020 publication reports that trial’s socioemotional results. Neither registration assigns a separate salmon arm or includes a standalone anxiety scale. No registry results are posted.
