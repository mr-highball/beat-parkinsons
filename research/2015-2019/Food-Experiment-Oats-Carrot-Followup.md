# Oats and carrot evidence

## 2015 Carrot juice control and executive and gait measurements

Schrager and colleagues compared six weeks of pasteurized carrot juice with blueberries in 20 older adults. The seven-person carrot arm changed from 48.71 to 47.39 seconds on Trail Making Test B (p = .804) and from 1.16 to 1.20 m/s in usual gait speed (p = .350). Neither result was significant, and the study reported no stride-length measure. The blueberry arm had fewer dual-task step errors than the carrot arm after adjustment for baseline, age and sex. The carrot arm did not test roasted or steamed carrots, and the sample was healthy and independently mobile. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1139/apnm-2014-0247) · [Full text copy and Tables 2–3](https://academy.miloa.eu/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/M.-Schrager-V.-E.-Kelly-al-2015.pdf)

## 2017 Oatmeal and aggregate Parkinson symptom burden

Mischley and colleagues' baseline dietary analysis included 1,053 people with self-reported idiopathic Parkinson's. Table 2 gives a one-cup oatmeal food-frequency item. The fully adjusted estimate per frequency category was −4.4 total PRO-PD points (95% CI −17.3 to 8.5, p = .501). This observational, inconclusive estimate does not resolve cooked versus rolled preparation or identify a separate change in dyskinesia, handwriting, apathy, voice volume, balance, dexterity, insomnia or rigidity. [Existing primary text, Table 2](Source-Text/PMC5610862.txt#L231)

## 2018 Registration for green oat extract cognition and stress

NCT03689348 first posted on September 28, 2018. The registered trial compared a wild green oat extract with placebo for cognitive and stress measurements. The 2020 article identifies this registration. It is a formulation and population distinction for cooked- and rolled-oat claims. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03689348) · [Later report](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Oats-Carrot-Followup.md#2020-green-oat-extract-and-cognitive-tracking-tasks)

## 2019 Purple carrot extract at a neuroscience congress

Custódio da Silva and colleagues reported a reserpine rat model with orally administered purple carrot extract. Their abstract reports shorter catalepsy measures and vacuous jaw movements and a favorable novel-object recognition result. The printed tongue-propulsion number is inconsistent with its stated direction. The author groups and preparation overlap with the 2024 report. The public summaries describe different treatment schedules, so participant overlap and replication are unresolved. Neither source measured clinical executive function or stride after eating carrot. [Primary proceedings, abstract 16.022, printed page 98](https://www.sbnec.org.br/site/arquivos/Resumos_SBNeC_2019-com.pdf#page=98) · [2024 report](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Oats-Carrot-Followup.md#2024-oral-purple-carrot-extract-in-reserpine-treated-rats)
