# JSDR primary abstracts on Parkinson swallowing and foods, 2015–2017

These are original case reports, cohorts or incident analyses from the Japanese Society of Dysphagia Rehabilitation. Some proceedings pages contain multiple independent abstracts. The presentation IDs identify the actual patients and methods within each page. [Annual meeting archive](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/meeting/meeting_annual.html)

## 2015 hospital food choking incidents by diagnosis

The full P12-11 abstract describes analysis of 119 detailed oral-intake choking reports selected from 247 Japan Council for Quality Health Care reports that matched choking plus a hospital ward or room. Eleven of the 119 (9%) involved patients classified as having parkinsonism (パーキンソン症候群). Across all 119, the reported obstructing materials were unmodified solids (51%), chopped food (20%), jelly-like material (3%), thickened liquid (4%), and food residue (22%). The parkinsonism cases were characterized by age 60 or over, events during meals, and both independent eating and eating with professional assistance. The abstract supplies no case IDs, individual foods, exposure denominators, or cross-tabulation of the eleven cases by food. It cannot identify how many cases involved idiopathic Parkinson's disease. The official program has a shorter P12-11 synopsis with 95 cases and different aggregate statistics. The full proceedings' 119-case analysis is the source for the 11-case finding. The program and full abstract describe versions of the same study. [Full proceedings, P12-11, S155](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no21_abstracts.pdf#page=156) · [Program, P12-11, p. 142](https://www.congre.co.jp/jsdr2015/html/program/img/program_0818.pdf)

## 2015 actual choking and separate aspiration of jelly

Machida and colleagues reported a 79-year-old man with stage III Parkinson's disease and ankylosing spinal hyperostosis, with cervical bony overgrowth. He was taken for emergency treatment after a choking episode and survived. The food involved was unspecified. Later, swallowing of solids was associated with aspiration. During videofluoroscopy and endoscopic swallowing assessment, thickened water cleared without pharyngeal residue and jelly left vallecular and pyriform residue and entered the trachea. Changing neck position did not resolve the latter finding. This is one patient with concurrent structural disease. [Proceedings, CC-6, S39–S40](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no21_abstracts.pdf#page=40)

A separate abstract on the same page, CC-5, reported two hospitalized patients with Parkinson's disease. One had coughing with thickened tea, then documented aspiration of saliva on endoscopy. The other had pharyngeal residue with a moderately thick bolus or jelly on endoscopy and continued blended food during the short admission. The tea species and thickener are unspecified. The adjacent CC-4 is a different case and supplies the 3 g jelly test cited on that page. [Proceedings, CC-5, S39](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no21_abstracts.pdf#page=40)

## 2015 rice cracker and manju after hip fracture rehabilitation

Shiohama and colleagues reported a 76-year-old man with Parkinson's disease, a history of aspiration pneumonia, and dysphagia diet level 3 before hospitalization for a hip fracture. Following interdisciplinary care, videofluoroscopy on postoperative day 27 informed the team's judgment that manju (filled confection) and senbei (rice cracker) could be eaten. The abstract gives neither the test recipes nor item-level swallowing counts and evaluates an episode of care. This is a separate case from the younger man with mealtime mouth-opening difficulty and a senbei observation in 2017. [Proceedings, P5-6, S122](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no21_abstracts.pdf#page=123) · [2017 case](Food-Experiment-Japanese-Swallowing-Followup.md#rice-cracker-puree-and-porridge-in-the-same-parkinson-case)

## 2015 jelly aspiration during recovery and transition to porridge

Ando and colleagues reported an 81-year-old woman with stage V Parkinson's disease who had a feeding tube after aspiration pneumonia. Endoscopy in January 2014 documented post-swallow aspiration from pharyngeal jelly residue. Repeat endoscopy in March 2014 found no aspiration, allowing supervised jelly training. She progressed to paste and then full rice porridge and soft foods in 2015. The jelly ingredients are unnamed. The sequential observations coincided with rehabilitation and clinical changes, so the abstract cannot isolate a food effect. [Proceedings, P6-6, S126](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no21_abstracts.pdf#page=127)

## 2015 recorded calorie intake during inpatient rehabilitation

Mishima and colleagues retrospectively examined 41 inpatients with Parkinson's disease who selected 1,600 or 1,900 kcal/day menus during a mean 63 days of rehabilitation. They stratified patients by obesity and selected energy intake. The 13 non-obese patients receiving 1,600 kcal/day had more severe symptoms and further BMI loss during rehabilitation. Drooling and time with dyskinesia emerged as BMI correlates. Rest tremor and swallowing were among the UPDRS items considered. Menu ingredients were not reported and the nonrandomized groups differed in baseline severity. A later 2017 abstract from the same hospital and overlapping author group describes body composition at 1,600–1,900 kcal/day. The reports do not establish independent cohorts. [2015 proceedings, P5-1, S120](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no21_abstracts.pdf#page=121) · [2017 proceedings, P37-3, S183](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no23_abstracts.pdf#page=184)

## 2016 alcoholic drink jelly during dysphagia rehabilitation

Matsuda and colleagues reported an 89-year-old woman hospitalized with Parkinson's disease, pneumonia, and urinary tract infection. Water was used for direct rehabilitation in the acute phase. In recovery, the family brought alcoholic drink jelly (酒ゼリー) and she managed oral intake at the training level. Follow-up helped maintain some intake of preferred foods at home. The authors did not specify the beverage base, dose, alcohol content or a jelly-specific aspiration measurement. [Proceedings, P12-6, S172](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no22_abstracts.pdf#page=173)

## 2016 white rice and bread paste in combined oral and tube feeding

Shinoda and colleagues described a 66-year-old man with Parkinson's disease and severe intellectual disability who continued eating white rice by preference while other foods were softened. After gastrostomy, he ate bread paste and mousse-like side dishes as the initial oral diet with tube feeding adjusted to intake. Weight rose from the 29 kg range to 34.3 kg after one year. The report gives no food-specific swallowing outcome and combines texture modification with tube nutrition. [Proceedings, P15-1, S183](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no22_abstracts.pdf#page=184)

## 2017 meal temperature and first swallow in parkinsonism

Fudetani and Tada provided foods of varying hardness, temperature and taste to four people with akinesia and rigidity described as parkinsonism. The endpoint was time from taking food to the first observed swallowing reflex. The authors reported that hot food favored this measure and found no consistent pattern for hardness or taste. The abstract gives no ingredients, exact temperatures, timing values, Parkinson disease subtype, or aspiration results. Its rice ball example cites a separate 2003 report. The cited patient had secondary parkinsonism after carbon monoxide exposure. [Proceedings, O7-2, S88](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no23_abstracts.pdf#page=89) · [Original cited report](https://doi.org/10.11334/jibi1954.49.5_358)

On the same page, Fukada and colleagues reported coughing when drinking water and instrumental liquid aspiration in a 76-year-old man diagnosed with Parkinson's disease. After endoscopic cricopharyngeal myotomy, they reported no aspiration and that he finished a soft side-dish meal. They provide no item-specific postoperative water comparison. [Proceedings, O7-1, S88](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no23_abstracts.pdf#page=89)

## 2017 silent aspiration of water and rice porridge

Umebayashi and Shimizu reported an 86-year-old with Parkinson's disease and prior lacunar stroke who was hospitalized with a lumbar compression fracture. The patient developed aspiration pneumonia after admission. Videofluoroscopy found marked vallecular and pyriform residue and silent aspiration with both 3 mL water and rice porridge. At repeat assessment, left head rotation helped and the patient progressed from one soft meal to three oral meals. The case gives no recipe or per-food follow-up counts and records multiple comorbidities. [Proceedings, P38-10, S386](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no23_abstracts.pdf#page=387)

## 2017 return to oral meals after aspiration pneumonia

The 2019 full report by Kaoru Akazawa and Kenichi Kashihara and the 2017 abstract O55-2 led by Kaoru Watanabe both draw on Okayama Kyokuto Hospital data, using the same eligibility, 37 cases, 17/20 outcome split, and Spearman r = .59 for improvement in swallowing and motor FIM efficiency. They likely describe the same or overlapping data. The reports give different recruitment windows (April 2014 to March 2015 in the journal and fiscal years 2014 and 2015 in the abstract). The journal also states that its 37 admission cases include repeat admissions. Neither should be counted as an independent replication. The journal issue is dated April 30, 2019 and J-STAGE release August 31, 2019. The 17 who resumed three oral meals were younger and had lower Hoehn and Yahr stages and higher scores on several measures of functional independence. No specific food exposure was assigned. [Earlier local abstract note](Food-Experiment-Japanese-Swallowing-Followup.md#jelly-and-return-to-oral-intake-after-aspiration) · [2019 full report](https://doi.org/10.32136/jsdr.23.1_3)
