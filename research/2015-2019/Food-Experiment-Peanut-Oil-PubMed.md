# Peanut oil comparators in arthritis and visceral pain experiments

## Oral groundnut oil and measured paw inflammation in adjuvant arthritis

Yadav and colleagues purchased market groundnut (*Arachis hypogaea*), sesame and rice-bran oils. Male Wistar rats received oral groundnut oil with or without a laboratory stripping step using silicic acid and activated charcoal. The starting grade was not identified as commercially refined or cold-pressed. The Results say that tested groundnut oil lacked the assayed minor compounds, although the Tables 2–4 labels distinguish groundnut arms with and without minor components. For example, Table 3 gives serum CRP of 7.60 mg/dL in the labelled native groundnut arm versus 15.80 for stripped groundnut and 16.80 for adjuvant alone, with a distinct significance group only for the native groundnut value. The study examined paw volume, imaging, histology and inflammatory measures. It did not measure pain behavior or self-reported pain. ## Rectal peanut oil comparator and mouse grimace ratings

Jain and colleagues used intrarectal peanut oil as vehicle for allyl isothiocyanate in wild-type and peptide-YY knockout mice. In Figure 2, freezing was longer after the irritant in both genotypes than after oil vehicle in wild-type mice. In Figure 3, the mouse grimace change was greater with the irritant than the oil vehicle in both genotypes (p < .001). The Methods and Figure 3 legend specify 1% irritant for that endpoint, while adjacent results prose says 2%. This experiment extends earlier work by the group with the same irritant and vehicle and adds a peptide-YY/genotype-related question and a grimace measurement. It did not compare oil with a matched neutral vehicle as an active treatment. Processing was unspecified and the route was rectal.
