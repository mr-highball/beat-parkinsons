# Seafood and Parkinson’s symptom evidence

## Fish oil and beam performance in parkinsonian rats

Denny Joseph and Muralidhara reported an oral fish-oil and quercetin study in a rotenone rat model on February 17, 2015. Fish oil alone did not clearly improve hind-limb impairment on a narrow beam. The combination improved narrow-beam performance and later grip strength. Beam scoring and grip strength differ from stride length and hand dexterity. The oil’s fish species was unspecified. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25687767/) · [Primary article copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/273952974_Combined_Oral_Supplementation_of_Fish_Oil_and_Quercetin_Enhances_Neuroprotection_in_a_Chronic_Rotenone_Rat_Model_Relevance_to_Parkinson%27s_Disease)

## Trout meal trial proteomic follow-up

The companion report first published in 2015 analyzed the plasma proteome in 30 healthy men selected from the 2010 trout feeding trial. After eight weeks, exploratory protein differences were reported across marine-fed trout, vegetable-fed trout and chicken arms. The proteome substudy supplies no symptom outcome. [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114514004152) · [Parent study](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Trout-Sardine-Shrimp.md#whole-trout-meals-in-healthy-men)

## Canned sardines in the Pilchardus trial

Balfegó and colleagues randomized 35 drug-naive adults with type 2 diabetes to a standard diet or the diet with a sardine target of 100 g five days weekly for six months. The intervention supplied canned sardines in olive oil and foods made from those sardines, including croquettes, burgers and pâté. Recipe materials also included fresh sardines. The study found no clear between-arm improvement in glycemic control, and reported changes in omega-3 status and gut microbiota. It provided no fatigue severity or Parkinson outcome. [2016 primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27090218/) · [Local CC BY article text](Source-Text/PMC4836051.txt)

## Canned sardine prevention trial registration

NCT03557541 first posted on June 15, 2018. It assigned 200 g of sardines weekly on top of diet advice in older adults with prediabetes. Listed outcomes include diabetes incidence, adiponectin, microbiome, and metabolic measures. Registry estimated enrollment was 182 and the paper randomized 152. The registry lists no fatigue endpoint or posted results. [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03557541) · [Local registry text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT03557541.txt) · [Published trial](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Trout-Sardine-Shrimp.md#canned-sardine-feeding-in-older-adults)

## Fatty fish meals and hand dexterity

The 2018 FINS-KIDS trial assigned 232 Norwegian preschoolers to herring and mackerel or meat meals. For the Nine-Hole Peg Test, the main-model table shows changes of −4.2 s with fish and −2.7 s with meat for the nondominant hand (between-group p = .047). The report’s prose gives −4.5 s for fish, which appears in the separate compliance-adjusted model in the table. The dominant-hand result was inconclusive. This study supplies a task-specific human comparison using two other fish species and children without Parkinson’s. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29530020/) · [Local CC BY article text](Source-Text/PMC5848440.txt)

## Fatty fish meals and childhood sleep and emotion

The FINS-KIDS companion report analyzed 170 children with complete parent-reported sleep and mental health data. Herring and mackerel meals did not clearly differ from meat for sleep duration, sleep onset latency, wake after sleep onset, or the Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire emotional problems scale. The paper did not use an adult daytime sleepiness or anxiety instrument. It is from the same trial as the dexterity result. [2018 primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30314281/) · [Local CC BY article text](Source-Text/PMC6213752.txt)

## Fish oil and human stride during exercise training

Schättin and colleagues compared fish oil plus exergame training with olive oil plus the same training in older adults without Parkinson’s. The 2019 paper includes 22 and 20 participants in its gait table. Fish oil did not clearly add to the training response on single-task or dual-task stride length (Table 8 time-by-group p = .753 and .537, respectively). The single-task time effect was p = .774. Table 7 gives fish-oil single-task medians of 1.42 to 1.43 m versus 1.40 to 1.37 m for olive oil. Dual-task medians were 1.31 to 1.35 m and 1.29 to 1.30 m, respectively. The study tested an oil supplement within a training program. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30866834/) · [Local CC BY article text](Source-Text/PMC6416848.txt)

## Supplied trout and depression scores in older adults

Shapouri-Moghaddam and colleagues’ December 15, 2017 trial supplied 480 g of trout weekly for three months to 54 adults aged 60 or older. The 42 comparison participants ate fish two or fewer times weekly. The 15-item Geriatric Depression Scale changed from 3.67 to 2.83 versus 4.05 to 3.31, with adjusted difference −0.17 (95% CI −0.48 to 0.15, p = .295) in Table II. The sample was recruited from senior housing and the report gives neither species nor cooking method. The article reports an inconclusive continuous comparison despite its favorable title. A distinct 2017 trial by Sharifan and colleagues used Caspian white fish and perilla oil. [Publisher and publication date](https://www.jgerontology-geriatrics.com/article/view/113) · [Original, Table II](https://www.jgerontology-geriatrics.com/article/download/113/145) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2017-Shapouri-Trout-Depression-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Separate white fish trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28124802/)

## Early report of purified DHA and rat stride

The 2019 FASEB meeting abstract reported a longer stride in a 6-OHDA rat model after DHA. The team’s January 2020 full report describes an injected purified DHA exposure and supplies the comparison statistics. The two reports describe the same line of work. [2019 primary abstract](https://doi.org/10.1096/fasebj.2019.33.1_supplement.501.10) · [Full report](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Trout-Sardine-Shrimp.md#purified-dha-and-rat-stride-length)

## Dysarthria in a cerebral angiostrongyliasis series

Yang and colleagues reviewed 27 clinical cases in China in 2019. Patients reported eating raw or undercooked snails or shrimp before symptoms, and one case had dysarthria. The report does not identify that patient’s food separately. This is infection-associated dysarthria without a speech-intelligibility test. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31313505/) · [Local CC BY article text](Source-Text/PMC6710201.txt)
