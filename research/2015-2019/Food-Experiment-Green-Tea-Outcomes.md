# Green tea fatigue comparisons and isolated caffeine in Parkinson disease

## Handwriting within Parkinson motor outcome assessments

The Chen brewed green tea pilot used the original UPDRS at baseline and follow-up. Table 1 reports a part II daily-living mean of 9.2 at baseline and 9.9 at three months in those with data. The handwriting item of part II and objective letter size were not reported separately. The broader motor trial of concentrated green tea polyphenols also published no accessible handwriting estimate. [Chen methods and Table 1](Source-Text/2015-Chen-Brewed-Green-Tea.txt#L164) · [Concentrated-polyphenol trial](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Tea-Followup.md)

The later Café-PD randomized comparison enrolled 121 people with Parkinson disease and administered caffeine capsules or placebo. Its six-month results state that no part II component differed between groups. MDS-UPDRS item 2.7 measures patient-reported handwriting readability. The report gives no separately quantified handwriting contrast or letter-size measure. This is an isolated caffeine comparison. [Café-PD Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5664303/) · [Original Parkinson handwriting measurement study](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/2/3/e000628)

## Accumulated fatigue after standard and low-caffeine green tea

Unno and colleagues compared two room-temperature infusions in a double-blind crossover with healthy middle-aged adults. Twenty entered and 19 contributed to the fatigue analysis in Figure 5. Participants reported less accumulated fatigue on Monday morning during the low-caffeine tea condition (p=.015), while the Friday difference was less clear (p=.074). The green teas were both active exposures and differed in measured composition beyond caffeine alone. No water-only control or Parkinson population was included. [Original Results, Figure 5 and full text](Source-Text/PMC5537891.txt) · [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu9070777)

A distinct 2017 Unno trial assigned 20 pharmacy students to low-caffeine green tea or barley tea. It assessed subjective stress, overall physical condition and achievement emotions during training. These measures do not give an independent fatigue score. A third report studied ten older adults and focused on sleep and stress. Do not combine these samples with the crossover. [Student trial](https://doi.org/10.1248/bpb.b17-00141) · [Older-adult report](https://doi.org/10.3164/jcbn.17-6)

## Acute espresso comparison in people with hyposmia

Meusel and colleagues compared caffeinated and decaffeinated espresso in 76 people with olfactory loss. Standardized testing found no clear difference after the single intake across postinfectious and sinonasal subgroups. The 2015 meeting abstract and the 2016 journal paper describe the same comparison. Coffee and these causes of olfactory loss differ from brewed green tea and Parkinson-related olfactory impairment. [Original meeting report](https://www.achems.org/web/downloads/programs/2015-Abstracts.pdf) · [Journal report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26742907/)

## Acute coffee crossover in healthy individuals with normal olfaction

Han and colleagues studied 49 normosmic adults in a caffeinated versus decaffeinated coffee crossover. Their Figure 2 reports no clear intervention difference in threshold, discrimination or identification. The trial used coffee and tested acute effects in people without smell impairment. The publisher metadata records first online publication in 2019 and the issue date in 2020. [Primary Methods and Figure 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7010495/) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30813710/)

## Muscle electrical activity during training-induced fatigue

The 2018 randomized athlete study administered encapsulated green tea extract or placebo. Surface electromyography was its measure of muscular activation and fatigability. The investigators describe changes in muscle activation and electrical fatigue indicators under experimentally induced fatigue. This differs from self-reported fatigue severity after a tea beverage. [Primary study and Figure 4](Source-Text/PMC6107802.txt) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30174618/)

## Retrospective registration for brewed green tea after gastrectomy

The official WHO ICTRP mirror identifies ChiCTR1800018294 as a randomized postoperative Chinese green tea trial at the Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University. It gives first enrollment on September 21, 2017, registration on September 10, 2018, and a September 17, 2018 mirror refresh. The mirror marks the registration as retrospective. Gastrointestinal recovery is primary. Secondary fields include fatigue scores, pain and other postoperative outcomes. It lists 40 planned participants per arm and a no-intervention comparison. The 2021 publication specifies an equal-volume water control. The mirror has empty results and protocol fields and supplies no detailed fatigue instrument.
