# Dietary trial follow-up from WHO ClinicalTrials.gov records, 2015–2019

## Whey and soy protein outcomes

Tosukhowong and colleagues published the six-month NCT01662414 trial in 2016. The analyzed comparison included 15 people receiving whey protein isolate and 17 receiving soy protein. Reduced glutathione and some amino acids increased in the whey group, while homocysteine decreased. UPDRS and striatal FDOPA uptake showed no significant improvement in either group. The result concerns a soy protein supplement used as an active comparator. No culinary soybean oil or symptom-specific estimate is reported. [Primary abstract, PMID 27423583](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27423583/) [2012 registration](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-WHO-CTG-Followup.md#whey-and-soy-protein-registration)

## Resistant starch registration

NCT02784145 first posted on May 26, 2016. RESISTA-PD prospectively compared eight weeks of resistant starch supplementation in Parkinson and healthy groups with dietary instructions alone in a separate Parkinson group. Outcomes included constipation, fecal short-chain fatty acids, and microbiota. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02784145) [Local registration](Source-Text/Trials/NCT02784145.txt) [2021 outcome](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-WHO-CTG-Followup.md#resistant-starch-and-bowel-outcomes)

## Ketogenic crossover registration in Padua

NCT02687698 first posted on February 22, 2016. The University of Padova registered a randomized crossover of an eight-week lipid- and protein-controlled ketogenic diet and standard diet, with energy expenditure, body composition, and Parkinson symptoms among the planned measures. The archived registry gives unknown status and no posted results. The 2005 VanItallie feasibility study cited by the registration is earlier independent work. An exact identifier search located no linked outcome for the Padua trial. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02687698) [Local registration](Source-Text/Trials/NCT02687698.txt) [Earlier independent study, PMID 15728303](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15728303/)

## Isolated DHA and dyskinesia results

NCT01563913 first posted results on May 29, 2018. The registry records 33 enrolled and forceplate-defined dyskinesia in 3 of 9 assessed DHA participants and 6 of 15 assessed placebo participants. There were withdrawals and incomplete visits, and the table supplies no between-group estimate or confidence interval. Isolated DHA and this incomplete outcome do not establish an effect of a culinary fish or oil on dyskinesia. [Posted results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01563913?tab=results) [Local outcome table](../2010-2014/Source-Text/Trials/NCT01563913.txt#L359)


## RASMET and KARMET registrations and early ENT-01 outcomes

RASMET first posted on February 9, 2017 (NCT03047629). Hauser and colleagues published the phase 2a results on July 2, 2019. Fifty people with Parkinson’s and constipation enrolled and 44 received the synthetic squalamine salt. In the repeated-dose stage, complete spontaneous bowel movements increased from 1.2 to 3.6 per week during treatment (p < .001). Nausea occurred in 21 of 44 treated patients and diarrhea in 18 of 44. The open-label report describes reduced arm or leg thrashing during sleep and improved hallucinations or delusions in five of six participants who had them. Those observations were exploratory and had no parallel placebo comparison. The results are for a synthetic drug, with no comparison of fish or fish oil intake. [RASMET registry and local text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT03047629.txt) · [2019 primary article and licensed local text](Source-Text/PMC8288812.txt#L102)

The larger KARMET registration first posted December 20, 2018 (NCT03781791). It specified a randomized, blinded placebo comparison in Parkinson constipation. The official record shows 151 enrolled and first posted its summary results on December 4, 2023. The earlier 2022 journal paper reports 150 treated participants. [KARMET registry and posted results](Source-Text/Trials/NCT03781791.txt) · [Dated randomized outcomes](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-WHO-CTG-Followup.md#randomized-and-rollover-ent-01-outcomes)

## ENT-01 Parkinson dementia registration

The open-label NCT03938922 registration first posted May 6, 2019. It specified a 10-week dementia severity outcome and secondary MoCA, psychosis, neuropsychiatric and quality-of-life measures. The March 1, 2024 revision lists the study as withdrawn and reports no results. The separate phase 2 follow-up publicized by Parkinson’s UK in 2022 was paused by 2024 because the sponsor could not secure sufficient investment. [Official registration and local text](Source-Text/Trials/NCT03938922.txt) · [Parkinson’s UK 2022 announcement](https://www.parkinsons.org.uk/news/2025/were-partnering-enterin-to-develop-potential-new-treatment-for-parkinsons-dementia) · [2024 funder update, p. 20](https://www.parkinsons.org.uk/sites/default/files/2024-08/PUK1837_Progress%20magazine_April%2024_Web%20%282%29.pdf#page=20)
