# Enteral feeding and nutrition in the 2015 Japanese clinical nutrition meeting

## Ginger-containing Kampo extract and swallowing

Kohashi and colleagues reported ten patients with dysphagia, including eight with cerebrovascular disease, one with Alzheimer disease and one with Parkinson’s. All received hangekobokuto by mouth or feeding tube. Six of eight inpatients had no reported pneumonia recurrence, and several resumed some oral intake. The report gives pooled findings and does not identify the Parkinson participant’s outcome or route. The Japanese formula standard names ginger and perilla leaf among five constituents. The report did not test culinary ginger or measure rigidity. Kawakami’s separate progressive supranuclear palsy case on the same page gives bolus-specific swallow scores after the formula. An earlier Parkinson-specific study tested the formula and measured swallowing-reflex latency. [2015 S10-04 and S10-01](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/30/1/30_223/_pdf/-char/ja#page=16) · [Formula standard](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/web/t_doc?dataId=00tb4640&dataType=1) · [2000 Parkinson report](../2000-2004/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#a-perilla-and-ginger-formula-and-the-swallowing-reflex)

## Oral Racol during lymphoma treatment

P-0218 describes a patient with Parkinson’s, marked fatigue and severe weight loss during lymphoma treatment. The nutrition team added oral Racol between meals. The patient was able to drink the supplement daily and later walked and went home. The report concerns concurrent chemotherapy and nutrition support. It gives no fatigue scale or Parkinson symptom contrast by exposure. [2015 P-0218, page 56](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/30/1/30_283/_pdf/-char/ja#page=56)

## Texture-adjusted menu in Parkinson with dementia

P-0667 describes a 91-year-old with Alzheimer disease and a history of Parkinson’s whose intake stabilized after a texture-adjusted iEAT menu, utensil and seating changes, nutrition support, and medication adjustment. The report names a product line and gives no specific menu item, speech assessment or food-specific outcome. The niacin and dementia studies on the same page concern other interventions. [2015 P-0667, page 67](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/30/1/30_384/_pdf/-char/ja#page=67)

## Pectin-containing formula and tube feeding

Seven patients who had trouble continuing their existing gastrostomy feeding, including patients with Parkinson’s, received a pectin-containing formula that gelled in the stomach. Five were reported to have fewer complications and established feeding. Diagnosis-specific counts and outcomes were absent. Nearby reports describe other gastric feeding groups. [2015 P-0678, page 70](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/30/1/30_384/_pdf/-char/ja#page=70)

## Agar-gelled feeding after stroke in Parkinson

P-0690 describes bench testing of a commercial agar gelling agent and two gastrostomy cases. An 83-year-old with Parkinson’s and prior cerebral hemorrhage had gradually fewer vomiting episodes after agar-gelled feeds were introduced. A separate post-stroke case reported less reflux. Both were uncontrolled, and the Parkinson case gives no repeated swallowing test or stand-alone ingredient effect. The earlier Okuda print title addresses bowel movements in a Parkinson-related population and describes a separate clinical question. [2015 P-0690, page 73](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/30/1/30_384/_pdf/-char/ja#page=73) · [2007 Okuda title](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-NHO-2007-Access.md#agar-gelled-feeding-and-bowel-movements)

## Inhaled black pepper oil during swallowing rehabilitation

Yokoyama and colleagues followed six patients at Kiba Hospital with aspiration pneumonia history or cerebrovascular dysphagia. They used a cloth carrying black pepper aroma oil during swallowing rehabilitation for a mean of 61.7 days. Repetitive saliva swallowing and dysphagia grade improved on the authors’ before-and-after tests (p = .012 and .045). The modified water swallow and food tests showed no detected differences. Four participants resumed some oral intake. The abstract reports no comparison group or documented Parkinson participants. The neighboring eight Parkinson patients belonged to an electrical-stimulation study. The oil exposure was inhaled. [2015 P-0712, page 78](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/30/1/30_384/_pdf/-char/ja#page=78) · [2016 Parkinson black pepper oil report](Food-Experiment-JSDR-Legacy-Later.md#2016-inhaled-black-pepper-oil-and-swallowing-in-parkinson-with-lewy-body-dementia)

## Oral texture progression after tube feeding diarrhea

An 82-year-old with Parkinson’s and cirrhosis had persistent diarrhea while receiving several tube formulas, including a soy-protein formula. With multidisciplinary care and swallowing rehabilitation she progressed from jelly to paste and soft meals. The report describes more speech and resolution of diarrhea after return to oral intake, without an ingredient-level or repeated speech assessment. [2015 P-0867, page 17](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/30/1/30_484/_pdf/-char/ja#page=17)

## Tube feeding and levodopa monitoring after gastrostomy

One patient with Parkinson’s, spinal cord injury and epilepsy received staged formula feeds after gastrostomy. The team drew blood around feeding and adjusted levodopa while managing nutrition. The abstract gives no named foods, concentration values or daily OFF-duration comparison. [2015 P-1078, page 70](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/30/1/30_484/_pdf/-char/ja#page=70)

## Underweight and prognosis at Utano

Park and colleagues studied underweight status in 93 of 111 Parkinson patients seen from May 2012 through September 2013. Twenty-two were underweight. Female sex and dyskinesia had adjusted associations with underweight, with reported odds ratios of 3.6 and 3.4. No individual food or intake was evaluated. A later survival paper came from the same hospital. Its design and accrual interval need comparison before treating it as the same cohort. [2015 P-1226, page 107](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/30/1/30_484/_pdf/-char/ja#page=107) · [2018 survival paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29784560/)

## Named snacks during home swallowing rehabilitation

Yamada and colleagues followed a 72-year-old with stage IV Parkinson’s and possible sarcopenic dysphagia after hospitalization. Home care combined exercises, oral motor training, texture adjustment, altered lunch timing and more calories. The report names ice cream, cream puffs and sweet red-bean soup as examples of snacks during the first month. By day 121, weight was 51.1 kg from 39.0 kg and Functional Oral Intake Scale rose from 4 to 7. The foods were examples within a combined program and no food-specific results were measured. Sweet red beans are a different exposure from boiled black beans. [Original 2018 full report](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/prm/3/0/3_20180019/_html/-char/en)

## Elemental tube feeding and chronic intestinal pseudo-obstruction

Sakiyama and colleagues described a 76-year-old with advanced Parkinson’s and chronic intestinal pseudo-obstruction. Marked gas accumulation followed resumption of a semi-elemental feed, which was stopped on hospital day five. A continuous elemental feed was started on day 28 amid treatment for other complications. Abdominal distension improved around day 60 and bowel dilation and peripheral edema improved by day 80. The report gives no formula ingredient, quantified constipation outcome or controlled comparison. An October case article from the same institution includes overlapping authors and appears to describe the same patient. Its body is open for follow-up. [Original February abstract P-1017, page 55](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/30/1/30_484/_pdf/-char/ja#page=55) · [October publication listing](https://www.ishiyaku.co.jp/search/detail/746320) · [Presenting institution’s bibliography](https://www.hospital-city.izumi.kagoshima.jp/med/gakkai.html)

## Thickened nasogastric feeding after aspiration pneumonia

Ishii reported one patient with Parkinson’s hospitalized for aspiration pneumonia and judged unable to eat safely by mouth. Fever followed the first administration of a GFO packet in unthickened warm water through a nasogastric tube. When GFO was restarted with an unspecified thickener, no reflux was reported. A semi-elemental formula preceded by a viscosity-adjusting food was then used to continue tube feeding. The abstract supplies no oral food, aspiration recurrence measure or independent comparison. Historical product material identifies glutamine, dietary fiber and lactosucrose in GFO, which was used in both sequences. Its later formulation changed. [Original P-1102, page 76](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/30/1/30_484/_pdf/-char/ja#page=76) · [Historical product material](https://www.peg.or.jp/lecture/enteral_nutrition/04-01.html) · [Manufacturer renewal notice](https://www.otsukakj.jp/healthcare/medicalfoods/gfo/gfo_info.pdf)

## Indirect calorimetry by Parkinson stage

Yoshida and colleagues measured 13 patients with Parkinson’s in the ON medication state. The basal energy estimates in Hoehn and Yahr stages II–III and IV–V were 1,161 ± 191 and 1,001 ± 255 kcal/day (p = .268). Resting estimates were 1,315 ± 185 and 1,250 ± 435 kcal/day (p = .748). The comparisons were inconclusive, and the study measured energy expenditure without testing a food. [Original P-1167, page 92](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/30/1/30_484/_pdf/-char/ja#page=92)
