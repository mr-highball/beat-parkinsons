# Fig fruit exposure and symptom outcomes, 2015–2019

## Dried figs and olive oil in a scopolamine memory model

Alharthy and Bawazir studied five groups of ten male rats. Animals in relevant arms received an oral formulation made from dried figs and olive oil over four weeks in a scopolamine amnesia model. The authors report improvements on maze behavior and lower hippocampal acetylcholinesterase activity. The food appeared in a mixture, and the model and outcomes concern memory and cholinergic effects. The experiment supplies no visual hallucination outcome and cannot establish a whole dried fig effect in Parkinson disease. ## Fig and bowel outcomes in irritable bowel syndrome

Pourmasoumi et al. randomized 150 adults with constipation-predominant irritable bowel syndrome to fig, flixweed or usual diet. The publisher excerpt reports 142 completers. Some bowel outcomes and pain frequency favored the fig group. Pain severity and C-reactive protein did not clearly differ. The discussion suggests dried fruit. The intervention methods and outcome tables remain open. The trial did not measure visual events. [Primary report](Source-Text/2018-Pourmasoumi-Fig-IBS.txt) · [Registered design](Source-Text/Trials/NCT02559245.txt)

## Dried fig preparations in animal studies

One extract sourced from dried figs lowered blood pressure in rat models. A separate feeding study reported less ethanol-associated liver injury. Its same-author lung and muscle follow-up found a mixed response across oxidative markers. The animal groups may have been reused. These outcomes supply no visual episode estimates. [Blood pressure study](Source-Text/2017-PMID28187689-Dried-Fig-Extract-Blood-Pressure.txt) · [Liver study](Source-Text/2016-PMID27268385-Dried-Fig-Rat-Liver-Study.txt) · [Lung and muscle study](Source-Text/2018-Turan-Celik-Dried-Fig-Lung-and-Muscle.txt)
