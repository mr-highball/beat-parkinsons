# Spanish-language dietary research follow-up

## 2016 swallowing and nutrition observations in a Havana Parkinson clinic

Jardines García and colleagues reviewed 40 adults aged 60 or older seen at the Havana Instituto de Neurología y Neurocirugía nutrition service in 2010–2011. They reported constipation in 34 people, dysphagia with liquids in 19 and dysphagia with solids in 17. The paper does not specify whether the two dysphagia groups overlap or identify test foods, potato preparation, aspiration counts, or outcomes from the texture changes discussed. The report cites the 2009 nutrition proposal. The issue is labeled January–June 2016 and the article lists August 10, 2016 acceptance. [Original report, pp. 175–180](https://www.medigraphic.com/pdfs/revcubalnut/can-2016/can161n.pdf) · [2009 proposal](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Spanish-Primary-Followup.md#2009-havana-proposal-for-parkinson-nutrition-care)

## 2016 biscuit portions and liquid boluses in Mexican Parkinson endoscopy

Rubio-Grayeb and colleagues compared cervical auscultation and flexible endoscopy in five volunteers with primary Parkinson’s. The tests used quarter and half biscuits and liquid, nectar and pudding consistencies at 5–20 mL. The article does not identify the biscuit recipe or liquid ingredients. Figure 2 records penetration with aspiration for one 10 mL nectar trial and one 5 mL liquid trial. One quarter-biscuit trial showed penetration without aspiration. The half-biscuit bars show no penetration or aspiration among four tested, with the fifth withheld for safety. The 20 mL liquid bar sums to six despite five participants, which limits interpretation of that bar. Table II summarizes 70 paired bolus opportunities from the five participants. The earlier Mexican study used a separate reported recruitment period and included milk. No potato was tested in either report. [Original article, Figure 2 and Table II](https://www.medigraphic.com/pdfs/audiologia/fon-2016/fon163b.pdf#page=4) · [Earlier Mexican report](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Multilingual-Followup.md)

## 2017 clinical volume and viscosity assessment in Barbastro

Zamora Mur and colleagues enrolled 266 people evaluated for suspected dysphagia in a Spanish geriatric service. Parkinsonism was the indication for 24 of those assessments. The test used liquid, nectar and pudding consistencies, and 228 people were diagnosed with dysphagia across the full cohort. The report includes swallowing and follow-up aspiration findings but does not stratify them by parkinsonism diagnosis, identify idiopathic Parkinson’s separately, or name a tested food or thickener. The original paper first appeared April 28, 2017 and the issue was published in April 2018. [Publisher full report, pp. 168–173](https://www.elsevier.es/es-revista-medicina-familia-semergen-40-pdf-download-S1138359317300783) · [Publication record, PMID 28457769](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28457769/)

## 2016 observed potato and pea purée preparation in Chiclayo

Aguinaga Torres, Llamo Vilcherrez and Vega Ramírez presented this ethnographic study at the November 11–12, 2016 nursing meeting in Granada. The full conference report follows 11 family caregivers of people with stage IV Parkinson’s in Chiclayo, Peru. Researchers used the Hoehn and Yahr scale to determine eligibility and observed care at home. In one visit, they watched a caregiver shred cooked chicken, mash potato, peas and squash from a soup with a fork, and mix the ingredients with broth. The report records how the meal was prepared. It gives no swallowing observation after that meal and no baked potato exposure. [Full conference report, methods and section II](https://www.index-f.com/para/n25/pdf/217.pdf#page=7)

In separate interviews a caregiver reported adding more water to white rice, arroz árabe and brown rice to make eating easier. Other caregivers reported serving cooked or blended broad beans, sweet potato and deboned fried fish. These are caregiver accounts of preparation and use, with no scored swallowing or motor response. [Full conference report, section II](https://www.index-f.com/para/n25/pdf/217.pdf#page=10)

## 2019 public release of the Quito food frequency and dysphagia study

Eliana Paulina Maldonado Garrido’s Pontificia Universidad Católica del Ecuador thesis is dated July 2018. Its archived repository record gives January 25, 2019 as the date it became available. The current repository record has a later accession date. The thesis uses hospital charts for 25 adults aged 65–90 who already had Parkinson’s and dysphagia. Figure 8 and Table 10 report 20 mild, three moderate and two severe cases. Figure 7 lists 80% liquid, 12% solid and 8% mixed dysphagia. Tables 8, 9 and 12 instead give 19/25 (76%) liquid, 3/25 (12%) solid and 3/25 (12%) mixed. [Primary thesis, Figures 7–8 and Tables 8–12](https://repositorio.puce.edu.ec/server/api/core/bitstreams/977a488f-dc6a-49ce-be1b-b33512c1e8b3/content#page=51) · [Archived original handle 22000/15685](https://web.archive.org/web/20200616013838id_/http://repositorio.puce.edu.ec/handle/22000/15685) · [Current repository item](https://repositorio.puce.edu.ec/items/b4647f88-0759-4186-800e-8cfb9180c467/full)

Table 14 gives dietary frequencies for the whole group. Twelve of 25 ate potatoes weekly and 13 monthly. Seventeen ate white bread daily and 21 ate rice daily. The table identifies neither potato preparation nor individual food intake by dysphagia severity. Table 10 compares BMI category with dysphagia grade (p = .67). Table 9 compares calf circumference with dysphagia type (p = .01). These results give no estimate for baked potato and swallowing severity in claim 0290. The repository provides the thesis under its distribution terms. No eligible Creative Commons reuse license was located. [Primary thesis, Table 14 and questionnaire](https://repositorio.puce.edu.ec/server/api/core/bitstreams/977a488f-dc6a-49ce-be1b-b33512c1e8b3/content#page=81) · [Repository license record](https://repositorio.puce.edu.ec/server/api/core/bundles/7a0243de-a8fb-48c2-8ff3-1c00db108965/bitstreams)

## 2019 Guayaquil nutritional assessment original tables

The Universidad Católica de Santiago de Guayaquil issued the Reyes Guerrero and Romero Baidal thesis on September 20, 2019 and lists it as publicly available October 10, 2019. The original repository record and PDF are reachable by HTTP. The cross-sectional study included 65 people with confirmed Parkinson’s disease. Table 4 classifies 14 as malnourished, 43 at risk and eight normal by Mini Nutritional Assessment (MNA). Table 7 gives normal BMI in 13 of 14 malnourished and 28 of 43 at-risk participants. It reports p = .041 for the BMI-category and MNA-category comparison. BMI is also a component of the MNA score. [Primary thesis, Tables 4–7](http://repositorio.ucsg.edu.ec/bitstream/3317/13572/1/T-UCSG-PRE-MED-896.pdf#page=26) · [Repository release date and license](http://repositorio.ucsg.edu.ec/handle/3317/13572?mode=full) · [OpenAlex W2981790794](https://openalex.org/W2981790794)

The tables analyze age, sex, BMI and MNA categories. The appendices reproduce the assessment instrument and aggregate charts. The report gives no item-level food intake, physical activity analysis, or link between eating a named food and change in swallowing or motor symptoms. A [later Piura thesis](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Multilingual-Followup.md#2025-nutritional-status-and-motor-severity-in-piura) attributes a physical activity finding to the Guayaquil study that does not appear in the original results. The repository specifies CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. [Primary thesis, Methods, Discussion and Annex 1](http://repositorio.ucsg.edu.ec/bitstream/3317/13572/1/T-UCSG-PRE-MED-896.pdf)

## 2017 reported coffee and prescribed levodopa in Costa Rican charts

Torrealba Acosta, Carazo Céspedes and Fornaguera Trías retrospectively reviewed 129 charts from the Parkinson movement disorders clinic at Hospital San Juan de Dios in San José, Costa Rica. The January–June 2017 article records whether patients drank coffee and the reported cups. It gives no brewing method. The report says coffee, postural instability, olfactory disorder and sleep disorders predicted higher prescribed levodopa dose, while female sex predicted a lower dose. The complete model explained 35.3% of variation in dose. The paper gives no coffee-specific coefficient or confidence interval. Its separate UPDRS model found no significant associations and its MoCA model did not report a coffee term. No fatigue measure was reported. The same-site later analysis is described in the [2020 coffee and cognition note](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Spanish-Primary-Followup.md#2020-reported-coffee-and-moca-in-a-costa-rican-clinic). [Primary report, Methods, Table 2 and regression results](https://www.neuroeje.com/articulos/83.pdf) · [University thesis record](https://kerwa.ucr.ac.cr/items/3cb01445-00a4-45d1-983d-a2ae650ed81b)

## 2019 coffee history in the María Auxiliadora case-control study

Lozano, Palacios and Loza’s April–June 2019 issue report reanalyzed 115 recent Parkinson diagnoses and 115 acute-care controls at a Peruvian hospital. Coffee was a yes/no chart variable from patient reports, with no preparation, quantity or duration. Table 1 gives 31 of 115 cases and 19 of 115 controls reporting coffee. The unadjusted diagnosis odds ratio was 1.86 (95% CI 0.98–3.54, p = .06). The multivariable table uses coffee as an adjustment variable in the overweight analysis and leaves the adjusted coffee coefficient unreported. The outcome is diagnosis. The parent dataset is attributed to an Astrid Loza Ávalos thesis, with its full text unresolved. SciELO links the journal’s CC BY-NC 4.0 license. [Primary report, methods and Tables 1–2](https://www.scielo.org.pe/pdf/amp/v36n2/a05v36n2.pdf#page=3) · [SciELO article and license](https://www.scielo.org.pe/scielo.php?pid=S1728-59172019000200005&script=sci_arttext)

## 2019 coffee and Parkinson covariates in a Cuban hip fracture series

Mesa Fernández et al. reviewed 453 older adults admitted to one Cuban hospital with a fall-related osteoporotic hip fracture. Coffee and Parkinson diagnosis appear as separate covariates in Table 3. The study reports no beverage preparation or coffee-by-Parkinson estimate. The table is headed as a description of fracture patients, while the text interprets some odds as risk of osteoporosis or further fracture. The sampling description includes only patients with an index fracture, and the comparison group for those estimates is unclear. The October–December issue prints a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license. [Primary paper, methods and Table 3](https://www.medigraphic.com/pdfs/medicadelcentro/mec-2019/mec194e.pdf#page=3)

## 2019 making filtered coffee in a Parkinson occupational assessment

Elena Díaz Cáceres defended the University of Oviedo thesis on October 10, 2019. The thesis reports an overall cohort of 60 people with Parkinson’s and 51 controls. The study observed how participants carried out two cooking tasks. F-4 required a grilled cheese sandwich and a drink. D-2 is titled scrambled or fried eggs, toast and boiled or filtered coffee or tea. Its actual instructions required a scrambled egg, two slices of toast with jam and coffee made through a disposable paper filter in an electric drip brewer. Espresso and single-cup pod machines were excluded by the task rules. [Primary thesis, §§4.3.2.2.1–2, printed pp. 67–72](https://digibuo.uniovi.es/dspace/bitstream/handle/10651/54050/TD_ElenaDiazCaceres.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1#page=83) · [University record and defense date](https://portalinvestigacion.uniovi.es/documentos/5e6979cc299952076996a0ad)

The task comparison reports lower motor and process scores in the Parkinson group (t with 83 df = 8.757 and 4.471, respectively, both p < .001). These measures describe cooking performance. The thesis gives no assigned coffee drinking, fatigue comparison or post-meal symptom change. [Primary thesis, §5.12 and Table 36](https://digibuo.uniovi.es/dspace/handle/10651/54050/TD_ElenaDiazCaceres.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)

## Thickened contrast in the Mataró Parkinson cohort

Sanz Cartagena’s 2017 dissertation describes 40 people with Parkinson disease who underwent clinical and videofluoroscopic testing. The clinical tests used mineral water with and without Resource ThickenUp starch. The fluoroscopy tests used thickened water and contrast. Table 20 reports penetration in 15 of 40 OFF medication and 19 of 40 ON, with no aspiration in either state. Oral residue affected 37 and 36 people, respectively. The dissertation describes improved swallow safety with the pudding-viscosity bolus and no clear medication-state effect. [Dissertation methods, pp. 74–76](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/tesis/2017/hdl_10803_457866/mpsc1de1.pdf#page=74) · [Table 20, p. 94](https://ddd.uab.cat/pub/tesis/2017/hdl_10803_457866/mpsc1de1.pdf#page=94)

Nascimento and colleagues’ 2019 Lisbon abstract reports 50 Parkinson participants, including 40 tested in both medication states, and 12 controls. It reproduces several exact results from the dissertation. The 2020 paper has matching group counts and substantially matching clinical results. The dissertation and final paper differ on age and the timing of the ON assessment, leaving exact participant linkage open. None used potato as a test food. [2019 Lisbon proceedings, p. 25](https://eu-central-1.linodeobjects.com/evt4-media/documents/Atas_Disfagia_2019.pdf#page=25) · [2020 journal report](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Spanish-Primary-Followup.md#thickened-contrast-in-the-published-mataró-cohort)
