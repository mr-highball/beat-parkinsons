# Cooking oil and Parkinson diet comparison follow-up

## Phillips Parkinson diet trial registration and primary appendix

ACTRN12617000027314 was submitted December 19, 2016 and registered January 9, 2017. ANZCTR’s public record associates the trial with Waikato Hospital and a final enrollment of 47. The 2018 paper compares two menus for eight weeks and reports MDS-UPDRS scores by part. Wiley lists four supplements. The study appendix contains recipe sources, menu changes, and Table S1 with sensitivity analyses for the four part totals. The other files contain the two standard diet plans and a CONSORT checklist. The conference publication repeats the same cohort and outcome comparisons. Its figures and tables do not supply a public participant-level dataset. ## Virgin coconut oil on the ketogenic menu and Parkinson urinary problems

The 2018 ketogenic diet plan calls for virgin coconut oil in a tea or coffee preparation, and several recipes name coconut oil. The Parkinson pilot reported a 0.70-point larger decrease in the broader MDS-UPDRS urinary-problems item for the ketogenic group versus the low-fat group. The assignment changed a whole diet and individual oil intake was not published. The urinary score includes several bladder symptoms and gives no urgency episode count. The direction of the broad score favors the diet containing the oil, while the independent effect of consuming virgin coconut oil on urgency remains unresolved. ## Preliminary report on dietary glycation and handgrip in Parkinson disease

Almeida and colleagues’ 2019 proceedings paper, pages 145–146, reports a 2018 symposium presentation. It analyzed 20 Parkinson participants and 20 controls using three dietary recalls, skin autofluorescence, MoCA, the Short Physical Performance Battery and handgrip. It lists ethics identifier CAAE 79991417.9.0000.0089. Mean total estimated dietary AGE was 18,311 ± 6,458 kU/day in Parkinson participants versus 24,544 ± 13,384 in controls (p=.07). The paper reports an inverse association with handgrip in Parkinson participants (r=−.26, printed p<.05). If computed as an unadjusted two-sided Pearson test on the 20 Parkinson participants, that magnitude would not reach the reported threshold. The 2024 publication names the same ethics approval and reproduces sample sizes and estimated AGE means. It prints a different coefficient. The 2019 report does not identify a cooking oil or report frying frequency.
