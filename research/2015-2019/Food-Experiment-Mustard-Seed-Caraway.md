# Mustard seed and caraway research

## Black mustard seed extract in drug-induced motor models

Paradkar and Sarawade compared an oral hydroalcoholic black mustard seed extract with chlorpromazine alone in rats. Day-21 mean actophotometer counts were 186.7 with extract and 48.33 with chlorpromazine alone, and mean block-test catalepsy scores were 0.833 and 1.917. The tables report six rats per group and significant differences. A separate dexamethasone experiment in the paper also measured spontaneous activity. Neither part reports falls, actual stride lengths, involuntary dyskinesias, rest tremor or postural responses. The two models and clinical endpoints remain distinct. [Primary article, Tables 1–4](https://wjpr.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/article_issue/1433488524.pdf)

## Black mustard seed extract and mouse tail suspension

Parthiban and colleagues reported tail-suspension results from groups of six mice. In Table 1, mean immobility was 233.00 seconds with saline and 109.16 seconds with the highest injected ethanolic seed extract dose. The combined fluoxetine and extract arm had no clear difference from fluoxetine alone (p = .758). The introduction refers to a forced-swim test, but the reported table is mouse tail suspension. Immobility under this test leaves clinical depression and eating ground mustard unresolved. [Primary article, Table 1 and Results](https://www.rjpbcs.com/pdf/2015_6%286%29/%5B23%5D.pdf)

## Mustard as an odor or flavor test stimulus

Zucco and colleagues presented a mustard-labeled odor diluted in distilled water to 12 people with early unilateral Parkinson’s and 12 controls. Table 1 verifies the stimulus, while the published main result pools odor scores and reports no separate mustard effect. De Rosa and colleagues tested flavor, odor and taste in 38 Parkinson participants and 36 controls in a separate cohort. Their Table 2 reports 34% versus 39% correct for mustard flavor (p = .809). The mustard item was a flavor-test stimulus. Neither study measures a change in clinical smell impairment after eating ground mustard. [Zucco local primary text, Table 1](Source-Text/PMC4503885.txt#L108) · [De Rosa primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30895397/) · [De Rosa article copy, Table 2](https://www.academia.edu/108980062/The_flavor_test_is_a_sensitive_tool_in_identifying_the_flavor_sensorineural_dysfunction_in_Parkinson_s_disease)

## Caraway in a Parkinson spice discrimination panel

Flisar, Trost and Brglez reported a matching-by-smell task in 30 people with Parkinson’s and 30 age- and sex-matched controls. A five-odor set that included caraway reached 80% sensitivity and 80% specificity for group discrimination. The report gives a separate correct-response proportion for coffee only. No separate caraway accuracy, cooking method or intake was reported. The Ljubljana study uses a different sample and matching task from the later Belgian study. [MDS 2017 abstract 115](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/home-made-olfactory-spice-discrimination-test-for-pd/)

## Caraway preparations and mouse rotarod

Showraki and colleagues injected a caraway seed aqueous extract or essential oil in a seizure experiment and separately measured rotarod retention before and after injection. Neither preparation significantly changed rotarod performance compared with its vehicle. The exposure, motor task and population do not address stride, freezing, tremor or hand function after caraway consumption. [Primary article and rotarod figure](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4876298/) · [WHO-hosted primary article copy](https://applications.emro.who.int/imemrf/Iran_J_Med_Sci/Iran_J_Med_Sci_2016_41_3_200_208.pdf)

## Caraway and coriander in an allergic hypotension case

Canabal and colleagues reported anaphylaxis with blood pressure of 76/52 mmHg after a mixed meal containing blood sausage. The authors attributed the episode to caraway and coriander in the sausage using the product formulation and immunoblot inhibition. The original case does not report dysphagia or an orthostatic measurement. This is evidence of an allergic reaction to a mixed food outside Parkinson’s. [Primary case, pp. 200–201](https://www.jiaci.org/revistas/vol26issue3_6_8.pdf)
