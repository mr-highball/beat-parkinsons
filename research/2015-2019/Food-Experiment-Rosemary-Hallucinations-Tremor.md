# Rosemary in Lumbee ethnobotanical interviews

De Rus Jacquet and colleagues published February 15, 2017. Researchers interviewed Lumbee traditional healers and other local participants about plants used for symptoms including tremor and mental concerns, and separately studied extracts in cell models. The rosemary entry in Table 1 describes leaves and a stem-and-leaf tea, with reported traditional uses for nonspecific nervous complaints and as a stimulant, among other uses. It does not attribute rest-tremor treatment or hallucination reduction to rosemary, count those symptoms, establish the participants had Parkinson disease, or compare outcomes after rosemary. The article's spelling of the plant name is Rosmarinus officinialis; the accepted synonym Rosmarinus officinalis refers to culinary rosemary. A tea and experimental aqueous extract also differ from dried leaves eaten as a food.
