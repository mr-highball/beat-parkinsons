# Ground clove and Parkinson symptom evidence

## Ground clove in an extruded snack and immediate emotion ratings

Human consumers tasted extruded apple-based snacks with 0%, 4% or 5% of a seasoning blend. Ground clove supplied 6% of the blend along with ground cinnamon, dried ginger and nutmeg. The 5% blend was associated with an increase in the food-evoked satisfied rating. The experiment captured acute emotion labels and product liking. A separate ten-person clinical arm compared antioxidant capacity and blood glucose after 4% blend or control. No diagnosed Parkinson sample, sustained apathy score or ground-clove-only arm was reported. [Bell full article](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/foods/foods-06-00070/article_deploy/foods-06-00070.pdf).

## Parkinson apathy and composite olfactory testing

The study evaluated 96 people with Parkinson disease and 51 controls. Apathy and motor scores were associated with composite olfactory identification, discrimination and TDI in regression. Exposure was odor testing. The accessible abstract neither identifies a separate clove test item association nor reports dietary clove intake. [Masala author abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00415-018-8913-9).

## Eugenol odor and judgments of other people's faces

Isolated eugenol served as the clove-like comparison odor and a mask for hexanal. Study 1 randomly assigned 90 women to odor groups in a trust game. Study 2 measured how 35 women rated pictures of other people's faces. The latter ratings were higher with masked hexanal than with eugenol alone. The effect is a comparison for adding hexanal in a sensory experiment. Facial motor function or expression produced by the participants was not measured and no culinary spice was consumed. [Van Nieuwenburg full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6710396/).
