# Spice evidence follow-up, 2015–2019

## Co-loaded curcumin and piperine nanoparticles in mice

Kundu and colleagues first published online in October 2016. They tested nanoparticles containing both isolated curcumin and piperine. The abstract reports improved motor coordination in a rotenone mouse model and changes in cell and neuronal outcomes. It does not give separate estimates for the two compounds or a culinary black pepper or turmeric comparison. [Primary abstract, PMID 27642670](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27642670/)

## Oral piperine and motor-task performance in Brazilian rats

Correia and colleagues published on October 28, 2015. They gave isolated oral piperine at 5 or 10 mg/kg for two weeks in a 6-OHDA rat model. Figure 1 shows approximately 4, 11 and 8 open-field crossings in five minutes for untreated lesioned, 5 mg/kg and 10 mg/kg groups, respectively. Its caption marks the 5 mg/kg comparison with the untreated lesioned group as significant. Figure 2 shows about 2.4, 0.4 and 0.5 rotarod falls per minute for the same groups. The rotarod comparisons with untreated lesioned animals gave p < .01 and p < .05 for 5 and 10 mg/kg, respectively. These are figure readings from two tasks measuring spontaneous movement and coordination. They leave clinical outcomes after culinary black pepper unresolved. [Primary publisher report, Figures 1 and 2](https://nobleresearch.org/Doi/10.14312/2397-1304.2015-1) · [Local licensed full text](Source-Text/2015-Correia-Piperine-Rats.txt#L354)

## Capsaicin injection in MPTP mice

Chung and colleagues published this study on March 3, 2017. Injected capsaicin increased mean rotarod retention in MPTP mice from 9.62 ± 0.53 to 16.27 ± 0.45 minutes (mean ± SEM, p < .001). The experiment also assessed striatal dopamine and glial responses. The rotarod endpoint is a motor coordination proxy. The route, isolated compound, and model leave ground cayenne and Parkinson’s dyskinesia (`0640`) unresolved. [Primary report, Figure 2h, PMID 28255166](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5382554/)

## Piperine with quercetin in 6-OHDA rats

Singh and Kumar first published online on October 19, 2017. Oral quercetin alone and with isolated piperine reduced rotational and motor deficits in a 6-OHDA rat model. The abstract describes no piperine-only comparison. The study gives no insomnia or dream enactment outcome for culinary black pepper. [Primary abstract, PMID 29056550](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29056550/)

## Ginger and enteric tissue outcomes in MPTP mice

Huh and colleagues first published online on September 19, 2018, ahead of the June 2020 issue. They gave ginger and isolated 6-shogaol in separate oral gavage groups and reported changes in tight-junction proteins, inflammation, and enteric dopaminergic neurons after MPTP. The accessible abstract does not report stool or transit outcomes. The study supplies related intestinal evidence for `0581`, without a grated ginger constipation estimate. [Primary abstract, PMID 30230979](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30230979/)

## Oral curcumin and depression-like behavior in rats

Madiha and Haider first published on November 30, 2018. They administered isolated curcumin orally before or after rotenone exposure. The abstract reports improvement in forced-swim, open-field, sucrose-preference, and social-interaction outcomes and changes in monoamine levels. A later rat study cites this article for improvements in catalepsy and narrow-beam latency. The accessible primary abstract gives no estimates for those tests, and the full report was unavailable for checking that citation. The available outcomes concern depression-like behavior in a toxin model and leave the turmeric preparation and clinical symptoms unresolved. [Primary abstract, PMID 30506334](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30506334/) [Citing local passage](../2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC11567558.txt#L553)

## Isolated thymoquinone in MPTP mice

Haque and colleagues reported oxidative and inflammatory findings at the July 2018 World Congress of Basic and Clinical Pharmacology. J-STAGE lists the proceedings publication year as 2018 and its own posting date as September 10, 2020. A 2019 journal report with overlapping authors and model details adds dopaminergic neuronal and alpha-synuclein results. Neither report supplies a nigella-seed or depressive behavior estimate for `0019`. [Primary congress abstract PO4-1-129](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpssuppl/WCP2018/0/WCP2018_PO4-1-129/_article) [Primary journal abstract, PMID 31028778](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31028778/)

## Isolated curcumin and levodopa dyskinesia in mice

The 2018 study tested isolated curcumin, anacardic acid, and garcinol in separate groups of 6-OHDA-lesioned mice receiving levodopa. The curcumin group had a lower combined axial, limb, and orofacial dyskinesia score. The abstract reports preserved early motor response to levodopa. The intervention and model give no culinary turmeric estimate for clinical rigidity or bradykinesia. [Primary abstract, PMID 29998409](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29998409/)

## Thomas Jefferson oral supplement arm with curcumin

Thomas Jefferson University’s NCT02445651 first posted May 15, 2015. The trial included an oral combination of curcumin phytosome, baicalin, reishi extract and omega-3 in people with Parkinson’s. The sponsor’s May 17, 2018 protocol describes the original three-group comparison with N-acetylcysteine and standard care, and the amended two-group plan. The 2016 and 2019 N-acetylcysteine publications share this registration and supply no oral combination result. [Local registration and study design](Source-Text/Trials/NCT02445651.txt) · [Sponsor protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/51/NCT02445651/Prot_SAP_006.pdf) · [2025 registry results](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Registry-Followup.md#swallowing-difficulty-in-the-oral-supplement-arm)

## Capsicum extracts in rotenone models

Abdel-Salam and colleagues reported lower brain 5-lipoxygenase measures and less neuronal damage with injected hot-pepper extract in rotenone-exposed mice. Ogunruku and Ogunyemi reported improved Y-maze spontaneous alternation after oral *Capsicum annuum* ethyl-acetate fraction in rotenone-exposed rats in 2019. The lower tested dose improved the measure by the last measurement, and the two higher doses improved it across the measured days. These experiments supply tissue or maze evidence for concentrated preparations. They give no clinical outcome for ground cayenne or paprika. [Injected extract primary report](https://cdn.fortunejournals.com/articles/capsicum-protects-against-rotenoneinduced-toxicity-in-mice-brain-via-reduced-oxidative-stress-and-5lipoxygenase-activationp.pdf) [Oral fraction primary report, Figure 1](https://www.ajol.info/index.php/ijs/article/download/185249/174569)

## Isolated curcumin in rat copper intoxication

Abbaoui and colleagues reported an acute copper-intoxication experiment at the June 7, 2017 MDS congress. Adult rats received copper for three days and oral curcumin-I at 30 mg/kg. The investigators reported improved open-field locomotion after curcumin. The September 15 journal report uses the same exposure, schedule and outcome, and is grouped with the congress abstract. [MDS abstract 923](https://www.mdsabstracts.org/abstract/curcumin-i-protect-against-copper-induced-neurobehavioral-features-of-parkinsons-disease-in-rat/) · [Journal abstract, PMID 28919537](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28919537/)

A June 27, 2018 report used a six-week copper exposure and again reported better locomotor performance with curcumin-I. A January 3, 2019 report from the same authors used the six-week exposure to assess anxiety-like behavior in the elevated plus maze and spatial learning and memory in the Morris water maze. The latter paper reports that curcumin attenuated copper-associated anxiety-like behavior. It reports no copper-induced learning and memory impairment and describes better spatial learning and memory with curcumin. The 2019 IAPRD poster combines the acute and six-week model results. These public descriptions leave animal overlap across the two six-week papers unresolved. The reported endpoints give no direct estimates for human rigidity (`0359`), bradykinesia (`0507`), or attention impairment (`0805`). [2018 report, PMID 29954586](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29954586/) · [2019 report, PMID 30611899](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30611899/) · [IAPRD abstract P 029](https://iaprd-parkinson.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/IAPRD2019_AbstractBook.pdf)

## Isolated carvacrol and thymol in early Parkinson models

Lins and colleagues first published in 2018. In a repeated reserpine rat model, injected isolated carvacrol reduced catalepsy and vacuous chewing movements. Open-field locomotion did not recover. Hamzehloei and colleagues first published July 29, 2019. In their 6-OHDA rat study, isolated carvacrol alone improved passive-avoidance memory (p < .01) and produced no clear change in apomorphine-induced rotations. Carvacrol combined with treadmill exercise reduced the rotations (p < .05). A separate 6-OHDA experiment by Manouchehrabadi and colleagues, published July 30, 2019, reports improvement on several motor tests including coordination and catalepsy. These studies tested an isolated compound found in oregano and thyme. The measured motor outcomes give no separate stride length, dystonia or daily off-time estimate for culinary oregano. [2018 reserpine report, PMID 29378222](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29378222/) · [Licensed 2019 exercise comparison text](Source-Text/2019-Hamzehloei-Carvacrol-and-Exercise.txt#L345) · [2019 motor report, PMID 31364033](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31364033/)

The July 2018 World Congress of Basic and Clinical Pharmacology abstract and the March 27, 2019 Nagoor Meeran et al. article share the same four-week rotenone regimen, isolated thymol preparation and investigator group. The reports give tissue, oxidative stress and inflammatory outcomes. No behavioral test is reported in the 2019 full text. [Congress abstract PO2-1-68](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpssuppl/WCP2018/0/WCP2018_PO2-1-68/_pdf) · [Licensed primary article text](Source-Text/PMC6480243.txt#L74)
