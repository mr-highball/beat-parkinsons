# Food experiment evidence for romaine lettuce, celery stalk, cabbage and Brussels sprouts

## 2018 Fresh lettuce meal trial registration

IRCT20160702028742N4 describes a crossover comparing fresh lettuce, fresh watercress and a cellulose placebo added to a high-fat meal in young men. The specified outcomes concern circulating inflammatory and oxidative markers. The registry gives March 7, 2018 as the registration date, while the later paper says March 3. The paper identifies the lettuce as raw romaine. [Official registration](https://irct.ir/trial/23233) · [2021 results](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Romaine-Celery-Stalk-Cabbage.md#2021-raw-romaine-with-a-meal-in-healthy-young-men)

## 2018 Raw and steamed red cabbage and in-mouth aroma

Frank and colleagues compared sulfur volatiles from raw and steamed red cabbage. In six participants sampling consecutive mouthfuls, later raw-cabbage mouthfuls produced more sulfur volatiles than earlier ones (p < .001). The cooked condition had no significant buildup. The 2019 corrigendum updates the enzyme attribution without changing the reported experiment. This measures aroma production during eating and does not assess neurological olfactory impairment or drooling. [Primary report, Figure 4](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2018.e01045) · [Corrigendum](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2019.e01258)

## 2019 Raw celery stalk and romaine in a mixed-salad crossover

Mayra and colleagues compared a fresh salad containing a celery stalk, spinach and romaine lettuce with canned green beans, corn or peas over two 10-day periods. Ten postmenopausal women completed the study. Flow-mediated dilation favored salad (+17% versus −8%, p = .047) and peripheral and derived central-aortic pressure did not differ by treatment. Neither ingredient was independently varied. The study reports no motor or swallowing outcome. [Primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30954341/)

## 2019 Red lettuce microgreen extract in a Parkinson fly model

Putri's Indonesian university thesis reports climbing scores of 97.33% in untreated controls, 94% in the red-lettuce-microgreen-extract group and 57.33% in the paraquat group. The study also reports survival, dopamine and malondialdehyde. The repository abstract identifies *Lactuca sativa* without establishing the romaine cultivar. The climbing test does not measure hypomimia, rest tremor, levodopa-induced dyskinesia or handwriting. [University thesis record and abstract](https://digilib.uinsgd.ac.id/21814/)

## 2019 Volatiles in raw and cooked Brussels sprouts

Wieczorek and Jeleń profiled five Brussels sprout cultivars and compared raw and cooked forms, with fresh and frozen handling comparisons. The cooked sprouts generally contained more sulfur volatiles than raw. The study measured food composition and did not administer roasted sprouts or assess human olfactory ability. [Primary report, Figures 2–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6385023/) · [Local article](Source-Text/PMC6385023.txt)

## Grouped lettuce intake and later physical function

Hagan and colleagues followed 54,762 women in the Nurses’ Health Study. In Table 3, the combined romaine or leaf lettuce intake category at two or more servings weekly versus less than monthly had adjusted HR 0.88 (95% CI 0.83–0.93) for incident SF-36 physical impairment. The composite includes gross mobility and activity items without handwriting or fine motor testing. The observational exposure did not isolate raw romaine, and no Parkinson stratum was analyzed.
