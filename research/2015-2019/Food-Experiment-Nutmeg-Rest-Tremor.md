# Nutmeg reports and tremor phenotyping

## Intention tremor after powdered nutmeg exposure

October 2018. Serrano Benavente, Pérez Ruiz and Ruiz Sánchez describe an adult presenting after intentional excess powdered nutmeg. Examination recorded intention tremor of the hands alongside other acute findings. The report follows recovery during observation. It gives neither a Parkinson diagnosis nor a quantified rest-tremor observation. The case has no comparison group and the route and severity limits keep the culinary hypothesis open. [Original source](https://www.samfyc.es/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/V19n1s2.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2018-Serrano-Benavente-Powdered-Nutmeg-Intention-Tremor-Extract.txt)

## Marseille poison-center signs and background animal tremor

September 19, 2019. Of 31 Marseille poison-center exposure reports, 22 had sufficient data for analysis. Eleven were intentional and eleven unintentional. Powder predominated, with some whole seed. Table I groups neurological and psychiatric signs and does not enumerate individual tremor findings. Tremor in the Discussion refers to earlier animal literature. The body supplies case-provenance and symptom-reporting context, with no Parkinson rest-tremor comparison. [Original source](https://www.em-consulte.com/showarticlefile/1326067/main.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2019-Reynoard-Marseille-Nutmeg-Case-Series-Extract.txt)

## General discussion versus direct clinical observations

The January 2015 report lists tremor when discussing prior toxicity literature. Its own case records no abnormality in the subsequent neurological examination. The Swiss poison-center report subsequently groups myoclonus with other clinical signs in moderate cases. Those are separate source observations and neither verifies a Parkinson rest-tremor outcome. [2015 original report](Source-Text/2015-PMID26219944.txt) · [Swiss primary report summary](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Nutmeg-Paprika.md)

## Possible overlap between two intention tremor reports

The 2017 full report and 2018 society abstract describe a patient of the same age treated at the same hospital after excess powdered nutmeg, both with hand intention tremor and recovery during observation. Two authors overlap. The reports differ in recorded intake, co-ingestion, chronic medication and urine toxicology. A shared patient identifier could not be verified, so the two reports are kept as potentially overlapping observations. The 2017 report cites the 2014 Ostabal Artigas letter, which describes a different presentation. [Original 2017 case](https://www.revista-portalesmedicos.com/revista-medica/intoxicacion-nuez-moscada/) · [Original 2018 proceedings, p. 400](https://www.samfyc.es/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/V19n1s2.pdf) · [2014 letter summary](../2010-2014/Source-Text/2014-Ostabal-Powdered-Nutmeg-Case-Extract.txt)

## Beckerman and Persaud overdose abstract

The authors describe psychiatric and neurological symptoms after a nutmeg overdose. The abstract does not itemize the observed motor signs. The main case text remains an open route for phenotype verification. [Local abstract summary](Source-Text/2019-Beckerman-Persaud-Nutmeg-Abstract-Extract.txt) · [Author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31519286/)

The original publisher's accessible case snippet gives an adolescent with no relevant history or regular medication. It does not describe a rest-tremor examination. The later conference listing repeats the same author abstract. The publishable clinical evidence remains at abstract and snippet depth. [Publisher case page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0965229919305540) · [Conference duplicate](https://www.clintox.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/HDS-Abstracts-Sept-2019.pdf)

## Separate Italian poison-center abstract

Sabatini and colleagues' original conference abstract records an adolescent with intentional nutmeg exposure followed by emesis, abdominal pain and headache. It gives no neurological examination or tremor assessment. Absence of a recorded sign in an abstract does not establish an examination result. [Abstract 21, p. 433](Source-Text/2019-Sabatini-Nutmeg-Poison-Center-Abstract-Extract.txt)
