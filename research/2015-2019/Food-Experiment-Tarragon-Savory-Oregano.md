# Tarragon, summer savory and oregano

## Tarragon ethanol extract and mouse nociception

Eidi and colleagues injected ethanolic extract of tarragon aerial parts into male BALB/c mice. They reported reduced responses to formalin and acetic acid at 50 and 100 mg/kg and increased hot-plate latency. This provides indirect evidence for pain (0285). The preparation, route and outcomes differ from fresh tarragon ingestion and clinical pain in Parkinson’s. [June 2015 online primary abstract, PMID 26079854](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26079854/)

## Injected oregano extract and awake rat behavior

Mombeini and colleagues injected male rats with an aqueous extract of *Origanum vulgare* subsp. *vulgare* leaves and flowering branches. At the highest tested dose the rats spent more time on open maze arms and entered them more frequently than the saline group (p < .001 and p < .05, respectively). They moved less in the open field (p < .05). The horizontal-wire test showed no clear muscle relaxation. The results run opposite to the anxiety direction in claim 0634 in a rodent proxy. Reduced awake activity is an indirect lead for 0842 and supplies no measure of daytime sleepiness. [2015 primary journal article, Figures 1–3](https://www.academia.edu/97985091/Pharmacological_Effects_of_Origanum_Vulgare_L_in_the_Elevated_Plus_Maze_and_Open_Field_Tests_in_the_Rat)

## Tarragon extracts in chronically restrained rats

Khosravi, Rahnema and Asle-Rousta reported effects of oral hydroalcoholic tarragon extract on elevated plus-maze and forced-swim behavior in rats exposed to chronic restraint. A later report by the same team gives improved Morris water maze performance under restraint. The reports share a six-group design with male Wistar rats, the 21-day stress schedule and the 100 and 500 mg/kg extract doses. Shared animals are possible. The tasks are indirect for the depressive symptom claim 0968 and for cognition in claim 0460. Spatial learning and memory tasks do not isolate executive function. [2017 primary behavioral paper](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/fb79/3b1501670644c854fa5a2681d4cc78e43fcd.pdf) · [2019 primary spatial learning paper](https://doi.org/10.32592/jbirjandunivmedsci.2019.26.3.101) · [2019 Persian journal issue](https://www.magiran.com/volume/149135)

## A Russian tarragon extract in a mouse social defeat model

Wang and colleagues incorporated an ethanolic extract of *Artemisia dracunculus* shoots, PMI 5011, into mouse feed before and during repeated social defeat. About one fifth of control mice and more than half of extract-fed mice met the prespecified social interaction criterion for resilience. The social interaction ratio differed between groups (p = .018), and extract-fed mice spent more time grooming after a sucrose splash (p < .001). Sucrose preference showed no clear difference (p = .565). The measures are indirect for depressive symptoms (0968) and do not isolate clinical apathy (0756). [May 2018 primary paper, Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5971253/) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC5971253.txt)

Figure 1 specifies controls and extract groups of 13 and 14 for grooming and sucrose preference. Its caption gives p < .0001 for longer grooming time, and the main text gives p < .001. Sucrose preference was inconclusive at p = .565. The caption reports a higher social interaction ratio with t(39) = 2.786 and p = .018. The reported statistic exceeds the usual two-sided .01 critical value of 2.708 at 39 degrees of freedom. The discrepancy leaves the exact significance estimate unresolved. [May 2018 primary paper, Figure 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5971253/) · [NIST critical values](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda3672.htm)

## Summer savory powder in a human reflux trial

IRCT2015072215860N2 registered July 30, 2015 while recruiting and lists a target of 60 adults. Its registered primary measure was reflux symptom severity using a visual analogue score and the Frequency Scale for Symptoms of GERD. Faghihi Kashani and colleagues’ February 2018 report randomized 58 adults to capsules of dried summer savory whole-plant powder or cornstarch, with both arms advised to make lifestyle changes. After four weeks the results analysis included 21 summer savory and 18 placebo recipients. Post-treatment Frequency Scale for Symptoms of GERD scores were 10.11 and 10.14 (p = .989). The global visual analogue score comparison gave p = .239. The questionnaire includes a swallowing question, but the report gives no separate response for it. The study estimates overall reflux symptoms and supplies no Parkinson’s pain result for 0659. [Official IRCT registration](https://irct.ir/trial/14997) · [Local registry summary](Source-Text/IRCT2015072215860N2.txt) · [February 2018 primary article](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323127915_Effects_of_Satureja_hortensis_L_on_Improving_Adult_Gastroesophageal_Reflux_Disease_A_Double-Blinded_Randomized_Controlled_Clinical_Trial)

## Oral summer savory powder and knee osteoarthritis pain

Mozaffari-Khosravi and colleagues randomized 60 adults with knee osteoarthritis to summer savory powder capsules or starch placebo for two months. The results include 19 of 30 summer savory and 20 of 30 placebo recipients. Table 2 gives reported within-person pain changes of −2 ± 3 with powder and −0.5 ± 2 with placebo (between-group p = .38). The rounded group values were 7 before and 5 after in both groups. The Methods and Discussion disagree on capsule dose. TNF-α change was inconclusive (p = .30). The outcome paper and registry share investigators, site, target size, intervention and pain scale. The article lacks an explicit accession. This offers indirect, inconclusive human evidence for 0659. The sample had knee osteoarthritis and used dried leaf powder. [2015 primary article, Figure 1 and Table 2](https://oaji.net/pdf.html?n=2016%2F2982-1455520556.pdf) · [2013 registration annotation](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Tarragon-Savory-Oregano.md#oral-summer-savory-leaf-powder-knee-osteoarthritis-trial-registration)

## Topical summer savory extract and chemotherapy mucositis pain

IRCT2016061813691N7 registered August 15, 2016 while recruiting for a double-blind trial in children receiving chemotherapy. The registry lists mucositis as the primary outcome and Oucher pain as a secondary outcome. Arshadi Bostanabad and colleagues analyzed 60 children in the May 2018 report. Both arms received routine care, with a topical 1% *Satureja hortensis* extract gel or a placebo gel added. Mean pain scores fell from 3.5 to 0.0 and from 3.1 to 0.4, respectively, over five days. Table 2 reports a repeated-measures comparison of p < .001. The preparation, route, pain source and population differ from claim 0659. [Official IRCT registration](https://irct.ir/trial/13482) · [Local registry summary](Source-Text/IRCT2016061813691N7.txt) · [May 2018 primary article, Table 2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331318909_The_Effect_of_Mucoadhesive_Gel_Containing_Satureja_Hortensis_Extract_1_on_Severity_of_Chemotherapy-induced_Mucositis_Pain_in_Children_A_Randomized_Clinical_Trial)

## Summer savory extract and behavior after cisplatin

Kumburovic and colleagues studied 35 rats assigned to seven groups, including cisplatin groups receiving oral methanolic summer savory extract or silymarin. The intermediate extract dose attenuated cisplatin-associated changes in elevated-plus-maze and open-field behavior. The authors linked the experiments to their team’s 2018 paper on the same study’s tissue outcomes. The later tail suspension paper uses the same animal counts, extract and cisplatin regimen. These reports may overlap and provide no clinical depression estimate for claim 0539. [July 2019 primary paper, PMID 31467638](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6701305/) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC6701305.txt) · [2018 tissue outcomes, PMID 29746935](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29746935/) · [Tail suspension report](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Tarragon-Savory-Oregano.md#summer-savory-extract-and-tail-suspension-after-cisplatin)

## Tarragon extract in mouse forced-swim and tail-suspension assays

Jahani and colleagues injected ethanolic tarragon extract and measured mouse immobility in forced swim and tail suspension. In forced swim at 400 mg/kg, mean immobility was 153.62 s versus 202.30 s in controls. In tail suspension at 200 mg/kg, means were 117.20 and 142.60 s. The higher 400 mg/kg dose did not produce a clear tail-suspension effect. The primary experiment used tail suspension, which one later review described as writhing. The behavioral measures are indirect for claim 0968 and leave effects of eating dried tarragon in Parkinson’s unresolved. [December 2019 primary article, PMID 32038734](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6937744/)

## Tarragon in autoimmune encephalomyelitis

Nowras and colleagues gave mice with experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis an oral aqueous extract of tarragon. The treated animals had lower clinical severity scores than controls on days 24–33 (Figure 2, n = 6 per group, p < .05). The ordinal score includes weakness and paralysis. This study supplies no Parkinson gait-velocity result for walking slowness (0461). [October 2019 primary paper, PMID 32245296](https://publish.kne-publishing.com/index.php/IJAAI/article/download/1922/1357/)

## Oregano odor in early unilateral Parkinson’s

Zucco, Rovatti and Stevenson compared odor identification and recognition in 12 people with early Parkinson’s and right-sided motor symptoms and 12 controls matched by age and sex. Oregano presented neat was one of ten target odors (Table 1). Parkinson participants performed worse with the left nostril across the tasks. The results are pooled over odors, with no separate oregano estimate and no ingestion. [July 2015 primary study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.01020/full) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC4503885.txt)

## A Parkinson case with oregano in a dietary plan

Neiworth-Petshow and Baldwin-Sayre described one person with Parkinson’s and constipation who was advised to change several foods and supplements. Oregano appeared in a seasoning list, and at five days the patient reported using more herbs and spices and improved stool passage. The report gives no oregano-specific exposure or result. Constipation is outside the six frozen oregano symptom pairs. [Existing primary case annotation](Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md)

## Oral tarragon aerial extract and formalin response in fructose-fed rats

Shahraki and colleagues published June 11, 2015. The original methods describe shade-dried aerial parts rather than leaf only, extracted in water. Male Wistar rats in the fructose model received the preparation orally over four weeks. Formalin-evoked scores were lower than fructose-only controls in the reported phases (p < .001 as stated in the abstract). The experiment is distinct from the earlier diabetic-rat study. It gives no fresh-leaf or clinical Parkinson pain contrast. Original paper: https://downloads.hindawi.com/journals/ecam/2015/895417.pdf. ## Isolated carvacrol and thermal hypersensitivity after a Parkinson lesion

2018. Haddadi and colleagues tested isolated carvacrol in a 6-hydroxydopamine-lesioned rat model. The higher tested condition improved passive-avoidance memory. The original report found no clear effect on tail-withdrawal hyperalgesia or rotations. This is a competing model result for 0659. It concerns an isolated constituent and evoked thermal responses. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29489959/)

## Oral tarragon extract and maze activity during restraint stress

Khosravi and colleagues compared a hydroalcoholic tarragon extract given orally with controls in a 2017 rat restraint-stress study. Figure 3 uses total open and closed maze entries as a general activity measure. A later primary article describes no clear locomotor change. This affords an oral-extract context for the earlier injected-oil motor findings, while actual balance or a Parkinson clinical population was untested. [Original report](https://doi.org/10.21859/acadpub.nbr.4.1.1) · [Local methods summary](Source-Text/2017-Khosravi-Tarragon-Maze-Activity.txt)

## Injected tarragon extract and open-field travel

Jahani and colleagues used injected ethanolic aerial-part tarragon extract in an acute mouse experiment. Figure 3 reports generally unchanged open-field distance with a reduction in travel at 100 mg/kg. The measure is total exploratory travel. It does not establish slower walking, fresh culinary exposure or a Parkinson-specific effect. [original Methods and Figure 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6937744/) · [Local text](Source-Text/2019-Jahani-Tarragon-Open-Field-Extract.txt)

## Dried tarragon leaf extract and immobility during rat restraint

The Persian Methods describe an oral gavage extract prepared from powdered dried tarragon leaves with 70 percent alcohol. Figure 4 shows less forced-swim immobility in both extract-plus-restraint groups versus restraint alone (both p < .01). Figure 3 shows no clear difference in total maze arm entries. The plotted figures provide no exact tabular effect estimates. This is a behavioral assay in stressed rats and a dried-leaf extract, with clinical mood after eating dried tarragon outside the design.
