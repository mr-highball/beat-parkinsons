# Grapeseed oil and measured sleep

## Grapeseed oil odor control in primary insomnia

Yang Ying, Wei Kai and Lü Daping randomized inpatients with primary insomnia to three lavender concentrations, grapeseed oil or no treatment. The 2016 Chinese-language author abstract reports one week of nightly inhalation in the four oil groups and polysomnography. The authors report no clear grapeseed versus untreated difference on their listed measures (p > .05). The abstract gives no arm-specific counts, estimates or precision. A Korean clinical guideline reports 75 participants in the five arms, but its published numerical estimates concern the 1% lavender comparison. The original tables remain access-limited. Inhaled grapeseed oil of unspecified processing was tested in primary insomnia. The study supplies no oral oil, Parkinson or dream-enactment estimate. ## Ambient grapeseed carrier in long-term care

Suyeon Bae's May 2019 dissertation reported 58 randomized residents and 42 with complete paired observations. During two weeks, room diffusers contained either grapeseed oil alone or grapeseed oil with lavender. Complete-pair Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index means were 5.90 then 5.19 in the grapeseed arm and 7.14 then 5.81 in the lavender arm. The baseline-adjusted intervention coefficient was −0.07 (SE 0.75), with no clear between-arm difference. Table 13 prints a placebo change of 0.62 despite the displayed means differing by 0.71, and the prose has a conflicting significance notation. A pleasant dream was mentioned by one lavender participant. There was no oil-free arm, oral administration, processing grade or dream-enactment outcome. The 2020 Bae and Asojo article reports the same sample size, design and duration and is treated as part of the likely same family.
