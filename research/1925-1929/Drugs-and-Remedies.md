# Drugs and remedies, 1925-1929

### 1926 — McCowan and Harris report hyoscine-associated symptomatic benefit

P. K. McCowan and J. S. Harris reported clinical improvement with hyoscine in postencephalitic parkinsonism and also observed altered glucose curves after drug administration. The patients without parkinsonism in their encephalitis series did not have the same clinical response. This historical comparison does not establish long-term benefit or a dietary effect.

[McCowan and Harris, British Medical Journal, scan](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2524122/)

### 1926 — A nicotine case series reports transient change and relapse

H. Moll described reduced rigidity and better gait in some postencephalitic patients given nicotine, with tremor and drooling often unchanged. The paper describes vomiting in one patient and return to prior state in responders by six-month follow-up. Symptoms also fluctuated spontaneously. The historical series provides no evidence that tobacco products treat Parkinson’s disease.

[Moll, British Medical Journal, scan](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2524155/)

### 1929 — Stramonium case reports less rigidity and signs of poisoning

Ross Robertson recorded a patient with improved rigidity and function while taking a stramonium preparation, with unchanged tremor. The case also reports blurred vision, dry mouth, incontinence, and a rapid pulse during treatment. The plant preparation is toxic. This is one historical case with no control.

[Robertson, Canadian Medical Association Journal, scan](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1710974/)
