# Soybean oil comparator and clinical endpoints

## Falls in an Alzheimer trial with a soybean oil blend

NCT00090402 results posted on 26 February 2010, and Shinto and colleagues published the clinical paper online on 29 October 2013. This Alzheimer pilot assigned 13 participants each to three groups. Placebo capsules contained soybean oil admixed with 5% fish oil and lemon flavor. During 12 months, two falls occurred in two of 13 placebo recipients, one in one of 13 fish-oil recipients and zero of 13 in the fish-oil plus alpha-lipoic-acid arm. The oil-placebo group also received a placebo alpha-lipoic-acid tablet. The registry and primary report agree on the fall counts. The preparation was a blend and the population had Alzheimer’s disease.

Several investigators, the center and the fish-oil supplier overlap with NCT01953705. Trial IDs, enrollment years, sample size and population are distinct. The smaller pilot observed a nominally higher fall count in the soybean comparator arm. The later trial observed nominally fewer participants with injurious falls in the soybean comparator arm.

## Registered soybean oil in the vascular aging trial

NCT01953705 first posted on 1 October 2013, with soybean oil as the placebo alongside marine omega-3. The registry has no results, and its protocol lists brain imaging and cognitive measures. Its linked 2019 and 2024 papers are from the same clinical study.

## Soybean oil comparator and null locomotion contrast before restraint stress

Pase and colleagues first published their rat experiment on 3 June 2013. During the perinatal period, the groups had soybean oil, fish oil or hydrogenated vegetable fat. At 41 days, half of each group underwent acute restraint. Unstressed animals had no significant between-diet difference in open-field crossing or rearing counts. Diet and stress interacted for crossings (F[2,36]=12.55, p<.001) and rearings (F[2,36]=11.02, p<.001), with increased counts after stress under hydrogenated fat. The open-field behavior is an exploration measure in adolescent rats.

This is a separate protocol from the older-rat dyskinesia comparisons. Investigators overlap. The rat developmental stage, intervention schedules and stress procedures differ, with individual animal provenance unavailable.

## Falls in the Alzheimer DHA trial with corn or soy oil placebo

NCT00440050 first posted results on 30 August 2010. Quinn and colleagues reported the same trial on 3 November 2010. The registry and Table 2 agree that 33 of 164 placebo recipients and 42 of 238 recipients of algal DHA had a recorded fall (20.1% and 17.6%, respectively, p=.60 in the journal report). The registry lists 33 and 42 events, with the same numbers of affected participants. The authors describe placebo capsules as containing corn or soy oil. The aggregate placebo group provides no oil-specific allocation or effect estimate. The trial measures adverse events in Alzheimer disease. Repeated falls after culinary soybean oil in Parkinson disease remain untested in this comparison.

## Probiotic oil drops and stimulated salivary flow

First published February 27, 2012. In 42 randomized periodontal patients stimulated salivary flow increased in both groups by day 15. The trial compared Lactobacillus salivarius WB21 oil drops with placebo. The accessible original abstract neither specifies the oil nor reports absolute or adjusted between-arm flow estimates. Two later reviews identify soybean oil as the vehicle and one describes a soybean-oil placebo. The primary Methods are still inaccessible, and refining remains unverified. Increased secretion does not establish drooling, and the trial gives no Parkinson result.
