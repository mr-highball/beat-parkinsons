# Watermelon flavor and salivary secretion during chewing source record

## Watermelon flavor and salivary secretion during chewing

Karami Nogourani and colleagues published March 11, 2012. Fifteen healthy dental students chewed five flavored gums. In the watermelon-flavored condition mean flow was 0.88 mL/min before chewing and 3.63 after six minutes (p = .001), and each flavor increased flow relative to its own baseline. The paper does not identify the flavor as watermelon flesh or include an unflavored-gum comparator. Collected salivary output is not oral loss or a clinical drooling rating. [Primary report, Methods and result tables](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2012/569327)

## Cinnamon flavor and chewing context

The same 15-person trial reports mean flow of 0.79 mL/min before and 3.76 mL/min during minutes 3–6 for cinnamon-flavored gum. All five flavors elicited more saliva relative to the reference within the chewing comparison. PMID 23372599 reports the same authors, student group size, flavors and collection windows. Count the publication family once until cohort independence is demonstrated. No inert flavorless chewing group or verification of ground cinnamon was available. [Primary results, Tables 1–2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2012/569327) · [Companion indexed report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23372599/)
