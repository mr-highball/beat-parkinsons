# Allspice and cumin evidence

## Cumin extract in rat memory and stress biomarker tests

Koppula and Choi measured avoidance-task memory after scopolamine and urine vanillylmandelic acid and ascorbic acid after forced-swim stress. The cumin extract attenuated memory loss and altered the urinary biomarker response. The biochemical results do not measure voiding, urinary urgency or bladder function. The memory test does not identify clinical attention impairment. [Primary report, PMID 21639683](https://doi.org/10.3109/13880209.2010.541923)

## Cumin essential oil drops and bowel symptoms in irritable bowel syndrome

Agah and colleagues' 2013 uncontrolled case series enrolled 57 people with irritable bowel syndrome and analyzed 50. The oral drops contained 2% cumin essential oil. In participants with constipation-predominant IBS, the authors reported higher defecation frequency and improved stool consistency during the intervention. Defecation frequency declined after withdrawal. The absence of a comparator, use of essential oil, and IBS diagnosis limit the comparison to ground cumin and Parkinson constipation. Fecal urgency was assessed as a gastrointestinal outcome. [Primary article, PMID 24829694](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3990147/)

## Cumin powder in a dietary randomized comparison

Zare and colleagues randomized 88 women with overweight or obesity to cumin powder in yogurt or yogurt alone for three months with dietary counseling. The study evaluates anthropometric and lipid measures. It verifies ordinary powder ingestion in people, while its available abstract supplies no constipation, orthostatic symptoms, or other frozen Parkinson outcome. [Primary abstract, PMID 25456022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25456022/)

## Isolated eugenol after a dopamine lesion and with levodopa

Kabuto and Yamanushi's 2011 study reported no improvement in levodopa-related abnormal behavior after oral eugenol and increased depletion of striatal dopamine and metabolites in the lesion model. An earlier eugenol pretreatment report found preservation of striatal dopamine. Neither intervention used allspice fruit. The direction depends on the model, timing and endpoint. [Existing primary-source comparison](Food-Experiment-Spice-Followup.md#zingerone-and-eugenol-after-6-ohda-lesion) · [Primary 2011 report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21769642/)

## Dizziness after oral cumin essential oil drops

Agah and colleagues mention dizziness after consumption of the 2% cumin essential-oil drops in the 2013 IBS series. They provide no event count, posture, measured blood pressure or event-specific timing. The Results start with 28 included, while the abstract gives 57 enrolled and the Results analyze 50. The uncontrolled series provides tolerability context for an oil preparation. It gives no comparison of ground cumin and standing lightheadedness in Parkinson disease.
