Title: European Union Herbal Monograph and Traditional Persian Heritage for Lavandula angustifolia Flower Infusions
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/herbal/lavandulae-flos
Source kind: Traditional herbal monograph and ethnopharmacological record
Publication date: 2012-06-13
Date basis: Official EMA/HMPC Community herbal monograph first publication date (EMA/HMPC/734125/2010, June 13, 2012; addendum EMA/HMPC/487394/2020), cross-linked with Traditional Persian Medicine citations in PMC7333290 (2020) and Italian Bologna ethnobotany PMC10936038 (2024)
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: Favorable traditional use for mild mental stress, exhaustion, and sleep aid; no Parkinson-specific clinical efficacy claim
Authenticity: Official European Medicines Agency Committee on Herbal Medicinal Products (HMPC) regulatory monograph documenting >=30 years of European traditional medicinal use, distinct from modern clinical proof
Efficacy evidence: Traditional-use registration based on longstanding herbal practice rather than controlled Parkinson's clinical trials; formal oral trials are evaluated separately

Community/context: European traditional phytotherapy documented by the European Medicines Agency HMPC (Community herbal monograph EMA/HMPC/734125/2010, first published June 13, 2012; 2020 assessment addendum EMA/HMPC/487394/2020), alongside Traditional Persian Medicine ('Ostukhudus' / Lavandula angustifolia in Avicenna's Canon and Aghili's Makhzan al-Advieh, cited in research/2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC7333290.txt) and Northern Italian rural ethnobotany (Bologna district informants citing Lavandula angustifolia flowers and leaves in dishes and calming preparations, research/2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC10936038-Chiocchio-Bologna-Ethnobotany.txt).
Exposure and reported symptom: Comminuted dried flowers (Lavandulae flos) brewed as an oral herbal tea infusion or tincture, traditionally indicated for relief of mild symptoms of mental stress and exhaustion and to aid sleep; Persian texts similarly describe oral lavender infusions for melancholy, nervous exhaustion, and headache.
Evidence and limits: EMA traditional-use registration reflects >=30 years of documented European use and plausible safety rather than controlled clinical efficacy trials, and does not cover diagnosed Parkinson's disease. Oral brewed tea differs substantially in essential oil dose from standardized encapsulated essential oil (Silexan).
Related research: Cross-linked with PMC7333290.txt, PMC10936038-Chiocchio-Bologna-Ethnobotany.txt, and Round 3 oral lavender tea RCTs (PMID:32444033, PMID:26523950, PMID:24578844).
