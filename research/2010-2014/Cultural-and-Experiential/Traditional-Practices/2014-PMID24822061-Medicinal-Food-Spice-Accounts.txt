PMCID: PMC4009199

DOI: 10.1155/2014/354264
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Traditional food practices reported in a published review
Publication date: 2014-04-17
Date basis: Date of the inspected review. Its described practices have no verified origin date.
Text coverage: Sections 2.1, 2.9 and 2.10, the introduction, and the review's references.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25, publication and reported-use scope
Reported direction: The author recounts reported relief of digestive complaints or diarrhea and diabetes management in distinct customary accounts.
Authenticity: Published source with named plants and multiple attributed traditions. No primary interview record or observed symptom series was established.
Efficacy evidence: The preparations were described as mixed remedies. The cited review does not measure Parkinson symptoms or independently identify the effect of a spice.

The review attributes a sugar-apple-leaf and black-pepper mixture to
customary diabetes management in Aligarh. That account concerns
Piper nigrum used together with leaves and gives no isolated
pepper contrast.

A pomegranate-bark combination including cinnamon and clove is
described for diarrhea. This is a different bowel complaint from
constipation and no result is attributable to the cinnamon alone.
Cumin and onion are named in a separate South Indian mixed-remedy
description for stomachache; it involves a different plant and
does not supply a Parkinson diagnosis or constipation comparison.
The sections cite a 2008 plant compendium and other secondary
accounts. The original book passages remain to be checked.
