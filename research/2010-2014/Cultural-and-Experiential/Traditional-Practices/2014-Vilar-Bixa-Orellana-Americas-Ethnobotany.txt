Title: Indigenous and traditional ethnobotanical uses of achiote/urucum (Bixa orellana) across the Americas
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4094728/
Source kind: Ethnopharmacological review
Publication date: 2014
Date basis: Peer-reviewed publication date (The Scientific World Journal 2014; PMID 25050404, PMC4094728; complemented by Stohs 2014 PMID 24357022 and Raddatz-Mota 2017 PMC5430180).
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional South and Central American practices document Bixa orellana leaf and seed preparations as anti-inflammatory, analgesic/pain-soothing, antidiarrheal, antipyretic, digestive, and (in Peru) for urinary/prostate discomfort.
Authenticity: Verified directly in open-access PMC full text (PMC4094728) and indexed PubMed companion reviews (PMID 24357022, PMC5430180).
Efficacy evidence: Ethnobotanical use documentation only; many medicinal uses rely on leaf infusions rather than culinary ground seeds, and a 12-month Peruvian RCT (Zegarra 2007) found no benefit of B. orellana leaf capsules for BPH urinary symptoms.

Vilar and colleagues (2014, PMC4094728), alongside Stohs (2014, PMID 24357022) and Raddatz-Mota et al. (2017, PMC5430180), document extensive pre-Hispanic and contemporary indigenous uses of Bixa orellana (achiote, urucum, annatto) across Brazil, Peru, Mexico, and Central America. Beyond culinary coloring and ceremonial body paint, traditional preparations—predominantly leaf decoctions/infusions and seed pastes—are used as antidiarrheal, anti-inflammatory, analgesic (headaches, joint/body aches), digestive, antipyretic, and Peruvian folk remedies for urinary/prostate complaints. Phytochemical characterization confirms bixin (up to 5% in seed aril), geranylgeraniol (~1% of dry seeds), and delta-tocotrienol as major seed constituents. Customary reports do not establish clinical efficacy in Parkinson's disease.
