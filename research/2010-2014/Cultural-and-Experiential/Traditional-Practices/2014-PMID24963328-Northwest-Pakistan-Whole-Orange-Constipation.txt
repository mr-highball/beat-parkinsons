Title: Whole sweet orange for constipation in a northwest Pakistan interview survey
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Ethnographic observation of traditional practice
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4055084/
Publisher original: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2014/635371
PMID: 24963328
PMCID: PMC4055084
DOI: 10.1155/2014/635371
Publication date: 2014-05-20
Date basis: First journal publication. Field interviews ran from October 2012 to October 2013.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25
Original locator: Table 1, Citrus sinensis row under the local name Malta, and Methods sections 2.1–2.3.
Community: Pashto-language interviews and discussions with selected local informants in northwest Pakistan.
Reported direction: Intended constipation relief.
Authenticity: The original article reports the oral whole-fruit use. It also describes plant identification and herbarium storage.
Efficacy evidence: The source is a report of customary use. Clinical diagnoses, baseline symptoms, separate outcomes and a comparator are unavailable.
Access and follow-up: The publisher and PMC originals are public. The source provides no patient records to establish an effect size.

The authors interviewed 55 people overall. This number describes the survey sample, with no separate denominator for people who used orange for constipation. The table identifies sweet orange and specifies eating the fruit. Its entry does not establish whether the fruit was cooked, what accompanied it or how the users' symptoms changed. The survey distinguishes the preparation from a juice-only practice.
