Efficacy evidence: Regulatory traditional-use monograph based on >=30 years of European herbal use (not clinical trial efficacy), explicitly restricted to dried rhizome/root (2-3 g comminuted root tea) and carrying Apiaceae allergy, edema/cardiac-renal fluid-restriction, and UV photosensitization warnings.

The European Medicines Agency (EMA) Committee on Herbal Medicinal Products (HMPC) established a European Union herbal monograph exclusively for Levistici radix—the dried rhizomes and roots of Levisticum officinale W.D.J. Koch (adopted 27 March 2012; periodic assessment addendum 2 March 2021; EMA page updated 15 March 2022). Its sole recognized traditional indication is as an oral herbal tea (2-3 g comminuted root) to increase urine volume for flushing of the urinary tract as an adjuvant in minor urinary complaints. Critically for Round 5 lovage leaf claims, EMA/HMPC recognizes NO herbal medicinal monograph for culinary lovage leaves (which are consumed primarily as a European soup/stew condiment, distinct from root decoctions documented in Lithuanian Samogitia interviews, Pranskuniene 2018). The EMA monograph notes contraindications in Apiaceae hypersensitivity and conditions requiring reduced fluid intake (severe cardiac or renal disease/edema) and advises avoiding intense UV/sunlight exposure due to furanocoumarin photosensitization.
