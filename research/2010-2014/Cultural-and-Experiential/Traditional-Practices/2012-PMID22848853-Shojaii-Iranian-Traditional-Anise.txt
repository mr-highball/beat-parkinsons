Title: Traditional Iranian Medicine uses of anise seed (Pimpinella anisum) for melancholia, migraine, sleep, and digestion
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3405664/
Source kind: Traditional-practice scholarly review
Publication date: 2012
Date basis: Peer-reviewed journal publication date (ISRN Pharmaceutics 2012; PMID 22848853, PMC3405664); summarizes classical Iranian traditional medicine texts.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Traditional Iranian sources describe Pimpinella anisum fruits as warm and dry, used for melancholia (depressed mood), nightmares, migraine analgesia, epilepsy, carminative digestive colic, and mild diuresis.
Authenticity: Verified via open-access PubMed Central full text (Shojaii & Abdollahi Fard, ISRN Pharm 2012; PMC3405664).
Efficacy evidence: Customary historical-text documentation only; does not establish clinical Parkinson's disease efficacy.

Shojaii and Abdollahi Fard (2012, PMC3405664) reviewed Iranian traditional medicine texts on Pimpinella anisum L. (aniseed / Apiaceae). Classical Persian sources classify aniseed as warm and dry in temperament and record traditional uses as a carminative for gastrointestinal spasms and bloating, an analgesic for migraine, a galactagogue, and a remedy mentioned for melancholia (depressed mood / black-bile imbalance), nightmares, and epilepsy, as well as increasing urine and sweat secretion. These customary accounts explain why contemporary Iranian randomized trials investigated anise formulations for functional dyspepsia, IBS constipation, and IBS-associated depression.
