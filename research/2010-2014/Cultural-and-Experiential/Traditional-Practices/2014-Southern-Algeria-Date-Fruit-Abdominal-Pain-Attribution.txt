Title: Attributed use of dates for abdominal discomfort in southern Algeria
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Traditional practice
Source: https://cms.idpc.ae/uploads/cn10iilffu0mvhtrhk4g/2024/7/193fc0cd-73a8-40a4-873b-7ce5c1b0878c.pdf#page=444
Publication year: 2014
Date basis: The proceedings disclose the practice in the paper dated to the 2014 conference. The date of the practice and the first documented account remain open.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20
Observed by: Ouafi and Bounaga attribute the practice to residents of southern Algeria in their conference discussion.
Reported direction: Use of date-palm fruit for abdominal pain relief.
Authenticity: Secondary attribution in a verified original proceedings paper. The report does not present individual accounts or identify the fruit preparation or frequency.
Efficacy evidence: Customary indication without measured human outcomes, a comparison group, or a Parkinson diagnosis.

The paper associates date use with relief of abdominal pain. Its separate mouse experiment used isolated Deglet Nour date fruit fractions. The paper gives no basis for assuming that the community used those fractions, the same cultivar, or a specific dried preparation.

The printed reference couples an apparent 2000 report about the medicinal plants of Béchar with pages 286–288 of volume 22 of Flavour and Fragrance Journal. The publisher assigns those pages to a 2007 Bubonium graveolens oil article, DOI 10.1002/ffj.1794. The 2000 CRSTRA report bearing the cited title remains an open access trail. Do not count this attribution as an independent confirmation of the 2000 report.
