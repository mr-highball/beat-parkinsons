Title: Indian Ayurvedic, Unani, and Mediterranean cooling, analgesic, and diuretic traditions for Cucumis sativus (cucumber)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23098877/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2013-01
Date basis: Peer-reviewed journal issue date of Mukherjee et al. (Fitoterapia, January 2013; PMID:23098877), supplemented by Arab-world ethnobotanical burn survey Al-Samydai et al. (2023; PMID:36864698); classical Ayurvedic and Unani texts predate modern reviews by centuries.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: Customary traditional cooling (refrigerant/Sheeta), soothing, analgesic (headache/sunburn/inflammation), hydrating, and diuretic uses of fresh cucumber fruit, juice, and seeds; distinguishes fresh fruit pulp from topical and seed preparations.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed pharmacognosy review (Mukherjee et al., 2013, Fitoterapia, PMID:23098877) corroborated by a 7,530-participant 18-country Arab ethnopharmacology survey documenting cucumber among topical cooling burn remedies (PMID:36864698).
Efficacy evidence: Traditional ethnobotanical and culinary heritage only; does not establish controlled clinical Parkinson efficacy for raw or pickled cucumber.

Community/context: Across South Asian Ayurvedic and Unani traditions as well as Mediterranean and Arab folk medicine, Cucumis sativus L. (Kheera/Trapusha/Khiyarain) has been valued since antiquity as a high-water, cooling (refrigerant), soothing, and tonic vegetable (Mukherjee et al., 2013, Fitoterapia, PMID:23098877). Traditional sources describe fresh cucumber fruit and juice for pacifying internal heat, thirst, swelling, localized discomfort, and skin irritation/burns (also reported in an 18-country Arab traditional-healer survey, Al-Samydai et al., 2023, PMID:36864698), while dried cucumber seeds (Tukhm-e-Khiyarain) are separately prepared in Unani medicine as cooling diuretics for dysuria and urinary tract irritation.

Evidence and limits: Separates modern publication date (2013/2023) from ancient medical systems and strictly distinguishes fresh fruit eating from seed formulations, topical applications, and brined pickles. Traditional cooling/analgesic reputation supplies cultural context rather than Parkinson disease clinical proof.
