Title: Green tea leaf in the European traditional-use monograph for fatigue
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/herbal-monograph/final-community-herbal-monograph-camellia-sinensis-l-kuntze-non-fermentatum-folium_en.pdf
Source kind: Traditional-practice documentation in an herbal regulatory monograph
Publication date: 2014-04-29
Date basis: EMA records 29 April 2014 as first publication of the final monograph. The committee adopted the document on 12 November 2013. The earlier draft and historical accounts are separate stages in the same source chain.
Source locator: EMA/HMPC/283630/2012 final monograph, pp. 2–4, especially 4.1. Companion assessment EMA/HMPC/283629/2012, pp. 7–9, 28–29, 35 and 40.
Community/context: The European Medicines Agency compiled medicinal market information and earlier materia medica for a traditional use decision. Its historical review cites a 1946 work that describes tea infusion as a tonic without separating green and black tea. It discusses Japanese powdered tea separately.
Exposure and reported symptom: Whole or comminuted unfermented Camellia sinensis leaf brewed as an oral infusion, and a separate powdered leaf preparation. The listed symptoms are tiredness and a feeling of weakness, without a diagnosed Parkinson population or a defined fatigue scale.
Reported direction: Traditional practice attributes relief of tiredness and weakness to the leaf preparations.
Authenticity: The agency original, adoption, final publication date and companion assessment are inspectable. The cited older 1946 work and earlier edition history were read through the agency summary.
Efficacy evidence: The monograph bases the indication on longstanding use. The companion assessment cites an enriched extract drink with EEG endpoints, a combined supplement with memory and attention endpoints, and a separate caffeine and L-theanine mixture with a short task-tiredness rating. These differ from an ordinary brewed green tea fatigue comparison in Parkinson disease. The assessment also describes suspected liver adverse reports with certain high-concentration extracts used for other indications. The monograph's leaf preparations and those extracts remain distinct.
