Title: Figs among foods discussed for dry mouth in a Persian medicine review
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2156587214558596
DOI: 10.1177/2156587214558596
Source kind: Traditional-practice interpretation in a modern literature review
Publication date: 2014-12-08
Date basis: Publisher first-online date. The journal issue appeared in April 2015.
Source locator: Heydarirad and Choopani, “Dry Mouth” From the Perspective of Traditional Persian Medicine and Comparison with Current Management, Lifestyle Modification and reference 15.
Community/context: Authors in an Iranian traditional medicine program reviewing Persian descriptions of dry mouth.
Reported direction: The review lists figs in a group of foods described as favorable for subjective dry mouth. The fig preparation is unspecified.
Authenticity: The article section and reference are publicly verifiable. The fig sentence cites the 2009 General Overview of Iranian Traditional Medicine by Naseri and colleagues. The 1869 Exir Azam lithograph is cited separately in the article.
Efficacy evidence: This is a report of a dietary recommendation. The authors report no standalone fig intervention, Parkinson cohort, saliva-flow test or drooling severity comparison for the food.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20, publisher text and reference inspection

Dry mouth can be perceived with different levels of measured saliva. Preserve the “dry mouth” indication and the unspecified fig preparation as given. The cited 2009 book's corresponding passage and any earlier chain for the fruit list are still unverified. The article mentions a registry identifier for related research, without presenting a comparative trial of figs. The separate 2024 Iranian review that lists Anjir among Parkinson candidates supplies no observed drooling change. The accounts leave both clinical preparation and symptom-specific effects open.
