Title: Chewed cardamom seeds for toothache in a West Yorkshire interview study
Source: https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctt9qcqq3.11
DOI: 10.2307/j.ctt9qcqq3.11
Publication date: 2010
Date basis: Publishing year of the chapter in Ethnobotany in the New Europe, pp. 112–146.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Ethnobotanical interview survey of customary use
Community/context: Interviews in a Bangladeshi community in West Yorkshire, United Kingdom.
Exposure and reported symptom: Chewed seeds of Elettaria cardamomum (elaichi) for toothache.
Reported direction: Customary use to relieve toothache. No clinical response data are reported.
Authenticity: Official chapter and book records checked. Appendix 6.1 p.136 read via a publicly indexed copy of the chapter.
Efficacy evidence: Seven people cited the practice. This is a count of use reports without observed response, Parkinson diagnosis or a ground-spice preparation.
Access and follow-up: Appendix 6.1 row 15. No individual identifiers recorded.
Graph ingredients: cardamom
Graph symptoms: pain severity
