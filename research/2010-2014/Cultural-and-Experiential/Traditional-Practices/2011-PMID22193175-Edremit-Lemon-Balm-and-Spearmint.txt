Community/context: Structured and semi-structured interviews, specimen collections and local surveys from Edremit Gulf, Balıkesir. The article lists 118 medicinal species and 218 uses.

Exposure: One lemon balm entry identifies Melissa officinalis subsp. altissima, with flowering branches as an oral infusion. The author separately reports Mentha spicata flowering-branch infusion for digestive and respiratory purposes and identifies the local sage as Salvia tomentosa. A separate olive-oil preparation combines it with bitter melon and has different oral and topical purposes. Sweet basil appears in a respiratory-use row.

Reported direction: Lemon balm was used with the intention of relieving insomnia and stomach ache, and was described as a sedative. These are stated indications. Spearmint was described as a carminative in the separate row. Neither source supplies an observed magnitude of effect.

Authenticity: Identified original article and its author copy, with relevant table cells and separate plant voucher entries checked.
Efficacy evidence: No treated Parkinson cohort, compared interventions or individual symptom measurements support transfer. Species, subspecies and recipes differ across locations.
Open trail: Track separate firsthand symptom outcomes, including reports that the remedies did not help. This record preserves observed practice.
