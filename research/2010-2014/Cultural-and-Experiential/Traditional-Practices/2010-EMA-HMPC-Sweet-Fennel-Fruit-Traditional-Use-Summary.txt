The European Medicines Agency Committee on Herbal Medicinal Products (HMPC) public summary (EMA/HMPC/411665/2008, first published 2010-02-16) documents traditional medicinal use of dried sweet fennel fruit (Foeniculum vulgare Miller subsp. vulgare var. dulce, comminuted, crushed, or powdered for herbal tea, tablets, or capsules) across traditional European phytotherapy and Chinese traditional medicine.

Recognized traditional indications based on at least 30 years of medicinal use (not clinical efficacy trials) are: (1) symptomatic treatment of mild spasmodic gastrointestinal complaints including bloating and flatulence; (2) symptomatic treatment of minor cramps associated with menstrual periods; and (3) expectorant use in cough associated with colds. Recommended short-term traditional dosing is generally limited to 1-2 weeks.

Safety precautions note potential allergic skin or respiratory reactions, contraindication in hypersensitivity, lack of safety data in children under 4 years, and avoidance during pregnancy and lactation as a general precaution. The summary emphasizes that clinical trials are limited and explicitly distinguishes whole dried sweet fennel fruit from isolated essential oil components (anethole, estragole).
