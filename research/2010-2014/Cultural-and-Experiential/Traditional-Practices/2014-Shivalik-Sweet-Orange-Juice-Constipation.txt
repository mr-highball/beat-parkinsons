Title: Sweet orange juice in a Shivalik ethnomedicinal survey
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Ethnographic observation of traditional practice
Source: https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=4150252b6df5d716a4df40ca38e5741cca1803a0&repid=rep1&type=pdf
Publication date: 2014
Date basis: American Journal of Ethnomedicine 1(4), pages 186–205, as printed on the original journal pages.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25
Original locator: Table 2, Citrus sinensis row of Assessment of Ethnomedicinal Plants in Shivalik Hills of Northwest Himalaya, India.
Community: People interviewed about medicinal plant use in the Shivalik Hills of Himachal Pradesh.
Reported direction: Intended use for constipation and piles.
Authenticity: The full journal copy contains the survey methodology and the plant-use table.
Efficacy evidence: The survey documents use. It does not report a constipation severity change after orange juice or identify a Parkinson sample.
Access and follow-up: The table is available in the institutional archive. The separate patient-level preparation and outcomes remain unspecified.

The table names Citrus sinensis as the botanical identity and gives the local name as Kinno/Malta. It records juice from the fruit in this row. The related entries for pomelo, lemon and citron remain separate. The reported 0.35 is the table's use value for sweet orange, which measures citations of use rather than relief or an outcome rate.
