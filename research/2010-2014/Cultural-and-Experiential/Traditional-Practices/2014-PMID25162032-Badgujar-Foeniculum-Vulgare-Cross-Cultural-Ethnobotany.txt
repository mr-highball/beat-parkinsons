Badgujar, Patel, Bandivdekar, and Mahajan (2014, BioMed Research International, PMID:25162032, PMC4137549) compiled vernacular names, culinary preparations, and ethnomedicinal uses of Foeniculum vulgare Mill. across 16 countries and traditional medical systems including Ayurveda (Madhurika, Shatapushpa), Unani, Siddha, Persian medicine (Razianeh), Chinese medicine (Hui xiang), Mediterranean Europe (Italy, Spain, Portugal), and Latin America (Bolivia, Ecuador, Mexico, Brazil).

Traditional digestive and symptom uses of fennel fruits/seeds include infusions, teas, chewed seeds, and culinary seasonings for abdominal pain, colic, flatulence, constipation/laxative support, gastritis, insomnia, arthritis, and calming/memory-associated rasayana tonics. Culinary records in Spain and Portugal also note fennel seeds used as bread/pastry spices and as a preservative for dried figs.

The review summarizes preclinical pharmacology (antispasmodic, anti-inflammatory, anxiolytic, scopolamine-amnesia rodent assays) alongside traditional attributions, but documents no controlled human Parkinson disease trials and separates historical ethnomedicinal usage from clinical proof.
