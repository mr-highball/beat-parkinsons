The Indian government-produced Classical Ayurvedic Prescriptions for Common Diseases labels kampavata as parkinsonism. Its single-formulation table identifies kapikacchu as Mucuna pruriens and ashwagandha as Withania somnifera, in powder preparations with water or milk vehicles. Both rows attribute the practice to Bhavaprakasha's Vatavyadhi Cikitsa.

Reported direction: Intended therapeutic use; no measured tremor outcome is given.
Authenticity: Original institutional handbook inspected, including the table visually.
Efficacy evidence: Traditional recommendation; no patient comparison or outcome denominator. Lower weight than controlled clinical evidence.
Open trail: Authenticate the cited historical passage, edition and botanical gloss. The 2010 document does not establish centuries of continuous use or a modern Parkinson diagnosis in an earlier text.
