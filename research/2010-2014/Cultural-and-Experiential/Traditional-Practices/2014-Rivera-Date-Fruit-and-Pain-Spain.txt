Title: Date-palm fruit and reported analgesic uses in Spanish ethnobotanical and historical literature
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1447564/2/RdF_14_1_Palmera.pdf
Source kind: Traditional-practice documentation in an ethnobotanical and historical synthesis
Publication date: 2014
Date basis: Revista de Fitoterapia 2014, volume 14 issue 1, pp. 67–81. The inspected publication is dated separately from the historical works it discusses.
Source locator: Rivera and colleagues, print p. 70, Phoenix dactylifera fruit discussion, and figures 5 and 6.
Community/context: The authors compile historical and ethnobotanical sources from Spain and discuss possible transmission of earlier texts into later material. Some uses attributed to Spanish literature may reflect the copying or adaptation of sources from elsewhere.
Exposure and reported symptom: The fruit discussion names Phoenix dactylifera dates for analgesic uses and postpartum pain. The relevant passage does not establish a dried-fruit preparation. Spathes from the date palm and sap from the Canary Island date palm are separate materials.
Reported direction: The documented indication was intended pain relief.
Authenticity: The institutional repository supplies the original Spanish publication. The source summarizes earlier use records. Figures classify references by use and plant part.
Efficacy evidence: The traditional indication does not report patient-level pain outcomes, an untreated comparison, use frequency among patients or a Parkinson diagnosis. Figure counts describe documented uses. Obstetric syrup trials and whole-date abdominal pain findings are independent material with different exposures.

Access and follow-up: Identify the underlying fruit-specific citation and any description of preparation or route. Separate references to fresh fruit, dried fruit and non-fruit plant material.
