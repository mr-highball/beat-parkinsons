Title: Ayurvedic botanical use described in a Parkinson model study
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Traditional practices discussed in a research article
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4310928/
Publication date: 2014-08-04
Date basis: NLM electronic date for the inspected publication. The age or continuity of the described traditions is separate.
Reviewed: 2026-11-19

Context: The authors describe plant use in Ayurveda for symptoms compared with Parkinson disease. They cite historical or modern accounts for Withania somnifera, Bacopa monnieri, Centella asiatica and Sida cordifolia. Their experiments also include a modern branded Mucuna pruriens seed powder. The publication documents the authors' account of the practice, with no original interview or participant follow-up.

Reported direction: Historically favorable reported use motivates the experiment. The paper does not report observations from people receiving those preparations in customary settings. The fly experiment recorded different climbing directions across plant and genotype.

Authenticity: The original article verifies that the authors describe this practice and tested preparations. It does not independently verify the age, continuity or uniformity of every plant use.

Efficacy evidence: The measured experiment is a fly climbing assay. Historical attribution, a model comparison and an open human study of a distinct Mucuna product are different evidence. None supplies a dried fig effect on visual hallucination frequency.
