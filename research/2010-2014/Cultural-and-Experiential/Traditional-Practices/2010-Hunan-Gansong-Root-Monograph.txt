Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Traditional practitioner and regulatory monograph

Date basis: 2010 edition on the cover, with a January 2010 preface. The current PDF hosting date does not date the practice.
Location inspected: Cover, preface and the Gansong entry on printed page 85.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25

Community/context: The Hunan provincial drug regulator's standard documents preparations for traditional Chinese medicine practice in that jurisdiction.
Exposure: The monograph identifies Gansong (甘松) as dried root and rhizome of Nardostachys chinensis or Nardostachys jatamansi, using the botanical names as printed. It describes preparing short dried slices and distinguishes decoction from topical routes.
Reported symptom or indication: Traditional use concerns epigastric or abdominal fullness, poor appetite and vomiting, with external use for toothache and swollen feet. The wording about regulating qi and relieving discomfort belongs to the monograph's traditional framework.
Reported direction: Intended symptomatic benefit. The entry includes preparation guidance instead of patient outcomes.
Authenticity: Original provincial standard hosted by Hunan's medical products administration.
Efficacy evidence: No patient comparison, verified Parkinson diagnosis, symptom effect estimate or adverse-outcome denominator appears in this entry. Gansong is outside the frozen culinary vocabulary. The traditional term for relieving constraint has no equivalent clinical depression measurement in this source.

Open trail: The 2026 paper cites the 2020 national Pharmacopoeia, a separate edition. Its tested isolated nardosinone and levodopa-induced dyskinesia model differ from the provincial monograph's preparation and indications.
