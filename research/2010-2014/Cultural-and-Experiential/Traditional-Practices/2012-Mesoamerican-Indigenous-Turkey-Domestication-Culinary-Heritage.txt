Zooarchaeological, ancient-DNA, and mitogenomic analyses place the earliest domestication of the wild turkey (Meleagris gallopavo) in Mesoamerica between roughly 700 and 200 BC, with Late Preclassic (300 BC-AD 100) remains at El Mirador in the Maya lowlands evidencing long-distance ceremonial and culinary exchange (Thornton et al. 2012, PMC3414452; Ferrari et al. 2024, PMC12674428). Following sixteenth-century introduction to Europe and later North American holiday traditions, roasted and baked turkey became a widely consumed lean high-protein festive and everyday meat. Historical culinary records document its role as celebratory sustenance rather than a targeted remedy for tremor, sleep, or neurodegenerative disorders.
