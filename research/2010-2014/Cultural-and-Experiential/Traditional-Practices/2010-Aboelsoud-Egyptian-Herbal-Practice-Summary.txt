Title: Herbal medicine in ancient Egypt
Source: https://journal-backups.lon1.digitaloceanspaces.com/uploads/main/article/article1380374686_Aboelsoud.pdf
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Historical review of traditional plant uses
Publication date: 2010-01-18
Date basis: Date on Journal of Medicinal Plants Research 4(2), pp. 82–86. No historical date is assigned to an individual use without its corresponding manuscript.
Text coverage: Publisher full text, plant list and concluding plant-use discussion.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25, document and indication scope
Reported direction: The modern historical review describes traditional uses of sweet basil in a heart context, cardamom for digestive discomfort or flatulence, and turmeric in wound care.
Authenticity: Traceable modern publication. Its plant-use summaries are separate from a directly verified passage in an ancient source.
Efficacy evidence: Reported uses without measured clinical comparators, a confirmed Parkinson population or the mapped symptom evaluations.

The paper identifies Ocimum basilicum and cardamom by scientific
names in its plant descriptions and attributes wound use to
Curcuma longa under a variant spelling. The 2021 historical review
repeats these attributions. The two papers are a citation chain,
rather than independent observations of symptom effects.

The 2021 review also names saffron among plants and lists cinnamon
for broader digestive and gum indications, with different cited
authorities. These entries do not establish that the same ancient
manuscript records every use. Plant identity, historical date and
individual efficacy require separate evidence.
