Title: Iranian Traditional Medicine dietary classification of wild trout as a recommended food for melancholia and low mood
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4110782/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2014-01-01
Date basis: Published peer-reviewed open-access review of classical Iranian Traditional Medicine texts (Tavakkoli-Kakhki et al., Avicenna J Phytomed 2014; PMC4110782; Winter 2014 issue).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Classical Iranian Traditional Medicine sources prescribe wild trout among moist, omega-3-rich foods favored for melancholia/depressed patients (contrasted with beef and lentils); modern controlled clinical trial Shapouri-Moghaddam 2017 in older Iranian adults yielded an inconclusive continuous GDS difference (p=0.295).
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed ethnopharmacological catalog of Persian medical texts inspected during Round 7 review.
Efficacy evidence: Historical humoral dietary classification only; melancholia does not map directly to modern Parkinson disease symptoms, and a subsequent Iranian trout feeding RCT (Shapouri-Moghaddam 2017) did not show a statistically significant continuous depression reduction.

In a 2014 peer-reviewed survey of classical Iranian Traditional Medicine (ITM) dietary authorities (Tavakkoli-Kakhki et al., Avicenna J Phytomed; PMC4110782), authors cataloged foods historically prescribed or prohibited for melancholia and depressed states and cross-referenced their USDA omega-6:omega-3 ratios. Wild trout was explicitly classified among prescribed dietary foods alongside milk, egg, lamb, chicken, saffron, and leafy herbs, whereas beef, broad beans, lentils, and eggplant were prohibited under humoral temperament theory. Traditional prescriptions reflect historical dietetic frameworks rather than controlled clinical proof: notably, a later 3-month Iranian RCT supplying 480 g/week trout to older adults (Shapouri-Moghaddam 2017) found an inconclusive adjusted continuous Geriatric Depression Scale difference (-0.17, 95% CI -0.48 to 0.15, p=0.295), and no study has evaluated trout for Parkinson disease mood or anxiety.
