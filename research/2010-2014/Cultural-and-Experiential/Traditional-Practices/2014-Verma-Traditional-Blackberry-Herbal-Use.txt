Title: Traditional European Herbal Framing of Rubus fruticosus (Blackberry) as an Astringent and Nutritional Tonic
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4127818/
Source kind: Peer-reviewed pharmacognosy review and Polish ethnobotanical laboratory study (PMID:25125882, PMC4127818; PMID:31221152, PMC6587261)
Publication date: 2014-07-01
Date basis: Published 2014 in Pharmacognosy Reviews (PMID:25125882, PMC4127818), cross-referenced with Polish traditional Rosaceae diarrhea study (2019, PMID:31221152, PMC6587261).
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Mixed / non-PD traditional framing (European and Polish herbal traditions use tannin-rich Rubus fruticosus parts primarily as antidiarrheal/astringent remedies rather than laxatives, while valuing the anthocyanin-, vitamin C-, and pectin-rich berries as a restorative tonic).
Authenticity: Verified directly via indexed PubMed/PMC open-access records (Verma et al. 2014; Komiazyk et al. 2019).
Efficacy evidence: Low (traditional European folk-medicine documentation; does not establish efficacy or harm for Parkinson disease symptoms, and tannin astringency applies primarily to leaves/roots/unripe extracts rather than ripe culinary berries).

Verma et al. (Pharmacognosy Reviews, 2014; PMID:25125882; PMC4127818) review traditional Northern European herbal uses of wild blackberry (Rubus fruticosus), noting longstanding folk applications as an astringent, antidiarrheal, antidysenteric, antimicrobial, and antioxidant remedy attributed to tannins, gallic acid, anthocyanins, vitamin C, and pectin across leaf, root, stem, and berry preparations. Komiazyk et al. (BMC Complementary and Alternative Medicine, 2019; PMID:31221152; PMC6587261) similarly document traditional Polish use of Rosaceae extracts including Rubus fruticosus for diarrhea. Traditional herbal literature does not document blackberries as a treatment for Parkinson motor symptoms or constipation, and distinguishes leaf/root tannin astringents from ripe whole culinary fruit.
