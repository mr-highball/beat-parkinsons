Title: External saffron-water use for sleeplessness in the Iranian traditional medicine literature
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Traditional practices, historical literature compilation
Publication date: 2011
Reviewed: 2026-11-19
Date basis: Autumn issue of the inspected modern source. The specific practice has no separately verified historical date.
Source: https://applications.emro.who.int/imemrf/Avicenna_J_Phytomedicine/Avicenna_J_Phytomedicine_2011_1_2_57_66.pdf
Citation: Mousavi and Bathaie, Historical uses of saffron: Identifying potential new avenues for modern research. Avicenna Journal of Phytomedicine 1(2), 57–66.
Coverage: Original journal scan, Iranian traditional medicine section, page 61. The authors' cited traditional books still need individual inspection.

Community and context: The authors summarize writings on Iranian traditional medicine and describe a saffron preparation for several head and neck symptoms. Their introduction identifies the culinary spice as dried Crocus sativus stigma.
Exposure and symptom: Page 61 describes external use of saffron water after boiling for insomnia and severe headaches. Saffron tea appears separately as a culinary custom. The account does not connect the tea to the insomnia claim or specify a modern diagnostic measure.
Reported direction: Benefit was traditionally attributed to the external preparation.
Evidence and limits: An identifiable published literature account verifies the attribution and external route. It supplies no patient series, denominator, comparator, adverse-event record, Parkinson diagnosis or insomnia severity measure.
Related research: The oral stigma-extract comparisons and the Parkinson powder trial use different exposures. The 2022 phytoconstituent review retells the practice and does not supply an independent traditional observation.
Access and follow-up: Trace the authors' cited editions and translations to determine which historical texts describe this route and symptom.

Authenticity: Identifiable original journal pages in the WHO archive. Underlying editions are cited by the authors and were not independently compared.
Efficacy evidence: The source reports customary external use without a measured effect or an oral Parkinson comparator.
