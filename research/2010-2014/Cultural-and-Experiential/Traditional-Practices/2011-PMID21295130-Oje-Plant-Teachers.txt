Title: Plantas con madre, plant teachers and the ojé account in East-Central Peru
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Traditional practices, primary ethnography
Publication date: 2011-02-01
Reviewed: 2026-11-19
Date basis: NLM electronic publication. The field interviews and observations took place in 2003–2008.
Source: https://bibliography.maps.org/resources/download/13848
PMID: 21295130
DOI: 10.1016/j.jep.2011.01.042
Coverage: Original article, pp. 739–752, including Methods, Table 1 and section 3.1.1.

Community and context: The team interviewed and observed 29 healers, three apprentices and four herbalists in the East-Central Peruvian Amazon. The study groups plants by reported roles in healing and apprenticeship, including initial cleansing and later strengthening.
Exposure and reported symptom: Table 1 lists crude latex and bark/trunk decoction for Ficus insipida and crude latex for Ficus schultesii. Both species can be called ojé. It lists an additional named renaco species, Ficus nymphaeifolia. That identification does not establish the species used in the different 1984 account.
Reported direction: The interviewees describe the two ojé species principally for intestinal parasites and cleansing. One participant recounts that the spirit of ojé presents itself to a person in bed during the dieta. The account does not specify wakefulness, sensory modality, a separate measured exposure or recurrence. It gives no clinical Parkinson symptom direction.
Evidence and limits: The 29 reports in Table 1 for crude latex in each of the two ojé entries are interview citations of use. They are not evidence of 58 independent outcomes or people with visions. The hospital toxicity discussion cites the 2005 retrospective series and does not create a second set of cases.
Authenticity: Full original journal copy with methods, plant identifications, and an attributed account. Preserve the authors' shared common names and species distinctions.
Efficacy evidence: The ethnographic observation documents a reported interpretation. It supplies no comparison of edible dried Ficus carica flesh and no assessment of visual episode frequency in Parkinson disease.
