Title: Ayurvedic Shatapushpa, Persian Medicine, and British Gripe-Water Carminative Traditions of Dill Seed (Anethum graveolens)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3249919/
Source kind: Ethnopharmacological review of Ayurvedic, Persian, and European traditional practices
Publication date: 2010
Date basis: Peer-reviewed ethnopharmacological review publication date (Jana & Shekhawat, 2010, Pharmacogn Rev, PMC3249919; corroborated by Rao et al., 2023, PMID:36944117 and Moroccan ethnobotany Najem et al., 2020); historical traditions predate publication
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: Favorable traditional carminative, stomachic, antispasmodic, menstrual-pain, and diuretic attributions; Moroccan literature review notes prolonged-use toxicity/convulsion caution
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed pharmacognosy and Ayurvedic reviews distinguishing seed vs foliage phytochemistry and separating modern publication dates from classical texts
Efficacy evidence: Traditional carminative and digestive context only; does not establish clinical Parkinson's symptom efficacy; formal human dysmenorrhea and labor RCTs are evaluated separately

Community/context: Ayurvedic medicine ('Shatapushpa' / Anethum graveolens and closely related Indian dill A. sowa, reviewed in Jana & Shekhawat, 2010, PMC3249919 and Rao et al., 2023, PMID:36944117), Traditional Persian Medicine ('Shevid' seed / 'Tukhm Soya'), Moroccan Middle Atlas folk medicine (dill fruit voucher 2041/IS/N38 for gastric complaints, research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2020-Najem-Dill-Fruit-Ethnobotany-Extract.txt), and 19th-century British/European 'gripe water' (historically dill seed oil carminative infusions for infant colic and gas).
Exposure and reported symptom: Dried fruits/seeds (whole, toasted, ground powder, or boiled water infusion) used orally as a carminative, stomachic, antispasmodic, diuretic, galactagogue, and soothing agent for abdominal gas, colicky cramps, menstrual pain, and mild digestive sluggishness. Phytochemical reviews emphasize separating carvone-rich dill seeds from alpha-phellandrene/dill-ether-rich fresh dill foliage.
Evidence and limits: Traditional accounts lack controlled Parkinson's evaluations, and Moroccan ethnobotanical safety tables cite older literature cautions regarding toxicity/convulsions with prolonged excessive fruit use (Najem 2020). Formal dill seed RCTs in dysmenorrhea (PMID:25097605, PMID:31383440) and childbirth anxiety (PMID:31705450) are assessed separately.
