Title: Traditional Chinese Medicine (Ba Jiao Hui Xiang), European Pharmacopoeia, and Colic Tea Traditions of Star Anise (Illicium verum) with Adulteration Cautions
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21549817/
Source kind: Ethnopharmacological review of traditional medical texts and folk remedies
Publication date: 2011
Date basis: Peer-reviewed ethnopharmacological review publication date (Wang et al., 2011, J Ethnopharmacol, PMID:21549817; corroborated by Sharafan et al., 2022, PMC8839413 and Zou et al., 2023, PMC10648513); historical TCM and folk heritage predates publication
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: Favorable traditional culinary/carminative/analgesic use of authentic Illicium verum; documented severe neurotoxicity (seizures, hypertonia, myoclonus) when adulterated with Illicium anisatum or given as concentrated infant colic teas
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed ethnopharmacological syntheses citing Chinese Pharmacopoeia/TCM classics, European Pharmacopoeia, and clinical toxicology case series
Efficacy evidence: Traditional culinary and carminative context only; does not establish efficacy for Parkinson's disease symptoms, while safety records confirm a major botanical adulteration hazard

Community/context: Traditional Chinese Medicine ('Ba Jiao Hui Xiang', 八角茴香, fruit of Illicium verum Hook. f.), Southeast Asian cuisine, European Pharmacopoeia (Anisi stellati fructus since 2002), and Hispanic/Caribbean household remedies ('anis estrellado'), as synthesized in Wang et al. (2011, PMID:21549817), Sharafan et al. (2022, PMC8839413), and Zou et al. (2023, PMC10648513).
Exposure and reported symptom: Dried star-shaped fruits and ground culinary spice traditionally used in TCM to warm Yang, disperse cold, regulate Qi, and ease abdominal colic, vomiting, stomachache, insomnia, and rheumatic pain, and in Hispanic/European folk practice as an infused tea for infant colic and adult dyspepsia/flatulence.
Critical safety and botanical boundary: True Chinese star anise (Illicium verum, Schisandraceae) is botanically unrelated to green anise seed (Pimpinella anisum, Apiaceae). Critically, dried or ground Illicium verum is visually indistinguishable from highly toxic Japanese star anise (Illicium anisatum / shikimi) and Illicium lanceolatum, which contain high levels of anisatin, a potent non-competitive GABA-A receptor antagonist. Adulterated or concentrated star anise teas have caused documented human seizure epidemics (Johanns 2002, PMID:12014242) and repeated infant poisonings with seizures, tremors, hypertonia, nystagmus, and liver injury (Ize-Ludlow 2004, PMID:15492355; Perret 2011, PMID:21652187; Obando-Pacheco 2016, research/2015-2019/Source-Text/2016-Obando-Pacheco-Mixed-Anise-Infant-Poisoning.txt).
Evidence and limits: Customary carminative and pain-easing attributions lack controlled human clinical trials in adults or Parkinson's disease.
