Reported direction: Communal, religious, and nourishing whole-grain culinary traditions for boiled whole wheat berries; Traditional Persian Medicine texts associate roasted wheat bran (Saviq) with digestive soothing; no historical Parkinson clinical claim.
Authenticity: Official UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage inscription (00388, 2011) corroborated by peer-reviewed food-science and Persian medicine publications (PMC9654948; PMID:39847133).
Efficacy evidence: Ceremonial and culinary-heritage record only; documents long-standing whole-kernel wheat consumption without establishing controlled efficacy on Parkinson disease symptoms.

Ceremonial and communal foodways of boiled intact wheat berries:
Intact whole-grain wheat kernels (wheat berries, Triticum aestivum / T. durum) occupy a central place in ceremonial foodways across Anatolia, the Eastern Mediterranean, the Caucasus, and Eastern Europe. In 2011, UNESCO inscribed Turkey's Ceremonial Keskek Tradition on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (RL 00388): for weddings, circumcision feasts, and holidays, communities collectively wash, stone-pound, and slow-boil whole wheat berries with meat in huge cauldrons accompanied by prayers, davul drums, and zurna music until reaching a dense porridge consistency (also analyzed in Ozcan 2022, PMC9654948).

Parallel ritual and traditional-medicine lineages:
In Eastern Orthodox Christian communities (Greece, Cyprus, Serbia, Romania, the Levant), Koliva—boiled whole wheat berries dressed with spices, nuts, raisins, and pomegranate seeds—is blessed at memorial liturgies to symbolize life, death, and resurrection. In Traditional Persian Medicine (TPM), roasted whole-wheat / wheat-bran preparations ("Wheat Saviq") are traditionally used alongside rosewater-saffron Jollab syrup to soothe gastrointestinal discomfort and weakness (recently evaluated as a bundled intervention in pediatric chemotherapy patients, PMID:39847133).

Evidence limits:
These traditions document deep cultural continuity and intact-grain cooking methods (long soaking and boiling) rather than controlled clinical efficacy for Parkinson disease, and traditional wheat foods remain strictly contraindicated in celiac disease.
