Title: Traditional and faith-healing accounts in a rural Tanzania Parkinson study
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Primary qualitative interviews and community discussions
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3088907/
Publication date: 2011-04-08
Date basis: Original journal publication
Existing accession: research/2010-2014/Source-Text/PMC3088907.txt
Reviewed: 2026-11-17

Mshana and colleagues interviewed 28 clinically examined Parkinson participants, 28 carers, health workers and two traditional healers. Accounts describe diverse explanations for shaking and walking difficulties, traditional treatments and faith healing. One participant reported no pain relief after an unspecified treatment involving incisions and applied medicine. Other accounts include prayer; botanical identity and a separate tremor response are not established.

Reported direction: Mixed or unspecified; the pain account is not a resting-tremor result.
Authenticity: Original open-access research already archived; narratives are attributed experiences rather than a treatment trial.
Efficacy evidence: No controlled tremor comparison. Lower causal weight than controlled intervention research.
Context: Access and cost affected conventional treatment. Community beliefs were not uniform.
Study family: Reuses the existing study; later overlapping reports require deduplication.
Open trail: Remedy identity, ritual details and symptom-specific outcomes.
