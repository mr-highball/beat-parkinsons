Title: Parkinson's UK Forum discussion on cinnamon, Ceylon versus Cassia coumarin safety and Parkinson diet lists
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Conversation
Source: https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/cinnamon-and-parkinsons/7567
Publication date: 2014-07-12
Date basis: Opening post date shown on the public forum (2014-07-12); replies reviewed through 2014-07-15.
Reviewed: 2026-11-21 UTC

A participant shared media coverage of a Rush University mouse study in which cinnamon was metabolized to sodium benzoate and associated with neuroprotection and motor preservation in mice, asking whether members should begin adding cinnamon to food. A caregiver replied they immediately purchased cinnamon to sprinkle on cereal, toast, and yogurt for their spouse, while another participant added cinnamon to an informal personal dietary list already containing sesame seeds, pumpkin seeds, blueberries, almonds, wholemeal bread, broad beans, bran, prunes, and licorice.

Multiple replies raised uncertainty and skepticism: participants questioned what dose would be meaningful, noted that sodium benzoate (E211) is a common food preservative with debated safety, and questioned whether mouse findings translate to humans. A key safety reply highlighted the distinction between Ceylon cinnamon and Cassia cinnamon, cautioning that Cassia contains substantially higher coumarin levels and that pre-ground cinnamon powder often leaves species identity unknown, prompting the original poster to acknowledge they had mistakenly assumed all culinary cinnamon was automatically safe. No participant reported observed clinical symptom changes after taking cinnamon.
