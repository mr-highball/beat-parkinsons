Title: Parkinson's UK Forum exchange contrasting perceived dexterity gains on coconut oil with acute PD worsening on a ketogenic fat regimen
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/coconut-oil/4164
Source kind: Conversation
Publication date: 2012-05-02
Date basis: Public thread opening post dated 2012-05-02; replies inspected through 2012-08-06 (Posts #1-#12).

In a Parkinson's UK public forum thread opened May 2, 2012 (all participant handles omitted), a member asked whether others had tried coconut oil after an occupational health contact mentioned Alzheimer's ketone reports.
Contrasting firsthand experiences preserved in the thread:
- Favorable account (Posts #3 and #6, May 3, 2012): One participant reported taking approximately 1 level teaspoon of coconut oil once weekly (off a spoon or spread on bread) and perceiving improved coordination, hand dexterity, touch sensitivity, and lower Parkinson medication requirements, noticing when a dose was missed. Oil grade (virgin vs refined) and objective motor scores were unspecified.
- Adverse/mixed ketogenic diet account (Posts #10 and #12, August 4-6, 2012): Another participant initiated a near-ketogenic high-fat regimen citing coconut oil, palm oil, butter, and cheese as ketone sources (explicitly flagging cardiovascular and constipation concerns). After briefly perceiving less daytime sleepiness and easier walking, two days later the member described the near-ketogenic regimen as a 'disaster' with markedly worse Parkinson symptoms and ineffective medication until eating a sugary biscuit.
Evidence and limits: Unverified forum self-reports with mixed exposure attribution (low-frequency spoonful vs whole ketogenic diet) and no clinical verification; illustrates both positive anecdotal expectations and acute adverse metabolic/medication deterioration in Parkinson's disease.
