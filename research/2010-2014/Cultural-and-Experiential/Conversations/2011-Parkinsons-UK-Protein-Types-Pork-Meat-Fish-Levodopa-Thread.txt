Title: Parkinson's UK forum discussion comparing pork, red meat, fish, and dairy effects on Madopar and Sinemet absorption
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/protein-and-levadopa/3778
Source kind: Conversation
Publication date: 2011-08-20
Date basis: Opening post timestamp on Parkinson's UK public forum (2011-08-20; firsthand replies through 2011-08-29).

In a public Parkinson's UK Community Forum thread opened in August 2011, a member asked whether different dietary protein sources—red meat, white meat, dairy, eggs, nuts, and beans—differed in how strongly they interfered with levodopa absorption. Multiple contributors described spacing Sinemet or Madopar 30 to 60 minutes before meals or 1.5 to 2 hours afterward, and shifting larger protein meals toward evening to reduce daytime wearing-off. One participant on Madopar judged meat to be among the stronger blockers and avoided eating bacon and eggs at breakfast, while another noted that high-fat, high-protein meals lingered longer in a slowed Parkinson's stomach and therefore shifted toward lighter fish and chicken meals with only occasional red meat. Conversely, two contributors reported striking inter-individual variation: one experienced severe medication blockade from milk, soft cheese, and scrambled eggs yet tolerated tuna and an occasional bacon sandwich without noticing reduced medication efficacy, and another taking controlled-release levodopa doubted dietary protein affected them. All personal identifiers are omitted. The thread aligns qualitatively with clinical cohort findings of substantial patient-to-patient heterogeneity in dietary protein-levodopa interactions.
