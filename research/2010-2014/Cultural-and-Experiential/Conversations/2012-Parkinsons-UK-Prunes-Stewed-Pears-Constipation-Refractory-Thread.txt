Title: Parkinson UK forum discussion contrasting temporary relief and refractory failure with prunes and stewed pears for PD constipation
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/constipation/2386
Source kind: Conversation
Publication date: 2012-10-03
Date basis: Opening post timestamp on Parkinson UK public community forum (2012-10-03; replies reviewed through 2012-11-04).

In a public Parkinson UK Community Forum thread opened on 3 October 2012 with replies through 4 November 2012, people with Parkinson disease and caregivers shared mixed firsthand experiences using dietary prunes and stewed pears for PD constipation. The opening contributor, diagnosed with Parkinson disease four years earlier, reported that prunes, stewed pears, and psyllium husks had each worked for a long while before losing effectiveness as constipation progressed, prompting sequential use of senna, macrogol, and lactulose. Two subsequent contributors provided explicit no-benefit counter-accounts: one caregiver noted that eating prunes every morning with breakfast alongside daily fruit failed to prevent severe constipation requiring high-dose macrogol and hospital attention for a multi-day bowel blockage, while another PD participant reported trying prunes, bran, and psyllium with no effect before finding consistent relief only with prescribed macrogol. All personal names and identifiers are omitted. These anonymized community observations document both initial perceived help from stewed pears and prunes and clinically important failure in refractory PD dysmotility.
