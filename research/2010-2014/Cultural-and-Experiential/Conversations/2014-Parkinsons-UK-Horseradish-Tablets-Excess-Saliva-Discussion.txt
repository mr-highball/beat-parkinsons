Title: Public Parkinson's UK forum discussion mentioning horseradish tablets among non-prescription tips for excess saliva
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/coughing-excess-saliva/7710
Source kind: Conversation
Publication date: 2014
Date basis: Opening post date (2014-09-01) on publicly accessible Parkinson's UK Discourse thread t/coughing-excess-saliva/7710.

In a September 2014 Parkinson's UK forum thread opened by a person with Parkinson's experiencing troublesome excess saliva and coughing who hoped to avoid adding another prescription tablet to an 18-pill daily regimen, a community member shared a list of speech-therapy and herbal tips: addressing stooped posture, elevating the head during sleep, chewing gum, sipping fluids, noting that acidic/citrus products may increase saliva, and listing horseradish tablets alongside golden rod drops, hibiscus tea, and sage tea as products reputed to reduce saliva. No participant in the thread reported actually taking grated horseradish or horseradish tablets or observing a saliva change from horseradish; another participant noted opposite dry-mouth symptoms, and the opening poster updated the thread in November 2014 reporting that low-dose prescription trihexyphenidyl (0.5 mg/day) relieved the excess saliva. This secondhand tablet suggestion contrasts with traditional herbal descriptions of pungent grated horseradish as an acute sialogogue and supplies no clinical evidence.
