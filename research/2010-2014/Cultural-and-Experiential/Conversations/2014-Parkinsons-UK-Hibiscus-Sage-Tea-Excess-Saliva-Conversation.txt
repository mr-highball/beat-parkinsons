Title: Public Parkinson's forum discussion mentioning hibiscus and sage teas for excess saliva
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/coughing-excess-saliva/7710
Source kind: Conversation
Publication date: 2014
Date basis: Opening post date (2014-09-01) on the public Parkinson's UK Discourse forum thread.

In a September 2014 public thread on the Parkinson's UK community forum discussing persistent excess saliva, coughing, and embarrassment in Parkinson's disease, a peer responder suggested speech-and-language therapy tips (posture, chewing gum, regular sips, avoiding acidic/citrus triggers) alongside informal herbal options reputed to reduce saliva, specifically listing horseradish tablets, goldenrod drops, and hibiscus and sage teas. The original poster expressed reluctance to add more pills on top of an existing regimen that included Parkinson's, blood-pressure, and vertigo medications, and considered herbal approaches before later updating the thread in November 2014 that low-dose prescription trihexyphenidyl relieved the excess saliva. No commenter reported confirmed firsthand efficacy from hibiscus tea for drooling, and the thread illustrates real-world polypharmacy overlap with antihypertensive and dizziness medications in Parkinson's disease.
