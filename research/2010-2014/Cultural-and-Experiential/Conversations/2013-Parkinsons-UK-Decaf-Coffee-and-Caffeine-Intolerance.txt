Title: Parkinson's UK forum discussion of decaffeinated coffee, caffeine intolerance, and preclinical coffee compounds
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/eicosanoyl-5-hydroxytryptamide/2875
Source kind: Conversation
Publication date: 2013-01-02
Date basis: Public thread opening timestamp on the Parkinson's UK Community Forum (2013-01-02).

In a January 2013 Parkinson's UK Community Forum thread referencing animal alpha-synuclein research on the coffee constituent eicosanoyl-5-hydroxytryptamide (EHT), a caregiver asked whether decaffeinated coffee retains EHT because regular caffeinated coffee caused severe agitation/intolerance in their father with Parkinson's disease ('doesn't tolerate caffeine very well... drives him almost literally crazy'). Community responders pointed to preclinical Drosophila research on decaffeinated coffee while warning that achieving mouse-study EHT doses from beverage coffee would require consuming implausible quantities (~2 kg of coffee per day). The discussion highlights both why some people with PD avoid caffeinated coffee in favor of decaf due to jitteriness/agitation and why preclinical decaf coffee compounds do not directly translate to clinical symptom relief.
