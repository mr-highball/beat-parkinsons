Title: Historical East and Southeast Asian Experience with Unpolished Brown Rice and Beriberi Neuropathy
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19892133/
Source kind: Peer-reviewed historical neurology review (PMID:19892133)
Publication date: 2010-01-01
Date basis: Publication year 2010 in Handbook of Clinical Neurology (PMID:19892133; documenting 19th-century Asian dietary reforms from the 1880s-1920s, distinct from modern publication date).
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Favorable historical nutritional attribution (unpolished brown rice and rice polishings prevented/cured beriberi sensorimotor neuropathy triggered by polished white rice diets, attributable to thiamin).
Authenticity: Verified peer-reviewed historical synthesis (Lanska DJ, Handbook of Clinical Neurology, 2010, DOI 10.1016/S0072-9752(08)02130-1).
Efficacy evidence: Low for Parkinson disease (historical nutritional-deficiency observations concern thiamin-deficiency beriberi polyneuropathy rather than idiopathic Parkinson disease motor or non-motor symptoms).

Lanska (Handbook of Clinical Neurology, 2010; PMID:19892133) documents how 19th-century mechanized rice polishing in Asia precipitated widespread beriberi (progressive sensorimotor neuropathy with weakness and gait impairment). Kanehiro Takaki's 1880s Japanese Navy dietary reforms and Christiaan Eijkman's 1889 Java observations showed that replacing polished white rice with unpolished brown rice or rice polishings prevented and reversed polyneuritis, leading to the isolation of thiamin from rice polishings in 1926. This historical record illustrates longstanding cultural and early medical recognition of unpolished brown rice's neurological B-vitamin value, distinct from modern Parkinson disease efficacy.
