Title: Traditional Lower Tapajos Amazonian Brazil-Nut-Rich Diet and Absence of Clinical Selenosis Signs
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21856002/
Source kind: Field ethno-nutritional and clinical community survey (PMID 21856002)
Publication date: 2012
Date basis: Journal publication year 2012 documenting ongoing traditional riverine food systems in 12 Amazonian communities.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: Safety/tolerability context (no cutaneous or breath signs of selenosis observed in traditional high-selenium Brazil-nut-consuming communities; no Parkinson's symptom efficacy measured).
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed field investigation of 448 participants across 12 Lower Tapajos River communities (PMID 21856002).
Efficacy evidence: Contextual local food-system safety record only; does not establish Parkinson's symptom efficacy and must be weighed alongside EFSA 2023 upper-limit cautions.

Field survey of 448 residents (ages 15-87) across 12 riverine communities along the Lower Tapajos River in the Brazilian Amazon where traditional diets regularly include Brazil nuts alongside local fish and game meat (Lemire et al. 2012, PMID 21856002). Although blood selenium spanned 103.3 to 1,500.2 ug/L (median 228.4 ug/L) and exceeded conventional U.S. EPA toxicity thresholds in some individuals, structured nurse examinations of hair, nails, skin, and breath found no clinical signs of selenosis associated with selenium biomarkers. The authors noted that dietary chemical form and co-exposures (such as mercury) may influence tolerability and called for cardiometabolic and long-term follow-up during dietary transition.
