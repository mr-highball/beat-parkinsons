Title: Summer Savory Traditional Claims Contrasted With Null Human Oral Clinical Trials and Zero PD Forum Reports
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4142450/
Source kind: Ethnomedicinal overview and experiential coverage check
Publication date: 2014
Date basis: Peer-reviewed publication year (Hamidpour et al. 2014, J Tradit Complement Med / PMC4142450), updated with Round 7 Parkinson's UK public forum check (2026-11-26).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Mixed/neutral: traditional Persian and European accounts credit summer savory with soothing GI cramps and mild aches, whereas subsequent randomized human oral trials found null effects in GERD (p=0.989) and knee osteoarthritis pain (p=0.38), and zero PD patient reports exist on Parkinson's UK forum.
Authenticity: Traceable open-access review (Hamidpour et al. 2014, PMID:25161917, PMC4142450) cross-checked against local Iranian trial records (IRCT2015072215860N2, IRCT2013112515542N1) and Parkinson's UK forum.
Efficacy evidence: Traditional lore has not translated into positive oral human clinical trials; zero PD experiential accounts documented.

Ethnomedicinal Claims vs Clinical Reality:
Hamidpour et al. (2014, PMC4142450) catalogued traditional digestive, antidiarrheal, antiseptic, and antinociceptive uses of Satureja hortensis while noting the predominance of animal and in vitro literature. Notably, because traditional use and Hajhashemi et al. (2000, PMID:10904162) emphasize antispasmodic and antidiarrheal (ileum-relaxing) properties, summer savory has no traditional reputation as a laxative for constipation. Subsequent controlled human oral trials of S. hortensis powder or extract were statistically NULL for gastroesophageal reflux symptoms (Faghihi Kashani 2018, IRCT2015072215860N2, p=0.989) and knee osteoarthritis pain (Mozaffari-Khosravi 2015, p=0.38).

Parkinson Community Coverage Note:
A Round 7 search of the Parkinson's UK public community forum (https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/) returned zero threads mentioning summer savory or Satureja hortensis for any Parkinson disease motor or non-motor symptom.
