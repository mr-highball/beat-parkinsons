Title: Alaska Native berry gathering in two community coping studies
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6080057.1/PMC6080057.1.pdf#page=393
Source kind: Ethnographic observation
Publication date: 2013
Reviewed: 2026-11-25
Date basis: Published Circumpolar Health proceedings for the August 2012 congress. The specific cross-project analysis occupies printed pages 391–392 and Table I.
Community and context: A community-based synthesis of interviews from two rural Yup'ik communities and a separate Alaska Native cancer-survivorship project.
Exposure: Gathering plants and berries, including named cranberries and raspberries, within a broader account of traditional practices and social support. Species, ingestion, amounts and preparation are unspecified.
Reported symptom: Coping with stressful situations and cancer survivorship. The projects did not diagnose Parkinson disease or measure clinical anxiety or depression.
Reported direction: The illustrated account describes culturally meaningful support and traditional medicine. The desirability attributed to gathering has no stand-alone symptom direction for either berry.
Authenticity: Table I in the official 2013 proceedings supplies the inspected interview example and states the qualitative method.
Efficacy evidence: The interview themes describe perceived support. There is no assigned berry exposure, comparison group or measured Parkinson symptom result.
Evidence limits: The cross-project paper draws on the existing interviews, including the cancer-survivorship group, and cannot be counted as two independent berry-effect observations. It reports qualitative experience without an assigned food, measured comparison or adverse event estimate.
Summary: The essay identifies subsistence, social relationships and traditional practices as themes in coping. An example in Table I groups cranberry and raspberry gathering with other valued plants and activities. The source authenticates that the account was documented. Effects of culinary cranberry or raspberry on named Parkinson symptoms remain open.
