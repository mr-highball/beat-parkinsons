# Food experiment evidence traced through Spanish-language records

## 2012 milk and biscuit during endoscopic swallowing assessment

Correa-Flores and colleagues used flexible endoscopy to assess swallowing in 27 people with idiopathic Parkinson’s disease in Mexico. The test used milk as the thin liquid, water with commercial thickener at nectar and pudding consistencies, and a circular biscuit as the solid. The results describe greater difficulty triggering a swallow and more vallecular and pyriform sinus residue with the biscuit condition. Thin liquid was the first consistency associated with observed aspiration, beginning at 5 mL. Seven of 27 participants aspirated during the examination overall. The biscuit and milk conditions differ in ingredients as well as texture. The report gives no baked-potato comparison for `0290` and no milk-related speech or facial-expressiveness outcomes. [Primary January–February 2012 report, Methods, Results and Figure 2](https://www.medigraphic.com/pdfs/circir/cc-2012/cc121f.pdf) · [Indexed record, PMID 22472150](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22472150/)

## 2013 and 2014 Argentine yerba mate case-control reports

Parisi and colleagues published a conference abstract on February 12, 2013. It analyzed 58 Parkinson’s cases with at least four years’ disease duration and 171 controls. The model adjusting for sex, alcohol and smoking reported a case-status odds ratio of 0.75 (95% CI 0.57–1.00) for yerba mate, 0.54 (0.30–0.97) for tea and 0.55 (0.28–1.07) for coffee. The abstract reports no symptom-change estimate by beverage. [Primary AAN abstract P03.064](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.80.7_supplement.P03.064)

Melcón and colleagues’ Spanish report first appeared online February 6, 2014. It analyzed 143 cases and 300 controls and allowed cases with at least one year of disease. Table 2’s age- and sex-adjusted case-status odds ratios were 0.684 (95% CI 0.557–0.840) for mate drunk through a bombilla and 1.123 (0.764–1.651) for mate cocido. The coffee estimate was 0.692 (0.467–1.026), and the tea estimate was 0.711 (0.480–1.053). The 2013 and 2014 reports share the core investigators and a similar design. The differing case definition and sample sizes leave the exact participant overlap unresolved.
