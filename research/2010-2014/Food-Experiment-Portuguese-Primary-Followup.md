# Primary Portuguese-language diet research

## Fiber and protein recall in Minas Gerais

Nilma Vargas Lessa, Géssica Lima Dias and Kamila de Paula Santos reported 15 adults with Parkinson’s disease aged 45–79 from Instituto Gruparkinson, Minas Gerais, in abstract PS-23-058 at the June 20–23, 2011 SBAN meeting. Three nonconsecutive 24-hour recalls yielded mean fiber intake of 12.01 g/day. Eight used dental prostheses, and 53.3% reported drinking more than six glasses of water a day. The printed mean protein intake is labeled 1.13 g/day while described as high. The intended units remain unclear. The abstract raises constipation and levodopa interaction as rationale, with no symptom outcome reported by diet exposure. [Original SBAN proceedings, p. 270](https://sban.org.br/uploads/Revista20200525073749.pdf#page=270), [local abstract text](Source-Text/2011-Lessa-SBAN-Fiber-Protein.txt)

## Dietary nutrients and motor symptoms in the 2013 SBAN proceedings

Iana Calou and colleagues presented abstract PS-16-049 at the Brazilian Society for Food and Nutrition meeting in Foz do Iguaçu, August 13–16, 2013. They used a food-frequency questionnaire, a 24-hour recall and clinical questions about symptoms and levodopa. The abstract associates daily consumption of foods rich in methionine and pyridoxine with the presence of dyskinesia (p < .04). It also associates higher vitamin B6 intake with greater severity of resting tremor (p < .05) and bradykinesia (p < .04). The 44.4% who reported constipation all reported daily intake of foods the authors classified as constipating. The abstract supplies no food names, explicit sample size, exposure-by-outcome counts, effect estimates or adjustment for medication or disease severity. It describes nutrient or food classes and cannot resolve individual-food claims. [Original SBAN proceedings, p. 364](https://sban.org.br/uploads/Revista20200525065933.pdf#page=364), [local abstract text](Source-Text/2013-Calou-SBAN-Diet-Motor-Symptoms.txt)

The 2013 abstract and the 2014 Picos article share researchers, a 24-hour recall with the same reference standard and identical percentages for high iron intake and inadequate zinc and magnesium intake. Their reported calcium figures differ, and the conference abstract does not identify the location or size of its sample. Treat the two reports as potentially overlapping. [SBAN proceedings, p. 364](https://sban.org.br/uploads/Revista20200525065933.pdf#page=364), [2014 article, Table 2](https://www.sigaa.ufpi.br/sigaa/verProducao?idProducao=729876&key=c86688c986f5cea6a3a6a946feaccb45)

## Dietary recall and levodopa use in Picos

Danielly Zilma de Sousa and colleagues reported a cross-sectional sample of ten levodopa users from the municipal drug program in Picos, Piauí. They collected a 24-hour dietary recall and a clinical survey covering symptoms, treatment and nutritional counseling in 2012. The article appeared in the July–September 2014 issue of *Boletim Informativo Geum* (5(3), pp. 23–30). Eight of ten participants consumed less than the energy benchmark. Table 2 classified iron intake as below the recommended benchmark for five, within it for one and above it for four. These are intake estimates from a recall. The tables give no symptom outcomes by iron intake or other individual food exposures. The article discusses post-meal motor blocks and protein redistribution using prior publications and says participants had received no instructions about redistributing their protein intake.
