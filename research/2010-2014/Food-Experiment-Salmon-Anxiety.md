# Salmon and anxiety follow-up

## Atlantic salmon and state versus trait anxiety in forensic inpatients

Hansen and colleagues, November 26, 2014. The Wisconsin forensic-facility trial randomized 95 men. Participants assigned to farmed Atlantic salmon were offered 150–300 g three times a week for 23 weeks, as grilled or boiled servings. Controls received chicken, pork or beef with the same sides. The report gives results from 42 fish and 43 control participants in Table 1. The salmon arm's State-Trait Anxiety Inventory state mean fell from 33.71 to 29.79. The control mean was 32.37 at baseline and 32.42 at follow-up. The group-by-time interaction was F(1,83) = 4.18, p = .043. The corrected follow-up for the salmon arm was reported as p < .03, Cohen's d = .43. Table 1 separately lists within-arm p = .006. Trait anxiety gave no significant treatment finding and changed from 37.60 to 36.93 in the fish arm and from 38.14 to 36.63 in controls. The interaction's printed η² is .46. The reported F and degrees of freedom imply standard partial η² of about .048. The paper gives no explanation for the discrepancy. [Primary paper and local text, PMID 25431880](Source-Text/PMC4276975.txt)

The paper does not identify a poached arm or report estimates by cooking technique. Table 1 uses PD for personality disorder, and the discussion of anxiety disorders uses PD for panic disorder. The paper identifies no Parkinson's cohort. The authors' earlier sleep report describes the same randomized sample, institution, July 2008 baseline and September–February intervention, salmon, meat comparator and meals. It reports sleep, daily function and heart-rate variability from the same trial. [Same-trial sleep report, PMID 24812543](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24812543/)

## Oily fish and anxiety in the ALSPAC pregnancy cohort

Vaz and colleagues, July 12, 2013. At 32 weeks, 9,530 pregnant participants in the Avon Longitudinal Study of Parents and Children completed diet and anxiety instruments. High anxiety used Crown-Crisp Experiential Index scores at or above 9. The FFQ dark or oily fish group included salmon with multiple other species. Compared with eating dark or oily fish at least one to three times weekly, the adjusted odds ratio for high anxiety was 1.38 (95% CI 1.19–1.62) for never or rarely eating it and 1.25 (1.07–1.47) for eating it once every two weeks. The study contains no separate salmon, cooking method or Parkinson's analysis. [Primary paper and local text, PMID 23874437](Source-Text/PMC3710017.txt)
