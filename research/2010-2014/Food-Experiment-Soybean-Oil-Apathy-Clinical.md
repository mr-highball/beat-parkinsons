# Oral oil comparisons and neuropsychiatric outcomes

## Corn or soybean oil comparator and total NPI in the ADCS DHA trial

In Quinn et al., 402 people with Alzheimer disease were randomized to algal DHA or placebo. The paper describes the placebo capsules as corn or soy oil. The NCT00440050 results posted August 30, 2010 give 18-month aggregate NPI changes of +2.93 (SD 13.62) in the placebo group and +5.09 (SD 15.08) in the DHA group. The primary paper reports the overall longitudinal model comparison p = .11. The 2025 secondary analysis uses the agitation or aggression item from the same trial. Public reports give no isolated apathy result, oil-by-participant estimate or processing grade. ## TEAM-AD soybean oil comparator and neuropsychiatric outcomes

Dysken et al. assigned 613 people with Alzheimer disease who used an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor to vitamin E, memantine, both or matching placebos. The vitamin E placebo capsule contained soybean oil sourced from Arista Industries. The memantine and dual-placebo arms received that oil capsule, while the other two arms received active vitamin E capsules. A 2018 analysis by Chen et al. verified distinct apathy items at baseline. It reported any apathy in 47% and NPI apathy scores of at least 4 in 152 of 613 (25%). These are baseline results by dementia severity. The main trial and posted registry compare only the total NPI across treatment arms. The registry lists a least-squares mean total change of +0.79 (SE 1.00) for vitamin E and +2.26 (SE 1.01) for dual placebo when averaged across visits. This does not identify an apathy-domain effect. The administered oil’s processing and its independent effect in Parkinson disease remain open.
