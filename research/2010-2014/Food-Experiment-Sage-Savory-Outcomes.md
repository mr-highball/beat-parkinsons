# Sage and summer savory symptom studies

## Sage extract and hot flash interference in prostate cancer

Vandecasteele and colleagues first published on June 1, 2011. Ten men on androgen deprivation received common sage extract following a pretreatment diary period. The weekly hot flash score declined, while the aggregate interference score showed no clear change. The interference scale includes sleep, concentration and enjoyment of life. Separate estimates for these items are absent. There was no concurrent control. [Primary report, Methods and Results](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1002/ptr.3528) · [Official registration](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Sage-Savory-Outcomes.md#sage-and-hot-flash-interference-registration)

## Summer savory and amyloid beta in a cell-free screen

Ishigaki and colleagues published their spice screen on August 26, 2013. Ethanolic summer savory extract inhibited amyloid beta aggregation in the fluorescence assay. The authors reported an EC50 of 0.049 ± 0.056 mg/mL and identified rosmarinic acid in activity-guided fractionation. The assay used an Alzheimer-associated protein and gives no behavioral or executive-function measurement for claim 0521. [Primary paper, Figure 8](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0072992) · [Local article text](Source-Text/PMC3753230.txt)

## Salvinorin A and animal pain assays

The 2011 review traces isolated salvinorin A to *Salvia divinorum*. Its animal pain evidence varies by assay and administration route. It supplies no result for fresh common sage leaf ingestion or Parkinson pain.
