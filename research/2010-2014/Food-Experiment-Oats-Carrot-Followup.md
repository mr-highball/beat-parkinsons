# Oats and carrot evidence

## 2012 Constipation with excessive carrot intake in one case

Sansone and Sansone described a man who reported eating six to seven pounds of carrots per week in a dieting effort. He presented with abdominal symptoms, constipation, hypercarotenemia and elevated liver enzymes. The liver enzymes normalized within a month after stopping the excessive intake. The report provides no standardized preparation, Parkinson cohort, controlled comparator, or symptom-severity change after ordinary steamed carrot consumption. It adds an adverse case to the earlier controlled stool-bulk data. ## Oat bran comparator in a coffee-fruit cognition protocol

First posted October 18, 2013. The completed trial compares coffee fruit concentrate with an oat-bran Nutrim comparator in 39 healthy adults. The protocol lists cognitive assessments, with no posted results or separate manual dexterity measure. Its oat-bran product and active comparator cannot estimate a clinical hand outcome after cooked oats. The exposure has no turmeric or curcumin. [Original record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01965795) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2013-NCT01965795-Oat-Bran-Cognition-Comparator.txt)

## Carrot frequency rhinitis and separately reported olfactory dysfunction

The 2010 Korean cross-sectional survey analyzed 2,305 people aged 10 or older. Figure 1 associates intake frequency above the median with allergic rhinitis after adjustment for sex, age, marital status, household size, stress and eating out. The original text gives a carrot odds ratio of 2.01 outside its printed 95% interval of 1.01–1.43 and gives p=.10 while calling the association significant. The forest plot differs from the text, so the exact carrot estimate remains unresolved. Table 3 separately estimates self-reported olfactory dysfunction in allergic rhinitis at 7.2% versus 1.8% in the healthy group (adjusted odds ratio 4.88, 95% CI 1.88–12.66). The two models do not estimate a carrot–smell association. The survey does not identify raw preparation or a Parkinson population.
