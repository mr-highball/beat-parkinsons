# Dietary trial follow-up from WHO ClinicalTrials.gov records, 2010–2014

## Isolated DHA and dyskinesia registration

NCT01563913 first posted on March 27, 2012. It compared isolated docosahexaenoic acid with a sugar pill in people with Parkinson disease receiving levodopa. The register includes forceplate assessment of dyskinesia. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01563913) [Local registration](Source-Text/Trials/NCT01563913.txt) [2018 results](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-WHO-CTG-Followup.md#isolated-dha-and-dyskinesia-results)

## Whey and soy protein registration

Chulalongkorn University first posted NCT01662414 on August 10, 2012. The randomized trial compared whey protein isolate with a soy protein supplement in people with idiopathic Parkinson disease. It included oxidative biomarkers, striatal imaging, and UPDRS Parts II and III. The soy supplement was a comparator, and the design gave no comparison with a soy-free inert control. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01662414) [Local registration](Source-Text/Trials/NCT01662414.txt) [2016 outcome](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-WHO-CTG-Followup.md#whey-and-soy-protein-outcomes)

## Protein redistribution during continuous levodopa infusion

NCT02274324 first posted on October 24, 2014. The registration describes an initial low-fat Western diet and crossover of two protein distribution patterns during continuous levodopa–carbidopa intestinal gel treatment. The trial includes motor diary and clinical outcomes. The archived November 2026 registry extract gives unknown study status and no posted results, and an identifier search located no linked primary outcome. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02274324) [Local registration](Source-Text/Trials/NCT02274324.txt)

## VIUSID and ALZER fatigue outcome

Giroud and colleagues identified NCT01016470 in their randomized trial of the two supplements alongside usual Parkinson treatment, published August 23, 2012. Of 100 randomized participants, 96 completed follow-up. The 12-month mean Parkinson Fatigue Scale scores were 2.20 with the combined supplements and 3.08 with placebo (p < .001, Table 2). For the separate threshold endpoint at 12 months, the narrative reports 22 placebo participants with fatigue, while the figure labels that group 38%. The groups differed in baseline disease duration. The registration describes motor UPDRS as primary, while the paper describes fatigue as primary. VIUSID contained unspecified mint, lemon, honey and other constituents. ALZER contained Ginkgo biloba, lettuce extract and other constituents. No arm isolated mint, and the authors did not identify Mentha species or use fresh spearmint. The fatigue result pertains to the combination and gives no effect estimate for claim 0518. The journal publishes a CC BY-NC policy. The repository retains this original summary and links to the publisher's full text.
