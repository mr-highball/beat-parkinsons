# Food experiment evidence for romaine lettuce, celery stalk, cabbage and Brussels sprouts

## 2011 Celery stalk reactions in a selected allergy cohort

Gadermaier and colleagues selected 32 people sensitized to the celery stalk protein Api g 2, with cross-reactivity to mugwort and peach. Ten reported reactions after eating celery stalks. Oral allergy syndrome was reported in eight, angioedema in two and anaphylaxis in one, with categories potentially overlapping. Twelve had uncertain tolerance or lacked clear raw stalk exposure. These are allergic symptoms in a selected cohort. The study did not measure Parkinson motor swallowing difficulty. [Primary report, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3163685/) · [Local article](Source-Text/PMC3163685.txt)

## 2012 Celery stalk as a laryngeal foreign body

Matadial and colleagues report finding a celery stalk above the vocal cords after a choking episode in a 74-year-old with a history of strokes and vascular dementia. The report does not describe how the celery was prepared. The airway obstruction documents a food-specific adverse event in a neurologically impaired adult. No Parkinson diagnosis, comparison or change in chronic dysphagia severity was reported. [Primary conference report, paper 458](https://wfsahq.org/wp-content/uploads/BJA-abstracts-2.pdf)

## 2012 Raw celery and microwave-cooked Brussels sprouts in a sensory test

Lim and Padmanabhan tested raw chopped celery and microwave-cooked chopped Brussels sprouts, alongside two other vegetables, in 132 adults screened for normal smell and taste. Participants rated flavor and liking with and without a nose clip and could swallow or spit out the samples. In 89 returning participants, liking the Brussels sprout odor was associated with liking the food. The outcomes characterize acute retronasal perception of a stimulus. The study did not measure a change in swallowing difficulty or the ability to smell after eating. First published online September 20, 2012. [Primary report, Methods and Figures 2–4](https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article-pdf/38/1/45/1049608/bjs080.pdf)
