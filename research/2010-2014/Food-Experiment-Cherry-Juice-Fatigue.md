# Cherry juice and measured fatigue in older adults

## Cherry and apple juice crossover and fatigue

Pigeon and colleagues first published May 3, 2010. Fifteen older adults with insomnia completed a randomized crossover comparing a Montmorency tart cherry and apple juice beverage with a cherry-flavored placebo. The two treatment periods each lasted two weeks. Table 1 lists Multidimensional Fatigue Inventory means of 49.1 at baseline, 44.1 after placebo and 46.2 after the cherry beverage. The reported fatigue time-by-group test was inconclusive, with no exact p value or interval supplied. It was a secondary outcome in a small population. The beverage, insomnia cohort and outcome differ from a dried-cherry effect in Parkinson disease. The 2008 conference report describes the same design and is not counted as a separate trial. ## Tart cherry juice and exercise-provoked pain in fibromyalgia

Elliot and colleagues compared a diluted Montmorency tart cherry concentrate with matched punch. Fourteen women with fibromyalgia completed both periods. Overall exercise-provoked pain and day-one strength comparisons were inconclusive. Five apparent responders were selected from the observed pain responses, and one person discontinued during cherry juice with gastrointestinal upset. The follow-up endpoints were pain, tenderness and strength. The paper supplies no treatment comparison for fatigue.
