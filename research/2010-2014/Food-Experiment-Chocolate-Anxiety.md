# Dark chocolate and measured anxiety outside Parkinson disease

## Dark chocolate and anxiety among trainee nurses

Lua and Wong report a short, open-label randomized comparison in 47 nursing students in the July–December 2011 issue. Participants assigned dark chocolate had lower post-intervention medians than the mineral-water comparison for both HADS anxiety (5 versus 7, p = .001) and DASS anxiety (10 versus 16, p < .001). The comparison was unblinded, used a different type of food in each arm, and lasted three days. Several descriptive ranges are internally inconsistent. The study enrolled no Parkinson cohort. ## Dark chocolate and acute state anxiety in healthy adults

Martin and colleagues first published June 20, 2012. The parallel comparison assigned 90 participants to 74% dark chocolate or one of two other snacks. Section 3.2 reports no statistically clear state-anxiety change after the dark chocolate in the two habitual anxiety strata. A conclusion in the abstract sounds favorable in the low-anxiety group, while the detailed results do not establish that effect. The experiment concerns acute responses to snacks in healthy adults. [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2012-Martin-Dark-Chocolate-Anxiety-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Original report, Results section 3.2](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/4/6/554)

## High cocoa chocolate and anxiety in a chronic fatigue crossover

Ten adults with severe chronic fatigue syndrome completed randomized eight-week exposures to high-cocoa chocolate and a simulated low-polyphenol iso-caloric chocolate, separated by two weeks. The active bar contained 85% cocoa solids. In Table 3, median HADS anxiety was 9.5 to 6 during active treatment (within-period p = .01) and 6 to 9 during simulated chocolate (p = .03). These are paired tests within each period. The report gives no direct between-treatment change estimate, and the starting medians differed by 3.5 points. The pilot has no Parkinson cohort and excluded DSM-IV psychiatric comorbidity. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3001690/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2010-11-22-Sathyapalan-High-Cocoa-Chocolate-Anxiety.txt)

## Registered brewed cocoa crossover and posted cognitive and vigor outcomes

The study record was first posted July 27, 2012 and lists completed status and posted results. It registers a crossover of brewed cocoa, cocoa with added caffeine, flavored water, and caffeinated flavored water. The registered main outcomes include serial subtraction and Bakan-task performance, and the secondary mood result is POMS vigor. The 2017 linked report additionally analyzes anxiety under a cognitive challenge. Registered and published outcomes should be kept distinct. This study used drinks and had no Parkinson participants. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01651793) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2012-07-27-NCT01651793-Brewed-Cocoa-Crossover.txt)

## Separate 2009 cocoa and anxiety-trait trials

Martin 2009 grouped healthy participants by initial trait anxiety and reported metabolic changes. It is independent of the 2012 Martin randomized snack study. Scholey 2009 studied cocoa beverages and measured state anxiety, with no clear treatment effect and a sequence difference. The original report gives a mental-fatigue signal only at its lower added flavanol dose. [Full source review](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Round-1-Cocoa-Cinnamon-Nov24.md)
