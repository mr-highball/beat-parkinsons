# Broad bean feeding and orthostatic measurements in healthy volunteers

## Garland feeding study and registry results

Garland and colleagues enrolled 14 healthy volunteers for a fixed-order feeding study. All ate the fixed-sodium control diet on day one. On day two they received the same diet with 100 g of microwaved and puréed fava beans and pods at both breakfast and lunch. Thirteen completed both days. The registry says one participant withdrew after blood was observed in his urine on the fava bean day. Parkinson’s disease and known glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency were exclusion criteria. The registered title mentions postural tachycardia syndrome, but the completed study and publication report healthy volunteers. The registry first posted on February 8, 2010. The article appeared online April 3, 2013, and the registry posted results on July 31, 2013. [Primary full article, Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3628380/) · [Registration and results, NCT01064739](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01064739)

The fava bean meals raised plasma levodopa and urinary dopamine. They did not increase sodium excretion. At six hours, Table 1 reports higher standing systolic and diastolic pressure on the fava bean day in 12 volunteers, with no clear between-day difference in supine pressure or in the change from lying to standing. A fixed order limits causal interpretation. The trial provides a physiological comparison in healthy adults and leaves lightheadedness in Parkinson’s untested. *Vicia faba* is outside the frozen ingredient pool.
