# Grapeseed and flaxseed oil evidence

## Grape seed hydroalcoholic extract and motor coordination in Parkinson model rats

Sarkaki and colleagues reported that gavage with a lyophilized hydroalcoholic red grape seed extract increased forepaw stride length and rotarod time after a 6-OHDA lesion compared with lesioned controls (p < .05 and p < .01, respectively). Figure 2 also reports shorter forepaw grid-descent latency and lower muscle-stiffness scores (both p < .01). These are measures of gait, coordination, catalepsy and rigidity. Skilled hand dexterity, fall counts and dystonia severity were unmeasured. The preparation used an ethanol extract from seed powder. [Primary report, Figure 2 and Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4075680/) · [Local licensed text](Source-Text/PMC4075680.txt)

## Topical grape seed oil in wristband hand motor tests

The 2014 WIPO application describes two small exploratory hand-function experiments in healthy men, with eight men selected for each experimental description and divided among four groups. After four weeks the grapeseed-oil wristband group reportedly improved 12% in finger tapping speed, 15% in precision speed and 14% in hand steadiness. The patent describes comparison with an oil-free band and no band. It supplies no variance or between-group inference for the grapeseed comparison. The oil was delivered in a wristband. The 2017 Gorin randomized publication specifies a vehicle-only comparator and does not identify it as grapeseed oil. [WIPO patent, Example 23](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2014043304A1/en) · [Later Gorin trial Methods](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29168264/)

## Red grape juice and drug-induced rat rotations

An Isfahan group studied red grape juice, treadmill exercise, or both in a 6-OHDA rat model with six per group. The clinical background mentions rigidity. The measured outcome was apomorphine-induced turning, and the original describes within-group reductions in the juice groups, without a verified juice-versus-lesion contrast. This cohort and endpoint differ from oral grape-seed extract and scored muscle stiffness in the Ahvaz study. [Original 2012 report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4075659/) · [Licensed text](Source-Text/PMC4075659.txt)
