# Almond oil, gastric emptying and drug-vehicle specificity

## Gastric emptying after almond oil and other almond matrices

Gallier and colleagues compared almond lipid preparations in growing rats with a magnetic-resonance gastric marker and analyzed ileal lipid digestion after administration. The original abstract reports a gastric half-emptying time of 172 ± 11 minutes with almond oil, compared with 194 ± 17 for crushed whole almonds and 157 ± 9 for almond milk. It describes the oil value as intermediate. The reported significant tests among selected emulsions and other forms do not establish a statistically clear oil-specific difference for every comparator. No cold-press specification, levodopa, Parkinson model or OFF diary appears in the accessible abstract. The publisher date is July 15, 2014, and PubMed labels the electronic record July 28. Both dates fall in this period. Complete processing and methods were unavailable. ## Almond oil listed among possible eltoprazine vehicles

The December 19, 2013 patent family WO2013188210A2 lists almond oil in a generic range of potential liquid formulation vehicles. Its human Parkinson trial example instead specifies oral study capsules and a levodopa challenge. It describes short videos and a study diary, with no verified almond-oil exposure. The 2015 clinical report describes dose comparisons of oral eltoprazine and placebo in 22 participants with levodopa-induced dyskinesia. Its formulation reporting does not establish that they received almond oil. Listing an excipient as possible supplies no ingredient-specific clinical effect. [First patent publication](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2013188210A2/en) · [Published clinical report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5014080/)

## Bitter-almond oil listed in topical apomorphine cases

The patent describes six people with Parkinson’s receiving topical apomorphine. It names bitter-almond kernel oil among the carrier ingredients, with processing unspecified. The example table reports total motor scores, although its illustrative UPDRS form includes voice and facial expression items. A separate example gives qualitative observations of speech and facial expression. It leaves the relationship to the six cases and an interpretable comparison unresolved. The later US20200030228A1 application repeats the six case examples. This formulation provides clinical context for the ingredient but leaves the effect of oral cold-pressed oil unanswered.
