# Curry leaf preparation and measured outcomes

## Atropine arm and overlap with the 2009 mouse report

The same authors describe the same specimen voucher and all five means and SDs for vehicle and extract-only arms as in their 2009 verapamil paper. Table 1 gives 40.53 ± 2.44% charcoal transit with atropine, 61.01 ± 2.96% with atropine plus ethanol leaf extract, and 58.50 ± 1.62% with atropine plus petroleum-ether leaf extract. The two papers are linked as one evidence family for their overlapping arms. Neither measured a dose of ground culinary leaf, gastric emptying or levodopa response. [Original source](https://sphinxsai.com/sphinxsaivol_2no.1/pharmtech_vol_2no.1/PharmTech_Vol_2No.1PDF/PT%3D51%20%28322-326%29.pdf) · [Local text extract](Source-Text/2010-Tembhurne-Curry-Leaf-Atropine-Transit-Extract.txt)

## Diabetic rat intestinal transit and colonic contractility

In male Wistar rats with streptozotocin-induced diabetes, curry leaf extract was reported to increase propagation of a charcoal meal and improve an acetylcholine response in isolated distal colon. The study also measured blood glucose and intestinal oxidation markers. The indexed abstract describes small-intestinal transit without a separate gastric emptying test. The full publisher endpoint from the DOI deposit was unavailable in the access checks. [Original source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21849153/) · [Local text extract](Source-Text/2011-Tembhurne-Diabetic-Rat-Curry-Leaf-Transit-Abstract-Extract.txt)

## Rat diarrhea paper with discrepant narrative and transit table

The authors interpret leaf-extract treatment as slowing small-intestinal propulsion. The accessible extracted Table 2 instead places mean charcoal travel at 18.48 in control, 54.80 with aqueous extract, and 71.64 with alcoholic extract. The corresponding labelled intestinal lengths do not resolve the reversal. The table also shows a distinct atropine row. The retrieved article text preserves an internal discrepancy. No direction is assigned from this source, and the original table still requires a successful visual replay. [Original source](https://oaji.net/articles/2016/3556-1469435382.pdf) · [Local text extract](Source-Text/2012-Sharma-Curry-Leaf-Charcoal-Transit-Extract.txt)

## Ground curry leaf mixture in a registered digestive trial

First posted August 16, 2011. This double-blind crossover registered a tea decoction made from curry leaves, pomegranate and Curcuma powders, compared against a different placebo tea. The planned measures included bowel and abdominal symptoms, quality of life, anxiety, depression and adverse events. The official record has no posted results module. It supplies no levodopa interaction, OFF time, dysphagia, handwriting or voice measure. [Original source](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01418066) · [Local text extract](Source-Text/NCT01418066-Curry-Leaf-Decoction-Registry-Extract.txt)

## Fresh curry leaf chewing and saliva pH

Sixty healthy volunteers were allocated to chew fresh tulsi, mint or curry leaves and then expectorate. Mean saliva pH increased shortly after chewing across groups. Immediate mint-versus-curry comparisons gave p < .01, and a 30-minute tulsi-versus-curry comparison gave p < .05. Later saliva pH comparisons were inconclusive. The measurements concern pH and tongue coating. There was no reported flow rate, bolus transit, swallowing safety or voice volume measure. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3807960/) · [Local text extract](Source-Text/2012-Ramesh-Curry-Leaves-Salivary-pH-Extract.txt)

## Drug-induced tongue movements and facial expressiveness

The 2011 online and 2012 Patil papers studied involuntary chewing or tongue protrusion in rats during neuroleptic or reserpine exposure. They link to extract-based motor evidence of a different kind from an intentional swallow or hypomimia. They do not give character size or human acoustic voice measures.
