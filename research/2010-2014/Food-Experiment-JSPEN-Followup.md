# Foods and enteral nutrition in early Japanese clinical nutrition reports

## Racol NF semisolid formula composition

The Japanese regulator’s 2014 formulation table lists 2.097 g soybean oil, 0.540 g shiso or perilla oil, 1.002 g palm oil and 2.250 g tricaprylin per 300 g bag of Racol NF semisolid formula. This provides ingredient identities and quantities for the named enteral formula. The table does not give a culinary oil grade or a Parkinson symptom outcome. [Regulatory review, Table 2](https://www.pmda.go.jp/drugs/2014/P201400015/130589000_22600AMX00523000_A100_1.pdf#page=5)

## 2010 Hida staged return to oral feeding

Hida and colleagues described a 70-year-old Parkinson patient recovering from altered consciousness after severe hypothermia. Following jelly-based swallowing training, the first meal stage included full rice porridge, soft side dishes and thickened liquids. The diet advanced, liquid thickener was later withdrawn, and the patient went home eating orally. The abstract identifies textures without a gel recipe, quantitative swallowing measure or isolated rice effect. [Original 2010 P-528, page 32](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/25/1/25_1_430/_pdf#page=32)

## 2013 Matsuoka combined enteral formula and MCT

Matsuoka and Shiota described a Parkinson case with recurrent aspiration and a pressure injury. Care included commercial MCT oil, Abound and multiple formula changes. Aspiration persisted after attempts to advance oral textures. After gastrostomy and persistent diarrhea during semi-elemental feeding, a thickened elemental regimen with added insoluble fiber and MCT oil was followed by resolution of diarrhea. Wound care continued. The report does not identify an isolated effect of the oil or a constipation, fatigue or executive-function outcome. [Original 2013 Hyogo NST abstract 3, page 2](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/28/5/28_SUP72/_pdf#page=2)

## 2012 reflux and retching during gastrostomy feeding

An 80-year-old patient with Parkinson’s and retching or reflux during gastrostomy feeding was switched to an unnamed semisolid formula. The abstract reports less retching and reflux during the subsequent course. A neighboring report names an agar-derived formula in a different population. The Parkinson case gives no brand, ingredient or isolated comparison. [Original 2012 O-0635, PDF p9](https://web.archive.org/web/20230510154913id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/27/1/27_1_432/_pdf/-char/ja#page=9)

## 2011 chopped meals and jelly in supranuclear palsy

The team reported thickened chopped half-meals and a named V CRES jelly after videofluoroscopy in one patient with progressive supranuclear palsy, parkinsonian syndrome and prior gastrectomy. The reported improvement concerned a pressure injury during broader nutrition and wound care. The abstract gives no idiopathic Parkinson diagnosis or food-specific swallowing result. [Original 2011 P-551, printed p543](https://web.archive.org/web/20251129232427id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/26/1/26_1_506/_pdf#page=38)

## Roasted sweet potato during post-stroke swallowing rehabilitation

The 2012 report O-1244 records some roasted sweet potato and pie consumption by a patient undergoing swallowing rehabilitation after subarachnoid hemorrhage. The report gives no Parkinson diagnosis, choking on those foods or comparison with baked common potato. [Original 2012 O-1244, PDF p12](https://web.archive.org/web/20210830044718id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/27/1/27_1_582/_pdf/-char/ja#page=12)

## 2013 umami solution and saliva swallowing

Seven patients with dysphagia, six with cerebrovascular disease and one with Parkinson’s, received drops of water or a 1% Ajinomoto umami solution on the floor of the mouth. Each condition was repeated. The authors reported a pooled increase of 0.36 swallows in 30 seconds with the umami solution, described as statistically significant. Times to the first three swallows showed no significant differences. Four patients reported easier swallowing and three reported no change. The abstract gives no individual Parkinson result or choking measure. [Original 2013 P-016, PDF p5](https://web.archive.org/web/20230517053100id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/28/1/28_413/_pdf/-char/ja#page=5)

## 2013 combined formula changes and diarrhea

A 76-year-old Parkinson patient had chronic diarrhea during gastrostomy feeding. The report describes improvement during combined use of glutamine, fiber and oligosaccharide, *Bifidobacterium longum* BB536, ursodeoxycholic acid and gradual peptide-based feeding. Enteral nutrition replaced parenteral nutrition by week eight. Several interventions changed, with no isolated ingredient effect. [Original 2013 P-276, PDF p70](https://web.archive.org/web/20230517053100id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/28/1/28_413/_pdf/-char/ja#page=70)

## 2014 black pepper aroma patches in a chart series

Kobayashi and colleagues reviewed 23 patients using black pepper aroma patches. The report classifies all cases as cerebrovascular disease, dementia, or cerebrovascular disease with dementia or disuse. Nine patients had reported benefits. Four had improved oral intake alone, three had improvement in coughing, choking or pooled saliva alone, and two had both categories. The report gives no Parkinson subgroup and no oral pepper exposure. [Tohoku JSPEN 2014 abstract 26, PDF p44](https://files.jspen.or.jp/sites/6/2019/05/dai29kai_touhoku_syouroku.pdf#page=44)

## 2010 combined sensory stimulation and pneumonia

Ebihara and colleagues report 17 dysphagic older adults, most with cerebrovascular disease, during successive clinical-care phases. The later phase combined black pepper aroma, a capsaicin troche, menthol jelly and staged changes to food texture. The table reports 16 pneumonia events in the earlier phase and five during intervention (P < .001), and a decrease in mean febrile days from 6.8 to 1.3 (P < .007). The comparison uses the same clinical group at different times. It gives no Parkinson subgroup or separate estimate for pepper, capsaicin, menthol or food texture. [2010 primary report](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1532-5415.2009.02648.x)

## 2013 Hata rehabilitation nutrition case published in 2014

Hata and colleagues reported a stage III Parkinson case with sepsis associated with megacolon. Diarrhea improved after enteral delivery rate and energy were adjusted. After levodopa escalation and rehabilitation, oral intake progressed from jelly to paste. The regional presentation took place in May 2013 and was published in the 2014 proceedings. The abstract gives no food ingredient or isolated effect. [Original regional abstract 20, page 10](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/29/4/29_SUP118/_pdf#page=10)

## 2014 Sugimoto taste stimuli and saliva swallowing

Sugimoto and colleagues described a 71-year-old man with stage V Parkinson’s, a gastrostomy and severe dysphagia. A 3 mL water test caused marked coughing and fluoroscopy showed small silent aspiration with liquid and poor oral transfer with liquid and semi-solid boluses. During rehabilitation, the team applied preferred flavors to the tongue tip and practiced saliva swallowing with oral care and exercises. They named homemade pickled plum, soup and coffee. The team observed spontaneous speech, opening of the mouth and facial expressions they interpreted as greater satisfaction. No clinical pneumonia was reported during training. The abstract gives no brewed coffee preparation, oral beverage dose, fatigue measure, repeated fluoroscopy or individual food effect. [Original 2014 P-0727, page 57](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/29/1/29_521/_pdf#page=57) · [Archived original](https://web.archive.org/web/20251229133813id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/29/1/29_521/_pdf#page=57)

## 2014 Ishida oral intake following gastrostomy

Ishida and colleagues reviewed 85 neurological patients after gastrostomy. The Parkinson and dementia with Lewy bodies group had five of 14 eating orally at one month and five of 13 at six months. The abstract states that these were the same individuals. It gives no named food or separate Parkinson estimate. A later report came from the same hospital and used a Parkinson-specific population. [Original 2014 P-0750, page 63](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/29/1/29_521/_pdf#page=63) · [Later hospital series](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-NHO-Later.md#2023-and-2024-oral-feeding-after-aspiration-procedures)

## 2014 Yokouchi multidisciplinary gastrostomy case

Yokouchi and colleagues describe a 68-year-old with Parkinson’s and repeated swallowing difficulty who regained oral intake after gastrostomy and multidisciplinary care. A semisolid nutritional supplement served as a backup. No individual oral food, formulation or comparative symptom estimate is given. [Original 2014 P-0954, page 114](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/29/1/29_521/_pdf#page=114)
