# Black tea, walking and tremor

## Caffeine tablet dose escalation and motor measures

Altman and colleagues' 2011 open-label study compared Parkinson participants' scores with their own baseline after increasing doses of caffeine tablets. Resting tremor decreased by 1.18 points (SD 2.4, p=.01) over the first two weeks in the per-protocol results. Worsening parkinsonian and emerging or enhanced physiological tremors were also cited as reasons for stopping. Twenty-five were described in the article. Seventeen tolerated 200 mg twice per day and three completed all six weeks. Timed Up and Go was measured, with a baseline mean of 10.39 seconds (SD 3.04). Numerical follow-up values were unavailable in the main text. Patient-rated PDQ-39 mobility improved by 2.8 points (SD 3.4, p=.01) in the subgroup tolerating 200 mg twice daily. The single-group design, attrition and specific constituent prevent attribution to brewed black tea. ## Black and oolong tea consumption and gait assessment

Ng and colleagues analyzed baseline data from 2,398 community-dwelling Singapore residents aged at least 55. The survey grouped English black tea and Chinese black or oolong tea. Participants walked six meters and returned for a Performance Oriented Mobility Assessment gait score capturing initiation, steps, symmetry and continuity. Table 3 records higher gait scores in the combined black or oolong category after adjustment (B=0.01, SE 0.006, standardized coefficient 0.05, p=.02), while accompanying prose gives p<.01. Table 3's adjusted balance p=.015 also differs from prose p=.008. Both presentations describe favorable associations. The analysis supplies no separate estimate for black tea, standardized brew, timed gait speed or Parkinson disease. ## Randomized caffeine trial and separately reported gait and rest tremor

In the separate six-week trial registered as NCT00459420, Postuma and colleagues randomized 61 people with Parkinson disease and daytime somnolence to caffeine capsules or placebo. Table 2 gives separate clinician-rated UPDRS Part III items. At week three the estimated rest-tremor contrast was −0.32 points (95% interval −0.73 to 0.10) and gait was −0.12 (−0.84 to 0.60). At week six, rest tremor and gait each differed by −0.28 points (rest-tremor interval −0.83 to 0.27 and gait interval −1.16 to 0.59). The gait item is an examiner rating. The reported whole motor exam and bradykinesia subscore improved at six weeks, and the separate gait and rest-tremor intervals span zero. The clinical effects of brewed black tea on these items remain unresolved.
