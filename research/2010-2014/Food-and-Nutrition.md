# Food and Nutrition

### 2011 — Nutrient intake and smell identification at diagnosis

Ådén and colleagues compared 87 people with newly diagnosed Parkinson’s and 28 age- and sex-matched controls. The study used three-day dietary records and 24-hour recalls, with the Brief Smell Identification Test. The published abstract associates lower protein intake as a proportion of energy and lower folate, magnesium and phosphorus density with lower smell scores in the combined sample. The Parkinson group performed worse on the smell test. The accessible results do not estimate smell changes by cinnamon, tea, or any individual food. [Primary report, PMID 21535918](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21535918/)

The original Results report mean Brief Smell Identification Test scores of 7.0 (SD 2.5) in the newly diagnosed Parkinson group and 9.4 (SD 2.0) in controls, p<.001. The group totals were 87 and 28. Participants used three-day food records and 24-hour recalls. In the combined sample, the Table 4 association between folate density and B-SIT was 0.063 (unstandardized coefficient, p=.022) after adjustment for age, sex, group and BMI. Table 4 also reports associations for protein energy share (p=.045), magnesium density (p=.012) and phosphorus density (p=.029). The study modeled nutrients, with no separate roasted kale estimate. The 2016 Umeå report used a different analysis, and individual overlap remains unquantified. [Original article and Table 4](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51091965_Dietary_intake_and_olfactory_function_in_patients_with_newly_diagnosed_Parkinson%27s_disease_a_case-control_study) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2011-Aden-Nutrient-Density-Smell-Identification-Extract.txt)

### 2010 — Ice cream preference in Parkinson’s disease

Meyers and colleagues’ March 2010 study collected anonymous questionnaires from 59 people with Parkinson’s and 57 controls. In the combined-sex analysis, 52.4% of the Parkinson group and 22.2% of controls reported increased desire for ice cream (p < .001). The increase in reported ice cream consumption did not differ clearly between groups. The authors offered ease of swallowing as one possible explanation. The study did not administer an ice cream bolus or measure swallowing, and the result describes changes in preference after diagnosis. Ice cream is outside the frozen ingredient set. [Primary report, pp. 91–92](https://www.rimed.org/medhealthri/2010-03/2010-03.pdf) · [PubMed, PMID 20415116](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20415116/)

### 2011 — Potato and olive oil in a protein-redistributed menu

Baroni and colleagues compared a plant-food menu in 12 people with Parkinson’s with a usual omnivorous menu in 13. The two menus concentrated protein-rich foods at dinner. The plant menu listed baked or boiled potatoes several times a week, 30 mL/day of olive oil, and legume choices including chickpeas and lentils. Those are menu options and allowances. The report gives no participant-level quantities or preparation choices. At four weeks mean UPDRS-III was 25.42 in the plant-food group and 46.46 in the comparison group (p = .001). The ordinal waking-day OFF item was inconclusive (p = .054), as was the dyskinesia item (p = .941). The study did not record timed OFF duration, publish individual swallowing or walking items, or assign participants randomly. It estimates differences between two menus and does not isolate any named food or oil. The January 2011 issue predates the publisher's 2013 online listing. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21535916/) · [Author-posted report, Tables 3 and 5–7](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51091963_Pilot_dietary_study_with_normoproteic_protein-redistributed_plant-food_diet_and_motor_performance_in_patients_with_Parkinson%27s_disease) · [Publisher issue listing](https://www.tandfonline.com/toc/ynns20/14/1)

### 2013 — Repository release of the Porto protein-timing study after stimulation

Eduarda Silva’s report is dated 2008 on the title page. The University of Porto lists July 30, 2013 as repository availability. The earlier public-access date is unresolved. The quasi-experimental study enrolled 57 people and compared dietary protein restriction and redistribution started immediately after deep brain stimulation with a program begun later. The report found changes in selected anthropometric measures. It gives inconclusive between-group comparisons for levodopa treatment evolution (p = 0.812) and UPDRS Part III motor change (p = 0.981). In the later-start group, pre- to post-diet comparisons were also inconclusive for treatment (p = 0.847) and motor scores (p = 0.359). Some motor assessments occurred with stimulation on and medication off. The study does not isolate foods or measure daily OFF duration. [Repository availability metadata](https://repositorio-aberto.up.pt/handle/10216/54800?show=full) · [Primary full report, Methods and Results](https://repositorio-aberto.up.pt/bitstream/10216/54800/3/120779_0816TCD16.pdf)

### 2013–2014 — Oral glucose and orthostatic testing in untreated Parkinson’s disease

Umehara and colleagues reported a 28-patient conference analysis in 2013. Their 2014 journal report analyzed 37 untreated patients and 10 controls. In the journal analysis, the magnitude of the systolic pressure decrease after a 75-g glucose drink correlated with the magnitude of the decrease on a separate tilt test (r = 0.359, p < 0.05). The journal and subsequent degree summary describe the same study. The correlation is between blood pressure responses in two tests and supplies no change in orthostatic symptoms attributable to rice or any other food. [2013 conference abstract, P12.34](https://cdn.ymaws.com/worldparkinsoncongress.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/abstracts/wpc_2013_abstract_book_final.pdf) · [Primary report, online March 22, 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24703893/) · [University degree summary](https://ir.jikei.ac.jp/record/4439/files/o_3106.pdf)

### 2012 — Flavonoids, berries and incident disease

Two prospective cohorts showed an inverse association for total flavonoids in men and pooled associations for anthocyanins and berries. Total flavonoids showed no clear association in women. A later extended follow-up revisited these associations. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22491871/)

### 2012 — Mediterranean diet adherence in a case-control sample

The study associated lower Mediterranean diet scores with Parkinson’s case status. Diet was measured in participants with established disease, leaving confounding and changes in eating after symptom onset possible. [Primary source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3349773/)

### 2012 — Caffeine for daytime sleepiness and motor symptoms

A six-week randomized trial found no statistically clear benefit on its primary sleepiness scale. Some secondary motor measures favored caffeine. The short follow-up and secondary endpoint make replication important. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22855866/)

### 2013 — Vitamin D supplementation and clinical staging

A 12-month double-blind trial randomized 114 people to vitamin D3 or placebo. The vitamin D group had less worsening on modified Hoehn and Yahr staging, and some effects varied by vitamin D receptor genotype. The result comes from a single small trial with several clinical and genetic comparisons. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23485413/)

### 2014 — Eclipta leaf extract and drug-induced movement in rats

Jena et al.'s July 2014 report assigned male Wistar rats to six groups, with six animals per group. The Methods describe oral 90% ethanol extract of shade-dried *Eclipta alba* leaves at 100, 200 and 400 mg/kg after 21 days of haloperidol, with a further haloperidol injection on the testing day. Table 2 lists 180-minute catalepsy scores of 3.08 ± 0.026 with haloperidol alone and 0.85 ± 0.247, 0.50 ± 0.562 and 0.50 ± 0.224 with extract at the respective doses (mean ± SEM, all marked p < .01). Figure 3 reports fewer vacuous chewing movements. The report gives conflicting timing for tissue collection and mentions reserpine in its interpretation despite describing haloperidol in the experiment. The model measures extrapyramidal responses to an antipsychotic. Kew treats *E. alba* as a synonym of *E. prostrata*. [Original, volume 6(7), pages 31–38](https://www.jocpr.com/articles/protective-effect-of-eclipta-alba-on-haloperidol-induced-extrapyramidal-movement-disorders-in-albino-rats.pdf) · [Kew taxonomic record](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/202735-1) · [Later nanoparticle formulation](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-MDS-Botanical-Followup.md#eclipta-derived-silver-nanoparticles-in-mice).

### 2013 — Edible nightshades and Parkinson disease odds

Searles Nielsen et al. first published this Washington State case-control study on May 9, 2013. Complete food and tobacco histories were available for 486 of 490 people with newly diagnosed Parkinson disease and 636 of 644 controls. The questionnaire grouped green, yellow, and red peppers together and combined baked and mashed potatoes. Table 1 reports adjusted odds ratios per additional usual daily eating occasion of 0.43 (95% CI 0.24–0.78) for peppers, 0.83 (0.56–1.24) for tomatoes, 0.77 (0.41–1.44) for tomato juice, and 1.12 (0.73–1.70) for potatoes. Eggplant intake was not measured. The nicotine-weighted group trend was p = .004. The outcome was Parkinson diagnosis, so the estimates supply no symptom contrast for [baked potato and swallowing difficulty, 0290](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0290.txt) or for any particular bell-pepper preparation. [Original article, Table 1](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/214509/cdc_214509_DS1.pdf) · [Author manuscript and supplement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4864980/) · [1999 nicotine-content measurements](../1995-1999/Food-and-Nutrition.md#1999--nicotine-measurements-in-nightshade-foods).
