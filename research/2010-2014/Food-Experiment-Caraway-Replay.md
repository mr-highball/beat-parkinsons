# Carvone toxicity observations and human drug exposure

## Body tremor as an acute carvone toxicity observation

A 2012 Dutch RIVM regulatory dossier summarizes a rat acute oral study listed in the 2000 draft assessment report. The isolated carvone test material had no reported ratio of its two isomers. Two animals showed occasional body tremor at one high acute dose, among other clinical toxicity observations. The endpoint was neither a quantified rest tremor nor a Parkinson phenotype, and there was no ground spice. NTP’s separately identifiable d-carvone study used gavage in mice and investigated systemic toxicity, pathology and cancer. These reports represent different experiments. ## Human tuberculosis drug exposure after a caraway extract

Choudhary and colleagues gave 20 healthy men a fixed-dose tuberculosis regimen alone, followed ten days later by the same drugs with 100 mg of a *Carum carvi* extract. The Methods identify the extraction and standardization procedure from Sachin’s rat paper. The participants had the same treatment order. Table 2 gives increased peak concentration and 0–24-hour exposure for rifampicin, isoniazid and pyrazinamide after adding the extract. The report proposes mechanisms without directly measuring P-glycoprotein function, and the trial did not test a Parkinson medication, motor outcome or culinary preparation.
