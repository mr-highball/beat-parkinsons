# Peach in clinical pharmacology

## Peach juice as a drug-study carrier

The ClinicalTrials.gov registration NCT01259973, first posted December 15, 2010, describes a healthy-volunteer pharmacogenetic protocol comparing single doses of haloperidol, risperidone and placebo. Each is masked in 250 mL peach juice. Its planned Simpson–Angus measure includes drug-induced postural instability, and a tapping test is among secondary psychomotor measures. The intervention compares drugs with placebo, with juice present in all arms; it does not compare peach with no peach, specify raw fruit, or study idiopathic Parkinson disease. The registry lists status unknown and has no posted results. ## Human motor results after antipsychotic doses carried in peach juice

Mas and colleagues first published this APSEP result on October 7, 2013. The official registry links the report and the paper independently matches the crossover study site and three arms. Thirty healthy volunteers enrolled and 25 completed. Each condition, including the placebo, used 250 mL peach juice as carrier. In Table 2 and the results, risperidone impaired dominant-hand tapping versus placebo and haloperidol (both post hoc p < .05, overall p < .0009). Both antipsychotics performed worse than placebo on the fine-motor task (overall p = .002). Actual juice intake was held constant. The results estimate drug effects without a raw-fruit contrast.
