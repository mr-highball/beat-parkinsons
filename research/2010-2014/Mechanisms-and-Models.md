# Mechanisms and Models

### 2012 — Induced α-synuclein pathology in a mouse model

Luk and colleagues reported progressive pathology and dopamine-neuron loss after administering α-synuclein fibrils in mice. The experimental model supported investigation of propagation, with human disease initiation and routes remaining open. [Primary source](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1227157)

### 2013 — α-synuclein staining in prediagnosis bowel samples

An archival biopsy study reported α-synuclein staining in bowel samples taken before some patients were diagnosed. The small retrospective design and assay variability limit how far it can establish a sequence in living patients. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24240814/)

### 2014 — EGCG and α-synuclein oligomer membrane effects

Lorenzen and colleagues reported that isolated epigallocatechin gallate reduced membrane permeabilization by preformed α-synuclein oligomers and reduced toxicity in a rat brain cell line. The paper found that EGCG reduced oligomer binding to membranes without a measurable change in oligomer size or secondary structure. The experiments used model membranes and cells. The August journal highlight describes the same study. [Primary report, online June 6, 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4118093/) · [Linked journal highlight](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4118094/)

### 2014 — Gut microbial profiles and Parkinson’s phenotype

A Finnish cross-sectional study reported differences in stool microbiota and links between some taxa and motor phenotype. Treatment, diet, bowel transit and other group differences can affect stool profiles. First published online in December 2014. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25476529/)
