# Posture, pain, urinary urgency and related outcomes

## Raspberry powder in a nicotine-withdrawal mouse experiment

The July 2011 Mexican patent application describes spray-dried Rubus idaeus juice and reduced closed-arm time in a raised-maze test after induced nicotine withdrawal. It also describes a powder comparison in mice without nicotine. The exposure is dehydrated juice and the measure is mouse behavior, with no Parkinson or frozen-fruit group. The 2013 application cites this antecedent. Related mouse claims in the two disclosures do not establish independent replication. ## Raspberry powder and reported anxiety during smoking cessation

The April 2013 international patent application reports two adults starting in its mild-anxiety category and in its no-anxiety category at the end of a 14-day raspberry-powder regimen. Other very small groups received zeolite, combination products or varenicline. The record describes cessation counseling and assessments timed around quitting, without a randomized, blinded control, raw anxiety scores or Parkinson population. The powder is prepared from dried juice. This is an uncontrolled self-reporting lead with substantial co-intervention and time confounding.
