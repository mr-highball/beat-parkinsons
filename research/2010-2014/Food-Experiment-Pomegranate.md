# Pomegranate in early Parkinson model and reward research

## Pomegranate juice worsened selected outcomes in a rotenone model

Tapias, Cannon and Greenamyre first published their rat study online October 21, 2013. Adult male Lewis rats received diluted commercial pomegranate juice in drinking water or a sugar-matched comparison. In the rotenone arms, juice was associated with fewer rears after five days (p < .05), lower striatal terminal fluorescence (378 ± 30 versus 474 ± 34 units) and fewer nigral dopaminergic neurons per hemisphere (8,833 ± 397 versus 12,950 ± 744). The paper found no clear juice contrast in postural instability, catecholamine pool, body weight or survival. Two animals that died during treatment were excluded from analysis. The outcomes concern experimental parkinsonism and juice. They provide no raw-aril or clinical nonmotor symptom estimates. [Author manuscript, Results and Figures 1–5](https://uvadoc.uva.es/handle/10324/63881) · [Publication metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24315037/) · [Later Poznań comparison](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Pomegranate.md#pomegranate-juice-and-postural-stability-in-poznań)

## Pomegranate peel extract in a mouse reward assay

Naveen and colleagues’ 2013 chronic mild stress experiment included five groups of eight mice. The primary report identifies flax seed and imipramine as the groups with significantly increased sucrose consumption. It describes a smaller response after pomegranate peel extract, which reduced selected biochemical measures. Sucrose consumption is a reward-related behavior and does not measure Parkinson apathy. A later review attributes a 60% sucrose increase to both extracts. The primary results should govern that interpretation. [Original article and Figure 1](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/pcn.12100) · [Later review and citation trail](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11358894/)

## Immediate allergy after pomegranate fruit ingestion

The 2011 Food Allergy and Anaphylaxis Meeting report describes erythema, ear swelling and pruritus shortly after pomegranate ingestion in one adult. The investigators compared IgE reactivity to different fruit fractions. This case documents an immediate allergy phenotype and supplies no estimate for saliva production, drooling or other Parkinson symptoms. [Primary conference report](Source-Text/PMC3354222.txt)
