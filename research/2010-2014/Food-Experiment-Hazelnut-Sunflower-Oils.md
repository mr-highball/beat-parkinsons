# Hazelnut and sunflower oil evidence

## Sunflower and fish oil comparator in major depression

Lespérance and colleagues published online June 15, 2010. Of 432 adults with a major depressive episode, 214 received sunflower oil containing 2% fish oil. Table 2 gives the comparator’s mean baseline IDS-SR30 of 43.28 and its adjusted mean across the eight-week trial of 32.26. The overall contrast against the EPA/DHA treatment was 1.32 points (95% CI −0.20 to 2.84, p = .088) on IDS-SR30 and 0.97 points (95% CI −0.012 to 1.95, p = .053) on MADRS. The subgroup without comorbid anxiety favored EPA/DHA on depressive symptoms. The mixed comparator had no oil-free reference or verified processing grade. The study gives no Parkinson subgroup. [Original paper, Table 2](https://www.psychiatrist.com/PublishingImages/v72n0805.pdf) · [Online publication date](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20584525/) · [2005 registration](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Hazelnut-Sunflower-Oils.md#sunflower-oil-comparator-registration-for-major-depression)

## Sunflower comparator in two Australian and New Zealand registrations

ANZCTR registered ACTRN12614000130662 on February 4, 2014. The residential-care crossover used unscented sunflower oil on a lapel as a comparison for lavender and lemon balm oils. It planned NPI and agitation scores. ANZCTR registered ACTRN12614000874617 on August 14, 2014. This distinct trial planned sunflower-oil capsules as the comparator to DHA and EPA in older adults with mild cognitive impairment, with Trail Making Test, Geriatric Depression Scale and Geriatric Anxiety Inventory among planned measures. The first study reports results in a 2017 thesis and the second in a 2018 thesis publicly deposited in 2020. [Watson registration](https://anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=365702) · [Watson registry summary](Source-Text/Trials/ACTRN12614000130662.txt) · [Mengelberg registration](https://anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=366439) · [Mengelberg registry summary](Source-Text/Trials/ACTRN12614000874617.txt)

## Hazelnut oil and mood in a controlled feeding crossover

Kien and colleagues published in 2013. The high-oleic arm used a fat supplement made entirely from hazelnut oil. The high-palmitic arm used palm, peanut and virgin olive oils, while the run-in included 19.3% high-oleic sunflower oil and 43.8% hazelnut oil. Table 3 compares the two experimental diets, reporting fatigue-inertia scores of 3.7 versus 5.3 (p = .330), tension-anxiety of 3.3 versus 3.1 (p = .400), depression-dejection of 2.7 versus 4.6 (p = .132) and anger-hostility of 2.2 versus 4.7 (p = .007). The oil's extraction method and roasting status were unspecified. Fatigue-inertia is distinct from daytime sleepiness. No Parkinson cohort or sleepiness outcome was studied, and the sunflower-containing run-in was not a randomized test arm. [Primary Methods and Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3607650/)

## Registered sunflower oil comparator in the Melbourne depression trial

ANZCTR first posted ACTRN12610000710022 on August 26, 2010. The protocol planned 90 adults with major depression, an eight-week randomized comparison of DHA, EPA and 2 g/day sunflower oil tablets, and a Hamilton Depression Rating Scale change outcome. The current official record still shows its historical recruitment status and lacks actual enrollment or results. The oil grade and a Parkinson subgroup are unspecified. [Official registration](https://anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=335873) · [Local registry fields](Source-Text/Trials/ACTRN12610000710022.txt)

## Corn oil comparator in a Parkinson depression trial

Pomponi and colleagues studied 24 people with mild to moderate Parkinson disease. At six months, the original abstract reports that 75% of patients on DHA plus EPA and 25% on the corn-oil comparison achieved a 50% or greater reduction on the Hamilton depression scale. The authors identify corn oil as the matched comparison. Arm-level means and analyzed denominators remain open at the available access depth. [Original comparison and limits](Source-Text/2014-Pomponi-Parkinson-Depression-Corn-Oil-Extract.txt)

## Registered high-oleic sunflower comparator and cognitive battery

The 2010 ANZCTR record ACTRN12610000212055 registered oral high-oleic sunflower oil as a comparator for DHA with cognitive measurements including working memory, Stroop and digit vigilance. Listed sunflower oil exposure was 2.25 g/day in capsules. Pressing grade was unspecified. The registration and Stonehouse 2013 publication describe one cohort. [Local factual text](Source-Text/Trials/ACTRN12610000212055.txt) · [Primary source](https://www.anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=335243)

## High-oleic sunflower comparator and working-memory reaction time

Stonehouse and colleagues first published March 20, 2013. The randomized study analyzed 176 healthy adults given DHA-rich oil or an oral high-oleic sunflower oil comparator over six months. The working-memory reaction-time active-minus-comparator estimate was −0.36 SD (95% CI −0.58 to −0.14). This contrast identifies relative supplement performance. The oil-free counterfactual, pressing grade and Parkinson-specific executive outcome were absent. Original record ACTRN12610000212055.
