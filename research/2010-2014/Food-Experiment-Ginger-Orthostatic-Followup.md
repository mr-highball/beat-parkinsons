# Ginger, blood pressure and standing symptoms

## Finely chopped ginger and postprandial blood pressure

Gregersen et al. first published this study online July 5, 2012, before the 2013 print issue. The randomized five-condition crossover enrolled 22 healthy young men. One meal arm added 20 g finely chopped ginger to stewed apples in the same brunch format. Blood pressure was recorded repeatedly around the meal; the methods describe supine rest for the respiratory measurements and no timed standing challenge. The Results report no significant overall systolic effect (p=.15). Among named post hoc diastolic comparisons, the 1.4 mmHg difference is horseradish versus ginger (p=.050). The authors report no other between-treatment differences for diastolic pressure. The preparation is fine chopping, and lightheadedness severity after standing is unmeasured. ## Ginger extract and acute pressure in healthy participants

Ojulari and colleagues assigned 60 healthy volunteers to water or one of two ginger extract doses. The preparation was mechanically powdered dried rhizome, extracted and concentrated. The 100 mg/kg arm's narrative reports mean systolic pressure 114.3 mmHg before treatment, 105.5 at two hours and 107.6 at four hours. Its table shows the same direction and slightly different rounded means. The abstract describes an increase after four hours without explaining the comparison. Methods specify a digital cuff and pulse monitor but no posture transition or lightheadedness measure. The reported before-after changes, incompletely presented comparator results and small study cannot establish a standing symptom effect.
