# Food experiment follow-up evidence

## Two dietary case histories from Colombia

Perea-Sasiaín and Hanfling Schwartz published an expanded account of two people with idiopathic Parkinson’s in 2014. The man described an improved response to his usual medication following one vegan lunch and adopted a diet emphasizing whole grains, vegetables and fruit with some animal protein. The authors describe apparently stable clinical signs and levodopa dose over two years of that diet. The report gives no timed off periods, standardized symptom scores, controlled rechallenge or measurements for an individual ingredient. It explicitly cites the 2004 Schwartz correspondence for this man. [Primary journal report and license](https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/revfacmed/article/view/43783) · [Local source text and tables](Source-Text/2014-Perea-Sasiain-Schwartz-Parkinson-Diets.txt) · [Earlier letter](../2000-2004/Food-and-Nutrition.md#2004--correspondence-on-a-mostly-plant-based-diet-in-parkinsons)

The other case describes a woman who regularly consumed milk and eggs, including heated milk-and-egg custard, and had a progressive ten-year clinical course. The authors report restful sleep with little protein plus levodopa medication in the evening. The 2007 account describes the same woman and attributes some constipation to a soft, low-residue diet. Neither report isolates the milk, eggs or another ingredient. Tables 1 and 2 summarize cited studies and use different outcome types, including incidence and case reports. They do not add clinical measurements to the two cases. [Materials and methods, p. 142](https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/revfacmed/article/download/43783/45050#page=2) · [Earlier 2007 account](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#2007-colombian-patient-history-and-diet-texture)

The 2014 table lists lower incidence for potatoes and cites Searles Nielsen et al. That primary report groups baked and mashed potatoes and gives an adjusted diagnosis odds ratio of 1.12 per additional daily occasion (95% CI 0.73–1.70). The inverse association was clearest for peppers. The 2014 table also lists N-methylcysteine next to cabbage, turnips and garlic. The cited Wassef experiment used isolated S-methyl-L-cysteine added to feed for transgenic fruit flies, with age-related locomotor outcomes. No named vegetable was tested. [2014 tables](Source-Text/2014-Perea-Sasiain-Schwartz-Parkinson-Diets.txt) · [Original diagnosis study](Food-and-Nutrition.md#2013--edible-nightshades-and-parkinson-disease-odds) · [2007 fly study](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#s-methyl-l-cysteine-added-to-feed-in-a-fly-model)

### Strawberry and handwriting citation check

The 2014 paper cites Renoudet’s 2012 case and names strawberries as a source of fisetin. It names wheat germ as a source of hexacosanol. Renoudet’s primary abstract confirms improvement in micrographia after a combined dietary change and gives neither the exact strawberry preparation nor a strawberry-only comparison. The publisher’s HTML exposes the abstract and references, while the PDF route requires access. The cited strawberry content is a lead for claim 0349, with the food as actually served and the effect of eating it still unresolved. [2012 primary abstract and references](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/jmf.2012.0060) · [2014 citation discussion, p. 144](https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/revfacmed/article/download/43783/45050#page=4)

## Constipation among reported blueberry powder reactions

Basu et al. first published this eight-week metabolic syndrome trial on July 21, 2010. Thirty-four participants were enrolled in the freeze-dried blueberry powder group and 32 in the water control group. Nine in the blueberry group withdrew with first-week gastrointestinal effects that included nausea, vomiting, constipation and diarrhea. The report does not give a separate constipation count, and the other group’s withdrawals were reported under different reasons. The study supplies an adverse-event lead for [0746, raw blueberry and Parkinson’s constipation](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0746.txt), with a different population and concentrated powder. [Primary report, Results and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2924596/) · [First publication date, PMID 20660279](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20660279/)

## Berry extract registration during Helicobacter pylori treatment

ClinicalTrials.gov gives January 11, 2010 as the estimated first posting of NCT01045408. The Shin Kong Wu Ho-Su Memorial Hospital trial registered berry extract or placebo alongside triple therapy and listed eradication as its primary outcome. The public record reports no results and names no constipation outcome. Its investigator, hospital, trial objective and comparator match Chua et al.’s 2016 blueberry and grape seed extract report. The report’s abstract supplies no trial identifier, so this linkage is probable. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01045408) · [2016 outcome note](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Blueberry-Followup.md#blueberry-and-grape-seed-extract-during-helicobacter-pylori-treatment)

## Methyldopa compatibility in banana pulp

Uesawa and Mohri reported in March 2010 that the antihypertensive methyldopa degraded during incubation in fresh banana pulp supernatant. Heating the supernatant prevented the visible discoloration. The investigators attributed the reaction to banana polyphenol oxidase. This experiment measured drug compatibility in vitro. Levodopa and clinical outcomes were outside its study design. [Primary report, Figures 2 and 4](https://doi.org/10.3390/ph3030441) · [Related levodopa compatibility tests](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#banana-juice-and-levodopa-exposure-in-rats)

## Salmon roe nucleoprotein and locomotion in mice

Kiriyama and colleagues gave mice a nucleoprotein-free diet or the same diet with 1.2% nucleoprotein extracted from salmon soft roe before inducing parkinsonism. Supplemented mice showed less locomotor impairment and more tyrosine hydroxylase-positive cells than controls after the challenge. The accessible primary abstract does not provide a numerical locomotor estimate. The food fraction, animal model, and locomotor endpoint give indirect context for grilled salmon and bradykinesia `0583`. [Primary report, published online October 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25303858/) · [Archived source review](../2015-2019/Source-Text/PMC4983279.txt)

## Raw carrot in a jaw velocity test

Robertson and colleagues included 27 people with idiopathic Parkinson’s and 27 matched controls in a study of stimulation site and jaw movement. Participants bit raw carrot pieces of two thicknesses in random order and then expelled each piece. The clinical comparison concerned deep brain stimulation and levodopa effects on biting velocity. The procedure provides no test of consuming roasted carrot or of the freezing-of-gait outcome in `0070`. [Primary report, Methods and Figure 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3517909/) · [Archived source review](../2025-2029/Source-Text/PMC12852375.txt)

## Sweet potato as a mastication test bolus

Adachi and colleagues filmed three monkeys with unilateral MPTP-induced parkinsonism and three untreated monkeys as they chewed sweet potato slices. The treated animals preferentially chewed on the side opposite dopaminergic injury and had slower horizontal jaw movements. Both groups chewed the same test food. The between-group comparison concerns induced parkinsonism and chewing, with no baked sweet potato intervention, fatigue estimate `0023`, or dyskinesia estimate `0442`. [Primary report, PMID 22350537](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22350537/) · [Archived source review](../2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC9826258.txt)

## Green tea after a laboratory fatigue task

Watanabe, Kimura, and Imanishi randomized the order of 30 mL of green tea and temperature-matched distilled water in five adults who completed a two-hour typing task before each drink. At 30 minutes, total subjective fatigue changed from 20.4 to 10.8 points during the green tea condition (within-condition p = .019) and from 18.0 to 15.2 during the water condition. Table 2 gives no significant beverage-by-time interaction for total fatigue. The later 2013 Uji green tea meeting abstract describes the same task, crossover schedule, timing, and measurements and is treated as a companion report. This very small acute study measured task-induced fatigue outside Parkinson’s and gives indirect context for `0948`. [Primary Japanese report, Methods and Table 2](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jcam/10/1/10_9/_pdf) · [Companion abstract PN29](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0033-1352372) · [Later independent matcha trial](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#matcha-and-task-related-fatigue-in-young-adults)

## First report of the brewed green tea Parkinson pilot

Chen and colleagues first publicly reported the pilot in an April 2013 Experimental Biology abstract. The 14 participants, age range 51–79, three cups of green tea daily for three months, investigators, and laboratory results match the 2015 paper. The abstract reports favorable within-person changes in UPDRS motor examination and MMSE, with no fatigue estimate. [2013 meeting abstract](https://doi.org/10.1096/fasebj.27.1_supplement.368.1) · [2015 paper and dissertation follow-up](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#green-tea-and-fatigue-in-parkinsons-disease)

## Ultramicronized palmitoylethanolamide in Parkinson’s disease

Brotini and Guidi presented late-breaking abstract LBA 21 at the June 8–12, 2014 International Congress of Parkinson’s Disease and Movement Disorders in Stockholm. Thirty Parkinson outpatients received sublingual ultramicronized palmitoylethanolamide alongside levodopa in an uncontrolled series. They reported improved aggregate motor and nonmotor scores over 12 months. The meeting abstract supplies no separate fatigue or rigidity estimate for `0348` or `0544`. [Official 2014 congress program](https://www.mdscongress.org/MDS-Files/IC14-final-v9_lores.pdf) · [Primary meeting abstract LBA 21](https://www.mdscongress.org/MDS-Files/pdfs/Late-BreakingStudyGroupAbstractsPublication.pdf) · [2017 journal report](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#2017-journal-report-of-sublingual-palmitoylethanolamide)

## Orange-flavored probiotic for functional constipation

Jayasimhan and colleagues randomized 120 adults with functional constipation to orange-flavored Hexbio or a similar placebo for seven days. Median stool frequency during treatment was six versus four per week (p = .001). The preparation combined bacterial strains and fructo-oligosaccharide. The study does not isolate the flavor or fruit, and the population was a functional constipation cohort. A later trial tested a related formulation in Parkinson’s. [Primary report, PMID 23561636](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23561636/) · [Full primary article, Methods and Figure 2](https://magistralbr.caldic.com/storage/product-files/1320028027.pdf) · [Later Parkinson’s trial](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#orange-flavor-in-a-parkinsons-constipation-trial)

## Chicken essence and task-induced fatigue

Yamano and colleagues randomized 20 healthy men in a placebo-controlled crossover study of four-week courses of essence of chicken and a casein drink. Fatigue visual analog scores were lower after the rest session with chicken essence at one week. Both courses improved relative to baseline at four weeks. An earlier study found a smaller increase in Profile of Mood States fatigue with extract after an arithmetic task, while the short-term memory task did not yield a significant fatigue contrast. These trials tested concentrated drinks in healthy participants. This evidence is indirect for poached chicken and Parkinson’s fatigue (`0348`). [2013 primary report, Figures 2 and 3](https://medscimonit.com/download/index/idArt/883971) · [1996 primary study](../1995-1999/Food-and-Nutrition.md#1996--chicken-extract-and-laboratory-induced-mental-fatigue) · [Later cognition trial](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#chicken-essence-in-workers-under-stress)

## Shrimp contaminant assays

Brand and colleagues detected β-N-methylamino-L-alanine (BMAA) in pink shrimp tissue from South Florida, with substantial variation across samples. Jiang and colleagues reported BMAA in six Swedish-market shrimp samples (0.11–0.46 μg/g wet tissue), including samples labeled cooked, cooked and frozen, or smoked. Cooking method was unspecified for the samples labeled cooked. These studies provide contaminant-occurrence data without a human fall comparison for `0322`. [South Florida assay, PMID 21057660](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2968748/) · [Swedish market assay, PMID 25373604, Table 1](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep06931.pdf) · [Later market assay](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#shrimp-contaminant-assay-in-swedish-markets)

## Orange flavor in a psyllium and levodopa comparison

Fernandez-Martinez and colleagues compared orange-flavored psyllium with placebo in an 18-person Parkinson’s crossover. They reported similar levodopa exposure by area under the curve and fewer multiple concentration peaks with psyllium. The study measured pharmacokinetics and laboratory outcomes. It supplied no constipation severity estimate for raw orange (`0878`). A 1997 comparison studied bowel outcomes with psyllium itself. [Primary report, PMID 25112783, Methods and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4139609/) · [Local full text](Source-Text/PMC4139609.txt) · [2007 registration](../2005-2009/Source-Text/Trials/NCT00507715.txt) · [1997 bowel comparison](../1995-1999/Food-and-Nutrition.md#1997--psyllium-and-constipation)

## Date fruit extract in a diabetic neuropathic pain model

In a 2013 rat study, Shabani and colleagues reported longer response latency on a hot-plate test after oral date extract than after vehicle in rats with induced diabetes (6.9 versus 3.7 seconds in the Results section). The tail-flick test showed no clear difference. The abstract and Results section give different p-value thresholds for the hot-plate comparison. These pain-related responses in a diabetic animal model provide indirect evidence for `0502`. The tested exposure was an extract and the study did not measure pain in people with Parkinson’s. [Primary paper, Figures 1 and 3](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=28563) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2013-Shabani-Date-Extract-Thermal-and-Open-Field.txt)

## Registered whole-date crossover in healthy adults

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT02288611 on November 11, 2014, after the stated study completion. The record describes a randomized crossover comparing whole Ajwa dates with a carbohydrate control, and lists bowel measures and colonic biomarkers. The registry describes the design as open-label. The 2015 paper calls it single-masked. The record has no posted summary results. [Primary registry record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02288611) · [Publication assessment](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#whole-date-fruit-and-gastrointestinal-symptoms-outside-parkinsons)

## Nutritional status and fatigue severity

Fereshtehnejad and colleagues enrolled 150 people with idiopathic Parkinson’s disease in a cross-sectional study. Mean Fatigue Severity Scale scores were 5.4 in the group with abnormal nutritional status and 4.2 in the normal group (p < 0.001). The nutritional assessment combined general questionnaire and anthropometric measures. The study did not measure individual food intake. This provides nutrition and fatigue context for `0348`, which asks about poached chicken. [Primary paper, PMID 24608130](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0091153) · [Local text, Table 4](Source-Text/PMC3946796.txt)

## Yogurt in diagnostic swallowing exams

Coriolano and colleagues tested 15 adults with idiopathic Parkinson’s disease and 15 age-matched controls using firm yogurt and water during surface electromyography. Seventy percent of the Parkinson’s group needed multiple swallows for 5 mL of yogurt, compared with none of the controls. Sampaio and colleagues used a yogurt drink among several boluses to test whether a wet-sounding voice could identify penetration or aspiration during an endoscopic swallowing exam. Yogurt was a test material in both studies. Frozen claim `0832` asks about handwriting after strained yogurt. [Coriolano primary abstract, PMID 22644084](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22644084/) · [Coriolano paper and Table 1](https://www.academia.edu/69703481/Swallowing_in_Patients_with_Parkinson_s_Disease_A_Surface_Electromyography_Study) · [Sampaio primary abstract, PMID 25001523](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25001523/)

## Pinto bean and carrot feeding studies outside Parkinson’s disease

Winham and Hutchins pooled gastrointestinal symptom reporting from two randomized crossover studies and a parallel-arm study. The feeding interventions included pinto beans and other legumes, with canned carrot or soup comparators depending on the study. Flatulence increased in some pinto bean participants initially and typically became less frequent with time. Changes in stool frequency were reported by only one or two people during later weeks of the crossover studies. The studies did not enroll a Parkinson’s cohort or use a Parkinson’s constipation-severity outcome. Frozen claims `0888` and `0623` specify boiled pinto beans and steamed carrots, respectively. [Primary report, PMID 22104320](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3228670/)

## Dietary pattern in a single patient with Parkinson’s disease

Renoudet and colleagues reported one patient whose initial diet included fruits, vegetables, grains, olive oil, milk products, tea, coffee, spices, and dark chocolate. The initial regimen was followed by clinical decline. The authors later reported improvement in selected motor signs, including micrographia, after adding foods rich in fisetin and hexacosanol, with little improvement in tremor. This report used no control group and could not attribute a change to any single baseline ingredient. The case offers indirect context for the dark chocolate claims `0100`, `0660`, and `0703` and for the handwriting outcome in `0126`. The abstract does not identify the tea variety or preparation. [Primary case report, PMID 22846082](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22846082/)

## Registered one-week dark chocolate comparison

NCT02275884 planned to compare dark and cocoa-free white chocolate for one week each, measuring plasma constituents, aggregate motor and nonmotor symptom severity, cognition, depression, and quality of life. The register listed unknown recruitment status and no posted results on 2026-11-11. This registration is distinct from the published single-dose trial. It contains no outcome data for `0100`, `0660`, or `0703`. [Primary registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02275884) · [Local single-dose trial summary](Food-Experiment-Evidence.md)

## Acute Concord grape juice crossover, 2013 conference report

Haskell and Stuart reported a double-blind crossover in 20 healthy young adults on 30 August 2013. A single 200 mL portion of Concord purple grape juice increased self-rated calm and shortened attention reaction time relative to a sugar- and flavor-matched drink after 20 minutes (both p < 0.05). Memory showed no clear difference. The sample size, investigators, exposure and methods match the 2017 full report closely enough to count the two reports together. The drink, healthy population and self-rated calm outcome make this indirect for raw grape and Parkinson anxiety (`0860`). [Primary proceedings abstract](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0029665113002206) · [2017 full report summary](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#acute-purple-grape-juice-and-mood-full-report)

## Freeze-dried grape powder and anxiety-like behavior in rats

Allam and colleagues used a standardized freeze-dried powder made from whole table grapes in a rat oxidative-stress experiment published on 17 April 2013. The journal retracted the article in 2025 after concerns about the reliability of multiple figure panels and the inability to supply the original image files. The retracted study carries no evidentiary weight for `0860`. [Retracted primary report, PMID 23596160](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3652881/) · [Retraction notice summary](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#retraction-of-a-grape-powder-rat-study)

Patki and colleagues reported a separate ovariectomy model on 9 September 2013. Rats given whole freeze-dried grape powder in drinking water showed reduced anxiety-like behavior in open-field and other assays relative to untreated ovariectomized rats. Solanki and colleagues reported a separate single-prolonged-stress experiment in November 2014, with fewer anxiety-like and depressive-like behaviors after grape powder. The models and reconstituted powder provide indirect context for raw grape and Parkinson anxiety. [Primary ovariectomy study, PMID 24040270](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0074522) · [Primary 2014 conference program, poster 846](https://www.sfn.org/-/media/SfN/Documents/Annual-Meeting/FinalProgram/NS2014/FullAbstractPDFs_2014/SFN2014_Abstracts_PDF_Wed_PM.pdf) · [Full stress-model report, PMID 25533441](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nutres.2014.11.008)

## Isolated theaflavin in MPTP mouse studies

Two Anandhan reports first available in 2011 and 2012 studied isolated theaflavin in chronic MPTP/probenecid mouse models. They report improved selected behavioral tests, including catalepsy and akinesia in the 2012 report, and favorable tissue markers. A separate 2012 subacute MPTP report is retracted. These experiments used a black-tea compound and did not test brewed black tea or clinical bradykinesia and rest tremor in people with Parkinson disease (`0082`, `0904`). [Chronic mouse report, PMID 22138428](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22138428/) · [Chronic mouse report, PMID 22669749](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22669749/) · [Retracted subacute mouse report, PMID 22634505](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22634505/)

## Dark chocolate and anxiety outside Parkinson’s disease

Lua and Wong’s 2011 open-label study analyzed 47 trainee nurses who received dark chocolate or mineral water for three days. Table 4 reports lower post-treatment anxiety scores in the chocolate group. Median HADS anxiety scores were 5 versus 7, and DASS anxiety scores were 10 versus 16. High attrition and an unblinded water comparison limit interpretation. The study involved no Parkinson cohort. [Primary report, Tables 3–4](https://www.aseanjournalofpsychiatry.org/articles/can-dark-chocolate-alleviate-anxiety-depressive-and-stress-symptoms-among-trainee-nurses-a-parallel-openlabel-study.pdf)

Martin and colleagues randomized 90 healthy young adults to a 20 g bar of 74% cocoa dark chocolate, milk-chocolate wafer fingers or cheese and crackers on three test days. The detailed Results section reports no significant dark-chocolate effect on state anxiety in either baseline trait-anxiety stratum during the first hour. This conflicts with the abstract’s suggestion of benefit in the lower-anxiety stratum. Pase and colleagues found greater calmness and contentedness at 30 days with the higher-polyphenol dark chocolate drink in healthy middle-aged adults, using a mood scale. These comparisons give indirect context for plain dark chocolate and anxiety in Parkinson disease (`0100`). [Martin primary report, Results and Figure 1](https://doi.org/10.3390/nu4060554) · [Pase primary report, PMID 23364814](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23364814/)

## Reported orange intake and Rome III constipation in older adults

Nesello, Tonelli and Beltrame studied 139 older adults at an Itajai community center in Brazil. The publisher records April 8, 2012 and the issue is labeled 2011. Mean age was 73.55 years and 84.9% were women. In Table 3, 31 of 120 respondents who reported consuming the orange item (laranja) met Rome III constipation criteria (25.83%), versus 9 of 19 nonconsumers (47.37%). The table gives an unadjusted chi-square p value of .054. The qualitative food-frequency item does not identify variety, raw preparation, serving amount or timing. Constipation classification was binary, with no severity change estimate or Parkinson subgroup. The cross-sectional comparison leaves diet, medications, other confounding and reverse causation unresolved. [Publisher record and date](https://www.e-publicacoes.uerj.br/ceres/article/view/2125) [Original source](https://www.e-publicacoes.uerj.br/ceres/article/download/2125/2113/11181) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2012-Nesello-Orange-Constipation-Table-3.txt)

## First conference report of the whole-Ajwa crossover

The June 2013 ISAPP Student and Fellows abstract by Eid and colleagues describes 22 volunteers and the same whole-date crossover later registered as NCT02288611. Twenty-one-day periods of dates and carbohydrate control were separated by a 14-day washout. Its outcomes concern bowel and microbial measures. The 2015 article mentions a mood diary without publishing its results. Both reports belong to the same source family. [Organizers’ 2013 author abstract book](https://isappscience.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/SFA_program_Abstract_booklet_2013.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2013-Eid-Whole-Ajwa-Conference.txt) · [2015 report](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#whole-date-fruit-and-gastrointestinal-symptoms-outside-parkinsons)

## Fresh date fruit extract in diabetic neuropathy models

The 2011 Zangiabadi study reports longer open-field travel and more rearing after aqueous fresh Bam-date extract than in diabetic sham Wistar rats (p < .05). Grooming remained lower than in healthy controls, and center/perimeter time showed no clear difference. The article’s 38.50 m/s diabetic-sham and 51.36 m/s healthy-control means imply about 25% slower motor nerve conduction, consistent with its Discussion. The Results text incorrectly calls the reduction 133.4%. Table 1 labels the sham arm as nine while the Methods and table footnote give eight. This model and oral extract provide no estimate of apathy severity following dried fruit. [Original open-access article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3236446/) · [Original author rendering, Results and figures](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51907511_Date_Fruit_Extract_Is_a_Neuroprotective_Agent_in_Diabetic_Peripheral_Neuropathy_in_Streptozotocin-Induced_Diabetic_Rats_A_Multimodal_Analysis) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2011-Zangiabadi-Date-Extract-and-Exploration.txt)

The 2013 Shabani study cites the earlier preparation and also reports longer travel and more rearing on extract than in the diabetic vehicle group (p < .05). The 2013 Methods name Sprague-Dawley rats and additional treatment and nociception endpoints. The strain difference and group composition distinguish the reported designs. Individual-animal identity cannot be verified across reports. Neither used an apathy scale. [Original 2013 Methods and Figure 5](https://www.scirp.net/journal/paperinformation?paperid=28563) · [Local 2013 factual text](Source-Text/2013-Shabani-Date-Extract-Thermal-and-Open-Field.txt)

## Early reports of Khalas extract in transgenic mice

Subash and colleagues’ 2014 conference presentation and 2015 behavioral paper describe the same investigation according to the later publication. The original conference abstract names the same maze and movement battery and includes conflicting age descriptions across sections. The June 2014 online oxidative paper reports 18 female transgenic and six wild-type mice, the matching 2% and 4% dried-fruit extract diets and the same ethics approval. It states that tissue was collected following behavioral assessments but omits their data. Its feeding duration of 15 months differs from 14 months in the 2015 behavior report. These sources are part of a closely matched research program with possible shared animals. An independent confirmation and an apathy effect are both unestablished. [2014 author abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/261886041_Memory_deficits_and_learning_skills_improved_in_transgenic_mouse_model_of_Alzheimer%27s_disease_after_date-rich_diet_supplementation) · [2014 university inventory](https://www.squ.edu.om/Portals/10/DNNGalleryPro/uploads/2019/4/24/CAMSResearchReport2014.pdf) · [Oxidative study metadata](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/effect-of-dietary-supplementation-of-dates-in-alzheimers-disease-/) · [2015 behavior paper](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#extract-of-dried-khalas-and-behavioral-testing-in-transgenic-mice) · [Conference factual text](Source-Text/2014-Subash-FASEB-Khalas-Behavior.txt) · [Oxidative study factual text](Source-Text/2014-Subash-Khalas-Oxidative-Study.txt)

## Isolated broccoli-associated sulforaphane and mouse motor tests

Morroni et al. first published PMID 23518299 on March 18, 2013. The author abstract reports improved rotation and motor coordination after isolated sulforaphane in 6-OHDA-lesioned mice. The experiment used a compound also found in cruciferous vegetables. It supplies no steamed broccoli exposure or clinical fall count for claim 1000. ## Isolated luteolin and combined capsaicin in cell studies

Guo et al., first published October 5, 2012, PMID 23035972, reported less cytotoxicity and apoptosis after exposing a rat PC12 cell model to isolated luteolin. The author abstract reports p < .001 for suppression of apoptosis in two of the tested concentration groups. This is a cell endpoint without a dried thyme exposure, clinical anxiety or postural instability. Lee et al., first published August 25, 2012, PMID 22943972, reported more viable cultured mouse cortical neurons with combined capsaicin and resveratrol than with either alone in a glutamate toxicity assay. This comparison supplies no paprika consumption or executive function data. Full article tables remain unverified. [Guo original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23035972/) · [Lee original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22943972/)
