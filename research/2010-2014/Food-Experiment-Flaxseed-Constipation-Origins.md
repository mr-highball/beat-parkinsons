# Flaxseed Constipation Origins

## Early Brazilian oil and constipation conference comparison

Grilli, Lima and Cuppari presented a four-week randomized comparison in hemodialysis. Of 47 people found constipated, 33 began and 29 completed, with nine mineral-oil, 11 olive-oil and nine flaxseed-oil completers. This report started at 8 mL/day with titration. Reported flaxseed-arm stool consistency changed from 1.56 to 2.06, p=.06. Hard or fragmented stools were reported less often in all groups. The date, starting dose and denominators differ from the later trial registry and journal article. The relationship between cohorts has not been established. ## Preliminary three-oil dialysis comparison

The investigators reported 36 randomized dialysis patients, 11 assigned mineral oil, 11 flaxseed oil and 14 olive oil, with four weeks of treatment at an initial 4 mL/day. The percentages of reported symptoms declined in the flaxseed arm, but that column has no significance markers and no item-specific between-arm test is supplied. The investigators and comparison resemble the later 50-person article. Whether participants overlap is unresolved, so the reports cannot be counted as independent replications. [Original source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/271607674_A_DOUBLE-BLINDED_RANDOMIZED_CONTROLLED_TRIAL_ON_THE_EFFECT_OF_FLAXSEED_AND_OLIVE_OILS_ON_CONSTIPATION_OF_HEMODIALYSIS_PATIENTS_PRELIMINARY_RESULTS) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/KRCP-2012-A88-Flaxseed-Oil-Constipation.txt)

## Registered three-oil dialysis constipation trial

The official record lists 50 hemodialysis patients, randomized to four weeks of oral mineral oil, olive oil or flaxseed oil at 4 mL/day initially. The primary outcome is Rome III constipation symptom total, range 0–24. Listed study dates are April 2012 to April 2013, followed by retrospective public registration. The matching journal abstract reports means of 10.5 to 4.1, 10.3 to 3.2 and 9.6 to 6.0, respectively, with an inconclusive group-by-time p=.15. It reports improved frequency and stool consistency in the flaxseed arm without separate numerical contrasts. No grade or registry results were available. The same-title UNIFESP thesis is indexed and its body remains inaccessible through checked routes.
