# Turmeric preparations and model outcomes

## Whole turmeric powder suspension in an MPTP injury experiment

Mythri and colleagues first published April 8, 2011. Mice received a boiled suspension made with whole turmeric powder for three months before an MPTP challenge. The primary figures report changes in brain glutathione, mitochondrial complex I activity and neuronal tissue. The authors report no apparent difference in general activity while animals received supplementation and give no activity data. That report supplies no formal bradykinesia, rigidity or pain measure. [Publisher original](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114510005817) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2011-Mythri-Whole-Turmeric-Extract.txt)

## Injected curcumin and locomotion in homocysteine-exposed rats

Mansouri and colleagues first published March 15, 2012. The authors began intraperitoneal isolated curcumin before homocysteine exposure. Figure 1 and the original text report better open-field distance and velocity relative to injury alone. The paper contains inconsistent untreated versus vehicle-control language and conflicting wording about the injury injection schedule. Numerical group effects remain unverified from the graph. The study supplies indirect locomotion context for the ground turmeric question. [Publisher record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12031-012-9727-3) · [Author-shared full report](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221896433_Curcumin_has_Neuroprotection_Effect_on_Homocysteine_Rat_Model_of_Parkinson) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2012-Mansouri-Curcumin-Movement-Extract.txt)

## Dietary curcumin and skilled pellet reaching

Schmitz and colleagues first published April 11, 2014. The full article and earlier institutional thesis describe six young-mouse groups. In the paper's Figure 1, the two isolated curcumin alone groups, the manganese alone group and the two combination groups made fewer successful forepaw pellet reaches than control. The paper reports no clear beam-walking or open-field difference and gives inconsistent sample notation between the Methods and Figure 1. The outcome is skilled reaching during dietary isolated exposure in mice. The study leaves Parkinson bradykinesia after ground turmeric unresolved.
