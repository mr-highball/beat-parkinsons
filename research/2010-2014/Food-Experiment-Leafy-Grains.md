# Leafy vegetable and grain evidence, 2010–2014

## Thawed spinach and measured attention in healthy adults

Bondonno and colleagues compared four energy-matched treatments in a randomized crossover of 30 healthy adults. The spinach was a batch of commercial frozen spinach thawed before serving with lunch. Its lower-nitrate control was rice milk. No cooking step is reported for the served spinach. At 150 minutes, power of attention was 1170 versus 1166 on the Cognitive Drug Research battery (p = .70). Continuity of attention was 92.1 versus 92.2 (p = .84). The nitrogen oxide biomarkers changed, while the attention contrasts were inconclusive. The exact cooked preparation and Parkinson population in claim 0641 were outside the comparison. [Primary accepted manuscript, Methods and Table 3](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/getauthorversionpdf/C3FO60590F) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24676365/)

## Gluten withdrawal in a case of parkinsonism and celiac disease

Di Lazzaro and colleagues described a 75-year-old with walking difficulty, instability, fatigability and biopsy-confirmed silent celiac disease. The title and subsequent review describe improvement after a gluten-free diet. The institutional primary preview stops before the treatment and outcome table. It does not assign rye bread or cooked wheat berries, or give a rest tremor or freezing episode frequency contrast. This dietary withdrawal in one person with celiac disease is context for the grain motor claims. [Primary case, university archive](https://publires.unicatt.it/en/publications/dramatic-improvement-of-parkinsonian-symptoms-after-gluten-free-d-9/) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24464413/)

First online January 25, 2014. The accessible original describes a 75-year-old with walking difficulty, instability, fatigability and baseline UPDRS II 8 and III 19. The reproduction stops before treatment and Table 1. The published title and a later review describe improved parkinsonism after gluten withdrawal in celiac disease, while the complete primary course and freezing outcomes remain unverified here. The case does not isolate rye bread. The overlapping authors' 2017 celiac cortical-excitability study is not a documented follow-up of this case. ## Registration for biofortified pearl millet feeding

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT02152150 on June 2, 2014, after the recorded study start. The two feeding groups received iron-biofortified or control pearl millet. The primary outcome was change in iron status. The investigators later reported attention and reaction-time tests in a 140-participant adolescent study. ## Talbinah and anxiety scales in long-term care

Badrasawi and colleagues’ crossover included 30 older residents. The barley-based product was a prepared talbinah mixture, with habitual diet as the comparison. Table 2 reports an inconclusive within-intervention change on DASS anxiety (11.2 to 8.8, p = .100) and a decrease on the POMS tension–anxiety subscale (11.7 to 7.9, p = .002). The table gives paired within-period tests, and the anxiety-related scores differed at baseline. The trial does not isolate boiled barley or report a Parkinson subgroup. [Primary article, Methods and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3593710/)
