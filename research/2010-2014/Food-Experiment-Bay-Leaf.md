# Culinary laurel leaf foreign body

## Laurel leaf in soup and acute swallowing difficulty

El-Tarchichi, Yafi and Debek published this case online April 8, 2010. A 43-year-old man with asthma developed acute swallowing difficulty, throat discomfort and a sense of suffocation after choking while eating soup. Endoscopy found a leaf lodged behind the arytenoid processes near the epiglottis. The clinicians identified it as laurel used to season the soup. The figure shows the removed leaf measuring just over 3 cm. Symptoms resolved immediately after removal. The authors suggested intermittent upper-airway obstruction during deep inspiration. The report gives no botanical species confirmation, Parkinson diagnosis, ground preparation or drooling measurement. The November 2010 issue date and September 2008 acceptance are distinct from the first online date. ## Search scope for ground bay leaf and Parkinson swallowing or drooling

Searches paired bay leaf, bay laurel and *Laurus nobilis* with ground, powdered and capsule terms, plus Parkinson, dysphagia, swallowing, drooling, sialorrhea and salivation. French, Spanish and Portuguese searches included *laurier moulu*, *laurel molido* and *louro moído*. A Europe PMC title, abstract and subject search for the plant names with Parkinson returned one essential-oil laboratory study. A title and abstract search for the plant names with swallowing, drooling or impaction terms returned the two 1990 reports and a duodenal case. Broader web and citation searches located the additional cases above. The retrieved primary human ground-leaf study measured metabolic outcomes in type 2 diabetes. A 2015 saliva study used a rinse made from *Syzygium polyanthum*, named *Eugenia polyantha* in the paper, and measured pH in students. The ground-bay-leaf swallowing and drooling outcomes in Parkinson disease remain unverified.
