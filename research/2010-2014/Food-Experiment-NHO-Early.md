# Food, nutrition and swallowing in early National Hospital Organization proceedings

## Meal protein and levodopa in advanced Parkinson disease

Risa Mizuta and colleagues at NHO Tokushima Hospital studied 22 people with advanced Parkinson's disease and difficult motor fluctuations. They examined blood levodopa and motor function two hours after each meal on a single day. The authors identified a possible meal protein association in 14 people, seven at one meal and seven at two or more meals. They proposed considering protein redistribution in the seven with a possible association at one meal. The abstract gives no meal ingredients, protein amounts, medication-to-meal intervals, comparative effect estimate or daily OFF duration. The investigators observed usual meals and did not test a protein-redistribution regimen. [2012 NHO O-01-4, PDF page 95](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100778.pdf#page=95)

The same group's 2012 Japanese Society of Neurology abstract used the same sampling schedule with nine patients. Five had a post-meal OFF state with low levodopa and a possible association with dietary protein. Three of the five had that pattern at one meal and two at more than one. The shared authors, site and methods suggest possible participant overlap. The 2011 NHO meeting and a February 2012 hospital program list a preceding Mizuta presentation about meals and levodopa, with no findings in the public programs. [2012 JSN P(1)-156, printed p. 1437](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/clinicalneurol/52/12/52_1397/_pdf/-char/ja#page=41) · [2011 NHO program, O-70-9](https://www.convention-w.jp/nms65/img/pro007.pdf) · [2012 hospital program, A-11](https://tokushima.hosp.go.jp/files/000024352.pdf)

## Neck posture and videofluoroscopic swallowing

Satoshi Tomita and colleagues examined seated videofluoroscopy in 59 Parkinson and 28 progressive supranuclear palsy patients. They measured neck extension and rigidity and adjusted for age, sex and MMSE. Greater extension was associated with a worse videofluoroscopic dysphagia score in Parkinson (p = .02), while the PSP neck-rigidity association was borderline (p = .06). A likely companion JSDR abstract with the same cohort sizes and demographics reports p = .049 for neck extension in Parkinson. The printed recruitment windows and results differ, and neither abstract identifies a test bolus or gives a measured response to a change in diet. [2012 NHO P1-56-6, PDF page 326](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100778.pdf#page=326) · [JSDR companion context](Food-Experiment-JSDR-Abstracts.md)

## Meal positioning in four Parkinson cases

Megumi Tada and colleagues described four Parkinson patients who had difficulty positioning themselves for meals. The reported changes included adjusting the wheelchair angle and supports, using pillows or a cushion, and changing table height and the shape of the dish. Two reported meal durations changed from more than 30 to 20 minutes and from 30 to 15 minutes. One participant resumed more comfortable self-feeding without a meal-time change. No meal ingredient or instrumental swallowing outcome was reported. [2012 NHO P4-08-2, PDF page 588](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100778.pdf#page=588)

## Mixed-diagnosis hospital swallowing service

Ōmuta Hospital implemented a team feeding and swallowing service in 2013. In 2014 Sakai and colleagues enrolled 25 mixed-diagnosis patients and excluded eight without paired questionnaires. Three months of therapy included direct swallowing practice with unspecified food, exercises and oral ice massage. Pooled individual questionnaire responses across severity categories changed (p = .04). Ueno and colleagues reported the same sample size, age distribution, sex distribution and recruitment dates. Diet texture level was maintained in 72% and advanced in 20%. The two reports likely describe the same program cohort. Their pooled data give no named food exposure or Parkinson-specific result. [2013 NHO P2-1P-793, PDF page 419](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100777.pdf#page=419) · [2014 NHO O-87-5, PDF page 274](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100776.pdf#page=274) · [2014 NHO O-64-6, PDF page 232](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100776.pdf#page=232)

## Powdered green tea in two tube-fed Parkinson cases

Kakami and Murakami screened five patients receiving tube feeds for postprandial hypotension. Two with Parkinson disease had a marked systolic blood pressure drop after feeds. Both received powdered green tea dissolved in the warm water normally given through their feeding tube. The powder supplied the equivalent of 250 mg caffeine. The authors observed less of a post-feed drop in both patients and reported p ≤ .05 at 30 minutes for one. The abstract gives no exact blood pressure difference, comparison beverage, fatigue measurement or effect from brewed tea taken by mouth. [2013 NHO P1-1P-290, PDF page 293](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100777.pdf#page=293)

## Tube-feeding route and gut microbiota

Murata and colleagues compared four orally fed and four tube-fed patients with advanced Parkinson disease and healthy adults. All tube-fed patients received Cz-Hi enteral formula. Total microbial counts were similar, but the tube-fed Parkinson group had lower bifidobacterial counts, with some taxa below detection. In one patient, *Clostridium perfringens* was detected during oral feeding and was below detection after a switch to tube feeding as disease worsened. The comparison confounds route, formula and disease state. It measured microbial abundance and gives no clinical symptom change or named ordinary-food exposure. [2013 NHO P1-2A-098, PDF page 484](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100777.pdf#page=484)

## Oral intake after aspiration-prevention surgery

A 2014 case report follows a person with Parkinson disease and recurrent aspiration pneumonia despite prior gastrostomy. Oral feeding resumed briefly after aspiration-prevention surgery and was interrupted by a passage problem. Four months later videofluoroscopy found no obstruction, and the patient resumed oral intake. At one year, weight had increased from 44.4 to 49.2 kg, albumin from 2.0 to 3.7 g/dL and hemoglobin from 9.6 to 12.2 g/dL. The abstract gives no meal ingredients or test bolus. The outcomes followed surgery and renewed food intake. [2014 NHO P4-16-6, PDF page 646](https://nho.hosp.go.jp/files/000100776.pdf#page=646)
