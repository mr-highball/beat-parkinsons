Source: Patil R, Hiray Y, Shinde S, Langade P. Reversal of haloperidol-induced orofacial dyskinesia by Murraya koenigii leaves in experimental animals.
Journal: Pharmaceutical Biology (2012; published June 2012)
Identifiers: PMID:22136413 | DOI:10.3109/13880209.2011.618841
Source URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22136413/
Access basis: Official PubMed XML abstract (paywalled full text).

Study summary and reported findings
- Evaluated oral ethanolic extract of Murraya koenigii (curry leaf) leaves (EEMK, 100 and 300 mg/kg p.o.) and its alkaloid fraction (AMK, 30 and 100 mg/kg p.o.) in rats chronically treated with haloperidol (1 mg/kg i.p. for 21 days) to model neuroleptic-induced orofacial dyskinesia (vacuous chewing movements, tongue protrusions, orofacial bursts).
- Chronic haloperidol reduced locomotion, induced vacuous chewing movements (VCMs), orofacial bursts (OBs), and tongue protrusions (TPs), decreased forebrain superoxide dismutase (SOD), catalase (CAT), and glutathione (GSH), and increased lipid peroxidation (LPO).
- Oral co-administration of EEMK (100 and 300 mg/kg) and AMK (30 and 100 mg/kg) significantly reversed haloperidol-induced locomotor deficits and oxidative-stress shifts (SOD, CAT, GSH, LPO) alongside attenuation of orofacial dyskinesia parameters.

Relevance and limits for Parkinson's food experiments
- Companion line of investigation to Patil et al. (2012, PMID:24250488 / PMC3832166, reserpine-induced orofacial dyskinesia and catalepsy).
- Limits: Models neuroleptic-induced tardive orofacial dyskinesia rather than Parkinsonian resting limb tremor; uses concentrated ethanolic leaf extract and isolated alkaloid fraction rather than cooked culinary curry leaves; exact per-group sample counts and full behavioral numeric tables require full-text access.
