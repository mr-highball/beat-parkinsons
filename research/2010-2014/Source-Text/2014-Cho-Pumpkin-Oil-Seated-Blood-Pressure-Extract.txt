Young Hye Cho and colleagues. Effect of Pumpkin Seed Oil on Hair Growth in Men with Androgenetic Alopecia: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Trial. Evid Based Complement Alternat Med 2014:549721. Published April 23, 2014. PMID 24864154, PMCID PMC4017725. DOI 10.1155/2014/549721.

The study randomized 37 participants to branded capsules containing a stated 400 mg/day pumpkin seed oil and 39 to visually matched placebo for 24 weeks. No cold-press grade or complete capsule formula is stated in the Methods. Blood pressure was measured seated after ten minutes of rest. Two readings separated by three minutes were averaged.

Table 2 mean systolic change was −1.2 mmHg (SD 11.3) in the intervention and +1.0 (SD 15.8) in placebo, with between-group p = 0.496. Diastolic changes were −0.1 (SD 10.0) and −0.8 (SD 13.9), respectively, p = 0.813. Safety reports describe itching in one participant in each arm and mild abdominal discomfort in one intervention participant. The reported symptoms and seated pressure do not establish an orthostatic endpoint. Participants were recruited for androgenetic alopecia, with no defined Parkinson cohort.
