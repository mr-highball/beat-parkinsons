Title: Sage leaf aqueous and butanol extracts and animal pain behaviors
Publication: 2010-10
PMID: 20860437
DOI: 10.3109/13880200903530763
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20860437/
Publisher: https://doi.org/10.3109/13880200903530763
Author-deposited article: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/46402485_The_antinociceptive_and_anti-inflammatory_effects_of_Salvia_officinalis_leaf_aqueous_and_butanol_extracts
Coverage: Primary article, pp. 1149–1156, including methods, Figures 1–5 and Tables 1–2. Factual summary.

Qnais and colleagues identified wild Salvia officinalis in Jordan with voucher HU-153. They air-dried the leaves before making the aqueous extract and used a powdered plant fraction for the solvent extract. Behavioral tests had six animals per group and used intraperitoneal extract administration, with saline control and morphine reference. The extracts delayed the paw-lick response in male mice on a hot plate and shortened paw licking in both formalin phases in male Wistar rats. The reported early-phase ED50 estimates were 28.5 and 20.2 mg/kg for aqueous and butanol extracts, respectively. Late-phase estimates were 100 and 26.5 mg/kg. The paper describes visual curve fitting and does not supply a human pain measurement.

Table 1 reports residual effects in both extract groups after naloxone. The table lacks parallel extract-alone rows for those dose levels. The cotton-pellet experiment measures tissue growth and has a route discrepancy between oral dosing in Methods and intraperitoneal dosing in Results. The injected behavioral results remain distinct from the inflammatory endpoint and from consumption of fresh sage in Parkinson disease.
