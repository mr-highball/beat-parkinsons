Title: Neuro-activity effects of Phoenix dactylifera fruit and pits extract in chronic stressed rat models
Source: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/287380342_Neuro-activity_effects_of_phoenix_dactylifera_fruit_and_pits_extract_in_chronic_stressed_rat_models

Publication year: 2012, as dated by the public abstract and chapter index
Citation: Mohamad RH, El-Bastawesy AM, et al. Asia Pacific Life Sciences Compendium, pp. 97–117, ISBN 978-1-62257-338-7.
Text coverage: Original summary of the primary abstract. The complete book chapter and possible journal version remain unverified.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

The abstract describes fruit and pit methanol extracts of date palm in stressed rats and identifies fluoxetine as a comparator. For the fruit-extract group the investigators report improved sucrose consumption. The abstract gives no consumption values, preference ratio, baseline denominator, dispersion or individually identified comparator for this observation. Intake of a sweet solution and a two-bottle preference ratio are distinct measurements. The separate seed and fruit preparations should be kept distinct.

The experiment studied animal stress-related behavior and tissue measurements. It provides a lead on reward-related behavior after a solvent-extracted preparation. It does not test clinical apathy in Parkinson disease or consumption of whole dried fruit. The extract preparation, animal-group allocations and any behavioral figure require the complete chapter or a verified original journal report. The abstract identifies a fluoxetine comparison for the study, without enough outcome-level detail to estimate a fruit-extract contrast against it. Keep this source separate from the 2025 Asdaq mouse study unless overlap evidence emerges.
