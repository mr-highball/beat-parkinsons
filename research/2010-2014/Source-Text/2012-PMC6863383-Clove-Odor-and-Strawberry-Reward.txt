Title: ACNP 2012 Poster Session I — clove detection and strawberry milkshake reinforcement
PMCID: PMC6863383
DOI: 10.1038/npp.2012.219
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6863383/
First published: 2012-11-30 online, December 2012 issue.
Coverage: Members M29 and M30 extracted from their original posted text.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

M29, Young et al., used strawberry milkshake as a common reward in a mouse probabilistic-learning task. The comparison varied striatal dopamine D1 receptor suppression. The suppression group took longer to reach the learning criterion. No fruit component was randomized. The 2017 primary paper reports the milk and flavoring recipe and distinguishes reward learning from motivation, with matching authors and study design.

M30, Woolley et al., reports a randomized intranasal oxytocin and placebo crossover with 22 men with schizophrenia and 20 healthy controls. The odor test included clove oil, anise oil and Lyral. The reported clove threshold showed no clear change under oxytocin. An earlier 2011 abstract reports 22 patients and eight controls with the same three odors. The 2015 journal paper gives a larger sample and presents two odors, without a clove result. Keep the two conference reports and journal observations as a likely study family with participant overlap unresolved.

The clove preparation is a sensory probe and the strawberry formulation a shared reward. These exposures yield no oral-food comparison for the frozen symptom claims.
