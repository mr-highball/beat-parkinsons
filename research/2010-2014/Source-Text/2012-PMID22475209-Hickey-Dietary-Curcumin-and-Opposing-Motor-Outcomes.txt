Original source scope
Dietary isolated curcumin shortened rotarod time in Huntington model and wild-type mice. At 20 rpm the genotype-matched controls and exposed groups averaged 139 versus 37 seconds in wild-type and 182 versus 34 seconds in knock-in mice. A separate adult wild-type cohort also deteriorated on the rotarod. Pole performance and other movement or pathology outcomes followed different patterns.

Evidence boundary or continuing gap
Time to fall from a rotating rod is a motor task outcome. The paper compares isolated dietary curcumin outside Parkinson disease and provides no clinical fall-frequency result or raw orange exposure.
