Aniwan S, Gonlachanvit S. Effects of chili on abdominal pain, abdominal burning and rectal sensation in diarrhea predominate irritable bowel syndrome. Thai J Gastroenterol. 2012;13(1):29–37. Original abstract mirrored at https://db.hitap.net/articles/1726. Full volume retrieval timed out during verification.
Ten IBS-D patients completed six-week chili and placebo periods. The daily active dose was 2.1 g chili powder, approximately 2.5 mg capsaicin, in three premeal doses. In thesis Table 6 (pp. 34–35), week-six abdominal pain scores were 30.10 (±8.55) versus 23.70 (±9.95) on the 100-mm scale and Figure 1 reports p>.05. Figure 5 shows a separate spicy-meal challenge burning outcome, reported as 4.3 ± 2.4 versus 14.1 ± 5.1 (p=.02) in the journal abstract. First rectal sensation was 12 versus 8 mmHg (p=.03). Similar protocol, institution, investigators and outcomes suggest overlapping reports and a likely relationship to the 16-patient 2014 cohort. No explicit participant linkage was found.
