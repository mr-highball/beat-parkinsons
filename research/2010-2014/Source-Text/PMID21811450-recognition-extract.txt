Title: True and False Recognition Memories of Odors Induce Distinct Neural Signatures
Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2011.00065/full
PMID: 21811450
PMCID: PMC3143719
DOI: 10.3389/fnhum.2011.00065
Publication: 21 July 2011. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 5:65.

The study recruited 16 younger and 22 older adults without known olfactory impairment or neurological disease. Tarragon was one of 50 target odors listed in Table 1, along with 50 distractors. Odorants were prepared in mineral oil. Participants first pressed a key on detecting each presented odor. In the retrieval phase they indicated whether odorants were previously presented.

Recognition was pooled across response classes and stimuli. Older adults produced more false recognition and less distinct patterns of activation across response classes than younger adults. The study reports no tarragon-specific detection threshold, no tarragon item recognition estimate, and no tarragon dosing or Parkinson disease intervention. The stimulus can be traced without interpreting it as a treatment.
