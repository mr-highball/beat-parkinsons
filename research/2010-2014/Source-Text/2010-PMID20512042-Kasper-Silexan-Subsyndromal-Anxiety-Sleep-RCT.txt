Title: Silexan, an orally administered Lavandula oil preparation, is effective in the treatment of 'subsyndromal' anxiety disorder: a randomized, double-blind, placebo controlled trial.
PMID: 20512042
DOI: 10.1097/YIC.0b013e32833b3242
First published: 2010
Authors: Kasper S, Gastpar M, Müller WE, Volz HP, Möller HJ, Dienel A, Schläfke S
Journal: International clinical psychopharmacology
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20512042/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Multicenter double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial (Kasper et al., 2010, Int Clin Psychopharmacol) across 27 practices enrolling 221 adults with subsyndromal anxiety disorder (HAMA >= 18 and PSQI > 5) randomized to oral Silexan (standardized Lavandula angustifolia essential oil softgel, 80 mg/day) or placebo for 10 weeks. Co-primary endpoints improved significantly more with Silexan vs placebo: HAMA total score fell 16.0+/-8.3 (59.3%) vs 9.5+/-9.1 (35.4%) (p<0.01) and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI) fell 5.5+/-4.4 (44.7%) vs 3.8+/-4.1 (30.9%) (p<0.01), with higher responder (76.9% vs 49.1%, p<0.001) and remitter (60.6% vs 42.6%, p=0.009) rates and improved SF-36 mental/physical health without sedation. Limits: proprietary oral essential oil preparation rather than brewed or dried culinary lavender; non-PD cohort.
