Institutional publication record: https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/effect-of-dietary-supplementation-of-dates-in-alzheimers-disease-/
Coverage: factual summary of author original Methods and outcome scope.

Eighteen female transgenic mice and six wild-type mice were split across 2% and 4% date-extract diets and two control groups. Dried Omani date flesh was acetone-extracted. The study collected tissue after behavioral assessments that were not included in the paper. Cerebral oxidative markers, acetylcholinesterase and ATP appear among the assayed outcomes. The 2015 behavioral publication shares sex, group numbers, extract, orchard, animal model and ethics approval. The oxidative paper describes 15 months of feeding and the 2015 paper reports 14 months before behavioral testing. Animal-level cohort identity remains unresolved.
