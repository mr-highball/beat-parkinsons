Title: Acute effects of mustard, horseradish, black pepper and ginger on energy expenditure, appetite, ad libitum energy intake and energy balance in human subjects
PMID: 23021155
DOI: 10.1017/S0007114512001201
First published: 2013
Authors: Gregersen NT, Belza A, Jensen MG, Ritz C, Bitz C, Hels O
Journal: The British Journal of Nutrition
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23021155/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 4 review).

Five-way single-blind placebo-controlled randomized crossover trial in 22 healthy normal-weight young men (mean age 24.9 +/- 4.6 y) consuming a brunch meal with 8.3 g horseradish, 1.3 g black pepper, 20 g ginger, 21 g mustard, or spice-free placebo. No significant effects on 4-h diet-induced thermogenesis, subjective appetite (p>0.85), ad libitum energy intake (p=0.63), energy balance (p=0.67), or overall systolic blood pressure (p=0.15). Horseradish acutely decreased heart rate (p=0.048) and increased supine resting diastolic blood pressure vs placebo (p=0.049; post hoc +1.4 mmHg vs ginger p=0.050 per local factual extract). Measurements occurred during supine rest without a timed standing orthostatic challenge or lightheadedness severity rating.
