Title: Oral piperine and instrumental swallowing in people with dysphagia
Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00535-013-0920-0
Original article copy: https://magistralbr.caldic.com/storage/product-files/1756815571.pdf
PMID: 24326980
DOI: 10.1007/s00535-013-0920-0
Publication date: 2013-12-11
Date basis: Publisher's first online date, before the 2014 issue.
Review basis: Complete publisher-formatted article copy, identities and results cross-checked with the Springer record and the investigators' university record. Methods, Tables 1 and figures inspected.
Source type: Controlled acute clinical comparison in 40 people with oropharyngeal dysphagia.

The researchers randomized participants to one of two isolated piperine concentrations. Everyone first received the unfortified nectar control, followed by the piperine test sequence. This was a randomized comparison of concentration groups with a fixed-order control sequence within each patient. Four people reported under neurodegenerative disease in Table 1 were all in the lower-concentration group. The paper does not report a separate Parkinson comparison. EAT-10 and SSQ characterize participants at baseline rather than a symptom change after repeated exposure.

For the penetration–aspiration scale, the 150 micromolar group had 2.10 to 1.95, p=.521. The 1 millimolar group had 3.25 to 1.85, p=.003. Laryngeal vestibule closure time shortened at both concentrations. Oral and pharyngeal residue outcomes were inconclusive. One abdominal pain event was recorded at the higher concentration and judged unrelated to the study product. That event alone provides no pain-severity contrast.

The 2018 Cochrane stroke review excludes this study from its synthesis on study and outcome eligibility grounds. The standardized nectar-thick stimulus and pure compound do not establish the effect of cracked pepper particles during eating. No matching culinary concentration, texture, or repeat clinical comparison was reported. This separates the documented favorable instrumental signal from the still-open frozen Parkinson claim 0124.
