Title: Acute consumption of walnuts and walnut components differentially affect postprandial lipemia, endothelial function, oxidative stress, and cholesterol efflux in humans with mild hypercholesterolemia
PMID: 23616506
PMCID: PMC3652880
DOI: 10.3945/jn.112.170993
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3652880/
First published: 2013-04-24 electronic publication in PubMed and PMC; June 2013 issue.
Coverage: Factual original Methods/Results extraction; registration and later posted results reviewed separately.
Reviewed: 2026-11-17

Berryman and colleagues randomized 20 adults to four crossover conditions; 15 completed. Meals supplied 51 g walnut oil, 85 g whole walnut, 5.6 g skins or 34 g defatted nutmeat. Methods explicitly describe high-purity n-hexane Soxhlet extraction from skinless walnuts, not verified cold pressing.

The paper reports vascular and lipid comparisons, including maintained Framingham reactive hyperemia with oil relative to skin/whole walnut and increased post-meal triglycerides under oil/whole walnut. Cholesterol efflux was evaluated only after whole walnuts.

NCT00938340's later posted RHI comparisons include an oil-versus-whole p=.011, above its stated Bonferroni threshold .0083, while oil-versus-skin p=.002 passes. Preserve metric and analysis distinctions. There is no no-walnut arm or measured Parkinson symptom, cognition or pain result. This is one registered trial family.
