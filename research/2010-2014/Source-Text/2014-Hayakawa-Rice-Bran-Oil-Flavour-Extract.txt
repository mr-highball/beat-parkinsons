Hayakawa, Kazami, Jimbo and Urata. Sensory Profiling of the Flavour of Fresh Rice Bran Oil. Journal of Cookery Science of Japan 47(6), 333–340, 2014. J-STAGE availability January 9, 2015.
Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/cookeryscience/47/6/47_333/_article/-char/en
Access and coverage: Publisher metadata and English author abstract. Oil-specific clinical sensory thresholds and a study-lot refining chain were not verified.
Reviewed: 2026-11-14 local date (America/New_York).

Eight trained panellists tracked the intensity of fresh rice bran, canola, corn and palm olein oils during eating, using soybean oil as the maximum reference. The authors characterize the rice bran oil as having moderate overall flavour intensity that quickly subsided. Nine trained assessors described a sweet flavour and rounded aroma. Rating the oil stimulus is distinct from measuring a change in olfactory impairment. The recorded preparation was unheated, and fresh does not specify crude, refined or cold-pressed grade.
