2013 | Figueroa et al. | Soybean-oil versus menhaden-oil feed and stepping in spinal cord injury

Source: full primary article, DOI 10.1089/neu.2012.2718. Publisher and NLM list different early 2013 online dates, both in the same date bucket.

The randomized experiments compared energy-matched Bio-Serv feed containing approximately 6% dry-weight soybean oil or menhaden oil. Four groups crossed each diet with thoracic contusion or sham. The oil grade is unspecified.

The blinded 0–21 Basso–Beattie–Bresnahan score combines joint movement, placement, step support, coordination and posture. At one week the authors report transformed mean scores of 1.53 (SEM 0.38) and 5.47 (SEM 0.62) for injured soybean-oil and menhaden-oil groups, respectively (p<.0001). At eight weeks the means were 8.20 and 11.88 (reported post hoc p<.05). The treatment contrast is a dietary fat substitution during spinal cord injury, with no measured length per step or stride.
