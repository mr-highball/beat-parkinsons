Title: Dopamine Agonist Withdrawal Syndrome in Parkinson Disease
PMID: 20065130
DOI: 10.1001/archneurol.2009.294
Source: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/799048
First published: 2010-01 issue, Archives of Neurology 67(1):58–63. The 2009 string in the DOI differs from the issue year.
Coverage: Factual extract from the publisher's original Methods, Results, Table 2 and discussion.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

Rabinak and Nirenberg reviewed the records of a convenience sample of 93 nondemented Parkinson outpatients enrolled in a longitudinal motor and nonmotor study. Forty received a dopamine agonist. Of the 26 who later had a dopamine agonist tapered, five developed withdrawal syndrome (19% in this tapered subgroup). The authors compared those five with 21 who tapered without the syndrome.

Reported withdrawal symptoms included depression along with anxiety, fatigue, pain and orthostatic hypotension. The original reports a temporal relationship between onset and dosage reduction. Beck Depression Inventory II was part of the cohort's general neuropsychiatric assessment. The study outcome was a withdrawal syndrome, and the small selected cohort limits prevalence or causal generalization. Depression appearing in this setting provides a separate clinical context for interpreting symptom change in Parkinson disease.
