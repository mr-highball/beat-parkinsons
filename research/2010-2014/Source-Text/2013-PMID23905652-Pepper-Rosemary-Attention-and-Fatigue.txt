Title: Short-term effects of black pepper and rosemary on sustained attention and energy and fatigue in young adults with low energy

Forty young adults with low energy completed a randomized blinded crossover of black pepper fruit, a rosemary preparation, and rice-flour placebo capsules. The study used nose clips to minimize odors. The pepper arm did not specify a cracked culinary preparation, and a Parkinson group was not enrolled.

Table 2 shows that primary-task mean reaction time went from 587.3 to 613.3 ms in the pepper condition and from 607.9 to 592.5 ms in the placebo condition by 90 minutes. Baseline means differed. The cognitive comparisons across condition and time were not statistically significant, with or without the motivation covariate. Individual descriptive contrasts do not establish a consistent pepper effect on sustained attention. This is the same study already represented in claim 0357, now linked directly by PMID and DOI.

The mood comparisons were also inconclusive overall. The reported rosemary effect sizes for mental fatigue at 60 minutes and for one false-alarm comparison are isolated, unadjusted signals. The study did not measure insomnia or sleep, and the fatigue and attention observations do not supply an insomnia effect for claim 0282.
