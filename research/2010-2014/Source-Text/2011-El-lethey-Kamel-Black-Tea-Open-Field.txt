Brewed black tea and open field crossings in healthy and fluoride exposed rats
Source: https://www.jofamericanscience.org/journals/am-sci/am0704/38_5210m0704_243_254.pdf
Coverage: Original publisher PDF, Methods and Table 2

El-lethey and Kamel allocated 20 male rats per group to water, fluoride, a brewed black-tea solution, or both for 12 weeks. Table 2 gives open-field behavior for ten per group. Horizontal crossings were 50.3 ± 3.93 with water and 46.9 ± 4.44 with tea alone, without a clear difference. The combined arm preserved some coordination measures after fluoride exposure. The source gives no individual tea consumption, Parkinson model or test of clinical movement slowness. The allocated and assessed denominators differ.
