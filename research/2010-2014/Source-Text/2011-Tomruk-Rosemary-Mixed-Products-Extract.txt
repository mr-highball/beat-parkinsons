Tomruk NB, Delice MA, Ozturk T, Alkan F, Alpay N. Turkish psychiatry congress abstract PB 63, 2011: https://psikiyatri.org.tr/uploadFiles/eventFile/15112011105928-UPK-Bildiri-Ozetleri-Kitabi.pdf . A 41-year-old woman without reported personal or family psychiatric history developed acute psychotic symptoms within days of taking a weight-loss dietary tablet labelled as a rosemary herbal mixture. The listed ingredients included Rosmarinus officinalis, Calluna vulgaris and Panax ginseng among others. Visual and auditory hallucinations, somatic, persecutory and referential delusions, and depersonalization were reported; symptoms rapidly receded with antipsychotic treatment. The abstract does not specify dose, plant part, fresh-leaf status, preexisting medications, Parkinson disease, frequency or individual causal agent. No controlled rechallenge or isolated rosemary comparison is described.

Tomruk et al., 4th World Congress on Women's Mental Health, Madrid, 16–19 March 2011, poster 2, Archives of Women's Mental Health supplement 14:S147, DOI 10.1007/s00737-011-0238-y, https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00737-011-0238-y.pdf . Same author list and institution describe two women in their 40s with acute psychosis and vivid hallucinations while taking complex weight-loss products listing rosemary; modality, individual doses and other ingredients are not stated in this abstract. Both received antipsychotic treatment and improved. The 41-year-old Turkish abstract may describe one of these two, but identity is unproven; do not count three independent individuals. Turkish and English descriptions are conference abstracts, not controlled trials. The 2007 Saatcioglu et al. case they cite is a referenced prior report, not automatically the same individual.
