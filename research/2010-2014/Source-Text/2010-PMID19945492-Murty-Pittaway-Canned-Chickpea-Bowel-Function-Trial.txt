Title: Chickpea supplementation in an Australian diet affects food choice, satiety and bowel health.
PMID: 19945492
DOI: 10.1016/j.appet.2009.11.012
First published: 2010
Authors: Murty CM, Pittaway JK, Ball MJ
Journal: Appetite
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19945492/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Ordered crossover dietary intervention (Murty, Pittaway & Ball, 2010, Appetite; companion cohort to Pittaway et al. 2008 PMID:18502235, n=45 enrolled / 42 completed healthy Australian adults) comparing 12 weeks of ad libitum daily canned, drained chickpeas (target 104 g/day, minimum 728 g/week) against 4 weeks of habitual diet. Perceived satiation and perceived bowel function improved during the chickpea phase, including greater perceived frequency of defecation, ease of defecation, and softer stool consistency. Limits: non-randomized ordered crossover design, healthy non-Parkinson adults, self-reported bowel perception rather than validated clinical constipation scales.
