Title: Propofol administration is safe in adult eosinophilic esophagitis patients sensitized to egg, soy, or peanut
PMID: 24447028
DOI: 10.1111/all.12360
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24447028/
Original reprint: https://serviciodigestivotomelloso.es/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/66.pdf
First published: 2014-01-21 online per Crossref. Allergy 69(3):388–394, March 2014.
Archive scope: Factual extract from the original author report and its matching 2013 poster.
Reviewed: 2026-11-19

Molina-Infante and colleagues examined 60 adults with eosinophilic esophagitis undergoing a total of 404 upper endoscopies with propofol. The full report describes a retrospective observational design and the same denominators as poster P1018 at UEG Week 2013. Egg, soy, peanut and other food sensitization testing is a cohort characteristic. Participants did not receive randomized food challenges as part of the sedation comparison.

The original article describes the fat emulsion as including egg lecithin and soybean oil. The report found no confirmed allergic event attributed to the drug. It describes transient bronchospasm following intubation in one participant with asthma and no positive follow-up reaction to the tested formulations. This is a procedural intravenous exposure with an allergy endpoint. It cannot establish a neurological or gastrointestinal effect of a standalone food in Parkinson disease.

Study-family rule: Count the 2013 poster and 2014 full report as one cohort. Preserve the full article's retrospective design label if the poster is consulted separately.
