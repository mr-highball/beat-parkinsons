Title: In-Patient Treatment of Fibromyalgia: A Controlled Nonrandomized Comparison of Conventional Medicine versus Integrative Medicine including Fasting Therapy
Source: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2013/908610
PMID: 23431352
PMCID: PMC3566607
DOI: 10.1155/2013/908610
First published: 2013-01-23
Authors: Andreas Michalsen, Chenying Li, Katharina Kaiser, Rainer Lüdtke, Larissa Meier, Rainer Stange, Christian Kessler
Coverage: Original publisher Methods, Results, and Table 2 verified.

Of 56 patients screened, 48 fibromyalgia inpatients volunteered. The nonrandomized comparison assigned groups by hospital department. The integrative medicine group included 28 and rheumatology 20. All 48 contributed baseline and week-two data. At week 12 there were 25 and 17, respectively. The integrative program included a patient-selected prefasting option of unspecified fruit, rice, or potatoes, followed by fasting and a phased reintroduction of food. The Methods names an apple on refeeding. The study reports no species or participant counts for the prefasting fruit option.

The adjusted between-group difference in change on the 100 mm pain scale was −17.5 mm at two weeks (95% CI −28.8 to −6.1) and −13.0 mm at week 12 (95% CI −28.0 to 2.1). The comparison concerns multi-component care in fibromyalgia and cannot isolate a fruit-specific effect. No Parkinson cohort or dried-date intake and pain data are provided.
