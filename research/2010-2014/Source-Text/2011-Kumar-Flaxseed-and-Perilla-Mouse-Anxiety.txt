Title: Evaluation and comparison of anxiolytic effect of flaxseed oil and perilla oil in mice
Source: https://www.europub.co.uk/articles/155826
Publication date: 2011
Date basis: International Research Journal of Pharmacy volume 2, issue 4, pp.161–164, corroborated by the Patil original bibliography.
Coverage: Indexed author abstract and citation. Full methods and tables remain unavailable.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26

The authors report that two levels of orally administered flaxseed oil increased male mice's entries and time in the light compartment of a light–dark test and in the open arms of an elevated plus maze compared with untreated controls. Perilla oil and diazepam were separate comparisons. They qualitatively describe a stronger or lower-dose response to perilla. They provide no numeric group means, confidence intervals, independently verifiable locomotor endpoint or documented pressing method at abstract depth.

The direction concerns anxiety-related behavior in mice. The untreated comparison, missing processing and full group data and lack of Parkinson outcomes keep it contextual for the cold-pressed-oil claim.
