Title: Differential effects of dopamine receptor D1-type and D2-type antagonists and phase of the estrous cycle on social learning of food preferences, feeding, and social interactions in mice
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3138658/
PMID: 21525863
PMCID: PMC3138658
DOI: 10.1038/npp.2011.50
Publication date: 2011-04-27
Retrieved (UTC): 2026-11-19
Text coverage: NLM original article HTML, full methods, results and figures. Factual extract.

This mouse experiment used familiarization, social encounters and learning about a flavored diet to compare the effects of dopamine receptor antagonists and estrous state on food preferences. Cinnamon and cocoa were diet flavors. The investigators observed stretched approaches and other behaviors during social interactions before the preference test. Food preference for the cinnamon-flavored diet is a separate measured outcome. The study does not isolate a ground-cinnamon effect on Parkinson anxiety.
