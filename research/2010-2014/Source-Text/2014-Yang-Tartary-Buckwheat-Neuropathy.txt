Tartary buckwheat products and peripheral neuropathy in a diabetes case series
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25300770/
PMID: 25300770
DOI: 10.1016/j.jns.2014.09.037
Coverage: factual extract from primary abstract

Fei Yang, Sheng-Yuan Yu, Yan Wang, Rong-Fei Wang, and Feng Jing. Journal of the Neurological Sciences, 347 (1–2), 155–158. Published online September 30, 2014. Issue December 15, 2014.
The report describes five men ages 40–66 treated for diabetes with Tartary buckwheat products for 1.5–4 months. All developed extremity numbness and weakness. Hoarseness was reported in four, dysphagia in one, and bilateral facial paralysis in one. Nerve-conduction findings showed greater motor than sensory involvement. Von Frey testing was abnormal in all. The authors use the term dyskinesia in their abstract within the presentation of polyneuropathy. Product form and dose, contaminant evaluation, dechallenge details and operational definition of dyskinesia remain unavailable in the retrieved abstract. There is no comparison group or separately reported Parkinson or levodopa-induced dyskinesia outcome. Hoarseness is not a speech intelligibility assessment.
