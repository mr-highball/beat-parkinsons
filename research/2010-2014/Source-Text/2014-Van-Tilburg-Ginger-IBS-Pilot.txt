Title: Is ginger effective for the treatment of irritable bowel syndrome? A double blind randomized controlled pilot trial
PMID: 24559811
PMCID: PMC3958926
DOI: 10.1016/j.ctim.2013.12.015
Published: 2014-01-08 online.
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3958926/
Archive scope: Factual summary of the primary manuscript, Methods, Results and Table 1.

Van Tilburg, Palsson, Ringel and Whitehead assigned 45 adults with IBS to one gram or two grams of pharmaceutical-grade ginger daily or brown-sugar placebo for 28 days. The author manuscript reports IBS severity responders of 57.1%, 46.7% and 33.3% for placebo, 1 g and 2 g, respectively, with no significant group difference. Adequate relief was 53.3% for placebo and 53.3% for the two ginger groups pooled. Difficulty passing stool was one of several adverse events listed without an arm-specific count. The main endpoint is a composite IBS scale and the report supplies no constipation-specific treatment estimate. The study included no Parkinson cohort and used capsules.
