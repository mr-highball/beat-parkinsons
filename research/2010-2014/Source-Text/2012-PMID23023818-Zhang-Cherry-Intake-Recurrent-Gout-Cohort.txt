Title: Cherry consumption and decreased risk of recurrent gout attacks.
PMID: 23023818
PMCID: PMC3510330
DOI: 10.1002/art.34677
First published: 2012
Authors: Zhang Y, Neogi T, Chen C, Chaisson C, Hunter DJ, Choi HK
Journal: Arthritis and rheumatism
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3510330/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Prospective 1-year case-crossover cohort study (Zhang et al., 2012, Arthritis Rheum, PMC3510330) of 633 individuals with gout comparing 2-day pre-attack hazard periods against matched control periods via conditional logistic regression. Fresh cherry intake over 2 days was associated with 35% lower odds of recurrent gout attacks (multivariate OR 0.65, 95% CI 0.50-0.85), and cherry extract with OR 0.55 (95% CI 0.30-0.98); combining cherry intake with allopurinol yielded OR 0.25 (95% CI 0.15-0.42). Limits: observational self-reported exposure and flare diaries in gout (inflammatory crystal arthritis pain rather than Parkinson pain), fresh/extract forms rather than specifically dried fruit.
