Title: Medicinal plants and dementia therapy: herbal hopes for brain aging?
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6493900/
PMID: 22070157
PMCID: PMC6493900
DOI: 10.1111/j.1755-5949.2010.00202.x
Publication: 2010-10-18
Coverage: Factual extract. Official PMC HTML main article and tables.

Perry and Howes reviewed botanical remedies in dementia. The sage section discusses Salvia officinalis and S. lavandulifolia extracts or oils, immediate recall studies in healthy adults and Alzheimer trials. It mentions a placebo comparison in 25 people with mild cognitive impairment and Parkinson disease. Both groups improved from baseline and the authors report no significant cognitive difference between arms. Reference 51 describes that study as in preparation. The review supplies no separate apathy or insomnia result for sage. Its discussion of apathy refers to ginkgo, and clinical sleep examples concern other preparations, including lemon balm with valerian.

The saffron row in the botanical comparison table mentions stigma or constituent research and an Alzheimer disease comparison with donepezil on overall cognition. It does not give a distinct executive-function, depression or insomnia outcome after saffron in Parkinson disease. Ginger appears in a proposed discussion of gastrointestinal tolerability. The review contains no dried-date intervention for apathy.

The overview calls the comparator placebo. A later public grant report identifies vitamin E as the administered comparator and separately reports a working-memory outcome. The investigator names and design identify the same cohort.
