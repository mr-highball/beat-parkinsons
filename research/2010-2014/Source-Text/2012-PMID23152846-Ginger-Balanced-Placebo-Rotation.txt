Title: Effects of ginger and expectations on symptoms of nausea in a balanced placebo design
PMID: 23152846
PMCID: PMC3496739
DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0049031
Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0049031
Published: 2012-11-13, PLOS ONE 7, e49031.
Archive scope: Factual summary from the original author methods and Table 3.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Sixty-four healthy participants, with 32 women, were allocated in a balanced placebo design crossing Zintona powdered-ginger 1 g or starch with what participants were told before chair rotation. Before sex and experimenter were added to the analysis, the authors found no main effect or interaction for the reported maximum symptom rating, head movements and rotation tolerance outcomes. The principal 2-by-2 multivariate comparison reported p=.784. Gastric electrical analysis was limited by signal loss, and subgroup analyses had small cells. This is a useful counterpoint to circular vection in people preselected for motion sickness. It measures induced nausea and behavior under a different stimulus without a clinical bowel result.
