Source: Kabuto H, Yamanushi TT. Effects of zingerone [4-(4-hydroxy-3-methoxyphenyl)-2-butanone] and eugenol [2-methoxy-4-(2-propenyl)phenol] on the pathological progress in the 6-hydroxydopamine-induced Parkinson's disease mouse model.
Journal: Neurochemical Research (published 2011)
Identifiers: PMID:21769642 | DOI:10.1007/s11064-011-0548-5

License note: Factual summary recorded from official NCBI PubMed metadata and abstract.

Study design and findings (critical contrary/safety observation)
- Intracerebroventricular 6-OHDA Parkinson's disease mouse model treated post-lesion with daily oral zingerone (a ginger constituent) or eugenol (a clove constituent) alongside intraperitoneal L-DOPA for 4 weeks.
- Neither zingerone nor eugenol improved L-DOPA-induced abnormal behaviors.
- Contrary to earlier pretreatment neuroprotection reports, post-lesion administration of zingerone or eugenol enhanced the 6-OHDA-induced depletion of striatal dopamine and its metabolites; zingerone also lowered catalase activity and increased oxidized L-ascorbate in the striatum.
- The authors explicitly cautioned that timing relative to lesion onset reversed the effect in mice and warranted caution regarding post-onset ingestion of these isolated spice constituents.

Relevance and limits for Parkinson's food experiments
- Important contrary preclinical safety record for ginger/clove constituents (e.g., 0778 ground clove / bradykinesia and ginger claims) demonstrating timing-dependent divergence between pretreatment and post-lesion dosing.
- Limits: Isolated essential-oil constituents rather than culinary ginger or ground clove; mouse 6-OHDA + L-DOPA model; abstract-level depth (full text paywalled).
