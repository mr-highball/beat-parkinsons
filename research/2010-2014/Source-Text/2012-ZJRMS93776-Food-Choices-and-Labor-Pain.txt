Title: The effect of food intake during labor on pain and anxiety in pregnant women
Source: https://brieflands.com/journals/zjrms/articles/93776
Original article: https://brieflands.com/journals/zjrms/articles/93776.pdf
Publication date: 2012
Date basis: Zahedan Journal of Research in Medical Sciences 13(8), pp. 44–47 and the printed English citation. The download stamp and migrated publisher page dates are later.
Checked: 2026-11-24
Review depth: Original Persian Methods and Results, English abstract, visual verification of Tables 1–2.

The report describes a quasi-experimental food-access comparison with 175 low-risk women, 87 given access to dates, orange juice and water and 88 fasting under the usual hospital policy. At 6–7 cm cervical dilation the printed pain means are 4.8 versus 6.2 and in transition they are 7.0 versus 9.0. Both table p values are .0001. Early pain scores were similar. The paper also reports measures of anxiety and one-hour postpartum fatigue.

The analysis is by access to the listed choices, with no arm-specific result for a defined dried-date preparation or a date-only group. Food, fluids, expectation and fasting conditions differ simultaneously. A separate Gonabad publication has overlapping authors and 87 in its intervention analysis, 90 controls, and endpoints of labor progression and analgesic use. Participant overlap between the two cohorts cannot be established from the reports. Treat them as an unresolved lineage and avoid counting them as independent efficacy replications.
