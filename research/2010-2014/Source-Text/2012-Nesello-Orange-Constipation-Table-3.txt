Reported orange intake and Rome III constipation in older adults
Source: https://www.e-publicacoes.uerj.br/ceres/article/download/2125/2113/11181
Coverage: Original Portuguese Methods and Table 3; factual text

Nesello, Tonelli and Beltrame studied 139 older adults at an Itajai community center in Brazil. The publisher records April 8, 2012 and the issue is labeled 2011. Mean age was 73.55 years and 84.9% were women. In Table 3, 31 of 120 respondents who reported consuming the orange item (laranja) met Rome III constipation criteria (25.83%), versus 9 of 19 nonconsumers (47.37%). The table gives an unadjusted chi-square p value of .054. The qualitative food-frequency item does not identify variety, raw preparation, serving amount or timing. Constipation classification was binary, with no severity change estimate or Parkinson subgroup. The cross-sectional comparison leaves diet, medications, other confounding and reverse causation unresolved. [Publisher record and date](https://www.e-publicacoes.uerj.br/ceres/article/view/2125)
