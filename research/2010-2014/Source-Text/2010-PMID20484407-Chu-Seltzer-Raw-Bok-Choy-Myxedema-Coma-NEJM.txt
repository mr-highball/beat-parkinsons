Title: Myxedema coma induced by ingestion of raw bok choy.
PMID: 20484407
DOI: 10.1056/NEJMc0911005
First published: 2010
Authors: Chu M, Seltzer TF
Journal: The New England journal of medicine
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20484407/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Clinical case correspondence (Chu & Seltzer, 2010, N Engl J Med, DOI:10.1056/NEJMc0911005) reporting an 88-year-old woman without prior thyroid disease who consumed approximately 1.0 to 1.5 kg/day of RAW bok choy daily for several months (hoping to control diabetes) and presented to the emergency department with 3 days of lethargy, inability to walk, and inability to swallow due to severe hypothyroidism / myxedema coma. Mechanistically, active myrosinase in massive raw crucifer intake hydrolyzes glucosinolates into goitrogenic thiocyanates/oxazolidines inhibiting thyroid iodine uptake, whereas cooking or steaming inactivates myrosinase. Extreme raw-exposure endocrine safety case only—not evidence for ordinary steamed or cooked bok choy affecting Parkinson's symptoms.
