Title: In vivo anti-inflammatory properties of aerial parts of Nasturtium officinale.
PMID: 24160746
DOI: 10.3109/13880209.2013.821138
First published: 2014
Authors: Sadeghi H, Mostafazadeh M, Sadeghi H, Naderian M, Barmak MJ, Talebianpoor MS
Journal: Pharmaceutical biology
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24160746/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 7 review).

Primary Abstract:
Nasturtium officinale R. Br. (watercress) has long been used in Iranian folk medicine to treat hypertension, hyperglycemia, and renal colic. Moreover, anticancer, antioxidant, and hepatoprotective properties of N. officinale have been reported. In this study, anti-inflammatory activity of the hydro-alcoholic extract from aerial parts of N. officinale was investigated. Oral administration of the hydro-alcoholic extract of N. officinale (250, 500 and 750 mg kg(-1)) was investigated on two well-characterized animal models of inflammation, including carrageenan- or formalin-induced paw edema in rats. Then, the topical anti-inflammatory effect of N. officinale (2 and 5 mg/ear) was studied on 12-O-tetradecanoylphorbol-13-acetate (TPA)-induced mouse ear edema. Finally, biopsy of the paw or ear was performed for pathological evaluation. Acute toxicity tests of N. officinale in rats established an oral LD50 of >5 g kg(-1). The extract of watercress (250, 500 and 750 mg kg(-1)) significantly inhibited carrageenan-induced paw edema 1, 2, 3 and 4 h after carrageenan challenge (p < 0.001). The extract (500 mg kg(-1)) also showed considerable activity against formalin-evoked paw edema over a period of 24 h (p < 0.001). Furthermore, topical application of N. officinale (5 mg/ear) reduced TPA-induced ear edema (p < 0.05). Histopathologically, the extract decreased swelling and the tissue damage induced by carrageenan or TPA. Our findings indicate potent anti-inflammatory activity of N. officinale in systemic and topical application and propose its potential as an anti-inflammatory agent for treatment of inflammatory conditions.

Verified Round 7 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Preclinical rat/mouse acute inflammation study (Pharm Biol 2014) evaluating oral hydroalcoholic extract of aerial Nasturtium officinale (250-750 mg/kg; oral LD50 >5 g/kg) on carrageenan- and formalin-induced hind-paw edema and topical extract on TPA mouse ear edema. Significantly inhibited inflammatory paw swelling (p<0.001) and histological leukocyte infiltration. Crucially, this study measured inflammatory edema volume/thickness rather than nociceptive pain behavior (no paw-licking/flinching, hot-plate, or writhing assay), making outcome_fit=remote/unusable for PD pain severity.
