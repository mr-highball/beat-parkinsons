Diane L. Elliot, Kerry S. Kuehl, Kim Dupree Jones and Kristen Dulacki. Original complete study of tart cherry juice during an eccentric exercise protocol in women with fibromyalgia. The available article shows Integrative Medicine 9(5), October/November 2010, pp. 40–44. A separately indexed issue citation gives 9(6), December 2010/January 2011, pp. 24–29. Exact first online date and issue correspondence remain unresolved.
Fifteen participants enrolled and fourteen were analyzed in a randomized and blinded beverage crossover at Oregon Health & Science University. Each 10-day period used two 10.5-ounce bottles daily of diluted, flavored Montmorency tart cherry concentrate or taste- and calorie-matched punch. The periods were separated by one week. The exercise protocol used a different arm in each period. One participant discontinued during tart cherry juice with gastrointestinal upset.
Pressure pain threshold and tolerance were inconclusive. Following exercise, the paper reports an overall VAS pain contrast of p = .10 and a day-one strength contrast of p = .06, with no overall strength interaction. Figure 4 analyzes five apparent responders selected from the study results. A Fibromyalgia Impact Questionnaire score characterizes participants at enrollment. Follow-up outcomes were strength, tenderness and pain. The report provides no treatment comparison for clinical fatigue. A 2009 conference abstract with the same authors, sample size and comparison may be an earlier version of this cohort.
