Primary study factual extract
Bortolotti M, Porta S. Effect of red pepper on symptoms of irritable bowel syndrome. Dig Dis Sci. 2011;56(11):3288–3295. First published May 15, 2011. PMID21573941. DOI 10.1007/s10620-011-1740-9.
Sources https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10620-011-1740-9 and the original paper at https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51128411_Effect_of_Red_Pepper_on_Symptoms_of_Irritable_Bowel_Syndrome_Preliminary_Study.
Fifty patients were allocated to ground whole dried Capsicum annuum fruit and seeds in colon-release pills (23) or appearance-matched placebo (27) for six weeks. Four pills daily each supplied 150 mg pepper and 0.50 mg capsaicin. Placebo composition was unspecified in the accessed paper. Six active participants discontinued for pronounced onset abdominal pain. Two placebo recipients discontinued for other reasons. Eight patients reduced from four pills to two because of pain, later described as the red-pepper subgroup. In the active arm, mean daily pain diary scores were lower versus baseline in weeks five and six. The Results and Discussion report no significant between-group pain score difference. The distinct global subjective effectiveness rating favored active treatment. The abstract conclusion claims superior pain control, beyond the between-group pain result.
Earlier conference report S1340 DOI 10.1016/S0016-5085(10)61056-6 uses the same 23/27 allocation, 150 mg pills, six-week schedule and withdrawal counts. The conference abstract reported no significant between-group diary symptom contrast. The conference and journal versions are counted once.
