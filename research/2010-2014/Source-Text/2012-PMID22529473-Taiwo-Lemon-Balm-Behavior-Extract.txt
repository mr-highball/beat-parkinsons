Title: Anxiolytic and antidepressant-like effects of Melissa officinalis (lemon balm) extract in rats: Influence of administration and gender
PMID: 22529473
PMCID: PMC3326910
DOI: 10.4103/0253-7613.93846
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3326910/
First published: March–April 2012 issue, Indian Journal of Pharmacology 44(2):189–192. No earlier online date was established. The index uses March 1 for sorting.
Coverage: Original body, Methods, Results and Figures 1–4.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

Taiwo and colleagues compared oral ethanol extract of dried lemon balm leaves with vehicle in male and female Wistar rats. Separate single-dose and 10-day schedules were tested. Fluoxetine was the positive comparator in the forced-swim experiment. Diazepam was used for the anxiety and locomotion comparisons. Figure 3 lists ten animals per group.

After one dose the forced-swim analysis found no significant treatment effect. After 10 days the higher two extract groups in males and all three extract groups in females showed shorter immobility than vehicle. Fluoxetine produced a larger reduction than the extract. The sex-by-treatment interaction for the forced-swim outcome was not significant (p=.94). Separate within-sex patterns do not establish an interaction. The paper reports no clear alteration in open-field locomotion.

These are isolated animal behavior measures after an ethanolic extract. The results preserve both the acute null and the repeated-dose findings. Neither schedule provides a depression score or a brewed exposure in people with Parkinson disease.
