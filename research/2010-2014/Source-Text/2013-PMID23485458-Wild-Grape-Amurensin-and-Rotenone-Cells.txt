Title: Wild-grape amurensin G in a rotenone cellular model
Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0006291X13003136
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23485458/
PMID: 23485458
DOI: 10.1016/j.bbrc.2013.02.053
Publication date: 2013-02-26
Date basis: NLM electronic publication date, before the March 29 journal issue.
Review basis: Original author abstract and available publisher summary. Full methods and quantitative figures could not be verified.
Source type: Experimental cell study.

Ryu and colleagues identified a compound derived from Vitis amurensis in an autophagy reporter screen. The author summary describes increased LC3-associated signals in the reporter system and reduced rotenone-associated toxicity in SH-SY5Y cells. Beclin1 suppression removed the observed protection in that experimental setting. The 2018 fermented grape-skin fly study cites this separately studied compound.

The original did not compare frozen whole grape, measure muscle rigidity, or establish that the isolated wild-grape constituent is present at an equivalent level in either the fly preparation or an edible serving. It supplies cellular context for Round 1 claim 0881.
