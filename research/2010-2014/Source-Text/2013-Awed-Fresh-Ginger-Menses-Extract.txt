The Use of Fresh Ginger Herbs As A Home Remedy To Relieve Primary Dysmenorrhea
Howida Awed, Tawheda El-saidy and Tahany Amro
Journal of Research in Nursing and Midwifery 2(8), 104–113. December 2013. DOI 10.14303/JRNM.2013.068.
Original full article: https://www.interesjournals.org/articles/the-use-of-fresh-ginger-herbs-as-a-home-remedy-to-relieve-primary-dysmenorrhea.pdf
Coverage: sampled Methods, pain Tables 5–6, Figure 3 and abstract. The issue prints December 2013 without a specific publication day.

The quasi-experimental convenience sample comprised 120 students, 60 in each arm. The intervention used a tablespoon of fresh ginger in 200 mL of warm water, about three cups daily beginning three days before menstruation through its second day, across two cycles. The paper does not specify grating or report randomized allocation. The control received no comparable beverage.

Table 5 reports baseline cycle-day-two pain of 3.9 (SD 2.7) for ginger and 3.7 (SD 2.5) for control. Table 6 gives an incompatible ginger baseline of 2.66 (SD 1.7) and values of 2.2 (SD 1.3) and 1.1 (SD 1.2) after the two cycles. The table reports p < 0.001 for the within-ginger change. Follow-up results sufficient for a between-arm comparison are absent. Figure 3 shows severe or very severe pain of 28.3% at baseline and 51.6% after the second cycle, inconsistent with the favorable report interpretation. These issues prevent a reliable causal or Parkinson symptom estimate.

A 2015 report attributed to Ahmed Webb, Omar Tantawy and Muhammad El-Tayeb repeats the abstract, methods and exact values in Tables 3, 5 and 6. The study relationship is unresolved. The reports cannot be counted as independent evidence. 2015 publisher record: https://www.internationalscholarsjournals.org/articles/4950748230092015
