Original factual extract from Methods and Tables 1–3

Air-dried locally obtained black tea material was refluxed with ethanol for 12 hours. The authors evaporated the solvent at controlled temperature and reduced pressure and stored the solid extract. Forty-nine Swiss albino mice were allocated to seven groups. The experimental extract groups received 4 or 8 mg/kg orally once daily prior to haloperidol over 12 days. Test days were 4, 8 and 12. Catalepsy was duration of externally imposed posture measured by the bar test. Locomotor count was beam interruptions in the 10 minutes after acclimation in a photoactometer.

Day 12, means and SEM, n = 7 per arm as tabulated
Haloperidol alone: bar catalepsy duration 237.14 ± 8.29 s, locomotor count 107.83 ± 7.48.
4 mg/kg extract with haloperidol: bar duration 76.4 ± 2.42 s, locomotor count 191.42 ± 8.07.
8 mg/kg extract with haloperidol: bar duration 64.28 ± 3.15 s, locomotor count 247.28 ± 11.29.
Both tables annotate p < .001 for the dose arms versus haloperidol alone.

Methods state each mouse was used once, which is inconsistent as written with seven groups of seven and the same n at all three test days. No walking velocity or tremor was measured. This ethanol extract and pharmacologically induced outcome model differ from brewed black tea and clinical Parkinson outcomes.
