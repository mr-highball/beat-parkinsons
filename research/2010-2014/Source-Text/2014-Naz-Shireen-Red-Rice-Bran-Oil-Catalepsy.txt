Original comparison
Naz and Shireen compared oral red rice bran oil with an oral saline comparison in rats receiving haloperidol, alongside parallel comparisons in rats receiving water rather than haloperidol. The source describes a research-supplied red rice bran oil. It does not document commercial refining or equivalent composition. It uses latency to move in an inclined-surface assay to quantify catalepsy. Figure 4.4 and the Results describe lower catalepsy in the haloperidol plus oil group than in the haloperidol plus saline group. The results narrative describes a difference during weeks 2–5. The figure's visible marks and the narrative's time-course wording do not align cleanly enough to assign an exact week-by-week significance claim. The reported haloperidol-by-oil term was nonsignificant when averaged across time, while the three-way time-dependent interaction was reported significant. The figure shows a large numerical separation in the haloperidol condition. The article uses n=12 in its figure conventions without clear per-arm denominators for this comparison.

Contrary observation
In the water rather than haloperidol comparison, the Results report greater catalepsy with oil at some weeks, and the graph shows a small numerical separation in that direction. The Discussion describes the oil-only group as not developing catalepsy, so the interpretation of this smaller increase is internally inconsistent. The publication also describes vacuous chewing and locomotor outcomes. These are different endpoints from clinical dystonia or Parkinson rigidity.

Relevance to Round 1
Claim 0476 tests refined rice bran oil and muscle rigidity severity in people with Parkinson disease. The oral comparison of red rice bran oil under a drug-induced motor impairment provides a weak indirect easing signal. The proxy, model, unverified refining, time-course reporting and contrasting water-group direction limit transfer. A human Parkinson comparison testing commercial refined oil against an oil-free or active oil control with a separate rigidity measure remains open. Assess the 2016 review (PMID 27540314) and this original as one study family.
Claims 0426 and 0801 concern dystonia and constipation. Neither endpoint was separately assessed in this experiment.
