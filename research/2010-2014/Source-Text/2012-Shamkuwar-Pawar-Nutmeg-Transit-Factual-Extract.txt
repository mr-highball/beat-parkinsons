Shamkuwar and Pawar, Methods and Table 3

Original source https://www.scholarsresearchlibrary.com/articles/significance-of-nutmeg-in-diarrhoea.pdf

The 2012 report gives mean charcoal travel of 85.53% of small-intestinal length in water controls and 67.85%, 58.46% and 51.35% across ascending doses of aqueous nutmeg seed extract, with six mice per group. The Methods for charcoal describe fasting and administration of charcoal and treatment without naming castor oil. The abstract describes the transit experiment as castor-oil induced. The challenge status therefore remains uncertain. Small-intestinal marker travel, ground-spice consumption and clinical constipation are distinct outcomes and exposures.
