Title: On the Fatigue-Reducing Effects of Green Tea
Publication: Watanabe, Kimura and Imanishi, Japanese Journal of Complementary and Alternative Medicine, 2013;10(1):9–16
Publication year: 2013
DOI: 10.1625/jcam.10.9
Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jcam/10/1/10_9/_article/-char/en
Full source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jcam/10/1/10_9/_pdf
Companion meeting abstract: https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0033-1352372
Coverage: Factual extract of Japanese Methods and Table 2

Five adults completed both conditions in randomized order. They underwent a 120-minute typing task and drank 30 mL of green tea infusion or the same volume of temperature-matched distilled water. Immediately after the task and 30 minutes after the drink, mean total subjective fatigue changed from 20.4 to 10.8 with green tea and from 18.0 to 15.2 with water. Table 2 gives p=.019 for the within-tea comparison. The beverage-by-time interaction did not reach significance. The five-person comparison used acutely induced fatigue outside Parkinson disease. The matching 2013 meeting abstract used the same task, crossover schedule, endpoints and measurement timing and is kept in this source family.
