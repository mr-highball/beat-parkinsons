Gastric and nutrition comparisons

- P1013 used a carbon-13 labeled egg breath test to measure gastric-emptying time during esomeprazole treatment in erosive reflux. The egg is a test meal rather than the assigned treatment. P1121 studied soy milk and cow milk in 16 people with unexplained dyspepsia. Intragastric administration resulted in a shorter reported half-emptying time for soy milk (82.4 ± 5.9 versus 139.9 ± 13.3 minutes, p=.026). The abstract reported no symptom-score or accommodation difference. Soy milk is chemically and nutritionally distinct from pure soybean oil, and the study did not test swallowing in Parkinson disease.
- P1323 measured pancreatic duct diameter before and after an orange–lemon drink in 24 patients or controls. The mean went from 2.092 to 3.108 mm after the drink (p<.001), with no single-fruit arm or untreated repeat imaging comparison. The endpoint was visibility during endosonography, not constipation or falls. P1611 used orange juice in a pH device comparison.
- P1439 reports dietary associations, including coffee, in inflammatory bowel disease. P1595 relates reported food intake to gastroesophageal reflux. Poultry, eggs and coffee appear in comparisons across clinical populations. These observational designs did not test whether the foods change fatigue, falls or Parkinson symptoms.
- P1473 reports a retrospective pediatric series with orofacial granulomatosis, with or without Crohn disease. Lip swelling, aphthous ulcers and cheilitis improved on a joint elemental regimen, followed by a diet excluding cinnamon and benzoates. The abstract cannot isolate cinnamon from the joint diet or other treatments.
- P1667 discusses gluten-related prolamines in barley and rye in a microscopic colitis and celiac disease context. No isolated barley test is reported.

Food-derived inputs, sensitization and other uses

- P1018 reports 404 endoscopies in 60 adults with eosinophilic esophagitis. The study tested safety of propofol sedation, with food sensitization measured separately (egg 41%, chicken 23%, lentils 64% in the poster). It describes egg lecithin and soybean oil as intravenous excipients. The 2014 article reports the same series and classifies the design as retrospective. See 2014-PMID24447028-Propofol-Food-Sensitization-Extract.txt.
- P760 used chicken tenderloin and liver to test a needle biopsy technique. P384 applied grape seed extract to colorectal cancer cells. P593 applied resveratrol in an oral-tissue substrate. P1106 tested extra-virgin olive oil phenols in activated macrophages, while P1678 evaluated mixed parenteral lipids in vitro. These are separate experiments and cannot determine a dietary exposure effect on an unrelated member's endpoint.

Parkinson disease is explicit in P241, which describes gastrointestinal procedure or device events during advanced Parkinson treatment with levodopa–carbidopa intestinal gel. It does not link those events to a food studied elsewhere in the supplement. No event, endpoint or denominator from one poster is transferred to a different member.
