Park and colleagues tested orally administered isolated 6-shogaol in a
mouse model of dopaminergic injury. They reported shorter turning and
descent times in a pole test compared with model controls. With normal
controls normalized to 100%, the model and compound-plus-model groups
had turning times of 164.44% and 108.02%, respectively, and descent
times of 127.86% and 105.01%. The report describes both comparisons
with model controls as statistically significant.

The methods, result text and legend differ in details of timing or
test cutoff. The measured turning and descent times concern movement
speed in the model. There was no grated culinary ginger arm, separate
passive rigidity outcome, or clinical Parkinson comparison. The source
provides mechanistic context for Round 1 claim 0626, with no supported
direction on its exact exposure and endpoint.
