Title: Maize porridge enriched with a micronutrient powder containing low-dose iron as NaFeEDTA but not amaranth grain flour reduces anemia and iron deficiency in Kenyan preschool children.
PMID: 22810982
DOI: 10.3945/jn.112.157578
Registry ID: NCT01224535
First published: 2012
Authors: Macharia-Mutie CW, Moretti D, Van den Briel N, Omusundi AM, Mwangi AM, Kok FJ, et al.
Journal: The Journal of nutrition
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22810982/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Sixteen-week randomized controlled trial (Macharia-Mutie et al., 2012, J Nutr, NCT01224535) in 279 Kenyan preschool children (12-59 months) comparing unrefined maize porridge control, 70% amaranth grain / 30% maize porridge (23 mg iron/meal, phytate:iron molar ratio 3:1), and maize porridge plus low-dose NaFeEDTA micronutrient powder (MNP). While MNP reduced anemia (-46%) and IDA (-75%), amaranth grain porridge produced no significant change in hemoglobin or iron status despite high iron intake, attributed to high phytic acid. Limits: pediatric hematologic trial; no fatigue, neurological, or GI symptom scales.
