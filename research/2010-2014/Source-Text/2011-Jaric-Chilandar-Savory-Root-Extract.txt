Jarić S et al., Phytotherapy in medieval Serbian medicine according to the pharmacological manuscripts of the Chilandar Medical Codex (15–16th centuries), J Ethnopharmacol 137:601–619, DOI 10.1016/j.jep.2011.06.016, PMID 21708242; first electronic publication 17 June 2011. Author-deposited full article Table 1, printed p.613: row 121 assigns Satureja hortensis (summer savory) fresh root, chopped and cooked with honey, or root/date/myrrh/honey preparation to sexual impotence, and a savory/vinegar/honey tea to arthritis. Separate row 123, Sinapis arvensis, records a traditional indication involving return of speech in acute fever/dementia. Botanical identification is the authors' retrospective interpretation of the historical manuscript, not a voucher for a clinical exposure. No fresh-leaf serving, measured speech intelligibility or acoustic intensity, Parkinson diagnosis or clinical response is recorded. This is a historical source analysis rather than a trial.
