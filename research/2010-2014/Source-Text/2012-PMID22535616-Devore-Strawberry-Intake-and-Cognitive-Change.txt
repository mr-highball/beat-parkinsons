Title: Dietary intake of berries and flavonoids in relation to cognitive decline
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3582325/
PMID: 22535616
PMCID: PMC3582325
DOI: 10.1002/ana.23594
First public article date: 2012-04-26
Accessed: 2026-11-19
Text coverage: Curated original methods and Table 2 contrasts

The Nurses' Health Study analysis includes 16,010 older women.
The authors use repeated food-frequency questionnaires and assess
change in cognitive test scores over four years. Table 2 separates
strawberries from blueberries. Comparing at least two weekly
strawberry servings with less than one weekly serving, the fully
adjusted global-score slope difference favors higher intake by
0.03 standard units (95% CI 0.00 to 0.06, p for trend .022).
The corresponding verbal-memory difference is 0.04 standard units
(95% CI 0.01 to 0.08, trend p=.015). The separate telephone status
score has an interval spanning zero.

The food exposure is self-reported, the comparison is observational
and preparation is unspecified. The paper reports cognitive measures,
with no separate Parkinson or fall-frequency contrast. Later berry
reviews citing this result refer back to this one cohort.
