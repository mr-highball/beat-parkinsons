Sasaki K, El Omri A, Kondo S, Han J, Isoda H. Rosmarinus officinalis polyphenols produce anti-depressant like effect through monoaminergic and cholinergic functions modulation. Behav Brain Res. 2013;238:86–94. First online 2012-10-17.
DOI: 10.1016/j.bbr.2012.10.010
PMID: 23085339
Primary sources: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23085339/ and https://rrc.nbrp.jp/references/6484?lang=en
Coverage: original author abstract and institutional bibliographic record. The full article route was gated.

The study addresses a rosemary polyphenol fraction and assays in tail-suspension-tested ICR mice and PC12 cells. The authors report reduced immobility and changes in monoamine, cholinergic and protein-expression measures. Luteolin, carnosic acid and rosmarinic acid appear in constituent assays. Leaf maturity, food preparation, detailed dosing and exact group contrasts are unverified at this source depth. There is no clinical Parkinson depression outcome.
