Title: DHA supplementation improved both memory and reaction time in healthy young adults
Source: https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.112.053371
PMID: 23515006
DOI: 10.3945/ajcn.112.053371
First published online: 2013-03-20
Record type: Distilled original journal full text
Citation: Stonehouse et al., American Journal of Clinical Nutrition 97(5), 1134–1143 (2013)
Publisher full text: https://ajcn.nutrition.org/article/S0002-9165%2823%2905523-5/fulltext
Linked prospective trial: ACTRN12610000212055

The randomized double-blind six-month trial compares DHA-rich capsules containing 1.16 g of DHA and 0.17 g of EPA per day with high-oleic sunflower oil capsules in healthy adults with low DHA intake. Table 4 has 85 active and 91 oil comparator participants with cognitive data at both times. The oil's pressing method is unspecified.

Adjusted active-minus-comparator contrasts in Table 4 include working-memory response time −0.36 SD (95% CI −0.58 to −0.14, p=.002) and working-memory score +0.10 SD (95% CI −0.04 to +0.25, p=.17). Faster responses have negative signed time contrasts. The oil arm's within-arm working-memory response-time estimate was −0.15 SD (95% CI −0.30 to 0.00). The main attention score contrast is +0.10 SD (95% CI −0.10 to +0.30), attention reaction time +0.04 SD (95% CI −0.11 to +0.19), and processing speed −0.11 SD (95% CI −0.29 to +0.07). The attention domain includes Stroop, choice reaction time and vigilance. The authors identify high baseline accuracy across these tasks and mixed subgroup results.

Faster responses after DHA do not establish that sunflower oil caused executive impairment. Cognitive speed, accuracy and inhibitory outcomes are distinct. There is no oil-free arm or Parkinson population. The registry and article count as one family.
