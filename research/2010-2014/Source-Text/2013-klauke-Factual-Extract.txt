Oral isolated beta-caryophyllene in mouse pain assays
2013-10-22
https://www.for926.uni-bonn.de/kunden/for926/content/e229/e1348/e1361/2013_Klauke_EurNeuropsychopharmacol.pdf

Klauke and colleagues administered purified beta-caryophyllene in olive oil. The five mg/kg dose reduced late formalin responses, while early responses were inconclusive and the lower dose did not clearly improve late phase. The Results and Figure 1 caption print different p-value thresholds. After nerve injury, repeated oral doses reduced mechanical hypersensitivity, with thermal latency improved at one dose only. Tail-flick testing did not find a significant effect. The procedures tested evoked responses in mice and did not include cinnamon or pepper consumption.
