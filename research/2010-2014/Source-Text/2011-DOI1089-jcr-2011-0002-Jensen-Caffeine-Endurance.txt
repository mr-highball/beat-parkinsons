Title: Caffeine Supplementation Had No Effect on Endurance Capacity in Elderly Subjects Who Had Abstained from Caffeine-Containing Nutrition for 8 Hours
Source: https://doi.org/10.1089/jcr.2011.0002
DOI: 10.1089/jcr.2011.0002
Publication date: 2011-06-28
Date basis: Publisher first-online date.
Checked: 2026-11-24
Review depth: abstract
Access evidence: Author abstract, publisher results and NCT01048515 registry. The complete journal Methods, results tables and participant history were not available.

Original source scope and evidence boundary
Thirty older people participated in a placebo-controlled crossover after eight hours of abstinence. Cycling endurance was 10.6% lower following caffeine with a wide confidence interval (95% CI −22.7% to 3.3%, p=.123). The abstract reports no clear difference in perceived exertion (p=.084) and reports increased postural sway. The 2005 study from the group used longer abstinence and reported better endurance. The studies do not randomize abstinence intervals against each other and their participant overlap is unresolved. The matching registry labels the Borg exercise rating as fatigue. That measure refers to exertion on an ergometer, with no estimate for clinical Parkinson fatigue or brewed coffee.
