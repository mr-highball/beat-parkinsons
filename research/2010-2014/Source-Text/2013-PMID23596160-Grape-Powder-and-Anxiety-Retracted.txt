Review depth: Full original at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3652881/ and the linked publisher retraction. Earlier review was limited to metadata and the notice.

The article tested grape powder in a rat oxidative-stress model and reported an anxiety-like behavioral result. The editor retracted the paper after concerns involving multiple figure panels and unavailable original image files. The reported findings are not usable for direction. PMID 26180583 reference 259 repeats this result and supplies no independent corroboration. The distinct grape-powder ovariectomy and single-prolonged-stress studies remain separate.

Related research: research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md
Round 1 claim 0860: Raw grapes and Parkinson anxiety. The exposure and the population differ, and this retracted source contributes zero evidentiary weight.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24

November 25 full-original follow-up. The model experiment used freeze-dried powder from mixed table-grape varieties. Figure 2 reports anxiety-like behavior in light-dark and open-field tests in ten animals per group. The paper historically attributed differences to grape powder. The journal retracted the full report. The 2014 phytochemical review repeats this animal study (research/2010-2014/Source-Text/2014-PMID24958636-Phytochemical-Stress-Review-Food-Scopes.txt). Its citation supplies no separate result. No raw table grape or clinical Parkinson anxiety outcome appears.
