Title: Black tea improves attention and self-reported alertness.
PMID: 21172396
DOI: 10.1016/j.appet.2010.12.011
First published: 2011
Authors: De Bruin EA, Rowson MJ, Van Buren L, Rycroft JA, Owen GN
Journal: Appetite
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21172396/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Two double-blind randomized placebo-controlled crossover trials (study 1 n=26; study 2 n=32; total n=58 healthy adults) testing commercially available brewed black tea bags (providing ~100 mg caffeine + 46 mg L-theanine across test doses) vs taste/appearance-matched hot-water placebo. Brewed black tea significantly improved executive attention-switching task accuracy in both trials (study 1 p<0.002; study 2 p=0.007) and Bond-Lader self-reported alertness (study 1 p<0.001; study 2 p=0.021), plus auditory (p<0.001) and visual (p=0.030) intersensory attention in study 1. Limits: acute single-day tests in healthy non-PD adults; industry-funded.
