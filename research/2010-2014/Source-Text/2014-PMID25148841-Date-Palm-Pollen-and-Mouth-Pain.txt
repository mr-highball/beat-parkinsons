Title: Date palm pollen and treatment-related oral mucositis in a controlled pilot
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25148841/
Publisher copy: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/1534735414547110
PMID: 25148841
DOI: 10.1177/1534735414547110
Publication date: 2014-08-22
Date basis: Publisher's first electronic publication.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Review depth: Original methods and Tables 1–4.

The first ten people enrolled with head and neck cancers received a swish-and-swallow suspension of Phoenix dactylifera pollen. The following ten received standard oral mucositis care. This was sequential allocation. Two pollen participants had radiation alone and every comparison participant had chemotherapy plus radiation. The comparison arm could receive other supportive care and analgesia.

Table 3 prints mean oral pain of 0.7 versus 1.5 at day 1, 0.07 versus 2.8 at day 15, and 0 versus 3.0 at day 29, with pollen listed first. Day 1 was reported as nonsignificant. The two later rows were marked significant without exact p values. Day 15 prints 0.07 ± 0.70 for the pollen group. The grade of certainty is limited by sequential allocation, co-treatment imbalance, small groups and no inert rinse control. The measured endpoint was treatment-related oral mucositis pain after a pollen suspension, distinct from the pain comparison after eating whole dried date fruit in Parkinson disease.
