Costa and colleagues studied orally administered Cymbopogon citratus essential oil in male mice, including acute and 21-day repeated treatment. Table 3, printed p. 834, records motor impairment on a rotating apparatus as the number unable to perform the test: vehicle 1/6 acute and 1/6 repeated; oil 10 mg/kg 0/5 acute and 0/7 repeated; oil 1 mg/kg 0/6 repeated; oil 100 mg/kg 1/7 repeated. The authors reported no statistically significant difference from vehicle. Small groups and absence of a prespecified equivalence margin do not establish equivalent effects. The rotating apparatus measures experimental motor performance, not observed incident falls. Distilled oil, mice, and outcome do not match minced culinary lemongrass and Parkinson fall frequency.
