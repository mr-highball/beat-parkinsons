Title: Effect of ginger on gastric motility and symptoms of functional dyspepsia
PMID: 21218090
PMCID: PMC3016669
DOI: 10.3748/wjg.v17.i1.105
Source: https://www.wjgnet.com/1007-9327/full/v17/i1/105.htm
Published: 2011-01-07, World Journal of Gastroenterology 17(1), 105–110.
Archive scope: Factual text from original Methods and Results including Figures 1 and 4.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Hu and colleagues tested ginger alone in a randomized blinded two-visit comparison in 11 patients with functional dyspepsia. After 1.2 g encapsulated ginger or placebo, participants received a liquid test meal. Median ultrasound-estimated half-emptying time was 12.3 minutes after ginger (range 8.5–17.0) and 16.1 after placebo (8.3–22.6), p≤.05. Antral contractions did not show a clear difference (p=.06). Figure 4 gives no clear between-visit difference in gastrointestinal sensation ratings. These results separate accelerated gastric emptying from symptom effects in this acute small experiment.

The study did not compare culinary grated ginger, colonic transit or Parkinson disease. Its acute capsule result cannot confirm or refute the 28-day ginger–artichoke pain contrast. An earlier healthy-participant experiment from overlapping investigators, PMID 18403946, had a distinct 24-person sample.
