Title: Chronic Dietary Supplementation of 4% Figs on the Modification of Oxidative Stress in Alzheimer's Disease Transgenic Mouse Model
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4090508/
PMCID: PMC4090508
PMID: 25050360
DOI: 10.1155/2014/546357
Publication date: 2014-06-19
Date basis: Electronic release on the original article
Source depth: Complete structured original, methods and section 3.1
Evidence type: Primary experimental animal study
Format: Factual digest of original material

The investigators obtained fresh Omani figs and separated and cabinet-dried the flesh. Section 2.1 also describes crushing, acetone extraction and evaporation before powdering. Section 2.2 describes ground figs shipped for incorporation at 4% into chow. The article does not clearly distinguish whether the referenced powder in the diet included an extract or consisted of dried flesh alone.

The report describes 12 female APPsw/Tg2576 Alzheimer-model mice and six nontransgenic controls, assigned to three diet/genotype groups, and measures outcomes after 15 months. Transgenic mice on the fig diet reached the hidden water-maze platform faster than transgenic controls. The brain analyses measured oxidative markers, antioxidants and membrane enzyme activities. The maze measures spatial learning and memory. Neither those outcomes nor the model provide a measurement of Parkinson visual hallucination frequency.

The 2015 biomarker report, PMID:25807081, describes freezing and grinding fresh figs in a five-group comparison. PMID:24938828 reports other behaviors after a fig diet in the same model. Similar investigators, sourcing, diet supplier and model make it inappropriate to count the papers as independent replications without a cohort crosswalk.
