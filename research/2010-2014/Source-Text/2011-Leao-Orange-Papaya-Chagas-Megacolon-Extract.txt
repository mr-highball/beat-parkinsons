Orange and papaya within combined care for Chagas megacolon
Source: https://www.scielo.br/j/ag/a/LcT3KDRNdPvRG4q3QmjPNmy/?format=pdf&lang=en
PMID: 21537543
DOI: 10.1590/S0004-28032011000100011
Coverage: Original publisher PDF Methods, Tables and Results

Leão and colleagues published this prospective study in the January–March 2011 issue. Twelve adults with Chagas megacolon underwent six weeks of pelvic physiotherapy and manual abdominal treatment alongside a daily unstrained drink containing a peeled, deseeded orange, papaya, prunes and cream. Constipation scores declined in 11 of 12 patients at six weeks (paired p<.0022) and in eight of 11 assessed at 12 months (paired p<.0100). During treatment seven reported softer stool and nine more frequent movements. Four reported flatulence or abdominal distension with the drink. The study had no untreated group or comparison separating orange, papaya, or the physical therapy. The population had Chagas megacolon and the fruit was blended. The [university thesis abstract](https://sites.uem.br/pcs/publicacoes/dissertacoes/EgleLeo.pdf) bears a 2010 cover date and reports the same sample, intervention and findings. A first public date for that abstract was not verified. It documents the same study.
