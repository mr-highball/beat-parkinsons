Title: Gum chewing improves swallow frequency and latency in Parkinson patients
PMID: 20385891
DOI: 10.1212/WNL.0b013e3181d9002b
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20385891/
Publisher: https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.0b013e3181d9002b
Publication: 2010-04, Neurology 74(15), pp. 1198–1202
Coverage: Structured author abstract. The complete original article remains unavailable.

South and colleagues studied 20 people with Parkinson disease who did not report significant swallowing difficulty during meals. In three successive five-minute periods, the mean counts of recorded swallowing events were 3.10 at baseline, 14.95 during gum chewing and 7.00 afterward. Mean latencies were 131.8, 24.1 and 60.74 seconds, respectively. The report classifies the comparison as Class III evidence.

The measured endpoints concern swallowing timing. The abstract does not report a separate graded drooling outcome, ingredient formulation, raw fig exposure or an isolated effect of flavor. The order of the tasks and the brief measurement period limit interpretation of differences over time.
