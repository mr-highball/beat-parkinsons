Title: Evaluation of anxiolytic activity of the essential oil of the aerial part of Foeniculum vulgare Miller in mice
PMID: 25149087
PMCID: PMC4156641
DOI: 10.1186/1472-6882-14-310
Publication date: 2014-08-23
Citation: Mesfin, Asres and Shibeshi. BMC Complementary and Alternative Medicine 14, 310.
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4156641/
Coverage: Primary article body, experimental design and reported observations reviewed.

The preparations used distilled oil from fresh leaves. A separate acute oral screen in five female mice reported no mortality, weight loss or overt toxicity signs after a single 2,000 mg/kg dose. There is no separate tremor count. In the behavioral experiments the male mice were assigned to six groups of six. The article reports increased central crossings in the open field at 200 mg/kg and different rearing or maze-entry results across doses. These are anxiety and activity measures in healthy animals. The plant part, concentration and tests differ from eating toasted fennel seeds and measuring Parkinson resting tremor. This preparation differs from the earlier sweet-fennel oil and extract toxicity lineage.
