Huang KM, Liu SM, Chen YJ, Huang YW, Deng JF, Hwang DF. Identification of causative agents and species in shrimp implicated in a food poisoning case in Taiwan. J Food Prot. 2010 Dec;73(12):2250–2255. PMID 21219744, DOI 10.4315/0362-028x-73.12.2250. Original primary full text accessible at https://www.researchgate.net/publication/49741350_Identification_of_Causative_Agents_and_Species_in_Shrimp_Implicated_in_a_Food_Poisoning_Case_in_Taiwan ; journal record https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21219744/ .

The report says a 35-year-old fisherman cooked a box of frozen bait shrimp, around 300 g, and ate it with rice. About an hour later he had vomiting, diarrhea, erythema and “marked central nervous system irritation”; it does not specify hallucinations or a detailed psychiatric exam. Vomitus PCR and sequencing matched Parapenaeus fissuroides. Boric acid was 36.8–37.1 mg/g in vomitus, 1.4–3.8 mg/g in independently sampled bait-store shrimp, and below detection in separately sampled food-market shrimp. The authors concluded preservative contamination was likely. Samples are not proof of a dose ingested, and other exposures or patient circumstances are not fully characterized. Cooking method other than cooked, any grilling, Parkinson and visual-hallucination outcomes are not reported. General review prose on severe boric-acid poisoning lists delirium; it does not attribute delirium to this patient.
