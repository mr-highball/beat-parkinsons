Title: Effects of dietary extra-virgin olive oil on behaviour and brain biochemical parameters in ageing rats
Source: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114509993655
PMID: 20070918
DOI: 10.1017/S0007114509993655
Publication date: 2010-01-14
Text coverage: Full publisher paper and visual check of Figure 2 on printed page 1680.

The three high-energy diets used maize oil, high-phenol extra-virgin olive oil (720 mg/kg total phenols), or low-phenol extra-virgin olive oil (10 mg/kg). The light–dark test analyzed 10, 10 and eight older rats, respectively. In the Results and plotted Figure 2(a), the high-phenol group stayed longer in the initial light chamber before passing into the dark. The Results report a significant increase in latency versus both other diets (p < 0.05). The abstract and discussion instead describe a decreased latency. The bars and the direction stated in Results agree. The longer latency was interpreted as reduced preference for the dark in this model. Differences in total time spent in the light did not reach significance.

The high-phenol group's longer rotarod time to first fall was inconclusive (p = 0.18). It is a timed apparatus outcome in aging rats. The paper distinguishes test behavior from a diagnosed Parkinson population or a clinical anxiety or fall measure.
