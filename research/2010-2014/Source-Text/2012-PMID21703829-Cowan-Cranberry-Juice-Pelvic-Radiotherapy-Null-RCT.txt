Title: A randomised double-blind placebo-controlled trial to determine the effect of cranberry juice on decreasing the incidence of urinary symptoms and urinary tract infections in patients undergoing radiotherapy for cancer of the bladder or cervix.
PMID: 21703829
DOI: 10.1016/j.clon.2011.05.009
First published: 2012
Authors: Cowan CC, Hutchison C, Cole T, Barry SJ, Paul J, Reed NS, Russell JM
Journal: Clinical oncology (Royal College of Radiologists (Great Britain))
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21703829/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial (Cowan et al., 2012, Clin Oncol) testing twice-daily cranberry juice vs placebo during and 2 weeks after radical pelvic radiotherapy for bladder or cervical cancer. Incidence of increased urinary symptoms (frequency/cystitis) or UTIs did not differ significantly (82.5% cranberry vs 89.3% placebo; adjusted OR 0.48, 95% CI 0.14-1.63, p=0.240; underpowered with poor compliance). Complemented by Campbell et al. 2003 (PMID:14524485, n=112 men prostate EBRT) finding no difference in IPSS urinary symptoms vs apple juice (p=0.39).
