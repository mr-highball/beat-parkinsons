Factual extract of Methods, Results and Table 2, checked 2026-11-14 (US Eastern).

Forty young adults with low energy completed three visits in a double-blind trial. Dried black pepper fruit blend 2.0 g (piperine 53 mg/g), rosemary 1.7 g and rice flour 3.1 g were supplied as oral capsules. The participants wore a noseclip during administration. A 16-minute dual task measured sustained attention at baseline and 60 and 90 minutes after ingestion. Primary task reaction times (mean in milliseconds ± SD) were 587.3 ± 65.6, 601.6 ± 57.8 and 613.3 ± 67.3 on the black pepper visit and 607.9 ± 74.0, 599.5 ± 75.4 and 592.5 ± 69.1 on the rice flour visit. The cognitive outcomes had no significant condition-by-time interactions (p > .05), including with statistical adjustment for motivation. The intervention was a single dose and the participants did not represent PD. The dried fruit article does not identify cracking or coarse grinding.

Rosemary arm reviewed from the same original study: 40 people each received two of rosemary, pepper and placebo. Twenty-six rosemary exposures used two rosemary species together. The report gives moisture 5.78% and leaves freshness, drying process and exact species fractions unspecified. Its rosemary mental-fatigue means were 135.1, 112.4 and 130.2 versus placebo 132.5, 135.3 and 138.0. Mood treatment-by-time tests were inconclusive (p>.05). The authors describe an exploratory standardized value at 60 minutes. The outcome is fatigue after capsule ingestion and the study does not supply a clinical depressive-symptom result.
