Aniwan S, Gonlachanvit S. Effects of chili treatment on gastrointestinal and rectal sensation in diarrhea-predominant irritable bowel syndrome. J Neurogastroenterol Motil. 2014;20(3):400–406. PMID24867591. PMCID PMC4102147. DOI 10.5056/jnm14022.
Source https://www.jnmjournal.org/journal/download_pdf.php?doi=10.5056%2Fjnm14022 and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24867591/.
Sixteen IBS-D patients received two six-week assignments in randomized double-blind crossover with four-week washout. Before each main meal, the two active capsules delivered 0.5 g Capsicum frutescens powder uncoated and 0.2 g powder plus 0.3 g lactose enteric coated. Both placebo capsules contained 0.5 g lactose each. The daily chili dose was 2.1 g powder. At six weeks, peak pain on the standardized spicy meal challenge was inconclusive (Figure 2B p=.799). Lower peak abdominal burning after the meal was a separate finding, 0.0 (0.0–0.5) versus 0.3 (0.0–1.6), p<.05. First rectal sensation occurred at 16 (12–16) versus 8 (8–16) mmHg (p<.05), with inconclusive rectal urgency and compliance differences. No ground cayenne cultivar or Parkinson pain data were given.
