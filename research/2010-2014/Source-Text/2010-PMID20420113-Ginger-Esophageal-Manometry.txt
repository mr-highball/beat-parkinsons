Title: Effect of ginger on lower esophageal sphincter pressure
PMID: 20420113
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20420113/
Original PDF copy: https://popline.org/node/12203.pdf
Published: 2010-03, Journal of the Medical Association of Thailand 93, 366–372.
Archive scope: Factual summary from original manuscript pages 366–372 including Methods and Tables 2–4.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Fourteen healthy men aged 21–29 received water first and then a suspension of 1 g dried, ground ginger in water. The methods specify fixed order and different observation durations despite the abstract’s randomized-design label. Lower esophageal sphincter resting pressure showed no clear change. Table 2 reports relaxation at swallowing rising from 66.59% immediately before ginger to 82.96% at 180 minutes, p=.003. Table 4 gives propagation velocity of 3.47 to 2.85 cm/s, p=.018, over that comparison. The discussion describes an increase in velocity, while the table, abstract and Results show a decrease. Several timepoints were tested. Bloating, nausea and discomfort ratings were 0–2 out of 10 during the experiment, without a clinical symptom treatment contrast. This is esophageal manometry, with no stomach-emptying, bowel or Parkinson outcome.
