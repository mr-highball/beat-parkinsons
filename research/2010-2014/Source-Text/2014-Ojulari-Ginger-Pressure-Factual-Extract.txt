Title: Ginger extract and acute pressure in healthy participants
Source: https://www.iosrjournals.org/iosr-jdms/papers/Vol13-issue10/Version-3/T0131037678.pdf


Ojulari and colleagues assigned 60 healthy volunteers to water or one of two ginger extract doses. The preparation was mechanically powdered dried rhizome, extracted and concentrated. The 100 mg/kg arm's narrative reports mean systolic pressure 114.3 mmHg before treatment, 105.5 at two hours and 107.6 at four hours. Its table shows the same direction and slightly different rounded means. The abstract describes an increase after four hours without explaining the comparison. Methods specify a digital cuff and pulse monitor but no posture transition or lightheadedness measure. The reported before-after changes, incompletely presented comparator results and small study cannot establish a standing symptom effect.
