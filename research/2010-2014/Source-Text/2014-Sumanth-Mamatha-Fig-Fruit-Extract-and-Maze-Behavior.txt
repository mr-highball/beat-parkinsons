Title: Learning and memory enhancing activity of Ficus carica (Fig)
Source: https://ddtjournal.net/content/108/2014/5/2/pdf/DrugDevTher_2014_5_2_123_139624.pdf
Publication: Drug Development and Therapeutics, 5(2), July–December 2014, beginning p. 123.
Coverage: Original article methods, results, Tables 1–2 and Figure 1.

Sumanth and Mamatha assign five groups of six rats to vehicle, scopolamine control, two oral fruit-extract amounts, or a piracetam comparison. The intervention uses an ethanolic fruit extract. At day 27, the high-extract group reached the reward chamber in a reported 13.50 seconds versus 110.83 seconds with scopolamine alone; the lower-extract group took 107.50 seconds. The day-27 elevated-plus-maze transfer latencies were 3.94, 39.00 and 22.50 seconds respectively. The authors mark high-extract comparisons in both tables as p<.05. Maze, escape and task timing can reflect multiple cognitive or motor factors. This is a rat drug-challenge model without culinary dried fig ingestion, Parkinson diagnosis or visual episode data.
