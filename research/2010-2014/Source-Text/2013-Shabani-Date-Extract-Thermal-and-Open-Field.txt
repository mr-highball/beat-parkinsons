Shabani M, Zangiabadi N, Asadi-Shekaari M. Evidence for positive effects of date extract that attenuates thermal hyperalgesia in a diabetic rat model of neuropathic pain. Neuroscience & Medicine. 2013;4:16–22.
DOI: 10.4236/nm.2013.41003
Primary source: https://www.scirp.net/journal/paperinformation?paperid=28563
Coverage: factual summary of full original, including Methods, Results and Figures 1, 3 and 5.

The report gives male Sprague-Dawley rats with induced diabetes and water-extracted fresh fruit orally. The Methods refer to the 2011 preparation study and call oral gavage the route. Figure 3's caption says intraperitoneal, which conflicts with Methods and Figure 1. Hot-plate latency was 6.9 ± 1.2 seconds versus 3.7 ± 0.19 seconds for vehicle, mean ± SEM. Results and Figure 1 use p<.05 for that contrast; the abstract uses p<.01. The tail-flick comparison was inconclusive. Extract-treated animals had greater total distance and rearing than vehicle-treated diabetic animals in the open field (p<.05); mobility, immobility and center/perimeter time were inconclusive. This is fresh-fruit extract in a nociception model. The 2011 original reports Wistar rats and different groups, and the papers supply no common animal identifiers.
