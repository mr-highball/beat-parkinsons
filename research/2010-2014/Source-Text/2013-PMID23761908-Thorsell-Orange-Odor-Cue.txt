Title: Thorsell et al. — orange odor as a conditioned cue in a rat alcohol experiment

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3682378/
First published: 2013-06-12.
Coverage: Complete original text, including cue conditioning methods and Figure 8 caption.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

The cue experiment used an orange scent during alcohol conditioning in 16 rats. The investigators restored the scent and tested lever responding after extinction. Restoring the trained cue increased responding. The antagonist-versus-vehicle comparison in that test was inconclusive, with a reported p value of 0.97. The composition and botanical provenance of the scent are unspecified.

The methods, compound and test statistics match the cue branch in 2011 ACNP member 220. Treat them as reports of the same experiment. The design addresses conditioned odor and alcohol seeking. It supplies no food intake or constipation result for raw orange.
