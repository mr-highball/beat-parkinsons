2010 repeated ground rhizome and exercise pain

Black et al. first published on 24 April 2010. The article reports two double-blind randomized experiments and separate placebos. Campus volunteers took capsules with 2 g/day of ground, unheated rhizome or ground and heat-treated rhizome for 11 days. Sample sizes analyzed were 34 and 40, respectively. The 24-hour eccentric-exercise arm-pain ratings were 25.3% lower with unheated rhizome (p=.041) and 22.5% lower with heated rhizome (p=.049), relative to each study’s own placebo. Capsules differed between experiments and the study did not randomize a head-to-head raw-versus-heated comparison. The McCormick Science Institute supported the work, supplied characterized ginger and made the capsules. The study excluded an unheated-trial placebo participant after inadequate exercise-induced injury. This is the closest located whole-rhizome result, with a capsule preparation, experimentally induced pain and a non-Parkinson sample.
