Title: The Effects of Powdered Fertilized Eggs on Depression
Author: Ester Solberg
PMID: 21631360
PMCID: PMC3133680
DOI: 10.1089/jmf.2010.0146
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3133680/
First published online: 2011-06-01
Text coverage: Primary study Methods, Results, Tables 1–2 and Discussion, distilled.

A 12-week double-blind randomized study recruited 66 adults with Beck Depression Inventory-II (BDI-II) scores at least 10. It compared fertilized egg isolate (YTE), the same YTE plus dried-leaf-derived Melissa officinalis, and lactose placebo. The two active arms received the same 1,680 mg daily YTE amount. The combination provided an additional 600 mg daily Melissa material. The process used to prepare that component is unspecified.

The report says 55 completed. Table 1 gives 18 YTE, 19 combination and 18 placebo. Initial assigned numbers by arm are unavailable. The paper describes 11 dropouts overall, provides reasons for four dropouts and two protocol exclusions, and reports five final telephone BDI-II assessments without Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAM-D) interviews. The exact HAM-D denominator by arm is unresolved. Methods describe returned-bottle counting. Discussion lists adherence as unverified.

Table 2 gives baseline to week-12 HAM-D means of 19.5 to 9.4 with egg isolate, 18.6 to 10.3 with the combination, and 18.4 to 16.5 with placebo. The authors report both active arms differed from placebo at week 12 (p<.01) and no significant difference between the two active arms. The article supplies no precise p-value or interval for the difference attributable to the added material. Week-12 BDI-II means were 11.9, 11.7 and 16.1. The authors report p=.49 and p=.31 for the active-arm BDI-II contrasts with placebo. The study did not use a Parkinson cohort, an isolated lemon-balm arm, or brewed tea.
