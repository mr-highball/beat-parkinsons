Title: Neurorescue effect of rosmarinic acid on 6-hydroxydopamine-lesioned nigral dopamine neurons in rat model of Parkinson's disease
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22205146/
PMID: 22205146
DOI: 10.1007/s12031-011-9693-1
First publication: 2011-12-29 online. The journal issue followed in 2012.
Coverage: Factual summary of the original author abstract. The full journal body and arm-level tables remain unverified.

Wang and colleagues tested oral isolated rosmarinic acid after a dopaminergic lesion in rats. The author abstract reports higher striatal dopamine and more tyrosine hydroxylase-positive neurons after treatment, alongside lower iron staining and changes in the Bax/Bcl-2 gene expression ratio. The study is a preclinical measurement of lesion and neuronal responses. The author abstract gives no fresh rosemary food group, diagnosed human Parkinson population, or depressive symptom measure.

The original 2020 review's Table 2 cites this experiment under Parkinson models. Its separate Table 1 cites Jin's chronic-stress rat behavior experiment under depression. These are distinct underlying study families with distinct outcome and disease models.
