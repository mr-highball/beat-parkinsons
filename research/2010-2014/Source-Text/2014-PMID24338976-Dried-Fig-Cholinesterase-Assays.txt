Title: Chemical composition and bioactivity of dried fruits and honey of Ficus carica cultivars Dottato, San Francesco and Citrullara
Source: https://scijournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/jsfa.6533

Date basis: Publisher online DOI deposit. August print issue, Journal of the Science of Food and Agriculture 94(11), 2179–2186.
Text coverage: Primary author abstract and publisher bibliography. Full laboratory methods and tables remain open.

Loizzo and colleagues characterized dried fig material from three cultivars and a fig preparation described as honey. The Dottato material had the strongest inhibition of acetylcholinesterase and butyrylcholinesterase among the reported cultivars. Its half-maximal inhibitory concentrations were 26.9 and 31.8 micrograms per milliliter, respectively. The authors also measured chemical composition and radical-scavenging activity. The assay concentrations characterize laboratory material. They establish neither exposure to those concentrations after eating dried fruit nor a change in a clinical symptom. The authors studied no Parkinson population or visual hallucination frequency in the reported assays.

The publisher full-text route resolves to the abstract. The extraction conditions, assay controls and per-cultivar uncertainty require the original body.
