Title: Caffeine for treatment of Parkinson disease: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Postuma and colleagues
Publication: Neurology 79(7), 651–658, 2012
Identifiers: PMID22855866, PMC3414662, 10.1212/WNL.0b013e318263570d, NCT00459420
Source: https://n.neurology.org/content/neurology/79/7/651.full.pdf
Coverage: Factual extraction of Table 2 and assigned exposures.

A blinded six-week trial enrolled 61 participants with Parkinson disease and daytime somnolence. Thirty received isolated caffeine capsules and 31 matching placebo. Daily caffeine dose was 200 mg through week three and 400 mg through week six. Between-arm rest tremor change was −0.32 points at week three (95% interval −0.73 to 0.10) and −0.28 at week six (−0.83 to 0.27). Clinician-rated UPDRS Part III gait change comparisons were −0.12 (−0.84 to 0.60) and −0.28 (−1.16 to 0.59), respectively. Separate six-week estimates include −3.15 (−5.50 to −0.83) for the Part III total and −1.70 (−3.10 to −0.31) for its bradykinesia component. Item outcomes were secondary. The trial did not assign brewed tea or measure timed walking speed.
