Earlier Japanese bay leaf odor vigilance experiment
Source: https://www.urakamizaidan.or.jp/research/jisseki/2007/vol17urakamif-05shimizu.pdf
Coverage: Original Japanese Methods and Figures 5-1 and 5-2

Shimizu and colleagues studied 11 healthy men aged 20–23 in counterbalanced odor sessions. Dried bay leaves were cut and placed in the olfactometer. The low-load 0.1 g odor condition differed from clean air on task accuracy over 30 minutes (p=.026), with a reported contrast at 20–30 minutes (p=.030). The 3.0 g odor condition had no significant accuracy contrast with control and produced less favorable odor impressions. The original prints volume 17 (2009) and [J-GLOBAL dates publication to March 2010](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/en/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=201002298082339061). The later nine-person report shares authors, leaf loads and task setup. Participant overlap has not been resolved, so the two reports form one evidence family. The measured effect is from breathing leaf odor, with no ground-leaf ingestion or Parkinson attention comparison.
