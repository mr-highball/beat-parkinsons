Olive oil control in a two-year older adult cognitive trial
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20410089/
PMID: 20410089
DOI: 10.3945/ajcn.2009.29121
Registration: ISRCTN72331636
Coverage: Original full Table 5 and trial methods

Dangour and colleagues first published April 21, 2010. The OPAL trial randomized 867 older people without cognitive impairment to long-chain omega-3 supplements or olive oil. Seven hundred forty-eight completed the two-year assessment. Table 5 shows mean letter-cancellation accuracy changing from 78.9% to 79.2% in the olive-oil arm. Simple reaction time changed from 0.31 to 0.29 seconds and choice reaction time from 0.72 to 0.68 seconds. The adjusted secondary cognitive comparisons between arms did not reach statistical significance. Without an oil-free arm or stated olive-oil grade, the measured changes do not identify a refined-olive-oil effect.
