The effect of mefenamic acid and ginger on pain relief in primary dysmenorrhea: a randomized clinical trial
Marjan Ahmad Shirvani, Narges Motahari-Tabari and Abbas Alipour
Archives of Gynecology and Obstetrics 291, 1277–1281 (2015 issue). First published November 16, 2014.
PMID 25399316. DOI 10.1007/s00404-014-3548-2.
Publisher identity and author abstract: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00404-014-3548-2
Authoritative original paper reviewed via a copy of the typeset article: https://magistralbr.caldic.com/storage/product-files/1879208423.pdf
Coverage: Methods, Results, Table 1, Figure 1 and discussion. This factual extract preserves selected measurements and limitations.

The study randomly allocated 122 students with moderate to severe primary dysmenorrhea, 61 per group. Participants took either Zintoma ginger capsules (250 mg every 6 hours) or mefenamic acid (250 mg every 8 hours) from the start of menses until pain subsided, across two cycles. Reported pain was the greatest pain during menses on a 100 mm visual analogue scale. Baseline means were 58.01 mm for ginger and 55.03 mm for mefenamic acid. Cycle 1 means were 43.49 and 39.01 mm, respectively. Cycle 2 means were 38.19 and 33.75 mm. Between-group pain comparisons were inconclusive (p > 0.05). The ginger group reported more days of menstruation during each treatment cycle (p = 0.01 and 0.04). Pain duration and bleeding amount differences were inconclusive.

Both groups received active interventions. No placebo group or prespecified equivalence margin was reported. The study gives no estimate for ordinary grated ginger, or for pain severity in Parkinson disease.
