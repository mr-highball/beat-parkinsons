Title: Finely chopped ginger and postprandial blood pressure
Source: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0007114512001201


Gregersen et al. first published this study online July 5, 2012, before the 2013 print issue. The randomized five-condition crossover enrolled 22 healthy young men. One meal arm added 20 g finely chopped ginger to stewed apples in the same brunch format. Blood pressure was recorded repeatedly around the meal; the methods describe supine rest for the respiratory measurements and no timed standing challenge. The Results report no significant overall systolic effect (p=.15). Among named post hoc diastolic comparisons, the 1.4 mmHg difference is horseradish versus ginger (p=.050). The authors report no other between-treatment differences for diastolic pressure. The preparation is fine chopping, and lightheadedness severity after standing is unmeasured.
