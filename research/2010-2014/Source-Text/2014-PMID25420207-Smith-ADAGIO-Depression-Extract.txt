Title: Combined Rasagiline and Antidepressant Use in Parkinson Disease in the ADAGIO Study: Effects on Nonmotor Symptoms and Tolerability
PMID: 25420207
DOI: 10.1001/jamaneurol.2014.2472
Source: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/1934721
First published: 2014-11-24 online, per the publisher's Article Information. JAMA Neurology 72(1):88–95 is the January 2015 issue.
Trial registry: NCT00256204
Coverage: Factual extract of the journal's main report and Table 3. Supplemental tables remain separate.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

Smith, Eyal and Weintraub analyzed the placebo-controlled first 36 weeks of ADAGIO. This post hoc subgroup covered 191 of 1,174 trial participants who received an antidepressant during phase 1 and contributed efficacy data. Assignment to rasagiline or placebo was the randomized trial comparison. Antidepressant treatment and timing define an observational subgroup.

The estimated change at week 36 on the MDS-UPDRS depression item was +0.57 with pooled rasagiline and +0.76 with placebo. The between-group difference was −0.19 points, 95% CI −0.38 to −0.002, p=.048. Both groups worsened on the single item. The smaller increase is not a measured reduction from baseline or a result from a standalone depression scale. The subgroup analysis reported a difference of −0.23 points (SE .09, p=.009) after adjustment for motor change. The report observed no serious adverse event suggestive of serotonin syndrome in the subgroup, which has limited ability to rule out rare events.

The study compares drug regimens, without a lemon-balm intervention. The publisher's ADAGIO investigator roster includes a given name matching Melissa. This explains a plausible lexical collision and supplies clinical context about interpreting a Parkinson depression item.
