Title: The effect of caffeinated versus decaffeinated drinks on overactive bladder: a double-blind, randomized, crossover study.
PMID: 24988515
DOI: 10.1097/WON.0000000000000040
First published: 2014
Authors: Wells MJ, Jamieson K, Markham TC, Green SM, Fader MJ
Journal: Journal of wound, ostomy, and continence nursing : official publication of The Wound, Ostomy and Continence Nurses Society
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24988515/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Double-blind randomized crossover pilot RCT in 14 randomized (11 completed) community-dwelling women newly diagnosed with overactive bladder comparing 14-day caffeinated vs decaffeinated drink periods (14-day run-in and washout). Decaffeinated intake significantly lowered day-3 bladder-diary urinary urgency (p<0.01), voiding frequency (p<0.05), and total ICIQ-OAB score (p<0.01), with ICIQ-OABqol trend (p=0.065), indicating caffeinated beverages worsen urgency. Limits: small non-PD sample; pooled caffeinated drinks rather than isolated brewed black tea.
