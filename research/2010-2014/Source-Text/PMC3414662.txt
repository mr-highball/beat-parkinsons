Title: Caffeine for treatment of Parkinson disease: a randomized controlled trial
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3414662/
Original PDF: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3414662/pdf/znl651.pdf
PMID: 22855866
PMCID: PMC3414662
DOI: 10.1212/wnl.0b013e318263570d
Publication date: 2012-08-01
Date basis: First online publication. August 14, 2012 is the issue date.
Coverage: Factual summary from the full original, including Table 2 and the registered comparison NCT00459420.

The investigators randomized 61 people with Parkinson disease and daytime somnolence to oral caffeine capsules or placebo. The primary analysis concerns daytime somnolence as measured by the Epworth scale. Fatigue was a separately measured secondary outcome. Table 2 gives the caffeine-versus-placebo Fatigue Severity Scale change contrast of -3.08 points at week three (95% credible interval -6.88 to +0.73) and -2.85 at week six (95% credible interval -7.73 to +2.06). Both intervals span zero. The point estimates are in the easing direction, with uncertainty that includes worsening.

The trial used isolated caffeine, distinct from brewed coffee and tea. Participants were selected for daytime somnolence. The estimates do not establish a sustained fatigue effect or a clinical benefit of brewed coffee.
