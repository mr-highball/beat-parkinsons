Title: Cinnamon-associated voice symptoms in the Martucci and Katial case
Source: https://studyres.com/doc/8018988/abstract-book?page=113
Publication date: 2010-11
Date basis: Printed November 2010 annual meeting abstract publication.
Source locator: American College of Allergy, Asthma and Immunology Annual Meeting, Annals of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology volume 105, page A76, poster P179.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25
Review depth: primary_source

The clinicians list voice changes and respiratory symptoms in a patient with vocal cord dysfunction. Reported triggers included cinnamon odor, oral cinnamon and smoke. They documented vocal fold dysfunction during a symptomatic evaluation. They subsequently reported tolerance of oral and inhaled cinnamon after treatment for the vocal fold condition.

The oral preparation was unspecified, the different triggers were not isolated, and the reported symptom was dysphonia. There was no Parkinson population or listener-rated speech intelligibility measure. This preserves both the original adverse observation and subsequent tolerance in one case family. No direction transfers to the ground cinnamon claim.
