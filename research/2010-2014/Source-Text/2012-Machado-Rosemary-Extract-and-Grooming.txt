A hydroalcoholic extract of dried Brazilian rosemary aerial parts was compared with vehicle and a fluoxetine group following olfactory bulbectomy. In the splash-test grooming readout, the surgery increased latency and reduced total grooming. Repeated oral extract and the comparator improved both observations. Figure 5 reports nine to eleven animals per group. The authors report treatment-by-surgery interaction p<.05 for latency, p<.01 for total grooming, and a latency treatment main effect of p=.10. The preparation shares a plant voucher with the 2009 report, and the behavioral model differs. No clinical depression outcome or fresh rosemary food was tested.
