Title: Melissa oil, donepezil and double placebo for agitation in Alzheimer disease
First published: 2011-02-19
PMID: 21335973
DOI: 10.1159/000324438
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21335973/
Archive scope: Author abstract. Arm-level tables and full text are separate access leads.

The blinded three-arm trial randomized 114 care-home residents to active aromatherapy plus placebo drug, donepezil plus placebo aromatherapy, or double placebo. Ninety-four were assessed at week 4 and 81 at week 12. Raters wore nose clips. The primary measure was the 4-week Pittsburgh Agitation Scale. Authors reported no statistically distinct arm effect at weeks 4 or 12. The improvement reported across all groups at week 12 was 18% on the agitation scale and 37% on the Neuropsychiatric Inventory. Those percentages are pooled improvements and cannot be read as the oil effect. The aggregate Neuropsychiatric Inventory result cannot stand in for a separately measured depressive domain. The comparison pertains to aromatherapy and agitation in Alzheimer disease.
