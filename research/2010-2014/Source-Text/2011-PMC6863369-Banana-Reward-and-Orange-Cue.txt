Title: ACNP 2011 Poster Session I — banana-flavored reward and orange scent in two different members
PMCID: PMC6863369
DOI: 10.1038/npp.2011.291
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6863369/
First published: 2011-11-30 online, December 2011 issue.
Coverage: Member 210 and member 220 extracted from the complete posted proceedings text.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

Member 210, Mello et al., reports four adult male rhesus monkeys in a buspirone and drug self-administration experiment. The food reinforcer was a banana-flavored pellet. Food-maintained responding under buspirone varied. The intervention was buspirone rather than a comparison of foods, and fruit content of the pellets is unspecified. Two later journal papers describe the same research program and pellet flavor. Treat animal overlap as unresolved.

Member 220, Thorsell et al., uses an orange scent during alcohol-conditioning and reinstatement tests in rats. Conditioned lever responding increased when the cue was restored. The assigned neuropeptide S receptor antagonist did not clearly change cue-induced responding. The abstract gives no ingredient formulation or oral exposure. The later journal article reports matching test statistics and is a companion report.

The two members use distinct species, stimuli and outcome measures. Neither estimates an effect of eating raw banana or orange on a Parkinson symptom.
