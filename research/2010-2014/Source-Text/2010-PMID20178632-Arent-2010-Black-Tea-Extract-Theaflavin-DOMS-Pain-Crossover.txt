Title: The effects of theaflavin-enriched black tea extract on muscle soreness, oxidative stress, inflammation, and endocrine responses to acute anaerobic interval training: a randomized, double-blind, crossover study.
PMID: 20178632
PMCID: PMC2837615
DOI: 10.1186/1550-2783-7-11
First published: 2010
Authors: Arent SM, Senso M, Golem DL, McKeever KH
Journal: Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2837615/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Double-blind randomized placebo-controlled crossover trial in n=18 resistance-trained young men taking 1,760 mg/day theaflavin-enriched black tea extract (BTE) vs placebo for 9 days around anaerobic Wingate interval bouts. BTE significantly lowered VAS delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS) pain at 24 h (p<0.001) and 48 h (p<0.001), raised peak power (p=0.013), and increased GSH:GSSG ratio (p=0.001). Limits: concentrated theaflavin extract rather than brewed black tea; acute exercise-induced muscle soreness in young athletes rather than chronic PD pain.
