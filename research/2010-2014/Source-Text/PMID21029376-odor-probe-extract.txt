Title: Serine racemase deletion disrupts memory for order and alters cortical dendritic morphology
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3348622/
PMID: 21029376
PMCID: PMC3348622
DOI: 10.1111/j.1601-183X.2010.00656.x
Publication: Online 2 November 2010. Genes, Brain and Behavior 10, 210–222, issue 2011.
Text coverage: Original factual extraction from Methods and Figure 2.

Tarragon was item N in the odor-object sequence. Probe trials included tarragon-orange for within-sequence order, cilantro-tarragon for between-sequence order, and tarragon-onion for novelty. The commercial herb stimulus was dispersed in sand at 1% weight. Figure 2 combines cases from multiple pairs. Serine racemase-deficient mice preferred recent odors in the pooled task where control mice preferentially explored the earlier ones, and the pooled novel-odor test found no clear genotype difference. Ingredient exposure and olfactory outcome were not assigned to test tarragon efficacy. The procedure included a food reward during training, separate from the tarragon odor.
