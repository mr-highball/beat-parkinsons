Title: Daikenchuto transit, stool and rectal sensitivity placebo comparison
PMID: 23451764
DOI: 10.1111/apt.12264
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23451764/
Published: 2013-03-04, first online date in the publisher record.
Publisher abstract: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/apt.12264
Archive scope: Evidence-focused summary of the investigators' indexed abstract and official registration.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

The investigators' abstract reports 45 women with functional constipation in a double-blind parallel comparison of placebo and two daily amounts of TU-100 for 28 days. The registry specifies total daily groups of 7.5 g and 15 g. The study did not find a clear effect on whole gut or colonic transit, stool frequency, stool consistency or quality of life. Rectal tests at the higher amount yielded nominal differences in first sensation and gas thresholds (unadjusted p=.045 and .024). Those sensory measurements have no isolated link to symptom improvement.

The tested product is a formula containing processed ginger alongside ginseng and Japanese pepper. The official registration supplies no posted results. The complete article and outcome intervals remain open.
