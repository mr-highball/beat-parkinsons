Title: Effects of Dates Pulp Extract and Palm Sap (Phoenix dactylifera L.) on Gastrointestinal Transit Activity in Healthy Rats
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24611963/
PMID: 24611963
PMCID: PMC4098977
DOI: 10.1089/jmf.2013.0112
First published: 2014-03-10
Authors: Abdellaziz Souli, Hichem Sebai, Kaïs Rtibi, Latifa Chehimi, Mohsen Sakly, Mohamed Amri, Jamel El-Benna
Coverage: Original author abstract and public article text excerpts. Exact figure values and full-text table access remain open.

The experiment compared an aqueous date-pulp extract and palm sap from Phoenix dactylifera with a saline control in fasted male Wistar rats. The author abstract reports faster gastrointestinal transit for pulp extract with increasing dose and a smaller rise with sap. Isolated intestinal contraction results and in vivo transit are different measurements. The study addresses neither intact dried fruit nor pain and apathy outcomes in Parkinson disease. Its separate discussion of Tunisian constipation traditions identifies a research rationale and provides no firsthand patient outcomes or field sample.

The pulp and sap treatments are distinct preparations. Faster transit in this animal comparison supplies gastrointestinal context for the healthy-adult dried-date diary and leaves clinical symptom effects open.
