Title: Effects of omega-3 supplementation on neurocognitive functioning and mood in deployed soldiers
Source: https://academic.oup.com/milmed/article/179/4/396/4160759
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24690964/
PMID: 24690964
DOI: 10.7205/MILMED-D-13-00395
Publication date: 2014-04-01
Reviewed: 2026-11-26
Coverage: Original author abstract and opening Methods preview. Numerical sleepiness results from a secondary review.

The opening Methods says 106 U.S. soldiers agreed to participate. The abstract describes a randomized comparison with baseline and 60-day assessments. The reported 44 and 34 are the overall analysis groups in the available flow-diagram excerpt. The Epworth Sleepiness Scale denominator remains unverified. The abstract calls the omega-3-index association with change in daytime sleepiness inverse while printing r=0.30. It provides neither the original Epworth outcome table nor an effect attributable to refined culinary corn oil. Participants were deployed adults outside a Parkinson cohort.

A later review lists Epworth means of 10.7 ± 4.5 for omega-3 and 10.0 ± 4.3 for the comparator, p=.247, under n=106. The preview shows that 106 denotes participation agreement in the original. The original Epworth timepoint and denominator remain unverified. A different review lists 72/78 complete in connection with a sleep-quality measure. That count cannot serve as an Epworth denominator. The trial comparison supplies no estimate for culinary corn oil itself.
