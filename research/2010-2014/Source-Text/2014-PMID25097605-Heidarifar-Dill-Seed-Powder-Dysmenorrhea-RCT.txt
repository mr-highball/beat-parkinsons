Title: Effect of Dill (Anethum graveolens) on the severity of primary dysmenorrhea in compared with mefenamic acid: A randomized, double-blind trial.
PMID: 25097605
PMCID: PMC4115348
First published: 2014
Authors: Heidarifar R, Mehran N, Heidari A, Tehran HA, Koohbor M, Mansourabad MK
Journal: Journal of research in medical sciences : the official journal of Isfahan University of Medical Sciences
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25097605/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Double-blind, randomized, placebo- and active-controlled trial (Heidarifar et al., 2014, J Res Med Sci, PMC4115348, IRCT201110205543N2) enrolling 75 female students (70 analyzed: n=23 ground dill seed powder, n=24 mefenamic acid, n=23 starch placebo) with moderate-to-severe primary dysmenorrhea. Intervention arm took two 500 mg capsules of ground Anethum graveolens seed powder every 12 hours (2,000 mg/day) for 5 days per cycle across 2 cycles. VAS pain dropped significantly from baseline at months 1 and 2 with dill seed (p<0.0001) and did not differ from mefenamic acid 250 mg q12h (p>0.05; Cochrane extraction PMC7387104: dill vs placebo MD -1.15 VAS points, 95% CI -2.22 to -0.08, n=46; vs NSAID MD 0.13, 95% CI -1.01 to 1.27, n=47). Side effects (2 menstrual bleeding changes, 1 GI discomfort) did not differ across groups (p=0.621). Limits: small sample, young dysmenorrhea cohort rather than Parkinson's musculoskeletal/neuropathic pain, Cochrane low/very low quality.
