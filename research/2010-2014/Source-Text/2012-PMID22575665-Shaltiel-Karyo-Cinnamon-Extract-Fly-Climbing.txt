Title: Cinnamon extract, alpha-synuclein aggregation and fly climbing
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22575665/
PMID: 22575665
DOI: 10.1016/j.bbagen.2012.04.021
Publication date: 2012-05-02
Date basis: NLM electronic publication May 2, 2012. Biochimica et Biophysica Acta 1820(10), 1628–1635, October issue.
Checked: 2026-11-25
Review depth: full original article in a publicly viewable copy, with identity and article text checked
Related conference: research/2010-2014/Source-Text/2012-ISFN-Eilat-Abstracts-Food-Scopes.txt, Shaltiel-Karyo and colleagues

The investigators tested a cinnamon extract precipitate in alpha-synuclein aggregation assays and a fly model with abnormal alpha-synuclein expression. The published article reports a shift in aggregate formation and more model flies successfully climbing after treatment. At the day-nine comparison in the text, 60% of untreated model flies and 78% of treated model flies climbed, compared with 87% of normal flies.

The related conference report has the same investigators, extract, aggregation readouts and fly-model comparison. Treat them as one underlying research family. The preparation is an isolated fraction from cinnamon. Climbing is a locomotor proxy. The records provide no ground cinnamon comparison for human Parkinson fatigue, pain, anxiety, apathy or olfactory impairment.
