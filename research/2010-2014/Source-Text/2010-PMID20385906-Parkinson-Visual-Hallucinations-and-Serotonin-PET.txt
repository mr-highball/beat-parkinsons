Title: Serotonin 2A receptors and visual hallucinations in Parkinson disease
Source: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/799964
PMID: 20385906
DOI: 10.1001/archneurol.2010.35
Publication date: 2010-04-12
Date basis: JAMA issue date
Source depth: Original publisher article including study methods, tables and results
Format: Factual digest of the primary report

This Toronto case-control study recruited seven nondemented Parkinson participants with formed visual hallucinations and seven age-matched Parkinson participants without them. The visual hallucination group retained insight and reported at least two months of symptoms. The investigators used the month-based hallucination subscale of the Neuropsychiatric Inventory for occurrence and severity. Neurological medications had been stable before study entry, but the hallucination group had greater motor severity and longer treatment duration.

The comparison measures serotonin 2A PET binding. It reports higher binding in ventral visual areas and several frontal and insular regions after modeling age, treatment duration and motor severity. This is a clinical association rather than a food or drug intervention. It provides no week-by-week visual episode trend after dried fig intake. The 2016 insula meta-analysis reused this published comparison.
