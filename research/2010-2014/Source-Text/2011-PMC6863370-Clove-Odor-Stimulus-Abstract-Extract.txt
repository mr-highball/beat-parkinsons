Woolley and colleagues report a randomized, double-blind intranasal oxytocin/placebo crossover involving 22 men with schizophrenia and eight healthy controls. Clove oil, anise oil and Lyral are odor-detection stimuli. Oxytocin improved detection of Lyral, without clear improvement for clove or anise in the reported comparison.

Clove is a genuine sensory exposure but is not the assigned oral treatment. Ground clove, Parkinson disease, and an ingredient-specific effect on pain, anxiety, sleepiness, apathy, falls or executive function are not tested. Social-cognition findings belong to the oxytocin comparison. A later study-family match remains unverified.
