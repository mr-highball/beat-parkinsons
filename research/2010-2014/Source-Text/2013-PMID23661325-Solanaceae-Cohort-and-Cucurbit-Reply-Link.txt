The population-based case-control study enrolled 490 newly diagnosed idiopathic Parkinson cases and 644 controls. Table 1 shows 486 and 636, respectively, for its main complete-case model. Its published results concern disease occurrence in relation to named nightshades and a broad comparison variable. Cantaloupe is absent from Table 1's enumerated other-vegetable variable. The companion reply, PMID 24122934, uses the same study to analyze a separate combined frequency of four cucurbits including cantaloupe. Its published estimate pools foods and concerns disease occurrence. The two reports belong to one observational family.

Original scope recheck, November 24, 2026

Original source scope
The main case-control model includes 486 Parkinson cases and 636 controls. Table 1 gives adjusted odds ratios per additional daily intake frequency of 0.83 (95% CI 0.56–1.24) for tomatoes and 0.77 (95% CI 0.41–1.44) for tomato juice.

Evidence boundary or continuing gap
The questionnaire captures habitual intake categories, not isolated cooked tomato, and the dependent variable is Parkinson diagnosis rather than rigidity severity. The analysis mentions absent sauce and salsa data. The prior cucurbit reply uses the same cohort.
