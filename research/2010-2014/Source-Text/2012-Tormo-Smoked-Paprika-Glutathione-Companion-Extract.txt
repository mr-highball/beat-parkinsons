2012 online / 2013 issue | Tormo et al. | Glutathione findings from smoked paprika consumption

Source: publisher methods and figures, DOI 10.1080/19476337.2012.700952. Published online July 13, 2012; journal issue 2013.

The report names the same healthy university male student population, mean age of 20.6 years, preparations and crossover intake schedule as Campillo et al. (2010), which it cites for the dietary protocol. It compares blood and urine taken before and after the smoked or unsmoked paprika intake periods. Available paired laboratory samples differ by outcome and preparation.

The report describes increased reduced glutathione and decreased oxidized glutathione in blood after the smoked preparation, additional measures of the glutathione cycle and enzyme gene expression, and urinary 1-hydroxypyrene. It contains no handwriting, gait, tremor, freezing, or speech measurement. Cohort description and protocol indicate a companion analysis.
