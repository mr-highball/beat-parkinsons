2014-08-04 — Harvey LR, Schulte J, Smith EA, Kleinschmidt K, Forrester MB, "Essential oil exposures reported to a poison system," abstract 120, 2014 North American Congress of Clinical Toxicology meeting, Clinical Toxicology 52(7):738, DOI 10.3109/15563650.2014.940163. Primary proceedings full PDF https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.3109/15563650.2014.940163 (abstract 120, printed p.738; indexed text shows published online August 4, 2014). The publicly accessible original is a conference abstract; no separate journal full report was identified by exact-title and author searches.

The authors reviewed 1,248 single-substance essential-oil exposures in children and adolescents aged 18 or younger reported to a statewide poison system, 2004–2013. They classified 234 as "allspice oil" exposures, most frequently oral irritation 29 (12%), cough 17 (7%) and vomiting 13 (6%). These are poison center exposure reports, not an exposure-controlled trial or population prevalence. The abstract does not identify Pimenta species, plant part, oil verification, routes or doses for those 234, Parkinson diagnoses, visual hallucination events, all neurologic symptoms, or hallucination counts. It would be incorrect to infer zero hallucinations from a list of three most frequent effects. It is also incorrect to attribute visual disturbances in the next abstract 121, a separate heroin-associated botulism case series, to allspice. The concentrated essential oil and pediatric exposure are not ground culinary fruit in Parkinson disease.

This secondary review's plant table gives Pimenta dioica, whole plant, a nonspecific neurotoxicity label, and cites its reference 19: DerMarderosian A and Beutler JA, The Review of Natural Products, Facts and Comparisons, 2002. That is a book citation, not a patient case or a statement of visual hallucination. The 2002 original entry and cited primary evidence have not been accessed; provenance, clinical event, dosage and identity underlying that label remain open. The same review's neuropsychiatric cases include a Datura inoxia seed case, a different species; do not transfer hallucinations to allspice.
