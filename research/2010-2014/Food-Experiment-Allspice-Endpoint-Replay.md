# Allspice preparation and endpoint search

## Eugenol odor during a manual Stroop test

Finkelmeyer and colleagues published the study on October 18, 2010. Twenty-five healthy volunteers performed a manual color-word Stroop task in separate eugenol and hydrogen sulfide runs, each rotating odor with ambient-air blocks. In the eugenol run, the effect of odor presence and its interaction with congruency were both reported as F<1. Hydrogen sulfide shortened responses to incongruent stimuli within its own run (p=.031). The eugenol result is an indirect human null for short inhalational exposure during this experimental task, with no Parkinson population, ground berry ingestion or clinical attention assessment. ## Acute speech loss in a clove oil and alcohol coexposure

The March 2012 clinical letter by Dyrbye and colleagues reports a 67-year-old man found confused and unable to speak following a clove oil and alcohol coexposure. The initial Glasgow Coma Scale components were E1, M3, and V1. The authors attributed the acute episode to the combined exposure and report full recovery. The correspondence PDF DOI identifies the full multi-letter correspondence item, and PMID 22417049 identifies this case specifically. The reported acute loss of speech occurred with depressed consciousness and combined intoxication. Parkinson speech intelligibility, a standardized speech measure, and ground allspice were outside this case. [Primary letter in publisher correspondence PDF, pages 365–366](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0310057X1204000223?download=true) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2012-Dyrbye-Clove-Oil-Alcohol-Speech-Case.txt)

## Allspice as an odor span test cue in rats

Davies and colleagues listed allspice among an array of dried household-spice scents in the rat odor span task. Rats responded to cups, and a food reward was delivered after correct choices. Pharmacological manipulations were selected NMDA and AMPA receptor antagonists, compared with vehicles. Allspice was a cue in the task, with no allspice-specific treatment arm, separately reported allspice effect, or culinary ingestion comparison.
