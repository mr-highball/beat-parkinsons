# Acute cumin seed extract and intestinal transit

## Aqueous cumin seed extract slowed charcoal progression in rats

Sahoo and colleagues tested oral aqueous *Cuminum cyminum* seed extract in acute antidiarrheal models. In Table 2, charcoal traversed 84.94% of small-intestinal length in the vehicle control and 62.19%, 60.09%, and 55.12% at 100, 250, and 500 mg/kg of extract, respectively (p < .001 versus control at each dose). Although the text calls the highest extract dose more effective than atropine, Table 2 shows less charcoal progression with atropine (49.48%) and no test of a direct contrast between those two arms. This transit direction differs from the 2001 study, which used ground spice in feed for six weeks and time to a marked stool. Neither setup measured gastric emptying, levodopa kinetics, or motor OFF time.
