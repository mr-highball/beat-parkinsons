# Pumpkin seed oil in early clinical blood-pressure and menopause studies

## Pumpkin seed oil and menopausal mood pain and blood pressure

Gossell-Williams and colleagues randomized 35 postmenopausal women to 2 g per day of *Cucurbita pepo* seed oil or wheat germ oil for 12 weeks. The paper gives the oil species but no extraction method. The study describes double-blinding and reports differently colored oil capsules. Symptom questionnaires were available for 18 of 21 pumpkin-oil recipients and 13 of 14 wheat-germ-oil recipients. In Table 3, the oil arm’s median depression item stayed at zero (IQR 0–1 at both visits, within-arm p = .364), while the comparator median rose from zero (IQR 0–0) to one (IQR 0–2, p = .009). The endpoint difference had p = .046. Median joint pain fell from two to one in the pumpkin arm (p = .030), with no clear endpoint difference from wheat germ oil (p = .663). The single-item scores were exploratory and the report does not adjust for the 18 symptom items. The paper also reports a decrease in resting diastolic pressure within the oil arm from 81.1 to 75.67 mmHg. It did not measure standing blood pressure change, orthostatic symptoms or Parkinson’s. [Primary article, Methods and Tables 2–3](https://www.styriangold.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/Pilot-study-on-PSO-suplement-2011.pdf) · [PubMed record and publication date](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21545273/)

## April 2010 pumpkin seed oil meeting report

Hyde and colleagues’ April 2010 Experimental Biology abstract reports 16 postmenopausal participants assigned to pumpkin seed oil and 10 to wheat germ oil, each at 2 g daily for 12 weeks. Its outcomes are fasting lipids. HDL increased within both arms, and LDL fell 13.42% within the pumpkin oil arm (p < .05). The abstract supplies no depression scores or identified participant overlap with the larger 2011 report. The shared research group, dosing and comparison suggest a related preliminary sample. The university’s annual report lists two additional 2010 and 2011 meeting presentations by the group. Their original text and cohort identities remain open. [Original FASEB abstract](https://faseb.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1096/fasebj.24.1_supplement.588.6) · [University annual report](https://uwispace.sta.uwi.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7c16ad27-ca29-4719-b462-3ced4af7c3ef/content)

## Premenopausal blood pressure registration

NCT02119429 first posted April 21, 2014 and records 50 enrolled premenopausal participants in a completed comparison of pumpkin seed oil versus wheat germ oil capsules. It lists systolic and diastolic blood pressure changes at 12 weeks, with no posted results or named orthostatic or mood outcome. Searches by accession, investigator and title did not identify a primary outcome publication. [Official registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02119429)

## Peanut oil vehicle in Parkinson animal experiments

Yuste and colleagues used injected peanut oil as the vehicle in sham and 6-OHDA-lesioned rats while investigating 7-nitroindazole and striatal dopamine signaling. The comparison does not isolate oral peanut oil. In a separate King’s College London dissertation, Tamara Hirsch’s marmoset priming experiment used subcutaneous peanut oil as the vehicle for the first four weeks, alongside oral levodopa. The investigators switched to saline and another inhibitor in weeks five to eight after injection-site sores developed. The study measured dyskinesia and composite motor disability. The sequential vehicle and drug changes prevent attribution of the observed outcomes to peanut oil itself.
