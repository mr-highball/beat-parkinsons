# Black pepper aroma, swallowing and salivation

## Poststroke swallowing rehabilitation and oil aroma

Park and colleagues assigned 20 poststroke patients to six weeks of rehabilitation with black pepper oil aroma or an exposure using distilled water. The report describes improvement in pharyngeal items of the Functional Dysphagia Scale in the pepper group. The English abstract reverses the group labels in places. Treatment identity is taken from the substances and the later team's description. It gives no Parkinson subgroup. [2010 journal record](https://journal.kci.go.kr/kjot/archive/articleView?artiId=ART001431481) · [Later authors' comparison](https://oak.go.kr/central/journallist/journaldetail.do?article_seq=22908)

## Registered geriatric aroma-patch study

UMIN000003918 was first posted July 16, 2010. It gives a target of 150 residents at three facilities and compares black pepper aroma sheets with odorless sheets. Endpoints include pneumonia, falls and cognition. The registry lists completion and no public results. It names no Parkinson subgroup. The recorded trial is distinct in design from the published 17-person sequential program using pepper, capsaicin and menthol. [UMIN trial record](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000004671) · [Sequential program](Food-Experiment-JSPEN-Followup.md#2010-combined-sensory-stimulation-and-pneumonia)

## Salivary measurements in healthy adults and xerostomia

The healthy-volunteer pilot randomized 16 young adults to pepper or another fragrance. Both groups had an approximately 4 mL rise in saliva at week four. A separate xerostomia interim report gives 31 enrolled and 22 analyzed. Mean gum-test change was −0.3 mL in eight pepper participants and +0.6 mL in 14 controls receiving a herbal fragrance. The report describes underenrollment. Neither experiment evaluates Parkinson symptoms. [Healthy pilot, p5](https://www.ncgg.go.jp/ncgg-kenkyu/documents/22/22xx-2.pdf#page=5) · [Xerostomia interim results, p9](https://www.ncgg.go.jp/ncgg-kenkyu/documents/23/22xx-2.pdf#page=9)

## Aroma patches in dementia and psychiatric inpatient samples

Okino and Iuchi reported ten people with dementia using a patch for one week. Saliva wetting and staple and side-dish consumption changes were inconclusive. A vitality score improved in the uncontrolled comparison. Ishibashi and colleagues describe seven psychiatric patients self-selecting capsaicin film or pepper aroma, with oral care and exercises. Six improved on the repeated saliva swallow test in the pooled sample and one was stable. Subjective symptoms changed little and no arm-specific swallowing result is given. Some investigators and the clinical setting overlap with the 2015 psychiatric report. Participant overlap is unresolved.
