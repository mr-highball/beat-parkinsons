# Black tea preparations and animal motor outcomes

## Ethanol black tea extract and haloperidol catalepsy

Ahmad Parwez and colleagues, October–December 2011. The study air-dried the tea sample, refluxed it with ethanol for 12 hours and evaporated the solvent to obtain a dry extract. The study assigned 49 Swiss albino mice across seven groups. It administered the 4 and 8 mg/kg extract doses orally before haloperidol for 12 days. On day 12, mean time in the imposed position was 237.14 s with haloperidol alone, 76.4 s with 4 mg/kg extract and 64.28 s with 8 mg/kg. Mean 10-minute photoactometer counts for the same groups were 107.83, 191.42 and 247.28. The paper marks both doses p < .001 versus haloperidol alone for these day-12 comparisons. These assays measure retention of a posed posture and frequency of spontaneous beam interruptions in a drug-induced extrapyramidal model. [Local primary study extract](Source-Text/RJPBCS2011-parwez-black-tea-catalepsy-extract.txt) · [Primary journal PDF](https://www.rjpbcs.com/pdf/2011_2%284%29/%5B108%5D.pdf)

Methods state each animal was used once and also describe assessment on days 4, 8 and 12. The tables show n = 7 on all three days for each of seven groups in a 49-mouse experiment. The article does not clarify how these statements fit together. The paper presents ordered group means at both extract doses without a reported pairwise dose comparison. The outcome and extract differ from both frozen clinical claims.

## Isolated theaflavin and narrow-beam crossing

Anandhan and colleagues, 2011 online and 2012 in print. Male C57BL/6 mice in a chronic MPTP/probenecid model received a water solution of purified theaflavin, 10 mg/kg orally by gavage for 35 days. At the end of treatment, lesion-model mice receiving theaflavin showed increased open-field movement and rearing (p < .05), longer rotarod retention, shorter narrow-beam crossing time and fewer slips (p < .05 for beam outcomes) compared with model animals receiving no theaflavin. Figure 3 describes mean and SD for six mice per group. Methods say twelve mice per group and report that all animals were used for behavioral tests. The article does not reconcile these denominators. Narrow-beam placement and crossing capture coordination and balance in this model. [Local primary study extract](Source-Text/PMID22138428-theaflavin-beam-motor-extract.txt) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22138428/) · [Primary article copy](https://www.academia.edu/108402980/Theaflavin_a_black_tea_polyphenol_protects_nigral_dopaminergic_neurons_against_chronic_MPTP_probenecid_induced_Parkinsons_disease)

A second 2012-online report from overlapping authors tested oral isolated theaflavin at 10 mg/kg in a closely matching chronic model and named catalepsy and akinesia as behavioral outcomes. The accessible primary abstract does not report the test contrasts or group estimates. Cohort independence remains unverified. [Primary record, PMID 22669749](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22669749/)

## Retracted subacute theaflavin report

Anandhan, Janakiraman and Manivasagam's 2012 subacute MPTP model paper originally described apparent open-field, rotarod and hang-test improvement after isolated theaflavin. The article was retracted and the reported measurements carry no evidentiary weight. [Original record, PMID 22634505](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22634505/) · [Retraction detail in the 2020–2024 bucket](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Black-Tea-Animal-Motor.md#retraction-of-subacute-theaflavin-model)

## Brewed black tea and open field crossings in healthy and fluoride exposed rats

El-lethey and Kamel allocated 20 male rats per group to water, fluoride, a brewed black-tea solution, or both for 12 weeks. Table 2 gives open-field behavior for ten per group. Horizontal crossings were 50.3 ± 3.93 with water and 46.9 ± 4.44 with tea alone, without a clear difference. The combined arm preserved some coordination measures after fluoride exposure. The source gives no individual tea consumption, Parkinson model or test of clinical movement slowness. The allocated and assessed denominators differ. ## Warm orange pekoe black tea and acute exploratory crossings

Ratnasooriya and colleagues tested a warm Sri Lankan orange-pekoe black tea brew in healthy male rats. In a 7.5-minute hole-board observation, mean movements across squares were 20.2 ± 2.3 with water and 29.0 ± 2.2, 31.2 ± 3.3 and 30.4 ± 2.4 with three tea doses. The source reports p < .05 versus water. The means were nonmonotonic despite a claimed dose relationship. The Methods assign tea to treated animals, although the abstract mistakenly calls all treatments water. Coordination tasks at the middle dose were inconclusive. The outcome reflects exploratory activity, with no Parkinson or bradykinesia measurement.
