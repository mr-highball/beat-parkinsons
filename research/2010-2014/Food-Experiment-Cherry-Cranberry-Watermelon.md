# Cherry preparations and human outcomes, 2010–2014

## Reconstituted freeze-dried cherries and self-reported mood

Garrido and colleagues’ 2012 crossover used a beverage reconstituted from a powder containing pitted freeze-dried sweet cherries, maltodextrin and ascorbic acid. The comparison was a commercial cherry-flavored drink. Thirty healthy participants were divided across three age groups. The investigators reported reduced anxiety and better ratings of selected mood or fitness items in some age groups. They did not use a validated Parkinson apathy, clinical fatigue or dystonia endpoint. The published study appears as a chapter in the first author’s thesis and is counted once. [Primary publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.exger.2012.05.003) · [University dissertation with the original paper](https://dehesa.unex.es/bitstream/10662/416/2/TDUEX_2012_Garrido_%C3%81lvarez.pdf)

## Cherry capsules and the cycling time trial

Clifford and colleagues’ December 2013 report tested capsules of a dried Montmorency cherry product against placebo and a pine bark plus bioflavonoid product in a small counterbalanced crossover with male cyclists or triathletes. The comparison across conditions on 20-km completion time was inconclusive (p = .115 in the institutional abstract). The faster final kilometer and higher final-segment power were reported for the pine bark blend. The study does not estimate subjective Parkinson fatigue or an effect of eating dried cherries. [Original institutional record and manuscript](https://researchportal.port.ac.uk/en/publications/the-influence-of-different-sources-of-polyphenols-on-submaximal-c/)
