# Sesame oil in a non-oral anxiety model

## Injected sesame oil and anxiety-like behavior

The December 2013 animal study compared acute intraperitoneal sesame oil with saline and measured elevated-plus-maze behavior. Injected oil decreased open-arm entries and open-arm time in the exposed rats. Prior antagonist administration partially attenuated the differences. The oil was a non-oral vehicle, and pressing, dietary exposure, and Parkinson disease were outside the comparison. The route-specific direction gives relevant competing context for food-oil studies.
