# Mustard seed and caraway research

## Caraway extract and seated blood pressure

Kazemipoor and colleagues randomized 70 physically active overweight or obese women to a steam-distilled aqueous extract of caraway fruit or placebo for 12 weeks. Sixty completed. In the 2013 weight report, systolic and diastolic blood pressure had no clear between-group changes. Blood pressure was measured after ten minutes seated. The 2014 safety report describes the same sample sizes, preparation, duration and two investigators and also reports no blood-pressure difference. These are treated as linked reports of one likely cohort. Standing pressure and lightheadedness were unmeasured. [2013 primary paper, Methods and Table 2](Source-Text/PMC3844184.txt) · [2014 safety report](https://doi.org/10.1002/ptr.5147)

## Mustard constituent and induced salivary secretion

Sobhan and colleagues found that allyl isothiocyanate perfused via the rat submandibular artery reduced carbachol-induced saliva secretion. The same experiment found that the capsaicin comparison increased secretion. The outcome was induced gland secretion in a laboratory preparation. The report does not measure saliva handling, drooling, or ground mustard ingestion in people with Parkinson’s. [Primary article abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23942896/)

## Caraway in a food allergy case

Chládková describes a 17-year-old with multiple food allergies and previous reactions to caraway. Oral burning, breathlessness, abdominal pain and collapse followed a meal containing caraway in curry seasoning. The clinical narrative does not measure swallowing performance. The mixed seasoning and allergic mechanism differ from the frozen ground-caraway and Parkinson’s dysphagia comparison. [Original Czech case, pp. 199–200](https://www.klinickafarmakologie.cz/pdfs/far/2011/04/09.pdf)

## White mustard in a yeast alpha-synuclein screen

Sohn and colleagues screened 270 commercially prepared water extracts using yeast expressing human alpha-synuclein. *Sinapis alba* was among 12 materials selected for follow-up on the basis of the initial growth assay. The authors identified five extracts with significant reductions in cytotoxicity during the follow-up screen, and white mustard was outside that group. Table 1 also reports initial growth for a perilla stem extract and no growth for the separately tested perilla leaf extract. These are model viability readouts. The available methods do not establish a culinary preparation, and the study reports no animal or human symptom outcome. [Institutional primary abstract](https://khu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/screening-herbal-medicines-for-the-recovery-of-alpha-synuclein-in/) · [Author-posted article, Table 1 and Figure 1](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257801302_Screening_herbal_medicines_for_the_recovery_of_alpha-synuclein-induced_Parkinson%27s_disease_model_of_yeast)
