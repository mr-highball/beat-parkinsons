# Food experiment choking evidence

## Oral intake after acute stroke in a Parkinson case

Kojima and colleagues presented case O-403 at the 17th Japanese Society of Metabolism and Clinical Nutrition meeting in January 2014. The indexed title reports that one person with Parkinson disease resumed oral intake after an acute cerebral infarct. The hospital’s August 2014 annual report identifies the presentation and describes the involvement of nutrition support and pressure-ulcer teams from acute through recovery care in the subtitle. The conference body is access restricted. The public sources identify no test food, diet progression, or swallowing outcome beyond resumption of oral intake. [Original meeting record, S196](https://mp.medicalonline.jp/products/detail.php?content_detail_key=cw3eiyou%2F2014%2F0017s1%2F573%2F5196-5196&content_kind=0) · [Hospital annual report, PDF p. 124](https://www.ishikaibyouin.or.jp/pdf/nenpo1.pdf#page=124)

## Clinical factors associated with swallowing symptoms in Milan

Cereda and colleagues published this chart study online October 6, 2014. It includes 6,462 people with complete data from the Milan Parkinson Institute between 2007 and January 2014. A questionnaire item identified symptomatic swallowing disturbances in 11.7% (95% CI 10.9%–12.5%). Longer disease duration and dementia were associated with swallowing symptoms in both gender strata. The later 600-person diet study recruited at the same institute and used a different clinical sampling design. The 2014 analysis helps identify possible confounders and supplies no dietary intake estimates. The degree of participant overlap is unknown. [Primary journal report](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S135380201400368X) · [Publication date and results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25456827/) · [Later food-intake analysis](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-MDS-Diet-Followup.md#food-intake-by-swallowing-status-in-the-italian-dietary-cohort)

## Obstructing foods in a Kameda hospital case series

Katata and colleagues’ 2010 conference report reviewed acute choking with respiratory deterioration in 15 adults admitted to Kameda General Hospital from April 2006 through March 2009. Five had Parkinson’s disease, including both people whose choking began in the hospital. The report lists four cases involving mochi, four sushi or sashimi, two mandarins, and one each involving beef, shiitake, cooked rice and pudding. One food was unidentified. Eight patients died or entered a vegetative state. The report estimates choking occurrence from admission denominators of 1.8% in Parkinson’s, 1.2% in dementia, 0.9% in psychiatric diagnoses and 0.1% in cerebrovascular diagnoses (Fisher P = .001). The abstract gives pooled food counts without linking individual foods or outcomes to diagnosis. It supplies neither the shiitake preparation nor a food-specific swallowing severity measure. [Primary conference abstract O-220, printed p306 and PDF p17](https://web.archive.org/web/20201201063326id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/25/1/25_1_290/_pdf/-char/ja#page=17)

## Food identity in the Japanese risk assessment

Figure 28 in the Food Safety Commission of Japan’s June 2010 risk assessment reproduces questionnaire rankings for 601 adults. The provisional English version calls a high-ranking food baked potato. Figure 28 of the Japanese original calls it 焼きいも, and Ogoshi’s author account discusses the corresponding cooked sweet potato. The ranking reports perceived swallowing difficulty in three age-and-residence groups and gives no Parkinson’s results. It concerns baked sweet potato. ## Food suffocation and Parkinson’s in national vital statistics

Kramarow, Warner, and Chen examined US death certificates from 2007–2010. They identified 2,214 deaths attributed to choking on food among people aged 65 and older. Figure 3 shows a positive association between Parkinson’s disease and food suffocation relative to expected co-occurrence on death certificates. The classification gives no food ingredient or preparation and can misclassify certificates that do not specify the obstructing substance. The later Wu analysis uses some of the same years. [Primary record, online September 3, 2013, PMID 24003082](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24003082/) · [Author-shared primary paper, Figure 3](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256425807_Food-related_choking_deaths_among_the_elderly) · [Later death-certificate analysis](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Choking-Followup.md#food-related-choking-in-us-death-certificates)

## Wafer and water boluses in a Parkinson endoscopy study

Gasparim and colleagues’ study, published online May 2, 2011, included 38 people with Parkinson’s and swallowing complaints and 38 controls without neurological or swallowing disorders. Each participant was offered 5 mL water, 5 mL water thickened with a starch-based product, and one-third of a wafer biscuit. Each bolus was offered twice and colored to aid endoscopic observation. The paper reports laryngeal penetration in nine Parkinson participants with water, six with thickened water, and one with the wafer. It reports tracheal aspiration in six Parkinson participants with water and none with the other tested consistencies. Tables 1–3 describe residue by consistency and location. These are observed swallowing responses to the named test foods and liquids. The study includes no potato. [Primary study, methods and results](https://www.scielo.br/j/aio/a/nct9VkGbVt54Mmh4PTg6H4R/?lang=pt)

## Family account of coughing during a baked potato meal

A family member reported that a person with Parkinson’s and existing swallowing difficulty sneezed while eating baked potato with chili, briefly coughed, and drank afterward. The account describes no further coughing that evening. The report provides no clinical confirmation of aspiration or obstruction, no comparison meal, and no measurement of change in swallowing severity. The publisher’s archived sitemap lists the exact question URL with a last-modified date of March 27, 2013. The question page’s relative age places the original exchange around 2010–2011, and its exact posting date is unavailable. [Original family account](https://www.justanswer.com/health/4jqb8-aunt-eating-bake-potato-chilli.html) · [Publisher’s archived question sitemap, shard 145](https://www.justanswer.com/sitemaps/com/sm-https-1-archivedquestion-145.xml.gz)

## Graham cracker and pudding in Florida stimulation assessments

Troche and colleagues’ March 21, 2014, publication describes 33 patients with idiopathic Parkinson’s who had videofluoroscopy before and after unilateral deep brain stimulation. Test boluses included thin liquids, pudding, pudding with graham cracker, and a barium tablet. Mean penetration–aspiration scores changed from 1.57 to 3.28 in the 14-person subthalamic group (p = .007) and from 3.79 to 3.84 in the 19-person pallidal group (p = .857). The groups differed in scores before surgery (p = .003). The report compares surgical targets, and Table 2 pools test boluses. It identifies no potato preparation. [Primary author manuscript, Methods and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4127154/) · [Publication date, PMID 24652582](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24652582/)

## Swallowing-related deaths in a Japanese Parkinson autopsy series

Matsumoto and colleagues’ 2014 report describes 16 postmortem examinations in people with Parkinson’s disease. Five deaths were attributed to aspiration pneumonia and three to asphyxia. Two of the asphyxia cases were identified at autopsy among people whose causes of death had been unclear. The publicly accessible abstract identifies no obstructing food. The individual case details and food preparation remain unverified. The journal lists May 31, 2014 for online publication and PubMed lists June 2, 2014. [Primary journal record](https://www.jns-journal.com/article/S0022-510X%2814%2900364-5/fulltext) · [Primary abstract, PMID 24928079](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24928079/)

## Lemon juice and cornstarch biscuit in serial swallowing assessments

Luchesi and colleagues’ report first appeared online in 2014 and in the January–February 2015 issue. The investigators followed 24 patients with idiopathic Parkinson’s and dysphagia in a Brazilian clinic. The clinical and endoscopic protocol offered colored lemon juice, water thickened to nectar, honey, and pudding consistencies, and half a cornstarch biscuit. At follow-up, ten participants improved on the Functional Oral Intake Scale, five had no change, and nine worsened. The clinic combined texture and volume recommendations with other swallowing interventions. The paper gives no separate outcomes by ingredient or potato preparation. [Primary article, Procedures and Results](https://www.scielo.br/j/bjorl/a/RR6QQ87H45zwLBJc5SRL6SP/?lang=en) · [Publication record, PMID 25450106](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25450106/)
## 2014 early Parkinson swallowing imaging in Lille

Danel Buhl and colleagues reported 62 people with Parkinson’s of less than five years’ duration in the December 2014 P195 conference abstract. None reported swallowing problems. Initial imaging showed oral-phase abnormalities in 69 percent, pharyngeal-phase abnormalities in 24 percent, and aspiration in 5 percent (95 percent CI 0–10 percent). In the 31 with two examinations, the earliest-phase impairment increased from 17 to 40 percent at two years. The publicly displayed abstract and paired table give no named food or bolus composition. The publisher lists a subscription for the PDF. A March 2012 report from several of the same investigators has a public issue entry, and a September 2016 paper identifies cold water and dilute barium. Participant overlap among these reports is unresolved. [2014 primary conference abstract and table](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/948164/tableaux/p195-etude-prospective-des-troubles-de-la-deglutit) · [2012 primary report metadata](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neurol.2012.01.279) · [2016 full publication and bolus protocol](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Choking-Followup.md#cold-tap-water-and-barium-in-early-parkinson-in-lille)

### Official original link and unresolved food attribution

The series identifies 15 acute choking events. Five patients had Parkinson disease and one implicated food was shiitake. Chronic aspiration pneumonia was excluded. The pooled results do not show which patient had shiitake, whether the mushroom was roasted, or a symptom difference attributable to it.
