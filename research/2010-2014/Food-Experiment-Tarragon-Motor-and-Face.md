# Tarragon motor and facial outcome follow-up

## Oral tarragon extract and hind limb nerve conduction in diet fed mice

Mice on a high-fat diet received the ethanolic tarragon extract PMI-5011. Table 2 reports 23-week sciatic motor conduction velocities of 51.0 ± 1.45 m/s for extract-treated high-fat mice and 45.9 ± 1.18 m/s for untreated high-fat mice (mean ± SEM). The extract group and untreated normal-chow groups had different diets. This is peripheral conduction in a neuropathy model. It does not record walking freezes or dysarthria.

Table 3 separately reports hypersensitivity to light touch and reduced sensitivity to heat and pressure in high-fat mice. After oral gavage with the ethanolic extract, mean light-touch withdrawal threshold was 1.20 g versus 0.76 g in untreated high-fat mice. Paw heat withdrawal latency was 11.4 versus 15.7 seconds, and pressure withdrawal threshold was 12.3 versus 15.2 g. These changes bring different assays toward normal chow values. They do not estimate spontaneous clinical pain after eating fresh tarragon.

## Tarragon extract and conduction in a separate diabetes model

A separate streptozotocin diabetic-mouse experiment with PMI-5011 measured peripheral motor and sensory conduction and sensory endpoints. The authors reported improvement in conduction deficits in extract-treated diabetic animals. The disease induction differs from the prior high-fat diet study. The intervention was an ethanolic herb extract, and the measurements concern peripheral neuropathy rather than freezing or dystonia.
