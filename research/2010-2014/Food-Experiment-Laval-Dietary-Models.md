# Laval dietary models, 2010–2014

## 2010 Fat-1 mice on a shared corn and safflower diet

Bousquet, Gué and colleagues first published online on November 29, 2010, before the February 2011 issue. They kept both Fat-1 mice and nontransgenic littermates on the same low-omega-3 feed, which contained 25 g/kg corn oil and 25 g/kg of the component labeled safflower in Table 1. The Fat-1 transgene raised the cortical omega-3 to omega-6 ratio by 28 percent. The investigators found no clear protection against MPTP-related dopamine loss, tyrosine hydroxylase measures, or nigral Nurr1 and dopamine transporter expression. The 92 percent rise reported for the high-omega-3 diet came from an earlier feeding experiment. The current study had a shared oil formulation, a genotype comparison, and tissue outcomes. [Journal article, Table 1 and Figures 3–7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3023546/) · [Earlier feeding experiment](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Laval-Dietary-Models.md#2007-mixed-dietary-oils-and-omega-3-powder-in-mptp-mice)

## 2011 safflower oil and lard in a high-fat mouse diet

Bousquet and colleagues first published online on September 21, 2011, before the January 2012 issue. Forty-nine mice received control or high-fat chow. Table 5-1 in Bousquet’s thesis gives 30 g/kg corn oil and 10 g/kg each of soybean and canola oil in control chow. The high-fat formulation omitted all three and contained 125 g/kg safflower oil and 135 g/kg lard. The intervention also changed the total fat, sucrose, and cholesterol content. The high-fat group gained weight and had more marked striatal dopamine and tyrosine hydroxylase depletion after MPTP. The study measured tissue and metabolic outcomes and did not isolate safflower oil, lard, refining grade, or a symptom. [Original report, PMID 21971528](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21971528/) · [Author’s thesis, Table 5-1, printed p. 191](https://corpus.ulaval.ca/bitstreams/fdc0f3c6-ed48-4386-a98c-77ef2c59796b/download#page=191)

## 2012 oral trehalose and sucrose after MPTP

Karl-Indel Gué’s 2012 Université Laval thesis incorporates the Fat-1 paper as chapter 2. Its separate chapter 3 gives two post-lesion trehalose studies. The second gave male mice trehalose, sucrose, or water by oral gavage daily for four weeks beginning two weeks after MPTP. The report gives about 40 percent striatal dopamine depletion across the MPTP groups, without measurable dopamine recovery attributable to trehalose. Nigral tyrosine hydroxylase cell counts showed no clear depletion in the MPTP-water control. The authors describe these limitations and report no motor tests. The preliminary experiment used injected trehalose and also had an inadequate lesion control. The indexed thesis filename `28680.pdf` comes from the retired Laval site.
