# Macadamia and olive oil in a healthy-volunteer transit study

## Markey dissertation results for the macadamia oil arm

Oonagh Markey submitted this University of Limerick thesis in November 2011. The institutional record dates its first online release to November 17, 2014. Chapter 6 reports the same 24-participant, three-group study as NCT01510223. Eight healthy adults in the macadamia arm received a daily milkshake containing 15.18 g macadamia nut oil and 74.82 g olive oil during the five-day high-fat phase between two lower-fat phases. The thesis identifies the supplier and does not specify the pressing method. [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01510223) · [University record](https://hdl.handle.net/10344/4174) · [University PDF](https://researchrepository.ul.ie/server/api/core/bitstreams/c742a440-440c-4812-95bf-881ac072c393/content#page=152)

Table 6.9 reports gastric-emptying half-times of 219 ± 39, 195 ± 26 and 201 ± 32 minutes across control, high-fat and deadaptation conditions in that arm. The transit table reports mouth-to-caecum times of 281 ± 71, 248 ± 65 and 276 ± 78 minutes in seven macadamia-arm participants with interpretable breath hydrogen. Before adjustment, there was no significant trial or trial-by-group result for either endpoint. Adjusted analyses found faster gastric emptying after high-fat feeding with data pooled across all three groups. The groups did not differ significantly in their response. These are healthy-adult transit measures through the stomach and small intestine. Parkinson symptoms and clinical constipation were outside the study. ## Earlier conference record for the hazelnut oil rat study

The first author’s institutional bibliography lists the team’s initial 2012 European urology conference communication on serum hormones and testis histology in rats fed canola or hazelnut oil. An accessible results text remains unavailable. This is the same study as the 2018 paper with the reported aggregate observation of behavior.
