# Edible oil evidence follow-up, 2010–2014

## Forum cases involving olive oil and constipation

An August 2011 Parkinson’s UK forum post describes one person with a reported Parkinson diagnosis and severe constipation whose bowel symptoms reportedly resolved a month after starting a mixture alongside vitamin B12 and probiotics. The author said some people in the broader informal group reported no benefit, with no count by symptom. A January 2012 proposed trial summary repeats the same case and identifies extra-virgin olive oil, rapeseed oil, fish oil, trehalose and ascorbyl palmitate in the mixture. The proposal describes 120 planned participants. No enrollment or results for that proposed study were located. The sponsor’s later CTRI record covers trigeminal neuralgia and a different intervention. [First public case account](https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/parkinsons-improvement/1919) · [Repeat case and proposed study](https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/food-additives-and-supplements-as-an-adjunctive-treatment-in-parkinsons-disease/3304) · [Separate sponsor registry record](https://ctri.nic.in/Clinicaltrials/showallp.php?EncHid=&mid1=22111&userName=Trigeminal+Neuralg)

An August 2014 contributor to a Parkinson’s UK constipation thread reported that constipation resolved and laxatives were no longer needed after changing several foods. The changes included adding olive oil of unspecified grade to a salad, removing wheat and replacing breakfast cereal with granola, nuts and seeds. The post gives no stool counts or verified clinical assessment. [Firsthand forum post](https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/constipation-and-parkinson/3694/20)

## Sesame seed oil pretreatment in a mouse model

Ahmad and colleagues first published online on November 17, 2011. Mice consumed a diet containing sesame seed oil for 15 days before a striatal 6-OHDA lesion. The authors reported higher dopamine and tyrosine hydroxylase and changes in oxidative and inflammatory markers relative to lesion controls. The accessible primary abstract describes tissue endpoints and does not report an estimate on a Parkinson symptom. It also gives no toasted or cold-pressed grade. [Primary report, PMID 22089932](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22089932/)

## Isolated sesamol in a rotenone rat model

Sonia Angeline and colleagues first published online on September 24, 2013. They studied separate sesamol and naringenin treatments after rotenone exposure and reported improved motor performance and changes in dopaminergic and stress-response markers compared with rotenone alone. This is a preclinical result for isolated sesamol, without a sesame oil contrast or a human symptom estimate. [Primary report, PMID 24070629](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24070629/)
