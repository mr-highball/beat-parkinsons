# Food and drug-response abstracts from the 2011 Japanese Society of Neurology meeting

These reports appear in the December 2011 proceedings, which J-STAGE published online on February 7, 2012. All three reports are on printed page 1260, the 61st PDF page. [Issue metadata](https://doi.org/10.5692/clinicalneurol.51.1199) · [Original page](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/clinicalneurol/51/12/51_12_1199/_pdf/-char/ja#page=61)

## Fragrant vinegar capsules with levodopa after gastrectomy

Yasuhiro Suzuki and colleagues described one person with stage V Parkinson disease after total gastrectomy. They compared fasting levodopa administration with and without three fragrant vinegar capsules (香酢). The original methods name 100 mg Menesit as the levodopa product. Peak levodopa concentration was unchanged, time to peak was 120 minutes without vinegar and 180 minutes with it, and the area under the concentration curve decreased slightly with vinegar. The authors report that UPDRS-III improved to 7 with vinegar and describe qualitative improvements in rigidity, akinesia and dysphagia. The abstract gives no starting UPDRS-III value, swallowing scale, capsule composition, or vinegar concentration. A single coadministration case gives no ordinary culinary vinegar effect. The authors cite a separate 1994 lemon juice series as rationale. [P1-224, original page](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/clinicalneurol/51/12/51_12_1199/_pdf/-char/ja#page=61) · [Hospital bibliography](https://asahikawa.hosp.go.jp/section/index02a.html) · [1994 lemon juice study](../1990-1994/Food-Experiment-JSN-Food-Abstracts.md#lemon-juice-with-levodopa-in-low-gastric-acid)

## Hasshou bean and acute motor response

Mayumi Sakata and colleagues examined plasma levodopa after Hasshou bean intake in four healthy men and compared Hasshou bean with levodopa plus a decarboxylase inhibitor in two Parkinson participants. The 11 g dose is stated for the healthy group. In the two Parkinson participants, the investigators reported higher peak plasma levodopa and about 80 minutes longer ON time after Hasshou bean, without worsening of dyskinesia. The abstract does not give the Parkinson group's bean preparation or dose, or individual outcome estimates. The same lead author later reported a seven-person crossover with roasted, ground Japanese *Mucuna pruriens*. Individual overlap is unknown. [P1-222, original page](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/clinicalneurol/51/12/51_12_1199/_pdf/-char/ja#page=61) · [2024 clinical study](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-East-Asia-Registry-Followup.md)

## Intravenous glucose in two snack-responsive patients

Yoshiaki Nakayama and colleagues selected two people with Parkinson disease who reported better symptoms after unspecified snacks during wearing off. During OFF periods the investigators administered intravenous glucose or saline under double blinding. After glucose, lower-limb end-of-dose dystonia disappeared on surface electromyography in one patient. Akinesia improved from 24 minutes after glucose in the other. The abstract does not report the glucose dose or case-level saline results. The tested intervention was intravenous, and the self-reports give no snack ingredients or measured oral-food effects. [P1-223, original page](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/clinicalneurol/51/12/51_12_1199/_pdf/-char/ja#page=61)
