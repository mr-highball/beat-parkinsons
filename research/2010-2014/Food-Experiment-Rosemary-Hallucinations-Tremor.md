# Dried rosemary powder in a cognitive crossover

Pengelly and colleagues first published August 30, 2011. Twenty-eight healthy older adults entered a randomized, blinded crossover with five single-dose sessions. Participants consumed tomato juice containing 0, 750, 1,500, 3,000 or 6,000 mg powdered dried rosemary leaves. The report identifies Turkish origin, authentication, and a 100% rosemary product; some doses exceeded customary dietary amounts. The study assessed cognitive performance and subjective alertness rather than rest tremor or visual hallucinations, and did not study Parkinson disease. It reports no serious adverse events but does not give a tremor-specific adverse-event result.
