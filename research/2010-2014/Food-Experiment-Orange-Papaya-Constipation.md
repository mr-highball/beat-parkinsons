# Orange Papaya Constipation

## Orange and papaya within combined care for Chagas megacolon

Leão and colleagues published this prospective study in the January–March 2011 issue. Twelve adults with Chagas megacolon underwent six weeks of pelvic physiotherapy and manual abdominal treatment alongside a daily unstrained drink containing a peeled, deseeded orange, papaya, prunes and cream. Constipation scores declined in 11 of 12 patients at six weeks (paired p<.0022) and in eight of 11 assessed at 12 months (paired p<.0100). During treatment seven reported softer stool and nine more frequent movements. Four reported flatulence or abdominal distension with the drink. The study had no untreated group or comparison separating orange, papaya, or the physical therapy. The population had Chagas megacolon and the fruit was blended. The [university thesis abstract](https://sites.uem.br/pcs/publicacoes/dissertacoes/EgleLeo.pdf) bears a 2010 cover date and reports the same sample, intervention and findings. A first public date for that abstract was not verified. It documents the same study. ## Pasteurized orange juice with and without levan

Niv and colleagues first published this trial July 4, 2012. The 48 healthy adults drank regular pasteurized orange juice (19) or sugar-reduced juice containing added levan (29) for eight weeks. In Table 3, the regular juice arm reports 9.37, 11.25 and 9.82 stools per week at baseline, week four and week eight. The levan juice arm reports 7.76, 9.09 and 8.37. The model gives p=.02 for time and p=.79 for the treatment-by-time interaction. Both groups received juice, and the formulations also differed in sugars. The study compares two products and does not isolate an orange juice effect. Raw whole fruit and Parkinson constipation severity were outside its design.
