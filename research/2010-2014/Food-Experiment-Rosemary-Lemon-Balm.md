# Rosemary and lemon balm evidence

## Rosemary oil blend and meridian massage in college students

Jeong and Choi reported a non-equivalent comparison of aroma massage and meridian massage in 38 college women with functional constipation in February 2011. The aroma treatment used lemon, lavender, rosemary and cypress oils. Both groups had lower constipation scores and more bowel movements over time. The design compared two forms of massage and leaves any effect of ground rosemary in Parkinson disease unresolved. [Original article and tables](https://jkan.or.kr/upload/pdf/jkan-41-26.pdf)

## Inhaled rosemary oil and seated autonomic measures

Sayorwan and colleagues published online in December 2012. In 20 healthy adults, the study compared inhaled rosemary essential oil with sweet almond oil as the carrier condition. The rosemary exposure increased systolic and diastolic pressure, heart rate and respiration in the reported acute comparison. The resting measurements provide no standing pressure change or lightheadedness data. [Original article](https://doi.org/10.3797/scipharm.1209-05) · [Local CC BY article text](Source-Text/PMC3700080.txt)

## Rosemary essential oil and primary hypotension

Fernández, Palomino and Frutos published online November 20, 2013, before the 2014 issue. They followed 32 Spanish pharmacy participants with primary hypotension for 72 weeks. Systolic and diastolic pressure rose during the reported treatment phase, and SF-36 physical and mental component scores improved. The accessible report does not give a randomized comparison or a posture-specific symptom estimate. Primary hypotension and concentrated oil are indirect evidence for fresh rosemary and lightheadedness on standing in Parkinson disease. [Original author abstract and journal locator](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24269249/)
