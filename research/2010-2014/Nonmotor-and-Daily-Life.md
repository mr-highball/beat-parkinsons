# Nonmotor and daily life

### 2010 — Impulse control disorders in the DOMINION study

In a cross-sectional study of 3,090 people with Parkinson’s, 13.6% met criteria for at least one assessed impulse control disorder. These disorders were more frequent among dopamine agonist users. The cross-sectional association provides a frequency estimate for the study sample and leaves individual causality to be assessed. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20457959/)

### 2010 — Sham-controlled trial of expiratory muscle training for swallowing

Troche and colleagues tested four weeks of active or sham expiratory muscle strength training. Sixty participants completed the intervention. Active training improved a videofluoroscopic measure of airway penetration and aspiration relative to sham. The short trial measured swallowing physiology and did not establish effects on pneumonia or hospitalization. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21098406/)

### 2011 — Cognitive behavioral therapy for depression in Parkinson’s

Dobkin and colleagues randomized 80 people with Parkinson’s and depression to individually delivered cognitive behavioral therapy or clinical monitoring. The therapy group showed a greater reduction in depressive symptoms over the 10-week treatment period. The study had brief follow-up and the monitoring group received less therapeutic contact. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21676990/)

### 2012 — Antidepressant trials in Parkinson’s disease

A double-blind trial of paroxetine, extended-release venlafaxine, and placebo reported greater improvement in depression scores with both active groups at 12 weeks, without measured motor worsening. It evaluated selected participants with depressive symptoms over short follow-up. [Primary source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22496199/)
