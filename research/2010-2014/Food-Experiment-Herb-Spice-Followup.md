# Herb and spice evidence follow-up, 2010–2014

## Sage and lemon balm infusion in a menstrual-pain study

IRCT2013012612278N1 was retrospectively registered November 30, 2013. The official record describes an unblinded, single-group design with no placebo and a target of 120. Its narrative identifies 50 selected participants who continued the two-herb intervention through follow-up. Kalvandi and colleagues’ summer 2014 paper reports mean menstrual-pain ratings of 6.30 before treatment and 3.94 and 3.24 in the first and second treatment cycles. The English abstract mentions sage alone in one methods sentence, while the official record, Persian report and article conclusion identify both sage and lemon balm. The comparison gives no sage-only estimate and concerns menstrual pain outside Parkinson’s. [Primary IRCT record](https://irct.ir/trial/12370) · [Journal record and full paper](https://sjh.umsha.ac.ir/article-1-89-en.html)

## Investigator account of the Newcastle sage pilot

Principal investigator Elaine Perry and coauthor Melanie-Jayne Howes first published their overview online on October 18, 2010. Its sage section describes a randomized pilot in 25 people with Parkinson’s and mild cognitive impairment, with cognitive improvements from baseline in both groups and no overall between-group cognitive benefit. Reference 51 cites a paper in preparation by Leung, Burn and Perry. The comparator is called placebo in the overview. The grant report identifies vitamin E as the administered comparator and describes an individual working-memory composite favoring that arm. The population, intervention and investigators link these accounts to one cohort. The overview gives no fatigue measurement. [Investigator account, sage section and reference 51](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6493900/) · [2007 protocol record](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#sage-cognitive-trial-registration) · [2019 public grant report](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#sage-cognitive-trial-results-attachment)

## Spanish sage essential oil and acute mental fatigue

Kennedy et al. first published their double-blind balanced crossover online on October 11, 2010. Thirty-six healthy adults took a single capsule of Spanish sage (*Salvia lavandulaefolia*) essential oil or an oil placebo in sessions one week apart. The cognitive task battery included a 100 mm visual analog rating for mental fatigue. The reported treatment-by-assessment interaction was F(1,748) = 5.90, p < .05, with an inconclusive treatment main effect (F(1,748) = 3.67, p < .10). Planned comparisons showed lower fatigue on three of six repetitions at one hour and five of six at four hours. Figure 4 reports the results as change from baseline during tasks. The study concerns an acute oil exposure in healthy people and leaves [0883, fresh culinary sage and Parkinson’s fatigue](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0883.txt) unresolved. [Publisher report and first publication date, Figure 4](https://doi.org/10.1177/0269881110385594) · [Investigator’s presentation with the fatigue curves](https://www.science-allemagne.fr/wp-content/uploads/2011/10/David-Kennedy-Essential-Nutrients.pdf)

## Fresh sage extract and exhaustion in an uncontrolled menopause study

The full report by Bommer, Klein and Suter appeared online May 16, 2011. The investigators enrolled 71 women at eight Swiss practices during 2008 and analyzed 69 in a single-arm study. Participants took tablets of a concentrated fresh common sage leaf extract for eight weeks. The Menopause Rating Scale physical and mental exhaustion item fell from a mean of 1.7 to 0.9 (reported change −0.8, SE 0.1, p < .0001). The item includes impaired memory and concentration as well as reduced performance. The open study supplies no comparison for change without the tablet and enrolled women with menopausal hot flushes. The same investigators first reported aggregate results in 2009. [Publisher record and date](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12325-011-0027-z) · [Full report, Tables 1 and 3](https://www.avogel.ca/download/Studies/First-Time-Proof-of-Sages-Tolerability-and-Efficacy-in-Menopausal-Women-Silvia-Bommer.pdf) · [2009 conference report](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#fresh-sage-extract-in-a-2009-menopause-conference-report)

## Botanical beverage and task-fatigue registration

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT02202629 on July 29, 2014. The registry specifies cognitive task performance and acute fatigue at one, three and six hours after a plant-extract beverage. The posted intervention field gives no separate sage arm. The registry still lists no summary results, and the 2020 publication identifies the active beverages as blends containing sage. [Official trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02202629) · [Local registry extract](Source-Text/Trials/NCT02202629.txt) · [2020 outcome report](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#sage-containing-beverages-and-acute-mental-fatigue)

## Oral cinnamon powder in an MPTP mouse model

Khasnavis and Pahan gave *Cinnamomum verum* powder by mouth to MPTP-exposed mice. The authors reported improved performance on open-field and rotarod tests along with protection of dopaminergic markers. These motor tasks give indirect context for ground cinnamon and walking slowness (`0889`). Clinical muscle rigidity (`0146`) and dyskinesia severity (`0962`) were outside the study design. [Primary report, first published June 20, 2014, PMID 24946862](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4167597/) · [2019 mouse follow-up](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#cinnamon-powder-and-astrocytic-gdnf-in-mice)

## Capsaicin aerosol in an early dysphagia case series

Diesener and Beck presented an observational series at the March 2013 DGD meeting. The investigators’ later slides describe 16 patients with neurogenic dysphagia and an elevated cough threshold assessed during 2009–2012 at two clinics, including the Parkinson center in Zihlschlacht. They describe using inhaled capsaicin to facilitate airway clearance and tracheostomy tube removal and report an association between sensory impairment on endoscopy and aspiration pneumonia. The available material gives no Parkinson-specific response count or controlled estimate. The preparation was an aerosol and the report is an airway clearance lead for [0724, ground cayenne and swallowing difficulty](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0724.txt). [Official 2013 program, R114, p. 59](https://www.dge-bv.de/files/media/2021/01/DGE-BV_hp_2013_web.pdf) · [2024 investigator presentation, slide 32](https://maik-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2024_MAIK_Kongress_Vortrag_Diesener.pdf#page=32)

## Red-pepper-seasoned cabbage and swallowing reflex timing

Shin’s July 2013 funder update reports capsaicin assays of three pepper-seasoned products, with detectable capsaicin in two. The update describes salivary sampling before and after a pepper-seasoned food in five healthy older and five healthy younger adults. The salivary analysis was pending, as was the subsequent swallowing-reflex study. [July 2013 primary funder status report](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/report/KAKENHI-PROJECT-23792740/237927402011hokoku/)

Shin’s July 2014 funder report records a 20-day pickled Napa cabbage intervention in 10 younger adults and two older groups, 10 with normal and seven with delayed swallowing reflexes. Within-group changes for both older groups were inconclusive. The age-defined groups and seasoned cabbage provide indirect evidence for [0724, ground cayenne and swallowing difficulty](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0724.txt). The later report used different analysis groups, and participant overlap remains uncertain. [July 2014 primary funder status report](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/report/KAKENHI-PROJECT-23792740/237927402012hokoku/) · [2015 analysis](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#swallowing-reflex-after-red-pepper-seasoned-cabbage)

## Ginger, temulawak, soy and shrimp shell versus diclofenac

Suryaningsih's 2013 randomized open-label, blinded-outcome study assigned 47 adults with knee osteoarthritis to capsules combining temulawak (*Curcuma xanthorrhiza*), ginger, soy and shrimp shell, or diclofenac, for two weeks. Joint-pain visual analog scores fell in both groups. The thesis abstract reports an inconclusive between-group difference (p > .05) and provides no complete arm-specific contrast. The comparison gives indirect evidence for grated ginger and Parkinson’s pain (`0725`). The combination does not isolate ginger and the shell and knee outcomes differ from boiled shrimp and falls (`0322`). [Primary institutional thesis abstract](https://etd.repository.ugm.ac.id/penelitian/detail/63578) · [Related comparison with meloxicam](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#ginger-temulawak-soy-and-shrimp-shell-versus-meloxicam)

## Daikenchuto with intravenous levodopa for fecal impaction

Ogawa et al. described a 69-year-old patient with Parkinson’s and longstanding constipation who developed fecal impaction and a malignant syndrome while taking prescribed antiparkinsonian medication. The authors reported improvement after treatment that included intravenous levodopa and oral dai-kenchu-tou. The formula contains processed ginger, Japanese pepper, ginseng, and maltose. The case leaves the separate effects of the formula and each component unresolved. It is near [0581, grated ginger and constipation](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0581.txt) and [0191, toasted Sichuan pepper and constipation](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0191.txt). The Japanese pepper is from a related *Zanthoxylum* species, and the exposure was a medicinal formula. [Case report, PMID 21773844](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10072-011-0710-5) · [National Cancer Institute formulation entry](https://www.cancer.gov/publications/dictionaries/cancer-drug/def/811069)

## Rikkunshi-to and gastric emptying

Doi et al. conducted an open pilot in 20 people with Parkinson’s. The authors reported shorter gastric emptying times after administration of rikkunshi-to, a multi-ingredient Kampo extract containing ginger. Fourteen participants had presented with constipation, but the trial abstract reports a gastric emptying result, which measures a different process. This is adjacent to [0581, grated ginger and constipation](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0581.txt). The study had no concurrent control and gives no effect estimate for ginger alone. [Primary report, PMID 24457529](https://doi.org/10.1159/000355608) · [Later crossover pilot](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#rikkunshito-and-gastrointestinal-symptoms)

The group’s 2013 Japanese report describes nine people with Parkinson’s who took Tsumura rikkunshi-to for three months. Table 3 gives median colonic transit of 80.4 hours before and 49.2 hours after treatment. Transit shortened in seven of eight participants with delayed transit at baseline and lengthened in the remaining delayed participant and the one without baseline delay. The small uncontrolled comparison measured transit and leaves the effects of grated ginger on constipation symptoms unresolved. Participant overlap with the 2014 English report is unknown, so these reports do not establish independent replication. [2013 primary report, pp. 300–305 and Table 3](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ans/50/4/50_225/_pdf) · [Article record at J-GLOBAL](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/en/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=201402261649404003)

## Eugenol as one component of olfactory training

Haehner et al. compared 35 people with Parkinson’s receiving 12 weeks of smell training with 35 historical controls. The training used four odorants, including eugenol for clove scent. The composite threshold, discrimination, and identification score favored training. The paper reports that eugenol detection threshold improved within the training group. The study used nonblinded, consecutively recruited groups and only measured response to the scent combination. Its endpoints concern olfaction, which is outside the frozen ground-clove endpoints. [Local primary full text](Source-Text/PMC3629137.txt#L109) · [Primary report, Methods and Results, PMID 23613901](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0061680) · [Later randomized study assessing anxiety](../2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#eugenol-as-one-component-of-olfactory-training)

## Turmeric in ZAZ2 granules

Pan et al. randomized 115 people with Parkinson’s to a 14-ingredient soluble granule containing turmeric (*Curcuma longa*) or a comparison granule containing five different botanicals. Of these participants, 110 completed the 13-week double-blind study. At week 13, the authors reported lower composite UPDRS Parts II and IV and lower total scores with ZAZ2. The week-13 between-group difference on Part III was statistically inconclusive. Table 3 reports exploratory secondary symptoms. At week 13, mean headache scores were 0.63 with ZAZ2 and 0.99 with the comparison granule, from baseline means of 0.92 and 1.03. One participant discontinued ZAZ2 because of its bitter taste. The aggregate motor results and headache scores give indirect evidence for [0507, ground turmeric and bradykinesia](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0507.txt) and [0812, ground turmeric and pain](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0812.txt). The comparison cannot isolate turmeric or estimate the effect of eating it ground or grated. The abstract omits the turmeric exposure and the individual symptom results. [Primary report, sections 2.2–3 and Tables 2–3, PMID 21687764](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3109418/)

## Oral piperine and swallowing physiology

Rofes et al. compared nectar boluses with and without isolated piperine by videofluoroscopy in 40 adults with oropharyngeal dysphagia. The authors reported fewer signs of impaired swallowing safety with the higher-concentration bolus. A later participant-data reanalysis identified neurodegenerative disease in 20% of the lower-concentration piperine group and none in the higher-concentration group. It supplied no Parkinson-specific results. This is a formulation and population lead for [0124, cracked black pepper and swallowing difficulty](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0124.txt). [Primary comparison, PMID 24326980](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24326980/) · [Reanalysis, Table 1 and references, PMID 36142680](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms231810773) The June 28, 2011 registration NCT01383694 lists the same 40 participants and 1 mM and 150 µM piperine comparisons in an aging or neurological dysphagia population. It gives no diagnosis-specific counts or posted summary results. [Primary registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01383694)

## Isolated carnosic acid and locomotor behavior in rats

Wu et al. reported in November 2014 that carnosic acid, a compound from rosemary, increased time and distance moved and reduced apomorphine-induced rotations in 6-OHDA-lesioned rats treated before the lesion. Movement time, distance and drug-induced rotations are indirect for walking speed and clinical balance. The isolated pretreatment leaves [0089, ground rosemary and walking slowness](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0089.txt) and [0733, fresh rosemary and postural instability](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0733.txt) unresolved. [Primary report, Figure 1, PMID 25446857](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cbi.2014.11.011)

## Anxiety measures in a Parkinson and depression formula trial

The alternate publisher record identifies article resource 9890867 and document version 9890868. Its public HTML contains the abstract, identifies the article as closed access, and supplies no intervention ingredient lists. Publisher metadata points to legacy PDF record 8908. The download timed out when checked, leaving the full prescription and numerical anxiety tables open. The journal's issue index and the separate 2014 thesis citation corroborate the publication and thesis identities. The thesis formula remains unverified. [Alternate publisher record](https://jzyl.publish.founderss.cn/zh/article/doi/10.3969/j.issn.2095-6606.2014.06.006/) · [2019 review bibliography, reference 19](https://pdf.hanspub.org/hjmce20190400000_77529702.pdf).

Yuan and colleagues randomly assigned 40 people with Parkinson’s and depression to two traditional treatment strategies alongside usual medication. The abstract reports greater declines on the Hamilton Anxiety Rating Scale with the kidney-tonifying and liver-regulating strategy at one and two months. It also reports a larger decline on the self-rated anxiety scale at one month. The abstract gives no numerical anxiety scores or ingredient lists. The full report and the related thesis were unavailable through the public routes checked, leaving ginger exposure unverified. [Primary journal abstract, 2014, volume 21, issue 6, pp. 19–22](https://xblc.bucm.edu.cn/zh/issue/2014/6/) · [Ginger formulation search](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/followup/ginger-gait-dyskinesia-anxiety-recheck.md#anxiety-and-formulation-checks)
