# Coffee consumption and time to dyskinesia in CALM-PD

Wills et al. analyzed self-reported caffeine intake in the CALM-PD trial and observational extension. The investigators adjusted for age, baseline Parkinson’s severity, site and initial pramipexole or levodopa treatment. Compared with the group consuming less than 4 ounces of coffee per day, the adjusted hazard ratio for developing dyskinesia was 0.73 (95% CI 0.46–1.15) for 4–12 ounces and 0.61 (95% CI 0.37–1.01) for more than 12 ounces. Both confidence intervals include 1. The participants’ caffeine intake was observational, and the outcome was onset of dyskinesia. The analysis supplies no fatigue estimate for the brewed coffee statement [0121](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0121.txt). [Primary report, PMID 23339054](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.25319) · [PubMed Central full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3608707/)

## Encapsulated caffeine and fatigue severity

Postuma and colleagues randomized 61 people with Parkinson’s and daytime somnolence to encapsulated caffeine or placebo in NCT00459420. The secondary Fatigue Severity Scale contrast was −3.08 points at week three (95% credible interval −6.88 to 0.73) and −2.85 at week six (−7.73 to 2.06). The point estimates favor lower fatigue with caffeine. Both intervals also allow worsening, and the trial used isolated caffeine. It supplies indirect context for [0121, brewed coffee and fatigue](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0121.txt) and [0948, brewed green tea and fatigue](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0948.txt). [Primary report, PMID 22855866, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3414662/) · [Accessible primary PDF](https://scispace.com/pdf/caffeine-for-treatment-of-parkinson-disease-a-randomized-xu5z2dqvp1.pdf) · [RCT registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00459420)

## Fatigue measurement in the caffeine dose-escalation pilot

NCT01190735 was first posted August 30, 2010. The single-group registry lists 28 actual enrollees and Fatigue Severity Scale at six weeks as a secondary outcome. The protocol supplied synthetic caffeine tablets while allowing habitual caffeine-containing drinks on the existing schedule. The registry cites the separate somnolence RCT, NCT00459420, and reports no posted results for the pilot. [Primary trial registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01190735) · [Registry API fields](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT01190735)

Altman and colleagues’ September 27, 2011 paper reports baseline data for 25 people with Parkinson’s in the uncontrolled dose escalation. Table 1 gives a mean Fatigue Severity Scale score of 35.72 (SD 12.47). The main text cites Supporting Table 1 in the secondary results and gives change estimates for sleepiness and sleep quality. The fatigue change remains unavailable, including after checks of OpenAlex, Crossref, publisher routes and the Wayback availability service. Attrition from intolerance and the single-group design would limit any recovered fatigue result. [Primary report, PMID 21953603](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.23873) · [Accessible main article, pp. 2428–2429](https://cafeesaude.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/Parkinson-R-D-Altman-et-al-2011-Movement-Disorders.pdf#page=2) · [Supplement access audit](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/followup/source-text-and-supplement-recheck.md#coffee-and-fatigue-access-recheck)

## Soluble coffee and acute mental fatigue in healthy older adults

Cropley and colleagues’ July 20, 2011 online report analyzed 39 healthy older adults in a double-blind randomized crossover. Each visit used 6 g of soluble caffeinated coffee (167 mg caffeine), regular decaf (5 mg caffeine and 224 mg chlorogenic acids), high-chlorogenic-acid decaf (11 mg caffeine and 521 mg chlorogenic acids), or coffee-flavored and colored maltodextrin in 300 mL hot water. Treatment visits were at least one week apart and post-dose testing started at 40 minutes. Table 3 reports mean changes on the Caffeine Research VAS mental-fatigue item of −6.20 with caffeinated soluble coffee, +13.30 with placebo, +9.24 with regular decaf and +1.40 with enriched decaf. The exploratory caffeinated comparisons favored lower mental fatigue versus placebo (reported p < 0.001) and regular decaf (Table 3 p < 0.01). The enriched versus regular decaf comparison was unclear (p = 0.074). The authors report multiple comparisons without Bonferroni adjustment. This provides indirect evidence for [0121, brewed coffee and Parkinson fatigue](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0121.txt). [Primary full article, Table 3 and Figure 2](https://www.academia.edu/121106505/Does_coffee_enriched_with_chlorogenic_acids_improve_mood_and_cognition_after_acute_administration_in_healthy_elderly_A_pilot_study) · [Publication date, PMID 21773723](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21773723/) · [2009 registry entry](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#soluble-coffee-and-mood-crossover-registration)

## Soluble decaffeinated coffee and chlorogenic acid crossover in healthy older adults

ANZCTR retrospectively registered `ACTRN12611000067976` on January 19, 2011. It specifies separate tiredness and mental-fatigue visual analogue items as secondary outcomes and describes microcellulose placebo. Camfield and colleagues’ December 9, 2013 paper reports flavored and colored maltodextrin plus rice powder as the placebo actually used. It reports 65 healthy adults aged 50 or older randomized, of whom 60 completed a double-blind acute crossover of 6 g soluble decaffeinated green and roasted coffee blend, a 540 mg nominal chlorogenic acid dose in the placebo matrix, or placebo, dissolved in 300 mL boiled water. Table 2 gives 532 mg and 530 mg chlorogenic acids per cup for the blend and extract arms, respectively. There was no caffeinated coffee arm or regular-decaf arm. [Primary registration](https://anzctr.org.au/ACTRN12611000067976.aspx) · [Primary report, PMID 24349389](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3857311/) · [Publisher PDF, Methods and Table 2](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0082897&type=printable)

The Results report no clear mental-fatigue treatment effect or treatment-by-time interaction at the pre- or post-cognitive testing assessments around 40 and 120 minutes. The Discussion mentions an exploratory trend toward greater mental fatigue with isolated chlorogenic acids versus the blend at 120 minutes. On the separate tiredness item, isolated chlorogenic acids had lower post-testing ratings than placebo at 40 minutes (p = 0.04, d = 0.46). The trial follows up the pilot’s decaf question with different comparators and supplies no estimate for brewed coffee and fatigue in people with Parkinson’s. ## Coffee history and fatigue measures in ParkWest

Skeie et al.’s 2010 report includes 212 people with incident Parkinson’s and 175 controls. The mean coffee intake comparison matches the earlier poster (3.3 ± 1.8 versus 3.8 ± 2.0 cups/day, p = .02). The article abstract reports p = .007 for coffee in a diagnosis model with alcohol and smoking. It gives no brewed preparation or fatigue association. Herlofson et al.’s 2012 report measured Fatigue Severity Scale scores at baseline in the same ParkWest study. These publications establish that ParkWest collected both measures, with no public linked estimate located for [0121](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0121.txt). [2010 primary article abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20669310/) · [2012 primary fatigue report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22340430/) · [2009 poster](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#reported-coffee-in-the-early-parkwest-cohort) · [2020 fatigue follow-up](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Beverage-Fatigue-Followup.md#longitudinal-fatigue-in-parkwest)

## UK Biobank brewed coffee and tiredness fields

The UK Biobank codebook dates its tiredness field to January 2012 and its separate 24-hour recall fields for filter/americano/cafetière and espresso to September 2012. Coffee recall collection ran from April 2009 to June 2012 across five instances. Field 100240 identifies respondents who reported drinking coffee the previous day and determines who receives the preparation questions. Field 2080 records frequency of general tiredness or lethargy over the prior two weeks at assessment visits, a different outcome from the Parkinson Fatigue Severity Scale. The later G20 first-recorded date field, debuted July 2019, can help establish timing for an approved analysis. The public codebook shows separate field counts. How many participants have both a postdiagnosis diet recall and a nearby tiredness response remains unknown. [Filter/americano/cafetière, 100270](https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/showcase/field.cgi?id=100270) · [Espresso, 100310](https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/showcase/field.cgi?id=100310) · [Coffee recall routing, 100240](https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/showcase/field.cgi?id=100240) · [Tiredness, 2080](https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/showcase/field.cgi?id=2080) · [First recorded G20, 131022](https://biobank.ndph.ox.ac.uk/showcase/field.cgi?id=131022)

## Habitual coffee and hemodialysis fatigue

Nikić and colleagues studied 86 adults on hemodialysis in Serbia. Sixty-seven reported habitual black coffee and 19 reported no coffee use. Table 2 reports mean FACIT fatigue scores of 30.1 (SD 10.2) and 29.2 (SD 11.6), respectively, p=.745. Higher scores indicate less fatigue. Coffee intake was self-reported. The cross-sectional comparison leaves confounding and the effect of brewed coffee in Parkinson’s unresolved. ## Registered fatigue item in the longer Café-PD trial

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted the Café-PD record on November 30, 2012. It names fatigue among planned MDS-UPDRS items in a Parkinson caffeine capsule versus placebo trial. No registry results are posted. The registry reports actual enrollment of 119, while the linked article reports 121 randomized. The article gives a separate daytime-somnolence result but no fatigue-specific comparison in the main publication. Original item results remain an open lead.
