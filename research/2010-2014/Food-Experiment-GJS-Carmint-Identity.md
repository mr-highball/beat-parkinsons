# Two distinct IBS product families

The earlier Carmint trial tests lemon balm, spearmint and coriander alongside subtype-specific conventional treatment. The GJS protocol instead lists 13 botanical rows and combines a manufactured water extract with active or placebo probiotics. The protocol names Agastache and Perilla and contains none of the Carmint trio. The 2013 report cites that protocol. [Existing Carmint original](../2005-2009/Source-Text/2006-Vejdani-Carmint-Abdominal-Pain-Extract.txt) · [GJS protocol](Source-Text/2011-PMID21978382-Ko-GJS-Protocol-Extract.txt) · [GJS results](Source-Text/2013-PMID24381638-Ko-GJS-IBS-Extract.txt)

The GJS paper reports 53 randomized and 48 completing, while the registry's actual enrollment is 48. The registry lists 11 ingredient entries, omitting the protocol's ginger and jujube entries. Those reporting differences do not prove a change in administered composition. Responder definitions also differ. Weekly symptoms, quality of life and dysphoria comparisons remain distinct from the reported responder proportions. [Registry and discrepancies](Source-Text/Trials/NCT01342718.txt)

The later review abstract places GJS and the Carmint trio together with ambiguous punctuation. A compressed list or omitted product name is plausible, but the intended mapping is unverified. The original products provide no basis for assigning the Carmint ingredients to GJS or for counting a new Melissa depression cohort.
