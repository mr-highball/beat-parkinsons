# Early pomegranate-related adverse event source trails

## Tacrolimus case and unresolved original methods

Khuu, Hickey and Deng's heart transplant case first published online November 30, 2012. The accessible PubMed and Elsevier metadata identify a pomegranate-containing product and a potential tacrolimus interaction. The indexed record has no abstract. The publisher denied the article body, the metadata API supplied no body and the targeted exact article archive queries found no snapshots. Secondary reports describe pomegranate concentrate in popsicles. Those accounts leave original preparation, event dates and neurological symptoms unverified. The 2012 ISHLT program lists a poster with overlapping authors and subject. Its patient and results cannot be matched to the journal case from the title alone. [Primary publication metadata](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23200637/) · [Meeting program copy, poster 841](https://studyres.com/doc/7908121/2012-final-program-pdf---the-international-society-for-heart)

## Hallucinations and an adulterated mixed product

Singapore's Health Sciences Authority identified hallucinations after use of a mixed slimming product in an alert dated December 10, 2014. The product label listed pomegranate. Authority testing identified undeclared sibutramine, benzyl sibutramine and phenolphthalein. Confirmed pomegranate exposure, visual subtype, raw arils and Parkinson disease were unreported. The 2015 bulletin gives a closely resembling auditory-hallucination case, with family identity uncertain.
