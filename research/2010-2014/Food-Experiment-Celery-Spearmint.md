# Food experiment evidence for celery leaf and spearmint

## 2010 Gum chewing and saliva swallowing in Parkinson’s

South, Somers and Jog studied 20 people with Hoehn and Yahr stage 2–4 Parkinson’s without significant meal-related dysphagia. During gum chewing, swallow frequency rose and latency decreased relative to the baseline period. The accessible original abstract does not specify the gum species or flavor and gives no separate clinical drooling rating. The result provides Parkinson swallowing context for 0952. [Primary report, PMID 20385891](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0b013e3181d9002b)

## 2012 Spearmint oil exposure and oral contact allergy

Gunatheesan and colleagues reviewed 1,467 people patch tested for spearmint oil. Seventy-three had positive patch tests, 19 were judged clinically relevant given use of spearmint oil products, and 14 of those 19 had erosive oral lichen planus. The original abstract describes oral lichen planus as a painful condition. It reports contact allergy and product history without a comparative pain outcome from fresh leaves. This gives adverse oral-pain context for 0728. [Primary report, PMID 22686635](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22686635/)

## 2014 Brewed spearmint and walking distance in knee osteoarthritis

Connelly and colleagues randomized 62 adults with knee osteoarthritis to commercial spearmint tea or high-rosmarinic-acid spearmint tea and analyzed 46. There was no significant six-minute walking test difference. The high-rosmarinic-acid group walked 22 m farther at week 16 than at its own baseline, a nonsignificant change. Knee pain improved relative to baseline in the high-rosmarinic-acid group, without a significant between-arm contrast reported. Both groups received brewed spearmint. This is a close preparation match for 0040 and a clinical pain lead for 0728, with no Parkinson sample or stride length result for 0525. [Primary article, Figures 1–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4259186/) · [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01380015)

## 2013 Supermint drops and pain during pediatric colonoscopy

Najafi and colleagues report that 51 children who received Supermint drops had mean procedural pain of 4.20 versus 5.60 in 50 controls who received no study preparation (Table 1, p = .01). The English abstract also reports fewer and shorter abdominal pain episodes. The paper identifies Barij Essence as the manufacturer and uses peppermint in its English title and text. The botanical identity of the study lot is unresolved. This is a procedure-specific comparator relevant to 0728, with a different preparation, population and outcome. [Primary full report, Table 1](https://journal.nkums.ac.ir/files/site1/user_files_358a93/nabavi1983-A-10-25-67-257cfed.pdf)

## Homeopathic celery preparation and a hoarseness report

The Central Council for Research in Homoeopathy's 2012–2013 annual report describes 6C and 30C Apium graveolens preparations in a randomized, double-blind proving in healthy volunteers. One prover in its reported symptom table had hoarseness with throat pain, tearing and dry cough. Group-specific counts and measured vocal loudness were unavailable. The fresh-leaf identity is unverified. The placement follows the report's stated period because its first online date is unverified. The 2014–2015 report from the same centers repeats related symptoms, with individual overlap undetermined. [CCRH annual report and Table 4.1](https://ccrhindia.ayush.gov.in/sites/default/files/site/default/files/annual_reports/Annual-report2012-23.pdf) · [Local text](Source-Text/2012-2013-CCRH-Apium-Hoarseness-Factual-Extract.txt)

## 2011 spearmint gum comparison and salivary flow timing

Karami-Nogourani and colleagues studied five sugar-free gum flavors in 15 dental students over six days. Collected saliva increased while subjects chewed compared with unstimulated samples. This report describes an early peak across flavors and no significant flavor differences in the three-to-six-minute window. Cinnamon and spearmint flavors raised saliva pH. A 2012 report shares the volunteer count, scheduled windows, named flavors and three authors, but describes a later spearmint flow peak. The reports may overlap. Neither provides a dried-leaf exposure, Parkinson cohort or a measure of lost saliva. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3556288/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2011-12-Karami-Spearmint-Gum-Flow.txt)

## 2012 spearmint gum and measured six-minute saliva production

In the 2012 Karami Nogourani report, mean salivary flow for the spearmint-flavored gum condition rose from 0.85 to 4.03 mL/min, a reported paired difference of 3.18 mL/min with 95 percent confidence interval 2.65 to 3.70. The reported six-minute comparison among flavors had p = .091. The study did not include an unflavored-gum condition. Its later spearmint peak differs from the 2011 description, while its team and design may overlap the earlier report. The source gives no authenticated spearmint leaf exposure or clinical drooling count.
