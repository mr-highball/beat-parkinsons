# Cinnamon and sesame oil evidence

## Cinnamon capsules and blood pressure in type 2 diabetes

Akilen and colleagues first published online in 2010. In their placebo-controlled trial, mean systolic and diastolic pressure changed from 132.6 to 129.2 and 85.2 to 80.2 mm Hg with cinnamon, respectively. Placebo changed from 134.5 to 134.9 and 86.8 to 86.1. The report concerns hypertension-related measures in adults with type 2 diabetes. It gives no estimate for lightheadedness or change in pressure on standing. [Original report](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1464-5491.2010.03079.x)

## Cinnamon and anxiety during a laboratory stressor

Kontnik, Marcus and Birkett reported the placebo capsule comparison at the 2011 International Behavioral Neuroscience Society meeting. Healthy adults took cinnamon capsules before a laboratory stress task. The group comparison on change in state anxiety gave p = .053 and the visual-analogue comparison reported a sex effect. An adjacent report from the same laboratory found no clear attenuation of heart rate, electrodermal response or blood pressure during the stressor. A third abstract in the same program reported no clear group difference in euphoria, sedation or stimulant ratings. Its dysphoria/psychotomimetic comparison gave p = .059, and the report does not identify hallucinations as a measured outcome. The abstracts describe different measures under similar conditions and may share participants. [Meeting abstracts 20–22, printed p. 40](https://www.ibnsconnect.org/assets/docs/pastprograms/ibnsprogram2011final.pdf)

## Ground cinnamon and post-meal supine pressure

The 2011 crossover randomized nine healthy participants to two breakfasts with powdered Cinnamomum zeylanicum spice capsules or flour placebo. Post-meal brachial pressures were measured in triplicate while supine. The report gives an inconclusive time-by-supplement comparison for systolic and diastolic pressure. No paired standing measure or lightheadedness result was collected. The authors identify NCT01350284. ## Registered natural flavouring and post-meal physiology trial

The registry first posted May 9, 2011. It covers natural food flavourings, post-meal physiology and gastric emptying. The linked cinnamon crossover publication supplies the actual powdered spice, comparator and supine-pressure protocol. The registry was Completed and had no posted tabular results at this check. This is the same crossover as the published meal result. ## Short-term pressure after a ground cassia beverage

The May 2013 thesis reports 26 adults aged 40–65 who completed beverage comparisons with and without ground cassia cinnamon. Blood pressure was compared at baseline and repeatedly over four hours. No time point showed a clear between-condition difference. The study did not report an active-standing challenge or a Parkinson lightheadedness outcome. A 2013 conference abstract has the same title and overlapping investigators.
