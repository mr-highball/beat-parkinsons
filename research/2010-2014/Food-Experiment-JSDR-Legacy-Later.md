# Primary sources traced from Japanese swallowing proceedings and grant reports

The 2010 Japanese Society of Dysphagia Rehabilitation meeting took place September 3–4 in Niigata. The MedicalOnline listing exposes titles, author lists and pages. Its linked abstract bodies require access. Primary publications and grant reports independently establish the preparations and results summarized below. [2010 issue](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/archive/search?issue=3&jo=cb5dvsph&vo=14&ye=2010) · [Society meeting archive](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/meeting/meeting_annual.html)

## 2010 respiration and thin barium in Parkinson

Troche and colleagues studied 39 people with Parkinson’s. Participants swallowed thin liquid barium in ten measured 5 mL portions and in a sequential cup-drinking task. The study used the cup-drinking task to rate penetration or aspiration. Participants with higher scores were more likely to inhale after a swallow and had shorter swallowing apnea. The July 11, 2010 online publication and the December Japanese listing share the English title and five original authors. This measures respiratory coordination and airway invasion with a contrast liquid. [PubMed, PMID 20623304](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20623304/) · [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3762686/) · [Japanese listing, p. 667](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2010/001403/726&name=0667-0667j)

## 2010 tongue pressure and barium gelatin jelly in Parkinson

Umemoto and colleagues tested 30 people with Parkinson’s during videofluoroscopy using a standardized 5 mL barium gelatin jelly. The Hoehn and Yahr II–III group had higher tongue pressure (P = .047) and shorter oral–pharyngeal transit (P = .045) than the IV–V group. Faster tongue movement correlated with shorter oral–pharyngeal transit (r = −.527, P = .003). The study compared disease-stage groups using one bolus. The online publication date was August 29, 2010. The overlapping investigator group presented related work in 2008, with participant overlap unverified. [Primary article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00455-010-9296-y) · [PubMed, PMID 20803220](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20803220/) · [Earlier conference trail](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-JSDR-Legacy-Later.md#2008-jaw-and-tongue-coordination-in-parkinson)

## 2010 depression and swallowing questionnaires in Parkinson

Han and colleagues’ 2009 conference title describes an association between depressive symptoms and swallowing difficulty after accounting for motor symptom severity. The 2010 Japanese and 2011 English papers have identical author lists, one additional author compared with the conference listing, and the same research question. The 2011 abstract compares Beck Depression Inventory responses by Swallowing Disturbances Questionnaire severity in Parkinson’s. It supplies no food exposure. The conference body is gated and linkage at the participant level is unverified. [2009 presentation I-7-20, pp. 332–333](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2009/001303/187&name=0332-0333j) · [2010 Japanese journal record](https://webview.isho.jp/journal/detail/abs/10.11477/mf.1552101813) · [2011 primary abstract, PMID 21458355](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21458355/)

## 2010 black pepper and cardamom oil odor with salivation

Ito and Yamamura tested 43 healthy adults using scented strips with black pepper oil or cardamom oil and odorless jojoba oil as a comparator. In the experiment with all three oils, the mean cotton-collected amounts were 0.175 g with black pepper oil, 0.182 g with cardamom oil, 0.118 g with jojoba oil and 0.130 g at rest. The two aromatic oils increased saliva secretion relative to control, with no detected difference between them. This was an odor exposure in healthy adults. The outcome was salivary secretion, with no Parkinson swallowing assessment. The Japanese journal lists August 31, 2010 as publication and June 26, 2020 as release on J-STAGE. [Primary article](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsdr/14/2/14_134/_article/-char/en)

## 2010 capsiate and aspiration pneumonia

Ebihara, Nagasaka and Kohzuki presented a report on capsiate and the swallowing reflex in people with aspiration pneumonia. An independent rehabilitation meeting index lists the same title and authors. The 2009 Yamasaki letter shares Ebihara and Kohzuki and provides results for a 12-person catheter challenge with CH-19 Sweet extract. The accessible conference records give neither the dose nor the cohort results. The diagnoses and participant overlap with the letter remain unverified. [2010 presentation 2-P1-8, p. 539](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2010/001403/513&name=0539-0539j) · [2010 rehabilitation proceedings, 2-6-27](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/archive/search?issue=suppl&jo=ch7remed&vo=47&ye=2010) · [Earlier primary letter](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-JSDR-Legacy-Later.md#2009-capsiate-and-swallow-reflex-in-older-people)

## 2011 jelly swallowing with metronome cues in Parkinson

Nozaki and colleagues reported a randomized crossover comparison of swallowing training in 20 people with Parkinson’s and moderate dysphagia. Ten began with metronome cues and ten without. Each two-week intervention included neck stretching, tongue exercises and swallowing jelly, with a two-week washout. Participants in the metronome period swallowed a spoonful of jelly on the sixth beat. Videofluoroscopy tested juice and jelly. The J-STAGE version reports less jelly residue in the valleculae and pyriform sinuses with cueing and shorter oral transit (P = .05). The society’s alternate abstract reports P = .056. Both print the transit-time unit as milliseconds, and the intended unit is unverified. Neither specifies the juice or jelly ingredients or gives a juice-versus-jelly effect. This report differs in population and follow-up from the six-month metronome study in Parkinson’s or cerebrovascular disease. The 2009 multicenter presentation is a possible precursor. [2011 Japanese Neurological Society abstract O7-201, printed p. 1318](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/clinicalneurol/51/12/51_12_1199/_pdf#page=119) · [Society abstract copy](https://neurology-jp.org/Journal/public_pdf/051121199.pdf) · [2009 multicenter presentation TL1-2](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2009/001303/049&name=0264-0265j) · [Separate six-month study](Food-Experiment-JSDR-Abstracts.md#2013-named-test-foods-in-a-combined-neurological-cohort)

## 2011 thickened barium after deep brain stimulation

Kitashima and colleagues followed three people with Parkinson’s before and after subthalamic nucleus stimulation. Videofluoroscopy used approximately 5 mL of diluted barium mixed with water and the commercial thickener Toromi Perfect, adjusted to a honey-like viscosity. The preparation contained no listed honey. Swallowing patterns differed among patients. The second patient’s oral–pharyngeal transit at one week was 7.0 seconds with stimulation off and 16.67 seconds with it on. The authors observed no deterioration from baseline at six months. The comparison concerns stimulation at a fixed test viscosity. The full report appeared in the December 31, 2011 issue. Its 2009 and 2010 conference predecessors share the lead investigators, with their exact samples unverified. ## 2013 and 2014 CH-19 Sweet capsinoid seasoning reports

The funder released a formulation report on July 10, 2013. It describes a powdered emulsion containing 0.36% capsinoids from the nonpungent CH-19 Sweet pepper. Cookies, porridge and other food vehicles were explored during development, without separate clinical effect measurements. A July 24, 2014 progress report describes videofluoroscopy in five people with dysphagia given 3 g jelly with and without the seasoning. The authors describe individual aspiration observations and a trend in laryngeal elevation speed (P < .08). It supplies no Parkinson subgroup or group aspiration estimate. The subsequent 17-person report belongs to the same project. ## Cardamom aroma and measured salivary secretion

Ito and Yamamura included 43 healthy adults across two experiments. Cardamom essential oil was presented on an odor strip near the nostrils. In experiment II the authors reported mean saliva weight of 0.182 g during cardamom exposure, 0.130 g at rest and 0.118 g with odorless jojoba. Black pepper oil gave 0.175 g. The paper reports higher secretion with aromatic oils and no clear difference between cardamom and black pepper. The intervention was aroma and the outcome was saliva collected by cotton. The reference to a shorter swallowing reflex comes from separate black-pepper literature. No clinical swallowing outcome or ingestion of ground cardamom was measured.
