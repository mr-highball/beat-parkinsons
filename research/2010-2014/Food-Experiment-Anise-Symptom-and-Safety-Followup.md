# Anise seed symptom and safety evidence, 2010–2014

## Registered anise extract trial measured menopausal hot flashes

The 2011 official record planned four weeks of anise-extract capsules or starch for menopausal hot flashes. It gives no prespecified insomnia, sleepiness, or REM outcome. A 2022 article cites the ID but describes enrollment in 2018–2019 and eight weeks of treatment. [Official IRCT record](https://irct.ir/trial/4583) · [Local source text](Source-Text/IRCT201103284317N4-Anise-Extract-Hot-Flashes.txt)

## Aniseed essential oil and CNS drug challenge in mice

Five days of oral aniseed essential-oil pretreatment altered separate mouse drug challenges. Pentobarbital-induced sleep was shorter, while diazepam-associated open-arm entries were lower and midazolam-induced motor impairment was greater. These endpoints and the concentrated oil differ from daily sleepiness, sleep-onset complaints, dexterity, and falls after ground culinary seed. [Primary abstract and publisher record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22926042/) · [Local source text](Source-Text/2012-Samojlik-Anise-Oil-CNS-Interactions.txt)

## Anise ethanol extract capsules and menopausal hot flashes

The preparation was a laboratory ethanol extract of powdered seed in starch capsules. The trial reported hot flash frequency and severity after four weeks. Its discussion of sleep-related consequences of hot flashes does not establish a sleep outcome or a ground culinary seed effect. [Primary article and Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3813162/) · [Local source text](Source-Text/2012-Nahidi-Anise-Hot-Flashes.txt)
