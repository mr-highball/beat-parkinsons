# Macadamia oil and upper gut transit

## Ground whole walnuts and reasoning in a student crossover

Pribis and colleagues published online September 19, 2011 (PMID **21923981**, DOI **10.1017/S0007114511004302**, 2012 issue). Sixty-four students were randomized to sequences of banana bread with ground whole walnuts or walnut-free bread. After each eight-week period, the whole-walnut condition favored the verbal-inference subtest by 1.0 point (95% CI 0.3 to 1.7, p=.009). The overall verbal critical-thinking score comparison was inconclusive (1.6 points, 95% CI −2.2 to 5.5, p=.399). The letter-number sequencing working-memory comparison was also inconclusive (p=.867). Bread composition differed, and the subtest result does not supply an oil or Parkinson estimate. The 2016 mood analysis used the same cohort based on matched randomizations, sequences and dropout details. ## Mixed-nut Mediterranean diet and new depression diagnoses

Sánchez-Villegas and colleagues published the PREDIMED incident-depression analysis in 2013 (PMID **24229349**, DOI **10.1186/1741-7015-11-208**). Over a median 5.4 years, 224 new cases were identified. The Mediterranean diet with mixed nuts versus low-fat advice yielded an adjusted hazard ratio of 0.78 (95% CI 0.55 to 1.10). The subgroup with type 2 diabetes yielded 0.59 (95% CI 0.36 to 0.98). The comparison concerns onset of depression in a cardiovascular prevention trial. It gives no separate walnut-oil arm, depressive symptom change or Parkinson subgroup estimate. The trial team later documented allocation deviations in the parent trial. The cited depression estimates are from the 2013 analysis. ## Healthy volunteer macadamia and olive oil transit trial

NCT01510223 first posted on January 16, 2012 and lists 24 healthy participants enrolled in 2010–2011. The macadamia arm used a daily high-fat milkshake with 15.18 g macadamia nut oil and 74.82 g olive oil. The alternative high-fat arms used olive oil alone or olive oil with fish powder. The protocol measured gastric emptying and mouth-to-caecum transit with breath tests after the dietary periods. Registry results are absent. Chapter 6 of Markey's dissertation now supplies the arm-specific measurements, with no significant trial-by-group interaction and no Parkinson constipation outcome. Markey and Shafat’s September 2011 conference title covers adaptation and deadaptation. A different 2011 Cambridge abstract analyzes dietary fatty acids using weighed food records. ## Osaka macadamia oil insulin sensitivity registration

UMIN000013142 first posted on February 12, 2014 and lists an anticipated start in November 2011 and last follow-up in November 2013. The completed study registered insulin sensitivity and circulating fatty acids. The record reports results as unpublished. This registration verifies human macadamia oil investigation outside Parkinson's with no constipation, movement or other frozen symptom outcome listed.
