# Early JSDR primary abstracts on Parkinson swallowing and food texture

The Japanese Society of Dysphagia Rehabilitation published these conference abstracts in its official proceedings. Page links use the PDF page numbers. Clinical descriptions of coughing, videofluoroscopic penetration or aspiration, and an obstructive choking event denote different outcomes. [Annual meeting archive](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/meeting/meeting_annual.html)

## 2012 jelly aspiration and neck position

Ishimoto and colleagues reported one man in his 70s with Hoehn and Yahr stage III Parkinson's disease and a history of deep brain stimulation. When he swallowed an unnamed jelly during seated videofluoroscopy at 45° cervical lordosis, the investigators observed laryngeal penetration, aspiration, and residue in the epiglottic and pyriform regions. After repositioning to 30°, they observed no penetration or aspiration with the jelly and continued to see residue. After about one month of rehabilitation, testing at 35° again showed residue without penetration or aspiration. This is a within-patient posture comparison with one preparation of unidentified jelly. [Proceedings, 1-J3-15, S156](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no17-18_abstracts.pdf#page=157)

The same proceedings contain a separate study of 59 patients with Parkinson's disease and 28 with progressive supranuclear palsy. In the Parkinson group, more head and neck extension was associated with worse videofluoroscopic dysphagia scores (p = .049), with no bolus named in that abstract. Thickened water, jelly and paste were described by a neighboring mixed-diagnosis study. A different report mentions roasted sweet potato at a nursing home event in a woman with Alzheimer's disease and a lollipop as the therapy exposure. [Proceedings, 2-G-3-3, S289–S290](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no17-18_abstracts.pdf#page=290) · [Proceedings, 2-N-3, S398](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no17-18_abstracts.pdf#page=399)

## 2013 rice textures and measured tongue pressure

Watanabe and colleagues recorded diet texture by interview and measured mean maximum tongue pressure over three efforts in 24 people with Parkinson's disease without cognitive impairment. They found no statistically significant tongue-pressure difference between the 20 eating a regular diet and the four eating soft rice or full rice porridge. The person with the lowest recorded tongue pressure (9.0 kPa) had bolus formation and propulsion deficits and pharyngeal residue on videofluoroscopy despite an unremarkable bedside screen. The design compared patients' existing diet assignments and gives no rice-specific swallowing or choking rate. [Proceedings, SO1-7-3-3, S90](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no19_abstracts.pdf#page=91)

## 2013 named test foods in a combined neurological cohort

Nozaki and colleagues enrolled 12 people with dysphagia due to cerebrovascular disease or Parkinson's disease in six months of home metronome-paced swallowing practice. Ten completed the protocol. The videofluoroscopic test preparations were simulated juice, jelly, and cookie. Epiglottic residue scores before and after training were 0.40 and 0.55 for juice, 1.50 and 0.40 for jelly, and 2.15 and 1.35 for cookie. The abstract pools the diagnoses and does not give Parkinson subgroup denominators or effects by food. The exposure being studied was rhythmic cueing. The 2012 Japanese Neurological Society abstract and the KAKEN final report describe the same arm, with matching sample and follow-up. [2012 primary abstract, O(3)-7-4](https://www.neurology-jp.org/Journal/public_pdf/052121397.pdf) · [2013 proceedings, SO1-7-1-9, S85](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no19_abstracts.pdf#page=86) · [Final grant report, PDF p. 2](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/en/file/KAKENHI-PROJECT-22500490/22500490seika.pdf#page=2)

## 2013 texture modification after aspiration pneumonia

Kishimoto and colleagues followed a 75-year-old woman with Parkinson's disease who received a feeding tube after aspiration pneumonia. With positioning, practice timed to alertness, medication management and rehabilitation, she later ate up to 200 g of blended food and could eat a preferred food prepared as a jelly when alert. The abstract identifies neither the favorite food nor a jelly-specific aspiration rate. A second case in the proceedings (Ito and Miyake, 73-year-old man with Parkinson's disease) describes pharyngeal residue on liquids and management using balloon dilation and alternating swallows. The latter patient progressed from paste and full rice porridge to soft foods and agar. [Proceedings, SP2-1-4-5, S278](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no19_abstracts.pdf#page=279) · [Proceedings, SP2-2-4-3, S290–S291](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no19_abstracts.pdf#page=291)

## 2014 mealtime coughing and aspiration assessed separately

Nakayama and colleagues evaluated 66 inpatients with Parkinson's disease. Videofluoroscopy found aspiration of liquid in 13 (19.7%) and pharyngeal residue of solids in 33 (50%). At ordinary meals, coughing after a solid had 30% sensitivity and 98% specificity for the separate finding of liquid aspiration, and 18% sensitivity and 100% specificity for solid residue. The abstract does not name the solids or liquids. A barium pancake described on the opening page belongs to an adjacent jaw resection and denture study. [Proceedings, 1NL-P4-01, S98–S99](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no20_abstracts.pdf#page=99)

## 2014 choking and aspiration after a change in medical status

Miyazawa and Masuda reported a man in his 70s evaluated for Parkinson's disease before cardiac arrest, tracheostomy and cardiac surgery. At transfer he was taking full rice porridge, chopped foods and thickened fluids with tube feeding for supplementation. Four days after tracheostomy tube removal, he developed aspiration pneumonia. The authors also reported aspiration and choking at meals without identifying the obstructing material. He continued to show signs of aspiration when paste was later reintroduced. The case records several concurrent causes of impaired intake and supplies no food-specific attribution. [Proceedings, 1NN-P14-10, S148](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/wp-content/uploads/file/meeting/annual_meeting_no20_abstracts.pdf#page=149)

## 2014 grant report on six months of capsaicin jelly

Sonoko Nozaki's KAKEN final report for project 22500490 describes a prospective open home study in 15 people aged 65–93 with dysphagia due to cerebrovascular disease or Parkinson's disease. It describes alternating swallows of capsaicin jelly during dinner, with clinician supervision at initiation. Twelve participants continued for six months. Videofluoroscopy showed less laryngeal penetration in three, with no significant change for the group. Four reported improved swallowing symptoms on SWAL-QOL, and two developed pneumonia. Three discontinued after a change in health status. No Parkinson-specific denominator, severity change, formulation dose, or placebo comparison is provided. The grant report lists different clinical selection factors for the separate metronome arm, so the two arms cannot be compared directly. The report is dated May 30, 2013 and the funder lists its public release on August 29, 2014. [Funder metadata and date](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/en/report/KAKENHI-PROJECT-22500490/22500490seika/) · [Original grant report, PDF pp. 2–3](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/en/file/KAKENHI-PROJECT-22500490/22500490seika.pdf#page=2)

The grant cites a prior one-month inpatient capsaicin jelly study, presented at the 2009 Japanese Neurological Society meeting. The authors' institutional bibliography gives the presentation title and identifies a parkinsonism sample. The society's archived abstracts for that year require a member login. The 2001–2002 original oral jelly challenges are documented separately. ## 2014 oral capsaicin film in chronic dysphagia

Takaya Shimano's author abstract describes a double-blind comparison of oral films containing 1.5 mg capsaicin with identical films without capsaicin for four weeks. The 42 gastrostomy-fed participants included people with Parkinson's disease, cerebrovascular disease and dementia. The author reports lower immediate post-dose swallowing reflex latency with the active film at the initial, two-week and four-week evaluations. The active group did not show a significant sustained change in pre-dose latency across visits. Some bedside measures changed acutely, while the cough test did not distinguish groups. No Parkinson subgroup outcome is available in the abstract. The institutional record dates the thesis's public release to June 16, 2014.
