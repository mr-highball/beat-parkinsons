# Visual outcomes in the 2013 originals and boundaries of fig preparations

The [tremor and visual symptoms cohort](../Source-Text/PMC3705145.txt) reports whether visual symptoms were present at two visits. It measures change in the number of affected people, with no event rate for an individual. Rum and comparison drinks both used orange juice. The alcohol comparison concerns tremor and separates essential tremor, Parkinson disease and DLB. The four supplemental files remain an access gap. The [repeated-measures study](../Source-Text/PMC3946909.txt) measures attention-related performance and sleep latency as distinct sources of fluctuation. The [drug-induced case](../Source-Text/PMC3604227.txt) records no observed visual or auditory hallucinations on rehospitalization in a traumatic brain injury setting.

The [secondary clinical-profile comparison](../Source-Text/PMC3808221.txt) reuses IDEAL, ADENA and EXPRESS databases. The earlier [EXPRESS randomized publication](../../2000-2004/Source-Text/2004-PMID15590953-EXPRESS-Hallucination-and-Tremor-Events.txt) counts patients with a broad hallucination adverse-event term. It also reports more tremor adverse events with active drug and an inconclusive difference on tremor items during formal examination. Those results depend on endpoint definition. The [later early-hallucination trial](../../2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC8413762.txt) contributes a separate research family.

The [dried fig diet animal experiment](../Source-Text/PMC4090508.txt) reports better maze performance in the treated Alzheimer-model mice. It describes dried flesh and an extraction step whose relationship to the chow remains ambiguous. The [2015 biomarker study](../../2015-2019/Source-Text/2015-PMID25807081-Fig-Fruit-Powder-and-Cytokines.txt) instead explicitly describes freshly sourced, frozen and ground fruit. Study-family overlap remains unresolved.

The original [1984 interviews](../../1980-1984/Cultural-and-Experiential/Traditional-Practices/1984-PMID6492831-Luna-Ficus-Plant-Teachers.txt) distinguish ojé identified as Ficus insipida from renaco identified only to genus in that account. The [2011 field study](../Cultural-and-Experiential/Traditional-Practices/2011-PMID21295130-Oje-Plant-Teachers.txt) describes separate species sharing the ojé name and includes an attributed report that the spirit of ojé presents itself during a dieta. Earlier [hospital admissions](../../2005-2009/Source-Text/2005-PMID15814256-Oje-Latex-Hospital-Cases.txt) document toxicity of a latex preparation, including a case involving loss of consciousness. These materials document different exposures and outcomes. Clinical visual episode frequency after eating dried Ficus carica remains unresolved.
