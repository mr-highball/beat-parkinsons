# Green tea leaf, tiredness and outcome selection

The final European herbal monograph records traditional use of brewed unfermented green tea leaf and powdered leaf for tiredness or weakness. The 1946 preparation citation is described as a tea tonic without separate black- and green-leaf identification. The 2014 document describes Japanese powdered tea in a distinct historical account. [Traditional-practice record](../Cultural-and-Experiential/Traditional-Practices/2014-EMA-Green-Tea-Leaf-Fatigue.txt)

The 2007 enriched drink measures EEG and evoked potentials. The 2011 combined supplement adds memory and attention outcomes. A different constituent blend yielded a lower acute task-tiredness rating in 2010. The original 1986 caffeine crossover separately reports less tiredness after 24 hours of abstinence. Preparation, outcome and withdrawal conditions matter for a clinical interpretation. [1986 caffeine comparison](../../1985-1989/Source-Text/1986-PMID3741730-Bruce-Caffeine-Tiredness-Crossover.txt) · [2010 task-tiredness result](../Source-Text/2010-PMID21040626-Tea-Constituent-Drink-and-Tiredness.txt) · [2011 cognition result](../Source-Text/2011-PMID21303262-Green-Tea-Theanine-Memory-Endpoints.txt)

A later Parkinson pilot measures the exact brewed beverage and Fatigue Severity Scale in 14 people without a control group. Its worsening observation and the conventional relief indication describe different measures and populations. The scorecard preserves both under their verified evidence strengths.
