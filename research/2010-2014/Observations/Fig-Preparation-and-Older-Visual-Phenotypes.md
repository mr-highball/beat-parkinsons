# Fig preparations and older visual symptom measures

The 1872 first edition describes fresh figs, sun-dried fruit and later pressed or stored preparations separately. The subsequent 2003 printing supplies the same historical account from different pagination. The 1984 clinical archive separately distinguishes visual hallucinations reported at presentation from symptoms recognized during the course of a different neurological disease. [Original 1872 pages](../../1870-1874/Cultural-and-Experiential/Local-Knowledge/1872-Hanoteau-Letourneux-Fig-Harvest-and-Storage.txt) · [1984 series and printed tables](../../1980-1984/Source-Text/1984-PMID6368752-Creutzfeldt-Jakob-Visual-Phenotype-Printed-Tables.txt).

The 2014 structural study compares current chronic visual hallucination groups matched on cognition and age. Other clinical papers use cognition, drug chronology or diagnostic imaging. Their comparisons describe phenotype and symptom reporting rather than exposure-specific rates. The earlier bibliographic record for recurrent autumnal psychosis names deadly nightshade ingestion as the reported exposure. Its complete clinical narrative remains open. [Matched MRI groups](../Source-Text/2014-PMID24480486-Visuoperceptive-Atrophy-and-Chronic-Visual-Hallucinations.txt) · [Clinical drug chronology](../Source-Text/2014-PMID24575030-PSP-Like-Creutzfeldt-Jakob-Disease-and-Hallucinations.txt) · [Earlier original abstract](../../2000-2004/Source-Text/2003-PMID12743348-Autumnal-Psychosis-Original-Abstract.txt).

The 2011 published case records unusually heavy dried fig consumption and recurrent elevated potassium. The 2015 poster reports an immediate clinical reaction to fig ingestion and separate skin test results for fresh and dried material. The 2013 laboratory study measures the accessibility of selected chemicals under simulated digestion, with changes varying by cultivar and analyte. These reports record different observations in different contexts. [Potassium case](../Source-Text/2011-Pavletic-Dried-Fig-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Fig ingestion and test preparations](../../2015-2019/Source-Text/2015-Gonzalez-Perez-Fig-Ingestion-and-Preparation-Tests.txt) · [Simulated digestion](../Source-Text/2013-Kamiloglu-Fresh-and-Sun-Dried-Fig-Bioaccessibility.txt).

One animal publication identifies fresh, frozen and ground fig fruit added to chow and measures inflammatory outcomes. A related behavioral report describes fig-supplemented chow, with full preparation details still open. The anti-Parkinson leaf-extract reference in the genus review uses another species. Check direct food form and population before interpreting model citations.
