# Oral red pepper and gastrointestinal pain

## Six-week Thai trial and its earlier reports

Aniwan and Gonlachanvit's 2014 trial enrolled 16 adults with diarrhea-predominant irritable bowel syndrome. Each took active capsules and lactose powder placebo in randomized six-week periods with four weeks of washout. The active dose contained culinary Capsicum frutescens powder rather than an extract. Before each meal participants took one stomach-dispersing gelatin capsule with 0.5 g powder and one small-intestinal enteric-coated capsule with 0.2 g powder plus 0.3 g lactose. Each corresponding placebo capsule contained 0.5 g lactose. The total daily exposure was 2.1 g powder with approximately 2.52 mg capsaicin. The cultivar was unspecified and the premeal delivery included an enteric formulation.

After the standardized spicy meal challenge at the end of treatment, peak abdominal burning was median 0.0 (IQR 0.0–0.5) after chili and 0.3 (0.0–1.6) after placebo (p<.05). The separately measured maximum pain score was inconclusive (Figure 2B, baseline p=.443, end-of-treatment p=.799). Balloon pressure at first rectal sensation was 16 (12–16) versus 8 (8–16) mmHg (p<.05). The rectal urgency thresholds and compliance comparisons were inconclusive. Early weekly burning and urgency transiently increased during the chili period. The meal-provoked burning outcome and first sensation threshold describe distinct effects from abdominal pain severity. [Primary article, Figures 2–4](https://www.jnmjournal.org/journal/download_pdf.php?doi=10.5056%2Fjnm14022) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/PMID24867591-Pain-Extract.txt)

Aniwan's 2010 thesis and the 2012 Thai journal report include 10 participants, the same dose and period lengths, and nearly identical reported six-week sensory and symptom results. They report no clear abdominal pain difference. The thesis reports 30.10 versus 23.70 on the 100-mm abdominal-pain symptom scale at week six (Table 6, no significant difference reported). The 2012 abstract labels an improvement in spicy meal-provoked abdominal burning, 4.3 ± 2.4 versus 14.1 ± 5.1 (p=.02), and a shift in first rectal sensation from 8 to 12 mmHg (p=.03). Treat these as one likely overlapping report series, including the 2014 supplement of six participants as a plausible expansion. Neither the 2014 paper nor the indexed Thai abstract states participant overlap explicitly. The thesis document identifies academic year 2010, and its original public release date remains unverified. The 2012 volume link timed out. ## Italian enteric-coated red pepper trial in IBS

In 2011 Bortolotti and Porta randomized 50 adults with irritable bowel syndrome to colon-release pills containing 150 mg of whole ground dried Capsicum annuum per pill (seed included, 0.50 mg capsaicin) or identical placebo pills. The plan was four pills per day for six weeks. The full text identifies the matching pill presentation and does not specify placebo ingredients. It excludes neurological diseases that could affect gastrointestinal function.

Six of the 23 red-pepper recipients discontinued after intense pain on starting treatment. Two of the 27 placebo recipients discontinued for other reasons. Eight participants reduced from four pills to two because of onset pain, assigned to the pepper group in the authors' later subgroup discussion. The diary pain score improved over the final two weeks within the active group compared with its own baseline. Results and Figure 1 report no significant between-group diary pain contrast. The separate subjective overall effectiveness rating favored red pepper, and the abstract conclusion attributes superior pain reduction to the ingredient. The difference between within-group baseline changes, patient-rated effectiveness, and between-group pain scores limits interpretation, especially given differential attrition. [Publisher abstract](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10620-011-1740-9) · [Original paper, Results and Figure 1](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51128411_Effect_of_Red_Pepper_on_Symptoms_of_Irritable_Bowel_Syndrome_Preliminary_Study) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/PMID21573941-Pain-Extract.txt)

The 2010 S1340 conference antecedent reports the same 23 versus 27 allocation, pill contents, six-week schedule, six versus two discontinuations and eight dose reductions. It similarly reports an inconclusive between-group pain comparison and a favorable overall effectiveness rating, 3.8 ± 1.1 versus 1.9 ± 1.1 (p<.05). These matching methods and counts identify one cohort.
