# Summer savory, speech and voice

## Summer savory herb tea in a 2011 Transylvanian survey

Papp and colleagues reported in 2011 on interviews with 70 inhabitants of Lövéte and Nagybacon, Romania, conducted in 2008–2010. Table 1 records Satureja hortensis herb tea for sore throat in Lövéte. It does not specify the herb's freshness, a Parkinson cohort, individual voice or listener-intelligibility assessment, or the number of users of this particular plant. Oregano is a separate row. The later 2026 Papp study covers overlapping years and region; participant or source overlap is possible and has not been resolved. This is a traditional-use report, not a measured vocal treatment effect. ## Summer savory essential-oil gel for denture stomatitis

Sabzghabaee and colleagues reported an 80-person randomized double-blind comparison of 1% Satureja hortensis essential-oil gel versus placebo for denture stomatitis. Forty participants per group were instructed to apply a thin 1 cm layer to the denture base and palatal mucosa twice daily for two weeks. The trial evaluated palatal erythema and Candida colonies; no validated speech intelligibility test, acoustic voice level, Parkinson subgroup or fresh-herb ingestion was reported. It tests a topical essential-oil gel, not eating fresh summer savory. [Original clinical report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3353698/)
