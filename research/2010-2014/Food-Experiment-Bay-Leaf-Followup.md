# Bay leaf symptom evidence

## Korean Parkinson model patents and related journal report

The March 29, 2010 application KR20100032943A describes a *Laurus nobilis* leaf hexane fraction injected intraperitoneally after a 6-OHDA lesion in rats. It reports retention of tyrosine hydroxylase-positive neurons in the substantia nigra relative to the lesion comparison. The April 7, 2010 application KR20100036052A describes cell experiments with separated leaf compounds and related histology following intraperitoneal reynosin in a 6-OHDA rat experiment. Neither motor gait results nor clinical observations are provided in the patent experiments reviewed. [Leaf fraction application](https://patents.google.com/patent/KR20100032943A/en) · [Isolated compounds application](https://patents.google.com/patent/KR20100036052A/en)

Ham and colleagues’ January 2011 journal report describes the same type of leaf fraction study, with histology of tyrosine hydroxylase-positive cells in lesioned rats and dopamine-triggered cell stress in SH-SY5Y cultures. Its abstract has no stride or walking-speed outcome. Shared methods, investigator names and results suggest the fraction patent and paper are overlapping reports. A December 2010 article on isolated spirafolide and a separate 2011 article on isolated acetoxyeudesmanolide measure survival and related changes in SH-SY5Y cells. These results are cellular or histological observations for 0032 and 0360. [January 2011 primary journal record](https://doi.org/10.4062/biomolther.2011.19.1.118) · [Spirafolide primary report](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12272-010-1210-5) · [Acetoxyeudesmanolide primary report](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11064-011-0523-1)

## Human visual vigilance during leaf volatile inhalation

Matsubara and colleagues tested nine healthy male university students in three sessions one week apart with no odor or two concentrations of *Laurus nobilis* leaf volatiles. At 30 minutes, the true-hit index was 93.8% in the lower-exposure session and 88.0% in the no-odor session (P < .05). There was no difference in that index during the first 20 minutes. Reaction time was faster at one time point in the higher-exposure session. The higher-exposure session also received less favorable ratings on several subjective items, including mental clarity compared with control. The questionnaire included an active–inactive rating but no validated fatigue or apathy scale. The controlled inhalation design provides adjacent evidence for 0734, 0815 and 0992. A March 2010 Japanese original report from the same research group used an analogous title. The [earlier Japanese original](#earlier-japanese-bay-leaf-odor-vigilance-experiment) is now available. It used a similar protocol and tested 11 men. Participant overlap remains unresolved, so the reports are tracked together. [February 2011 primary paper, pp. 20–25 and Figs. 2 and 5](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/biomedres/32/1/32_1_19/_pdf) · [March 2010 bibliographic record](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/en/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=201002298082339061)

## Intestinal transit and diarrhea in rats

In November 2011, Qnais and colleagues reported that aqueous *Laurus nobilis* leaf extract inhibited castor-oil-induced diarrhea and slowed a charcoal marker in rats. The methods specify intraperitoneal extract administration for the transit comparison. Delivery of the charcoal by mouth was the readout procedure. The authors also observed relaxation of isolated rat ileal smooth muscle with increasing concentration. These results document a direction of motility change in the model and route studied. Parkinson constipation severity and oral ground leaf were outside the experiment. This provides an opposing mechanistic lead for 0477. [Primary journal record](https://doi.org/10.1089/jmf.2011.1707) · [Primary article methods mirror](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51797071_Antidiarrheal_Activity_of_Laurus_nobilis_L_Leaf_Extract_in_Rats)

## Drooling and reduced responsiveness after infant laurel infusions

Cortini and colleagues reported four cases to the Pavia Poison Center in a 2013 meeting abstract and a 2014 clinical toxicology congress abstract. One infant given concentrated laurel decoction repeatedly had regurgitation, vomiting, drooling, tremor, reduced neck tone and irritability. The other three had hyporeactivity, hypotonia, or lethargy after infusions, with breathing difficulty in one. All four recovered after observation. The report identifies the plant as *Laurus nobilis* and does not describe laboratory authentication or a clinical fatigue, REM-sleep or drooling severity scale. These oral adverse events provide context for 0154, 0299 and 0992. The 2017 reproduction is the same report. [2013 meeting abstract, p. 55](https://www.centroantiveleni.com/formazione/abstract/Abstract%20book%20SITOX%202013.pdf) · [2014 primary congress abstract 220](https://www.clintox.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/XXXIV-International-Congress-of-the-European-Association-of-Poisons-Centres-and-Clinical-Toxicologists-EAPCCT-27-30-May-2014-Brussels-Belgium.pdf)

## Earlier Japanese bay leaf odor vigilance experiment

Shimizu and colleagues studied 11 healthy men aged 20–23 in counterbalanced odor sessions. Dried bay leaves were cut and placed in the olfactometer. The low-load 0.1 g odor condition differed from clean air on task accuracy over 30 minutes (p=.026), with a reported contrast at 20–30 minutes (p=.030). The 3.0 g odor condition had no significant accuracy contrast with control and produced less favorable odor impressions. The original prints volume 17 (2009) and [J-GLOBAL dates publication to March 2010](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/en/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=201002298082339061). The later nine-person report shares authors, leaf loads and task setup. Participant overlap has not been resolved, so the two reports form one evidence family. The measured effect is from breathing leaf odor, with no ground-leaf ingestion or Parkinson attention comparison.
