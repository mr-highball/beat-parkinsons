# Observations for 1905-1909

## Major observations

- The nine-case autopsy series found no distinctive parathyroid structural change. This is a specific negative finding for the anatomical hypothesis and leaves endocrine function untested. [Mechanisms](../Mechanisms-and-Models.md)

## Minor observations

- Stewart’s whisky anecdote illustrates how task context can affect tremor reporting. Harris’s young patient shows continued diagnostic work on unilateral signs. [Food and nutrition](../Food-and-Nutrition.md) · [Clinical source](../Clinical-and-Diagnosis.md)

## Comparison with 1900–1904

- The unilateral phenotype remained a diagnostic challenge in young patients. Hyoscine was still described as symptomatic treatment, and Thompson supplied a postmortem comparison for one proposed mechanism. [Prior observations](../../1900-1904/Observations/README.md)

## Collection gaps

- Collect full reports of other endocrine tissue studies and extract outcome data for remedies such as formic acid. No controlled dietary effect was verified.


Cushny’s April 1908 article brings earlier nutmeg case observations into the dated record and describes acute drowsiness or stupor. The case collection is attributed to Wallace and supplies no systematic ordinary-use comparison. [Original source and attribution](../Food-Experiment-Nutmeg-Poisoning.md)

Separate 1907 publications describe three distinct routes or settings. One discusses historical injected olive oil and oral eggs mixed with other foods for gastric ulcer. The BMJ issue proposes olive oil to aid fat assimilation in colitis. The ulcer scan lacks printed page 376.
