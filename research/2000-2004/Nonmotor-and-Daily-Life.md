# Nonmotor and Daily Life for 2000–2004

### 2000 — Determinants of reported quality of life

Schrag and colleagues examined quality-of-life measures in a population-based Parkinson’s sample. Depression, disability, postural instability, and cognitive impairment showed strong associations. The analysis characterized reported burden without establishing causal effects. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10945804/)

### 2001 — Bowel movement frequency preceding diagnosis

Abbott and colleagues followed men in the Honolulu cohort. Infrequent bowel movements were associated with later Parkinson’s diagnosis. The study established temporal precedence of the report of constipation while leaving early disease, susceptibility, and shared exposures as possible explanations. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11502913/)

### 2003 — Eight-year dementia follow-up

Aarsland and colleagues followed an epidemiologically identified cohort and found a high cumulative frequency of dementia. Earlier hallucinations and akinetic-dominant or mixed motor presentation were associated with higher risk. The cohort estimate depends on enrollment and follow-up methods. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12633150/)
