# Clinical symptom measures and case boundaries

## Aortic rupture case and neighboring collection matches

September 2000. The assigned Edrees and Lee case describes leg weakness and pain before diagnosis of ruptured aortic aneurysm. Its report has no fall-frequency count or examination of resting tremor. Sweetened orange juice and a resting-tremor finding occur in different cases printed in the same collection. The exact title, answer pages and authors separate those terms from this patient. ## Pain history in an axillary nodule case

February 2001. Mazumdar, Vasishta and Bose report occasional axillary pain over six months in the patient investigated for occult breast carcinoma. The case offers no quantitative pain estimate or Parkinson population and no food comparison. Figure 1 is a tissue image. The case and its matched answers give no drooling outcome. ## Back pain in a pulmonary diagnosis case

February 2001. Al-Adsani and colleagues describe one patient with back pain and pulmonary findings involving malignancy and tuberculosis. The assigned case does not measure a food effect or report drooling for this patient. A neighboring angioedema case includes drooling language. Separate title, case pages and answers establish that those are separate patients. ## Pain and fatigue ratings during a brain stimulation comparison

2001. Strafella and colleagues assessed eight healthy adults during prefrontal and occipital stimulation sessions. The main comparison uses PET dopamine binding. Table 2 separately tabulates fatigued-to-rested ratings and a pain-to-no-pain item before and after stimulation. Occipital fatigue ratings are 2.00 to 2.16 and prefrontal 1.25 to 2.08. Occipital pain ratings are 1.63 to 1.75 and prefrontal 1.13 to 1.50. The original outcome table concerns acute stimulation in healthy adults. These ratings provide symptom context without a cherry or date exposure. [Original source](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/jneuro/21/15/RC157.full.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2001-Strafella-rTMS-Behavioral-Ratings-Extract.txt)

## Baseline rest tremor flags in a handwriting experiment

March 2002. The study compares handwriting adjustment under experimental distortion of visual feedback across Parkinson, older-control and younger-control groups. Table 1 for the 11 Parkinson participants distinguishes baseline presence of action tremor from tremor at rest. Stroke size and adaptation are the outcomes measured during the experiment. Baseline symptom flags do not quantify resting-tremor severity, and visual-feedback conditions do not measure hallucination frequency. The named food was outside the reported experimental exposure. [Original source](https://www.academia.edu/19430264/Adaptation_of_handwriting_size_under_distorted_visual_feedback_in_patients_with_Parkinsons_disease_and_elderly_and_young_controls) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2002-Teulings-Handwriting-and-Rest-Tremor-Flags.txt)

## Resting tremor in a postencephalitic case

2002. Hsieh, Lue and Lee describe one child who developed resting tremor and bradykinesia in an encephalitic illness. The reported signs improved over approximately three weeks. Drooling appears in the introductory description of parkinsonian symptoms and is not documented as a finding in this child. The case offers no dietary intervention or estimate for idiopathic Parkinson disease.
