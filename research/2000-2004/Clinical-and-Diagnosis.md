# Clinical and Diagnosis for 2000–2004

### 2000 — Multicenter dopamine transporter imaging

The Parkinson Study Group evaluated dopamine transporter imaging by SPECT across centers. Uptake helped distinguish Parkinson’s disease and progressive supranuclear palsy together from healthy controls and people with essential tremor. The analysis grouped degenerative parkinsonian diagnoses rather than separating them. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11094111/)

### 2000 — Prevalence in seven European population-based cohorts

De Rijk and colleagues pooled seven surveys with in-person screening and clinical examination. Prevalence was 1.8 per 100 people aged 65 and older and increased with age. The estimate concerns an older European population under the ascertainment methods used. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10854357/)

### 2002 — Autopsy confirmation in a movement-disorders specialty service

Hughes and colleagues compared clinical and postmortem diagnoses in 143 people with parkinsonism evaluated by a specialist service. Clinical diagnosis of Parkinson’s disease had high positive predictive value, with some cases recognized only at autopsy. Referral and brain donation patterns matter when comparing this series with general clinical settings. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11912118/)
