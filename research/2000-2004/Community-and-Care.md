# Community and Care for 2000–2004

### 2000 — Predictors of nursing home placement in western Norway

Aarsland and colleagues followed 178 community-dwelling people with Parkinson’s disease. Hallucinations were the strongest independent predictor of later nursing home admission. Motor and neuropsychiatric factors both contributed, and the observed predictors did not establish how an intervention would affect placement. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10968298/)

### 2002 — Community Parkinson’s nurse specialists in a randomized trial

Jarman and colleagues studied specialist nursing across 438 general practices over two years. People assigned a specialist nurse reported better global well-being, with little change in clinical status and no significant difference in health care costs. Different outcome measures captured different aspects of the service. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11991913/)

### 2002 — Caregiver burden and perceived social support

Edwards and Scheetz studied associations between caregiver burden, daily functioning, and social support. Greater patient disability and lower perceived support were among the correlates of burden. The survey could identify care needs without determining which changes would alleviate them. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12197259/)

### 2002 — Use of complementary therapies in a UK Parkinson’s clinic

Ferry and colleagues surveyed complementary therapy and nonprescribed medication use. Nonpharmacological therapies were often sought for Parkinson’s disease, while nonprescribed products were more often used for other concerns. Self-reported use described patient behavior and did not measure efficacy. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12415085/)
