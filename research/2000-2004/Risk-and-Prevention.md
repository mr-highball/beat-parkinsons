# Risk and Prevention for 2000–2004

### 2001 — Parkinsonism in welders seen in a movement-disorders clinic

Racette and colleagues compared 15 career welders with parkinsonism against groups diagnosed with idiopathic Parkinson’s disease. The welders had earlier average onset and similar clinical features on most measures. Case selection and the absence of a workforce incidence comparison limited inference about welding as a risk factor. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11148228/)

### 2001 — Smoking history and incidence in two prospective cohorts

Hernán and colleagues found an inverse association between reported cigarette smoking and later Parkinson’s diagnosis in men and women. Repeated questionnaires allowed comparison by smoking duration, amount, and time since cessation. The design leaves selection and prodromal changes possible, and it addressed an exposure history with established serious harms. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11761476/)

### 2004 — GBA variants in an Ashkenazi Jewish Parkinson’s sample

Aharon-Peretz and colleagues found that selected glucocerebrosidase gene variants were more frequent among Ashkenazi Jewish patients with Parkinson’s disease than among comparison participants. The study connected Gaucher disease carrier status with Parkinson’s susceptibility. Population selection limits generalization of the frequency and effect size. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15525722/)
