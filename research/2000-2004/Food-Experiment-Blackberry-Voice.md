# Blackberry and voice outcomes

## Blackberry pie in a speech elicitation sentence

Carter and colleagues reported a motion-tracking demonstration in December 2000. Three healthy 69-year-old men repeated a sentence about an apple and blackberry pie. The investigators used markers to quantify lip distances at bilabial closure. The berry exists as a word in the task. The study did not administer food, record voice volume as an endpoint or enroll people with Parkinson's disease. ## Mixed berry anaphylaxis case and throat swelling

Armentia and colleagues described food anaphylaxis followed by antiphospholipid syndrome in four patients in July 2001. The FDA's subsequent records for allergy-extract products discuss a single patient from the paper with urticaria and throat swelling after eating blackberries, strawberries and raspberries. Skin prick testing was positive, with no oral challenge to separate individual foods. The 1999 report from the same lead author describes a plausibly overlapping patient and mixes botanical labels. The FDA's repetition of the 2001 report under several products gives no further species confirmation. The public information lists no freezing, Parkinson diagnosis or voice-volume outcome for the reported patient.
