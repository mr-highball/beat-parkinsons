# Procedures and Devices for 2000–2004

### 2001 — Sham-controlled fetal dopamine neuron transplantation

Freed and colleagues randomized 40 people with severe disease to transplantation or sham surgery. The overall primary global rating did not show a significant difference, although younger participants had benefit on some secondary measures. Persistent dyskinesias occurred in some graft recipients. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11236774/)

### 2001 — Subthalamic and pallidal deep-brain stimulation

A multicenter study examined motor effects of stimulation at either subthalamic or pallidal targets in people with advanced disease. Motor function improved with stimulation. Intracranial hemorrhage and lead infection were reported. The study did not randomize participants between targets. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11575287/)

### 2002 — U.S. approval expanded to bilateral subthalamic or pallidal stimulation

The FDA approved an Activa supplement for bilateral stimulation of the internal globus pallidus or subthalamic nucleus as adjunctive therapy for selected levodopa-responsive advanced disease insufficiently controlled by medication. This historical approval concerned a broader indication than the original thalamic tremor system. [Original source](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpma/pma.cfm?id=P960009S007)

### 2003 — Second controlled fetal nigral transplantation trial

Olanow and colleagues randomized 34 people in a two-year placebo-controlled study. The overall primary motor outcome did not significantly favor transplantation despite evidence of graft survival. Off-medication dyskinesia occurred in over half the transplant recipients. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12953276/)

### 2003 — Five-year outcomes after subthalamic stimulation

Krack and colleagues reported durable benefit for off-medication motor scores and on-medication dyskinesia. Between years one and five, speech, gait freezing, balance, and cognition worsened on several measures. The uncontrolled follow-up could not separate disease course from treatment effects in these domains. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14614167/)

### 2003 — Small open study of putaminal GDNF delivery

Gill and colleagues reported motor and imaging improvements in a five-person open-label study of direct delivery of glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor to the putamen. The uncontrolled study used a different route from the negative ventricular trial. A controlled comparison was needed. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12669033/)
