# Early Japanese dysphagia proceedings and primary reports, 2000–2004

## Takamatsu videofluoroscopy and the 2001 and 2003 proceedings

Noriko Ichihara and colleagues compared eight people with Parkinson's disease, eight with progressive supranuclear palsy and ten controls in their November 2000 journal study. The primary abstract reports tongue pooling, impaired bolus formation and premature pharyngeal transfer in both patient groups. More frequent laryngeal entry and longer transfer from mouth to pharynx distinguished the PSP group from the Parkinson group. The abstract supplies no ingredient list. In a separately published lecture, the investigator describes her hospital's use of coffee jelly and Calpis, a cultured milk-based drink, as examples of simulated foods mixed with iopamidol. The lecture cites the eight-person Parkinson group among several studies and does not identify its bolus sequence or food-specific results. [2000 author abstract, PMID 11332186](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11332186/) · [Investigator lecture, item 9](https://www.jsdnnm.com/study-abst/)

The 2001 JSDR volume 5 supplement lists the Takamatsu team's Parkinson and ALS dysphagia presentation, p. 117. The 2003 proceedings list the team's comparison of stroke, Parkinson's disease and ALS, I-P4-6, p. 210. Ichihara's 2005 ALS surgery paper cites the latter by title and page. Both original JSDR abstracts require MedicalOnline membership. Patient overlap, preparation and the food-specific results are unresolved. [2001 record](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2001/0005s1/128&name=0117-0117j) · [2003 record](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2003/000702/118&name=0210-0210j) · [2005 citing paper, reference 9](https://iryogakkai.jp/2005-59-03/120-125.pdf)

## 2001 capsaicin jelly and recurrent aspiration leads

The 2001 supplement lists a Toneyama presentation by Sonoko Nozaki and colleagues about capsaicin jelly in patients with feeding and swallowing disorders, p. 56. The abstract requires member access. A separate conference series from the same hospital studied capsaicin jelly in 2001 and 2002 and is available as original proceedings. The authors differ between the 2001 JSDR and rehabilitation medicine entries, and the sample overlap is unknown. The same JSDR supplement also lists Yoshiura and colleagues' young-onset Parkinson swallowing therapy report, p. 117, which names repeated aspiration pneumonia in the title. It gives no public case details or food exposure. [Nozaki record](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2001/0005s1/016&name=0056-0056j) · [Yoshiura record](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2001/0005s1/129&name=0117-0117j) · [Local note on the 2001–2002 primary experiments](Food-Experiment-Japanese-Swallowing-Followup.md#2001-and-2002-capsaicin-jelly-swallowing-comparisons)

## 2003 chili and ponzu ice massage case listing

Masayo Nakata's I-P1-8 title in the 2003 JSDR proceedings attributes the return of oral intake in one patient to ice massage using chili-treated ponzu ice. The title describes its use for massage and does not establish ingestion, a Parkinson diagnosis, timing or a measured outcome. The provider requires member access to read the abstract. No independent public report emerged from title, author and preparation searches. [Original listing, p. 188](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/archive/search?issue=2&jo=cb5dvsph&vo=7&ye=2003) · [Member abstract](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2003/000702/065&name=0188-0188j)

## 2004 comparison of contrast jelly with sugar water

In a May 2004 publication, Nagaya and colleagues studied 25 people with Parkinson's disease referred for dysphagia at Chubu National Hospital and separately reported 23 people with degenerative cerebellar ataxia. Videofluoroscopy used iopamidol contrast diluted with sugar water and administered from a cup in 5, 7 or 10 mL amounts, plus an unnamed jelly mixed with contrast and administered with a teaspoon. The authors recorded two swallows of each test form and examined Parkinson patients 90–120 minutes after a levodopa dose. Among the 25 Parkinson participants, 13 aspirated the liquid contrast. None of those 13 aspirated during jelly trials. Six of the 13 could perform the tested compensatory maneuvers. Only one avoided liquid aspiration with them. Table 1 matches the count of 13 aspirators on the published page. This is a short instrumental comparison of test forms and delivery methods. The jelly's recipe and potato exposure are unspecified, and the paper reports no longer-term choking or clinical severity outcome. [Publisher text, pp. 18–20 and Table 1](https://www.med.nagoya-u.ac.jp/medlib/nagoya_j_med_sci/6712/p17_23.pdf#page=2) · [Primary citation, PMID 15279064](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15279064/)

## 2000 barium and water during swallowing training

Nagaya, Kachi and Yamada's March 2000 full paper describes ten Parkinson participants with dysphagia and 12 healthy comparison participants. Four of the ten aspirated during videofluoroscopy with liquid barium, and the paper pools five test volumes. Separate cued electromyography used the same 5 mL water stimulus before and after an exercise session. Premotor time fell in the Parkinson group (p = .0051), EMG burst duration showed no significant change, and eight participants reported that swallowing felt easier. The tests give no post-training aspiration outcome or food comparison. [Primary methods and results](https://www.medicaljournals.se/jrm/content_files/download.php?doi=10.1080%2F165019772000321115#page=2) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10782935/)

The 1996 proceedings describe the same training protocol in eight Parkinson participants and eight controls. Four case profiles match across the 2000 and 2004 reports on sex, age, stage, disease duration and aspiration status. The common center and authors make overlap plausible. Participant identities and overlap remain unverified. [1996 abstract](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjrm1964/33/11/33_11_818/_pdf/-char/ja#page=59) · [2004 Table 1](https://www.med.nagoya-u.ac.jp/medlib/nagoya_j_med_sci/6712/p17_23.pdf#page=4)

## 2004 choking and capsaicin proceedings with gated abstracts

Wakabayashi, Nakagawa and Tsukui presented feeding and swallowing rehabilitation in late parkinsonism at the September 2004 JSDR meeting in Niigata. The title does not specify a Parkinson disease subgroup. Separate proceedings entries report a choking case review (I-P10-12) and a pair of capsaicin reports from Midori Hospital (II-3-9 and II-3-10). The two Midori reports share institution and authors and may describe one project. These three public records give no specific diagnoses, meal foods or individual outcomes. A separate capsaicin presentation, I-P1-2, names cerebrovascular disease. All of these MedicalOnline abstract links require member access.
