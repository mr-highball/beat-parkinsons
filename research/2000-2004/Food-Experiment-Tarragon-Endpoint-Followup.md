# Tarragon endpoint follow-up

## Oral Russian tarragon extract and rat grip strength

Ribnicky and colleagues gave male and female Sprague–Dawley rats vehicle or 10, 100 or 1,000 mg/kg/day of Tarralin, an ethanolic Russian tarragon extract, by oral gavage for approximately 90 days. In the week-13 functional observational battery, female rats at 10 and 1,000 mg/kg/day recorded lower forelimb grip than female vehicle controls (p < .05 for each). The 100 mg/kg/day group did not show the reported difference. The authors discounted the isolated findings because they lacked a dose-response pattern. Total photobeam locomotor activity and habituation looked comparable in treated and control groups. This is a related adverse-direction result for 0504, with a sex-specific, nonmonotonic response and different species, preparation and endpoint. Grip force measures strength. The study leaves fine manual dexterity after eating dried leaves unresolved. ## Tarragon in rotated odor enrichment and olfactory memory

Rochefort and colleagues studied adult male C57BL/6J mice in standard housing or with a different fragrance presented daily. Tarragon was one of the 20 enrichment odors in Table 1. Each odor was delivered from a hanging container and the herb's preparation was unspecified. Standard-housed mice recognized a reintroduced test odor after 30 minutes. Enriched mice showed recognition through 240 minutes (p < .001 at 240 minutes), while both groups lacked clear recognition at 480 minutes. The odor-recognition tasks assess memory and do not isolate tarragon's contribution or the effect of eating dried tarragon. Related to 0982. [April 2002 primary paper, Table 1 and Figure 5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6758329/)



## Benzodiazepines measured in tarragon cell culture

Kavvadias and colleagues used HPLC-ESI-MS/MS to identify delorazepam and temazepam in cultured *Artemisia dracunculus* cell tissue, approximately 100–200 ng/g for the reported compounds. This is a preparation-specific analytical lead and supplies no oral bioavailability, dietary tissue concentration or sleep endpoint for 0927. [March 2000 primary report, PMID 10694515](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10694515/)
