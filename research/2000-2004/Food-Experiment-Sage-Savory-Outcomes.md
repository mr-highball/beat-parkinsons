# Sage and summer savory symptom studies

## Sage leaf extract and cognition in Alzheimer disease

The Methods identify dried Salvia officinalis leaves in an alcoholic extract administered orally. The abstract describes 42 randomized, while the Results report 39 randomized and 30 completing. Agitation was reported in one treated and six placebo participants (p=.09). The outcome is separate from a measured anxiety comparison. The placement of this citation in a later aromatherapy review does not establish inhaled oil exposure. [Original record and local factual text](Source-Text/2003-Akhondzadeh-Sage-Leaf-Extract.txt)

Akhondzadeh and colleagues randomized 42 people with mild to moderate Alzheimer disease to placebo or an alcoholic extract of dried common sage leaves for 16 weeks. Thirty completed. The report gives a favorable between-arm difference in ADAS-cog (p = .03) and Clinical Dementia Rating Sum of Boxes (p < .003). Table 2 describes agitation as an adverse event, with the difference inconclusive (p = .09). Apathy severity was not reported. The extract, population and outcomes differ from claim 0906. ## Abdominal pain checklist in the sage extract trial

The 2003 Alzheimer cognition trial used an oral extract of dried common-sage leaves. Table 2 lists abdominal pain in two extract recipients and no placebo recipients (Fisher P=.23). The abstract and Results give different trial counts, so these few adverse events cannot be translated into a reliable group rate using the reported enrollment numbers. The report did not measure symptom severity. This is the same trial cited above. [Local original-source summary](Source-Text/2003-Akhondzadeh-Sage-Leaf-Extract.txt)
