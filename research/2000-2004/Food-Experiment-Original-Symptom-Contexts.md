# Original symptom reports and source context

## Resting tremor and visual hallucinations in parkin-linked parkinsonism

October 2001. On page 532, Bonifati and colleagues report that the sister in a parkin-linked family had mild bilateral resting upper-limb tremor and a three-year history of visual hallucinations and confusion at age 89. Recorded medication included levodopa, amantadine and biperiden. Her brother had no tremor at examination. The paper gives no episode count, serial tremor estimate, or edible fig comparison. The indexed original journal PDF supplied the clinical pages. [Original source](https://jnnp.bmj.com/content/jnnp/71/4/531.full.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2001-Bonifati-Resting-Tremor-and-Hallucinations.txt)

## Visual hallucination status in a diagnostic comparison

November 2001. Table 2 on page 585 in Yoshita and colleagues gives presence or absence of visual hallucinations for each of 14 clinically diagnosed dementia-with-Lewy-bodies patients. The study compares cardiac MIBG uptake and autonomic features with probable Alzheimer-type dementia. It gives no hallucination episode rate or edible fig or salmon exposure. The final table row has a merged token in the indexed PDF rendering, so this review does not tally the number with hallucinations. [Original source](https://jnnp.bmj.com/content/jnnp/71/5/583.full.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2001-Yoshita-Lewy-Body-Hallucination-Status.txt)

## Postural tremor and bradykinesia in a treated parkinsonism case

January 2002. On page 111 in Yamada and colleagues, a 41-year-old man with parkinsonism had mild bradykinesia and postural hand tremor. The exam explicitly records absence of resting tremor. Following trihexyphenidyl the report describes improvement in gait, rigidity and bradykinesia, with total UPDRS motor score changing from 33 to 18 at four weeks. No individual bradykinesia score or food comparison is given. The indexed original PDF supplied the clinical pages. [Original source](https://jnnp.bmj.com/content/jnnp/72/1/111.full.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2002-Yamada-Postural-Tremor-and-Bradykinesia.txt)

## Dystonia and rigidity in slowly progressive aphasia cases

2002. Nagaratnam and colleagues describe four progressive aphasia cases with striatal signs on pages 18–22. Case 1 developed limb and axial rigidity with neck dystonia. Case 2 had increased limb tone and dystonic right-hand posture. Case 3 developed rigidity. The retrospective cases include no sage exposure or symptom comparisons by food. The publisher-supplied preview gave the complete article narrative and citations. Figures were unavailable in the readable rendering. [Original source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11530107_Slowly_progressive_aphasia_with_striatal_involvement) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2002-Nagaratnam-Aphasia-Dystonia-and-Rigidity.txt)

## Visual hallucination prevalence in dementia with Lewy bodies

May 2002. Doubleday and colleagues report current visual hallucinations in 71% of 41 selected dementia-with-Lewy-bodies cases. They excluded cases whose hallucinations coincided with changes in antiparkinsonian medication. None of the 26 selected Alzheimer’s disease comparators had a history of visual hallucinations. Pages 602–603 concern symptom prevalence and eligibility. They give no episode frequency or fig or salmon food comparison. The indexed original journal PDF supplied the full report. [Original source](https://jnnp.bmj.com/content/jnnp/72/5/602.full.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2002-Doubleday-Hallucination-Prevalence.txt)

## Tremor scores after thalamotomy and publication lineage

July–September 2002 issue. Baik and colleagues assessed 15 people with medically refractory parkinsonian tremor before and about four months after unilateral thalamotomy. On the 0–4 tremor scale, three patients scored 2, ten scored 3 and two scored 4 before surgery. On follow-up thirteen scored 0 and two scored 1. Scale anchors include action and postural tremor, so they do not isolate rest tremor. The paper reports imaging measures and does not quantify rigidity or examine edible fig or sage. The 2007 journal notice identifies the 2003 European Journal of Radiology paper as the same study. [Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2713882/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2002-Baik-Thalamotomy-Tremor-Scale.txt)

## Fatigue scores during dopamine agonist add-on treatment

2001–2002 date discrepancy. The volume is indexed as 2001, the original prints 2001/2002 and Wiley dates first publication September 28, 2002. Abe and colleagues compare five weeks of adjunctive pergolide in 25 and bromocriptine in 16 people receiving levodopa/carbidopa. Fatigue changed from 5.1 to 4.4 in the pergolide group (paired p < .05) and from 4.8 to 4.7 in the bromocriptine group (reported nonsignificant). The report does not clearly give a between-arm change estimate. Baseline pergolide fatigue SD is 0.7 in the prose/figure and 1.0 in Table 2. Mean doses also differ between the Methods and Table 2. Total UPDRS motor scores do not provide a separate rest-tremor result. The original does not evaluate culinary fig or apple. [Original source](https://downloads.hindawi.com/journals/bn/2002/473140.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2002-Abe-Fatigue-and-Tremor-Context.txt)

## Hallucination history and motor signs in a Lewy pathology cohort

July 2003. Colosimo and colleagues selected 38 patients from 276 brains with Parkinson pathology. Visual hallucinations were recorded at some point in 24 of 38 patients, including 8 of 17 without recorded cognitive impairment and 16 of 21 with late dementia. Twenty-nine patients had resting tremor and all had akinesia and rigidity. The full case histories and Table 1 give clinical occurrence without episode frequencies or a quantified bradykinesia comparison. Salmon is a cited author surname. No salmon food exposure was evaluated. [Original source](https://jnnp.bmj.com/content/jnnp/74/7/852.full.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2003-Colosimo-Hallucination-and-Motor-History.txt)

## Baseline bradykinesia and mood review before stimulation

November 2003 issue. The original PDF states first published November 14. Whelan and colleagues compare language test performance in five stimulation patients and 16 nonsurgical Parkinson’s disease controls. Table 1 lists bradykinesia as the leading motor symptom in two of the five surgical patients. The baseline evaluation reported no indication of anxiety or depression in the surgical patients. There were no anxiety severity estimates or food comparisons, and the treatment outcomes were linguistic rather than bradykinesia scores. Source pages 1544–1545. [Original source](https://jnnp.bmj.com/content/jnnp/74/11/1543.full.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2003-Whelan-DBS-Motor-and-Mood-Context.txt)
