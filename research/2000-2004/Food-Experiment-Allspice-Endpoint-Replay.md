# Allspice preparation and endpoint search

## Eugenol and orange odor in an attention oddball task

Masago and colleagues published this study in the January 2001 journal issue. Seven healthy male students, aged 21–23, took part. Exposures were isolated eugenol and orange oil odor, delivered in an oddball paradigm with instructions to attend or ignore. The primary paper printed no odor-quality difference in component latencies. It reported a larger P3 for orange than eugenol and earlier responses and larger P3 with the attend instructions. The P3 result is an olfactory ERP contrast between odorants and depends on task context. The measured outcome is distinct from changing the severity of Parkinson attention impairment after ingestion. ## Allspice plant parts in a methyleugenol position paper

The position paper lists *Pimenta dioica* leaf and *Pimenta officinalis* oil as separate entries in its constituent table. Its discussion of CNS effects refers to isolated methyleugenol. Species nomenclature, plant part and extraction matter when using these table entries for exposure identification. The document supplies regulatory context for the compound and leaves the two ground-allspice symptom outcomes unmeasured. [Original EMA position paper and constituent table](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/position/working-party-herbal-medicinal-products-final-position-paper-use-herbal-medicinal-products-containing-methyleugenol_en.pdf) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2004-EMA-Methyleugenol-Allspice-Exposure-Context.txt)
