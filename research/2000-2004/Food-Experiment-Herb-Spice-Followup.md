# Herb and spice evidence follow-up, 2000–2004

## Dai-kenchu-to and constipation

Sakakibara and colleagues first published their results on December 13, 2004, ahead of the February 2005 issue. They followed six men with Parkinson’s disease and four with multiple system atrophy for 12 weeks in an unblinded, nonrandomized before-and-after study. The granulated formula combined ginger, ginseng and Japanese pepper. All six participants with Parkinson’s reported improved bowel movement frequency and ease of defecation. Their mean total colonic transit time in Table 1 fell from 88.7 to 64.6 hours. The significance markings apply to pooled measurements across diagnoses. The participants served as their own treatment comparison. The total formula dose was 15 g per day split across three administrations. Neither grated ginger nor toasted Sichuan pepper was tested separately, and Japanese pepper is a related species. [Publisher first-public date](https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.20352) · [Primary article text, Methods and Table 1](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8133216_Dietary_herb_extract_Dai-kenchu-to_ameliorates_constipation_in_parkinsonian_patients_Parkinson%27s_disease_and_multiple_system_atrophy)

Table 1 also gives Parkinson subgroup mean rectosigmoid transit of 41.1 hours before treatment and 23.3 hours after. No Parkinson-only uncertainty interval or p-value was reported. The 2018 review describes transit as unchanged in Parkinson’s. That wording differs from the numerical means and does not establish a significant subgroup result. The printed pooled baseline total transit of 92 hours also differs from the 95.94-hour weighted average of the subgroup means. The cause is unknown, so the published values remain as printed. All six Parkinson participants were taking levodopa, and none of the ten participants took anticholinergics. [Original Table 1](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8133216_Dietary_herb_extract_Dai-kenchu-to_ameliorates_constipation_in_parkinsonian_patients_Parkinson%27s_disease_and_multiple_system_atrophy) · [2018 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5856748/)

An April 2004 Japanese record in *The Autonomic Nervous System* lists the same author group and related transit and manometry topics. Its full body and cohort information remain unverified. The later conference report supplies a possibly overlapping six-Parkinson, four-MSA design, with demographic and reported significance differences. [April 2004 bibliographic record](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/en/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=200902225304569216) · [Later conference report](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#daikenchuto-conference-report-and-possible-cohort-overlap)

## Cinnamon odor in a brief smell identification test

Double et al. compared 49 people with Parkinson’s and 52 age- and sex-matched controls using the 12-item Brief Smell Identification Test. A set of five odor stimuli including cinnamon, banana, and pineapple distinguished the groups. Participants identified an odor during testing, so the comparison gives no estimate of the effect of consuming ground cinnamon on [0979, olfactory impairment](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0979.txt). [Primary report, PMID 12707068](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12707068/)

## A perilla and ginger formula and the swallowing reflex

Iwasaki et al. evaluated swallowing-reflex latency before and after four weeks of Banxia Houpo Tang, also called Hangekobokuto, in Parkinson’s disease. The abstract reports 23 patients assessed. The recovered journal text describes 28 Parkinson patients, 22 given the extract and six given lactate, plus five healthy controls given the extract. The article does not explain the discrepancy between these counts or specify how patients were allocated. Examiners were unaware of assignment. After four weeks, mean latency in the extract group fell from 3.66 to 2.27 seconds. The lactate group stayed near 3.6 seconds. The report gives within-group significance and no formal between-group change test. Salivary substance P and Hoehn and Yahr stage did not show a detected change. The boiled and freeze-dried five-ingredient extract included dried ginger rhizome and perilla leaf. The outcome was reflex latency after pharyngeal water infusion, with no isolated culinary ingredient, rigidity result or pneumonia follow-up. [Primary report, PMID 10969718](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10969718/) · [Author article scan and text, pp. 260–261](https://www.academia.edu/20237656/The_effects_of_the_traditional_Chinese_medicine_Banxia_Houpo_Tang_Hange_Koboku_To_on_the_swallowing_reflex_in_Parkinsons_disease)

The five-component formula includes medicinal perilla herb and ginger. The Japanese medicinal plant database identifies perilla herb as shiso leaf. The result gives human Parkinson’s evidence about a mixed formula and an objective swallowing reflex. The frozen shiso and ginger claims specify culinary preparations and other endpoints, such as [0398, shiso and bradykinesia](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0398.txt) and [0581, ginger and constipation](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0581.txt). [Prescription product information](https://www.tsumura.co.jp/english/products/pi/JPR_T016.pdf) · [Japanese medicinal plant database](https://mpdb.nibn.go.jp/mpdb-bin/view_crude_drug.cgi?id=41&lang=ja)

## Sasaki Dai-Kenchu-To primary report trail

A 2001 article from Sasaki and colleagues in *The Autonomic Nervous System* 38(4), 348–352, addresses abdominal fullness and constipation in Parkinson disease. A meeting item earlier that year has the same title and five authors. A 2002 report has three overlapping authors. Public records establish the publication trail and leave outcomes and participant overlap unresolved. The 2011 Japanese Neurological Society guideline's Dai-Kenchu-To citation is Sakakibara and colleagues, a different group whose article was first published in 2004. Its outcome values cannot be assigned to the Sasaki reports. ## Rosemary aroma and mood after an acute laboratory stressor

Burnett, Solterbeck and Strapp published in October 2004. In 73 healthy adults, the study compared rosemary scent, lavender scent and water after an anxiety-provoking task. The original abstract reports higher tension and confusion and lower vigor with rosemary than comparators after odor-pleasantness adjustment. It gives no depression-specific numbers or adjusted depression comparison. A later secondary table supplies a numerical depression estimate with a wide interval, but the original body remains inaccessible. The experiment did not administer a culinary leaf preparation or enroll people with Parkinson disease.
