# Allspice and cumin evidence

## Allspice fruit suspension and acute animal observations

Al-Rehaily and colleagues tested an aqueous suspension of market-sourced allspice fruit in animal assays of inflammation, pain behavior, fever and gastric injury. Their acute toxicity screen reported no death or overt adverse effect at the observed doses through 7.5 g/kg. The experiments did not include sleep recordings, motor assessments relevant to Parkinson's, or an antidepressant test. Statements about allspice massage and depression in the introduction describe traditional use. [Primary report, Methods and Results](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1076/phbi.40.3.200.5829)

## Roasting and microwave heating of cumin seeds

The comparative food-chemistry study heated *Cuminum cyminum* seeds conventionally or with microwaves and analyzed volatile composition. This verifies that processing conditions matter when comparing culinary cumin with an unheated extract or an isolated volatile. The report measures chemistry. No symptom outcome was recorded. [Primary publisher report](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0308814603005405)
