# Mechanisms and Models for 2000–2004

### 2000 — Mitochondrial inhibition and Parkinson-like features in an animal model

Betarbet and colleagues reported that a pesticide exposure model in rats produced motor changes, dopaminergic injury, and inclusions containing alpha-synuclein. The study linked mitochondrial dysfunction with several Parkinson-like features in that model. It could not quantify the effect of human exposure on disease incidence. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11100151/)

### 2003 — Braak staging from human postmortem pathology

Braak and colleagues compared distributions of alpha-synuclein pathology in incidental and symptomatic cases and proposed stages based on the regions affected. The earliest proposed stages included olfactory and lower brainstem structures. The inferred sequence came from cross-sectional autopsy patterns. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12498954/)

### 2003 — DJ-1 variants in recessive early-onset parkinsonism

Bonifati and colleagues identified DJ-1 mutations in inherited early-onset parkinsonism. The team proposed a connection with the cellular stress response. Family segregation establishes relevance to those inherited forms and does not determine prevalence in typical disease. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12446870/)

### 2004 — PINK1 variants and hereditary early-onset disease

Valente and colleagues identified PINK1 mutations in families with recessive early-onset disease. Cell studies suggested a mitochondrial stress-response role. The report linked a human genetic finding with mitochondrial hypotheses. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15087508/)

### 2004 — PARK8-linked variants in five families

Paisán-Ruíz and colleagues found variants in the gene later widely referred to as LRRK2 that segregated with Parkinson’s disease in families from England and Spain. This was one of two reports identifying the gene in the same issue. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15541308/)

### 2004 — LRRK2 variants and variable autopsy pathology

Zimprich and colleagues identified dominant LRRK2-associated parkinsonism and reported different postmortem patterns among carriers, including cases without characteristic Lewy-body pathology. The observations showed pathological variability within a genetic form. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15541309/)
