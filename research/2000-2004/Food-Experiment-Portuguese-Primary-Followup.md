# Primary Portuguese-language diet research

## Farhud dietary assessment at USP

Cláudia Carvalheira Farhud defended *Avaliação dietética de indivíduos com doença de Parkinson* at Universidade de São Paulo on August 2, 2001. The university abstract describes 100 participants with Parkinson’s disease, mean age 68.8 years. Three-day food records supplied the diet estimates. The comparison variables were sex, age and time since diagnosis. The abstract reports inadequate energy, macronutrient and calcium intake and adequate vitamins A and C. The publicly retrievable abstract gives no individual food or symptom comparison, and the record supplies no thesis download. [USP thesis record 001195056](https://repositorio.usp.br/item/001195056)

Farhud and her advisor Maria de Fátima Nunes Marucci also appear on the [1999 iron conference record](../1995-1999/Food-Experiment-Portuguese-Followup.md). The thesis abstract names calcium and vitamins A and C among the micronutrients assessed and gives no iron result. A 2004 *Gerontologia* paper bears the same title as the thesis and credits Farhud and Marucci. D’Oliveira and colleagues’ 2007 review attributes the same sample size of 100 and the same finding of insufficient energy and macronutrient intake to the 2004 paper. The two reports may reuse one sample. The review proposes motor and swallowing difficulties as possible reasons for insufficient intake. It gives no measured food-by-symptom association for the cohort and does not connect it to the 1999 iron abstract. The original 2004 paper remains unrecovered. [USP 1999 proceedings record](https://repositorio.usp.br/item/001177146), [2007 Nutrire review, p. 82](https://sban.org.br/publicacoes/155.pdf#page=6)

## Pereira Farhud and Marucci nutrition publication

OpenAlex work W2220696248 and its LILACS pointer attach all three authors to the title *A alimentação na Doença de Parkinson* and include magazine-like volume, issue and page fields. Contemporary references distinguish two works of this title. The July–August 2001 *Nutrição em Pauta* article credits only Cláudia Carvalheira Farhud and Maria de Fátima Nunes Marucci. Rodrigues and Cechella cite it as issue 49, pp. 54–56. They separately cite a 2001 second-edition booklet by Estefânia Maria Soares Pereira, Farhud and Marucci from Boehringer Ingelheim. Pereira’s bibliography lists a 2000 Associação Brasil Parkinson booklet by the same three authors. The association of W2220696248 with either edition remains unverified. [OpenAlex work](https://api.openalex.org/works/W2220696248), [BVS record LIL-283203](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/portal/resource/pt/lil-283203), [2002 review, bibliography](https://periodicos.ufn.edu.br/index.php/disciplinarumS/article/download/845/788#page=9), [Pereira bibliography](https://www.escavador.com/sobre/938654/estefania-maria-soares-pereira)

The archived publisher page carries the two-author magazine summary and identifies the July–August 2001 print issue as the location of the full article. The summary describes Parkinson’s symptoms, feeding difficulty and nutritional implications. It includes no original cohort, diet intervention or food-by-symptom result. The 2002 review attributes advice on protein redistribution and energy-dense meals to the full article, without reproducing primary data. Neither booklet text nor the full magazine article was located through the archived publisher, the cited course PDF URL or the accessible library records. [Archived publisher summary, article 398](https://web.archive.org/web/20140313075115id_/http://www.nutricaoempauta.com.br/lista_artigo.php?cod=398), [2002 review, diet and levodopa discussion](https://periodicos.ufn.edu.br/index.php/disciplinarumS/article/download/845/788#page=8), [course citation and original PDF URL](https://ric.cps.sp.gov.br/bitstream/123456789/14549/1/TALHER%20ESTA%CC%81VEL%20-RUBIA%20ARCHILA%20LIMA%20DE%20ALMEIDA%20MECATRONICA.pdf)

## Early Lisbon report of regular and decaffeinated espresso

Ferreira and colleagues’ oral abstract CO1 appeared in *Sinapse*, volume 2, issue 2, in November 2002, for the Portuguese neurology congress held November 7–10 in Espinho. Four Lisbon patients with Parkinson’s disease and Epworth Sleepiness Scale scores above 9 entered randomized single-patient crossovers of caffeinated and decaffeinated espresso, with two days of washout between treatments. The primary outcome was a seven-point daytime sleepiness rating. The abstract reports a preliminary benefit from caffeinated espresso for two participants. It lists a secondary visual analog scale without naming its domain or giving fatigue scores. The coffee supplier provided espresso machines, coffee and unmarked sachets for blinding. [Publisher issue, abstract p. 71 (PDF p. 73)](https://sinapse.pt/index.php/journal/issue/download/6/3#page=73) · [Congress program, PDF p. 59](https://sinapse.pt/index.php/journal/issue/download/6/3#page=59)

The report says all four participants completed three crossover sequences. The 2009 and 2016 reports describe four participants, three of whom completed three pairs while one completed two. The four-person enrollment, coauthors, location, intervention and two apparent sleepiness responders indicate probable overlap, with exact participant correspondence unverified. The 2016 report provides separate fatigue ratings.
