Title: Increased stool frequency occurs when finely processed pea hull fiber is added to usual foods consumed by elderly residents in long-term care.
PMID: 12963953
DOI: 10.1016/s0002-8223(03)00977-5
First published: 2003
Authors: Dahl WJ, Whiting SJ, Healey A, Zello GA, Hildebrandt SL
Journal: Journal of the American Dietetic Association
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12963953/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 4 review).

Primary Abstract Facts: Six-week uncontrolled before-and-after intervention in 114 elderly long-term care residents adding finely processed pea hull fiber (1-3 g/serving, 3-4 foods/day) to usual meals. Mean bowel movements/month rose from 18.7 +/- 9.4 to 20.1 +/- 9.6 (P=0.034), and among 17 low-frequency residents rose from 8.8 +/- 1.0 to 12.6 +/- 3.8 (P=0.001), with reduced prune-based laxative use (P<0.001). Critical Appraisal: Uncontrolled before-and-after design (no placebo arm), non-PD institutionalized elderly, and isolated finely processed yellow pea hull fiber (~85-92% cellulose-rich hull fiber) rather than whole steamed green peas; subsequently tempered by blinded randomized crossovers (Alyousif 2020, van den Belt 2025).
