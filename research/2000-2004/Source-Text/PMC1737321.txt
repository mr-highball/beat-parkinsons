Title: Pentagon copying is more impaired in dementia with Lewy bodies than in Alzheimer's disease
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11254771/
Publisher: https://jnnp.bmj.com/content/70/4/483
Source alias: https://www.academia.edu/58203502/Pentagon_copying_is_more_impaired_in_dementia_with_Lewy_bodies_than_in_Alzheimers_disease

Publication date: 2001-04
Date basis: NLM, JNNP 70(4), 483–488
Text coverage: Original article rendering, pages 483–488, including methods, results, figures and references. Research summary.

Ala and colleagues reviewed initial screening records for people with neuropathologically confirmed Lewy body dementia or Alzheimer disease. Two cases with Parkinson disease dementia were excluded by the timing of their motor symptoms before dementia. Using original scoring criteria, 2 of 17 Lewy body patients and 16 of 27 Alzheimer patients copied the interlocking pentagons acceptably (Fisher exact p=0.002). Relaxed criteria yielded 8 of 17 and 20 of 27 (p=0.11). The study discusses visual hallucinations as a clinical diagnostic feature but measures visuospatial construction. It supplies no fig exposure or visual hallucination episode count.

The January 2002 letter and author reply discuss possible grading and model-orientation effects without furnishing another estimate of a dietary effect. See 2002-PMID11784847-Pentagon-Orientation-Correspondence.txt.
