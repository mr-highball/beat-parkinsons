Title: Neurotoxicities in infants seen with the consumption of star anise tea.
PMID: 15492355
DOI: 10.1542/peds.2004-0058
First published: 2004
Authors: Ize-Ludlow D, Ragone S, Bruck IS, Bernstein JN, Duchowny M, Peña BM
Journal: Pediatrics
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15492355/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Clinical case series and botanical investigation (Ize-Ludlow et al., 2004, Pediatrics, DOI:10.1542/peds.2004-0058; companion JAMA letter PMID:14762034) reporting 7 infants presenting with acute adverse neurological reactions (seizures, jitteriness, abnormal movements, vomiting) after home administration of star anise tea for colic, with botanical/chemical evidence that Chinese star anise (Illicium verum) was contaminated with toxic Japanese star anise (Illicium anisatum). Landmark safety record establishing the critical adulteration neurotoxicity boundary; prompted FDA warning against star anise teas for infants.
