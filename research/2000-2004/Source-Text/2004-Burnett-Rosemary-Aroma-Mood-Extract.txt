Burnett et al. Psychological Reports 95:707–722 (2004). PMID15587240. DOI 10.2466/pr0.95.2.707-722. Factual text from https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2466/pr0.95.2.707-722
Seventy-three nonsmoking participants aged 18–30 received rosemary, lavender or water scent during an anxiety-provoking task. The original abstract gives a higher tension-anxiety and confusion rating and lower vigor with rosemary; fatigue-inertia was lower with the two scent conditions than control. The primary abstract does not give an estimate for depression or disclose the specific rosemary preparation.
Full article remains restricted. A secondary extraction reporting a depression score does not confirm the original adjusted analysis. No oral fresh leaf intervention or Parkinson cohort.
