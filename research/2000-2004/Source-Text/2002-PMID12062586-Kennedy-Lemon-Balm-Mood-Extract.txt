Title: Modulation of mood and cognitive performance following acute administration of Melissa officinalis (lemon balm)

Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

Kennedy and colleagues used a randomized, double-blind, counterbalanced crossover in 20 healthy young adults. Each volunteer received placebo and three single-dose conditions seven days apart. Six matched capsules contained either placebo or standardized extract, for 300, 600 or 900 mg total in the active conditions. The extract comparison did not use a brewed infusion.

The Bond–Lader scales assessed calmness, alertness and contentedness. Against placebo at one hour, the calmness change was more favorable after 300 mg (mean change −2.25 versus −12.53 mm, planned comparison p=0.002) and after 900 mg (−4.80 versus −12.53 mm, p=0.019). At 2.5 hours, the 300 mg change was −1.05 versus −8.30 mm (p=0.028). The sample means dropped from each visit's baseline in those comparisons. The favorable result was a smaller drop than with placebo. The 600 mg comparisons did not show a clear mood effect.

The original reports lower alertness after 900 mg at each follow-up time and after 300 mg at six hours. Contentedness showed no clear effect. The computerized battery had a favorable attention-accuracy result with 600 mg and some unfavorable memory outcomes. These within-study findings have distinct endpoints. The acute mood ratings provide no estimate on a clinical depressive-symptom scale or in Parkinson disease.
