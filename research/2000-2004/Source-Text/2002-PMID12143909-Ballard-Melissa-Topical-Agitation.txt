Title: Topical Melissa essential oil for agitation in severe dementia
First published: 2002-07-16
PMID: 12143909
DOI: 10.4088/jcp.v63n0703
Source: https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/aromatherapy-safe-effective-treatment-management-agitation/
Archive scope: Publisher author abstract. Main trial body remains a separate access check.

Seventy-two UK care-facility residents with severe dementia and significant agitation were randomized to Melissa essential oil or sunflower oil in lotion. Staff applied the preparation to the face and arms twice daily for four weeks. Seventy-one completed. Twenty-one of 35 active and five of 36 control participants reached the reported 30% reduction in the Cohen–Mansfield Agitation Inventory. Mean percentage reductions were 35% and 11%, respectively (reported p<0.0001). Time spent socially withdrawn or constructively engaged also favored active treatment in the abstract. These outcomes concern agitation and observed behavior, without a Parkinson-specific or depression measure. The published 2003 discussion identifies gaps in how the original subscales were described. The larger 2011 trial provides a separate comparison with a different rating and blinding design.
