Title: Saffron stigma extracts and constituents in the mouse forced-swim test
Source: https://www.actahort.org/books/650/650_54.htm
Original article copy: https://safranerio.fr/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/650_54.pdf
DOI: 10.17660/ActaHortic.2004.650.54
Publication date: 2004
Date basis: Acta Horticulturae volume 650, proceedings publication year.
Review basis: Complete publisher-formatted original copy, checked against the publisher record. Methods, Results, Tables 1–2 and Figures 1–6 reviewed.
Source type: Animal experiment in male BALB/c mice, with saline, fluoxetine and imipramine comparisons as applicable.

Aqueous or ethanolic saffron stigma extracts, and the isolated compounds safranal and crocin, were administered by intraperitoneal injection. Figure 2 reports five mice for each behavior comparison. Its higher safranal arm has lower mean immobility than saline with the p<.001 marker. The two lower safranal arms do not show a favorable significance marker in that figure. Figure 4 shows a favorable swimming-time result at the higher safranal arm. In the open-field comparison, Table 2 reports reduced total locomotion in its highest safranal group, with means of 65.1 versus 151.1 for saline and an indicated p<.05. The open-field and forced-swim measures capture different behaviors.

The study is internally inconsistent about the safranal dose unit. Figures 2, 4 and 6 label the safranal arms in mL/kg, while Table 2 and parts of the Results use mg/kg. The Results and figures also differ about the significance pattern at intermediate crocin or extract levels. The original labels remain unresolved. No unit conversion or pooling across tests was applied.

This is a preparation-specific animal behavioral proxy. It provides no clinical Parkinson depressive-symptom assessment and no test of orally consumed ground stigma. This original is distinct from the seizure study cited as reference 110 in the 2019 saffron bioavailability review, PMID 31382514.
