Title: Toxicological evaluation of the ethanolic extract of Artemisia dracunculus L. for use as a dietary supplement and in functional foods
Citation: Ribnicky DM et al. Food Chem Toxicol. 2004 Apr;42(4):585–598.
DOI: 10.1016/j.fct.2003.11.002
PMID: 15019182
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15019182/

Text type: local factual extraction from sections 2.2.4 and 3.5.4–5

Male and female Sprague–Dawley rats received Russian tarragon ethanolic extract (Tarralin) by gavage for approximately 90 days at 0, 10, 100 or 1,000 mg/kg/day. The extract was made from previously frozen, above-ground hydroponic *Artemisia dracunculus*. A week-13 functional observational battery measured forelimb and hindlimb grip and foot splay. Female rats at 10 and 1,000 mg/kg/day had lower mean forelimb grip than female controls (individual p < .05). The authors considered this pattern of uncertain biological significance because it did not increase with dose. Total automated photobeam movement and habituation appeared comparable across conditions. The outcome is grip strength rather than dexterity, with a different exposure and population from claim 0504.
