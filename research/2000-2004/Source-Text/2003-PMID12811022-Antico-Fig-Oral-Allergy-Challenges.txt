Title: Oral allergy syndrome to fig
PMID: 12811022
DOI: 10.1159/000070929
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12811022/
Publisher: https://doi.org/10.1159/000070929
Publication: 2003, International Archives of Allergy and Immunology 131(2), pp. 138–142
Coverage: Structured author abstract and publisher issue contents. Complete case descriptions remain open.

Antico and colleagues investigated two patients with fig-associated oral allergy and pollinosis. Both developed oral allergy symptoms followed by respiratory symptoms during fig challenge. Testing with the study's fig preparations indicated stronger allergenicity of skin than pulp. Immunoblotting detected candidate soluble allergens in the skin. Tests using commercial fig extracts and tests to weeping fig and rubber latex were negative in both cases. These two reports add an oral-challenge comparison to the later case of acute drooling after fresh fig.

The abstract gives no separate drooling grade, saliva measurement or Parkinson population. The challenge preparation, symptom sequence at the patient level and test results beyond the abstract require the original full paper.
