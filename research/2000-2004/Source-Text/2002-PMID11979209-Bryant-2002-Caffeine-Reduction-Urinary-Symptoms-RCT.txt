Title: Caffeine reduction education to improve urinary symptoms.
PMID: 11979209
DOI: 10.12968/bjon.2002.11.8.10165
First published: 2002
Authors: Bryant CM, Dowell CJ, Fairbrother G
Journal: British journal of nursing (Mark Allen Publishing)
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11979209/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Prospective randomized trial in n=95 adult continence-clinic patients with urinary symptoms comparing caffeine-reduction education vs control over 1 month. Experimental group significantly reduced caffeine intake (p<0.0001) and significantly improved urinary urgency (p=0.002) and voiding frequency (p=0.037), supporting a worsening effect of dietary caffeine on urinary urgency. Limits: non-PD adults; mixed dietary caffeine sources rather than brewed black tea alone.
