Title: Fennel and chamomile drops compared with an untreated menstrual cycle
Source: https://www.virascience.com/en/article/359261/
Citation: Yazdani M, Shahriyari M and Hamedi B. Hormozgan Medical Journal 8(1):57–61, 2004.
Publication year: 2004
Date basis: Journal volume and year corroborated by the 2019 journal citation.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26
Coverage: Author abstract in Persian. The full journal article and its tables remain unavailable.

The study followed 60 people with premenstrual syndrome across five cycles. The first cycle was untreated. The participants took fennel drops in the next two cycles and chamomile drops in the final two. They completed a 16-item symptom questionnaire after each cycle. The abstract calls the first cycle placebo in the results and explicitly describes it as untreated in the methods.

The authors report lower abdominal or pelvic pain, fatigue and lethargy, and depression with anger across the treatment-cycle comparisons (reported p < .05). The abstract attributes the stronger improvement in fatigue and lethargy to fennel, without giving arm means, variance, an exact fatigue p value or a period-adjusted contrast. Fixed treatment order, no inert placebo, previous-cycle carryover and multiple outcomes limit attribution.

This is an indirect premenstrual fatigue finding. The drops are a different preparation from toasted fennel seed. No Parkinson population was studied.

Citation cross-reference: The Motavalli journal article cites both a thesis and the journal report. Treat these as a single study family unless the original records establish independent recruitment.
