Title: Anticonvulsant activity and chemical composition of Artemisia dracunculus L. essential oil
Citation: Sayyah M, Nadjafnia L, Kamalinejad M. J Ethnopharmacol. 2004 Oct;94(2–3):283–287.
DOI: 10.1016/j.jep.2004.05.021
PMID: 15325732
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15325732/
Full text reviewed: https://www.academia.edu/28296804/Anticonvulsant_activity_and_chemical_composition_of_Artemisia_dracunculus_L_essential_oil
Text type: local factual extraction from sections 2.1–2.4, 2.8 and 3.3 and Figure 3

Aerial parts of tarragon collected fresh in Karadj were hydrodistilled. Male NMRI mice received essential oil by intraperitoneal injection with sesame oil as vehicle. The rotarod methods list four groups of ten using vehicle or 0.4, 0.8 or 1.0 mL/kg of the essential oil. Figure 3 reports an evaluation 30 minutes after injection, with p < .001 marked at specific plotted comparisons. The authors observed motor impairment and sedation. Section 3.3 gives an oil level of 0.1 mL/kg and higher, incompatible with the lowest oil dose in the rotarod methods. The exact threshold is unresolved. Separately, PTZ clonus and electroshock-induced hind limb tonic extension index seizure responses. These measures do not assess Parkinson-specific manual dexterity, dystonia, gait freezing or insomnia severity.
