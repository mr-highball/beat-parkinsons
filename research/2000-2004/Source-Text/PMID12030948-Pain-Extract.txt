Primary study factual extract
Bortolotti M, Coccia G, Grossi G, Miglioli M. The treatment of functional dyspepsia with red pepper. Aliment Pharmacol Ther. 2002;16(6):1075–1082. PMID12030948. DOI 10.1046/j.1365-2036.2002.01280.x.
Sources https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12030948/ and https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1046/j.1365-2036.2002.01280.x. Earlier same-study letter PMID11907302, https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM200203213461219.
Thirty patients with functional dyspepsia, excluding IBS and gastroesophageal reflux disease, were assigned 15 per arm after two weeks of washout. Five weeks of 2.5 g/day red pepper powder in capsules before meals was compared double-blind with placebo capsules. The prior letter specifies Capsicum annuum powder with 0.7 mg capsaicin per gram. From week three the reported epigastric pain score favored active treatment. The early report gives week-three pain means of 1.25 ± 0.6 versus 2.30 ± 0.5 and week-five means of 1.15 ± 0.5 versus 2.32 ± 0.6 (mean ± SD). Two of 15 active patients discontinued for epigastric pain and one of 15 placebo patients discontinued for another reason. The primary abstract did not identify placebo constituents or a cayenne cultivar.
