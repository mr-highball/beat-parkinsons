Title: Anaphylaxis associated with antiphospholipid syndrome
Authors: Armentia and colleagues
Primary original abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11476463/
Case description preserved by the FDA (2011-09-09, printed pages 99–100): https://www.fda.gov/media/81223/download
Identifiers: PMID 11476463, DOI 10.1016/S1081-1206(10)62323-X
Date: July 2001
Coverage: factual extract of primary abstract and associated regulatory dossier, prepared 2026-11-14

The authors report on four patients with severe reactions after ingestion of plant foods and later thrombosis with antiphospholipid syndrome. The measurements included patient-specific food IgE, skin prick tests and anticardiolipin antibodies. The abstract mentions one blackberry allergy case. In its dossier, the FDA describes urticaria and throat swelling after exposure to blackberry, strawberry and raspberry in a single patient, with a positive skin prick test and no oral provocation challenge. The same patient appears in more than one FDA product entry. These are repetitions of the cited case, each under a different product heading. Establishing the fruit species, whether the berries were frozen, individual trigger or speech loudness requires evidence beyond the accessed text. The complete 2001 article was unavailable in this review.
