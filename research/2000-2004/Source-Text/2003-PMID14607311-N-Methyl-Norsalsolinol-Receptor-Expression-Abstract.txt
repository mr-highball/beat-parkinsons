Title: Modulation of striatal serotonin and opioid receptor mRNA expression following systemic N-methyl-norsalsolinol administration
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14607311/
PMID: 14607311
DOI: 10.1016/s0022-510x(03)00226-0
Publication date: 2003-12-15 (journal issue listed by NLM)
Source depth: original abstract via NLM; full text not verified

The rat experiment measured striatal receptor transcript levels after systemic N-methyl-norsalsolinol exposure. Serotonin 5-HT2A and delta-opioid transcripts rose; mu-opioid expression fell. The clinical hallucinosis link is the authors' interpretation. The abstract provides no measurement of food-derived salsolinol, fig ingestion or observed visual episodes.

The abstract identifies Wistar rats, drug administration outside the diet and receptor mRNA as the outcome. Its proposed connection to levodopa-associated hallucinosis is an inference. It does not establish that the administered derivative occurs in edible figs or that it changes clinical visual hallucinations. The experiment is separate from the older urinary salsolinol comparison in people with Parkinson disease.
