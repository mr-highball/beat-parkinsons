Title: Studies on the influence of dietary spices on food transit time in experimental rats
Authors: Kalpana Platel and K. Srinivasan
Citation: Nutrition Research. September 2001;21(9):1309–1314.
DOI: 10.1016/S0271-5317(01)00331-1
Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0271-5317(01)00331-1
Public original article copy: https://eurekamag.com/research/003/570/003570367.pdf
Text coverage: factual summary of Methods and Figure 1.

The investigators powdered market-bought Cuminum cyminum and other spices to pass through a 50-mesh sieve. Each spice had its own diet arm. Adult female Wistar rats were fed for six weeks. The ground cumin arm used 1.25 g per 100 g diet, replacing the same mass of cornstarch. On the final day, ferric oxide was included as an unabsorbed marker; the interval from feeding to appearance of the first marked fecal pellet supplied the endpoint. Figure 1 reports 570 ± 22 minutes in the cumin arm and 780 ± 25 minutes in the associated control, mean ± SEM of eight animals per group (p < .05). The experiment quantified overall food transit in the rats. It did not separately measure gastric emptying, drug pharmacokinetics, motor fluctuations, or Parkinson OFF time.
