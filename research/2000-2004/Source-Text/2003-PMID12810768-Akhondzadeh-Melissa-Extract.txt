Title: Melissa officinalis leaf extract in mild to moderate Alzheimer disease
First published: 2003-07
PMID: 12810768
PMCID: PMC1738567
DOI: 10.1136/jnnp.74.7.863
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1738567/
Author article reprint inspected: https://www.academia.edu/33772112/Melissa_officinalis_extract_in_the_treatment_of_patients_with_mild_to_moderate_Alzheimers_disease_a_double_blind_randomised_placebo_controlled_trial
Archive scope: Publisher abstract and full article reprint checked. Methods, trial flow, efficacy and adverse-event table summarized.

Forty-two participants were randomized, 21 per arm. Thirty-five completed 16 weeks. The leaf preparation was a 1:1 extract in 45% alcohol, standardized to at least 500 micrograms of citral per mL. The study compared 60 drops daily with matching placebo. The two main efficacy measures were ADAS-cog and CDR-SB, with the observed-cases primary analysis and a last-observation-carried-forward sensitivity analysis. Both scales favored extract in the published comparisons. Agitation in Table 2 was a recorded adverse event in one extract and six placebo participants (reported p=0.03). The separate dropout comparison, also one versus six, had reported p=0.09. The similarly designed Salvia study is a different trial. Neither the adverse event nor the cognition comparison measures depressive severity in Parkinson disease. Other neurodegenerative disorders were exclusionary.
