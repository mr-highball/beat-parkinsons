Title: Comparison of Lavandula angustifolia Mill. tincture and imipramine in the treatment of mild to moderate depression: a double-blind, randomized trial.
PMID: 12551734
DOI: 10.1016/s0278-5846(02)00342-1
First published: 2003
Authors: Akhondzadeh S, Kashani L, Fotouhi A, Jarvandi S, Mobaseri M, Moin M, Khani M, Jamshidi AH et al.
Journal: Progress in neuro-psychopharmacology & biological psychiatry
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12551734/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 3 review).

Double-blind randomized controlled trial (Akhondzadeh et al., 2003, Prog Neuropsychopharmacol Biol Psychiatry) in 45 adult outpatients with DSM-IV mild-to-moderate major depression (HAM-D >= 18; n=15 per arm) randomized for 4 weeks to Lavandula angustifolia hydroalcoholic tincture (1:5 in 50% alcohol, 60 drops/day) + placebo tablet, imipramine 100 mg/day + placebo drop, or imipramine + Lavandula tincture. Lavandula tincture monotherapy was less effective than imipramine (F=13.16, p=0.001), whereas tincture + imipramine was superior to imipramine alone (F=20.83, p<0.0001). Dry mouth and urinary retention were more frequent with imipramine, whereas headache occurred more often with Lavandula tincture. Limits: no pure placebo arm, hydroalcoholic tincture rather than brewed tea or dried buds, small sample.
