Title: Dronabinol crossover for central pain in multiple sclerosis, with a shared sesame-oil carrier
Source: https://www.bmj.com/content/329/7460/253

PMID: 15258006

Access evidence: Original BMJ and PMC publication. Methods, Figure 2 and primary outcome results verified.

The randomized double-blind crossover included 24 people with multiple sclerosis and central neuropathic pain. Both periods used sesame oil as the oral capsule carrier. The active capsule added dronabinol and the control capsule contained the oil alone. Median last-week pain was 4.0 in the drug period and 5.0 in the oil-carrier period (p=.02). The reported interquartile intervals were 2.3–6.0 and 4.0–6.4. The study tested the drug contrast and supplies no oil-free comparison. The original does not identify the oil's toasting or refining.

The Danish 2005 secondary publication (PMID 16014264) reports the same size and pain result. The 2022 Cochrane review includes this crossover with risk-of-bias and imprecision concerns. The later summaries and the original are one trial family.

Continuing evidence gaps
This outcome is from multiple sclerosis. The trial cannot identify a sesame-oil effect from its design. A separate refined or toasted-oil comparison matched to Parkinson pain remains open.
