Title: Declared Ficus species in the Bangladeshi medicinal-plant laboratory survey
Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/bbb/64/4/64_4_837/_article
PMID: 10830501
DOI: 10.1271/bbb.64.837
Publication date: 2000-04
Date basis: PubMed issue month and original volume 64(4), pp. 837–840. The Oxford platform separately lists January 1, 2000.
Source depth: Original scanned pages, with printed tables 1 and 2 visually verified.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25

Table 1 on printed page 838 names Ficus religiosa and gives the part as fruit. The listed uses include paralysis, piles, tuberculosis and fever. The footnote attributes botanical identification and uses to the Bangladesh Institute of Herbal Medicine. The paper gives no patient-level observation, preparation for that particular use, clinical diagnosis, dose timing or treatment outcome. The food species Ficus carica has a different botanical identity.

Table 2 on printed page 839 summarizes laboratory scavenging and enzyme assays. It lists Piper longum among the samples and gives no Ficus-labelled result row. The original therefore does not supply fig assay values to transfer, or a quantified treatment response. The 2014 review of Ficus carica cites this paper in its table of common-fig uses. The original row supplies the species and part needed to interpret that attribution.
