Title: Aqueous leaf extract of Ocimum gratissimum and intestinal transit in rats
DOI: 10.4314/ajbr.v7i1.54063
Source: https://www.ajol.info/index.php/ajbr/article/view/54063
Original printed copy: https://africanjournalofbiomedicalresearch.com/index.php/AJBR/article/download/997/964/1883
Publication date: 2004-01
Date basis: African Journal of Biomedical Research 7(1), printed pp. 31–33. A later issue-site date indexes the same paper.
Checked: 2026-11-25
Review depth: Original article, Tables 1–2, with independent issue metadata.

The comparison administered aqueous extracts derived from oven-dried Ocimum gratissimum leaves to three rat groups with five animals each. The three mean dye transit percentages were 68.88%, 72.48%, and 69.10%, versus 85.14% for saline. The paper reports each extract group below saline. A separate Hibiscus sabdariffa series produces a different dose pattern.

Ocimum gratissimum is a distinct species from the sweet basil Ocimum basilicum in the Round 1 culinary claim. One author appears on both this article and the later University of Ibadan study of sweet basil, but the extraction study reports different species, distinct groups and a different control transit mean. The comparison documents opposite direction for a related species, without providing evidence that dried sweet basil worsens constipation.
