Visual hallucinations, drooling and pain in a Parkinson surgery series
Publication: February 2003, Journal of Neurology, Neurosurgery and Psychiatry 74(2), 170–174
Authors: T. R. K. Varma and colleagues
DOI: 10.1136/jnnp.74.2.170
PMID: 12531942
PMCID: PMC1738303
Source: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/10946989_Deep_brain_stimulation_of_the_subthalamic_nucleus_Effectiveness_in_advanced_Parkinson%27s_disease_patients_previously_reliant_on_apomorphine
Coverage: Coauthor-uploaded original Methods, Results and Discussion matched to journal identity

Seven patients with advanced Parkinson disease who relied on apomorphine underwent bilateral subthalamic deep brain stimulation. Assessments were made before and six months after surgery. Two people developed visual hallucinations several weeks after surgery and received quetiapine after reducing antiparkinsonian medication failed to resolve them. The two people still using an apomorphine pump had qualitative reports of less drooling and some improvement in off-period pain. The report also describes two people stopping morphine previously used for off-period pain. Overlap among the adverse-event and pain cases was not established. The study gives no repeated hallucination count, drooling severity scale or dietary intervention.
