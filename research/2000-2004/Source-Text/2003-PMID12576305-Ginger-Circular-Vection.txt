Title: Effects of ginger on motion sickness and gastric slow-wave dysrhythmias induced by circular vection
PMID: 12576305
DOI: 10.1152/ajpgi.00164.2002
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12576305/
Original PDF copy: https://academy.miloa.eu/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/H.-C.-Lien-C.-Owyang-al-2003.pdf
Published: 2003-03, American Journal of Physiology Gastrointestinal and Liver Physiology 284, G481–G489.
Archive scope: Factual summary of the nine-page original, exposure methods and Figures 1–6.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Thirteen selected volunteers with motion-sickness histories and susceptibility to the vection stimulus took Nature’s Way capsules containing 1 or 2 g of ginger, or matched starch, in a randomized blinded crossover. Figure 1 reports maximal 0–3 nausea scores of 2.5 on placebo, 1.7 at 1 g and 1.8 at 2 g. Mean latency to nausea was 5.6, 8.5 and 9.7 minutes, respectively. The article reports longer latency and less high-frequency gastric electrical activity with ginger during visual vection. Four participants from this sample separately underwent intravenous vasopressin testing, with no clear attenuation of the provoked response. These are induced nausea and electrical outcomes. The preparation, stimulus and endpoint differ from the 1991 rotating-chair and nuclear gastric-emptying protocol and from chronic Parkinson constipation.
