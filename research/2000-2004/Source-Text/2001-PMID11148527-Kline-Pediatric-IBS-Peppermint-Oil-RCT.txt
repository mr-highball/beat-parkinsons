Title: Enteric-coated, pH-dependent peppermint oil capsules for the treatment of irritable bowel syndrome in children.
PMID: 11148527
DOI: 10.1067/mpd.2001.109606
First published: 2001
Authors: Kline RM, Kline JJ, Di Palma J, Barbero GJ
Journal: The Journal of pediatrics
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11148527/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 6 review).

Primary Abstract:
In a randomized, double-blind controlled trial, 42 children with irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) were given pH-dependent, enteric-coated peppermint oil capsules or placebo. After 2 weeks, 75% of those receiving peppermint oil had reduced severity of pain associated with IBS. Peppermint oil may be used as a therapeutic agent during the symptomatic phase of IBS.

Verified Round 6 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Double-blind placebo-controlled RCT (J Pediatr 2001; USA; n=42 children with IBS) testing enteric-coated pH-dependent peppermint oil (187-374 mg TID) vs placebo for 2 weeks. 76% vs 19% reported reduced abdominal pain severity (p<0.001). Small short pediatric IBS oil trial later unconfirmed by Vermeijden 2026 (n=228).
