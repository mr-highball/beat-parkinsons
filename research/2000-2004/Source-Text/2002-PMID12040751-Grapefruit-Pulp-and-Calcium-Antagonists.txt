Title: Effect of grapefruit pulp on the pharmacokinetics of the dihydropyridine calcium antagonists nifedipine and nisoldipine
PMID: 12040751
DOI: 10.1248/yakushi.122.323
Publication date: 2002-05-01
Citation: Ohtani M and colleagues. Yakugaku Zasshi 122:323–329.
Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/yakushi/122/5/122_5_323/_article/-char/en
Primary PDF: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/yakushi/122/5/122_5_323/_pdf
Coverage: Original methods, English abstract, and whole-pulp preparation.

Eight healthy men crossed over between no grapefruit and eating fresh white grapefruit pulp before or after a calcium-channel blocker. The before-drug comparison showed about 1.3 times the 24-hour drug exposure for both medicines compared with no grapefruit. The associated peak concentrations were about 1.4 times for nifedipine and 1.5 times for nisoldipine. The authors report a larger effect when pulp preceded the drug and a smaller nisoldipine change than a previously reported juice comparison. The pulp result gives drug-specific evidence for a real culinary exposure. It leaves the relevant medication metabolites, mechanism and symptom effects for Parkinson disease open.
