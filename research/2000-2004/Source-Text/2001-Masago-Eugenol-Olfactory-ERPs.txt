Eugenol and orange odor in an attention oddball task
Primary source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpa/20/1/20_1_7/_pdf
PMID: 11320780
DOI: 10.2114/jpa.20.7
Coverage: factual extract from primary Methods and Results. The text below records source facts and scope.

Masago and colleagues published this study in the January 2001 journal issue. Seven healthy male students, aged 21–23, took part. Exposures were isolated eugenol and orange oil odor, delivered in an oddball paradigm with instructions to attend or ignore. The primary paper printed no odor-quality difference in component latencies. It reported a larger P3 for orange than eugenol and earlier responses and larger P3 with the attend instructions. The P3 result is an olfactory ERP contrast between odorants and depends on task context. The measured outcome is distinct from changing the severity of Parkinson attention impairment after ingestion.
