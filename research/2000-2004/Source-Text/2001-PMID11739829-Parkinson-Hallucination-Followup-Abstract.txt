Title: Prospective longitudinal assessment of hallucinations in Parkinson's disease
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11739829/
Publisher: https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.57.11.2078
PMID: 11739829

Publication date: 2001-12-11
Date basis: NLM journal issue
Source depth: Primary structured abstract; complete article body remains open
Format: Factual digest of the original abstract

The Rush team stratified people with Parkinson disease into five groups using a modified UPDRS thought-disorder item. Sixty enrolled people had no baseline hallucinations across three sleep and behavioral strata. Twenty-nine had baseline hallucinations, classified by whether they retained insight. Sequential Rush Hallucination Inventory interviews were scheduled at enrollment and at months 6, 18 and 48. The authors report follow-up accounting for 87 of 89 participants at four years. They used generalized estimating equations to model symptoms over time.

This is a different cohort and outcome instrument from the later Johns Hopkins DSM-IV psychosis paper (PMID 29756003). The abstract gives no dried fig exposure and does not verify a separate diet-linked visual episode count. Follow the journal body for exact visual modality, scoring and denominators.
