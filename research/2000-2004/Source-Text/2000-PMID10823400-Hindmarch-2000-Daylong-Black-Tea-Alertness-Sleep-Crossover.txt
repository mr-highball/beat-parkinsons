Title: A naturalistic investigation of the effects of day-long consumption of tea, coffee and water on alertness, sleep onset and sleep quality.
PMID: 10823400
DOI: 10.1007/s002130000383
First published: 2000
Authors: Hindmarch I, Rigney U, Stanley N, Quinlan P, Rycroft J, Lane J
Journal: Psychopharmacology
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10823400/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Five-way randomized naturalistic crossover trial in n=30 healthy volunteers consuming equal-volume 1-cup or 2-cup brewed black tea (37.5 or 75 mg caffeine), coffee (75 or 150 mg caffeine), or water at 09:00, 13:00, 17:00, and 23:00. Caffeinated tea sustained daytime CFF and reduced subjective sedation (LARS, p<0.05) similarly to coffee (with higher CFF than 75 mg coffee 30-90 min post-first drink, p<0.01). Evening/day-long caffeinated beverages caused dose-dependent worsening on Leeds Sleep Evaluation Questionnaire sleep onset (p<0.001), sleep time (p<0.001), and sleep quality (p<0.001) with wrist actigraphy monitoring, though black tea disrupted sleep less than higher-caffeine coffee. Limits: healthy non-PD adults; included late 23:00 dose.
