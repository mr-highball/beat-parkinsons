Title: 40th EASD Annual Meeting of the European Association for the Study of Diabetes
Publication year: 2004 (printed supplement)
PMID: 27770180
PMCID: PMC7096100
DOI: 10.1007/BF03375463
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7096100/
Text coverage: Distilled observations from the complete A1–A464 archive. The printed meeting supplement dates to 2004 and the publisher's electronic posting to 2016.

58, A24. Twenty-two men consumed a supplied Mediterranean diet for 28 days. The menu included olive oil, fish, vegetables and fruit. Systolic blood pressure changed from 124.3 to 121.2 mmHg (p = 0.02) in the before-and-after comparison. There was no isolated olive-oil comparison and no Parkinson outcome.

242, A93. The closed-loop insulin delivery protocol used orange juice in four episodes below 60 mg/dL. The abstract measured the performance of the insulin-control system, without a raw-orange comparator or gait and bowel outcomes.

503, A184. Chicken insulin was a species-derived ligand in an insulin-antibody binding assay. It was a weak competitor for one antibody pattern, with a reported median inhibitory concentration substantially above that of human insulin. No edible chicken was administered.

522, A191. Salmon melanin-concentrating hormone (MCH) was a peptide agonist tested at a cloned pig receptor. The report describes calcium mobilization with salmon and human peptides. It gives no dietary salmon or anxiety comparison.

662, A240. Male C57BL/6J mice ate semisynthetic high-fat diets with several base fats. The corn-oil arm was compared with corn-oil diets partly replaced by EPA-rich or DHA-rich marine concentrates. In the printed report the corn-oil-only group had higher gonadal fat than either mixed-oil arm. The recorded weights were 721.7 mg for corn oil alone, 355.4 mg for the DHA-rich mixture, and 449.7 mg for the EPA-rich mixture. The base oil's refining grade and Parkinson motor or nonmotor outcomes were not reported. PMID 15736913 is a closely related full publication from the same group.

667, A242. In a 37-person study of diabetes with peripheral neuropathy and obesity, 16 people received olive-oil comparator capsules and 21 fish oil with vitamin E. The studied outcomes were platelet function, oxidative markers and metabolic measures. There was no neutral or oil-free comparison for the olive-oil group.

671, A243. Normal rats receiving an olive-oil-enriched diet had a larger meal-triggered increase in circulating GLP-1. The abstract gives a 1.39 ng/mL between-group difference in the mean increment at 10 and 20 minutes (p < 0.001). The authors report a smaller glucose rise and a larger insulin rise at day 36, more weight gain at day 50, and no detected day-50 intestinal GLP-1 content difference. PMID 15888922 is the full report from the same series. A separate neonatal streptozotocin cohort in PMID 16523410 lacked a comparable glucose-tolerance improvement. Neither series measured bowel symptoms, insomnia or anxiety.

684, A247. Six adults with type 1 diabetes completed two standardized exercise sessions. Tomato juice was given before the second session. Total radical antioxidant parameter (TRAP) was higher before that session. The paper reports no significant difference in the exercise-associated fall in TRAP across sessions. The same original page prints two post-juice exercise values, 347.67 and 374.67, with the same SD of 64.88. The original page image confirms both values. The intervention was juice, the order was fixed, and muscle rigidity was not measured.

799, A289. Isolated turmeric-derived curcumin reduced glucose production from different substrates in isolated mouse hepatocytes. The conference report lists reduced glucose-6-phosphatase activity and no effect on phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase (PEPCK). The later same-group report, PMID 18221818, shows lower PEPCK in a specified exposure condition. The two reports document biochemical assays with potentially differing experimental conditions and no clinical turmeric consumption.

801, A290. A diabetes case-control analysis compared coffee preparation and consumption in 216 acute coronary syndrome cases and 196 matched controls. Authors report different associations for filtered and unfiltered coffee, without a fatigue outcome. Coffee also occurs as a covariate in a separate risk analysis.

1195 and 1196, A426–A427. The reports use randomized order, 240 mL instant coffee with 80 mg caffeine versus water, and assessments before and at one, two and three hours after consumption in adults with type 2 diabetes. The main reports give no significant difference in arterial wave reflection or pressure in 1195, and longer QTc at one and two hours in 1196. Their protocol and author overlap warrant caution about independence. The abstracts give no fatigue measure.

1275, A454. In a distinct cultured human endothelial-cell assay, curcumin was among the pretreatments examined against linoleate-induced LOX-1 expression. The authors report inhibition of the induced signal. The closer endpoint is a molecular assay, without oral turmeric or a Parkinson symptom. PMID 15855339 is the subsequent article.

Two apparent cumin matches come from line breaks inside curcumin. True sage matches are a sequencing acronym and a broken message token. Date terminology is calendrical. These do not establish the corresponding food exposures.
