Title: Apomorphine monotherapy in the treatment of refractory motor complications of Parkinson's disease: Long-term follow-up study of 64 patients
Source: https://movementdisorders.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mds.10281
Source alias: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12465062/
PMID: 12465062
DOI: 10.1002/mds.10281
Publication date: 2002-07-23
Date basis: Publisher online deposit. November print issue, Movement Disorders 17(6), 1235–1241.
Text coverage: Publisher author abstract and publication references. Main body remains open.

Manson and colleagues retrospectively analyzed 64 people with Parkinson disease treated with apomorphine infusion, of whom 45 successfully converted to daytime apomorphine monotherapy. Mean follow-up was 33.8 months. The abstract reports a greater observed maximum reduction in dyskinesia in the monotherapy group (64% versus 30%) and longer on time in both groups. Treatment-group membership was determined by successful conversion, so the comparison is not randomized. The abstract lists some reasons for failure and does not provide a visual hallucination frequency comparison. This is a medication study.

The references cite the earlier Colzi 19-person series. The reports share investigators and participant reuse remains open without the complete recruitment and cohort information. Do not pool their outcomes.
