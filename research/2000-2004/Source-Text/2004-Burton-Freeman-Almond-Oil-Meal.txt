Burton-Freeman B, Davis PA, Schneeman BO. Interaction of fat availability and sex on postprandial satiety and cholecystokinin after mixed-food meals. Am J Clin Nutr. 2004 Nov;80(5):1207–1214. DOI 10.1093/ajcn/80.5.1207. PMID 15531667.

Local factual summary from the original methods, Table 1 and results. Fifteen adults, eight men and seven women, each received three breakfast meals in a randomized crossover with at least one week between visits. The test arm baked almond oil into a muffin. Table 1 records 110 g of muffin for men and 75 g for women, and provides no extraction method for the almond oil. Other arms used whole almonds or a corn and safflower oil comparison. Researchers recorded appetite ratings and blood measures over six hours. The cholecystokinin response differed by meal and sex (interaction P = 0.02). Women had higher cholecystokinin and greater satiety after the oil meals than after whole almonds. The cohort and outcomes did not measure Parkinson voice volume or facial expressiveness.
