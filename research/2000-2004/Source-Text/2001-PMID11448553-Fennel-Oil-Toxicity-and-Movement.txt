Title: The effect of fennel essential oil on uterine contraction as a model for dysmenorrhea, pharmacology and toxicology study
PMID: 11448553
DOI: 10.1016/S0378-8741(01)00249-5
Publication date: 2001-08
Citation: Ostad and colleagues. Journal of Ethnopharmacology 76(3), 299–304.
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11448553/
Publisher: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378874101002495
Coverage: Author abstract and publisher preview supplemented by attributed regulator details.

The study examined isolated rat uterine contraction and acute fennel essential oil toxicity in a separate rat component. Its authors estimate oral median lethality at 1,326 mg/kg in female rats. A European Medicines Agency assessment attributes temporary tremor, muscle fasciculation, sedation, reduced responsiveness and other systemic signs to the highest dose in the acute study and identifies sedation as the most evident lower-dose observation. The author abstract gives no tremor data, and available publisher framing leaves the original clinical-sign methods and arm counts unverified. The 2000 manufacturer pamphlet has the same three lead investigators and a related toxicity result. The relation between cohorts remains open. No diagnosed Parkinson population, resting-tremor score or toasted culinary seed comparison appears in the accessible material.
