Parsley seed extract and rat colon water absorption
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11695882/
PMID: 11695882
DOI: 10.1078/0944-7113-00058
Coverage: Author abstract and original body text, including Table 1, through alternate host

The article appeared in Phytomedicine, volume 8, issue 5, pages 382–388, in September 2001. The publisher preview, PubMed, and Penn State author record agree about the identity.

The experiment measured net fluid uptake during rat-colon perfusion after adding aqueous parsley seed extract directly to the buffer. Table 1 (p. 386) reports 0.254 ± 0.058 mL/cm² with control buffer and 0.061 ± 0.010 with parsley (mean ± SEM, eight rats per group, different significance letters at p < .05). Sodium-free buffer gave 0.098 ± 0.008 and adding parsley to it gave 0.060 ± 0.006. Furosemide alone gave 0.408 ± 0.018 and furosemide with parsley gave 0.22 ± 0.06. Sodium-free buffer with furosemide gave 0.210 ± 0.032, and with both furosemide and parsley it gave 0.155 ± 0.015.

The laboratory enzyme experiments separately reported inhibition of Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase in rat-colon homogenate and partially purified dog kidney enzyme. The clinical endpoints of bowel movements, constipation and oral fresh-leaf exposure were outside the preparation and measurements.

Methods, results and Table 1 were checked against original pages transcribed by a third-party host. The publisher route offered abstract access and a full-text access gate. The 2002 diuretic report by two of the same authors (PMID 11849841) used different outcomes.
