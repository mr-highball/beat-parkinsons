Title: Comparison of benzydamine hydrochloride and Salvia officinalis as an adjuvant local treatment to systemic nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug in controlling pain after tonsillectomy, adenoidectomy, or both
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3964578/
DOI: 10.1016/j.curtheres.2004.07.002
Publication date: 2004-07
Reviewed: 2026-11-26
Text coverage: Original article, methods, results and pediatric and adult contrasts.
PMCID: PMC3964578
PMID: 24672091

The active-comparator study randomized 420 people (278 children and 142 adults) after tonsil or adenoid surgery. Both groups received systemic pain relief by age group. The local treatments were sage infusion rinse and benzydamine. Moderate or severe pain was more frequent in children assigned sage rinse on all four assessed postoperative days, with p < .01 on days 1 and 4 and p < .001 on days 2 and 7. The comparator is an active analgesic. This relative finding gives no absolute effect against no rinse, and the route, population and pain mechanism differ from a culinary fresh-sage comparison in Parkinson disease. The original cohort is already represented in Round 1.
