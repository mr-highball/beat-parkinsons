The study compares oral methanolic and water extracts of the edible date portion and a methanolic seed extract with controls in a rat arthritis model. Table 2 measures the increase in paw thickness. The authors report 67.8% and 61.3% suppression for the two edible-part extracts and 35.5% for the seed extract. The means in the table are 0.1938 for the arthritic control and 0.0625, 0.075 and 0.125 for the three extract groups on the reported thickness scale.

The study measures swelling, inflammation-associated blood measures, oxidative markers and nutritional outcomes. It has no pain behavior endpoint, patient-rated pain or Parkinson cohort. Oral extracts from the edible portion are useful preparation context while leaving the effect of whole dried dates on Parkinson pain unresolved.
