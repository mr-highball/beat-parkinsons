Title: Mucuna pruriens in Parkinson's disease: a double blind clinical and pharmacological study
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1738871/
Verified journal reprint: https://magistralbr.caldic.com/storage/product-files/1183586854.pdf
First published: 2004-12
PMID: 15548480
PMCID: PMC1738871
DOI: 10.1136/jnnp.2003.028761
Text coverage: factual extraction from Methods and Tables 1–3 in the six-page journal reprint. The DOI, journal, pagination and PubMed record agree.

Eight people with Parkinson disease who completed the blinded crossover received two seed-powder doses and a levodopa/carbidopa comparison. The highest powder dose reached full ON at a mean 34.6 minutes versus 68.5 minutes and had mean full ON of 204.0 versus 167.4 minutes (p=.021 for each comparison). Table 1 gives best motor scores of 15.5 versus 15.4 with an inconclusive contrast. It also records different baseline motor scores for these two sessions (46.9 versus 49.8, p=.046). An additional participant did not complete the study following brief vomiting with the higher powder dose.

The UPDRS motor measure, tapping, ON periods and dyskinesia comparisons are distinct. No separate resting-tremor outcome is reported in the methods, results tables or figures. The plant powder contains its own levodopa. The trial compares drug-containing preparations with different exposure profiles.
