Title: Attenuation of laboratory-induced stress in humans after acute administration of Melissa officinalis (Lemon Balm)
PMID: 15272110
DOI: 10.1097/01.psy.0000132877.72833.71
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15272110/
Author deposit: https://core.ac.uk/download/9985745.pdf
First published: 2004 July–August issue, Psychosomatic Medicine 66(4):607–613. An earlier public date was not established. The period index uses the first day of the indexed issue month as a sorting value.
Coverage: Author abstract. The Northumbria deposit exposed the same abstract and a cover page.
Reviewed: 2026-11-18 UTC

Kennedy, Little and Scholey randomized 18 healthy volunteers in a double-blind, balanced crossover. Separate visits compared a single 300 mg or 600 mg dose of standardized Melissa officinalis extract with placebo. Seven-day washouts separated conditions. Participants completed the Defined Intensity Stressor Simulation before dosing and again at one hour.

The 600 mg condition gave higher self-rated calmness and lower alertness during laboratory stress. The 300 mg condition increased speed on the mathematical task without a reported drop in accuracy. A 2024 cognitive review described a gain in accuracy and a smaller gain in speed. The primary abstract reports the speed comparison, and the original cognitive and mood tables remain open.

This is acute task-evoked mood in healthy adults. It supplies neither a depressive-symptom severity measure nor a Parkinson population. The exposure was a standardized extract, with no brewed-infusion comparison in the abstract. The 2002 and 2003 Kennedy experiments are separate reports.
