Source depth: Author-affiliated abstract and publicly readable rendering of the primary methods, results, tables and discussion
Format: Factual digest of the original material

Thirty newly diagnosed Parkinson patients were randomized to a single-vine B. caapi extract or a matched manzanilla control, with 15 in each group. The trial tracked symptoms over four hours. The extract group improved on the UPDRS motor total versus the control in the short observation period.

The body's Results section and Table 3 report worsening of resting tremor and emergence of action and postural tremor in the extract group. The results describe resting tremor exacerbation in all 15 extract recipients, while the abstract uses the less specific description that some experienced exacerbation. The paper reports one extract recipient with a transient hallucination adverse event and reports adverse effects only in the extract group. The primary event label does not specify modality or the number of episodes. The 2015 viewpoint (PMID 30713897) classifies the event as visual. The trial's adverse-event observation period and motor summary do not establish longer-term rates.

The introduction reports a distinct clinical observation in 13 patients who described occasional use of a multi-plant ceremonial beverage and perceived relief of Parkinson symptoms. The report describes improved rigidity alongside worse tremor, involuntary movements and hallucinations after a customary preparation was observed. This preliminary series has incomplete individual exposure and outcome documentation and should not be treated as a randomized arm. The clinical trial tested a single-vine extract with a different composition. Neither exposure was dried fig.

Follow-through: The 2003 laboratory assay compares enzyme and tissue responses. The 2018 marmoset study tests a separate model and pharmacologic context. Seek a publisher or library scan to reconcile the citation pagination and the full event reporting.
