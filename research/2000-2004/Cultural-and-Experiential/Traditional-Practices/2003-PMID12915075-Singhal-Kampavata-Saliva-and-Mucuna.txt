Title: Kampavata symptom and Mucuna context in a 2003 Indian Parkinson review
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1353802003000245
PMID: 12915075
DOI: 10.1016/S1353-8020(03)00024-5
Source kind: Traditional-practice interpretation in a published clinical review
Publication date: 2003-08
Date basis: The journal issue date for Singhal, Lalkaka and Sankhla. The authors discuss much older sources.
Source locator: Parkinsonism and Related Disorders 9 Suppl. 2, S105–S109, opening historical discussion.
Community/context: Indian neurologists relate a syndrome in Ayurvedic medical writing to Parkinson disease, including reports of tremor, stiffness and drooling.
Exposure and reported symptom: Traditional use of Mucuna pruriens seed powder for the syndrome appears in the review and is cited in the later Soumyanath assay. The reviewed historical account does not quantify a preparation-specific saliva-loss change.
Reported direction: The authors describe a therapeutic tradition for Kampavata. A specific effect on drooling is unspecified.
Authenticity and efficacy: The modern bibliographic and historical discussion is inspectable. The exact original Sanskrit witnesses, dates, and wording require independent comparison. Neither the traditional name nor the later interpretation establishes a modern diagnosis for each historical case.
Related research: See the 2004 blinded crossover and the 2012 and 2017 supplement assays. They examine different preparations, study designs and outcomes.
Access and follow-up: Follow the earlier textual references and distinguish any treatment-specific observations from symptom lists. No raw fig appears in the inspected account.
