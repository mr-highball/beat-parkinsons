Title: Traditional Javanese distinction between soybean tempeh (tempe kedelai) and indigenous velvet-bean tempeh (tempe benguk)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11708313/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 2001
Date basis: Peer-reviewed journal publication year (Asia Pacific Journal of Clinical Nutrition, 2001; PMID:11708313).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Ethnographic and food-chemistry boundary record distinguishing ordinary culinary soybean tempeh from traditional Javanese Mucuna pruriens (velvet bean) tempeh; supplies no Parkinson symptom efficacy claim for soy tempeh.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed publication (Handajani, 2001, PMID:11708313) inspected during Round 7 review.
Efficacy evidence: Botanical and cultural taxonomy safeguard only; prevents false attribution of Mucuna pruriens L-DOPA pharmacology to culinary Glycine max soy tempeh.

Handajani (2001, PMID:11708313) documents indigenous Javanese tempe benguk fermented from velvet bean (Mucuna pruriens) seeds using traditional ragi tempe inoculants, contrasting its composition and sensory properties against conventional soybean tempeh (tempe kedelai). Because Mucuna pruriens naturally contains pharmacologically active levodopa—and has been investigated in separate preclinical fermented-velvet-bean models (such as Suaidah 2018 and Astawan 2024, PMID:39816105)—preserving this traditional Indonesian distinction is essential in Parkinson's evidence review. Culinary soybean tempeh (Glycine max) does not share Mucuna pruriens L-DOPA content, and traditional or preclinical dopaminergic findings from Mucuna tempeh cannot be transferred to soy tempeh.
