Title: Fresh and dried figs in Hanoteau and Letourneux's Kabylie account
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Historical ethnographic account
Source: https://extraits.immateriel.fr/files/serve_book_preview/465340.pdf#page=476
Publication date: 2003
Reviewed: 2026-11-19
Date basis: Copyright page of the Éditions Bouchène edition actually inspected. The BnF catalogue identifies the earlier multi-volume Imprimerie nationale edition as 1872–1873.
Location inspected: Volume I, printed pages 402–403, on fig harvesting, processing and seasonal consumption.
Community and context: Historical account of land use and food practices in Kabylie. The description reflects the authors' colonial-era outsider perspective.
Exposure: Fresh common figs during the harvest and sun-dried common figs for use during the year, with some high-grade dried figs dipped in oil. Prickly pears appear separately.
Reported direction: The authors attach a comparison with intoxication and increased nervous excitement to heavy consumption of fresh figs during the harvest. They describe use of dried figs without reporting that effect for dried figs.
Authenticity: The passage is visible in the specified edition. The earlier edition's wording remains to be inspected.
Efficacy evidence: Cultural context for distinct preparations. There is no Parkinson diagnosis, description of a clinical visual hallucination or individual count of episodes.
Related research: Duhousset's 1867 description and Vilbort's 1875 book use similar harvest imagery. The repeated motif cannot be counted as independent clinical confirmation.
Earlier edition catalogue: https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb30806665f
Open trail: Inspect the corresponding passage in the original print and seek firsthand accounts that identify preparation and observed experience.
