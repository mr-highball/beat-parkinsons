# Drugs and Remedies for 2000–2004

### 2000 — Five-year ropinirole versus levodopa comparison

Rascol and colleagues compared initial ropinirole with initial levodopa in a randomized double-blind study. Dyskinesia was less frequent in the ropinirole group. Supplemental levodopa was allowed, so the study compared strategies for starting therapy. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10816186/)

### 2000 — CALM-PD initial pramipexole versus levodopa

The Parkinson Study Group found fewer early dopaminergic motor complications with initial pramipexole and stronger overall symptomatic improvement with initial levodopa. Both treatment groups could receive supplemental levodopa. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11035889/)

### 2001 — Subcutaneous apomorphine for off episodes

Dewey and colleagues compared apomorphine with placebo in people with refractory off episodes despite oral therapy. Apomorphine improved motor scores rapidly and relieved more off episodes during outpatient use. Dyskinesia, yawning, and injection-site reactions occurred. The study addressed short-term rescue treatment. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11559309/)

### 2001 — Placebo-controlled nicotine patch trial

Vieregge and colleagues randomized 32 nonsmoking participants to nicotine or placebo patches as adjunctive treatment. They found no significant difference on clinical ratings or quantitative motor measures over the study period. The design tested symptom relief with this intervention in established disease. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11571330/)

### 2002 — Pilot trial of coenzyme Q10 in early disease

Shults and colleagues studied coenzyme Q10 versus placebo in a small dose-ranging randomized trial. Clinical disability scores worsened less at the highest tested dose. The investigators called for confirmation in a larger trial. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12374491/)

### 2002 — Unblinded acupuncture pilot

Shulman and colleagues evaluated acupuncture for Parkinson’s symptoms in an unblinded pilot. Testing did not show broad motor or symptomatic improvement. Some participants reported better sleep or rest. The small uncontrolled design could not separate a specific treatment effect from expectation or measurement variation. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12210879/)

### 2003 — Ventricular GDNF delivery in a randomized trial

Nutt and colleagues studied glial cell line-derived neurotrophic factor delivered into a cerebral ventricle in 50 people with advanced Parkinson’s disease. Clinical scores did not improve with treatment, and adverse effects occurred. Delivery to the intended brain tissue remained a concern. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12525720/)

### 2003 — Pathological gambling during dopamine agonist treatment

Driver-Dunckley and colleagues reported a clinical series of pathological gambling associated with dopamine agonist treatment in Parkinson’s disease. The report identified a potential behavioral adverse effect. It could not estimate its frequency or isolate drug effects from patient factors. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12913220/)

### 2004 — Four-year CALM-PD treatment trade-offs

Holloway and colleagues followed the randomized pramipexole and levodopa groups for four years. Initial pramipexole was associated with less wearing off and dyskinesia, while initial levodopa produced better overall motor control. Quality-of-life scores were similar. The groups differed in adverse events, including more somnolence with pramipexole. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15262734/)

### 2004 — Delayed-start TEMPO rasagiline study

The Parkinson Study Group compared early rasagiline with treatment delayed six months. Clinical scores favored earlier treatment at one year. The delayed-start design addressed progression, but the measured difference alone could not establish neuroprotection. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15096406/)

### 2004 — ELLDOPA clinical scores and imaging diverged

Fahn and colleagues randomized 361 people with early disease to carbidopa–levodopa or placebo. Clinical scores favored levodopa after a two-week withdrawal. Dopamine transporter imaging in a subset showed greater decline with levodopa than placebo. Persisting symptomatic effects or treatment-related changes in tracer binding complicated interpretation of progression. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15590952/)

### 2004 — Restrictive heart-valve disease during pergolide treatment

Van Camp and colleagues compared echocardiograms in people taking pergolide and a comparison group. Restrictive valve abnormalities were more common with pergolide. This observational study extended earlier safety reports and raised questions about exposure and reversibility. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15081648/)

### 2004 — Rivastigmine for Parkinson’s disease dementia

Emre and colleagues conducted a placebo-controlled trial in people with Parkinson’s disease dementia. Rivastigmine produced modest improvements in cognitive and global assessments. Nausea, vomiting, and tremor were more common. The results concern symptomatic treatment of established dementia. [Original source](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa041470)
