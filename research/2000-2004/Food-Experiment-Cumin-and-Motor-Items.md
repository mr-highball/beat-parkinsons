# Cumin, edible oils and specific motor outcomes

## Cumin seed aqueous extract and wet stool output

Published in 2002, volume 45 issue 4. In intact young rats with four per group, both oral aqueous seed-extract groups produced more wet stool than saline. Figure 1 shows approximately six for the lower extract arm and eight to nine for the higher arm, with the control curve at zero. The small preclinical extract experiment gives no verified whole-ground-spice dose or Parkinson constipation severity. [Original journal report and Figure 1](https://pjsir.org/multidisciplinary-archive/Volume%2045%202002/Issue%204/Article%209%20Vol%2045%20Issue%204%202002%20pp%20256-258.pdf) · [Local text](Source-Text/2002-Nworgu-Cumin-Laxation.txt)

## Soybean oil versus butter and gastric retention

Published in 2000. Twenty-seven functional dyspepsia patients received solid meals with soybean oil or butter on separate days. Reported retention at 90 minutes was 0.600 versus 0.648, paired p > .05. The abstract inconsistently labels one mention of the retention variable. There was no no-fat arm, refined-oil verification, levodopa or Parkinson daily OFF measurement.
