# Common sage and pain outcomes

## Topical sage and rhubarb creams for cold-sore pain

Saller and colleagues published in December 2001 (PMID **11799306**, DOI **10.1159/000057255**). Of 149 participants, the authors included 145 in their intention-to-treat evaluation of topical sage extract, rhubarb–sage extract or acyclovir cream. At the second follow-up, the original Table 6 records any residual pain in 4/40, 0/64 and 1/41, respectively. The rhubarb–sage versus sage-only comparison had unadjusted p=.020. The table specifies multiplying that value by two for its stated correction. Some missing observations were carried forward. This comparison addresses residual pain after topical treatment of herpes labialis, with no inert arm, oral fresh sage or Parkinson pain measurement. ## Sage rinse and pain after tonsil or adenoid surgery

The 2004 original compared sage infusion with benzydamine as locally applied postoperative oral antiseptic and analgesic regimens. In the randomized open-label study, both arms also received systemic anti-inflammatory analgesics. Among children, moderate or severe pain was more frequent with sage at days 1, 2, 4 and 7. The trial estimates relative performance against another active treatment in surgical recovery.
