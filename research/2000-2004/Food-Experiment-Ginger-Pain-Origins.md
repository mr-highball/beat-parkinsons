# Oral ginger preparations and measured pain

## 2000 extract crossover with osteoarthritis

Bliddal et al. compared ginger extract, ibuprofen and placebo in a double-blind, double-dummy crossover in people with hip or knee osteoarthritis. The overall ranking favored ibuprofen, but the ginger–placebo comparison across the full crossover was unclear. An exploratory first-period analysis favored ginger and ibuprofen over placebo. Review as an indirect extract result with a neutral full-crossover ginger–placebo finding. The primary abstract was verified, and full methods on preparation and sequencing are unverified. [Original trial abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10607493/). [Local factual text](Source-Text/2000-Bliddal-Ginger-Pain-Extract.txt)

## 2001 combined extract and knee osteoarthritis

Altman and Marcussen randomized 261 patients with knee osteoarthritis and moderate-to-severe pain to a concentrated standardized extract of `Z. officinale` and `Alpinia galanga` or placebo. Among 247 evaluable participants, standing-pain responders were 63% versus 50% (p=.048) after six weeks. Mean standing pain fell by 24.5 versus 16.4 mm (p=.005), while gastrointestinal adverse events were more frequent with extract. The two-botanical formulation and osteoarthritis endpoint limit transfer to the frozen exposure and population. The primary abstract was verified.
