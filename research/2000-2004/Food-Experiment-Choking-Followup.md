# Food experiment swallowing evidence

## Potato and beverage intake in a Japanese Parkinson dietary survey

Ueki and Otsuka’s October 2004 cross-sectional study recruited 94 people with Parkinson’s and 69 age-comparable healthy controls. The dietary analysis in Table 1 included 80 of the Parkinson participants and reported mean potato intake of 21.7 g (SD 18.1) versus 20.9 g (SD 15.1) in controls in the table’s reported units. The difference was marked nonsignificant. The study did not record potato preparation or potato-specific swallowing outcomes. [Original article, Table 1](https://mhlw-grants.niph.go.jp/system/files/2004/043021/200400337A/200400337A0004.pdf) · [Publication record, PMID 15505750](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15505750/)

Mean drinking-fluid intake was 604.0 versus 909.5 mL/day (p < 0.0001) and the Parkinson group frequently reported constipation. The intake measure included coffee, green tea, other tea, juice, and water. The authors report no statistically significant difference in drinking categories between the small subsets with and without constipation. Separate coffee and green-tea effects cannot be estimated. The article used recall and did not administer a beverage or food intervention. Its text and two tables also use different denominators for some water behavior and constipation history counts. [Original article, Methods and Results](https://mhlw-grants.niph.go.jp/system/files/2004/043021/200400337A/200400337A0004.pdf)

## Potato handling and swallowing accounts in older Swedish women with Parkinson’s

Andersson and Sidenvall observed meals and collected repeated 24-hour recalls from ten Swedish women with Parkinson’s disease. Six reported swallowing difficulty. In Table 1 and the case narrative, participant E’s husband peeled potatoes and cut her food at the table because tremor and hand weakness limited these tasks. She described swallowing difficulties when she ate hurriedly and found that chewing thoroughly helped. The study does not specify how the potatoes were cooked or attribute her swallowing difficulty to eating them. Table 2 also identifies participant J’s difficulty swallowing coffee, tea and tablets. The case series gives qualitative observations and no comparative food-specific swallowing scores. The issue is dated July 2001 and Wiley dates its web publication December 20, 2001. [Primary bibliographic record and abstract, PMID 11442684](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11442684/) · [Publisher record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1046/j.1365-2648.2001.01823.x) · [Indexed copy of primary article, pp. 72–75](https://www.scribd.com/document/1027952060/Journal-of-Advanced-Nursing-2001-Andersson-Case-studies-of-food-shopping-cooking-and-eating-habits-in-older-women)

## Potato consumption in five-day Swedish food records

Two 2002 Uppsala dissertations by Jenny Andersson and Kerstin Gustafsson report the same dietary study of older women recruited through the MENEW project. Two 24-hour recalls and a three-day food diary covered five days. Andersson’s Table 3 gives 21 women with Parkinson’s, 24 with rheumatoid arthritis and 18 with stroke among the 63 analyzed outpatients. Gustafsson’s Study II summary reports that 97% of the women ate potatoes. With the percentage rounded to the nearest whole number, this is 61 of 63. At least 19 of the 21 women with Parkinson’s therefore recorded eating potatoes, even if all 42 women with the other diagnoses did. This is a lower bound calculated from the published totals. The summaries do not specify preparation or report swallowing outcomes after the potato meals. [Andersson’s 2002 dissertation, Table 3 and dietary methods](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A161894/FULLTEXT01.pdf) · [Gustafsson’s 2002 dissertation, Study II results](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A161985/FULLTEXT01.pdf)

The 2004 journal article reports that the Parkinson’s group had 6.1 eating events per day, compared with 5.5 for the rheumatoid arthritis group and 4.9 for the stroke group. Its public abstract does not break down potato consumption by diagnosis or preparation. Neither Uppsala deposit manifest supplies the full journal article or individual meal records. This cohort is distinct from Lorefält’s Linköping cohort. [2004 primary article abstract, PMID 15359362](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15359362/) · [Uppsala deposit uu-69169](https://uu.diva-portal.org/dice/oai?verb=GetRecord&metadataPrefix=mets_kb&identifier=oai:DiVA.org:uu-69169) · [Uppsala deposit uu-89989](https://uu.diva-portal.org/dice/oai?verb=GetRecord&metadataPrefix=mets_kb&identifier=oai:DiVA.org:uu-89989) · [Lorefält cohort](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#food-texture-and-weight-loss-in-a-swedish-parkinsons-cohort)

## Mashed potato as an example in a dysphagia questionnaire

Wallace, Middleton and Cook’s April 2000 questionnaire validation study included 12 people with Parkinson’s among 48 people with dysphagia of neuromuscular origin. The soft-food item includes mashed potato among examples, alongside scrambled egg and mornay dishes. Participants also had a videofluoroscopic swallowing assessment, but the paper does not describe using mashed potato as the test bolus or give a potato-specific result for Parkinson’s. [Primary article, Tables 1–2](https://www.gastrojournal.org/article/S0016-5085(00)70137-5/pdf) · [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1053/gg.2000.5949)

## Earlier Linköping Parkinson weight-loss analysis

Lorefält and colleagues’ 2004 report describes 26 people treated with levodopa and 26 matched controls assessed twice one year apart. Nineteen people with Parkinson’s lost weight even though energy intake increased. The abstract discusses disease severity, cognition and energy intake and gives no ingredient-level or swallowing results. Its sample sizes, interval and number with weight loss match the later doctoral and swallowing reports. [Primary 2004 article abstract, PMID 15285776](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15285776/) · [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0404.2004.00307.x) · [Later Linköping reports](../2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#food-texture-and-weight-loss-in-a-swedish-parkinsons-cohort)

## Solid and liquid bedside swallows in early Parkinson disease

Volonté, Porta and Comi's September 2002 study, *Clinical assessment of dysphagia in early phases of Parkinson's disease*, enrolled 65 people with idiopathic Parkinson disease at Hoehn and Yahr stages 1.5–3.0. Table 1 of the modified Dysphagia Rating Scale includes six solid and six liquid swallows. Items 7–8 record cough around liquid and solid intake, items 9–10 record the bolus trials, and item 11 rates voice after liquid intake. The Results report cough after liquid in 40% and after solid in 5%, wet voice after liquid in 40%, and a mean rating scale score of 55.3 (SD 3.1) out of 60. The abstract's combined wording leaves the solid result ambiguous. On the questionnaire, 35% reported occasional swallowing difficulty with solids and liquids, 30% reported food sticking, and the authors report wet voice and cough after liquid together at 20%. [Publisher abstract and date](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s100720200099) · [Primary publication record, PMID 12548373](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12548373/) · [Indexed original Results and Table 1, printed pp. S121–S122](https://www.parkinsonlimpedismov.it/web/image/28349/SyllabusCongressoAlba2001.pdf)

The indexed full report specifies neither bolus ingredients nor size. It describes clinical signs without instrumentally confirmed aspiration, choking or an ingredient-specific comparison. The full article text was readable in the search index for the Italian society's selected-papers volume. Its cover dates the Alba meeting to November 7–9, 2001, and the article pages carry the 2002 journal imprint. The individual 2001 presentation is unverified. The direct society URL now serves a placeholder image, and the review used the indexed article text.
