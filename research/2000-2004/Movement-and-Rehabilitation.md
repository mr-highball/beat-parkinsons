# Movement and Rehabilitation for 2000–2004

### 2001 — Two-year follow-up of intensive voice treatment

Ramig and colleagues compared voice-focused treatment with respiratory effort treatment in 33 people. Vocal loudness and pitch variation improved more with Lee Silverman Voice Treatment and remained improved at two years. Outcomes primarily measured voice production. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11561033/)

### 2002 — Swallowing measures after intensive voice therapy

El Sharkawi and colleagues measured voice and swallowing before and after intensive voice therapy in eight people. Some oral and pharyngeal swallowing measures improved. This uncontrolled pilot did not determine whether aspiration or everyday eating outcomes changed. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11784821/)

### 2002 — Body-weight-supported treadmill rehabilitation

Miyai and colleagues compared supported treadmill training with conventional physical therapy in a randomized study. Walking speed improved at early follow-up and step count over a short course remained better for several months. The study addressed gait performance in a small inpatient rehabilitation sample. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12370870/)

### 2002 — Prospective study of fall frequency and predictors

Wood and colleagues followed 109 people with Parkinson’s disease to document falls. Prior falls, longer disease duration, dementia, and loss of arm swing predicted falling in multivariable analysis. The study identified prospective correlates without testing a prevention strategy. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12023412/)

### 2003 — Short multidisciplinary rehabilitation and group support

Wade and colleagues tested a multidisciplinary rehabilitation and support program in a randomized crossover design. Mobility showed a possible short-term benefit. Disability, quality of life, and caregiver strain worsened by six-month follow-up, leaving durable benefit uncertain. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12531939/)
