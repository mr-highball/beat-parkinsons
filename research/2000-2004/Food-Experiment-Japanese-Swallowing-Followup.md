# Japanese primary swallowing evidence for the food thought experiment

## Sugar solution agar and cookie in the Toyota Memorial series

Takeshi Yasuda’s study appears in the fiscal-1999 Japanese Ministry of Health and Welfare grant report H11–長寿–034, whose cover is dated March 31, 2000. The clinical series included ten older inpatients and outpatients labeled as having Parkinson’s disease with suspected dysphagia. The authors considered dementia with Lewy bodies possible in four of the ten. Videofluoroscopy used contrast-mixed sugar solution with and without a commercial thickener, barium agar as the solid and a barium cookie for mastication and bolus formation. Progression through the sequence depended on swallowing safety, and the report gives no denominator for each bolus. [Dated grant report cover](https://mhlw-grants.niph.go.jp/system/files/1999/000115/199900172A/199900172A0001.pdf#page=1) · [Yasuda’s primary report](https://mhlw-grants.niph.go.jp/system/files/1999/000115/199900172A/199900172A0002.pdf)

The investigators describe small amounts of liquid entering the trachea in three participants, all while cup-drinking the unthickened sugar solution. They do not identify whether these three were among the four with possible dementia with Lewy bodies. After individualized swallowing rehabilitation, eight improved clinically over two to three months. Four of five people reexamined with fluoroscopy improved. Five advanced from blended to chopped food, and three from isotonic jelly to thickened fluids. The series had no control group. The post-therapy changes concern swallowing rehabilitation and accompanying texture changes. Baked potato was not among the named boluses. [Primary methods and results](https://mhlw-grants.niph.go.jp/system/files/1999/000115/199900172A/199900172A0002.pdf)

The fiscal-2000 grant synthesis lists ten Parkinson participants with the same sex split and mean age (eight men, two women, 72.2 years) alongside five with progressive supranuclear palsy and five with multiple system atrophy. The synthesis reports that many Parkinson participants improved after training. It supplies no new food-level results. The exact match in group characteristics and grant series indicates likely reuse of the Parkinson group. [Following grant report, project 5 methods and results](https://mhlw-grants.niph.go.jp/system/files/2000/000136/200000193A/200000193A0001.pdf)

## 2001 and 2002 capsaicin jelly swallowing comparisons

Nozaki and colleagues' 2001 rehabilitation proceedings report a sequential capsaicin jelly challenge in nine participants with suspected neurogenic dysphagia, five with Parkinson's disease, three with cerebrovascular disease, and one with motor neuron disease. They recorded repeated green tea swallows by electroglottography before, immediately after, and five minutes after the capsaicin jelly. Pooled swallow latency fell from 428 to 319 ms at five minutes (p < .05) and mean duration from 5,115 to 4,044 ms (p < .01). The abstract supplies no Parkinson-only estimates or parallel control. Four participants had videofluoroscopy, and the authors report less phlegm and coughing in one subsequently treated patient whose diagnosis is unspecified. [Original proceedings, I-H-72, S206, PDF p. 13](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjrm1964/38/Supplement/38_Supplement_S194/_pdf/-char/ja#page=13)

The same investigators' May 9, 2002 conference presentation compared an iopamidol contrast jelly with the same 1.6% jelly containing capsaicin (65 µg per 200 mL) in 15 people with Parkinson's disease and swallowing difficulty, stages III–V. Mean pharyngeal delay was 0.48 seconds with plain contrast jelly and 0.18 with capsaicin. Mean pharyngeal passage time was 1.48 and 1.09 seconds, and oral passage time was 1.39 and 1.48 seconds, respectively. The abstract gives dispersion but no significance tests for the comparison. None of the participants showed overt aspiration on imaging. The study measures immediate swallow timing in a selected sample. It gives no ground cayenne exposure or sustained change in dysphagia severity. The 2002 report cites the 2001 study as its precursor. Participant overlap is unspecified. [Original proceedings, I-7-8, S157, PDF p. 3](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjrm1964/39/Supplement/39_Supplement_S155/_pdf/-char/en#page=3)
