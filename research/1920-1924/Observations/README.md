# Observations for 1920-1924

## Major observations

- Published cases explicitly described a parkinsonian syndrome following encephalitis lethargica. Legrand questioned emotional origin claims and recognized potential confusion between syndromes. [Clinical sources](../Clinical-and-Diagnosis.md) · [Risk](../Risk-and-Prevention.md)

## Minor observations

- A pallidal autopsy finding opened a tissue-iron question, with the authors explicitly leaving causality unresolved. A nasal obstruction case recorded a patient-reported symptom outside the usual limb signs. [Mechanisms](../Mechanisms-and-Models.md) · [Daily life](../Nonmotor-and-Daily-Life.md)
- Critchley distinguished general discomfort, tremor, rigidity, and excess saliva as treatment outcomes, and recorded little motor benefit for one historical remedy. [Remedies](../Drugs-and-Remedies.md)

## Comparison with 1915–1919

- The earlier bucket documented the acute encephalitic illness. The 1920 case presentations supply a link between that illness and the later motor syndrome in individual patients. The autopsy literature continued to consider several basal-ganglia regions. [Prior observations](../../1915-1919/Observations/README.md)
