# Clinical findings and diagnosis, 1920-1924

### 1920 — Wilson documents symptomatic parkinsonism after encephalitis

S. A. Kinnier Wilson presented cases with rigidity, altered posture and gait, and sometimes tremor after encephalitis lethargica. These case histories show that a known prior neurological illness could precede a Parkinson-like clinical syndrome. They do not establish an infectious origin for idiopathic Parkinson’s disease.

[Wilson, Proceedings of the Royal Society of Medicine, scan](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2152436/)

### 1922 — Russell examines the Parkinsonian syndrome

Colin K. Russell’s two-part clinical account argued that several causes might produce the same clinical syndrome. He described rigidity, postural change, and altered facial expression and distinguished classical paralysis agitans from forms following encephalitis. This was a historical synthesis and anatomical interpretation of clinical and autopsy literature.

[Russell, Canadian Medical Association Journal, part one, scan](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1524438/) · [Part two, scan](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1524526/)

### 1924 — Critchley surveys diagnosis and treatment in the encephalitis era

Macdonald Critchley discussed recognition and treatment of the Parkinsonian syndrome in a clinical paper. The report documents a contemporary differential and individualized treatment of symptom clusters, including tremor and excess saliva. Its recommendations reflect contemporary practice without comparative trial data.

[Critchley, Bristol Medico-Chirurgical Journal, scan](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5058020/)
