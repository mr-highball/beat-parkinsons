# Nutmeg and paprika evidence

## Sleep after a mixed hot sauce and mustard meal

Edwards and colleagues compared an evening meal seasoned with Tabasco sauce and mustard with a control meal in six healthy young men. The seasoned meal was followed by less slow-wave and stage-two sleep, more time awake, and a trend toward longer sleep latency. Neither seasoning was tested individually. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1399758/)

The original full Table 1 gives total sleep time of 423.3 versus 445.2 minutes (p < .002) and sleep efficiency of 0.90 versus 0.94 (p < .03) for the spicy and control meals respectively. Mean sleep onset latency was 34.8 versus 16.1 minutes (p between .05 and .10). The meal contained 3 g each of Tabasco sauce and prepared English mustard. The protocol leaves the effect of either component, its preparation and transfer to clinical insomnia open. [Original full report and Table 1](https://kratosathletics.com/blog/articles/Edwards_%281992%29_SpicySleep.pdf)

## Nutmeg seed extracts and dry stool output in rats

The 1991 original used serial solvent extracts of powdered nutmeg seed. The stool-output experiment in rats gives dry stool mass of 0.976 ± 0.048 g per 100 g body mass with vehicle and 0.207 ± 0.049 with the highest ethanol extract group, with 15 animals in each group. Petroleum ether and chloroform findings varied by dose. Table 2 separately tested castor oil-induced diarrhea. The authors' comparator wording for the significance marks is ambiguous and the collection interval is not clear. These outcomes describe extracts and stool output rather than clinical constipation severity.
