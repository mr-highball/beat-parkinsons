# Movement and Rehabilitation for 1990–1994

### 1992 — Hydrology and hydrotherapy proposals in Parkinson’s

Ceballos Hernansanz discussed exercise, rehabilitation, and hydrotherapy for rigidity, akinesia, tremor, and functional independence. The narrative also describes constipation as an autonomic symptom. It gives no tested dietary intervention, cohort, or measured outcome. Later communications add examples of spa modalities and a protein-timing proposal. [Publisher scan, pages 127–130](https://hidromed.org/hm/images/pdf/BSEHM%201992_7%283%29127-130_Ceballos-MA.pdf) · [1995 communication](../1995-1999/Movement-and-Rehabilitation.md#1995--spa-treatment-and-movement-proposals) · [1997 diet note](../1995-1999/Food-and-Nutrition.md#1997--diet-during-a-parkinsons-spa-regimen)

### 1994 — Intensive physical rehabilitation in a controlled crossover trial

Comella and colleagues compared four weeks of intensive rehabilitation with usual physical activity, using a blinded clinical assessor. Motor and activities-of-daily-living scores improved after rehabilitation. Scores returned to baseline over six months without regular exercise in this cohort. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8145901/)

### 1994 — Stride length as a contributor to slow gait

Morris and colleagues compared people with Parkinson’s disease and matched controls in gait experiments. Short steps explained much of the gait slowness, while increased cadence appeared partly compensatory. The experiments motivated rehabilitation strategies targeting step length and external cues. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7953597/)
