Title: Lemon balm and nutmeg in historical pharmacy as described in the Harveian Oration
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5400928/
Publication date: 1994
Date basis: Publication of the 1993 oration. The writer cites the 1618 pharmacopoeia and older pharmacy practices.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source kind: Modern historical article describing a traditional apothecary practice
Community and context: British learned pharmacy and its written botanical materia medica.
Reported direction: Unspecified for Parkinson symptoms.
Authenticity: The original published article gives botanical terms and explicitly names the historic pharmacopoeia. The earlier edition was not independently inspected.
Efficacy evidence: A retrospective account of ingredient selection and a compounded preparation. It supplies no comparative individual or clinical outcome.
Access and follow-up: Inspected the original published oration. Historical claims are attributed to the oration and its cited period.

The oration lists Melissa leaves and nutmeg among earlier medicinal ingredients. It separately identifies nutmeg among the components in a 1618 compound named Aqua Mirabilis. That mention does not establish that both ingredients were taken as an isolated oral preparation, or that either affected depression in a Parkinson population.
