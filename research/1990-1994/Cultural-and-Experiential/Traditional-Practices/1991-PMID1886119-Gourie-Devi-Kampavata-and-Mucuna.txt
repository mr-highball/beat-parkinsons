Inspected passage: Page 491 for historical terminology and treatment context, and page 492, references 5–10, for text editions and the earlier clinical paper.

Community and context: A neurology department and Ayurvedic research unit in India published this interpretation of Sanskrit works and modern translations. They read kampavata as a shaking syndrome resembling parkinsonism. They place kampa (tremor), stambha (stiffness) and vishada (depression) in a larger grouping of vata disorders. The cited descriptions do not grade or isolate rest tremor, and their fit to modern diagnostic criteria remains uncertain.

Reported practice: The discussion describes oral medicated fats and other combined measures, including oil massage, with various additional routes. It names ashwagandha root, Mucuna pruriens seed, Bala root and the dried fruit called Paraseekayavanee, identified in the article as Hyoscyamus reticulatus. The article gives no ingredient breakdown for the medicated edible fats or a specific oil identity. These are descriptions of customary preparations rather than single-exposure comparisons.

Reported direction: The authors say the practices were intended to relieve the described syndrome. They report broadly favorable outcomes and infrequent, mild adverse effects from a separate 1978 oral Mucuna seed-powder publication involving 23 people. The cultural account has no separately measured direction for rest tremor, stiffness, depression or any particular preparation.

Authenticity: The two-page journal discussion and its bibliographic references are verified. Its historical interpretation is a secondary account of earlier texts.
Efficacy evidence: The 23-person result is attributed to the 1978 authors. Later repetitions and the 1978 cohort are one clinical citation trail.
