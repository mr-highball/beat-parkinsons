Title: Coriander in Encyclopaedia Iranica and its Heravi juice attribution
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/coriander-coriandrum-sativum-l/
Source kind: Scholarly report of traditional practice
Publication date: 1993-12-15
Date basis: Encyclopedia volume VI, fascicle 3, p. 273. The online entry notes a 2016-06-14 update.
Reviewed: 2026-11-27
Community/context: Scholarly account of coriander in Persian medicinal and culinary writing.
Exposure and reported symptom: Fresh coriander juice in the cited Heravi text, with a warning about mental confusion and lethargy. The encyclopedia discusses leaves in nearby text without specifying that the juice comes from leaves.
Reported direction: Adverse effects attributed to the traditional source.
Efficacy evidence: Secondary witness to the same historical account. No independently observed efficacy or Parkinson comparison.
Evidence and limits: The entry is a secondary attribution and contains no observed patient outcomes or verified Parkinson comparison.
Authenticity: Published encyclopedia entry by Houshang Alam. It cites a 1967 Persian edition on pp. 266–267, which was not independently inspected.
Related research: Achundow's 1893 German translation of Heravi, section 474, supplies a dated witness to the same lineage. Shared attribution does not constitute independent confirmation.
Access and follow-up: The formula, symptom translation and plant-part attribution in the Persian edition remain open.

The reported lethargy is contextual for fresh cilantro and apathy. It supplies neither an apathy measurement nor reduced facial expressiveness after dried cilantro.
