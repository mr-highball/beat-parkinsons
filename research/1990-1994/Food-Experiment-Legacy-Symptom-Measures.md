# Recovered original reports of Parkinson symptoms and clinical context

## Pain and hallucinations during acute levodopa withdrawal

July 1, 1993. The JNNP report assessed nine patients with motor fluctuations during acute levodopa withdrawal. On p. 772 the authors describe one participant who withdrew after 23 hours with severe off-period confusion and visual hallucinations. Another patient reported increasing leg pain and discomfort after 96 hours despite little change in the recorded motor score. These are individual events during medication withdrawal. The study yields no food-effect estimate or hallucination-frequency outcome. The surname Sage in a citation is outside the exposure assessment. ## Instrumented stride in a levodopa formulation comparison

April 1993. Weller et al., Br J Clin Pharmacol 35(4), 379–385. Nine patients with idiopathic parkinsonism received two controlled-release levodopa formulations in random order with placebo balancing. Printed p. 382 reports adjusted grand mean stride of 1,009 (SE 14) mm with Madopar CR and 1,058 (SE 14) mm with Sinemet CR (p = .04). Pages 380 and 384 discuss earlier work on fallers and possible mechanisms involving rigidity, respectively. The trial quantified gait and leaves fall frequency, rigidity severity and a sage effect unevaluated.
