# Dietary context for chive claims in 1990–1994

## Protein redistribution and peak-dose dyskinesia

Giménez-Roldán and Mateo reported an 8–12-week protein-redistribution study in 1991. The 26 participants had Parkinson's disease and motor fluctuations despite treatment with levodopa and bromocriptine. Fifteen were evaluable. Ten of those participants benefited, with a reported 79% reduction in mean off time based on their on/off charts. The investigators reported that nocturnal akinesia and peak-dose dyskinesia often worsened. The abstract does not give a dyskinesia effect estimate or the number affected.

The diet changed the distribution of protein over the day. The study contains no chive-specific exposure or result. The divergence between off time and peak-dose dyskinesia shows why a general motor benefit cannot substitute for a chive-specific dyskinesia outcome in claim 0543.
