# Risk and Prevention for 1990–1994

### 1992 — Calgary case-control study of agricultural work

Semchuk and colleagues compared 130 Parkinson’s cases with 260 age- and sex-matched community controls. Several agricultural exposures were associated before adjustment, and occupational herbicide use remained associated in multivariable analysis. Retrospective interviews, exposure overlap, and confounding limit causal interpretation. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1620342/)

### 1994 — Horticultural occupations in British Columbia

Hertzman and colleagues compared 127 cases with 245 controls. Likely direct occupational contact with pesticides was associated with idiopathic parkinsonism, but the study identified no individual chemical. Case definitions, recalled exposures, and the two different control groups affect interpretation. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8139607/)
