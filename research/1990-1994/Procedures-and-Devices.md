# Procedures and Devices for 1990–1994

### 1990 — Fetal dopamine graft imaging and motor observations

Lindvall and colleagues reported imaging consistent with graft activity and reduced rigidity, bradykinesia, and motor fluctuations in one recipient. The greater motor change was opposite the graft. The uncontrolled clinical observation was an early demonstration of feasibility. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2105529/)

### 1991 — Longer-term thalamic stimulation for tremor

Benabid and colleagues followed 26 people with Parkinson’s disease and six with essential tremor after thalamic stimulation. Most treated sides had sustained tremor suppression or marked improvement, with adjustable adverse effects. The series focused on tremor and included two disorders. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1671433/)

### 1992 — Posteroventral pallidotomy outcomes and visual injury

Laitinen and colleagues assessed 38 people after posteroventral pallidotomy. Rigidity, movement slowness, tremor, and dyskinesia often improved. Seven people had complications, including six with permanent partial visual-field loss. The uncontrolled design and variable follow-up limit comparison with other procedures. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1727169/)

### 1992 — Follow-up of fetal dopamine tissue recipients

Freed and colleagues reported clinical improvement and PET findings compatible with graft survival in a small series of people with advanced disease. Open treatment and lack of sham control left the magnitude of graft-specific benefit uncertain. [Original publication via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1435881/)
