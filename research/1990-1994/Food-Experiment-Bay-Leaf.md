# Bay leaf foreign bodies

## Esophageal and hypopharyngeal impaction reports in 1990

Buto and colleagues published their esophageal and hypopharyngeal bay leaf case report on July 1, 1990. PubMed has no abstract, and the publisher body was inaccessible. A secondary consumer summary describes five endoscopically observed leaf impactions after meals. The original case count, descriptions, symptoms and diagnosis histories remain to be checked against the full paper. [Primary publication record, PMID 2350114](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2350114/) · [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.7326/0003-4819-113-1-82) · [Secondary summary](https://www.readabstracts.com/Health/Tracheobronchial-foreign-bodies-in-adults-Barrett-esophagus-a-sequela-of-chemotherapy.html)

Belitsos's September 15, 1990 reply presents a separate case. A woman experienced severe midchest pain after eating chicken cacciatori. Endoscopy found a sizable bay leaf wedged in the mid-esophagus, with its rim cutting into esophageal muscle. The clinician removed the leaf endoscopically. The letter reports no Parkinson diagnosis, powdered exposure or measured swallowing or drooling severity. [Original letter, printed pp. 483–484](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=4bf28f9caa61987b8a80c08e1fdce36e07053dce&repid=rep1&type=pdf) · [PubMed record, PMID 2386346](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2386346/)

## Two esophageal bay leaf obstructions in 1993

Ahmed and McCormick's February 1993 abstract reports two esophageal obstructions caused by bay leaf foreign bodies. The public abstract gives no meal preparation, diagnosis history, individual symptom details or case outcomes. Cambridge dates its web version to June 2007. The 1993 issue is the first verified publication. Its full case text remains inaccessible through the publisher and the French ISTEX download route. [Original abstract, PMID 8496654](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8496654/) · [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022215100122492) · [ISTEX archive record](https://api.istex.fr/ark:/67375/6GQ-9W9HKS7Z-L)

## Whole bay leaf in potato soup and three other hypopharyngeal impactions

Awerbuck, Briant and Wax presented these cases in May 1993 and published them in March 1994. Their first page describes four patients with hypopharyngeal bay leaf impaction and no history of swallowing disorder or earlier foreign body impaction. A 42-year-old man inhaled one bay leaf while cooling a spoonful of potato soup, then swallowed it. He had marked dysphagia but could swallow his secretions. The leaf was removed from the postcricoid area and esophagoscopy showed no mucosal lacerations. The identified foreign body was the leaf. The soup's potato preparation is unspecified. [Original article first-page scan, printed p. 338](https://journals.sagepub.com/cms/10.1177/019459989411000314/asset/f6268c98-57a2-4c35-a450-7f7e7fe78a15/assets/019459989411000314.fp.png) · [Publisher citation and presentation date](https://aao-hnsfjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1177/019459989411000314)

In case 2, a man swallowing a leaf from soup had marked dysphagia and could still swallow his secretions. One end of the leaf was embedded in the right piriform sinus. Case 3 expelled a horizontally lodged leaf by coughing. In case 4, a leaf spanning the hypopharynx rotated during breathing and intermittently obstructed the airway, with exertional stridor and respiratory distress. The public scan ends before case 4's resolution. The report gives no Parkinson diagnosis or ground leaf exposure on the accessible page. [Original article first-page scan, cases 2–4](https://journals.sagepub.com/cms/10.1177/019459989411000314/asset/f6268c98-57a2-4c35-a450-7f7e7fe78a15/assets/019459989411000314.fp.png) · [PubMed record, PMID 8134147](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8134147/)
