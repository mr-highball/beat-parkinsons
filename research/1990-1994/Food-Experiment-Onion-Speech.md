# Onion exposure and speech intelligibility

## Raw onion and esophageal acid exposure

Allen and colleagues studied 16 patients who reported heartburn and 16 people without heartburn in April 1990. Participants ate the same hamburger-and-water meal on counterbalanced days, once with a slice of raw onion and once without it. Esophageal pH was monitored for two hours. The raw onion increased esophageal acid exposure, reflux episodes, heartburn and belching in the heartburn group. Measures in the comparison group did not show an increase. The study did not test cooked onion, laryngopharyngeal exposure, speech intelligibility or Parkinson disease. ## Airborne onion reactions and negative oral challenges

Valdivieso and colleagues reported a clinic series in November 1994. An allergen skin panel identified onion reactivity in eight of 106 people tested. Four had a history of symptoms associated with onion handling or smell. Two subjects underwent double-blind oral provocations with onion powder and did not react. The oral challenges, handling histories and inhalation tests concerned different exposures. The report did not quantify speech or study cooked onion meals or Parkinson disease.
