Title: Effects of increasing dietary palmitoleic acid compared with palmitic and oleic acids on plasma lipids of hypercholesterolemic men
PMID: 8006520
DOI: 10.1016/S0022-2275(20)41179-4
Publication date: 1994-04
Citation: Paul Nestel, Peter Clifton and Manny Noakes. Journal of Lipid Research 35(4):656–662.
Source: https://web.archive.org/web/20180417073139id_/http://www.jlr.org:80/content/35/4/656.full.pdf
Index: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8006520/
Text coverage: Original body, all seven printed pages inspected. Methods and Tables 1–5 summarize the blend and plasma endpoints.

The 34 men had mildly or moderately elevated cholesterol. After a 7–10 day baseline, all participants received three diets for three weeks each in randomly ordered sequences. The study provided milk drinks and margarines with test fats formulated from macadamia oil, high-oleic Trisun oil and palm oil. The drinks were homogenized and pasteurized. The margarines used partially hydrogenated fats. The oil pressing method is unspecified. Under the palmitoleic formulation, the oil and margarine contained 18.4% and 13.9% palmitoleic acid, respectively, by the Table 1 composition measurements. The contrast changes several fatty acids and food forms.

On page 657, two participants reported transient gastrointestinal discomfort during the macadamia-rich diet. The paper gives no symptom breakdown for that discomfort. It reports no diagnosed Parkinson population or resting tremor measure. One participant's dietary records were excluded for failure to maintain low background fat intake. That exclusion concerns dietary records.

On page 659, Table 4 reports means of 4.06, 3.89 and 4.05 mmol/L LDL cholesterol for the palmitoleic, oleic and palmitic periods, respectively. The authors report a 0.17 mmol/L palmitoleic-minus-oleic difference (confidence limits 0.01 to 0.35) and 0.01 mmol/L palmitoleic-minus-palmitic difference (−0.18 to 0.17). Triglycerides did not significantly differ across periods. Table 5 reports increased plasma palmitoleic acid under the palmitoleic-rich diet. These blood findings address lipid exposure and response. The report does not estimate an effect of culinary cold-pressed macadamia oil on movement.
