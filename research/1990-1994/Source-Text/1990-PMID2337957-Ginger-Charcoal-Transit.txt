Title: Gastrointestinal motility enhancing effect of ginger and its active constituents
PMID: 2337957
DOI: 10.1248/cpb.38.430
Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/cpb1958/38/2/38_2_430/_article
Original PDF: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/cpb1958/38/2/38_2_430/_pdf
Date basis: 1990-02-25 issue publication, pages 430–431.
Coverage: Factual extract of the two-page original experiment and Tables I–III.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Yamahara and colleagues compared an oral acetone extract from ginger rhizome, fractions, isolated constituents, and a comparator in separate arms in male ddY mice. The outcome was the percentage of small intestine traversed by activated charcoal, measured from the pylorus at the acute sample point.

At selected tested amounts, the extract and isolated 6-shogaol and 6-, 8-, or 10-gingerol increased transport relative to vehicle. Table III shows 65.4% traversed versus 48.9% for the reported 2.5 mg/kg 6-shogaol comparison, and 62.6% versus 48.9% for the 5 mg/kg 8-gingerol comparison. The analogous tabulated 6- and 10-gingerol comparisons are at 10 mg/kg. The abstract compresses the dose description. The paper measured small-intestinal transport after a marker meal, with no stool symptom or clinical Parkinson endpoint.
