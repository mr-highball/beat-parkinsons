Mandrake mixed with spinach and chard leaves in a poisoning series
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2087109/
PMID: 2087109
Coverage: factual extract from primary abstract

M. E. Jiménez-Mejías and colleagues, Med Clin (Barc), November 24, 1990, 95(18):689–692.
Two family groups comprised 15 patients with acute anticholinergic syndrome. Symptoms started one to four hours after ingestion. The pooled clinical category of hyperactivity or hallucination occurred in 14 individuals and agitation or delirium in nine. Plant examination identified Mandragora autumnalis intermingled with Spinacia oleracea and Beta vulgaris leaves. The investigators detected atropine and hyoscyamine. The abstract gives neither the preparation of each edible leaf nor an isolated hallucination frequency, visual modality, or Parkinson subgroup. This is a source-adulteration example for claims 0104 and 0352.
