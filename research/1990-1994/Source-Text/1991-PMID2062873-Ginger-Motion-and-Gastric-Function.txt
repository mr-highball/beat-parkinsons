Title: Effects of ginger on motion sickness susceptibility and gastric function
PMID: 2062873
DOI: 10.1159/000138781
Source: https://karger.com/pha/article-abstract/42/2/111/269950/Effects-of-Ginger-on-Motion-Sickness
Published: 1991, Pharmacology 42, 111–120. Online archive date 2008-06-06.
Archive scope: Factual summary of the original publisher abstract. Full methods and tables require further access.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Twenty-eight volunteers participated in the rotating-chair study program. Powdered whole root at 500 or 1,000 mg and fresh root at 1,000 mg did not increase the head-movement tolerance endpoint versus placebo. The positive-control scopolamine comparison showed 147.5 more movements on average, p < .01. The nuclear liquid-emptying comparison used 500 mg powdered ginger and reported no clear change. That dose partially reduced motion-induced tachygastria without restoring the normal postdrink amplitude response. The original abstract does not allocate all 28 people across these experiments. Motion tolerance, electrical activity, and gastric emptying are different outcomes. A fresh-root motion comparison does not measure grated ginger and constipation in Parkinson disease.
