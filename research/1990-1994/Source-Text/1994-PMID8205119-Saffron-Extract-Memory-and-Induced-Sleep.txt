Title: Oral saffron extract, memory and drug-induced sleeping time in mice
Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/bpb1993/17/2/17_2_217/_article
Original page images: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/bpb1993/17/2/17_2_217/_pdf/-char/en
PMID: 8205119
DOI: 10.1248/bpb.17.217
Publication date: 1994-02-15
Date basis: Publisher's February 1994 journal issue record.
Review basis: Complete original page images, printed pages 217–221, with Methods, Results, Table I and Figure 4 inspected.
Source type: Controlled acute experiment in male mice.

The authors gave a single oral 50% ethanolic extract of pistils from plants cultivated in Japan. It improved some memory effects after an ethanol challenge and did not clearly improve normal or scopolamine-challenged memory. The experiment measured different behavioral tasks, including memory acquisition and retrieval.

Figure 4 records sleeping time as duration of loss of the righting reflex after hexobarbital, with 10 animals per tested group. The middle extract arm is marked p<.05 versus control. The other two extract dose arms do not carry a significance mark in the figure. The authors also report reduced spontaneous locomotor counts. Table I, experiment B, gives fewer fecal pellets after selected doses. The findings are acute behavior and excretion observations following an extract. The study does not measure spontaneous insomnia, culinary ground-saffron effects or Parkinson disease.

The 2006 online seizure report cites this article in its discussion of customary use and earlier extract research. The 1994 first page attributes the fear, trance and depression uses to the 1977 Dictionary of Traditional Chinese Medicine, volume 2, pages 2622–2623. The experiment and the historical citation are separate.
