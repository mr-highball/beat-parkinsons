Title: Subcutaneous apomorphine in the treatment of Parkinson's disease
Source: https://jnnp.bmj.com/content/jnnp/53/2/96.full.pdf
PMID: 2313313
DOI: 10.1136/jnnp.53.2.96
Publication date: 1990-02
Date basis: NLM, JNNP 53(2), 96–101
Source depth: Complete publisher original PDF
Format: Original-source factual extract

Frankel and colleagues report a series of 57 levodopa-treated patients with refractory motor fluctuations. Clinical and patient diary measures tracked time spent in the disabling off state. The adverse-event section describes visual hallucinations or visual pseudo-hallucinations in three patients receiving or starting continuous treatment and separate mental-status symptoms in another patient. The report separately describes improvement in off-period hallucinations in one person receiving intermittent injections and withdrawal of another patient after hallucinations with continuous infusion. The adverse-event and withdrawal descriptions may overlap. The prior treatment history and change in motor state complicate comparisons across individuals. No standardized visual episode rate or food exposure was measured. The 2001 Dépatie and Lal review cites this same series.
