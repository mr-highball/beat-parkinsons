PMCID: PMC464082
Citation: Thorax. 1992 Oct. 47:845–896. The meeting was held July 8–10, 1992.
Coverage: All 52 original printed pages. Individual abstract data checked on pages 885, 889 and 891. Distilled factual summary.
Reviewed: 2026-11-22 UTC

Page 885, Thien and colleagues, reports a fish-oil versus olive-oil placebo comparison in pollen-sensitive asthma. Thirty-seven entered and 25 completed. Bronchial reactivity and respiratory symptoms gave no clear between-group differences. A displayed bronchial comparison uses 12 and 9 observations. The paper does not report olive-oil grade or a clinical anxiety endpoint.

Page 889, Henderson and colleagues, compares isolated caffeine with placebo in eight people with mild asthma. Baseline breathing values were similar and FEV1 did not clearly change after the interventions. Histamine challenge thresholds were higher after caffeine at 2.5 and four hours (reported p = .014 and .042). Coffee appears as a contextual amount comparison. No brewed-coffee or fatigue outcome was assessed.

Page 891, Thomas and colleagues, reports a randomized crossover with the respiratory drug ipratropium applied to the oral cavity in healthy volunteers. Dental rolls measured saliva output. The between-regimen change did not reach statistical significance (reported p = .43), and reported oral dryness did not clearly differ. Palatability scores did differ. These outcomes distinguish saliva flow, self-reported dryness and taste. The poster does not estimate clinical drooling or include a raw fig exposure.
