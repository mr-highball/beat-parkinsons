Title: Pathogenetic aspects of persimmon bezoars. A case-control retrospective study.
PMID: 8409318
DOI: 10.1097/00004836-199309000-00012
First published: 1993
Authors: Benharroch D, Krugliak P, Porath A, Zurgil E, Niv Y
Journal: Journal of clinical gastroenterology
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8409318/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 6 review).

Primary Abstract:
To prove that ingestion of unpeeled persimmon is necessary for the development of a persimmon phytobezoar, we interviewed 15 patients, in most of whom this condition developed after peptic ulcer surgery. We compared the study group with a control group of 15 patients who had undergone peptic ulcer surgery but did not have a bezoar. In contrast with the control group, most patients with bezoars had ingested unpeeled fruits (p < 0.01). While ingestion of persimmon carried a 9.8-fold risk of bezoar development, ingestion of the unpeeled fruit increased the risk of this complication 56 times over that of age- and sex-matched controls. We recommend that patients who have undergone ulcer surgery be warned particularly against eating unpeeled persimmons.

Verified Round 6 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Matched retrospective human case-control study (J Clin Gastroenterol 1993;16:124-127) comparing n=15 patients with persimmon phytobezoars (diospyrobezoars) vs n=15 age/sex-matched ulcer-surgery controls without bezoars. Persimmon ingestion carried a 9.8-fold increased risk of bezoar formation, and ingesting UNPEELED raw persimmon increased diospyrobezoar risk 56-fold (p<0.01). Primary causal human risk study.
