Title: The effect of ginger on serotonin induced hypothermia and diarrhea
PMID: 2074539
DOI: 10.1248/yakushi1947.110.12_936
Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/yakushi1947/110/12/110_12_936/_article/-char/ja/
Original PDF: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/yakushi1947/110/12/110_12_936/_pdf
Date basis: 1990-12-25 journal publication, pp. 936–942.
Coverage: Factual summary of the author abstract and original printed Tables V–IX, pp. 939–941.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Huang and colleagues measured serotonin-provoked diarrhea in ddY mice. In Table V the 0–2 diarrhea index was 1.00 in seven mice given the lower tested oral ginger extract amount and 0.22 in nine given the higher amount, versus 2.00 in the separate saline controls. The isolated 6-shogaol and several other constituent arms also showed reductions in Table IX. The isolated 6-gingerol row has no marked difference from its comparator. Table IX contains inconsistent route wording in its footnote. The hypothermia test in Table IV was a separate endpoint.

The diarrhea experiment uses provocation and a pharmacological extract. It supplies no graded constipation outcome. Its endpoint differs from the charcoal transport measure in the separate February 1990 publication.
