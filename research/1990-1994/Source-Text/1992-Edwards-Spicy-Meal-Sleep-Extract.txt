Mustard and Tabasco at an evening meal
Primary source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1399758/
PMID: 1399758
DOI: 10.1016/0167-8760(92)90048-G
Coverage: Factual primary methods and outcome extract.

Edwards et al. studied six healthy men with and without Tabasco sauce and mustard in the evening meal. They reported less slow-wave and stage 2 sleep and more wake time in the spicy condition. The original formulation does not assign black pepper. This is neighboring dietary evidence for spice timing and sleep, with no ingredient-specific comparison or Parkinson cohort.

The original full Table 1 gives total sleep time of 423.3 versus 445.2 minutes (p < .002) and sleep efficiency of 0.90 versus 0.94 (p < .03) for the spicy and control meals respectively. Mean sleep onset latency was 34.8 versus 16.1 minutes (p between .05 and .10). The meal contained 3 g each of Tabasco sauce and prepared English mustard. The protocol leaves the effect of either component, its preparation and transfer to clinical insomnia open.
