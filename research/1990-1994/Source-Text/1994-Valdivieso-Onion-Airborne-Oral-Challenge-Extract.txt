Title: Bronchial asthma, rhinoconjunctivitis, and contact dermatitis caused by onion
Authors: R Valdivieso and colleagues
Primary article record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7963160/
Publisher-format article rendering: https://www.academia.edu/130283957/Bronchial_asthma_rhinoconjunctivitis_and_contact_dermatitis_caused_by_onion
Supporting agency synopsis: https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/food-nutrition/reports-publications/food-labelling/garlic-onions-insufficient-evidence-include-list-priority-food-allergens-canada-systematic-review.html
Identifiers: PMID 7963160
Date: November 1994, pages 928–930
Coverage: factual extract of the original article and agency case description

Of 106 allergy clinic subjects tested for onion sensitization, eight had a positive skin test. Four had symptoms involving onion contact or inhalation. The paper describes positive airway or skin responses after route-specific testing in some participants. Double-blind oral provocations in two patients were negative at the maximum 2 g dose of onion powder. These findings concern sensitization and clinical effects by exposure route. The oral challenge did not use cooked culinary bulb or collect speech outcomes.
