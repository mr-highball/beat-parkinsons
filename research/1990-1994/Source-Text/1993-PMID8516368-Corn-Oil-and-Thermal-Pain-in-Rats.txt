Title: Diet and estrous cycle influence pain sensitivity in rats
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8516368/
PMID: 8516368
DOI: 10.1016/0091-3057(93)90116-b
Publication date: 1993-05
Reviewed: 2026-11-26
Source depth: original author abstract. Full article and numeric group values remain unverified.

The authors studied 124 female rats overall. After deprivation, acute consumption of the study corn oil was associated with longer tail-flick latency than water or sucrose, both before morphine and at the first reported post-morphine interval. This measures an evoked thermal pain reflex. The abstract does not report group-specific sample sizes, numerical oil effect estimates, uncertainty or refining grade. The chronic results explicitly described for sucrose cannot be attributed to corn oil.

The observed acute comparison favors lower thermal pain sensitivity in its animal setting. The restricted experimental conditions, uncertain oil grade and absent disease-specific or patient-reported endpoint make its sign indeterminate for Round 1 claim 0278. This abstract is distinct from the 1994 review of the same experiment and does not create a second evidence family.
