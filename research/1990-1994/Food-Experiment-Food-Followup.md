# Food experiment follow-up evidence

## Cooked broad beans and acute motor response

In 1992, Rabey and colleagues measured motor features and plasma levodopa after six people with Parkinson’s disease and five healthy volunteers ate cooked broad beans (*Vicia faba*). Motor features improved acutely in the Parkinson’s group, plasma levodopa rose, and three of the six participants with Parkinson’s developed severe dyskinesias. A 1993 publication from the same group reports matching sample counts, baseline means, feeding protocol, and dyskinesia counts. The two publications likely describe the same short feeding study. [1992 primary report, PMID 1527547](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC489215/) · [1993 report, PMID 8420210](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8420210/)

In a separate 1993 single-dose study, Kempster and colleagues tested a broad bean pod mixture with carbidopa in six patients. Five had a motor response comparable in magnitude to their conventional levodopa dose, with a mean onset of 39 minutes and mean duration of 104 minutes. Carbidopa coadministration, the pod preparation, the small sample, and short follow-up limit generalization. [Primary report, PMID 24352104](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24352104/)

## Broad bean seedlings and levodopa bioavailability

In 1994, Vered and colleagues reported that plasma levodopa rose after seven people with Parkinson’s and nine healthy controls ate *Vicia faba* seedlings. Motor status improved on the Webster scale in the Parkinson’s group. This small acute study evaluated seedlings and pharmacokinetics. Its comparison included healthy controls and supplied no long-term symptomatic estimate. Two citation paths led to the same publication, identified by DOI. [Authors’ institutional publication record and abstract](https://scholars.mssm.edu/en/publications/bioavailability-of-levodopa-after-consumption-of-vicia-faba-seedl-2/) · [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1097/00002826-199404000-00004)

The frozen experiment contains no *Vicia faba* claim. Green beans, black beans, kidney beans, pinto beans, peas, and edamame are different foods. The feeding findings apply to the named broad bean preparations and help identify what the experiment’s ingredient pool missed. The early chemistry entry records isolation of the constituent. [1910–1914 nutrition entry](../1910-1914/Food-and-Nutrition.md)

## Carrot preparation and fecal bulk outside Parkinson’s disease

In two controlled feeding studies, Wisker and colleagues compared a low-fiber basal diet with diets containing raw, frozen, blanched, or canned carrots. Carrot-containing diets increased stool weight by approximately 2.4–3.7 g per gram of carrot fiber, and the range was similar across the tested preparations. Frozen claim `0623` asks whether steamed carrots reduce constipation severity in Parkinson’s. The report measured fecal bulk in a different population and did not test steamed carrots or clinical constipation. [Primary report, PMID 7986789](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7986789/)
