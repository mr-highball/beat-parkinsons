Title: Bedtime warm milk-cereal beverage tradition and early sleep testing (Brezinova & Oswald 1972)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1788018/
Source kind: Traditional-practice record
Publication date: 1972-05-20
Date basis: British Medical Journal publication date (1972 May 20;2(5811):431-433; PMID:5067672, PMC1788018).
Reviewed: 2026-11-25 UTC
Reported direction: Customary bedtime practice linking warm milk drinks with calmer sleep; however, the 1972 BMJ study tested a malted milk-cereal formulation (Horlicks) rather than plain unfortified pasteurized cow's milk alone.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed historical publication in the British Medical Journal (PMID:5067672, PMC1788018).
Efficacy evidence: Cultural background and bundled malted-cereal beverage testing only; does not isolate plain pasteurized cow's milk or establish insomnia efficacy in Parkinson's disease.

Warm milk before bedtime is a longstanding household sleep custom in Britain and other Western settings. In a classic 1972 British Medical Journal investigation (Brezinova & Oswald, PMID:5067672, PMC1788018), all-night electrophysiological sleep recordings across 18 adults (10 young, 8 older mean age 55 y, 10 nights each) compared an inert capsule against a hot bedtime milk-cereal drink (Horlicks), observing less late-night restlessness in young adults and longer, less broken sleep in older adults. Crucially for plain pasteurized cow's milk claims, this customary bedtime drink bundled malted cereal carbohydrates with milk rather than testing plain cow's milk alone, and modern systematic reviews (Komada 2020; Peres 2025) and pre-sleep casein/alpha-lactalbumin trials (Paterson 2026) find no isolated sleep-quality benefit from ordinary unfortified milk.
