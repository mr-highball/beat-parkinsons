Title: Some initial animal and human pharmacological studies with benapryzine (BRL 1288)
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1776283/

Citation: Brown DM, Hughes BO, Marsden CD, Meadows JC, Spicer B. British Journal of Pharmacology. 1973 Mar. 47:476–486.
Coverage: Complete 11-page PMC scan with OCR. Human salivation Methods, Results and Figure 6 at pp. 478 and 482–483. Distilled factual summary.
Reviewed: 2026-11-22 UTC

In six patients with Parkinson disease, bench measurements used the area wetted on standard blotting paper held at the lips and recorded subjective mouth dryness. The report describes no difference in measured salivation with benapryzine versus placebo and a decrease with benzhexol versus benapryzine. Separate mouse experiments measured drug effects on pharmacologically induced salivation. The human comparison tested medicines and did not include fig intake. Salivary flow and a reported dry mouth differ from a distinct rating of daily-life drooling severity.
