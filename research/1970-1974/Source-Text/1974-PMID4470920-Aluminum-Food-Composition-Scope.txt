Sorenson and colleagues review environmental exposure, analytical methods, food and water measurements, and toxicology. Table 5 compiles aluminum concentrations from prior food analyses. It does not report a dietary intervention on Parkinson symptoms. Concentrations describe the assayed sample and its preparation.

Table 5 on page 30 includes uncooked egg, oats of reported origins, and frozen broccoli. Page 31 includes the kale standard and blackberry samples. Page 32 includes basil, orange sections and juice, and dried pitted dates. Page 33 includes cardamom, cinnamon, clove, ginger, nutmeg, paprika, parsley, rosemary, sage, tarragon leaves and turmeric. The kefir entries on that page include added sugar or added cottage cheese. Printed labels confirm several entries missed by OCR.

The rainbow-trout example on page 57 concerns aluminum exposure in living fish. It supplies no culinary trout measurement or depressive symptom outcome. A cited publication by an author named Parkinson near the end of the review does not establish a Parkinson disease cohort.

Round 1 context. The individual food measurements are relevant to ingredient composition. The paper supplies no estimate for oral exposure to the named recipe on any of the mapped Parkinson outcomes, including clinical pain, falls or depression.
