# Japanese pepper bark in Gotoh's original toxicity series

## Bark identity in the volume 17 paper

Jiro Gotoh, Japanese Journal of Veterinary Science 17(6):205–216, DOI 10.1292/jvms1939.17.205. J-STAGE assigns this volume to 1955. Its listed December 30 publication precedes the January 17, 1956 receipt date printed on the first page. Both dates fall within this period. The original p.205 identifies dried bark of Japanese Xanthoxylum piperitum collected in Iwate and Fukushima as the source of the material. The author's comparisons also mention fruit and leaves. The studies evaluated animal toxicity, including convulsions and respiratory arrest in frogs, and distinguished the named toxic bark fraction from separate crystals. The isolated fraction is distinct from ground culinary fruit. No Parkinson rigidity outcome was measured. [Primary source](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jvms1939/17/6/17_6_205/_article/-char/ja)

## Bark fraction in the volume 18 companion

Gotoh, Japanese Journal of Veterinary Science 18(5):171–177, DOI 10.1292/jvms1939.18.171. The 1956 issue's listed October 30 date precedes the printed December 25, 1956 receipt. The original pp.171–172 applied the precursor crude bark fraction in water or saline to fish and earthworms. Earthworm activity initially increased and then declined to paralysis. Fish had respiratory disturbance and convulsions. This is a related toxicology report with no oral culinary or patient rigidity comparison. It documents preparation lineage for the 1960 mouse report. [Primary source](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jvms1939/18/5/18_5_171/_article/-char/ja)
