# Mechanisms and models

### 1957 — Montagu detects dopamine in brain tissue

Katharine Montagu used chromatography to investigate catechol compounds in rat tissues and brains from different species, including a human specimen. The report identified evidence of dopamine in brain tissue. It supplied a biochemical starting point for work on dopamine distribution and function. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1038/180244a0)

### 1957 — DOPA reverses reserpine-associated motor suppression in animals

Carlsson, Lindqvist and Magnusson reported that DOPA reversed the motor suppression seen after reserpine treatment in animals. A serotonin precursor produced different effects. This was an acute pharmacological model and motivated study of dopamine and movement in humans. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1038/1801200a0)

### 1958 — Dopamine as a candidate brain transmitter

Carlsson, Lindqvist, Magnusson and Waldeck reported brain 3-hydroxytyramine (dopamine), linking its depletion after reserpine and restoration after DOPA to observed behavior. Their evidence supported investigating dopamine as an active transmitter in its own right. Human Parkinson’s tissue measurements followed in the next period. [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13529006/)

### 1959 — Regional distribution of dopamine

Bertler and Rosengren examined dopamine distribution in brain and other tissues. The finding that dopamine concentrated in striatal regions suggested a connection to basal ganglia function. Regional measurements in this study motivated a direct disease-tissue comparison. [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13619664/)
