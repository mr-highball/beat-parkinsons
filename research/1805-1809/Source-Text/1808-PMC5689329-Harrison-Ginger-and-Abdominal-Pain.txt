Title: Harrison’s 1808 firsthand report of ginger and the course of abdominal pain
PMCID: PMC5689329
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5689329/
Published: 1808-06
Archive scope: Complete two-image original print object, volume 19, pages 527–528. The opening and continuation belong to Harrison's one letter.
Reviewed: 2026-11-20

Harrison reported a sudden painful abdominal episode that he interpreted as rheumatic gout. After he took finely powdered ginger with sago and brandy, he initially reported heat and more severe pain, followed by a change in location and relief later in the episode. He used his customary opium at bedtime, after the relief he described, and reported mild urinary discomfort the next morning. These observations are from one uncontrolled course with mixed exposures and an unverified historical diagnosis. There is no bowel-movement count, constipation rating or Parkinson assessment.
