# Drugs and remedies, 1880-1884

### 1881 — Mann reports recovery after a combined regimen

Edward C. Mann described one patient who reportedly recovered after electrical treatment, sodium bromide, and henbane. He acknowledged that the condition may have been functional and doubted that the favorable result would recur. The combination, uncertain diagnosis, and lack of a comparator make the apparent recovery uninterpretable as a treatment effect for idiopathic Parkinson’s disease.

[Mann, Journal of Nervous and Mental Disease, scan](https://archive.org/details/paper-doi-10_1097_00005053-188101000-00009)

### 1883 — Erlenmeyer reports different responses for sleep, sweating, and tremor

A contemporary digest describes a 64-year-old with shaking palsy treated for tremor, insomnia, and excess sweating. A sedative combination reportedly aided sleep without reducing movement, silver nitrate had no effect, and the author reported temporary tremor reductions with other remedies. Hyoscyamine benefit was reported only with toxicity in this case. The digest is a single uncontrolled multi-remedy observation.

[Erlenmeyer, Upon the Symptomatic Treatment of Paralysis Agitans, contemporary digest scan](https://archive.org/details/paper-doi-10_1097_00005053-188310000-00149)
