William B. Hazard. A Trustworthy Combination of Coca, Viburnum and Celery, With Notes of Cases. Southern Medical Record 13(7), 253–256. July 20, 1883. PMID 36022070, PMCID PMC9079538.

The title on page 253 includes “With Notes of Cases.” Page 254 describes celery, including food stalks and a proposed fluid extract, before introducing a commercial product combining celery with coca and viburnum. The article does not establish the celery tissue or extraction used in the product. Its description of dried leaves on page 253 refers to coca.

Page 255 describes uncontrolled cases including dysmenorrhea, nervous exhaustion, hypochondria with constipation, and recurring melancholia. The melancholia case had prior recoveries. The author reports impressions, with differing prior or concurrent care across patients. There is no isolated celery, Parkinson diagnosis, or recorded frequency of visual hallucinations. Page 256 discusses possible uses for chorea, epilepsy and delirium tremens without an observed endpoint for those conditions. The article ends before the unrelated case below the dividing rule.
