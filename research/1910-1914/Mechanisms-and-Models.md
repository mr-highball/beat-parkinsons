# Mechanisms and models, 1910-1914

### 1911 — Funk synthesizes racemic DOPA

Casimir Funk synthesized the racemic mixture of D- and L-3,4-dihydroxyphenylalanine in a chemical investigation. The work established a preparation of a compound whose L form later became a Parkinson’s drug. The 1911 experiment had no Parkinson’s patients or therapeutic outcomes.

[Funk, Journal of the Chemical Society, Transactions](https://doi.org/10.1039/CT9119900554)

### 1912 — Lewy reports intracellular inclusions in paralysis agitans

Friedrich H. Lewy described inclusions in neurons from paralysis agitans autopsies, including in the dorsal vagal nucleus and substantia innominata, in the pathology section of a chapter coauthored with E. Förster. The original plates depict several inclusions. The microscopic observations predate methods for establishing protein composition.

[Förster and Lewy, Paralysis agitans, Handbuch der Neurologie, vol. III, pp. 920–933, scan](https://archive.org/details/handbuchneurologie1912v3/page/920/mode/2up)

### 1913 — Gauthier retains an endocrine explanation in a clinical thesis

Jean Gauthier reviewed pathogenesis and argued that thyroid and parathyroid function could help explain paralysis agitans. The reasoning combines case observations and contemporary interpretations of endocrine therapy. It is an historical hypothesis, without a direct comparative test of hormone function or treatment response.

[Gauthier, Recherches sur la pathogénie et l’étiologie de la paralysie agitante, scan](https://archive.org/details/IA41552620_0071)
