Title: Symptomatic Magnesium Deficiency in Surgical Patients
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1408588/
PMID: 14129387
PMCID: PMC1408588
DOI: 10.1097/00000658-196403000-00012
Publication date: 1964-03
Source depth: complete NLM printed-page images, Annals of Surgery 159(3), pages 402–406; local research digest

Page 403 documents prolonged postoperative gastrointestinal losses and limited intake in one patient. Parenteral phenobarbital controlled the seizures. Oral magnesium sulfate was subsequently given in orange juice as a vehicle, with recovery and normalization as the gastrointestinal losses subsided. The report does not isolate orange juice as an intervention.

Pages 403–404 describe another patient with extensive bowel resection, ongoing losses, confusion, tremor and seizures. Initial calcium treatment did not relieve the symptoms. Magnesium sulfate replacement was followed by improvement and later relapse. During recurrence, oral sulfate and then chloride failed to reverse the symptoms in this patient. Intramuscular replacement was followed by improvement in measured magnesium and clinical signs.

Neither case identifies Parkinson disease, separately measures visual hallucination frequency or describes dried fig intake. Acute severe deficiency and short-bowel absorption affect what can be inferred about route. Page 406, reference 11 cites the 1960 Vallee NEJM paper as a separate clinical report.
