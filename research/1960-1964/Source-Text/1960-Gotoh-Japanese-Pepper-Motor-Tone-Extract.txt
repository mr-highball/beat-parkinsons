Sanshotoxin and motor tone in mice

Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jvms1939/22/6/22_6_333/_article/-char/en
Coverage: Primary English author abstract, factual extract.

Jiro Gotoh, Japanese Journal of Veterinary Science 22(6), December 25, 1960. DOI 10.1292/jvms1939.22.333. The English author abstract reports inhibited spontaneous movement and akinesia in intact mice after administration of a crude sanshotoxin fraction. A motor recording showed transient increased activity followed by inhibition, with decreased limb contraction and tone in that comparison. Severe exposure produced abnormal posture, tremor and convulsions. The report includes intraperitoneal and local brain administration. Reduced recorded tone and convulsive stiffness cannot be equated with Parkinson rigidity. The material was a crude fraction from Japanese Zanthoxylum piperitum, and the abstract does not establish dose correspondence to ground culinary Sichuan pepper. The closely related 1955 and earlier-series reports remain follow-up targets.

Recovered full-body Methods p.333 connects the injected or locally applied crude fraction to the earlier Japanese pepper bark studies. On p.334 Table 1 records no akinesia in the single intact mouse at the tested dose described as producing akinesia in the adjacent prose and abstract. The discrepancy remains unresolved.
