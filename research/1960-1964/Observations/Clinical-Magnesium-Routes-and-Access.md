# Clinical magnesium routes and access

The 1964 postoperative case report records a response to parenteral replacement after oral replacement failed in one patient with intestinal loss. It also describes a second oral exposure in which orange juice was a vehicle and the clinical course included phenobarbital and resolving gastrointestinal losses. The published outcomes include seizures, confusion, tremor and measured magnesium in severe postoperative deficiency. The report identifies no Parkinson cohort, culinary dried fig exposure or visual hallucination endpoint. [Complete printed-page source](../Source-Text/PMC1408588.txt).

The September 1960 Portuguese synopsis adds an early distinction between tetany relief and subsequent delirium after normalization. The underlying NEJM case descriptions remain unverified at body depth. A 1961 same-title record shares two authors and has a separate accession and pagination. Its participant independence is unresolved. Preserve these as separate access routes to clinical information without counting a citation or bibliography as an additional patient.
