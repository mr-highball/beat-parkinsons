# Observations for 1960–1964

## Major observations

Human postmortem tissue measurements identified striatal dopamine depletion in both Parkinson’s disease and postencephalitic parkinsonism. Intravenous L-DOPA showed rapid symptomatic effects in one series, and a small later DOPA series reported insufficient benefit under its tested regimen. [Models](../Mechanisms-and-Models.md) · [Remedies](../Drugs-and-Remedies.md)

## Minor observations

The reports on severe orthostatic hypotension and progressive supranuclear palsy provided more specific alternatives to idiopathic Parkinson’s disease for some overlapping presentations. Pallidal lesion series continued alongside new drug studies. [Diagnosis](../Clinical-and-Diagnosis.md) · [Procedures](../Procedures-and-Devices.md)

## Comparison with 1955–1959

[Previous observations](../../1955-1959/Observations/README.md)

Dopamine work moved from experimental preparations and regional mapping into human disease tissue and early symptomatic treatment. The different clinical results exposed the importance of route, formulation and duration when comparing trials.

The 1959 chlorphenoxamine case series reported occasional tremor improvement and stronger impressions of movement and endurance benefit. The 1961 blinded trial found clearer change in rapid hand movement and inconclusive differences in tremor and rigidity. The reports used different outcome measures. [1959 clinical series](../../1955-1959/Drugs-and-Remedies.md#1959--chlorphenoxamine-in-a-clinical-series) · [1961 controlled trial](../Drugs-and-Remedies.md#1961--controlled-chlorphenoxamine-trial)
