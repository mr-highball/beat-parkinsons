# Motor toxicology of a Japanese pepper extract

## Sanshotoxin and motor tone in mice

The recovered full body identifies the 1960 mouse material as a crude fraction carried forward from the [earlier bark series](../1955-1959/Food-Experiment-Japanese-Pepper-Bark-Preparations.md). Page 333 documents injected or local brain exposure to the emulsified fraction. Page 334 Table 1 gives no akinesia in the single intact mouse at the dose described as producing akinesia in the adjoining prose and author abstract. This internal difference limits interpretation of that dose. Results on reduction in tone, movement inhibition and higher-exposure convulsions do not supply a clinical rigidity comparison. [Original full scan](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jvms1939/22/6/22_6_333/_pdf/-char/ja)

Jiro Gotoh, Japanese Journal of Veterinary Science 22(6), December 25, 1960. DOI 10.1292/jvms1939.22.333. The English author abstract reports inhibited spontaneous movement and akinesia in intact mice after administration of a crude sanshotoxin fraction. A motor recording showed transient increased activity followed by inhibition, with decreased limb contraction and tone in that comparison. Severe exposure produced abnormal posture, tremor and convulsions. The report includes intraperitoneal and local brain administration. Reduced recorded tone and convulsive stiffness cannot be equated with Parkinson rigidity. The material was a crude fraction from Japanese Zanthoxylum piperitum, and the abstract does not establish dose correspondence to ground culinary Sichuan pepper. The closely related 1955 and earlier-series reports remain follow-up targets. [Original source](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jvms1939/22/6/22_6_333/_article/-char/en)
