# Drugs and remedies

### 1961 — Controlled chlorphenoxamine trial

Uldall, Walton and Newell’s blinded comparison began with 21 participants with parkinsonism, of whom 17 completed treatment. Seven completers reported greater improvement with chlorphenoxamine, six favored placebo and four detected no difference. The objective assessment found no clear tremor or rigidity improvement, while rapid hand opening and closing improved. The paper tested chlorphenoxamine without added caffeine and reports no clinical fatigue scale. [Primary report, June 10, 1961, pp. 1649–1652](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1954253/) · [Summary and outcomes, p. 1652](https://www.bmj.com/content/bmj/1/5240/1652.full.pdf) · [Later caffeine combination](../1965-1969/Food-and-Nutrition.md#1967--caffeine-in-a-chlorphenoxamine-comparison)

### 1961 — Intravenous levodopa and a short-term improvement in akinesia

Birkmayer and Hornykiewicz reported rapid symptomatic relief of akinesia after intravenous L-DOPA in parkinsonian patients. The original short communication and Hornykiewicz’s first-person account document an acute proof of concept. The short time frame did not establish durable outcomes. [Original study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13869404/) · [Investigator account](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5345651/)

### 1964 — An early DOPA series judged benefit insufficient

McGeer and Zeldowicz treated ten patients with oral or intravenous DOPA and judged the observed improvement insufficient to regard the tested regimen as useful. This records a discordant early result. Regimens, drug composition, routes and duration need to be compared before treating early DOPA reports as direct replications. [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1922247/)
