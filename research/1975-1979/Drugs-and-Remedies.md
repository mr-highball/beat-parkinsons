# Drugs and remedies

### 1975 — Early evaluation of deprenyl added to levodopa

Birkmayer and colleagues reported that the monoamine oxidase B inhibitor deprenyl (later called selegiline) potentiated levodopa’s effect on akinesia. This early adjunct report motivated trials targeting wearing-off. It evaluated symptomatic response and did not establish slowed degeneration. [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1172524/)

### 1977 — Bromocriptine compared directly with levodopa

A blinded comparative study analyzed 18 people with Parkinson’s disease. Group-level effects did not differ significantly, individual responses varied, and four participants could not tolerate bromocriptine during the lead-in. The small analyzed sample and lead-in withdrawals limit claims of comparable efficacy and tolerability. [Source](https://jnnp.bmj.com/content/jnnp/40/5/479.full.pdf)

### 1979 — Adjunct responses varied by motor fluctuation

Controlled comparisons found some benefit from bromocriptine in early-morning dystonia and from L-deprenyl in end-of-dose deterioration. On–off episodes responded less consistently, and neither drug helped freezing or levodopa-induced involuntary movements in the reported groups. Subgroups were small and the results apply to the defined complications. [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/121706/)
