# Chives, related Allium species and dystonia

## 1977 — Meal observations in two levodopa dystonia patterns

Muenter and colleagues evaluated 87 people with Parkinson’s disease during levodopa treatment. Among 39 patients with a short-duration response, 34 showed a single dystonia phase near peak levodopa concentrations. The other five had two episodes at lower concentrations, around the onset and loss of benefit. The report uses dystonia broadly for levodopa-induced involuntary movements, including some described as choreiform. In one Figure 2 and 3 comparison, the same person received the same levodopa dose with water alone or with a protein-rich meal. The plasma peak was 5.9 µg/mL within 18 minutes with water and 2.9 µg/mL after more than two hours with the meal. The prolonged low concentration coincided with a longer dystonia episode. The authors also report aggravation after coffee. One patient reported about one-fifth lower intensity of both dystonia phases after high-carbohydrate meals and occasional abrupt cessation after sugar. These dietary observations do not identify chives or estimate a single ingredient’s effect. The publisher preview and prose were available, while the original Figure 3 image could not be reached.
