# Nutmeg and paprika evidence

## Nutmeg and secretory diarrhea in one cancer case

Barrowman and colleagues described a reduction in severe secretory diarrhea after nutmeg in one patient with medullary thyroid carcinoma. The authors examined water and electrolyte absorption and considered several explanations for the response. Baseline bowel function and the disease mechanism differ from Parkinson constipation. [Primary case report, pp. 11–12](https://www.bmj.com/content/bmj/3/5974/11.full.pdf)

## Pending original letter on ground nutmeg and constipation

The Dietz and Stuart letter is indexed February 26, 1976, at page 503. The original has no PubMed abstract and no full body was verified. A secondary Martindale summary attributes constipation in two people after unusually large amounts of freshly ground nutmeg to this letter. An April 1976 NEJM response corroborates self-administration at high intake but does not independently verify the bowel outcome. Dose frequency differs between the secondary account and response. The constipation account stays pending original verification. ## Bowel frequency after the 1975 treatment switch

After surgery, the 1975 case report describes replacing codeine and diphenoxylate/atropine with repeated large amounts of ground nutmeg. Stool frequency fell from 6.7 to 2.6 per day over two to three days and consistency became more solid. The authors report stool mass of 315 g. Other bowel treatments were added after a month, so the lower later frequency cannot identify a separate nutmeg effect. No withdrawal or rechallenge was described. The follow-up did not test usual culinary quantities or Parkinson constipation. [Local factual extract](Source-Text/1975-Barrowman-Ground-Nutmeg-Bowel-Factual-Extract.txt)
