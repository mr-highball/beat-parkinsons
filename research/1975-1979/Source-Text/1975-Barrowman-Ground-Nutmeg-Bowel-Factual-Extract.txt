Original report pp. 11–12 https://www.bmj.com/content/bmj/3/5974/11.full.pdf

After surgery, the 1975 case report describes replacing codeine and diphenoxylate/atropine with repeated large amounts of ground nutmeg. Stool frequency fell from 6.7 to 2.6 per day over two to three days and consistency became more solid. The authors report stool mass of 315 g. Other bowel treatments were added after a month, so the lower later frequency cannot identify a separate nutmeg effect. No withdrawal or rechallenge was described. The follow-up did not test usual culinary quantities or Parkinson constipation.
