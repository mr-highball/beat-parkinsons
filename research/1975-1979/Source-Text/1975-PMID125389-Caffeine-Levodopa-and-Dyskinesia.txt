Title: Caffeine and the antiparkinsonian response to levodopa or piribedil
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/125389/
PMID: 125389
DOI: 10.1212/wnl.25.8.722
Citation: Shoulson I, Chase TN. Neurology. 1975 Aug. 25(8):722–724.
Coverage: Author abstract from PubMed. Local factual summary.
Reviewed: 2026-11-22 UTC

Six patients received oral caffeine in a study of response to levodopa or piribedil. The authors did not observe a change in the antiparkinsonian response despite central stimulant effects. Among participants with levodopa-related dyskinesia, longer involuntary movement duration was reported during concurrent caffeine use. The abstract does not give the subgroup denominator or a severity scale, and it does not separately report the resting-tremor outcome.

The exposure was isolated caffeine in a medication context. The abstract does not specify an assigned brewed-coffee or tea preparation. This was reported duration of dyskinesia, in a different context from measured resting tremor. The older report adds primary historical context for adverse motor observations alongside the separate 2017 Café-PD trial.
