Title: The effect on intestinal transit and the feces of raw and cooked bran in different doses.
PMID: 998556
DOI: 10.1093/ajcn/29.12.1474
First published: 1976
Authors: Wyman JB, Heaton KW, Manning AP, Wicks AC
Journal: The American journal of clinical nutrition
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/998556/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 7 review).

Primary Abstract:
Ten healthy male subjects on a low fiber diet were given two doses of raw wheat bran (12 and 20 g/day) and two doses of cooked bran (13.2 and 22 g/day). Both doses of raw bran increased fecal dry weight but only the higher dose decreased transit time and increased stool volume. Individual stool size was increased only by raw bran 12g/day. Neither bran influenced fecal wet weight or stool frequency. This study suggests that the cereal manufacturing process alters wheat bran so that cooked bran has less effect on the intestine than does a comparable amount of raw bran.

Verified Round 7 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal (PREPARATION BOUNDARY): Controlled feeding study in 10 healthy men comparing raw wheat bran (12 & 20 g/d) vs cooked wheat bran (13.2 & 22 g/d). Only high-dose raw bran shortened transit time and increased stool volume; neither altered fecal wet weight or stool frequency, demonstrating that thermal cooking diminishes wheat bran transit/bulking effects relative to raw bran.
