Title: Identification of salsolinol as a major dopamine metabolite in the banana
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1245664/
PMID: 1245664
DOI: 10.1021/jf60203a027
Publication date: 1976-01/02, first issue.
Complete original journal scan inspected: https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Magazine/J/Journal%20of%20Agricultural%20and%20Food%20Chemistry/1976_24/No01%28001-202%29/189.pdf
Text coverage: Paraphrased original methods, Table I and study interpretation on pages 189–191.

The chromatography and identity checks compare pulp and peel sampled at different stages of banana ripeness. The table gives less than 0.1 micrograms per gram of wet tissue for salsolinol in both climacteric samples. In the later-stage samples, it gives 40 ± 1.5 in pulp and 260 ± 14 in peel in the same units. The reported variability is repeat-assay precision and does not represent variation across cultivars or people.

The results concern sampled banana tissue and its state of ripeness. They do not quantify this compound in dried figs or measure neuropsychiatric symptoms after eating bananas. The clinical literature and the botanical measurements are separate source families.
