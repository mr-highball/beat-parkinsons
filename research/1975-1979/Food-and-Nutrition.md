# Food and nutrition

### 1977 — Mealtime dizziness and blood pressure in a parkinsonism case

Seyer-Hansen described a man with severe parkinsonism and recurrent dizziness and visual changes during meals. Ward monitoring recorded systolic blood pressure as low as 65 mm Hg during meals. In a separate oral glucose test, systolic pressure fell from 200 to 105 mm Hg within 10 minutes without reported discomfort. The primary report distinguishes the symptomatic meal episodes from the glucose challenge and also describes basilar insufficiency. It gives no comparison by food type or standing symptoms. [Primary case report, November 12, 1977](https://www.bmj.com/content/bmj/2/6097/1262.1.full.pdf)

### 1975 — Higher protein intake blunted levodopa response in some patients

Mena and Cotzias studied dietary protein in a small group on levodopa. In the eight-patient comparison described in the abstract, higher protein intake was associated with worse afternoon motor scores, and the lowest-protein regimen reduced the difference between better and worse parts of the day. This early medication-interaction study measured short-term symptoms in treated people and did not measure disease risk or progression. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM197501232920404)

### 1977 — Fruit and roughage in reports of constipation during a drug trial

A blinded bromocriptine-versus-levodopa trial incidentally reported that constipation in some participants improved after more fruit and roughage. This was an uncontrolled dietary change within a drug trial, with constipation occurring during bromocriptine use. It supplies a dietary lead for a daily symptom, with limited evidence for attribution. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1136/jnnp.40.5.479)
