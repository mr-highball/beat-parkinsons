# Food form and nutritional status in early JSPEN reports

## 2005 Aoyama dietary form and body weight

Aoyama and colleagues reported 76 patients with Parkinson’s, 50 at stages III–IV and 26 at stage V. A separate printed sex breakdown sums to 66, a discrepancy the abstract does not explain. Their five-level diet scale ranged from regular oral meals to parenteral nutrition and correlated with albumin (r = .385) and BMI (r = .333), both p < .05. It did not correlate with measured nutrient intake. In the 26 patients with data before and after a change of feeding level, the 20 whose level fell lost weight over the study interval (p < .05). The six whose level rose showed no clear weight change. Dysphagia and disease severity can influence both feeding level and weight. The abstract does not identify ingredients. [Original 2005 abstract 230, page 13](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/20/Supplement/20_Supplement_s248/_pdf/-char/ja#page=13)

## 2009 Takigawa nutritional status and videofluoroscopy

Takigawa and colleagues compared 163 patients with Parkinson’s and 186 with stroke. Mean BMI was 21.6 versus 23.1 (p < .001). In the imaging subsets of 25 Parkinson and 31 stroke patients, the Parkinson group had longer swallow-reflex latency and more pharyngeal residue. The abstract gives no named dietary exposure or effect of a food on the difference. [Original 2009 O-219, page 20](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/24/1/24_1_278/_pdf#page=20)

## 2009 Parkinson inpatient dysphagia correlates

In a chart review of 83 Parkinson inpatients at Kameda, dysphagia severity was associated with age, Hoehn and Yahr stage, and dysarthria. The abstract reports no association with disease duration or medication types. It tests no foods. An appetite menu described on the same page belongs to a separate oncology report. [Original 2009 O-128, printed p275](https://web.archive.org/web/20250119214824id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/24/1/24_1_244/_pdf#page=32)

## 2009 frozen juice tube as a thermal stimulus

Seven patients with dysphagia, including one with Parkinson’s, were assessed with two thermal stimuli, an unopened frozen juice tube and an ice swab. Coughs per minute were 0.38 and 0.61 respectively, and swallows per minute were 1.7 and 1.2. The abstract reports neither difference as statistically significant. No juice was consumed, and no individual Parkinson result was reported. [Original 2009 P-705, PDF p76](https://web.archive.org/web/20231229204458id_/https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/24/1/24_1_413/_pdf/-char/ja#page=76)

Iizuka and colleagues had reported 17 patients with dysphagia in 2007. Fifteen accepted a frozen juice tube and nine elicited a swallowing reflex. In a 79-year-old man with parkinsonian syndrome, dementia and recurrent aspiration pneumonia, a water test showed aspiration. The abstract reports no aspiration during training with a small opening in the frozen juice tube and later progression to starter and thickened foods. No juice type was given. A 2008 report by the same team describes 48 patients and a protocol that punctured the tube after eliciting a reflex. It gives no separate Parkinson outcome. These two bodies were checked through publisher-indexed text. [2007 P-494, PDF p8](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/22/Supplement/22_Supplement_s523/_pdf#page=8) · [2008 P-374, PDF p9](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjspen/23/Supplement/23_Supplement_s327/_pdf#page=9)

## Pectin and semisolid tube feeding in 2007 and 2008

One 2007 case reports refractory diarrhea in a 75-year-old with parkinsonian syndrome following prostate cancer surgery. Diarrhea and reflux subsided during gastrostomy refeeding with adjustments to volume, formula and viscosity, including a pectin-containing product. A separate 2008 report describes diarrhea during liquid gastrostomy feeding above roughly 700 kcal/day in a 65-year-old with Parkinson’s. The team reached roughly 900 kcal/day after switching to an unnamed semisolid formula and adjusting delivery when loose stools recurred. In a second 2008 case, an older man with Parkinson’s and traumatic hemorrhage reached 1,200 kcal/day through gastrostomy after changing from parenteral nutrition and adding glutamine, fiber and oligosaccharides to staged feeding. Mild diarrhea and fever were reported during the transition. These cases describe changes to clinical tube feeding with no independent ingredient comparisons.
