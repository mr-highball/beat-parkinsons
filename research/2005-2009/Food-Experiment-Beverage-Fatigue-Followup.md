# Beverage and fatigue follow-up, 2005–2009

## Caffeine and auditory gating in a separate healthy sample

Ghisolfi’s 2006 UFRGS thesis combines distinct P50 auditory sensory-gating studies. Its summary table lists 36 people with Parkinson disease with higher P50 test-to-conditioning ratios than healthy controls. The Parkinson manuscript was still in submission in the thesis. The study indexed under caffeine is a separate experiment in 24 healthy volunteers and was first published online on November 8, 2005. It tested oral caffeine against placebo. P50 suppression was reduced at 200 and 400 mg and the 100 mg comparison was inconclusive. This gives no caffeine assignment in the Parkinson cohort and no fatigue or brewed coffee result. ## Habitual caffeine questionnaires in early Parkinson trials

Simon and colleagues combined intake from coffee, tea and other drinks in observational analyses of the randomized NET-PD FS1 and FS-TOO cohorts. Baseline caffeine data were available for 189 and 213 participants, respectively. High versus low caffeine quartiles gave inconclusive total UPDRS change estimates with opposite signs across the two cohorts. The authors did not publish an individual fatigue result or separate brewed-coffee comparison. The original forms or linked cohort measurements would be needed for further work. [Original manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3056438/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2008-Simon-NET-PD-Caffeine-and-Progression.txt)

## Added caffeine in decaf and finger motor endurance

Van Duinen and colleagues’ February 19, 2005 first online report describes 23 healthy young crossover completers. The active drink was decaffeinated coffee with caffeine added. Decaf alone was the comparator. Reaction time improved, with inconclusive comparisons for sustained finger endurance (F[1,21]=1.20) and absolute force (F[1,21]=.15). This motor task did not measure patient-reported fatigue severity.
