# Seafood and Parkinson’s symptom evidence

## Farmed trout intervention registration

NCT00445614 first posted on March 9, 2007. It compared meals containing marine-fed or vegetable-fed farmed rainbow trout against poultry in healthy men. The protocol names the plasma proteome, vascular measures, and cardiovascular biomarkers. The registry has no posted results. The 2010 paper reports the feeding trial. ## Chitosan bowel outcomes and adverse events

Tapola and colleagues compared two oral chitosan doses against glucomannan and placebo in adults with mildly elevated cholesterol. Defecation frequency was similar across groups. An overall association between treatment arm and reported constipation during the first four weeks (p = .021) did not yield a clear pairwise difference. Constipation and loose stools occurred across arms. The extracted shell-derived product and population differ from boiled shrimp and Parkinson’s constipation. [2008 primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18460478/) · [Primary article copy](https://www.healthrcb.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Tapola-N-2008.pdf)

## Fish oil and daytime sleepiness in Parkinson’s

Santos, Mateus, Ferraz and Louzada reported a double-blind fish-oil capsule comparison in the first 2009 issue of Sleep Science. Of 44 selected participants, 37 completed eight weeks, with 19 receiving fish oil and 18 mineral oil. Both groups improved on the Epworth Sleepiness Scale, and the fish-oil comparison was inconclusive (F = 2.18, p = .15). The Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index comparison was also inconclusive. Four of the seven withdrawals cited gastrointestinal alterations, without arm attribution in the report. The paper does not identify the fish species or address grilled trout. [Primary article copy, pp. 36–39](https://www.academia.edu/129541916/Double_blind_study_on_the_effects_of_fish_oil_upon_daytime_sleepiness_and_sleep_quality_in_parkinson_patients)
