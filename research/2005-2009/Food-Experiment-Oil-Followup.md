# Edible oil evidence follow-up, 2005–2009

## Sesamin and olive oil vehicle in a rotenone rat study

Fujikawa and colleagues reported in January 2005 that oral isolated sesamin and a separate *Acanthopanax senticosus* bark extract reduced pole-test delays and catalepsy in rotenone-treated rats. Sesamin was dissolved in a water and olive oil mixture also given to its control groups. The Methods describe motor and tissue measures. The paper refers to depressive behavior in its framing but does not describe a separate depression test. The design gives no isolated dietary olive oil or sesame oil estimate. [Primary report, Methods and Figures 1–3](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/bpb/28/1/28_1_169/_pdf)

## Oil mixtures in a Parkinson’s patent application

The U.S. patent application US20070004639A1 was published January 4, 2007. It proposes a fatty-acid blend that could include safflower or sunflower oil with flaxseed oil, in a regimen that also included oral supplements, a restrictive diet, and intravenous agents. Example 1 presents individual descriptions of symptom changes. Example 4 describes 20 participants but gives a follow-up narrative for one participant, whose description appears to overlap the first vignette. The filing supplies neither a controlled comparison nor a group outcome analysis. It is a historical record of a proposed combined treatment and cannot establish a direction for any of the individual culinary oils. [Original published application, Examples 1 and 4](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20070004639A1/en)
