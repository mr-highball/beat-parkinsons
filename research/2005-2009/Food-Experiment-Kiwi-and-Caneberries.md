# Kiwifruit, blackberry and raspberry studies

## Blackberry feeding and motor tasks in aged rats

Shukitt-Hale, Cheng and Joseph fed aged Fischer 344 rats a diet with 2% blackberry for eight weeks. The abstract reports better performance on the accelerating rotarod, wire suspension and small plank walk and better short-term spatial performance in the Morris water maze. These are balance, strength, coordination and memory measures in an aging model. The original diet preparation, comparator estimates and unreported tests require the full text. The June 2009 issue and USDA publication record precede the publisher platform’s 2013 posting. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19356316/) · [USDA author record](https://www.ars.usda.gov/research/publications/publication/?seqNo115=232598) · [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1179/147683009X423292)

## Gold kiwi ingestion and acute hypotension

Henríquez Santana and colleagues describe a 35-year-old woman with itching, hives, dyspnea and uvular edema within minutes of eating gold kiwifruit. Hypotension was measured, but the paper supplies no pressure value or postural comparison. Skin tests supported sensitization and no oral challenge was reported. The distinct cultivar and acute allergic episode provide context for kiwi safety rather than a chronic symptom effect. ## Raspberry vinegar and unreported blood pressure outcomes

Gerber, Hutchins and Johnston’s March 2006 abstract compares red raspberry vinegar and cranberry juice before meals over eight weeks. Four of eight assigned vinegar and five of eight assigned cranberry juice completed the intervention. Both groups had blood pressure scheduled at baseline and weeks four and eight. The abstract prints no pressure values or orthostatic assessment. A secondary summary gives different dosing and duration. The full original poster and pressure estimates remain open.
