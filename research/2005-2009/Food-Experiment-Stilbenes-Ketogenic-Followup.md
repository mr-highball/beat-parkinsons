# Ketogenic diet evidence traced through HAL

## 2005 hyperketogenic diet feasibility study

VanItallie and colleagues published this open-label feasibility study on February 22, 2005. Five of seven volunteers with Parkinson’s completed 28 days on a home-prepared hyperketogenic diet. The prescribed energy distribution was 90% fat, 8% protein and 2% carbohydrate, with substitution of unsaturated for saturated fats where practical. The report gives fatty acid proportions but no named oil or ingredient list. [Primary report, Methods and Tables 1–2](https://coconutketones.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/VanItallie_Parkinsons_Ketogenicdiet.pdf) · [PubMed, PMID 15728303](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15728303/)

Total UPDRS scores decreased by an average of 43.4% among the five completers, with individual decreases from 21% to 81%. The authors list resting tremor, freezing, balance, gait, mood and energy among the symptoms that improved. The study used a whole diet with no concurrent control. There is no separately measured coconut oil exposure or comparison. The [2018 review](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Stilbenes-Ketogenic-Followup.md#2018-ketogenic-diet-review-and-its-parkinson-citations) cites the same five completers.
