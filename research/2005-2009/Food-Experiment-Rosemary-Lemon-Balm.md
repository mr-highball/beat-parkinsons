# Rosemary and lemon balm evidence

## Rosemary oil blend massage and bowel symptoms in older adults

Kim and colleagues reported a randomized pretest–posttest comparison in February 2005. The experimental group received abdominal massage with a blend of rosemary, lemon and peppermint essential oils. The comparison group received placebo massage. The authors reported lower Constipation Assessment Scale scores and more weekly bowel movements with the blend. The treatment involves massage and three oils, and the report supplies no estimate for ingested ground rosemary in Parkinson disease. [Original journal report](https://doi.org/10.4040/jkan.2005.35.1.56) · [Korean Citation Index](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART001103521)

## Brief rosemary fragrance exposure and cardiovascular measures

Mori and colleagues reported in 2009 that among 60 healthy volunteers divided between rosemary, lavender and control conditions, heart rate rose in the rosemary group after brief fragrance exposure. The authors report pressure and pulse changes in a comparison based on participants' dislike of the scent. The study offers a short resting autonomic comparison and gives no standing lightheadedness result. [Japanese original and figures](https://doi.org/10.1625/jcam.6.137)

## Gastric perforation and liver abscess after swallowing a rosemary twig

Karamarkovic and colleagues reported in November 2007 that a 59-year-old man developed a liver abscess and sepsis after a rosemary twig perforated the stomach and migrated into the liver. The report documents a mechanical foreign-body injury with a large woody piece. A separate 2021 case describes the same type of injury. These cases provide no estimate for a Parkinson symptom. [Original 2007 report](https://www.wjgnet.com/1007-9327/full/v13/i41/5530.htm) · [Separate 2021 case](../2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Rosemary-Lemon-Balm.md#second-case-of-a-migrating-rosemary-twig)
