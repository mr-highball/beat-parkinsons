Efficacy evidence: Qualitative community field interviews (n=30 respondents) without controlled clinical outcome measurement.

Lans (2006, PMC1624823) conducted ethnobotanical field interviews between 1996 and 2000 with 30 respondents in Trinidad and Tobago examining plants used for urinary problems and diabetes mellitus. Community respondents reported traditional use of Bixa orellana for diabetes, hypertension, and jaundice, whereas Bixa orellana was not listed among the Caribbean plants identified for kidney or urinary problems in that survey. These qualitative regional accounts document cultural scope and contrast with Peruvian prostate-focused folk use without providing clinical efficacy evidence.
