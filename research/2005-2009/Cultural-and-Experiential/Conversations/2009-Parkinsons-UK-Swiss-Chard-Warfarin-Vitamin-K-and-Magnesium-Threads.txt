Title: Parkinson's UK and HealthUnlocked community discussions of Swiss chard regarding warfarin/vitamin K safety, dietary magnesium, and oxalates
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/vitamins/4190
Source kind: Conversation
Publication date: 2009-04-25
Date basis: Opening post timestamp on Parkinson's UK public forum (2009-04-25; follow-up reply 2016-07-11; cross-referencing Parkinson's UK 2017-08-16 magnesium checklist https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/battle-briefing/9818 and HealthUnlocked 2024-01-30 oxalate thread https://healthunlocked.com/cure-parkinsons/posts/150483145/oxalates).

In a Parkinson's UK Community Forum thread opened April 25, 2009 ("Vitamins"), a caregiver asked which vitamins and foods were safe for a newly diagnosed spouse taking warfarin; replies urged clinician/pharmacist consultation and quoted standard warfarin dietary guidance flagging Swiss chard and spinach (alongside broccoli, Brussels sprouts, collards, and cabbage) as high-vitamin-K leafy greens that reduce warfarin effectiveness if intake changes abruptly, echoed by a July 11, 2016 follow-up reply. In a separate Parkinson's UK thread ("Battle briefing", reply dated August 16, 2017, https://forum.parkinsons.org.uk/t/battle-briefing/9818), an older member taking Madopar shared a 12-item peer-compiled wellness checklist recommending eating spinach, chard, almonds, avocado, pumpkin seeds, bananas, and dark chocolate for dietary magnesium alongside probiotics, coffee, stress reduction, and B vitamins, without reporting any chard-specific symptom change. Finally, a January 30, 2024 HealthUnlocked Cure Parkinson's discussion on oxalates (https://healthunlocked.com/cure-parkinsons/posts/150483145/oxalates) cited laboratory tables classifying Swiss chard alongside spinach and rhubarb as high-oxalate leafy vegetables warranting hydration and moderation. All usernames and personal identifiers are omitted. Across both major PD forums, Swiss chard appears strictly in anticoagulant-safety, oxalate-caution, and multi-food wellness contexts rather than firsthand symptom-relief reports.
