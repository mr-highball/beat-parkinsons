Title: Polish, Eastern European, and Ashkenazi Culinary-Ceremonial Traditions of Grated Armoracia rusticana (Chrzan / Chrein) Root

Date basis: Publication year of peer-reviewed Polish ethnobotanical review (Luczaj & Szymanski 2007, PMID:17433114, PMC1859986) synthesizing Ethnographic Atlas of Poland village surveys.

Reported direction: Widespread culinary condiment and ceremonial holiday food (raw grated root); clinical case literature also records rare acute vasovagal 'Seder syncope' after large pungent ceremonial portions; no traditional Parkinson motor indication.
Authenticity: Traceable peer-reviewed open-access ethnobotanical synthesis (J Ethnobiol Ethnomed 2007;3:17; PMID:17433114, PMC1859986) drawing on the 380-village Ethnographic Atlas of Poland, cross-linked with Northern Italian Bologna ethnobotany (PMC10936038) and clinical Seder-syncope case reports (PMID:36179141, PMID:3336137).
Efficacy evidence: Ethnographic and culinary-ceremonial context only; documents preparation traditions and acute pungent vasovagal syncope cautions without clinical efficacy evidence for Parkinson's symptoms.

Ethnobotanical survey data across 380 villages in the Ethnographic Atlas of Poland (Luczaj & Szymanski 2007, PMC1859986) document Armoracia rusticana P. Gaertn., B. Mey. & Scherb. (chrzan pospolity; local names chrzan, krzan) as widely gathered throughout Poland for its raw, grated root. Grated raw horseradish root is traditionally eaten with chopped boiled eggs, pickled cucumbers, soups, and meat dishes, and holds prominent ceremonial importance at Easter across Slavic Central and Eastern Europe, paralleling the Bologna Northern Italian culinary use of 'cren' / 'rafan' (Chiocchio et al. 2024, local archive PMC10936038) and Ashkenazi Jewish Passover Seder consumption of freshly grated horseradish ('chrein' / 'maror') as bitter herb. Notably, medical case reports (Lehmann et al. 2022, PMID:36179141; Spitzer 1988, PMID:3336137) document acute transient vasovagal syncope ('Seder syncope' / 'sushi syncope') triggered by abrupt mucosal/vagal stimulation after eating large servings of freshly grated horseradish. No Eastern European ethnographic record documents grated horseradish treating Parkinson's disease tremor, rigidity, or bradykinesia.
