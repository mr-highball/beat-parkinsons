Title: Saffron in the Bencao Gangmu and in reported Tibetan incense practice
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/1934578X0600100112
Source kind: Traditional practice, historical text and modern attribution
Source ID: XI-QIAN-2006-SAFFRON-HISTORY
Publication date: 2006-01
Date basis: Publication month of Xi and Qian's inspected review. The compendium's reported first edition is 1596.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24
Ingredients: saffron
Symptoms: anxiety severity, depressive symptom severity
Community and context: Ming Chinese materia medica and a separate Tibetan medicinal-incense use attributed by Xi and Qian.
Exposure and reported symptom: The entry for 番紅花 in 草之四 describes customary use for accumulated sorrow and fright with palpitations, as well as a circulation-related use. It mentions use in dishes during the Yuan period. Xi and Qian separately describe Tibetan medicinal incense and a traditional tonic role. They give no measure of neurological change from the incense.
Reported direction: The historical entry describes seeking relief from distress. Its concepts do not identify modern anxiety or depression diagnoses. The historical botanical narrative compares 番紅花 with local 紅藍花. The transcription separates the two entries. The modern identification as saffron comes from the inspected review and pharmacognostic reference, not from a specimen.
Efficacy evidence: Historical indications and attributed practices, with no scored Parkinson observation. The reports do not establish ground saffron, an administered dose, a comparison group, or a Parkinson outcome.
Authenticity: Xi and Qian's original page 65 gives 1596. An indexed rendering of scanned volume 15, folio 33, and a modern transcription contain the 番紅花 passage. The downstream crocetin review gives 1956 in error. The Tibetan incense attribution has no underlying ethnographic method in the inspected passage. Keep the separate 1994 dictionary citation distinct while avoiding treating repeated historical descriptions as clinical replications.
