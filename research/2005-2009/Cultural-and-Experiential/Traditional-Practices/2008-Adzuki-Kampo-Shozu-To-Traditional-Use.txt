Title: East Asian Kampo and Chinese Traditional Use of Adzuki Beans (Shozu-to / Phaseolus angularis)
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2459248/
Source kind: traditional_practice
Publication date: 2008
Date basis: Peer-reviewed biomedical publication date (Maruyama et al. 2008, PMC2459248; corroborated by Yu et al. 2011, PMID:21821108) documenting historical East Asian Kampo and folk medicinal practices
Reviewed: 2026-11-24 UTC
Reported direction: favorable_traditional_context_only
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed biomedical papers citing documented Japanese Kampo and Korean/Chinese ethnomedicinal prescriptions; historical age distinct from 2008/2011 publication dates
Efficacy evidence: Customary traditional context only; does not establish clinical or Parkinson's-specific symptom efficacy

In Maruyama et al. (2008, J Clin Biochem Nutr, PMC2459248), the authors document Shozu-to, a traditional Chinese and Japanese Kampo medicinal decoction composed primarily of boiled azuki beans (Phaseolus angularis / Vigna angularis), historically recognized as having antidotal, diuretic, and laxative properties and traditionally used for constipation, beriberi, nephritis, and insufficient postpartum lactation. Similarly, Yu et al. (2011, J Ethnopharmacol, PMID:21821108) record traditional Korean, Chinese, and Japanese folk-medicine use of P. angularis beans for edema and inflammation of the joints, appendix, kidney, and bladder. Neither record documents Parkinson's disease patients or controlled symptom outcomes; traditional laxative, diuretic, and anti-inflammatory attributions remain customary context rather than clinical efficacy evidence.
