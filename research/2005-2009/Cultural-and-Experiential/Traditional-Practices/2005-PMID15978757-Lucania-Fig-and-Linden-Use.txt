Title: Southern Italian household reports distinguish common fig from linden for shaking

Date basis: Available-online date printed in the original article. The journal issue is dated October 3, 2005.
Reviewed: 2026-11-22 UTC
Inspected: Table 2, printed pages 264 and 266, and Tables 3–4.
Community/context: Household remedy interviews in Ginestra/Zhurë and Castelmezzano in Lucania. The authors distinguish the two local populations and identify the plant materials at the row level.

Exposure and reported symptom: Table 2 lists common fig fruit and sap for respiratory, digestive and topical uses. Table 3 singles out dried fig among frequently named remedies. The separate small-leaved linden (Tilia cordata) flower entry in Table 2 is the one describing traditional use for body shaking in Castelmezzano.

Reported direction: A traditional easing use is attributed to linden for body shaking. The inspected common fig entry has no stated tremor direction.
Evidence and limits: The study establishes that these uses were reported in the surveyed communities. The linden entry supplies no verified diagnosis, distinction between rest and other shaking, measured response, or comparison. Do not attribute the linden account to fig.
Authenticity: Original published interview survey with identifiable entries.
Efficacy evidence: The interview source does not establish efficacy for Parkinson rest tremor.

Access and follow-up: Check distinct primary sources if investigating linden and clinically characterized tremor. A preparation and Parkinson endpoint for raw common fig are still needed.
