Efficacy evidence: Descriptive practice without a participant denominator or observed outcome. It does not identify a fig-only comparison or olive oil grade.

Jeddi describes rural households eating dried figs in the morning in winter, often with olive oil, as a way to withstand cold weather. The same section mentions other medicinal uses for figs generally and supplies no preparation-by-indication mapping for those uses. The drying section describes traditional sun exposure and distinguishes a more controlled process. Its maturity table credits the 2005 guide, so that table provides no independent replication.

Open trail: Seek a primary field account linking that winter preparation to a symptom observation. The report leaves cultivar, drying method for the household servings, portions, duration and visual outcomes open.
