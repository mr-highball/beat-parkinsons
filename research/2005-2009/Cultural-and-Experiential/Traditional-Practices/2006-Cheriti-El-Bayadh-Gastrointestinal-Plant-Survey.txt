Authors: Cheriti A, Belboukhari N, Sekkoum K, Hacini S

The investigators interviewed people in El-Bayadh, southwestern Algeria, about traditional remedies for gastrointestinal disorders. They list 62 species across 29 families and report confirming plant identities using the district agriculture office herbarium and published floras. Their Table 1 catalogs common names and parts used. Entries include cumin fruit, fennel leaves, saffron flowers, cassia bark, and clove flowers as printed. The survey describes gastrointestinal uses collectively, with a general account of preparations and no plant-specific efficacy comparisons or Parkinson cohort.

Neither Phoenix dactylifera nor a palm-family row appears in the printed table on pp. 8–9. This survey concerns El-Bayadh. The first author's laboratory separately lists a 2000 CRSTRA report about the Béchar region, with pp. 1–12 cited in an institutional thesis. Direct access to that report and verification of the abdominal-pain attribution remain open.
