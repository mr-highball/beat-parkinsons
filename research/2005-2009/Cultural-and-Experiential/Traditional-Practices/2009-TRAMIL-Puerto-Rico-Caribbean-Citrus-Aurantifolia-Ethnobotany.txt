Title: TRAMIL Caribbean ethnopharmacological survey documenting traditional uses of Citrus aurantifolia (lime) in Puerto Rico
Collection: Cultural and experiential
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4136639/
Source kind: Traditional-Practice
Publication date: 2009-12-01
Date basis: Issue publication date (December 2009) of Alvarado-Guzman et al., Puerto Rico Health Sciences Journal 28(4):329-339 (PMID:19999241, PMC4136639).
Reviewed: 2026-11-26 UTC
Reported direction: Significant community-reported traditional medicinal use of Citrus aurantifolia across digestive (gastritis/reflux), nervous (nervousness/depression), migraine, and respiratory complaints in southeastern Puerto Rico; no Parkinson-specific claim.
Authenticity: Peer-reviewed TRAMIL ethnopharmacological field survey (PMC4136639) with statistical frequency validation; corroborated by Odonne et al. 2021 (PMID:34217796) documenting lime-juice phytotherapy in French Guiana.
Efficacy evidence: Ethnobotanical survey documenting customary Caribbean practices rather than controlled clinical efficacy; distinguishes true Citrus aurantifolia from European Tilia (linden/lime-blossom) herbal teas.

In the peer-reviewed TRAMIL ethnopharmacological field survey of southeastern Puerto Rico (Alvarado-Guzman et al. 2009, PMC4136639), Citrus aurantifolia (Christm.) Swingle (key lime) was identified alongside Citrus aurantium as one of only four botanical species reaching statistically significant community-use thresholds across 118 recorded medicinal plants used for gastritis, gastroesophageal reflux, nervousness, depression, migraine, and upper-respiratory complaints. Separately, Caribbean and Guianese culinary-medicinal traditions employ fresh lime juice in marinades, digestive tonics, and fever/malaria adjuncts (Odonne et al. 2021, PMID:34217796). Crucially, tropical citrus lime (Citrus aurantifolia / Citrus latifolia) is botanically distinct from European 'lime flower' (Tilia spp., linden blossom). Customary use provides cultural context without establishing efficacy for Parkinson's symptoms.
