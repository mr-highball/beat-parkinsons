Aspalathus linearis (Burm.f.) R. Dahlgren (Fabaceae), endemic to the fynbos biome of South Africa's Cederberg mountains (Western Cape), was traditionally harvested wild by indigenous Khoisan and Khoekhoe communities long before 20th-century commercialization (Joubert et al., 2008, PMID:18621121; Street & Prinsloo, 2013, PMC3866779). Traditional processing involved gathering the fine needle-like stems and leaves, bruising/chopping them with axes, heaping the damp material to undergo enzymatic oxidation ("fermentation" to red rooibos, versus unfermented green rooibos), and sun-drying on flat rocks prior to brewing as a hot-water herbal infusion. Because rooibos is naturally 100% caffeine-free and low in astringent tannins compared with Camellia sinensis (green/black tea), South African household and Cederberg local traditions widely used brewed rooibos as a gentle daily drink and folk remedy for infantile colic, abdominal spasms and stomach cramps, allergic/dermatological complaints, nervous tension/sleep winding-down, and elevated blood pressure (Joubert et al., 2008; Pretorius & Smith, 2024, PMID:37923252; Pretorius et al., 2025, PMID:39260709). Modern pharmacology links its antispasmodic gut folklore to K_ATP-channel-activating flavonoids (Gilani et al., 2006, PMID:17076689), while clinical human efficacy for gastrointestinal, sleep, anxiety, or Parkinson disease symptoms remains unestablished.
