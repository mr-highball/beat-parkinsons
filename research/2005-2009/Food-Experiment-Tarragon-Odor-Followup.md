# Tarragon odor follow-up

## Recognition after rotating or stable odors including tarragon

Veyrac and colleagues randomized mice to empty tea balls, a continuously repeated mixture of 20 odors, single odors rotated daily, or the daily rotation with labetalol. The 47-day enrichment set included tarragon. The individual-odor sequence restarted after 20 days. Sixty-minute recognition used limonene and carvone as test stimuli. In the rotating group (n = 20), mice explored the novel odor longer (one-sided paired p = .03) and visited it more often (p = .004). Preference indices were above 50% for time and visits (p = .004 and .002). The standard, stable mixture, and rotation-plus-labetalol groups showed no clear recognition preference under the authors’ tests. The behavioral comparisons supporting that pattern used within-group tests. The authors reported 40% higher BrdU-labeled cell density in olfactory bulb granule layers for rotating enrichment, with comparable immediate subventricular-zone proliferation. The protocol establishes a response to differences in odor schedule and mixture. A tarragon-only arm, drying specification, dietary intake and Parkinson population were absent. ## Tarragon in a mouse neurogenesis odor set

Alonso and colleagues assigned mice to an empty odor pot or three different fragrances per day for three weeks. Tarragon appears in Table 2 alongside many other scents. The enriched group had higher BrdU-labeled candidate stem cell densities in the two tested rostral migratory stream regions and subventricular zone (treatment F(1,30) = 55.37, p < .0001, six animals per group). The paper measured proliferation and electrophysiology. A tarragon-specific olfaction result remains unavailable. Their longer labeling procedure targeted a slower-dividing population than the immediate proliferation measure in the Veyrac work.
