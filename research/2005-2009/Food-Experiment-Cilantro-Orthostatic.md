# Cilantro, blood pressure and orthostatic symptoms

## 2007 — Aqueous coriander seed extract infused into rats

Aissaoui and colleagues gave aqueous seed extract intravenously to anesthetized Wistar rats over 120 minutes. Dose groups of 40 and 100 mg/kg had diuretic indices of 2.43 and 3.96 against saline. The intravenous furosemide comparison was 7.38. Measured outcomes were urine flow, urinary electrolytes and estimated filtration rate. The authors discuss arterial pressure as a possible mechanism. The experiment does not report a measured posture-related blood pressure response. The article appeared online September 16, 2007 and in its January 2008 issue. ## 2008 — Intravenous dried coriander fruit extract lowered arterial pressure in anesthetized rats

Jabeen and colleagues ground dried coriander fruit and produced an aqueous-methanol extract. Intravenous injections in normotensive, anesthetized rats lowered systolic and diastolic arterial pressure within the tested 1–30 mg/kg range (reported maximum 40.84 ± 6.34%, n = 5, Figure 5). The paper also reports more urine after a separate extract treatment. This acute arterial pressure result concerns fruit extract and intravenous dosing. The study did not measure oral dried-leaf intake, standing blood pressure or clinical lightheadedness. The article was available online December 25, 2008.
