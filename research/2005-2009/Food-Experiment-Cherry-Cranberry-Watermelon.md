# Cranberry juice and cognitive testing, 2005–2009

## Cranberry beverage and attention-related tests in older adults

Crews and colleagues’ April 2005 pilot assigned 50 cognitively intact adults aged 60 and over to a 27% cranberry juice beverage or placebo for six weeks. Trail Making A and B, Stroop, Digit Symbol Coding and several memory tests showed no clear group-by-time differences. The abstract reports a nonsignificant trend in perceived memory improvement. The article-level numerical test tables were unavailable in the public copies checked. The study tested a beverage outside Parkinson disease. [Primary journal record, PMID 15865497](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15865497/)

## Cranberry extract and motor tests in aged rats

Shukitt-Hale and colleagues’ 2005 experiment compared fruit extracts in the diets of aged rats. The cranberry arm showed better motor performance and enhanced stimulated striatal dopamine release. An inclined-screen test measures strength, balance and the ability to hold position. The public abstract gives no bradykinesia or cooked cranberry result. [Primary article, PMID 23598603](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23598603/)
