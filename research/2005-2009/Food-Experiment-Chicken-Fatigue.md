# Chicken soup and measured fatigue

## Reconstituted chicken soup and ordinary fatigue ratings

Midoh and Noguchi's study was first available on J-STAGE on February 1, 2009. Thirty healthy adults took each of two soup preparations in a randomized crossover. The active soup used freeze-dried extract from heated whole chickens reconstituted in hot water. Table 3 gives fatigue T-score changes of −0.5 ± 0.9 with chicken soup and +0.1 ± 0.8 with placebo, as means and standard errors (p = .61). The tension–anxiety subscale showed a separate favorable comparison. This was oral soup without an imposed fatigue task. No meat was assigned and Parkinson fatigue was outside the study. ## Chicken breast extract and exercise-induced fatigue

Tanaka and colleagues reported a randomized, double-blind placebo crossover of the CBEX-Dr chicken-breast extract drink in healthy volunteers in the March 20, 2008 issue. The abstract and Methods describe 20 randomized. Figure 2 shows 20 assessed for eligibility, 18 randomized and 17 analyzed. Participants took 60 mL daily of a chicken-breast extract drink containing 400 mg imidazole dipeptides, or placebo, for 29 days. After four weeks, they completed four hours of cycling followed by recovery. On a 100 mm fatigue visual analogue scale, the change from before exercise after four hours was +18.1 ± 15.8 mm with extract versus +26.3 ± 18.1 with placebo (p < .05). After four hours of recovery the change from the pre-exercise score was +9.8 ± 17.3 versus +20.1 ± 20.7 mm (p < .01), means ± standard deviations. The two-hour and next-day changes had no significance mark in Table 4. The recovery change measured from the end of the exercise did not show a significant difference. The small cohort and exercise-induced fatigue outcome limit transfer to poached chicken and Parkinson fatigue.
