# Bell pepper preparation and symptom outcomes

## Nonpungent pepper extract and acute sleepy and alert ratings

Galgani, Ryan and Ravussin first published this randomized, double-blind crossover on August 12, 2009. Thirteen healthy men received four single doses of capsinoids (1, 3, 6 or 12 mg) or placebo on five test days. The capsules used an extract of the nonpungent cultivar CH-19 Sweet. The extract contained capsiate, dihydrocapsiate and nordihydrocapsiate in the reported ratio 70:23:7. This was a purified extract in a capsule. [Primary article and formulation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19671203/) · [Primary full paper, pp. 39–40](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/05745EBB8406CD5D6A51E37A482FBEB6/S0007114509991358a.pdf/effect_of_capsinoids_on_energy_metabolism_in_human_subjects.pdf)

The investigators administered a validated 34-item symptom questionnaire the night before testing, immediately after the 165-minute metabolic session and at 72 hours after the final test. Items included sleepy, alert and tired. Each was rated on four levels from absent through greatest intensity. The paper gives an overall adverse-event comparison (p=.09) and supplies no item means, change estimates or separate p values for sleepy or alert. A healthy-adult short-term rating does not resolve Parkinson daytime sleepiness after grilled bell pepper.
