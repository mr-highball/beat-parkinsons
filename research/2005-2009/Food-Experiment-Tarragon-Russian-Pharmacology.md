# Russian tarragon tincture pharmacology

## Kadatskaya tarragon tincture dose and behavioral assays

In a 2005 primary conference report, D. V. Kadatskaya gave rats tarragon tincture by mouth at a stated 50 mg/kg after dilution in distilled water. The report identifies a week of pretreatment for open-field testing and two weeks for the elevated-plus-maze and forced-swim tests. Compared with controls, mean grooming was 2.7 times as high, and first-day exploration and rearing were 1.7 and 2.3 times as high (p ≤ .05 for the latter two reported comparisons). The author gives forced-swim activity findings for St. John's wort and peony rhizome and reports no activity for the remaining preparations, including tarragon. The report explicitly attributes dose selection to preliminary pentobarbital- and chloral-hydrate-induced sleep experiments, with no tarragon timing values in the paper. Dissertation section 3.1 labels the barbiturate thiopental. The accessible sources cannot establish whether the two names reflect separate experiments or inconsistent reporting. No ordinary sleep or daytime sleepiness endpoint was reported. [2005 primary proceedings, pp. 348–350](https://pmedpharm.ru/upload/iblock/a0e/p75gt2p85p5twouqjhyhaglyrawpvnfu.pdf) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/Kadatskaya-2005-proceedings-extract.txt)

## Aglarova French tarragon extracts and behavioral outcomes

Aglarova’s 2006 author synopsis, pp. 15–16, describes 30 male rats weighing 180–200 g in open-field and burrow-reflex assays of alcoholic extracts from French tarragon. It reports more emotional lability, stabilized orientation, less searching and lower aggression. Zilfikarov’s subsequent Table 30 reports the same behavioral assay types and a similar pattern of orientation, search and emotional lability. Its aggression scores are slightly higher for both 40% and 70% ethanol tinctures (6.0 and 6.2 versus 5.6 in water controls), in the opposite direction from the 2006 synopsis. The two publications also share the same numerical results in separate urine-output, gallbladder and gastric-ulcer experiments. Treat them as a likely shared research lineage. The behavioral description provides no timed sleep or spontaneous sleepiness measure. [2006 original synopsis, pp. 15–19](https://new-disser.ru/_avtoreferats/01003028544.pdf) · [Later Table 30](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328191458_REROURSE-SAVING_TECHNOLOGIES_IN_PHARMACEUTICAL_Modern_methods_of_standardization_and_complex_processing_of_raw_materials_containing_essential_oil) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/Aglarova-2006-synopsis-pharmacology-extract.txt)

The synopsis separately reports that injected 40% and 70% ethanol tinctures reduced acetic-acid-evoked writhing counts by 87% and 93%, respectively, in groups of five. The authors reported control latency of 4.8 minutes and around 55 writhes. Latencies after those tinctures were 291% and 312% of control. This is an acute chemical nociception assay and the preparation was injected. The experiment provides no Parkinson pain or culinary-exposure comparison. Urine output was higher after both tinctures in another experiment. Blood-pressure changes with posture and lightheadedness were unmeasured. [Primary synopsis, pp. 15–18, Figures 2 and 5](https://new-disser.ru/_avtoreferats/01003028544.pdf)

## Russian holdings for the missing primary tables

The Russian State Library holds Kadatskaya’s full 176-page dissertation and 24-page author synopsis. The synopsis record identifies Samara as its imprint and Bashkir State Medical University in the statement of responsibility. The available public reproduction of the Contents and conclusions preserves candidate assay wording without the numerical tables. The full thesis record limits access to authorized reading rooms. The 2004 short papers in Pyatigorsk conference issue 59 (pp. 277–278) and *Аспирантский вестник Поволжья* no. 1 (pp. 38–39) are additional bibliographic leads. [Kadatskaya full thesis](https://search.rsl.ru/ru/record/01004069777) · [Kadatskaya author synopsis record](https://search.rsl.ru/ru/record/01003008512) · [2004 proceedings record](https://search.rsl.ru/ru/record/01002563953)

The same library holds Aglarova’s 176-page thesis and her separately printed author synopsis. Her 2006 coauthored *Фармация* paper is identified in the issue table of contents and by a library reference service, which lists full text through a registered eLibrary account. Its full pages and the dissertation chapter were unavailable on the public routes checked. The retrieved author synopsis concerns tested extracts rather than fresh or dried culinary tarragon.
