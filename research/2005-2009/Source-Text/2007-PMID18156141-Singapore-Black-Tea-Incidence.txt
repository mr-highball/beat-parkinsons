Original source scope
In 63,257 Singapore Chinese Health Study participants followed for incident Parkinson disease, 157 cases were identified. The highest black-tea tertile versus no black tea had adjusted relative risk 0.29 (95% CI 0.13–0.67). No association was reported for green tea.

Evidence boundary or continuing gap
Brewed black tea is exposure context. The endpoint is incident diagnosis, with no separately measured bradykinesia severity among people with established Parkinson disease.

The prospective cohort of 63,257 participants identified 157
incident Parkinson disease cases. Compared with nondrinkers,
the highest black-tea intake category had an adjusted relative
risk of 0.29 (95% CI 0.13–0.67) in the final model in Table 3.
The highest green-tea category had a relative risk of 0.93
(95% CI 0.55–1.57). These are observational disease-incidence
contrasts. The data do not compare symptom severity after
Parkinson disease onset.

Rest tremor appears in the clinical and diagnostic discussion.
The report does not provide a tea-by-rest-tremor severity
analysis among the people with Parkinson disease.
