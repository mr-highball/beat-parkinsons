Title: Dietary epidemiology of essential tremor: meat consumption and meat cooking practices
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2821442/?report=reader
PMCID: PMC2821442
PMID: 18382115
DOI: 10.1159/000122333
Publication date: 2008-04-02
Accessed: 2026-11-17
Text coverage: Curated full original author manuscript review

The 125 essential-tremor cases and 125 controls answered current
meat and cooking questions. The authors excluded Parkinson disease.
Among men, total meat intake averaged 135.3 g/day in cases versus
110.6 in controls (p=.03). Among women, the corresponding values
were 80.6 and 79.3 (p=.76). Predisease intake recalled by the small
short-duration male subset differed inconclusively (p=.27).

Among cases, current total meat intake and the aggregate tested
action-tremor score had Spearman rho −0.15 (p=.10). A separately named
grilled, barbecued or broiled chicken item differs from the frozen
baked-turkey exposure. These are observational comparisons. The
participating research program overlaps earlier essential-tremor
work, with exact participant overlap unresolved.
