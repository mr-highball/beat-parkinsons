The 76 prospective dementia cases had annual clinical assessments and later autopsy. Clinicians used participant and informant history and the Neuropsychiatric Inventory to ascertain whether formed visual hallucinations had occurred. Table 1A reports 35/43 in clinically probable dementia with Lewy bodies, 6/9 in possible dementia with Lewy bodies and 0/24 in probable Alzheimer disease. Visual symptom findings help define the clinical groups.

Among the 52 autopsy cases grouped by likelihood of dementia with Lewy bodies pathology, 76%, 65% and 33% in the high, intermediate and low groups had visual hallucinations. The corresponding proportions with onset within three years of disease onset were 62%, 35% and 0%. The authors reported a significant contrast for early onset and no significant overall difference for visual occurrence across those pathology groups. The denominators were 29, 17 and 6.

These measures describe participant history and symptom onset relative to disease, without within-person visual episode counts or dried-fig exposure.
