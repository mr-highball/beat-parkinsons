Title: Effect of clarithromycin on the pharmacokinetics of cabergoline in healthy controls and in patients with Parkinson's disease
PMID: 16410677
DOI: 10.1254/jphs.fp0050711
Publication date: 2006-01-13
Citation: Nakatsuka A and colleagues. Journal of Pharmacological Sciences 100:59–64.
Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jphs/100/1/100_1_59/_article/-char/en
Primary PDF: https://mhlw-grants.niph.go.jp/system/files/2005/057011/200500270B/200500270B0007.pdf
Coverage: Paper methods, separate patient results, tables and Figure 3. The separate government PDF contains adjacent publications.

In Study 2, mean cabergoline at the sampled time rose from 145.4 ± 52.9 to 252.7 ± 100.6 pg/mL (SD) in seven Parkinson patients, a 1.74-fold increase in Results. Four returned for postwithdrawal samples. Three patients' overall UPDRS improved by 2, 6 and 4 points and four were unchanged. The authors mention resting tremor or rigidity among the changes, without assigning those individual changes or giving an item score. The comparison was open, and the added exposure was an antibiotic.

The approximately 2.7-fold result in the Discussion corresponds to pharmacokinetic measures reported for the ten healthy volunteers. Another Discussion sentence instead assigns 2.7-fold to Parkinson patients, which conflicts with the population-specific means and Figure 3. The abstract and full paper also give differing clarithromycin dose descriptions. The full paper notes primary hydrolysis and proposes a transporter explanation. Its own analysis does not isolate the mechanism. A 2004 conference abstract (DOI 10.1016/j.clpt.2003.11.300) used the same research group and nearly identical healthy-volunteer measurements; it adds no independent evidence family.
