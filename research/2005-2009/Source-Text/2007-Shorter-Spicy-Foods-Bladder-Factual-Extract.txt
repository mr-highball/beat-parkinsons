Shorter and colleagues, primary questionnaire and Table 2

Original source https://www.researchgate.net/publication/6333394_Effect_of_Comestibles_on_Symptoms_of_Interstitial_Cystitis

First online May 11, 2007. The study administered a food sensitivity questionnaire to 104 participants with interstitial cystitis. Table 2 uses an attribution scale from −2 (worse) through +2 (better). Scores for spicy food, chili and hot peppers were −1.64 ± 0.69 (75 respondents), −1.51 ± 0.72 (61) and −1.44 ± 0.85 (52), respectively. These were symptom reports attributed to foods by participants. The table does not isolate urinary urgency frequency or smoked paprika, and the sample was outside Parkinson disease.
