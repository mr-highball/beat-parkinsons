Di Matteo et al. — Intake of tomato-enriched diet protects from 6-hydroxydopamine-induced degeneration of rat nigral dopaminergic neurons
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20411791/
Primary full chapter: https://www.academia.edu/4670222/The_Nigrostriatal_Pathway_Axonal_Collateralization_and_Compartmental_Specificity
Publication: J Neural Transm Suppl. 2009;73:333–341. DOI 10.1007/978-3-211-92660-4_28.
Coverage: Original factual extract of full chapter Methods, Results and Figures 1–2.

The preparation was chopped and homogenized ripe tomatoes that had been stored frozen, followed by freeze-drying. Standard Altromin chow, or chow containing 5% dry-weight Red Setter or High Carotene tomato, was fed for 14 days. Methods give no cooking step. Male Fischer 344 rats were randomly allocated. Treatment began a week before the unilateral lesion. The systemic and striatal measures were taken after a further week.

Figure 2 compares lesioned and intact sides within each animal, n = 9 per group. Dopamine depletion, percent mean ± SEM: standard 86.5 ± 5.0, High Carotene 86.2 ± 5.0, Red Setter 56.0 ± 9.0. Red Setter versus standard p = .006 and versus High Carotene p = .005. DOPAC depletion: standard 85.6 ± 5.0, High Carotene 83.0 ± 6.0, Red Setter 58.9 ± 10.0, with Red Setter comparisons p = .018 and p = .026 respectively. Figure 1 finds higher absolute dopamine and DOPAC in the contralateral intact side with High Carotene. Figure 2 supplies the distinct relative lesion-depletion outcome. Plasma lycopene was 4.7 ± 0.2 ng/mL with Red Setter and under the reported 2.5 ng/mL detectability threshold in the other groups (n = 5 per group). The Results report no gait, stride or executive task.
