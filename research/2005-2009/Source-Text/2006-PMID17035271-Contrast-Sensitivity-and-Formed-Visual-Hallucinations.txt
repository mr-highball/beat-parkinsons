Title: Contrast sensitivity and visual hallucinations in patients referred to a low vision rehabilitation clinic
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1857683/
PMID: 17035271
PMCID: PMC1857683
DOI: 10.1136/bjo.2006.104604
Publication year: 2006
Text coverage: Paraphrased findings checked against complete official PMC HTML.

A low-vision rehabilitation cohort asked 225 first-time referrals about formed visual hallucinations. Seventy-eight (35%) reported them. Worse measured contrast sensitivity retained an association after adjustment for acuity and other covariates. The outcome is presence in a vision-clinic population. The study does not compare fig intake or Parkinson episode rates.

The original paper includes adjustment for age, sex, depression and independence. The denominator is a single clinic's first-time referrals, mostly older people with visual impairment.
