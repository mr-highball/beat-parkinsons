Simon DK et al. Caffeine and progression of Parkinson disease. Clin Neuropharmacol. 2008 Jul–Aug;31(4):189–196.
PMID 18670242. PMCID PMC3056438. DOI 10.1097/WNF.0b013e31815a3f03.
Primary full manuscript: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3056438/
Coverage: factual summary of the original Methods, Tables 1–3 and Discussion.

NET-PD FS1 randomized 200 people with early untreated Parkinson disease to creatine, minocycline or placebo. FS-TOO randomized 213 to coenzyme Q10, GPI-1485 or placebo. Caffeine was observed by questionnaire, separately from the randomized intervention. Baseline caffeine information was available for 189 from FS1 and 213 from FS-TOO. FS1 focused on the previous week, and FS-TOO on typical intake over six months. Both asked about coffee, tea and other caffeinated beverages and derived combined caffeine intake. The article does not give separate brewed-coffee coefficients. The original item forms and formulas are described as available upon request.

Change in total, motor and activities-of-daily-living UPDRS and need for symptomatic treatment were analyzed. The highest versus lowest caffeine quartile total UPDRS changes in Table 2 were 6.0 versus 8.1 in FS1 (p=.279) and 8.5 versus 7.1 in FS-TOO (p=.418), with opposite directions and imprecise comparisons. No individual fatigue outcome or coffee-specific fatigue estimate is reported. The published longitudinal analysis supplies no individual-level linkage needed to estimate it.
