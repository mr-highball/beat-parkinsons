Primary study factual extract
Gonlachanvit S, Mahayosnond A, Kullavanijaya P. Effects of chili on postprandial gastrointestinal symptoms in diarrhoea predominant irritable bowel syndrome. Neurogastroenterol Motil. 2009;21(1):23–32. Published online in 2008. PMID18647268. DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2982.2008.01167.x.
Sources https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18647268/ and https://doi.nrct.go.th/ListDoi/Download/548432?Resolve_DOI=10.14457%2FCU.res.2007.114 (author report, appendix 3).
In 20 adults with diarrhea-predominant IBS, researchers compared a standard fried-noodle-and-pork meal with placebo capsules, the same meal incorporating 2 g commercial red chili powder with placebo capsules, and the same unspiced meal with 2 g chili in capsules. The study used a randomized crossover with one-week washouts and two hours of post-meal symptom ratings. The report manuscript specifies 1.87 mg of capsaicin per 2 g powder. Both chili presentations raised maximum abdominal pain and burning compared with the standard meal (p<.05). The report identifies pain in 5 of 20 after the standard meal, 11 of 20 after the mixed chili meal, and 13 of 20 after chili capsules. The final journal abstract lists 38 healthy controls while the earlier manuscript includes 20, both with 20 IBS-D participants. Cultivar and effects in Parkinson disease remain unverified.
