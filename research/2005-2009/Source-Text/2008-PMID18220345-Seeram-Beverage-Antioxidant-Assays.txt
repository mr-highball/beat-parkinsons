Seeram and colleagues analyzed commercial ready-to-drink beverages with four antioxidant potency tests, a lipid oxidation test and a total phenolic assay. Table 4 gives the composite across the four potency assays. Pomegranate juice ranked highest overall, followed by red wine and Concord grape juice. The individual assay order varied, including the reported ORAC variation. Figure 1 and the accompanying text group blueberry juice ahead of black cherry, açai and cranberry juice for average total phenolics. Orange juice, iced tea products and apple juice appear in the lower phenolic grouping. Figure 1 and Table 4 have different endpoints. The iced tea products include commercial black, green and white tea preparations. The publication lists a beverage-company affiliation.

The primary research measured beverages in vitro. It assigned no human exposure and measured no constipation, depressive symptoms, anxiety, Parkinson rigidity or bradykinesia. Its beverage measurements cannot establish a dose or symptom effect for fresh, raw, frozen or dried whole fruit. Claim contexts include Round 1 0722, 0746, 0860, 0878 and 0881.

Citation resolution: PMC13340997 attributes a ranking to Seeram et al. 2008. Its bibliography identifies the human pomegranate exposure paper PMID 18598186. This analytical beverage study is the matching original for the ranking. The two studies answer different questions.
