Original source scope and evidence boundary
The placebo-controlled crossover recruited 24 habitual consumers and 24 low or nonconsumers. Reported mean usual caffeine intake was 217 versus 20 mg/day. All abstained overnight. The authors report a main treatment effect on mental fatigue, F(2,88)=4.27, p=.017. Planned overall comparisons favored less mental fatigue at 75 and 150 mg (p=.010 and .020). Separate secondary results identify 150 mg for habitual users and 75 mg for low or nonconsumers. The authors did not find a mental-fatigue treatment-by-group interaction. Exposure is isolated caffeine in a matched beverage rather than brewed coffee or green tea. Low habitual users still reported some caffeine. Different selective subgroup results and baseline abstinence do not establish a universal effect independent of withdrawal.
