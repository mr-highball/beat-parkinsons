Title: Laboratory examination of passed material following an olive-oil flush
Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(05)66373-8
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15836886/
PMID: 15836886

Date basis: Lancet issue date
Reviewed: 2026-11-25
Review depth: full_text
Source locator: Sies and Brooker, The Lancet 365(9468), page 1388. Case account, clinical chemistry and accompanying figure.

The patient had stones seen on ultrasound. The authors examined soft material passed following a nonclinical olive oil and citrus regimen. The passed material lacked observable crystals and detectable cholesterol, bilirubin and calcium on the methods reported. Fatty acids accounted for about three-quarters of the sample. The patient later had separate cholesterol stones removed surgically. The authors attributed the passed material to fatty acid aggregates from the regimen.

This single clinical case and its laboratory measurements challenge the use of appearance alone to identify a passed gallstone. It provides an analytical comparison for the historical 1871 observation and no result on Parkinson symptoms.
