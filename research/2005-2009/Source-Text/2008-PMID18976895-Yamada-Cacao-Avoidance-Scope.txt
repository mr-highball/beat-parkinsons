Title: Anxiolytic effects of short- and long-term administration of cacao mass on rat elevated T-maze test
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18976895/
Publisher: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0955286308001927
PMID: 18976895
DOI: 10.1016/j.jnutbio.2008.08.007
Publication date: 2008-10-31
Date basis: First electronic publication. Print issue December 2009.
Checked: 2026-11-24
Review depth: abstract

The author abstract reports separate short-term and two-week rat comparisons on the elevated T-maze. The acute result altered delayed avoidance in the direction the authors interpret as less conditioned fear, with no change in escape latency. The longer comparison trends in the opposite direction for avoidance, with no change in escape. The authors describe no chronic behavioral benefit. The task measures different aspects of conditioned fear behavior in rats.

Claim 0100 concerns plain dark chocolate and anxiety severity in people with Parkinson disease. Cacao mass and the animal task are indirect. The follow-up still needs the complete article for arm sizes, full statistics, comparison design, composition and group-level results. The 2012 Nehlig review cites the same experiment.
