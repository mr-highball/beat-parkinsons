Text coverage: Distilled original proceedings member; reviewed all 162 embedded members in the available collection.

Member T1:PO.44 (SA42, printed p. 65) reports a blinded trial in 60 postmenopausal women with hyperlipidemia. The three groups received yogurt, yogurt with 16 g soy lecithin, or yogurt with 10 g sunflower oil matched to the lecithin's linoleic acid. Strawberry syrup was included for blinding. The authors report no significant between-group lipid differences. Reported within-group improvements refer to soy lecithin. The poster gives no sunflower pressing grade, executive task, mood or anxiety outcome, or Parkinson cohort.

Supplementary member review
T1:PO.26 reports a Mediterranean-style diet high in virgin olive oil against conventional lower-fat guidance in adults at high cardiovascular risk. The reported total antioxidant capacity was 3.88 versus 2.09 mmol/L (adjusted p<.001). The trial does not isolate the oil or measure constipation or anxiety. T2:PO.11 relates several Mediterranean food items, including olive oil, to metabolic-syndrome prevalence. T1:PO.58 compares a reduced-energy fish menu with other reduced-energy guidance. The fish menu allowed salmon, anchovy, tuna or sardine and measured inflammatory markers and anthropometry. J. Salmon appears as an author in a separate abstract. The menu is the genuine fish exposure.
