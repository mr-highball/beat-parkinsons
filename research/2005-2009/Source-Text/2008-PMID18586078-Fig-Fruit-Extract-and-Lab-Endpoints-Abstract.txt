Title: Ethnopharmacological studies on antispasmodic and antiplatelet activities of Ficus carica
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18586078/
PMID: 18586078
DOI: 10.1016/j.jep.2008.05.040
Publication date: 2008-06-11, first electronic publication.
Text coverage: Selected results from the original abstract. Complete methods and tables remain open.

The original describes an aqueous-ethanolic extract of ripe dried common fig fruit. The isolated rabbit gut tissue relaxation differed with the contraction conditions, and the separate human platelet experiment measured aggregation outside the body. These are extract assays. They do not estimate the effects of eating intact dried figs on a clinical outcome or in Parkinson disease.

The 2016 human fig-paste study cites the gut-tissue work when discussing other model findings. Keep the species, extract, route and comparison separate from the supplied oral paste contrast.
