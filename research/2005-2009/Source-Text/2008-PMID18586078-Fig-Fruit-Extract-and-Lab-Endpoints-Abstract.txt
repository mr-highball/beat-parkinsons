Text coverage: Selected study facts from the complete methods, results and figure captions. The path preserves the earlier abstract entry.

Section 2.1 identifies ripe dried common figs bought from an herbal store in Multan and a taxonomically identified specimen archived under voucher FC-F-0905. The assayed material was an aqueous-ethanolic crude extract. The authors report an extract yield of approximately 25% by weight. This preparation differs from the intact food and from the 2016 supplied dietary paste.

In isolated rabbit jejunum, the reported median concentration producing half the maximal effect against spontaneous contraction was 0.74 mg/mL (95% CI 0.52–1.0, five experiments). Suppression differed between the low- and high-potassium contraction conditions, and the authors propose potassium-channel involvement. A separate ex vivo human platelet experiment reports reduced aggregation in response to tested stimuli.

The abstract and the body print different concentration values for one platelet comparison. The difference is retained as unresolved and no oral dose or clinical outcome is inferred. The 2016 human fig-paste trial cites this study for gut-tissue evidence. These experiments measured exposure to an extract in isolated preparations, with no clinical Parkinson visual episode outcome.
