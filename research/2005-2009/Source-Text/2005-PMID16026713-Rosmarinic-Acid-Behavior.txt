Title: Neurobehavioral and genotoxic aspects of rosmarinic acid
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16026713/
Publisher: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1043661805000708
PMID: 16026713
DOI: 10.1016/j.phrs.2005.03.003
Publication date: 2005-09 (indexed issue; PubMed record released 2005-07-20)
Retrieved (UTC): 2026-11-19
Text coverage: Author abstract and publisher preview. Original complete results tables remain open.

Investigators tested isolated rosmarinic acid by injection in rats. They used open-field, elevated plus-maze and inhibitory-avoidance tasks. The authors interpreted higher-dose locomotor changes as increased motivation. Other doses did not show that activity pattern. The available material does not establish an independent apathy or effort-valuation measure. It provides a dose- and route-specific behavioral lead for a constituent found in several plants, including common sage, without a dried-leaf ingestion comparison or diagnosed human Parkinson population.
