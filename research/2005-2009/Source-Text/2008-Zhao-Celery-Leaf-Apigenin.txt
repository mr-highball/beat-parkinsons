Title: 芹菜素人体内代谢研究 (Study on metabolism of apigenin from celery leaf in human)
Authors: 赵秀娟, 夏薇, 赵艳, 张宇秋, 张宇, 常佩亮, 吴坤
Citation: Chinese Journal of Public Health. 2008 April;24(4):446–447.
DOI: 10.11847/zgggws2008-24-04-38
Source: https://www.zgggws.com/cn/article/pdf/preview/10.11847/zgggws2008-24-04-38.pdf
Companion journal HTML: https://html.rhhz.net/ZGGGWS/html/20080438.htm
Text coverage: selected facts paraphrased from the primary report, methodology, Figure 1 and Table 1.

Ten healthy adults, five women and five men aged 24–28, ate green celery leaf at 2 g/kg body weight on one morning with unsweetened whole-wheat bread after a low-flavone run-in. The edible leaves were taken from market celery, washed, allowed to dry superficially and held at 4 °C. A separate portion was oven-dried at 40 °C for compositional analysis. Participants ate a mean of 114.70 g (SD 17.15) leaves. Apigenin was below detection in baseline fasting plasma, appeared in post-meal samples, and was below 2.32 nmol/L again by 28 hours. The authors report a mean time to peak of 7.70 h (SD 0.82), an AUC of 89.02 min·µmol/L (SD 40.57), and a highest individual measured concentration of 396.25 nmol/L. They found no significant differences in erythrocyte measurements across time. The study measured apigenin disposition without levodopa dosing, a Parkinson cohort, or dyskinesia scoring.

The Cao report (PMID 19747418) appeared online in 2009 and names 20 participants in its celery-leaf subsample. Its Table 3 duplicates all 35 displayed values in Zhao Table 1, with the same peak time and area under the curve. Consider this one reported plasma profile. Participant overlap and the effective denominator remain unresolved.
