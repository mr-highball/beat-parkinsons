Title: Effect of fish oil on cognitive performance in older subjects: a randomized, controlled trial
Publication date: 2008-08-05 (journal issue)
PMID: 18678826
DOI: 10.1212/01.wnl.0000324268.45138.86
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18678826/
Original investigator thesis: https://edepot.wur.nl/14784
Text coverage: Distilled primary article and author thesis Chapter 2, including Table 2.3.

Van de Rest and colleagues randomized 302 cognitively healthy adults aged at least 65 to 26 weeks of high-oleic sunflower oil or two fish-oil doses. There were 299 week-26 observations. Executive-domain mean standardized changes at 26 weeks were +0.13 (SD 0.39, n=103) on sunflower oil, +0.09 (0.39, n=96) on the 1,800 mg/day EPA+DHA comparator and +0.07 (0.46, n=100) on the 400 mg/day comparator. The 13-week changes were +0.09, +0.05 and +0.16 respectively. The investigators report no significant differential changes by cognitive domain. At 26 weeks attention scores moved in the opposite direction from the nominal executive comparison. The trial lacks a zero-oil arm and the processing grade and Parkinson-specific executive effect remain unverified.
