Title: Glucocerebrosidase mutations in clinical and pathologically proven Parkinson's disease
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2702833/

Publication date: 2009-03-13
Date basis: NLM electronic publication date. Brain 132(7), 1783–1794, appeared in the July issue.
Source depth: Complete PMC HTML main article, with methods, Table 2 and discussion. The separately linked supplements were not used.
Format: Original-source factual extract

The screening sample contained 790 Parkinson cases and 257 controls. The investigators reported a GBA variant in 33 cases and three controls. Two neurologists reviewed carrier clinical notes independently. Two carriers lacked usable clinical information, leaving 31 in the clinical analysis. The assessment omitted visual hallucinations attributed to levodopa or an agonist that resolved after a medication change and episodes in febrile illness or delirium.

Fourteen of 31 carriers with Parkinson disease had a history of visual hallucinations after motor onset. The earliest onset after motor symptoms was 42 months and the authors reported a 125-month mean lag. Table 2 records individual symptom histories. There is no matched noncarrier visual-symptom denominator for this clinical measurement, and neither the number of participants affected nor onset latency measures within-person episode frequency. The article's separate noncarrier autopsy comparison cannot supply that denominator. The paper does not assess dried fig intake.
