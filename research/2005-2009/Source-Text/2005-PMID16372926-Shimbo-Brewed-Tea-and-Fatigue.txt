Title: Green tea consumption in everyday life and mental health
Source: https://doi.org/10.1079/PHN2005752
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16372926/
PMID: 16372926
DOI: 10.1079/PHN2005752
Publication date: 2005-12
Date basis: Original journal issue.
Coverage: Paraphrased original Methods and Tables 2–3.

Shimbo and colleagues analyzed interviews with 380 Japanese adults aged 20–69 from a 2002 community sample. Brewed green tea was reported in four consumption bands. Table 2 compares habitual intake with a four-point perceived physical fatigue item. Spearman correlations were −0.09 in 180 men and −0.14 in 200 women. The table flags neither result as statistically significant. Separate multivariable estimates concern the general mental health endpoint.

The paper distinguishes brewed green tea from other caffeinated drinks. Its fatigue results are unadjusted ranks, with no validated Parkinson fatigue scale, repeated within-person measure or diagnosed Parkinson stratum. Neither direction transfers to change after drinking brewed green tea in Parkinson disease.
