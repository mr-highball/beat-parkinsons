Title: Potent Odorants Characterize the Aroma Quality of Leaves and Stalks in Raw and Boiled Celery
Authors: Yoshiko Kurobayashi, Emi Kouno, Akira Fujita, Yasujiro Morimitsu, and Kikue Kubota
Citation: Bioscience, Biotechnology, and Biochemistry. 2006;70(4):958–965.
First public availability shown by journal: 2006-04-23.
PMID: 16636464
DOI: 10.1271/bbb.70.958
Source: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/bbb/70/4/70_4_958/_pdf
Text coverage: selected facts paraphrased from the journal's public primary report, analytical methods and Table 2.

The researchers quantified phthalides in raw leaves, closed-system boiled leaves, raw stalks, and closed-system boiled stalks. Results in those four groups, in mg/kg of original whole material, were 0.87, 1.05, 0.29 and 0.36 for 3-n-butylphthalide (Table 2). For sedanenolide they were 17.57, 15.87, 4.19 and 4.63. For summed cis- and trans-sedanolides they were 4.56, 5.24, 0.09 and 0.12. Total measured phthalides were 23.01, 22.15, 4.57 and 5.11. The different isolation procedure used in the separate water-extract experiment produced different concentration estimates. The authors report no significant raw-versus-boiled differences for phthalides under their closed-system test conditions. The report examines plant composition and sensory qualities. It does not administer celery to people with Parkinson's or test levodopa response.
