Inspected extent: Original methods, Tables I–IV, results and discussion. Cross-checked Spanish and English article versions.

The survey in Albacete, Spain, sampled residents over 50. There were 414 questionnaires and 408 responses usable for the oil and bowel-frequency comparison in Table II. The results text defines infrequent bowel movements as three or fewer weekly. An English table heading uses a less-than sign, while Table I, the English narrative and the Spanish narrative use an inclusive threshold.

Table II counts at or below versus above the threshold were 16 and 381 in the high virgin olive oil intake group (397 total), and 2 and 9 in the low intake group (11 total). Thus 4.0% of higher consumers and 18.2% of lower consumers had three or fewer weekly movements. Viewed by bowel-frequency category, 381/390 (97.7%) above the threshold and 16/18 (88.9%) at or below it reported high intake. Table IV reports means of 8.22 (SD 3.50) versus 5.73 (SD 1.90) movements weekly, high versus low, with reported Mann–Whitney p = 0.004. These are two analyses of the same survey.

The narrative reports chi-square 5.083, one degree of freedom, and p = 0.004 for Table II. The cell counts reproduce Pearson chi-square 5.083, corresponding to p approximately 0.024. A two-sided Fisher calculation on the printed 2-by-2 table gives approximately 0.080. These are reviewer calculations from published cells. Given the 11 low-intake respondents, the association is imprecise. No isolated intervention, adjustment for diet and other confounding, Parkinson-specific analysis, or verified extra-virgin grade was reported.

Peppas et al. (PMID 18269746), Table 2, places consumption of olive oil under factors associated with increased odds of constipation and cites this same survey. The primary counts and mean bowel frequency point in the opposite direction. Count the two documents as one family, and use the primary comparison for direction. This is contextual evidence for claim 0072 (extra-virgin olive oil and Parkinson constipation severity), with no claim-level effect assigned.
