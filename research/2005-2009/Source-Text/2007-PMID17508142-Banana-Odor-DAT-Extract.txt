Title: Selective hyposmia and nigrostriatal dopaminergic denervation in Parkinson's disease
PMID: 17508142
DOI: 10.1007/s00415-006-0284-y
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17508142/
First published: January 2007 issue, Journal of Neurology 254:84–90. Crossref lists a February 14, 2007 online date.
Archive scope: Original author abstract and referenced study identity. Full source body remains open.
Reviewed: 2026-11-19

Twenty-seven participants with Parkinson disease and 27 matched controls took a 40-odor identification test. The investigators selected banana, licorice and dill-pickle odor items as particularly discriminating in that sample. The Parkinson participants also underwent dopamine transporter imaging. The study concerns a diagnostic odor measure and association with denervation. A separate repeated-measures apathy association was later reported in a Taiwanese cohort. Neither is an assigned raw-banana dietary exposure.
