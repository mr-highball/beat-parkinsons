There were 35 older post-stroke participants assigned to each inhalation arm. Baseline odor identification was 2.2 ± 2.0 with black pepper oil, 1.7 ± 2.3 with lavender, and 2.1 ± 2.4 with odorless comparison. This characteristic preceded treatment. Follow-up outcomes were swallowing or related measures. The original report provides no longitudinal clinical odor-identification effect.

Swallowing comparisons from the verified full report
Figure 1 allocates 35 people per arm, with 34 black pepper oil, 33 lavender oil and 33 odorless control participants completing follow-up. Table 1 reports mean reflex latencies of 6.4, 13.2 and 15.2 seconds immediately after exposure, with overall p=.03. Day-30 means were 4.4, 13.6 and 14.4 seconds, respectively (overall p<.001). Cough reflex sensitivity showed no significant change. The broader dysphagia review PMID 34337829 uses the black pepper and water follow-up counts. Its crossover exclusion, reference 22, is the separate menthol experiment. The original pepper trial has three parallel arms. It concerns inhaled oil and a provoked reflex in older post-stroke residents, with no cracked culinary pepper ingestion or Parkinson-specific severity result.
