Primary-study factual extract for 17275394 (2007; doi 10.1016/j.parkreldis.2006.11.004) and companion 18434232 (first public 2008; doi 10.1016/j.parkreldis.2008.02.011). Kandinov, Giladi and Korczyn. Full abstracts and publisher excerpts at https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1353802006002689 and https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S135380200800093X.
The progression study followed 278 recruited PD patients with retrospective tea histories and time between motor symptoms and Hoehn and Yahr stage 3. It did not find a clear tea association with time to stage 3. The companion onset publication explicitly says it used the same study population and reports onset 7.7 years later in people drinking more than three tea cups per day (p<.01). These are different outcomes in one patient sample. Accessible article text did not supply black-tea-specific brewing variables or separate gait and rest tremor severity estimates.
