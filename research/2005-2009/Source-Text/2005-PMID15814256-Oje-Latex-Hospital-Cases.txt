Title: Reevaluation of risks with the use of Ficus insipida latex as a traditional anthelmintic remedy in the Amazon
Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378874105000607
PMID: 15814256
DOI: 10.1016/j.jep.2004.12.029
Publication date: 2005
Date basis: Original journal publication in volume 98, issue 3, pp. 251–257, with NLM issue date 2005-04-26.
Source depth: Original NLM author abstract and publisher article sections. The summary uses the article's case-series methods and reported index case.
Evidence type: Primary retrospective hospital records and index case
Format: Factual digest of original material

The hospital review covers a 12-year period starting in 1992 and identifies 39 reactions to the traditional antiparasitic preparation made from Ficus insipida latex. The authors classified most admissions as probably related to excessive intake. The case prompting their review concerned a child with loss of consciousness and cerebral edema who recovered after hospitalization. This series is limited to recorded admissions and does not supply a measured community exposure denominator or a reliable event rate for users.

The material studied was latex from a separate fig species. Reduced consciousness and documented edema are clinically distinct from recurrent formed visual hallucinations. The inspected sections do not provide a visual episode-frequency comparison in Parkinson disease or a test of eating dried figs.
