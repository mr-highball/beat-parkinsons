Title: The perceived effect of various foods and beverages on stool consistency.
PMID: 15647650
DOI: 10.1097/00042737-200501000-00020
First published: 2005
Authors: Müller-Lissner SA, Kaatz V, Brandt W, Keller J, Layer P
Journal: European journal of gastroenterology & hepatology
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15647650/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Cross-sectional German questionnaire survey of n=1,088 adults (200 healthy controls, 122 chronic constipation, 766 IBS-C) assessing perceived food effects on stool consistency. On systematic questioning, 29-48% of respondents perceived bananas as constipating (behind chocolate at 48-64% and ahead of black tea at 14-24%), whereas prunes (41-52%) were perceived as stool softeners. Limits: unblinded cross-sectional belief survey without dietary intervention, ripeness specification, objective stool measurement, or PD cohort.
