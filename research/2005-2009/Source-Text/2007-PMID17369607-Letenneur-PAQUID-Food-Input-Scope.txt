The PAQUID cohort included 1,640 older adults with usable diet records and cognitive data and without dementia at enrollment for this analysis. At one visit, the questionnaire included coffee, asparagus, sweet pepper and other foods. Food amounts were imputed using a dietary survey subsample. The comparison grouped total estimated intake of five flavonoids into quartiles rather than testing foods separately.

The primary longitudinal MMSE model in Table 2 includes 1,617 participants. The flavonoid by time trend had p=0.046 in the age-, sex- and education-adjusted model. The paper's predicted 10-year MMSE changes were about −2.1 points in the lowest and −1.2 in the highest quartile. A separate joint cognitive model gave p=0.06 for the change-over-time trend. The cohort is observational, uses a single dietary measurement and does not isolate food preparations or treatment effects.

Coffee (0121), asparagus (0837) and sweet pepper (0879) are questionnaire inputs. Fatigue is not the measured outcome. The review PMID:22775434 describes the same cohort and should not be counted separately.
