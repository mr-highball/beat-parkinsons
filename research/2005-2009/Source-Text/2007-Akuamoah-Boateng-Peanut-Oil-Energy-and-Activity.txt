Title: Effect of Peanut Oil Consumption on Energy Balance
Publication year: 2007
Citation: Akuamoah-Boateng and colleagues. The Journal of Applied Research 7(2):185–195.
Source: https://www.jarcet.com/articles/Vol7Iss2/MattesVol7No2.pdf
Text coverage: Original author paper and study Tables 1–2, Methods and Results.

Table 1 lists 129 adults in Ghana, Brazil and the United States. The study assigned daily milkshakes containing peanut, olive or safflower oil, or a no-load control, during eight weeks. The paper reports increased energy intake during the intervention and weight gain by week eight in each active intervention group. It reports no significant change in general physical activity, resting energy expenditure or the thermic effect of food. The study did not enroll a Parkinson cohort, report pressing grade or separately measure tremor. The same 129-person intervention has an appetite report (PMID 16314877).
