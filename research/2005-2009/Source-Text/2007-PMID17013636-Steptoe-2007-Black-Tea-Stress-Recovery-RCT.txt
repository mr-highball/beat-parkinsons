Title: The effects of tea on psychophysiological stress responsivity and post-stress recovery: a randomised double-blind trial.
PMID: 17013636
DOI: 10.1007/s00213-006-0573-2
First published: 2007
Authors: Steptoe A, Gibson EL, Vuononvirta R, Williams ED, Hamer M, Rycroft JA, et al.
Journal: Psychopharmacology
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17013636/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Parallel-group double-blind RCT in n=75 healthy nonsmoking men (active black tea 4 cups/d n=37 vs caffeinated placebo n=38 for 6 weeks after 4-week washout). Acute behavioral task stress BP, HR, and subjective stress responses did not differ, but black tea lowered post-stress cortisol (p=0.032), lowered baseline and post-stress platelet activation (p<0.005), and increased post-task subjective relaxation during recovery (p=0.036). Limits: non-PD healthy men; acute stress recovery rather than clinical PD anxiety.
