Text coverage: Curated original methods, results, figure captions and bibliography from the publisher-formatted paper's text

Seventy male Fischer 344 rats entered the experiment at 19 months of age. The five diets were unsupplemented control or the control diet with 2% aqueous blueberry, blackcurrant, boysenberry or cranberry extract. At the end of the eighth week the researchers assessed rod walking, wire suspension, plank walking, an inclined screen and accelerating rotarod performance. Several tests measured seconds until the animal fell from the apparatus.

Only the inclined screen had a significant overall diet-group contrast among the motor measures (F(4,64) = 3.76, p < .01). The reported post-hoc contrasts versus control favored blueberry (p = .038) and cranberry (p = .001). This was a laboratory measure of grip, strength and balance in aged rats. The strawberry experiments appear as separately cited work, including the 1998 and 1999 Joseph reports. Orange and strawberry were absent from the five assigned diets.

The authors also measured water-maze performance, tissue responses and signaling. The 2002 USDA conference summary describes the same four extracts and similar preliminary findings. The duration wording and author list differ, with cohort identity unresolved. Treat the conference abstract as a possible precursor when counting independent sources.
