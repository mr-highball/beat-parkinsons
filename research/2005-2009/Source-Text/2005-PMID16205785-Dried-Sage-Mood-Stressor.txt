Title: Effects of cholinesterase inhibiting sage (Salvia officinalis) on mood, anxiety and performance on a psychological stressor battery
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16205785/
Publisher: https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.npp.1300907
PMID: 16205785
DOI: 10.1038/sj.npp.1300907
Publication date: 2005-10-05 (electronic; 2006 issue)
Retrieved (UTC): 2026-11-19
Text coverage: Eight-page published article verified against the indexed DOI. Factual extract checked against Table 1 and Figures 3 and 4.

Kennedy and colleagues used a counterbalanced, double-blind placebo crossover in 30 healthy adults. They compared 300 mg and 600 mg of dried common-sage leaf in capsules. The Bond–Lader scale and state anxiety measurements were assessed before and after a computerized multitasking stressor at baseline and at one and four hours after dosing.

At one hour and before the task, alertness changed by +4.68 in the higher-dose condition and -2.81 with placebo (planned test p=0.003). The same dose produced a lower change in task-associated alertness at one hour, -3.04 compared with +4.18 for placebo on the different change-of-change contrast (p=0.02). At 300 mg, alertness increased before the task at one hour and decreased on the during-task contrast at both times. Anxiety and computerized performance also varied by dose and test conditions. These findings describe acute mood and performance. The experiment did not assess Parkinson disease, clinical apathy or persistent motivation.
