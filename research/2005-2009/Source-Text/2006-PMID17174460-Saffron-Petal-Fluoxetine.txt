Title: Saffron flower petal extract and fluoxetine in major depression
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17174460/
Public transcription of the publisher-formatted original: https://www.academia.edu/65706849/Comparison_of_petal_of_Crocus_sativus_L_and_fluoxetine_in_the_treatment_of_depressed_outpatients_A_pilot_double_blind_randomized_trial
PMID: 17174460
DOI: 10.1016/j.pnpbp.2006.11.010
First published online: 2006-12-15. Issue date: 2007.
Coverage: Factual extract from the public transcription of pages 439–442, including Methods and Table 2.

Akhondzadeh Basti and colleagues randomly assigned 40 outpatients with mild to moderate major depression, 20 per arm, to extract from Crocus sativus flower petals or fluoxetine for eight weeks. Thirty-eight completed follow-up. The original Methods list 15 mg dried hydroalcoholic petal extract per capsule twice daily, and 10 mg fluoxetine per capsule twice daily. The trial did not use the stigma that is sold as culinary saffron.

Mean eight-week Hamilton Depression score changes were −12.00 (SD 4.10) for petal extract and −13.50 (SD 4.91) for fluoxetine, p=.30 for that contrast and p=.19 for the group-by-time interaction. Depression scores improved within both arms. The study had no placebo group or prespecified equivalence margin.

Table 2 records insomnia in three participants in each arm. It records tremor in two on petal extract and five on fluoxetine (p=.40), with no tremor subtype. The authors could not isolate insomnia as an adverse effect rather than a feature of depression. Insomnia and tremor severity were not tested as efficacy outcomes. There was no Parkinson cohort. The separately published petal-versus-placebo trial is PMID 16979327, with different treatment and analysis duration. Participant overlap could not be verified.
