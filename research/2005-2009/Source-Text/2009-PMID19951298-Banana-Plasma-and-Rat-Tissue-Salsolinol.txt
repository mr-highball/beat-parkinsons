Title: Banana intake and salsolinol in human plasma and rat tissue
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2858379/
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19951298/
PMID: 19951298
PMCID: PMC2858379
DOI: 10.1111/j.1530-0277.2009.01087.x
Publication date: 2009-11-24
Date basis: First electronic publication in NLM. The print issue is February 2010.
Reviewed: 2026-11-24
Review depth: Full author manuscript via PMC, including methods, results, Figures 3–4 and Table 1.

The dietary analysis followed six healthy adult volunteers before and after banana intake. Figure 3 reports much higher mean circulating salsolinol after the meal. The mean for the S form changed from 0.16 to 5.8 ng/mL and the mean for the R form from 0.23 to 6.6 ng/mL. The paper reports large individual differences. Human sampling was from plasma.

A separate rat experiment also observed plasma increases. In the sampled rat striatum and nucleus accumbens, Table 1 shows no clear corresponding change in salsolinol or dopamine. The measured rat tissue result is distinct from human central nervous system effects. Neither branch gives a clinical anxiety or other Parkinson symptom comparison. The study helps separate dietary exposure markers from evidence of symptom benefit or harm.
