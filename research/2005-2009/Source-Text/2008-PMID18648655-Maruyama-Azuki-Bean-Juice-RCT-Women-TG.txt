Title: Azuki bean juice lowers serum triglyceride concentrations in healthy young women.
PMID: 18648655
PMCID: PMC2459248
DOI: 10.3164/jcbn.2008039
First published: 2008
Authors: Maruyama C, Araki R, Kawamura M, Kondo N, Kigawa M, Kawai Y, et al.
Journal: Journal of clinical biochemistry and nutrition
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18648655/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 2 review).

Randomized parallel-group human trial (Maruyama et al., 2008, J Clin Biochem Nutr, PMC2459248) in 33 healthy young Japanese women randomized for one menstrual cycle to control (n=10), azuki bean juice (n=11, 150 g/day prepared from 35 g boiled Erimosekishozu azuki beans), or concentrated azuki juice (n=12). Serum triglycerides declined by 0.170 mmol/L (15.4%, p<0.05) in azuki juice and 0.159 mmol/L (17.9%, p=0.055) in concentrated azuki juice, inhibiting pancreatic lipase 29.2% and 56.9%. Explicitly documents traditional Kampo/Chinese prescription Shozu-to (Phaseolus angularis) used as antidotal, diuretic, and laxative for constipation. Limits: measured lipids only; no bowel, urinary, or neurological symptoms recorded.
