Title: Caffeinated beverages and olfactory performance in relatives of Parkinson patients
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17876851/
PMID: 17876851
DOI: 10.1002/mds.21707
Publication date: 2007
Coverage: Exact publisher author abstract. Full original outcome tables remain unavailable.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26

Siderowf and colleagues examined 173 first-degree relatives of people with Parkinson disease. Lifetime consumption of caffeinated beverages was observational. Compared with less than one cup per day, the adjusted smell-identification score differences reported for two to three and at least four cups per day were 2.6 (95% CI 0.5–4.5) and 3.7 (0.6–6.7) points. The exposure categories concern caffeinated beverages, and the participants were relatives of Parkinson patients.

The later review PMC8524700 incorrectly describes the cohort as Parkinson patients. Its quoted odds ratio is outside its own quoted interval and is not used here. The authentic comparison gives no isolated coffee effect in people with diagnosed Parkinson disease.
