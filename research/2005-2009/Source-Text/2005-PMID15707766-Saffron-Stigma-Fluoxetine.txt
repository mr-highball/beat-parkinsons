Hydro-alcoholic saffron stigma extract and fluoxetine in major depression
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15707766/
Original publisher pages: https://www.tas-saff.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/noorbala_2005_1.pdf
PMID: 15707766
DOI: 10.1016/j.jep.2004.11.004
First published online: 2005-01-06
Coverage: Factual extract from the original four-page report, Methods and Tables 1–2.

Noorbala and colleagues randomly assigned 40 adult outpatients with DSM-IV major depression to a dried saffron stigma extract or fluoxetine, 20 per group, for six weeks. The lab extracted milled dried stigmas and standardized the capsule to safranal. The stigma extract was 15 mg per capsule, given twice daily as a study treatment. Thirty-eight people completed the study. Hamilton Depression scores improved in both groups. The mean six-week changes were −12.20 (SD 4.67) with extract and −15.00 (SD 5.88) with fluoxetine, p=.10 for the difference and p=.17 for the overall group-by-time interaction. This small active-comparator study cannot establish equivalence or an effect versus placebo.

Table 2 reports sedation in one extract participant and none on fluoxetine, and tremor in none versus four (p=.10). Tremor was an adverse event without a subtype or severity measure. The table supplies no separate insomnia row. The study used a concentrated stigma extract in major depression outside Parkinson disease.
