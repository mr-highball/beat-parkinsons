Title: Comparative effects of aqueous sweet basil leaf extract and loperamide on intestinal transit in rats
Source: https://repository.ui.edu.ng/bitstreams/0591d3a0-fcdf-40a0-8d33-f32d30b8ed0d/download
Publication date: 2006-12-30
Date basis: University of Ibadan repository reprint cover; Nigerian Journal of Experimental and Applied Biology 7(2), pp. 141–144.
Study key: ABBAH-2006-OCIMUM-BASILICUM-TRANSIT
Checked: 2026-11-25
Review depth: Original reprint, pp. 141–144, Materials and Methods, Results and Figs. 1–2.

Abbah and colleagues report 70 conventional albino rats, ten per each of seven parallel groups, with one saline control, three sweet basil groups and three loperamide groups. The authors botanically authenticated Ocimum basilicum. The leaves were dried and powdered before water extraction. The oral test exposure was the filtered extract, without the powdered leaf residue. The researchers assessed one-hour charcoal marker progress as a percentage of intestinal length. This assay is a transit proxy and does not measure stool frequency, ease of defecation or a Parkinson disease outcome.

Saline mean: 66.69% (reported SEM 3.20 percentage points, p. 143; 66.68% in the abstract). The three ascending extract groups reported 73.32%, 74.84%, and 78.30% (SEM 3.77, 3.92, and 4.30 percentage points, respectively). The high group differs from saline by 11.61 percentage points based on the p. 143 control value. Loperamide groups reported shorter marker progress (approximately 58%, 56%, and 51%). Values for the middle loperamide group differ slightly between abstract and Results. Precise animal exposures remain available in the linked report and provide no human-equivalent serving.

The authors report all extract groups differed at p<0.05. The point means show an ordered favorable contrast, but if the printed uncertainty values are interpreted as SEMs, ordinary independent two-sample checks do not reproduce p<0.05 for the two lower-dose comparisons. Individual observations, assignment concealment, assessor masking and adjustment for testing several comparisons are unavailable. The reported high-group contrast is compatible with nominal significance using the printed SEMs, before considering multiple comparisons.

Relevance: Faster measured marker progress gives a minimal directional easing lead for a dried sweet basil source and a slow-transit component of constipation. Preparation, species of patient and outcome differ sharply from the frozen culinary Parkinson claim. The 2026 ex vivo leaf study measures contraction amplitude, not net transit. The 2004 oil-free aqueous study of a separately identified Ocimum species had the opposite result. These are retained as distinct evidence rather than counted as replications.
