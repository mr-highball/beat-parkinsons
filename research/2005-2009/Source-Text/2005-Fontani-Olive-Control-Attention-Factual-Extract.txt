Olive oil as comparator in an early sustained attention experiment
Source: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2362.2005.01570.x
PMID: 16269019
DOI: 10.1111/j.1365-2362.2005.01570.x
Coverage: Original full article Methods and Tables 1–2

Fontani and colleagues studied 49 healthy adults for 35 days. Thirty-three took fish oil and 16 received olive oil. Both supplements were given at 4 g per day. The task battery included sustained attention and choice reaction measures. The article tabulates change on the sustained-attention task within the fish-oil arm and provides no separate olive-arm means for the same table. No oil-free arm, processing grade for olive oil, or Parkinson attention comparison was reported.
