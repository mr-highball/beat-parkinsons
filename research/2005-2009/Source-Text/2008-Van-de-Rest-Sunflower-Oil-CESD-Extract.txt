High-oleic sunflower oil comparator in an older-adult trial
Source: https://physicians-strength.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Effect-of-fish-oil-supplementation-on-mental-well-being-in-older-subjects.pdf
PMID: 18779287
DOI: 10.1093/ajcn/88.3.706
Registration: NCT00124852
Coverage: Published Methods, mental well-being Results and Table 2 reviewed; original factual extraction

In September 2008, van de Rest and colleagues reported a 26-week blinded trial in 302 community-dwelling adults aged 65 or older. The 106-person comparator arm received high-oleic sunflower oil in six 900 mg capsules daily, with tocopherol. The authors do not specify refining. The two fish-oil doses enrolled 96 and 100 participants. The sunflower, high-dose and low-dose arms had mean CES-D changes of −0.4, −0.2 and +0.2, respectively (overall p = .87). The MADRS and GDS-15 group comparisons had p = .73 and .90. The cohort excluded people above the screening depression threshold or taking antidepressants. The study tests fish oil against sunflower oil under those conditions. It has no oil-free arm or Parkinson depression outcome.
