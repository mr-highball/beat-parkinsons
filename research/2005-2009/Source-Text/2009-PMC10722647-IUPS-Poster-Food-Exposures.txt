Reviewed: 2026-11-20

The publisher's recovered PDF is the 102-page original poster block, printed pp. 440–541. P5AM-21-16 (p. 538) reports 30 days of oral aqueous ginger extract in diabetic rats, with seven animals per group. It measured plasma glucose, blood counts and liver or kidney markers. Neighboring posters on sheep stomach motility and avian tissue properties are separate experiments. Neither supplies a ginger symptom endpoint.

P4PM-3-14 (p. 454) compared a grape-seed extract in a rat vascular model, measuring aortic response. P4PM-7-4 (p. 462) measured tissue distribution after administration of isolated resveratrol. Neither measured anxiety or skeletal muscle rigidity. P4PM-12-16 (p. 470) compared instant coffee and hot water in two healthy adults, measuring facial skin temperature and heart activity without a fatigue endpoint. Trout cardiac physiology is a separate animal model. SAGE is a gene-expression technique rather than the culinary plant.

The related 2011 paper on aqueous ginger and diabetic rat carbohydrate metabolism reports the same first three authors, three extract amounts and 30-day design. Its endpoints are partly different and participant reuse remains unresolved.
