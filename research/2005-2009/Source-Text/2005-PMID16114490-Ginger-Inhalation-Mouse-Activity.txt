Title: Inhaled ginger essential oil and mouse forced-swim activity
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16114490/
Publisher: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02977341
PMID: 16114490
DOI: 10.1007/BF02977341
Publication date: 2005-07
Date basis: Publisher issue month, Archives of Pharmacal Research 28(7), 770–774.
Source depth: Primary abstract and publisher references. The full article is subscription content.
Reviewed: 2026-11-25

Lim and colleagues compared inhaled essential oils in a mouse forced-swimming test. Ginger oil was among the exposures that reduced immobility (p < 0.05). The 5–22% range applies to several oils together. The abstract does not supply a ginger-only percentage. The report also describes tests in mice with caffeine-induced activity. The authors interpret the panel in terms of stimulant-like and sedative-like activity.

The route was inhalation and the outcome was immobility. There was no culinary ginger arm, Parkinson cohort, anxiety assessment, clinical rigidity measure or dyskinesia measure in the visible report. This source is the original cited in the 2010 stem-cell and neuroprotection review, PMID 20559500. The study and the review represent the same experimental evidence family.
