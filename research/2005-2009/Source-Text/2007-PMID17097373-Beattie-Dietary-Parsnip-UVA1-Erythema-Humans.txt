Title: Can dietary furanocoumarin ingestion enhance the erythemal response during high-dose UVA1 therapy?
PMID: 17097373
DOI: 10.1016/j.jaad.2006.07.003
First published: 2007
Authors: Beattie PE, Wilkie MJ, Smith G, Ferguson J, Ibbotson SH
Journal: Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17097373/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 5 review).

Primary Abstract:
As phototoxic skin reactions caused by psoralen are induced by wavelengths within the UVA1 spectrum, we assessed the potential of the small amount of psoralen in a normal diet to provoke phototoxicity in volunteers with skin types I and II. Threshold erythema was unaffected by ingestion of a 200-g portion of parsnip.

Verified Round 5 Primary Facts & Critical Appraisal:
Human photobiology challenge study (J Am Acad Dermatol 2007) in skin-type I-II volunteers testing whether a normal dietary 200 g portion of parsnip root lowered minimal erythema dose during high-dose UVA1 phototherapy. Threshold erythema was UNAFFECTED by 200 g oral parsnip ingestion—providing key reassurance that ordinary culinary cooked root intake carries far lower phototoxicity risk than topical sap contact (PMID:10572825, PMID:31173794, PMID:32383195 perioral parsnip mash, PMC11355232).
