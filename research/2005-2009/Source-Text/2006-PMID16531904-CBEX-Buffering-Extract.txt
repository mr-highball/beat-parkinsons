Title: Carnosine and anserine ingestion enhances contribution of nonbicarbonate buffering
PMID: 16531904
DOI: 10.1249/01.mss.0000185108.63028.04
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16531904/
First published: February 2006, Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise 38(2):334–338.
Archive scope: Original author abstract and checked citation contrast from the 2021 chicken broth paper. Full Methods and tables remain open.
Reviewed: 2026-11-19

The investigators compared chicken breast extract with placebo during intense intermittent exercise in healthy men. They report a higher nonbicarbonate component of buffering and a bicarbonate response difference. Performance did not improve in the tested sprint set. Acid–base measures and cycling output answer separate questions. The paper concerns a concentrated extract, exercise challenge and physiological outcomes. Everyday Parkinson fatigue and consuming poached chicken were outside the comparison.

The 2021 broth trial discusses an earlier 2004 favorable sprint report from the same investigator group. That report used a different sprint structure and still needs source-body and participant-overlap verification.
