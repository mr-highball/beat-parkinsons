Title: Consumption of cocoa flavanols results in acute improvements in mood and cognitive performance during sustained mental effort
Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0269881109106923
Original accepted manuscript: https://researchportal.northumbria.ac.uk/ws/files/3024739/Scholey_Consumption%20of%20cocoa%20flavanols.pdf
PMID: 19942640
DOI: 10.1177/0269881109106923
Publication date: 2009-11-26 (online, publisher)
Verification: Author manuscript methods, Table 1, Results, Figure 2h and Discussion, checked November 24, 2026.

Thirty healthy adults completed a randomized, double-blind three-period crossover. A cocoa drink with 46 mg flavanols served as the comparison for drinks with 520 or 994 mg. Other drink ingredients, including caffeine and theobromine, were closely matched. There was one cognitive work session per treatment.

The primary paper reports no clear state-anxiety treatment effect (all F statistics below 1 in the comparisons reported). State anxiety varied across treatment sequences (p=.047), limiting interpretation in either direction. The 520 mg drink reduced task-evoked mental fatigue at five of six time points. The 994 mg drink did not show this result, which corrects the broader wording in Katz et al. PMID 21470061. Neither measure evaluates chronic Parkinson symptoms.

Round 1 claim 0100. This is cautiously weighted related evidence about acute state anxiety after cocoa beverages. The exposure is more remote than an ordinary solid bar, and the sequence effect limits the null result. Count the primary trial once.
