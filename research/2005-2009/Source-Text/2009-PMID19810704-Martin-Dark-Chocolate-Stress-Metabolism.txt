Thirty healthy adults ate dark chocolate for two weeks using two daily portions totaling 40 g. The methods call the parent design open and parallel. In the published chocolate analysis, the reported contrasts compare time points or baseline anxiety-trait strata among recipients. No non-chocolate comparison appears in those reported contrasts. Four participants' metabolic data were excluded after gastrointestinal adverse events.

Anxiety trait from the State-Trait Anxiety Inventory defines the baseline strata. The reported changes concern urine and plasma chemistry. Table 4 gives nominal paired time comparisons for urinary markers including cortisol and adrenaline. The high-trait subgroup accounts for key reported stress-metabolism changes. The paper provides no treatment comparison for state or trait anxiety severity. Do not convert a biomarker direction into a clinical anxiety signal.

Round 1 claim 0100. Whole dark chocolate adds exposure context. Population and measurements do not establish the effect of culinary plain dark chocolate on Parkinson anxiety. Katz et al. PMID 21470061 cite this cohort and are not another comparison.
