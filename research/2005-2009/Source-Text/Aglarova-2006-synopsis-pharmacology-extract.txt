The animal work relied on extracts of green biomass of the French cultivar. Open-field and burrow-reflex assays used 30 male rats weighing 180–200 g. Reported effects included greater emotional lability described as 2.7 to 3 times higher, stabilized orientation, reduced searching and a decrease in aggression. The synopsis gives no seconds of sleep and no behavioral dose/administration route in its brief central nervous system paragraph. A later monograph's investigation also used the same assay types and describes intragastric dosing with tinctures made in 40% or 70% ethanol. Its relative change figures for lability and its aggression outcomes require care. Table 30 prints lability scores 0.6, 2.4, and 2.2, corresponding to relative increases of 300% and 266.7%, and aggression scores 5.6, 6.0, and 6.2. The later source describes a slight increase rather than the reduction in the original synopsis. Shared numerical results elsewhere support likely lineage, not verification of identical behavioral group data.

In the separate acetic-acid-evoked writhing test, groups comprised five animals and received 25 mL/kg intraperitoneally according to Figure 2. In the control group latency was 4.8 min and the mean count reached about 55. Tarragon tinctures made in 40% and 70% ethanol increased latency to 291% and 312% of control while counts were reduced by 87% and 93%. The range for the aqueous extracts was 46–87% longer latency and 64–82% fewer writhes. Do not transfer the Figure 2 dose and route to the separate open-field or burrow-reflex work.

In a separate experiment (n = 5 per group, Figure 5) output over 24 h was 143% of control after the 40% tincture and 173% after the 70% tincture. Postural blood pressure and lightheadedness were not measured. The corresponding diuresis ratios, gallbladder values at three time points, and total gastric ulcer counts appear with the same values in the later monograph. A 2006 Farmatsiya article with similar preparation is indexed, but article pages 37–39 remain unread.
