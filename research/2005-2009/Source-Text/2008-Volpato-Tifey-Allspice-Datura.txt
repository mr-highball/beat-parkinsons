Volpato G, Godínez D, Beyra A. Migration and Ethnobotanical Practices: The Case of Tifey Among Haitian Immigrants in Cuba. Human Ecology 37:43–53 (2009). Published online 13 December 2008. DOI 10.1007/s10745-008-9211-4. Open publisher original: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10745-008-9211-4

Field interviews report an alcohol-based multispecies drink with variable recipes. Table 1 lists Pimenta dioica fruit, local names pimienta dulce, pimienta bomba, Pwa duz and pimá duz, and two citations for stomachic use. A distinct table row lists Datura stramonium flowers with two citations under hallucinogenic use; the narrative says flowers were sometimes included for ritual trance. The report does not document an identified batch combining the two species, clinical visual hallucinations, an actual allspice-linked neurological event, or Parkinson disease. “Tifey” cannot be treated as a fixed formulation. The reported historical/ethnobotanical use does not imply controlled consumption or causal efficacy.
