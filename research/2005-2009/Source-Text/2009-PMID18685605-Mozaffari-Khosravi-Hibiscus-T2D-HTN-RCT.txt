Title: The effects of sour tea (Hibiscus sabdariffa) on hypertension in patients with type II diabetes
PMID: 18685605
DOI: 10.1038/jhh.2008.100
First published: 2009
Authors: Mozaffari-Khosravi H, Jalali-Khanabadi BA, Afkhami-Ardekani M, Fatehi F, Noori-Shadkam M
Journal: Journal of Human Hypertension
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18685605/
Coverage: Local factual extract from verified primary abstract/record (Round 4 review).

Source basis: PubMed primary abstract. Double-blind randomized controlled trial in 60 adults with type 2 diabetes and mild hypertension not on antihypertensives (53 completed), assigned to brewed Hibiscus sabdariffa (sour tea) infusion or black tea infusion twice daily for 1 month. Mean SBP fell from 134.4 +/- 11.8 to 112.7 +/- 5.7 mmHg (P<0.001) in the hibiscus group while rising from 118.6 +/- 14.9 to 127.3 +/- 8.7 mmHg in the black tea comparator; pulse pressure also decreased significantly (52.2 to 34.5 mmHg, P<0.001). Baseline imbalance between groups and lack of orthostatic BP measurements limit transfer to Parkinson's orthostatic hypotension.
