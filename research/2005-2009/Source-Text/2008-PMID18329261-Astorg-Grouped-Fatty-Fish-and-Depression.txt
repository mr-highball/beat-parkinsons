Title: Association of fish and long-chain n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acid intakes with the occurrence of depressive episodes in middle-aged French men and women

Review depth: Original methods, adjusted models and subgroup results.

Six repeated dietary records from 1,864 SU.VI.MAX participants supported a nested observational analysis of dietary intake. The parent trial randomized a supplement, not dietary fish. The paper groups trout with other fatty fish and estimates associations for the group. It does not split trout intake, fish species or poaching into separate outcome comparisons.

The endpoint counted self-reported antidepressant prescriptions as a proxy for depressive episodes over eight years. For recurrent episodes in the subgroup model, consumers of fatty fish had an odds ratio of 0.48 (95% CI 0.31–0.74) among nonsmokers and 3.63 (1.00–13.2) among smokers, compared with nonconsumers in the respective groups. The broad smoker interval and possible residual confounding limit the subgroup comparison. Single episodes had no clear association. The exposure was not randomly assigned and the endpoint differs from graded depression severity.

Claim 0874. Trout was included among the foods grouped by the exposure definition. The aggregate estimate, differing signs by smoking subgroup and absent symptom and preparation specificity leave the frozen culinary Parkinson outcome open.
