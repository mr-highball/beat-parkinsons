Gonlachanvit S, Fongkam P, Wittayalertpanya S, Kullavanijaya P. Red chili induces rectal hypersensitivity in healthy humans. Aliment Pharmacol Ther. 2007;26(4):617–625. PMID17661765. DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2036.2007.03396.x.
Sources https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17661765/ and the article reproduced in https://doi.nrct.go.th/ListDoi/Download/548432?Resolve_DOI=10.14457%2FCU.res.2007.114.
Eighteen healthy adults underwent a double-blind randomized crossover of oral placebo capsules with intravenous saline, 5 g/day ground Capsicum frutescens in capsules for three days with intravenous saline, and chili with 1 mg intravenous granisetron. The washout was seven days. The article identifies 1.44 mg of capsaicin per 5 g powder. It reports first, moderate and severe rectal urgency pressure thresholds of 18 ± 0.9, 24 ± 1.2 and 38 ± 1.5 mmHg after chili compared with 22 ± 0.9, 31 ± 1.3 and 45 ± 1.4 mmHg after placebo (p<.01). No participant reached a pain threshold within the distensions evaluated. This urgency assay and the Thai IBS trials measure distinct populations and endpoints.
