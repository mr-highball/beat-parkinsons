Title: Effect of grapefruit juice on the pharmacokinetics of nilotinib in healthy participants
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19948946/
Publisher: https://accp1.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1177/0091270009336137
PMID: 19948946
DOI: 10.1177/0091270009336137
Publication date: 2009-11-30
Date basis: NLM electronic article date before the February 2010 issue. The current Wiley platform also displays a March 2013 date.
Source depth: Original abstract and publisher method excerpts. Complete result tables and registration identity remain open.
Format: Factual digest of the inspected primary material

In a crossover study of 21 healthy men, participants received a single dose of nilotinib with double-strength grapefruit juice in one period and with water in another. Compared with water, the juice condition increased peak serum nilotinib concentration by 60% and total exposure by 29%. Time to peak and elimination half-life showed no reported difference. Headache and vomiting were among the reported adverse events and had similar apparent frequency between conditions.

This is a drug exposure study in healthy volunteers. It tests concentrated juice rather than raw fruit and supplies no resting tremor, hallucination frequency or Parkinson outcome data. A separate Georgetown Parkinson protocol, NCT02954978, excluded grapefruit juice as an interaction concern and was not a comparison of juice intake. The crossover and the Parkinson trial are distinct evidence families.
