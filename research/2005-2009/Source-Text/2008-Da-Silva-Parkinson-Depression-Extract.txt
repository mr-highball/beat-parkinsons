Title: Depression in Parkinson's disease: a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled pilot study of omega-3 fatty-acid supplementation
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18485485/
PMID: 18485485
DOI: 10.1016/j.jad.2008.03.008
First publication: 2008-05-15, as indexed by Europe PMC.
Coverage: Factual extract from the original Methods, Results and figure captions.

The pilot enrolled 31 people with Parkinson disease and a major depressive episode; 29 completed three months. Allocation to fish oil or mineral oil was stratified by existing antidepressant use. The primary outcome was MADRS. CGI and Beck Depression Inventory were secondary outcomes.
After supplementation the four-group tests favored fish oil for MADRS (p=.01) and CGI (p=.02). The post-treatment Beck comparison was inconclusive (p=.26), despite improvements within all four groups. Pooled MADRS response of at least 50% was reported in 42% of fish-oil participants and 6% of mineral-oil participants, with two-sided p=.075 and one-sided p=.04. Preserve both tests and the completer analysis.
The comparison capsules contained mineral oil. The trial does not assign sunflower oil. Original article copy: https://www.academia.edu/13962282/Depression_in_Parkinsons_disease_A_double_blind_randomized_placebo_controlled_pilot_study_of_omega_3_fatty_acid_supplementation
