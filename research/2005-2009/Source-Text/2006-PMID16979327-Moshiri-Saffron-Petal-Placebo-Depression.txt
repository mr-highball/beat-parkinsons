Title: Crocus sativus L. (petal) in the treatment of mild-to-moderate depression: A double-blind, randomized and placebo-controlled trial
Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0944711306001279

Publication date: 2006-09-18
Date basis: Original online journal record.
Reviewed: 2026-11-26
Text coverage: Distilled original methods, enrollment, follow-up and Tables 1 and 2.

The single-center study recruited adults with major depression at Roozbeh Psychiatric Hospital from July 2004 to March 2006. It randomly assigned 40 participants to capsules of dried ethanol extract of Crocus sativus petals or placebo for six weeks. Thirty-six completed follow-up (1 withdrawal with petal extract and 3 with placebo). Analysis used last observation carried forward. Mean HAM-D change was −14.01 (SD 5.53) with petal extract and −5.05 (SD 4.63) with placebo. The reported comparison of changes is t=5.59, p<.0001, and group-by-time p<.001. Tremor appeared on the adverse-event checklist in 3 versus 1 participants (p=.60). This incidental count does not measure Parkinson tremor. The preparation, diagnosis and short follow-up limit transfer to eating ground stigma for Parkinson depression.

The 2005 stigma study used the same center and a similar design with a different plant part. Its original dates are January 2002 to March 2004. This report recruited from July 2004 to March 2006.
