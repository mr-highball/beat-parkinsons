Nocturnal tongue angioedema after aniseed liqueur
Source: https://www.jiaci.org/issues/vol17issue06/10.pdf
PMID: 18088024
Coverage: factual extract from case narrative and immunoassays

V. Gázquez García, P. Gaig Jané and B. Bartolomé Zavala. J Investig Allergol Clin Immunol 17(6):406–408, 2007.
The patient recalled five episodes at dawn over two years. The final two included difficulty swallowing and breathing. The episodes coincided with aniseed liqueur use according to the subsequent history. Alcohol and recent aspirin were documented as coexposures, and the patient later tolerated aspirin. Skin prick testing was positive to aniseed. Seed extract bound patient IgE on immunoblot, whereas neither of two liqueur extracts showed a specific band. The patient remained asymptomatic during avoidance. The report describes episodic tongue swelling and airway or swallowing symptoms, without measured speech intelligibility, Parkinson status, or ground-anise food exposure. Nocturnal timing alone gives no parasomnia end point.
