Text coverage: selected facts paraphrased from the original study, intervention methods and Table 3.

The dietary study enrolled 92 students, including a randomly selected subsample of 20 students aged 24–27 in the celery leaf intervention. The preparation is described as leaf from supermarket celery. Participants consumed 2 g/kg body weight of celery leaf after a three-day apigenin- and luteolin-free diet. The study reports bread meals on the test day. In Table 3 the mean plasma apigenin concentration was 190.41 nmol/L (SD 98.54) at 7 h and it was below the 2.32 nmol/L detection limit at baseline and 28 h. The text gives a mean time to peak of 7.70 h (SD 0.82), an AUC of 89.02 min·µmol/L (SD 40.57), and an individual maximum of 396.25 nmol/L. The paper measured circulating flavonoids with no Parkinson cohort, levodopa dosing, or dyskinesia measurement. Plasma preparation included hydrolysis. The measurements describe recovered apigenin rather than an unbound plasma or brain concentration. Cao Table 3 (p253) and Zhao 2008 Table 1 (p447) give identical values across all 35 displayed entries at the seven time points. Both studies also give the same mean peak time, area under the curve, detection limit and maximum. Cao describes 20 participants and Zhao describes 10. Treat the numbers as a single reported plasma profile with unresolved participant provenance. Neither report tests raw stalk or Parkinson resting tremor.
