# Portuguese-language Parkinson swallowing studies, 2005–2009

## Barium-coated cracker and three contrast textures

Bigal and colleagues reported 25 adults with Parkinson disease at Hoehn and Yahr stages II–IV and 82 older controls referred with swallowing complaints and without neurological disorders. The methods identify thin barium contrast, thicker undiluted contrast, barium with added thickener, and a cracker or cookie coated in barium paste. Table 1 records 19 Parkinson participants reporting choking. Tables 1–2 allocate eight reports to liquids, two to solids and nine to any consistency. The discussion instead describes 17 as choking only with liquids, so the paper gives inconsistent liquid-only counts. Table 4 gives videofluoroscopic aspiration in 10 of 25 Parkinson participants versus 21 of 82 controls, pooled across test boluses. The cracker recipe and a baked-potato exposure are absent. The original issue is dated August 2007 and the publisher's online citation uses 2012. ## Applesauce, cracker and milk in the original Parkinson swallowing questionnaire validation

Manor and colleagues' June 22, 2007 report clinically assessed 57 people with Parkinson disease using applesauce, cracker and water. The 33 participants who reported swallowing difficulty also underwent endoscopy with colored applesauce, a cracker taken in three bites, and milk given by spoon and cup. The clinicians checked for signs including coughing and choking. Endoscopy was classified as abnormal if penetration or aspiration occurred. Table 3 gives 41 abnormal physical assessments across the full group, pooling clinical and endoscopic findings. The article gives no event count for a specific food and uses no potato bolus. This is the Israeli study that developed the questionnaire later adapted in Brazil.
