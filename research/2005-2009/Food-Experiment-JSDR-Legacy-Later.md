# Primary sources traced from the 2008 and 2009 Japanese swallowing proceedings

MedicalOnline exposes titles, author lists and pages for the 14th and 15th Japanese Society of Dysphagia Rehabilitation meetings. The abstract bodies linked from those issues require access. The society’s annual-meeting archive establishes the dates and locations. Later reports below identify related work where the original conference body is unavailable. [2008 issue](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/archive/search?issue=3&jo=cb5dvsph&vo=12&ye=2008) · [2009 issue](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/archive/search?issue=3&jo=cb5dvsph&vo=13&ye=2009) · [Society meeting archive](https://www.jsdr.or.jp/meeting/meeting_annual.html)

## 2008 black pepper oil inhalation in pediatric tube feeding

Kobayashi and colleagues’ conference title describes improved feeding or swallowing in two children after exposure to the odor of black pepper oil. The original body is gated. An April 26, 2008 journal study by Munakata and colleagues shares four authors and enrolled ten children with neurological disorders and long-term tube feeding. Five of eight who continued the scent exposure before meals for three months increased oral intake. Two discontinued because of cough or because the family found the smell intolerable. The study provides no Parkinson subgroup or comparison for eating cracked pepper. Participant overlap with the two-case conference report remains unverified. A later conference title by a different team describes increased fluid intake in three people with severe motor and intellectual disabilities after black pepper oil use. The accessible title does not give their diagnoses, route, amount or swallowing scores. [2008 presentation I-5-14, p. 308](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2008/001203/122&name=0308-0308j) · [2008 primary journal report](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tjem/214/4/214_4_327/_article/-char/en) · [2009 presentation I-P6-7, p. 412](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2009/001303/351&name=0412-0412j)

## 2008 expiratory muscle training and thin barium in Parkinson

Pitts and colleagues enrolled ten men with Parkinson’s and prior videofluoroscopic penetration or aspiration in four weeks of expiratory muscle strength training. Penetration–aspiration scores decreased after training during a sequential 30 mL thin barium task. Cough volume acceleration also increased. The single-group intervention was respiratory training. The barium was a diagnostic stimulus. PubMed dates the original article to November 24, 2008 online. The May 2009 *Chest* issue and the Japanese abstract listing carry the same title in English and Japanese and the same six author names. [PubMed publication history, PMID 19029430](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19029430/) · [Original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5931232/) · [2009 Japanese listing, p. 243](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2009/001303/012&name=0243-0243j)

## 2008 jaw and tongue coordination in Parkinson

Umemoto, Tsuboi and Kikuta presented a study of jaw–tongue coordination and feeding or swallowing on September 13, 2008. The university record corroborates the date. Their later journal study provides results for a standardized barium gelatin jelly. The conference cohort, preparation and participant overlap remain unverified. [2008 presentation I-6-3, p. 313](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2008/001203/132&name=0313-0313j) · [University researcher record](https://kenkyusha-db.fukuoka-u.ac.jp/search/detail?lang=ja&systemId=fdc8db794cbd0c0c520e17560c007669) · [2010 report](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-JSDR-Legacy-Later.md#2010-tongue-pressure-and-barium-gelatin-jelly-in-parkinson)

## 2009 Parkinson swallowing after rhythmic cueing and deep brain stimulation

Nozaki and colleagues presented a multicenter study of metronome-paced swallowing training. A later meeting abstract reports a Parkinson-only jelly comparison. A separate 2009 abstract from Kitashima and colleagues describes chewing and swallowing after deep brain stimulation. The Fukuoka team published a later case series with videofluoroscopic data. The 2009 abstract bodies and participant overlap with the later reports remain unverified. Sugishita and colleagues’ same-session presentation about stimulation on versus off has a different investigator group. ## 2009 cooked banana and rice or bread texture

Fujimura’s presentation title specifies preparing a cooked banana for people with dysphagia. The source verifies the named preparation and leaves the cooking method, diagnostic mix and outcomes open. Kawamura and colleagues’ separate presentation concerns rice and bread texture in obstructive choking. An agency report by overlapping investigators presents laboratory texture measurements for rice and bread. The conference body and exact overlap with the agency report are unverified. Neither accessible source reports a banana, rice or bread effect on a Parkinson symptom. [Cooked banana I-6-5, p. 315](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2009/001303/153&name=0315-0315j) · [JST bibliographic record](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=201002286400818731) · [Rice and bread I-P6-23, p. 420](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2009/001303/367&name=0420-0420j) · [Primary agency report](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/topics/bukyoku/iyaku/syoku-anzen/chissoku/dl/04.pdf)

## 2009 capsiate and swallow reflex in older people

Wiley first published Yamasaki and colleagues’ letter on December 21, 2009. The full article in a Japanese health ministry grant annex describes 12 older patients recovering from aspiration pneumonia. The investigators administered 1 mL of room-temperature liquid to the pharynx through a nasal catheter, using blinded randomized administration of distilled water or capsiate at 1, 10 or 100 nM. The capsiate was extracted from dried CH-19 Sweet pepper. Mean swallowing-reflex latency was 16.3 ± 5.1 seconds with water and 11.0 ± 4.2, 6.9 ± 1.6 and 6.1 ± 1.2 seconds at the three concentrations. The authors reported a concentration-dependent shortening and no pungency in the taste test. They did not give underlying diagnoses or a Parkinson subgroup, and the study measured an acute reflex with extracted capsiate. The 2010 presentation shares investigators, with participant overlap unverified.
