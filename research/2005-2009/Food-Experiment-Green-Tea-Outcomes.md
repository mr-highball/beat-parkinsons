# Green tea, fatigue and sensory or handwriting comparisons

## Asthenia recurred after resuming a green tea and peppermint infusion

Martínez-Sierra and colleagues reported jaundice and asthenia in a 26-year-old who had consumed two daily infusions of a product made from 75% dried green tea leaves and 25% peppermint for four months. Symptoms and laboratory values improved after she stopped and returned about two weeks after unintentional resumption. Liver biopsy supported toxic liver injury. The exposure was a blend, and the authors considered both plants as possible contributors. This documents a symptom during probable liver injury, without a fatigue scale or a Parkinson comparison. [Original Spanish case](https://static.elsevier.es/multimedia/00257753/0000012700000003/v0_201307291744/13090268/v0_201307291746/es/2v127n03a13090268pdf001.pdf) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16828006/)

## Isolated caffeine and digitized handwriting in healthy adults

Tucha and colleagues compared placebo with three doses of isolated caffeine in a crossover involving 20 healthy habitual caffeine consumers. The primary abstract reports greater writing fluency, higher maximum pen velocity and higher maximum accelerations at 4.5 mg/kg versus placebo. These changes concern how the pen moved. The accessible abstract supplies no letter-size effect, and the university catalog lists no deposited full text. The study used a single constituent outside Parkinson disease. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17010462/) · [University publication catalog](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/view/people/Tucha%3D3AOliver%3D3A%3D3A.type.html)

## Caffeine consumption and odor identification in Parkinson relatives

Siderowf and colleagues surveyed 173 first-degree relatives of Parkinson patients using the 40-item UPSIT. Relative to reported intake below one cup of caffeinated beverages a day, adjusted scores were 2.6 points higher (95% CI 0.5 to 4.5) for two to three cups and 3.7 points higher (0.6 to 6.7) for four or more. Neither a green-tea-only intake estimate nor an assigned exposure was reported in the primary abstract. The sample consisted of relatives and the association may reflect differences other than diet. [Primary article abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17876851/)

## Asthenia during hepatitis after several green tea infusions

Vanstraelen and colleagues described a 76-year-old long-term tea drinker with jaundice, asthenia and weight loss. He reported six to seven cups daily and several preparations, including Sencha Fuji, Gyokuro and Lung Chin Imperial. The authors classified the tea–liver injury relationship as probable. Clinical and biochemical recovery followed withdrawal over seven months. The report gives no Parkinson diagnosis or separate graded fatigue course and cannot identify a single tea variety as responsible. [Original Belgian report](https://www.ageb.be/Articles/Volume%2071%20%282008%29/Fasc4/08-vanstraelen-.pdf) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19317284/)

## Aqueous green tea infusion and a distinct hepatitis presentation

Amariles and colleagues reported abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting and other acute symptoms in a 43-year-old who had used aqueous green tea infusions for constipation for about eight months. The authors classified the mixed liver injury as probably related. The case lists neither fatigue nor asthenia at presentation and provides no constipation response measurement. Separate it from the 2006 and 2008 reports in this bucket. [Original Spanish article](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/3659/365961792011.pdf) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19775582/)

## Existing smell impairment and green tea preparation behavior

Seo and Hummel studied 90 women in three groups of 30. Older participants with pre-existing olfactory dysfunction rated green tea odor and flavor as less intense and added more tea powder to optimize their drink than younger normosmic participants. Table 5 reports an additional 0.42 versus 0.16 g of powder in those two groups. Sniffin’ Sticks scores were positively associated with sniffing frequency for green tea. Smell status was a grouping factor and not a post-ingestion change. These relationships identify a possible reverse path from existing smell function to tea choice or preparation. [Primary paper and original Table 5](https://www.academia.edu/12755994/Effects_of_olfactory_dysfunction_on_sensory_evaluation_and_preparation_of_foods) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19619597/)
