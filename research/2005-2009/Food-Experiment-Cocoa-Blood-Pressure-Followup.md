# Cocoa, chocolate, and blood pressure outside Parkinson’s disease

## 2006 Zutphen Elderly Study

Buijsse and colleagues estimated cocoa intake from the dietary histories of 470 older men in the Netherlands who were free of chronic disease at baseline. In adjusted cross-sectional comparisons, the highest cocoa intake tertile had systolic pressure 3.7 mm Hg lower (95% CI −7.1 to −0.3) and diastolic pressure 2.1 mm Hg lower (95% CI −4.0 to −0.2) than the lowest tertile. The study pooled cocoa from different foods and also followed mortality. It provides no estimate of how eating plain dark chocolate changes orthostatic lightheadedness in Parkinson’s. [Primary report, PMID 16505260](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16505260/) · [Journal full article](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/409867)

## 2007 low-dose dark chocolate trial

Taubert and colleagues randomized 44 adults with untreated upper-range prehypertension or stage 1 hypertension to 6.3 g per day of dark chocolate containing 30 mg of polyphenols or matching white chocolate for 18 weeks. The covariate-adjusted contrasts were −2.9 mm Hg (95% CI −3.9 to −2.0) for systolic pressure and −1.9 mm Hg (95% CI −2.7 to −1.1) for diastolic pressure. This trial assessed clinic blood pressure and biochemical outcomes in participants selected for elevated pressure. [Primary report and Results](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/207783) · [Primary report, PMID 17609490](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17609490/)

## 2008 high-polyphenol dark chocolate crossover

Grassi and colleagues studied 19 hypertensive adults with impaired glucose tolerance. They compared 100 g per day of high-polyphenol dark chocolate with an isocaloric portion of flavanol-free white chocolate for 15 days per phase. Mean clinic systolic and diastolic pressure fell by 3.82 and 3.92 mm Hg in the dark chocolate phase. Mean 24-hour ambulatory systolic and diastolic pressure fell by 4.52 and 4.17 mm Hg. These four changes were reported at P < 0.0001, and the white chocolate phase showed no corresponding decrease. The ambulatory averages did not measure standing symptoms. [Primary report, PMID 18716168](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18716168/) · [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jn/138.9.1671)

## 2008 cocoa flavanol beverage in medicated diabetes

Balzer and colleagues compared flavanol-rich cocoa with lower-flavanol cocoa three times daily for 30 days in 41 patients receiving medication for diabetes. The rich beverage provided 321 mg of flavanols per serving. The investigators reported improved brachial artery flow-mediated dilation with the higher-flavanol drink and no detectable effect on blood pressure. The vascular endpoint and beverage composition differ from the dark chocolate trials. [Primary report, PMID 18510961](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18510961/) · [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2008.01.059)

The frozen plain-dark-chocolate statements concern anxiety (`0100`), low voice volume (`0660`), and speech intelligibility (`0703`) in Parkinson’s. These four cardiovascular studies provide no symptom-specific estimate for those statements.
