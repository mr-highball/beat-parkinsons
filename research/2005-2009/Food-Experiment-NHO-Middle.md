# Japanese Parkinson nutrition and swallowing reports, 2005–2009

## 2005 temporary nutrition and return to oral intake

Yamaoka and colleagues at NHO Higashinagoya Hospital reported a woman with Hoehn and Yahr stage 4 Parkinson disease at the August 20, 2005 inaugural Japanese neuromuscular swallowing and nutrition meeting. She developed dysphagia at 67, reduced her intake and took an elemental formula by mouth. Aspiration pneumonia and heart failure at 68 temporarily required ventilation. After ventilator weaning, her nutrition progressed from central IV to gastrostomy feeds. Swallowing subsequently improved, oral intake resumed at two months, and the gastrostomy tube was removed. The abstract gives no formula brand or ingredient composition, item-specific assessment, or control for recovery from the acute illness. [Primary conference abstract](https://www.jsdnnm.com/congress-nagasaki/#abst-3), [society chronology](https://www.jsdnnm.com/about_jsdnnm/).

Two other abstracts at the same meeting describe two Parkinson cases recovering oral feeding and function after impaired consciousness during team-based swallowing training, and a Parkinson/parkinsonian syndromes chewing-training assessment. The online reports provide no test food in either case series and no numerical result for the chewing study. [Team-based rehabilitation abstract](https://www.jsdnnm.com/congress-nagasaki/#abst-5), [chewing-training abstract](https://www.jsdnnm.com/congress-nagasaki/#abst-11).

## 2009 gelatin test bolus and drooling in Parkinson disease

Umemoto and colleagues first presented this study on June 20, 2009 at the Japanese Society of Gerodontology meeting in Yokohama. The 2009 journal report was posted to J-STAGE on October 20, 2010. Sixteen patients with Hoehn and Yahr stage III–V Parkinson disease swallowed a 5 g barium-containing gelatin jelly bolus during videofluoroscopy. Higher drooling scores correlated with longer oropharyngeal transit time (r=.659, p=.011). Longer transit also correlated with worse oral-phase swallowing scores (r=.540, p=.037) and slower tongue movement (r=−.522, p=.046). All participants used the same test bolus, so these comparisons describe differences among patients and provide no gelatin effect or comparison with another food. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.11259/jsg.24.306), [primary methods, PDF p. 2](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsg/24/3/24_3_306/_pdf/-char/ja#page=2), [results, PDF p. 3](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsg/24/3/24_3_306/_pdf/-char/ja#page=3), [presentation date, PDF p. 5](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsg/24/3/24_3_306/_pdf/-char/ja#page=5).
