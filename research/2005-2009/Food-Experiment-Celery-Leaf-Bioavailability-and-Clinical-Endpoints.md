# Human apigenin exposure after eating celery leaves

## Oral celery leaves and plasma apigenin in healthy adults

In 2008, Zhao and colleagues followed ten healthy adults after a single serving of green celery leaves. Market leaves were separated, washed, surface-dried and held at 4 °C. A separate oven-dried portion was used to estimate leaf flavonoid content. Participants ate the leaves with unsweetened whole-wheat bread. The reported mean time to peak plasma apigenin was 7.70 hours. Levels fell below the 2.32 nmol/L detection limit by 28 hours.

A Harbin Medical University group with a shared author published another celery leaf substudy online in 2009, listing 20 healthy students. Table 3 reports a mean plasma apigenin level of 190.41 nmol/L at 7 hours. The authors report the same peak timing (7.70 ± 0.82 hours), 0–28-hour AUC (89.02 ± 40.57 min·µmol/L), individual maximum (396.25 nmol/L) and detection limit as in 2008. Participant counts differ and the relationship between samples is unresolved. Keep the two reports together as a potentially overlapping study family. Both measured apigenin pharmacokinetics. They give no result for levodopa exposure or Parkinson dyskinesia.
