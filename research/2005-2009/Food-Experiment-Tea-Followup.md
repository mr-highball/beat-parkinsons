# Green tea polyphenols in a delayed-start Parkinson’s trial

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted NCT00461942 on April 18, 2007. The trial registered motor progression as its primary outcome and cognition, mood, and quality of daily life as secondary outcomes. The registry lists completion in March 2009 and has no posted summary results. [Local registration](Source-Text/Trials/NCT00461942.txt) · [Official registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00461942)

Chan and colleagues presented results in December 2009. The project funder reports that 410 untreated participants at 32 sites received oral green tea polyphenols or placebo in a delayed-start comparison. The report describes a more favorable total UPDRS change with polyphenols at six months, no group difference at 12 months, and slightly more insomnia with polyphenols. Fatigue was absent from the listed outcomes and the public outcome summary. The proceedings entry was located by DOI, and the full conference abstract was unavailable during review. The trial used a concentrated preparation and does not resolve [0948, brewed green tea and fatigue](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0948.txt). [Conference proceedings P2.204](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1353-8020(09)70555-3) · [Funder’s final outcome summary](https://www.michaeljfox.org/grant/ability-slow-disease-progression-and-safety-and-tolerability-green-tea-polyphenols-early)

## Black tea as a breakfast option during breath testing

The Charlett study includes black tea, coffee or water in a standardized breath-testing context. It describes resting and postural tremor as separate clinical observations. The published analysis concerns immune and gastrointestinal variables, with no assigned tea comparison or measured post-tea rest-tremor result. [Primary article, Methods and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2795757/) · [Local factual text](Source-Text/2009-PMID19941660-Tea-Breath-Test-Extract.txt)
