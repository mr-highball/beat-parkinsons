# Basil and parsley evidence

## Raw basil in pesto and immediate allergic reactions

The 2007 case report describes two adults who developed immediate respiratory or oropharyngeal symptoms after homemade pesto with raw basil. Both reported tolerating the other listed ingredients after the reaction. Both reacted to basil on prick testing. The case series records allergic throat and respiratory findings without an assessment of speech intelligibility or a Parkinson cohort. [Primary case report](https://www.jiaci.org/issues/vol17issue05/11_14.pdf)

## Parsley seed extract and arterial pressure in rats

De Campos and colleagues published in 2009. The aqueous seed extract lowered arterial pressure and increased urinary sodium excretion in anesthetized Wistar rats. This tested blood pressure under anesthesia and used seeds. No standing pressure, orthostatic symptoms or dried parsley leaf were tested. [Primary paper](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbfar/a/Hf44qBpCVJFyPZf6ZBjqZLw/)

## Dried parsley and asparagus in hypertension surveillance

Chrubasik, Droste and Black reported six weeks of surveillance in 163 patients receiving a commercial mixture of pulverized dried parsley leaf and asparagus root. The authors found no clinically useful antihypertensive effect and report seven early withdrawals for renal complaints. The intervention was a combination product and the abstract gives no standing pressure, orthostatic symptom result or Parkinson cohort. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19274702/)
