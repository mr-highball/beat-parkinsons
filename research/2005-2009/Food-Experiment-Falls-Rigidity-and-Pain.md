# Corn oil procedure pain and pepper odor testing

## Colonoscopy lubricant and procedure pain

A prospective colonoscopy study in 346 patients compared usual lubricant with the addition of corn oil through the endoscope. The primary abstract reports lower procedure pain with the added lubricant. The exposure acts locally during the procedure, with no comparison of ingested corn oil and no evidence about refining grade or Parkinson pain. ## Baseline olfaction in the black pepper oil swallowing trial

The 105-person post-stroke study randomized 35 participants per arm to inhaled black pepper oil, lavender or odorless control. Olfactory identification in Table 1 is a baseline characteristic, with values 2.2 ± 2.0 for pepper oil, 1.7 ± 2.3 for lavender and 2.1 ± 2.4 for the odorless comparator. The original follow-up results examine swallowing or SPECT outcomes. There is no comparison of changes in clinical olfaction, and the oil is inhaled.
