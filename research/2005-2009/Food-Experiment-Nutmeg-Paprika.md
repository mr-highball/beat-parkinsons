# Nutmeg and paprika evidence

## Oral nutmeg extract and depression-like behavior in mice

Dhingra and Sharma reported reduced immobility in forced swim and tail suspension after oral n-hexane nutmeg extract, with no significant spontaneous locomotor change. The experiments used healthy mice and behavioral assays of stress responses. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16579733/)

## Capsaicin and motor activity after reserpine or levodopa in rats

The 2006 animal study reports that systemic capsaicin suppressed spontaneous motor activity and the high motor activity elicited by levodopa in reserpine-treated rats. Reserpine alone already markedly suppressed activity and capsaicin did not significantly change activity in that condition. The measured motor activity provides a different endpoint from rated clinical dyskinesia. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16806299/)

## Oral nutmeg solvent extracts and activity in a mouse tetrad assay

First online August 22, 2009. Healthy mice were assigned nutmeg seed fractions prepared with methanol, hexane or dichloromethane. At 500 mg/kg by mouth, all three preparations increased general locomotor activity. For dichloromethane, the lower oral dose increased activity (post hoc q 4.71, p < .001) and 1,000 mg/kg did not show a significant effect. A delipidated methanolic fraction gave an inconclusive test of locomotion (omnibus p = .30). The tetrad captures spontaneous activity and other pharmacological behaviors in healthy animals. It supplies no culinary ground-spice comparison or bradykinesia result in Parkinson disease.
