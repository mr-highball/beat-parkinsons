# Tomato preparations in Parkinson models, 2005–2009

## Red Setter and High Carotene tomato feed

Di Matteo and colleagues randomized male Fischer 344 rats to standard chow or chow containing 5% freeze-dried Red Setter or High Carotene tomato by dry weight for 14 days. The full chapter describes harvesting ripe tomatoes, freezing, chopping, homogenizing and freeze-drying. It specifies no cooking step. The reported preparations were incorporated into chow. [Publisher chapter](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-211-92660-4_28) · [Full chapter copy, pp. 333–341](https://www.academia.edu/4670222/The_Nigrostriatal_Pathway_Axonal_Collateralization_and_Compartmental_Specificity) · [Local factual extract](Source-Text/2009-DiMatteo-Tomato-6OHDA.txt)

Figure 2 shows ipsilateral relative to contralateral striatal dopamine depletion of 86.5% with standard chow, 86.2% with High Carotene and 56.0% with Red Setter. Red Setter differed from standard chow (p = .006) and High Carotene (p = .005). DOPAC depletion gave 85.6%, 83.0% and 58.9% respectively, with Red Setter differing from standard chow (p = .018) and High Carotene (p = .026). The figures give n = 9 per group. In Fig. 1, High Carotene increased absolute dopamine and DOPAC on the unlesioned side. The relative depletion and absolute unlesioned values answer different questions. The full chapter measures striatal biochemistry and plasma carotenoids. It reports no gait, stride or executive task.
