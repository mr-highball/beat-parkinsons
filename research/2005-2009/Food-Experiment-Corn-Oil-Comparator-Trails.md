# Oral corn oil comparator in an equine stride experiment

## Walk and trot stride with oral corn oil in horses

Woodward and colleagues paired 18 Arabian horses on age, sex, baseline stride, and lameness for mature animals and then randomized within pairs. The 75-day comparator included 49 g of corn oil. The other diet included 5.95 g of stabilized omega-3 fatty acids and a carrier giving 19.4 g total supplemental fat. Table 5 gives mean walking strides of 168 to 172 cm for the corn oil group and 161 to 166 cm for the omega-3 group, with no clear between-group difference. Mean trot strides moved from 238 to 240 cm and 228 to 243 cm, respectively. The report gives p=.08 for the comparative trend and p<.01 for the omega-3 group's within-arm increase. Oil grade was unspecified.

The feeding comparison measures stride directly for claim 0324 and distinguishes walking from trotting. It has a different species, health context and simultaneous dietary change from the Parkinson question.
