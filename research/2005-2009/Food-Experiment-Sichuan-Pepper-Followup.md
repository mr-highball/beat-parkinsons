# Sichuan pepper follow-up

## Immediate reactions after eating Sichuan pepper

A February 8, 2006 Chinese case report described two patients with acute allergic reactions after eating food containing *huajiao*. Specific IgE testing was reported as strongly positive. One patient had lip swelling, altered consciousness and blood pressure of 8/6 kPa, approximately 60/45 mmHg, during the reaction. The paper does not authenticate a botanical species or characterize the culinary preparation. Acute allergic hypotension and oral swelling are relevant adverse-event context for 0216 and 0137. Standing blood pressure and Parkinson’s voice volume were outside the report. [Primary report and case descriptions](https://doi.org/10.11569/wcjd.v14.i4.457)

## Resting blood pressure after daikenchuto

Takayama and colleagues’ December 2009 examiner-blinded randomized crossover compared oral daikenchuto, a different Kampo formula, and water in healthy adults. Table 2 and the main Results section report no significant change in blood pressure relative to resting values after either formula. Daikenchuto increased superior mesenteric artery flow relative to the comparator formula and water. Daikenchuto contains Japanese *Zanthoxylum piperitum* with processed ginger, ginseng and maltose. The measured pressures were taken at rest and the study did not evaluate postural lightheadedness or ground Sichuan pepper. This is contextual evidence for 0216.
