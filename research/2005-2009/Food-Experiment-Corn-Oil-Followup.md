# Corn oil and Parkinson’s symptom follow-up

## OmegAD apathy outcomes with a corn oil comparator

Freund-Levi and colleagues published the neuropsychiatric analysis online on June 21, 2007 (2008 issue, PMID 17582225). OmegAD randomized 204 participants with mild to moderate Alzheimer disease to omega-3 oil or a corn oil comparator for six months. The original methods describe four 1 g comparator capsules daily, each containing 0.6 g linoleic acid and the same 4 mg vitamin E as an active capsule. The corn oil refining grade was unspecified. In Table 4, mean Neuropsychiatric Inventory apathy among participants with a positive score at each visit moved from 5.1 to 5.8 in the comparator arm and from 5.3 to 5.4 in the omega-3 arm. The six-month contrast had p=.13. Changing item-positive denominators limit interpretation of the descriptive means as individual worsening. The placebo group switched to omega-3 after six months.

The measured apathy endpoint and oral oil comparator make this useful for claim 0862. Alzheimer disease, the active comparator, and variable endpoint denominators leave the proposed Parkinson apathy reduction unestablished. The 2006 original trial and this report describe the same cohort. ## OmegAD depression and nighttime behavior

The same 2007 OmegAD neuropsychiatric report gives full-sample mean MADRS scores of 1.9 at baseline and 1.6 at six months in the corn-oil group, compared with 1.8 and 1.5 under omega-3. Table 3 reports p = .49 for the first six-month contrast. The authors describe a different result in a small APOE ε4 noncarrier subgroup (p = .005), favoring omega-3. Table 4 reports a nighttime-behavior comparison of p = .69 at six months. Among participants who had a positive nighttime score at each visit, the corn-oil means were 4.9 and 3.7, with denominators 33 and 19. This measure groups nighttime behaviors and does not estimate insomnia severity. The same trial had no corn-oil-free comparison, no reported refining grade, and an Alzheimer rather than Parkinson population.
