# Thyme and clove evidence, 2005–2009

## Ground clove gel and dental needle pain

Alqareer and colleagues tested a glycerin gel prepared with ground clove in 73 adult volunteers assigned to clove or benzocaine, with corresponding placebo applied to the opposite side of the mouth. After topical treatment, they assessed immediate pain from needle insertion. Both active gels yielded lower mean pain scores than their placebos (reported p = .005). The study reported no statistically clear clove-versus-benzocaine difference, with no equivalence design. The ground spice matches the ingredient form in claim 0956 more closely than isolated eugenol does. Topical dental anesthesia and Parkinson’s pain following ingestion are different outcomes and routes. [Primary article, Methods and Results](https://hortus-medicus.ch/wp-content/uploads/Girofle_benzocaine.pdf) · [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16530911/)

## Clove oil cream and chronic anal fissure healing

Elwakeel and colleagues first published June 15, 2007. The single-blind randomized trial compared six weeks of topical 1% clove-oil cream (30 participants) with conventional care including stool softeners and 5% lignocaine cream (25 participants). The primary abstract reports fissure healing at three months in 60% versus 12% (p < .001) and reduced anorectal pressures in the oil group. It does not report a pain score or an arm-level pain difference. This comparison addresses a topical oil and local fissure healing outside Parkinson disease.
