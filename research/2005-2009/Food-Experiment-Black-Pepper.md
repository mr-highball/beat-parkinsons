# Black pepper aroma in aspiration care

## 2008 Oita report on black pepper aroma patches

Kai and colleagues reported better bolus swallowing time and less choking during assisted meals with an aroma patch in people with current or past aspiration pneumonia. The abstract reports P < .01 and describes a combination with imidapril and amantadine. It gives no group sizes, effect estimates, allocation details or Parkinson subgroup. [Oita NST original, abstract 3](https://plaza.umin.ac.jp/~Oita-NST/siryou/pro8.pdf)

## Isolated piperine in rat paw and arthritis tests

Bang and colleagues published March 30, 2009, in volume 11, issue 2, article R49. Oral isolated piperine in a corn-oil vehicle was tested at 20 and 100 mg/kg in rat models of induced paw hyperalgesia and ankle arthritis. At 100 mg/kg, the authors reported greater tolerated paw pressure and fewer vocalizations during ankle movement; 20 mg/kg had little effect on the latter. Hind-limb weight distribution was a pain-related proxy. The work also tested cells from people with rheumatoid arthritis in vitro; it did not treat those people. These animal measures do not establish an effect of cracked black pepper on pain in Parkinson disease.
