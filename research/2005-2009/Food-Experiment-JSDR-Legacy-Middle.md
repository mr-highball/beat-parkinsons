## Food form and Parkinson severity in two 2005 presentations

At the September 2–3, 2005 meeting, Koichiro Matsuo, Toshiyuki Yamamoto and colleagues presented two adjacent reports on feeding and swallowing in Parkinson’s disease. Part 1 addresses the influence of food forms, and part 2 addresses association with disease severity. Both are on p. 369. The public index leaves the sample, individual foods, comparisons and findings unspecified. Fujita Health University lists an overlapping author team presenting swallow initiation during eating versus drinking at the October 2004 Dysphagia Research Society meeting. The collective volume is dated October 2005 and its publisher online date is March 18, 2006. The publisher requires subscription access. The available records do not establish participant overlap. [2005 part 1](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2005/000903/171&name=0369-0369j) · [2005 part 2](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2005/000903/172&name=0369-0369j) · [Fujita bibliography](https://www.fujita-hu.ac.jp/~rehabmed/ref_gyouseki/society/society00_04.html) · [2004 meeting proceedings record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00455-005-0056-3)

Saitoh, Matsuo, Fujii, Palmer and colleagues published a related food consistency study in February 2007. Their 15 participants were healthy. Videofluoroscopy compared liquid, solid, and mixed boluses. Part of the liquid frequently reached the hypopharynx before swallowing when the participant chewed or ate a mixed bolus. The study gives context on bolus transport in healthy adults. The gated Parkinson presentations remain the source to consult for patient outcomes. [Primary paper, PMID 17347905](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17347905/) · [Primary paper in government grant annex](https://mhlw-grants.niph.go.jp/system/files/2007/071031/200705030A/200705030A0003.pdf)

## Candy and dried squid during a Parkinson meal in 2006

At the September 8–9, 2006 meeting, Mihoko Mizuno and Hiroyuki Ōki of Kakeyu Hospital reported one Parkinson case in which oral transport of food improved when candy and dried squid were used as adjuncts during eating. This is the outcome stated in the presentation title on pp. 398–399, II-4-1. The accessible record leaves exposure sequence, food amounts, intervention details and the measurement of improvement unspecified. Independent effects of the candy and squid and any change in aspiration are unresolved. Exact-title, author, and institution searches did not recover a publicly readable abstract. Neither adjunct is a frozen ingredient. [Issue index](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/archive/search?issue=3&jo=cb5dvsph&vo=10&ye=2006) · [Individual record, subscriber access](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2006/001003/248&name=0398-0399j)

The same issue includes Yuri Watanabe and colleagues’ presentation on swallowing sliced bread, okaki rice crackers, and candy at a long-term care facility, p. 318, I-5-1. Its index gives neither diagnoses nor swallowing results. Naoko Yaji and colleagues describe one patient with disproportionately severe Parkinson dysphagia, p. 363, I-P3-11. Their public title gives no food or bolus. ## Aspiration during videofluoroscopy in parkinsonism in 2006

Yamamoto, Yoshitsugu Aoki, Yoko Kobayashi, Eiichi Saitoh and Miho Murata presented a study of aspiration during videofluoroscopic testing in parkinsonism, p. 410, II-5-6. The title alone does not give the Parkinson subgroup, bolus, or findings. A May 2007 presentation from an overlapping National Center team concerned prediction of aspiration risk in Lewy body disease. A 2010 paper from Yamamoto, Kobayashi and Murata includes 90 patients with idiopathic Parkinson’s and 45 with Lewy body dementia selected from patients admitted March 2004 to June 2007. It observed aspiration in 15 of 90 with Parkinson’s and 16 of 45 with Lewy body dementia during liquid videofluoroscopy. The recruitment dates and investigators make the paper a possible follow-up. Confirmation of cohort overlap and 2006 bolus composition requires the original abstract. [2006 presentation](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2006/001003/272&name=0410-0410j) · [2007 meeting record in JST grant report](https://projectdb.jst.go.jp/file/JST-PROJECT-08003916/JST_1111034_08003916_2007_%E6%88%B8%E7%94%B0_PER.pdf) · [2010 paper, PMID 20584622](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20584622/) · [2010 full paper in government grant annex](https://mhlw-grants.niph.go.jp/system/files/2010/103071/201024066A/201024066A0004.pdf)

## Tuber and meat-and-potato pastes in 2006 and 2007

Keiko Asano and colleagues’ 2006 presentation, p. 456, II-P2-18, concerns sensory evaluation of a paste mixed with a tuber. The public title uses the nonspecific Japanese word *imo* and does not identify the species, diagnosis, or clinical outcome. Affiliations include a child development center and a special needs dentistry department. Mizue Soeda, Ryoichi Yasui, Yoshie Yamagata and Jun Kayashita examined how ingredients affect the physical properties of a puréed meat-and-potato stew (*nikujaga*) on September 15, 2007, p. 371, II-P3-10. Their JST grant report confirms date and presentation identity. [Asano presentation](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2006/001003/373&name=0456-0456j) · [Soeda presentation](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2007/001103/360&name=0371-0371j) · [JST grant report, conference list](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/ja/file/KAKENHI-PROJECT-19500664/19500664seika.pdf)

A related 2008 university report by the same four researchers describes steam-oven cooking and food-processing the stew into a paste, with beef or pork and with or without 10% medium-chain triglyceride oil. Eight healthy assessors rated swallowing ease. Oil addition lowered adhesion by 45% for pork and 40% for beef. The beef paste was rated easier to swallow after oil addition, with no clear difference for pork. Freeze and reheat comparisons did not yield clear sensory differences. The report identifies a related 2008 journal article and meeting presentations. It does not establish that the entire 2007 protocol or sample was identical. This food-texture work gives no Parkinson-specific measurement or separately served baked potato result for `0290`. [University primary report](https://www.pu-hiroshima.ac.jp/uploaded/attachment/622.pdf) · [2008 journal contents](https://jushojisha.jp/ronbunichiran/) · [2008 follow-up meeting index](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/archive/search?issue=3&jo=cb5dvsph&vo=12&ye=2008)

## Jelly and juice during rhythmic swallowing training in Parkinson’s disease

Shuhei Sugishita and colleagues presented one Parkinson case at the September 14–15, 2007 meeting, pp. 324–325, II-4-8, and published a matching case report on August 31, 2008. A 66-year-old man at Hoehn and Yahr stage III had aspiration pneumonia in 2006. He underwent four weeks of indirect rehabilitation, a two-week withdrawal interval, then four weeks of metronome-cued swallowing training with 1.6% gelatin jelly. His medication and feeding allocation were held stable after training began. Test boluses were 3 mL of 50% juice or 1.6% gelatin jelly, presented twice per test. The article does not identify the fruit in the juice. [2007 presentation](https://mol.medicalonline.jp/journal/abstract?GoodsID=cb5dvsph/2007/001103/259&name=0324-0325j) · [2008 primary paper, methods pp. 142–143](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsdr/12/2/12_141/_pdf/-char/ja#page=3)

At baseline juice caused penetration and aspiration, and jelly caused neither. Both left pharyngeal residue. After indirect training, the table marks aspiration with both test foods. After the withdrawal period it still shows juice aspiration. After rhythmic training, both test foods were free of aspiration and measured residue, while juice still penetrated. The main table gives oral transit time shortening from 3.97 ± 0.54 to 0.92 ± 0.08 seconds for jelly and from 2.76 ± 0.39 to 0.94 ± 0.02 seconds for juice, both reported at p < .05 versus baseline. The abstract gives 0.94 seconds for jelly after training, where the main body and table give 0.92. The article reports no clear change in pharyngeal transit time. This was a fixed-order single-case rehabilitation comparison with few repeat swallows. The outcomes do not isolate an ingredient or generalize to a Parkinson population. [2008 primary paper, Tables 1 and 2](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsdr/12/2/12_141/_pdf/-char/ja#page=4) · [2008 primary abstract](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsdr/12/2/12_141/_article/-char/en)

## Clinical chewing comparisons in 2007 and 2008

George Umemoto and colleagues compared chewing and swallowing in Parkinson’s and motor neuron disease at the September 15, 2007 meeting, p. 308, II-2-28. Fukuoka University independently records the presentation. The public title gives no bolus or result. A distinct 2008 paper by Takashi Yamano, Umemoto, Yoshio Tsuboi and colleagues analyzed video swallow studies in 16 people with Parkinson’s, with 12 comparison participants. The paper reports oral propulsion impairment associated with severity and disease duration. It used 10 mL of iopamidol contrast liquid in the test. That paper has a different objective and coauthor list, with no confirmed overlap and no culinary exposure.
