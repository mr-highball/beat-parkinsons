# Caffeine fatigue and prior abstinence

The [1986 coffee substitution study](../../1985-1989/Source-Text/1986-PMID3772801-Griffiths-Coffee-Withdrawal-and-Fatigue.txt) reported effects after abruptly replacing caffeinated coffee with decaffeinated coffee. A [1992 short cessation comparison](../../1990-1994/Source-Text/1992-PMID1528206-Blinded-Caffeine-Cessation-Fatigue.txt) reported abnormal fatigue profiles in some adults during blinded placebo. The [1997 comparison of normal caffeinated drinks and no morning intake](../../1995-1999/Source-Text/1997-PMID9264092-Lane-Caffeine-Abstinence-and-Fatigue.txt) uses a related but distinct design.

The [2009 controlled-maintenance comparison](../Source-Text/2009-PMID19241060-Caffeine-Maintenance-and-Fatigue.txt) measured a fatigue item and a subscale under four conditions. Their post-hoc findings differ. The [2009 usual-diet versus prior abstention comparison](../Source-Text/2009-PMID19777214-Caffeine-Usual-Use-and-Abstention.txt) finds that the tiredness effect appeared after restriction. Later [cycling studies without imposed restriction](../../2025-2029/Source-Text/2025-PMID40704635-Caffeine-Cycling-and-Perceived-Fatigue.txt) reported inconclusive exertional fatigue comparisons. Everyday beverage exposure, mood ratings during abstinence, exertional fatigue and Parkinson fatigue remain different endpoints.
