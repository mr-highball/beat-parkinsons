# Food experiment evidence for cod, pineapple, plum, and cantaloupe

## Pineapple fruit and juice in a bladder symptom survey

Shorter and colleagues' May 11, 2007 primary report gave 104 adults with painful bladder syndrome or interstitial cystitis a questionnaire about 175 foods, drinks and supplements. Table 2 scored pineapple fruit at −1.13 ± 0.84 (78 respondents) and pineapple juice at −1.31 ± 0.92 (61), on a scale from −2 for worse symptoms to +2 for improvement. The composite Pelvic Pain and Urgency/Frequency scale was not separately itemized for consumption events. The reported worsening is self-attributed and the fruit preparation, exposure timing and Parkinson urgency counts are unavailable. [Primary article record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17499305/) · [Primary full copy, Figure 2 and Table 2](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/6333394_Effect_of_Comestibles_on_Symptoms_of_Interstitial_Cystitis)

## Melon concentrate and fatigue in a four-week trial

Milesi and colleagues randomized 70 adults reporting daily stress and fatigue to 10 mg/day of an Extramel melon juice concentrate capsule or placebo. The September 15, 2009 report describes favorable results in several stress and fatigue questionnaire domains and no reported adverse effect. The product was a proprietary concentrated melon preparation. The cohort did not have Parkinson and there was no raw cantaloupe arm. [Primary article text](Source-Text/PMC2757026.txt)

At day 28, improvement in the FARD weariness item was reported as 26% higher with the supplement than placebo, while Epworth sleepiness did not clearly differ. The producer identifies the melon as a proprietary cantaloupe variety and describes the protective coating used to preserve activity through digestion. These product details limit transfer to ordinary raw cantaloupe. [Primary trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2757026/) · [Manufacturer formulation description](https://www.robertet.com/en/health-beauty-2/health/extramel/our-ingredient/)

## GliSODin fatigue trial registration

ACTRN12607000420448 was registered August 21, 2007 for a randomized blinded GliSODin comparison in women aged 50–65 with unexplained fatigue. The intervention used a melon-derived supplement and compared general fatigue on the Multidimensional Fatigue Inventory. The linked primary paper was published online in 2010. [Official registration](https://www.anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=82210) · [Dated result](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Cod-Fruit.md#gliadin-combined-melon-extract-and-fatigue)
