# Hazelnut and sunflower oil evidence

## Sunflower oil comparator registration for major depression

ISRCTN registered the Omega-3-D trial October 31, 2005. It compared fish-oil capsules with a matched sunflower-oil placebo over eight weeks in adults with major depression. The record planned depressive symptoms and tolerability. Its 2010 results report specifies that the comparator contained 2% fish oil. [Official ISRCTN record](https://www.isrctn.com/ISRCTN47431149) · [Local registry summary](Source-Text/Trials/ISRCTN47431149.txt) · [2010 trial results](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Hazelnut-Sunflower-Oils.md#sunflower-and-fish-oil-comparator-in-major-depression)

## High-oleic sunflower oil comparator in an older-adult trial

In September 2008, van de Rest and colleagues reported a 26-week blinded trial in 302 community-dwelling adults aged 65 or older. The 106-person comparator arm received high-oleic sunflower oil in six 900 mg capsules daily, with tocopherol. The authors do not specify refining. The two fish-oil doses enrolled 96 and 100 participants. The sunflower, high-dose and low-dose arms had mean CES-D changes of −0.4, −0.2 and +0.2, respectively (overall p = .87). The MADRS and GDS-15 group comparisons had p = .73 and .90. The cohort excluded people above the screening depression threshold or taking antidepressants. The study tests fish oil against sunflower oil under those conditions. It has no oil-free arm or Parkinson depression outcome. ## Sunflower oil blend in a midlife distress trial

Lucas and colleagues enrolled 120 women aged 40–55 with psychological distress. The 61-person comparator arm took 500 mg sunflower-oil capsules three times daily for eight weeks. The author methods specify 0.2% fish oil for masking and leave refining grade unspecified. The adjusted EPA-minus-comparator changes were −0.08 for HSCL-D-20 (95% CI −0.27 to 0.12) and −1.5 for HAM-D-21 (95% CI −3.7 to 0.7). Both groups improved on symptom scales. The trial used an active oil blend and did not recruit for Parkinson disease. ISRCTN69617477 was posted February 13, 2007, after enrollment began. The separate ISRCTN47431149 study used a different cohort and formulation. ## Mineral oil comparator in the Parkinson depression pilot

Da Silva and colleagues enrolled 31 people with Parkinson disease and major depression. Fish oil favored lower MADRS and CGI scores at three months, while the Beck comparison gave p=.26. The groups completing the comparison received fish oil or mineral oil, with stratification by antidepressant use. The original Methods identify the comparator. [Original trial and exact outcomes](Source-Text/2008-Da-Silva-Parkinson-Depression-Extract.txt)

## Verified oil identity in the Nemets childhood depression trial

Nemets and colleagues report two comparator capsule formulations, identified as 500 mg olive oil and 1,000 mg safflower oil. The 2017 Trebatická paper (PMC5497377) names sunflower oil in one description of this earlier study and safflower oil in another. The original Methods establish safflower or olive oil as the comparator identities. The Nemets trial cannot be counted as a sunflower-oil comparison.
