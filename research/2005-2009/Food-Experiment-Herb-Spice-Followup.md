# Herb and spice evidence follow-up, 2005–2009

## Turmeric rhizome curcuminoids versus diclofenac in knee osteoarthritis

Kertia's 2009 Indonesian randomized open-label, blinded-outcome trial assigned 80 people with knee osteoarthritis to extracted turmeric-rhizome curcuminoids (39, 30 mg three times daily) or diclofenac (41, 25 mg three times daily) for 28 days. Visual analog pain scores fell by 33.76 mm (SD 21.73) and 29.54 mm (SD 21.57), respectively (between-arm p = .23). This active-comparator study gives an imprecise relative estimate without an untreated arm. The diagnosis and extracted preparation differ from the frozen claim for ground turmeric and Parkinson’s pain (`0812`). The author’s 2013 conference abstract repeats the same trial results. [Primary institutional dissertation abstract](https://etd.repository.ugm.ac.id/home/detail_pencarian/49367) · [2013 conference report](https://www.longdom.org/conference-abstracts-files/2167-0501-S1.002-050.pdf)

## Sage cognitive trial registration

EudraCT 2007-002899-34 was entered on June 26, 2007. The listing gives September 18, 2007 as a start date, and the grant report dates the trial conduct to March through August 2008. The Newcastle pilot compared oral *Salvia officinalis* with vitamin E in people with Parkinson’s and mild cognitive impairment. Protocol field E.5.1 lists computerized memory quality, memory speed and power of attention as primary endpoints. The EU Clinical Trials Register opened to the public on March 22, 2011, and the listing gives no record-specific first public display date. This entry is filed under the recorded database entry year. An investigator described the results publicly in October 2010, and the register posted the grant report in 2019. [Official trial listing](https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/trial/2007-002899-34/GB) · [EMA notice of register launch](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/press-release/eu-clinical-trials-register-goes-live_en.pdf) · [2010 investigator account](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#investigator-account-of-the-newcastle-sage-pilot) · [2019 results note](../2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#sage-cognitive-trial-results-attachment)

## Fresh sage extract in a 2009 menopause conference report

Bommer, Klein and Suter reported an eight-week uncontrolled trial of fresh common sage extract tablets in the abstract book for the August 16–20, 2009 Society for Medicinal Plant and Natural Product Research congress in Geneva. The abstract counts 71 women enrolled and the poster describes 69 analyzed. The poster lists exhaustion within the Menopause Rating Scale and reports aggregate scores. The 2011 article gives the exhaustion result for the same cohort. [Congress abstract PJ159](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0029-1234964) · [Congress abstract book](https://ga-online.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/c6241c_81b2e7c1290f490eac20935c7c8908e3.pdf) · [Investigator poster](https://www.avogel.ca/download/Studies/menopause_sage_tablets_efficacy_tolerability_2009.pdf) · [2011 item-level report](../2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#fresh-sage-extract-and-exhaustion-in-an-uncontrolled-menopause-study)

## Cinnamon in a multi-ingredient honey pill disclosure

The 2009 patent application CN101524493A lists cinnamon bark among dozens of ingredients ground together into a honey pill. Its three brief Parkinson’s case descriptions claim improvements in tremor, and one reports restored ability to walk. The application gives no diagnostic criteria, comparator, baseline scale, concurrent treatment information, or follow-up interval for the cases. This is an uncontrolled combination lead for [0889, ground cinnamon and walking slowness](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0889.txt). It leaves the rate of walking and the contribution of cinnamon unresolved. [Original patent, clinical examples](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN101524493A/zh)

## Inhaled black pepper oil and the swallowing reflex after stroke

Ebihara et al. reported a randomized comparison in 105 older post-stroke residents of Japanese nursing homes. Inhaled black pepper oil shortened swallowing-reflex latency in the immediate comparison with lavender oil and distilled water. The authors also reported shorter reflex latency after a month of exposure. This is a swallowing-physiology lead for [0124, cracked black pepper and swallowing difficulty](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0124.txt). The trial evaluated volatile oil by nasal inhalation in a post-stroke cohort and supplied no estimate for eating cracked pepper in people with Parkinson’s. [Primary report, PMID 16970649](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1532-5415.2006.00840.x)

## Dill pickle odor as an assessment stimulus

Bohnen et al. compared 27 people with Parkinson’s with 27 age- and sex-matched controls on the 40-odor UPSIT. Dill pickle, banana, and licorice were among the odors that the Parkinson group had greater difficulty identifying. A later report from the same group reused the three-odor score in an analysis of regional dopamine transporter activity. The odor exposure was part of the test and fresh dill consumption was unmeasured. Both reports inform the measurement context for [0600, fresh dill weed and olfactory impairment](../../experiments/1000_see_what_sticks_food/statements/0600.txt). [2007 study, PMID 17508142](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00415-006-0284-y) · [2008 follow-up report, PMID 18838108](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2634293/)

## Perilla and ginger formula and aspiration pneumonia in a mixed cohort

Iwasaki et al. compared Banxia Houpu Tang with placebo in older patients with dementia and cerebrovascular disease, Alzheimer’s disease, or Parkinson’s disease. The observer-blinded randomized study reported less pneumonia in the formula group, with a pooled relative risk of 0.51 (95% CI, 0.27 to 0.84). The abstract did not provide a Parkinson-specific estimate. This study addresses a mixed medicinal formula and a clinical complication. The separate 2000 report measured swallowing-reflex timing in Parkinson’s. [Primary report, PMID 17944889](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1532-5415.2007.01448.x) · [2000 study summary](../2000-2004/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#a-perilla-and-ginger-formula-and-the-swallowing-reflex)
