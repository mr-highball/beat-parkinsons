# Food experiment evidence for green beans, pinto beans, tempeh, and garlic

## Dietary garlic intake and benign prostatic hyperplasia odds

The October 2007 Galeone case–control study compared 1,369 benign prostatic hyperplasia cases and 1,451 hospital controls. The highest garlic frequency category had adjusted odds of 0.72 (95% CI 0.57–0.91) relative to nonusers. The study classified diagnosis rather than measuring changes in individual urinary symptoms and did not identify cooked whole garlic. [Primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17991535/)

## Pinto bean feeding cohorts and fecal fermentation

Winham and colleagues reported the 17-person pinto, black-eyed pea and carrot crossover in 2007. The parallel 12-week pinto and chicken-soup comparison also reported that year, with 80 completers. The latter used cooked pinto beans canned by the producer and reported a change in *Eubacterium limosum* and limited effects on fecal fermentation measures. Winham and Hutchins’ 2011 symptom analysis identifies both reports as the underlying studies. Counting the 2007 clinical and 2011 symptom reports separately would double count these cohorts.
