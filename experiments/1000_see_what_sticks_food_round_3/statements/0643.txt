Statement: Consuming steamed collard greens reduces executive function impairment in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Unknown
Confidence: 50
Rationale: Evidence for steamed collard greens (Brassica oleracea var. viridis) affecting executive function impairment is Unknown (Researched). Although the prospective Rush Memory and Aging Project cohort (Morris et al. 2018, PMID:29263222, PMC5772164; n=960 older adults) found slower cognitive decline with higher green leafy vegetable consumption, its FFQ pooled spinach, kale/collards/greens, and lettuce together and cannot isolate collard greens alone (exposure_fit: unusable). Furthermore, analytical chemistry refutes borrowing broccoli sulforaphane signals for collards: Đulović et al. 2023 (PMC9965355) found only 2.64 umol/g DW total glucosinolates in edible collard leaves with NO detectable leaf glucoraphanin, Lee et al. 2017 (PMC5612653) documented extreme cultivar variation (as low as 1.0% glucoraphanin), and Song & Thornalley 2007 (PMID:17011103) showed boiling leaches ~90% of Brassica glucosinolates and inactivates myrosinase. Human bioavailability studies confirm collard phylloquinone (Erkkilä 2004; Ellis 2019), lutein (Lienau 2003), and alpha-tocopherol (Traber 2015) absorption without cognitive trials.
