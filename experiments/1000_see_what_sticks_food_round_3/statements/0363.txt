Category: food
Ingredient: chia seed
Preparation: soaked
Symptom: pain severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming soaked chia seed reduces pain severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Two independent preclinical rodent pain studies provide a consistent antinociceptive signal for chia seed lipid fractions: Chel-Guerrero et al. 2025 (PMID:40507154, PMC12157707) demonstrated dose-dependent oral antinociception for Salvia hispanica seed n-hexane extract alone in rat 1% acetic acid writhing (100-300 mg/kg p.o., ED50 = 112.9 +/- 2.0 mg/kg, n=6/group), and Cavalli et al. 2021 (PMID:34600695) found ALA-rich (>62%) chia seed oil attenuated carrageenan mechanical hyperalgesia, ASIC/PGE2/TRPA1 nociception, spinal IL-6, and preventive EAE mechanical hyperalgesia in mice. Human RCT meta-analysis (Pam et al. 2024, PMID:39703891) shows modest CRP lowering without measuring clinical pain. Because both pain signals come from rodent lipid/oil fractions rather than soaked whole chia seed in human PD pain trials, evidence is graded minimal_signal (50.005).
