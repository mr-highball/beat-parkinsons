Category: food
Ingredient: cod
Preparation: baked
Symptom: daily off time
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming baked cod reduces daily off time in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 51
Rationale: Maybe False (51): Contrary to the frozen claim that baked cod reduces daily OFF time, four controlled Parkinson's disease protein/LNAA clinical trials (Barichella 2006 n=18; Tsui 1989 n=10; Carter 1989 n=5; Eriksson 1988) establish that daytime dietary protein and large neutral amino acids (LNAAs) compete with levodopa transport and WORSEN daily OFF time. This mechanism bridges directly to baked Atlantic cod (Gadus morhua) fillet via HPLC amino acid profiling confirming high lean protein density and abundant competing LNAAs (Quaresma 2023), a human 250 g cod fillet meal study (43 g protein) demonstrating substantial postprandial plasma amino acid elevations (Soucy & Leblanc 1999, n=10), and a human meal crossover verifying fish protein elevates plasma essential/LNAAs (r=0.90, peaking more slowly than beef/chicken, p<0.05; Uhe 1992). Effect is tempered by patient-level sensitivity (~12-20% overall, higher in advanced fluctuating PD; Virmani 2016), normal-diet nuances (Nutt 1989), chronic fasting BCAA dynamics (Schmedes 2018), and meal-medication spacing (Cucca 2015).
