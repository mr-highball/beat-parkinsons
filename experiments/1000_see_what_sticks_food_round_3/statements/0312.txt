ID: 0312
Category: food
Ingredient: cherry
Preparation: dried
Symptom: pain severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming dried cherry worsens pain severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Maybe False (minimal_signal, 50.005): human DB-RCTs show lower VAS pain with tart cherry concentrate/juice in aromatase-inhibitor arthralgia (-34.7% vs -1.4%, p=0.034; Shenouda 2022), distance running (p<0.001; Kuehl 2010), and eccentric muscle injury (p=0.017; Connolly 2006), plus 35% lower gout flare odds with cherry intake (Zhang 2012, n=633), tempered by null between-group knee OA (Schumacher 2013 p=0.16), fibromyalgia (p=0.10), powdered-capsule exercise trials (Hagele 2026 p=0.838, Squires 2024 p>0.3), gout RCT (Stamp 2020 p=0.76), and absence of dried-cherry Parkinson pain trials.
