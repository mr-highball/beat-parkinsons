Ingredient: clams
Preparation: cooked
Symptom: daily off time
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming cooked clams reduces daily off time in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 51
Rationale: Opposing clinical evidence leans against the claim that cooked clams reduce daily off time in levodopa-treated PD. Multiple controlled Parkinson's clinical trials—including Barichella et al. 2006 (PMID:16773618, n=18; postprandial/total OFF time 271 vs 164 min/day on normal- vs low-protein diets, P<0.0001), Tsui et al. 1989 (PMID:2494567, n=10 blinded crossover), Carter et al. 1989 (PMID:2648188, n=5), and Eriksson et al. 1988 (PMID:3135513)—establish that dietary protein rich in large neutral amino acids (LNAAs) competes with levodopa transport and increases daily OFF time in fluctuating PD. Analytical composition confirms bivalve clams are protein-dense (55.36-68.01 g protein/100 g dry weight; ~25.5 g/100 g cooked clams) and rich in competing LNAAs (leucine, isoleucine, valine, phenylalanine, tyrosine; Tabakaeva 2018; How 2023; Venugopal 2017). Tempering PD evidence shows protein sensitivity affects a subset of patients (Virmani 2016: 5.9% overall on levodopa, 12.4% with motor fluctuations; Nutt 1989; Cucca 2015 spaced amino acids null). Because no trial specifically isolated cooked clams versus matched low-protein meals, certainty is capped at limited_clinical_link (51).
