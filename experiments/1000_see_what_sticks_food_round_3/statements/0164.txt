Statement: Consuming ground cayenne pepper worsens executive function impairment in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Unknown
Confidence: 50
Rationale: Unknown (Researched): Evidence on dietary chili or capsaicin and executive function is directly conflicting and lacks any Parkinson's disease trial. In the 12-week Capsifen RCT (Thornton 2026, n=34 completers), core executive flexibility (Dimensional Change Card Sort p=0.999), inhibitory control (Flanker p=0.209), and Trail Making Part B minus Part A set-shifting (p=0.315) were null, Trail Making Part B did not survive multiple-comparison correction, and List Sorting/TMT-A interaction effects reflected pronounced baseline-swap regression-to-the-mean without baseline-adjusted ANCOVA. Meanwhile, the 15-year prospective Chinese cohort (Shi 2019, n=4,582) linked high chili intake (>50 g/d) to faster cognitive decline (beta=-1.13), opposing cross-sectional positive surveys (Tian 2020; Yuan 2025).
