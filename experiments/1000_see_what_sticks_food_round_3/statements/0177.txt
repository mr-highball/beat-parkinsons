Ingredient: cayenne pepper
Preparation: ground
Symptom: urinary urgency frequency
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming ground cayenne pepper worsens urinary urgency frequency in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Maybe True (50.005): Four independent human urology patient cohorts consistently identify oral hot peppers, chili, or spicy foods among the most frequent dietary triggers worsening urinary urgency, urinary frequency, and bladder discomfort—led by a nationwide chart-validated US VA controlled cohort (Jarman 2023, N=425: Hot Peppers worsened bladder symptoms in 30% of IC/BPS vs 5% healthy controls, Chili 25% vs 3%, spicy foods 35% vs 7%, p<0.001, with food-provoked urinary urgency in 40% and frequency in 46%) and corroborated by Shorter 2007 (n=104, validated SMQ ranking hot pepper <-1.0), Bassaly 2011 (n=598 ICA cohort), and Lai 2019 (n=53 MAPP cohort). Signal remains minimal because cohorts enrolled non-PD IC/BPS patients via self-report rather than blinded oral challenge RCTs, the Ha 2025 overactive-bladder systematic review found insufficient OAB-specific spicy-food studies, healthy Capsimax tolerability was null (Deshpande 2016), and intravesical bladder instillation (Soontrapa 2003) is an invasive non-oral route.
