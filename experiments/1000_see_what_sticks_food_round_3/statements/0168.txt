Statement: Consuming ground cayenne pepper worsens insomnia severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Maybe True (50.005): Two controlled human polysomnography laboratory experiments point toward sleep disruption from oral chili/capsaicin: an evening meal containing 3 g Tabasco (Capsicum frutescens) sauce plus 3 g prepared mustard cut total sleep time (423.3 vs 445.2 min, p<0.002), lowered sleep efficiency (0.90 vs 0.94, p<0.03), reduced slow-wave and stage 2 sleep, and trended toward longer sleep-onset latency (34.8 vs 16.1 min) in healthy men (Edwards 1992), and intraoral capsaicin during sleep triggered complete EEG awakenings in 30 of 32 stimuli (94%, Stuck 2016). Signal remains minimal because Edwards 1992 was small (n=6) and co-exposed with mustard, cross-sectional spicy-food insomnia odds attenuated after full adjustment (Beigrezaei 2022), non-oral topical pain patches are irrelevant, and no Parkinson's insomnia trial exists.
