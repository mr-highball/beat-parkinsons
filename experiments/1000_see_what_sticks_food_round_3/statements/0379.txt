Ingredient: chicken

Symptom: daily off time
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming poached chicken reduces daily off time in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 51
Rationale: Maybe False (51): Contrary to the frozen claim that poached chicken reduces daily OFF time, four controlled Parkinson's disease dietary protein/LNAA clinical trials (Barichella 2006 n=18; Tsui 1989 n=10; Carter 1989 n=5; Eriksson 1988) establish that high daytime dietary protein and large neutral amino acids (LNAAs) compete with levodopa for intestinal and blood-brain-barrier transport and WORSEN daily OFF time. This clinical mechanism bridges directly to poached chicken via a human meal crossover showing cooked chicken produces rapid postprandial rises in plasma essential/LNAA concentrations (r=0.90, faster than fish, p<0.05; Uhe 1992) and compositional confirmation of ~22.5 g high-LNAA protein per 110 g steamed chicken breast (Uchida 2024; Li 2021). Effect is moderated by individual sensitivity (~12-20% overall prevalence, higher in advanced fluctuating PD; Virmani 2016), normal-diet fluctuation nuances (Nutt 1989), and proper meal-medication spacing (Cucca 2015).
