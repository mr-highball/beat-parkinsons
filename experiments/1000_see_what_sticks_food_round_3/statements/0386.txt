ID: 0386
Category: food
Ingredient: chicken
Preparation: poached
Symptom: executive function impairment
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming poached chicken reduces executive function impairment in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Maybe True (50.005): Four independent double-blind human RCTs of aqueous Essence of Chicken or hydrolyzed chicken meat extracts report improvements in working memory and executive tasks—Suttiwan 2018 (n=235, Digit Span Backward p=0.028, +0.60 digits at Day 7), Wu 2020 (n=73, CANTAB Spatial Working Memory errors p=0.045), Azhar 2013 (n=46, CMI-168 Digit Span Backward & Letter-Number Sequencing p<0.01), and Szczesniak 2014 (n=51 older adults >=65 y, STMS abstraction/construction/recall p<0.04)—contextualized by meta-analysis (Toh 2021, 8 RCTs n=794, working memory SMD 0.31 [0.16, 0.46]) and bridged to heated/poached chicken breast carnosine/anserine by Kouriki-Nagatomo 2019. Signal remains minimal because competing RCTs were null (Chan 2016 n=102; Young 2015 n=46), Teoh 2016 flagged risk of bias, trials tested bottled/hydrolyzed extracts rather than ordinary poached chicken meat, and no Parkinson's executive-function trial exists.
