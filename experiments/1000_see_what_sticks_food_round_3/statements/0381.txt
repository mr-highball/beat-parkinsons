Ingredient: chicken
Preparation: poached
Symptom: depressive symptom severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming poached chicken worsens depressive symptom severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Maybe False (50.005): Contrary to the frozen claim that poached chicken worsens depressive symptoms, a double-blind human RCT of aqueous Essence of Chicken (Young, Benton & Carter 2015, n=46 completers) reported significantly lower POMS/VAS depressive mood ratings vs placebo (VAS Depressed/Elated F(1,41)=6.544, p<0.01, though GHQ-Depression was null), bridged to moist-heat poached chicken breast carnosine/anserine by Kouriki-Nagatomo 2019 and consistent with cross-sectional poultry intake meta-analysis data (Torabinasab 2026, RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.78-0.98, cohort n.s.) and preclinical chicken-extract/carnosine monoamine studies (Tomonaga 2008; Tsuruoka 2012). Signal remains strictly minimal and heavily tempered because a larger 6-week hydrolyzed chicken extract RCT was null on BDI-II depression (Wu 2020, n=73, p=0.499) and no Parkinson's depression trial exists.
