Category: spice
Ingredient: coriander seed
Preparation: toasted
Symptom: pain severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming toasted coriander seed reduces pain severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Human migraine RCT evidence plus multiple controlled rodent seed-extract pain experiments support a consistent analgesic signal for Coriandrum sativum fruits/seeds: Delavar Kasmaei et al. 2016 (PMID:26889386, PMC4752800; companion mixture-model re-analysis Mansouri 2020, PMID:32884919, deduplicated in the same cohort family) conducted a triple-blind placebo-controlled RCT (n=68 randomized, 66 analyzed) in which coriander fruit syrup (15 mL TID x 1 month alongside sodium valproate) reduced migraine VAS pain severity by 3.65 units, duration by 5.7 hours, and weekly frequency by ~50% versus placebo syrup (all P<0.001). Three independent rodent seed studies concur: Kajal & Singh 2020 (PMID:32413574, oral hydroalcoholic seed extract attenuating STZ diabetic neuropathic hyperalgesia in rats), Rojas-Armas et al. 2025 (PMID:41113205, PMC12535457, seed essential oil/linalool reducing mouse writhing), and Boujbiha et al. 2025 (PMID:39901986, PMC11788492, oral hydroethanolic seed extract analgesia). Zhang 2015 bridges roasting retention, while Persian headache and Sri Lankan joint-pain traditions (Sobhani 2022; Napagoda 2018) add ethnobotanical context. Because the human RCT tested liquid fruit syrup (not toasted culinary seed) as add-on therapy in non-PD migraineurs, certainty is minimal_signal (50.005).
