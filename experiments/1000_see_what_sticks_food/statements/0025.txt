ID: 0025
Category: herb
Ingredient: sage
Preparation: fresh
Symptom: pain severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming fresh sage worsens pain severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Two independent placebo-controlled menopause trials report less joint and muscular discomfort with oral Salvia officinalis fresh-tip extract (n=48 per-protocol of 80, p<.05) and dry extract (n=59, p<.001 with reversed table statistics), supported by a dose-comparison sage leaf throat-spray trial (n=164, p=.0021) and preclinical antinociception. Competing safety signals include one excessive-intake abdominal pain case (6/10) and a non-significant adverse-event count in an Alzheimer dry-extract trial (2 vs 0, p=.23), while perioperative/cancer rinse and mixed-herb trials give no isolated oral pain effect. Because no study evaluates fresh culinary sage or Parkinson pain severity, the controlled non-Parkinson discomfort reductions sustain only a minimal_signal against worsening.
