ID: 0232
Category: food
Ingredient: mango
Preparation: dried
Symptom: depressive symptom severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming dried mango worsens depressive symptom severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Unknown
Confidence: 50
Rationale: A dried mango crossover measured glucose and appetite without depressive severity. The newer experiment offers dried mango among optional snacks and reports only aggregate intake and momentary affect. Fresh-mango and MiniMeal protocols have no posted depression results. The mango leaf extract trials give mixed mood estimates. An isolated dried mango effect on Parkinson depressive symptoms remains unresolved. The Boston Puerto Rican cohort's questionnaire includes mango and its Mediterranean diet score relates to depression measures. The overall score comparison omits an estimate for mango, its drying state and the Parkinson population. The separate acute leaf-extract trials add mood context with no clinical depression comparison. Three separately sourced animal studies report favorable behavioral proxy outcomes after isolated mangiferin. The original Cao abstract gives increased sucrose consumption, where a later review says decreased. These compounds and populations do not estimate dried mango flesh effects on Parkinson depressive symptoms. The fresh mango questionnaire registry still has no posted results.
