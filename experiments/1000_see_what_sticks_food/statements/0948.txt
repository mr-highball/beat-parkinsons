ID: 0948
Category: food
Ingredient: green tea
Preparation: brewed
Symptom: fatigue severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming brewed green tea worsens fatigue severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 51
Rationale: The uncontrolled 14-person Parkinson brewed-green-tea pilot reports mean fatigue increasing from 28.4 to 32.1 and higher medians, with no paired variance or exact fatigue p value. Opposing evidence includes a postoperative whole-tea comparison and customary relief of tiredness. Some isolated caffeine and caffeine–theanine contrasts favor lower task fatigue. Controlled withdrawal comparisons suggest that short abstention can contribute to that signal. The usual-diet cycling outcome showed no caffeine condition effect and the taper trials do not report a separate fatigue contrast. The acute comparisons do not replicate a prepared green-tea Parkinson exposure. The closest pilot retains a tentative worsening lean. Controlled brewed-tea Parkinson results and the remaining original measures stay open. An isolated catechin crossover in multiple sclerosis reported exercise efficiency and metabolic differences, with no dedicated fatigue result. A separate signaling review supplies EGCG constituent context, not a beverage fatigue measure. Neither changes the closest brewed tea pilot's tentative worsening signal. The added flavonoid review covers catechins and EGCG. Its fatigue example instead concerns quercetin treatment in an oncology setting. Keep the original brewed-green-tea evidence separate. The xanthine review names tea sources and discusses stimulant compounds without an independent fatigue estimate. The earlier brewing and caffeine comparisons remain separate. The P49 abstract, 2016 study and two patents share one acute isolated-theacrine crossover. A separate matching P36/full-report family showed higher overall lethargy ratings at the lower isolated-theacrine condition with an inconclusive condition-by-time term. The separate eight-week trial's fatigue group-by-time p=.41 does not replicate the earlier acute result. These isolated-supplement results leave the closer brewed-tea Parkinson pilot as the basis of the existing lean. The cited green tea extract animal paper tests a developmental model and reports aggregate behaviors rather than a clinically defined fatigue contrast. Model extraction and brewing differ. The nearer brewed tea observation remains the basis of the current tentative direction. The postinfectious fatigue review mentions green tea among botanical candidates, with no brewed exposure or separate Parkinson fatigue estimate. The prior limited clinical link rests on the specific uncontrolled brewed-tea pilot and its existing contrary evidence. The Janssens extract-capsule trial had 58 analyzable adults and an inconclusive measured microbiota contrast. It did not administer brewed tea or measure fatigue in Parkinson disease.
