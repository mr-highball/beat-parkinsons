ID: 0139
Category: spice
Ingredient: nigella seed
Preparation: ground
Symptom: fatigue severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming ground nigella seed worsens fatigue severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: The open-label 2022 seed-oil softgel comparison reports day-four fatigue in 12/30 versus 25/30, adjusted OR 0.15 (95% CI 0.04–0.48). Its endpoint is fatigue presence on a particular day. The acute-illness setting, formulation and reporting limit transfer to ground culinary nigella and Parkinson fatigue severity. The open-label 2021 oil trial randomized 183 (91 oil and 92 usual care). Table 2 reports 6.3 ± 4.1 days of lethargy or fatigue in 62 participants with that symptom versus 5.4 ± 3.2 days in 56 controls with the symptom, p=.18. The numerical duration was longer with oil. The estimate is inconclusive and covers a symptomatic subset during acute illness. The closer ground-seed trial found mixed day-ten findings. Other families report no separately attributable fatigue result. The public EVACUR registration gives 59 enrolled, completed May 7, 2025, and no posted results as of the November 27 recheck. The FACIT-F protocol compares a botanical blend with starch placebo and does not assign nigella separately. The day-four report retains a minimal lean against worsening with substantial outcome and preparation uncertainty.
