ID: 0471
Category: food
Ingredient: brown rice
Preparation: cooked
Symptom: postural instability severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming cooked brown rice worsens postural instability severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Unknown
Confidence: 50
Rationale: Cooked brown rice and related feed experiments give a favorable timed-climbing signal in iron-exposed flies. Matched wild-type brown-versus-white comparisons are descriptively favorable and the normal cornmeal comparison differs. Rat evidence and a processed human hydrolysate trial give general motor outcomes without a separate clinical postural-stability result. A bridge to the frozen posture endpoint remains unresolved. EXPRESS reports a rice-protein-powder comparison under matched exercise but its accessible original gives no numerical standing result. A black-rice supplement mobility study and the whole-grain lifestyle pilot do not isolate cooked brown rice. A closer cooked dewaxed-rice study measures cognition. Exercise and nutrition education improved standing balance relative to control. Brown rice is a dietary questionnaire example. There is no attributable effect of cooked brown rice. The comparative EXPRESS trial combines rice protein or whey with shared exercise in older adults. Secondary sources confirm SPPB coverage. Original standing-balance group results remain unavailable and cannot be derived from the composite result.
