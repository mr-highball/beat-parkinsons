ID: 0812
Category: spice
Ingredient: turmeric
Preparation: ground
Symptom: pain severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming ground turmeric reduces pain severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Ground turmeric powder with naproxen had a larger pain reduction than naproxen alone in an unblinded dysmenorrhea comparison. A double-blind whole-powder hand osteoarthritis pilot found no clear difference, and the Parkinson nanomicelle pilot had no separate pain item. The Cochrane synthesis found 0.72 points less pain on a 0–10 scale, below its 1.5-point threshold, with low certainty. Supplementary materials identify the Thanawala extract trial, but final four-trial membership remains open, so that overlap cannot be resolved. Older documented turmeric use for skin and muscle pain and the diabetic mouse combination study remain contextual. The whole-powder comparison and a separate formulated-curcumin clinical signal support a minimal easing tier with no direct culinary Parkinson pain result. The independent Arora rat comparison reports relief in a mechanical pain proxy after injected curcumin at author-abstract depth. The route and pain model limit transfer. Full group estimates remain open.
