ID: 0949
Category: food
Ingredient: raspberry
Preparation: frozen
Symptom: executive function impairment
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming frozen raspberry worsens executive function impairment in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: One randomized older-adult meal crossover reports fewer six-hour post-meal spatial working-memory errors and better strategy after whole freeze-dried red raspberry powder versus control (NCT03879213, p<.05). A black-raspberry Alzheimer pilot measured global CDR without executive subscales, four-berry and multicomponent capsule trials do not isolate raspberry, and rodent studies are split between high-fat-diet benefit and Alzheimer-model null results. Acute meal testing, powder vs intact frozen fruit, and non-Parkinson population keep the lean against worsening at minimal_signal. The final abstract describes fewer errors and better strategy after a freeze-dried red raspberry powder meal. The 2023 investigator report explicitly identifies NCT03879213 and plots about 7.0 versus 9.9 total eight-box errors and 6.9 versus 9.5 between-search errors at six hours. The official USDA investigator record states 36 completed both visits. The registry reports 30 enrolled and provides no results. The design and publication history support one likely crossover family. Journal table values, cognitive-analysis denominator and enrollment reconciliation remain open.
