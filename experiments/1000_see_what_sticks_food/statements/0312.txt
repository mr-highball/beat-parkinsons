ID: 0312
Category: spice
Ingredient: black pepper
Preparation: cracked
Symptom: pain severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming cracked black pepper worsens pain severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: A randomized study in 54 adults with heterogeneous pain reported lower immediate pain after black pepper essential oil inhalation than after sesame oil (median 3 versus 6 on a 0–10 scale, p < .001), with scent masking unverified. The matched culinary comparison added ground black pepper to shared turmeric and gave an inconclusive pain result (p = .157). Animal pepper extract and piperine studies are indirect. Shoba’s randomized crossover measured an increase in curcumin exposure with isolated piperine, but it measured neither pain nor cracked pepper. The inhalation comparison yields a minimal easing lean against the frozen worsening hypothesis. The culinary Parkinson pain effect remains open.
