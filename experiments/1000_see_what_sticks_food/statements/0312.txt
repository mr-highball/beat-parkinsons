ID: 0312
Category: spice
Ingredient: black pepper
Preparation: cracked
Symptom: pain severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming cracked black pepper worsens pain severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: A randomized study in 54 adults with heterogeneous pain reported lower immediate pain after black pepper essential oil inhalation than after sesame oil (median 3 versus 6 on a 0–10 scale, p < .001), with scent masking unverified. The matched culinary comparison added ground black pepper to shared turmeric and gave an inconclusive pain result (p = .157). Animal pepper extract and piperine studies are indirect. Shoba’s randomized crossover measured an increase in curcumin exposure with isolated piperine, but it measured neither pain nor cracked pepper. The inhalation comparison yields a minimal easing lean against the frozen worsening hypothesis. The culinary Parkinson pain effect remains open. The piperine swallowing study's one abdominal pain event was judged unrelated and there is no comparative pain-severity endpoint. Keep the earlier pain assessment and the swallowing outcome distinct. The neurogenic dysphagia meta-analysis has an isolated inhaled black pepper oil swallowing comparison. The autism paper tests a different piperine model with aggregate behavioral findings and no separate pain effect available from the abstract. Neither supplies a new cracked culinary pepper Parkinson pain result. A small oral trial of a proprietary black-pepper-derived liposomal compound reported less exercise soreness at day four. An animal constituent comparison found a later but not an early inflammatory effect. The fresh search found pain studies of mixed oral or topical preparations with no isolated pepper estimate. A firsthand account reports worse abdominal pain after meals with ground pepper, with meal attribution and the cause of pain unresolved. The immediate clinical inhalation comparison still gives a minimal easing lean. The closest oral ground-pepper comparison remains inconclusive and the direction of cracked pepper in Parkinson disease remains unverified.
