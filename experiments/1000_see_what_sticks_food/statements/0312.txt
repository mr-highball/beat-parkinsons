Category: spice
Ingredient: black pepper
Preparation: cracked
Symptom: pain severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming cracked black pepper worsens pain severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: One randomized controlled trial in 54 adults with heterogeneous pain (DOI10.15806/j.issn.2311-8571.2015.0032) reports lower immediate post-inhalation 0-10 pain after 15 minutes of Piper nigrum essential oil versus sesame oil (median 3 vs 6, Mann-Whitney U=117.5, p<.001; baseline 6 vs 7, p=.930) without verifying scent masking, supported by oral standardized black-pepper seed extract in diabetic-rat tail-flick testing (PMID35733641) and high-dose oral isolated piperine in rat paw/arthritis models (PMID19327174). Conversely, the closest oral culinary comparisonâ€”a 29-adult chronic-pain crossover adding 300 mg/day ground black pepper to background ground turmeric for one-week periods (PMID41599836)â€”gave an inconclusive pepper-addition contrast (p=.157, treatment-by-period p=.5508), and no clinical study shows black pepper worsening pain. Inhalation route, unverified scent blinding, the inconclusive ground-pepper add-on crossover, and non-Parkinson cohorts limit the easing lean contradicting the worsening hypothesis to minimal_signal.
