ID: 0979
Category: spice
Ingredient: cinnamon
Preparation: ground
Symptom: olfactory impairment severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming ground cinnamon worsens olfactory impairment severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Unknown
Confidence: 50
Rationale: Parkinson cohorts used cinnamon strictly as a diagnostic scratch-and-sniff odorant; an oral ground Cinnamomum cassia crossover in diabetes (Moreira 2024, n=19) found inconclusive breakfast-smell pleasantness (all p>=0.80) without a clinical olfactory scale, inhaled multi-odor smell training (Chao 2025, n=60) did not test ingestion or isolate cinnamon, and mixed essential-oil registration NCT07518394 posts no results; oral ground cinnamon effects on Parkinson olfaction remain open. A 162-person comparison across healthy, iRBD and Parkinson groups gives correct cinnamon odor recognition rates of 46.3%, 29.6% and 31.5%, with three-group p=.154. Another iRBD cohort used the cinnamon odor item but reported aggregate scores. Familiarity with the odor is a possible measurement factor. These diagnostic tests do not estimate a change after eating ground cinnamon. The acute-illness study found fewer total correct odor identifications on a 12-odor battery including cinnamon. The comparison is by health status, with no isolated cinnamon result or therapeutic change. It addresses diagnostic assessment without an ingested ground-cinnamon Parkinson intervention.
