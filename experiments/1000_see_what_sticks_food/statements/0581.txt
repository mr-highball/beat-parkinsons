Category: spice
Ingredient: ginger
Preparation: grated
Symptom: constipation severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming grated ginger worsens constipation severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Two independent small IBS trials measured a separate constipation severity domain and favored oral ginger capsules over placebo. Al-Jassim Table 1 and Rostamkhani Table 4 address the same patient-reported symptom and use the same ginger rhizome as a food, with different processing, dosing and diagnoses. Separate tablet and extract trials in multiple sclerosis, hypothyroidism, dyspepsia and occasional constipation provide related comparisons. The IBS pilot did not show a clear composite benefit and one constipation adverse event was reported with extract in migraine. Public accounts describe varied responses and simultaneous exposure changes. The registered Parkinson comparisons supply no reported constipation treatment estimate. Taken together the non-Parkinson symptom comparisons support only a minimal easing lean for the frozen question.
