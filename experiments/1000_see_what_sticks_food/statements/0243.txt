ID: 0243
Category: spice
Ingredient: cinnamon
Preparation: ground
Symptom: anxiety severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming ground cinnamon worsens anxiety severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Unknown
Confidence: 50
Rationale: Taiwanese cinnamon leaf oil and purified linalool favored anxiety-like behavior in healthy mice without a measured open-field movement change. Authenticated Ceylon bark oils sometimes contain linalool, with variable relative compositions and no detection in one direct survey of bark and commercial products. Neither chiral identity nor a transfer dose from an ordinary serving of ground bark to the positive mouse exposure is established. Existing constituent and extract comparisons also include null behavior results. Human and Parkinson data provide no attributable ground cinnamon anxiety contrast. A verified ingredient relationship survives as context and the symptom direction stays open. Retraction and corrected republication preserve one traumatic-brain-injury study lineage using sodium benzoate. Its reported behavior does not provide a ground-bark anxiety contrast. The original Guadalajara field inventory separately records intended use of true cinnamon for a local altered-nerves category, with no individual response. The 2025 follow-up is the same fieldwork lineage. Ground cinnamon and Parkinson anxiety remain open.
