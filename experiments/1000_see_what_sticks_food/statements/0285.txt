Category: herb
Ingredient: tarragon
Preparation: fresh
Symptom: pain severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming fresh tarragon reduces pain severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: One randomized trial in 90 adults with knee osteoarthritis (PMID42866378, PMC13657636) reports lower immediate post-three-week WOMAC pain with topical 3% tarragon oil extract in sweet almond oil versus vehicle and routine care (10.13 vs 13.73 and 13.43, p=.001), while one- and three-week post-stop contrasts were inconclusive (p=.376 and .858), scent prevented blinding, and the 2021 registry disagrees on chronology, randomization, schedule and blinding. Supporting oral animal evidence shows aqueous dried tarragon aerial-part extract lowered formalin-evoked pain in fructose-fed rats (PMID26170888, p<.001), whereas ethanolic extract PMI-5011 in high-fat-diet mice normalized evoked neuropathy thresholds rather than spontaneous pain (PMID20396384), and a completed dental-gel registry (IRCT20241115063712N1) has no public results. Topical and dried-extract exposures, non-Parkinson cohorts, transient effect, and registration discrepancies keep the easing signal at minimal_signal. Two diabetes-model or high-fat-model cohort publications investigated an ethanolic extract using evoked sensory tests. A community chapter describes toothache and menstrual contexts. These sources do not measure oral fresh tarragon for Parkinson pain. A complete 245-record PubMed retrieval surfaced a separate abstract of injected tarragon essential oil in rodent nociception. It is an indirect exposure and animal outcome and lacks accessible group tables. The earlier topical knee study remains the favorable context. A later review records attributed customary toothache use, without measured pain response. Three ethnobotanical source tables and a separate supplementary species inventory remain held.
