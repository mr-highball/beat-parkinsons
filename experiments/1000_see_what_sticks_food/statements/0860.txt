ID: 0860
Category: food
Ingredient: grape
Preparation: raw
Symptom: anxiety severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming raw grape worsens anxiety severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: An older-adult placebo-controlled fruit-extract trial found lower Hamilton anxiety scores with low baseline anxiety. Whole-grape powder in a 12-week trial and a Parkinson-model grape concentrate comparison were inconclusive. Dried currants gave discordant open-field and elevated-plus-maze results, and the acute grape juice study has order effects. A later review's favorable rat powder citation traces to a retracted 2013 source and contributes no further direction. Distinct powder animal models remain indirect. The human extract contrast retains minimal easing with no direct raw-grape Parkinson anxiety trial. The grape and wine polyphenol review links formulations to cancer mechanisms. It supplies no estimate for raw grapes and anxiety severity. The review describes grape constituents and preclinical antioxidant or depression studies. It adds no raw-grape anxiety comparison to the existing evidence. The autophagy review appraises isolated resveratrol and other experimental compounds. That link provides no ordinary grape intake or anxiety comparison in Parkinson disease. The grape review separates whole grape, juice, seed extract, seed oil and multicomponent polyphenol preparations. The review provides no independent estimate of raw grape and Parkinson anxiety. The cited 19-person grape-powder study measured microbiome and metabolic endpoints. It contributes no additional raw-grape and anxiety contrast. The original Papa grape seed extract report compares tension at four weeks in 32 healthy adults. Descriptive improvement favors extract, with an inconclusive reported interaction (p=.549). Its Ginkgo companion omits the grape seed arm. Raw grape and Parkinson anxiety evidence remains distinct.
