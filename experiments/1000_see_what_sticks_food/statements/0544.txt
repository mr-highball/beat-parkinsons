ID: 0544
Category: food
Ingredient: tomato
Preparation: cooked
Symptom: muscle rigidity severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming cooked tomato reduces muscle rigidity severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Unknown
Confidence: 50
Rationale: Whole tomato feed altered striatal dopamine in animal models. The Solanaceae study estimated disease occurrence for habitual tomato and juice intake with imprecise estimates. Sofrito was bundled in a self-reported diet item, with an inconclusive total symptom estimate. Isolated lycopene favored several preclinical motor outcomes, including pole timing, rotarod, coordination or grip, while the other studies report water-maze, broad motor or induced dyskinesia outcomes. None isolates examiner-rated rigidity or a rigidity proxy specific enough to carry a direction to cooked tomato. Two public conversations describe raw tomato and joint symptoms or a multi-food change with uncertain diagnosis. Neither supplies a cooked-tomato outcome. The cooked-tomato rigidity hypothesis remains open. The prospective nicotine cohort separates tomatoes and processed tomatoes in its food-frequency instrument. Pooled highest-versus-lowest Parkinson incidence estimates were 0.91 (95% CI 0.67–1.25) and 0.86 (0.61–1.22). Incident diagnosis and processed forms do not measure rigidity after eating cooked tomato. The tomato sources involve a laboratory lectin reagent or list tomato skin as a source of resveratrol. There is no cooked tomato and rigidity estimate.
