ID: 0063
Category: spice
Ingredient: saffron
Preparation: ground
Symptom: depressive symptom severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming ground saffron worsens depressive symptom severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 55
Rationale: The Isfahan trial randomized 92 adults with Parkinson disease and directly compared orally consumed ground dried Sargol stigma powder with placebo. The adjusted DASS depression difference was −4.58 points (95% CI −8.34 to −0.81), favoring less depression. The ground powder was delivered in a fixed-dose tablet. Depression was a secondary endpoint and the trial was powered for a biomarker. An independent Tehran trial favors saffron on Hamilton depression scores, with two other depression measures inconclusive and capsule processing unverified. The Shiraz non-Parkinson stigma adjunct comparison is inconclusive and the isolated crocin mouse result is indirect. The matched Parkinson comparison supports easing and therefore opposes the frozen worsening claim. Effects at ordinary meal quantities and independent replication with the same preparation remain open.
