Category: spice
Ingredient: nigella seed
Preparation: toasted
Symptom: depressive symptom severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming toasted nigella seed reduces depressive symptom severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: One randomized non-Parkinson depression trial reports lower DASS-21 scores when nigella oil extract is added to sertraline versus sertraline alone (PMID35548175, 18.6 vs 23.4, p<.001, with a one-point arithmetic discrepancy in printed change). A ground-seed premenstrual trial favored a composite mood domain rather than depression severity (PMID41267796), while crushed-seed Bond-Lader mood (PMID24412554) and mixed-product depression trials in hypothyroidism (p=.79) and multiple sclerosis were inconclusive, and a hemodialysis trial has unreported HADS results. Oil preparation, co-treatment, reporting discrepancy, and absence of toasted-seed or Parkinson trials keep the easing lean at minimal_signal. A distinct 2025 rat experiment reports lower forced-swim immobility under oral ethanol seed extract. Group estimates are graph-read and a separate general locomotor control is unavailable. It remains contextual for toasted seed and measured Parkinson depression. The matched 2022 hemodialysis results confirm cold-pressed oil, 20 and 21 analyzed, and no posted arm-level HADS values. The protocol and outcome paper are one cohort. The interview study tabulates professional application of black seed for low mood or depression. It gives no preparation-specific or measured change, including for toasted seed in Parkinson disease. The April 2021 preprint prespecifies HADS for the same registered oil-versus-paraffin hemodialysis trial as the later protocol and metabolic paper. The results article reports 20 and 21 analyzed for that report. It contains no arm-level HADS estimate. The three records count as one clinical study family. The registry could not be reopened on this check. Formulation and population remain remote from toasted nigella for Parkinson depressive symptoms.
