ID: 0348
Category: food
Ingredient: chicken
Preparation: poached
Symptom: fatigue severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming poached chicken reduces fatigue severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: One double-blind randomized placebo-controlled crossover in healthy volunteers (Tanaka 2008; 18 randomized, 17 analyzed) reports smaller visual-analogue fatigue increases after 29 days of oral chicken-breast extract drink (400 mg/day imidazole dipeptides) versus placebo following four hours of cycling (+18.1 +- 15.8 vs +26.3 +- 18.1 mm, p<.05) and four hours of recovery (+9.8 +- 17.3 vs +20.1 +- 20.7 mm, p<.01), whereas two-hour, next-day, and end-of-exercise recovery contrasts were null; food chemistry confirms anserine and carnosine retention in heated chicken breast (DOI10.3136/nskkk.66.210). Conversely, a 30-adult randomized crossover of reconstituted whole-chicken soup found inconclusive routine POMS fatigue changes (-0.5 vs +0.1, p=.61; DOI10.1248/jhs.55.56), a sprint extract trial had inconclusive power outcomes (PMID16531904), and a culinary patent tasting report (KR20050019309A) lacks controlled symptom measurement. Concentrated dipeptide drink versus poached chicken, healthy exercise-induced fatigue rather than Parkinson fatigue, small sample, and competing null chicken-soup crossover keep the easing signal at minimal_signal.
