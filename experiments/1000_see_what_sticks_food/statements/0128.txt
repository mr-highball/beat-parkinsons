Category: herb
Ingredient: rosemary
Preparation: fresh
Symptom: depressive symptom severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming fresh rosemary worsens depressive symptom severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: One dried rosemary ground-leaf capsule trial added to SSRIs outside Parkinson disease reports a favorable BDI-II group-by-time contrast (PMID36343423, n=51 completers, p=.005) despite baseline imbalance without covariate adjustment, alongside a student capsule abstract reporting lower depression whose poster-extracted endpoint CI crosses zero (PMID29389474, SMD -0.30). Competing controlled comparisons are inconclusive for rosemary extract in methadone maintenance (Heidarzadeh 2026), a post hoc healthy-worker reanalysis (UMIN000036762, baseline-adjusted p=.058, no adjusted estimate; raw medians 46.5 versus 46.0), and a botanical blend (PMID38887495, p=.149), while fresh-derived rosemary-water trials omit clinical depression and a 2026 review pools heterogeneous end scores at SMD -0.29 (95% CI -1.01 to 0.42). No evidence indicates worsening; the dried-leaf SSRI trial retains a minimal_signal against worsening. Isolated carnosic acid is linked to rosemary and common sage, with inflammatory or tissue injury measures in a high-fat mouse model. The preparation and endpoints do not establish a further depressive symptom estimate. The ursolic acid review includes forced-swim and tail-suspension results with the isolated compound and names rosemary among natural sources. The preclinical findings do not estimate the effect of fresh rosemary on human depressive symptoms. An isolated carnosic-acid review and lab report gives no depressive symptom outcome. The indexed Nematolahi abstract and publisher preview belong to the previously assessed student family, whose original numerical HADS depression table remains open. The modest indirect lean continues to rest on the separate Azizi dried-leaf SSRI add-on comparison with baseline imbalance.
