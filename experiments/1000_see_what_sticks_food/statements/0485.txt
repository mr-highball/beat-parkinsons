ID: 0485
Category: spice
Ingredient: saffron
Preparation: dried
Symptom: fatigue severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming dried saffron worsens fatigue severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 55
Rationale: The Isfahan trial directly assessed Parkinson fatigue after swallowing tablets containing dried Sargol stigma powder versus placebo. The adjusted difference was −9.20 points (95% CI −13.76 to −4.65), favoring less fatigue. The primary sample-size calculation was for a biomarker. The original registry and September 2024 journal submission omit the standalone fatigue scale. It first appears in the archived July 2025 registry revision and August 9, 2025 journal R1, both after the outcome manuscript was received. The fixed preparation and single eligible Parkinson trial keep the direction provisional. Non-Parkinson extract comparisons are mixed or inconclusive. The Ashtiani syrup lacks a control and its initial fatigue mean exceeds the scale range described in its own methods. The matched Parkinson sample supports easing and opposes the frozen worsening claim. Internal outcome-selection and study completion dates and independent Parkinson fatigue comparisons remain open. The original fibromyalgia extract-versus-duloxetine report supplies an inconclusive Global Fatigue Index contrast with a wide interval. Its active comparator and non-Parkinson population limit transfer. Three Alzheimer trials report sparse fatigue adverse-event counts after stigma extract, and the perimenopausal SF-36 energy/fatigue comparison is inconclusive. Each remains contextual to the direct Parkinson fatigue measurement. The separate standardized-extract trial reports a favorable broad sleep-related impairment result with a tiredness item. No fatigue-specific estimate is reported, and the registration excludes Parkinson disease.
