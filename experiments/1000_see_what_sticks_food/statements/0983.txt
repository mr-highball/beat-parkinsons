ID: 0983
Category: spice
Ingredient: sichuan pepper
Preparation: toasted
Symptom: pain severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming toasted sichuan pepper reduces pain severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: One controlled mouse experiment (Bian 2019; 10 mice per group across six arms, including a positive-control arm) reports lower 20-minute acetic-acid-evoked writhing counts after oral volatile fractions from dry-fried (toasted) and raw huajiao versus vehicle (18.40 and 21.10 dry-fried, 19.50 and 27.20 raw vs 49.60 vehicle), although full-text methods, fried-versus-raw comparison, and botanical species voucher remain unverified. Local application of isolated hydroxy-alpha-sanshool reduced inflammatory mechanical hypersensitivity without altering heat hypersensitivity in mice (PMID23652591), an uncontrolled nine-woman xerostomia series of mixed Zanthoxylum oil capsules noted oral-discomfort gains without placebo (PMC6392883), and a 40-woman placebo-controlled IBS trial of multi-herb daikenchuto was null for abdominal and rectal pain without isolating Sichuan pepper (PMID26486374). Volatile-fraction rather than whole toasted spice, unverified full-text details, multi-herb clinical nullity, and absence of Parkinson pain data keep the easing signal at minimal_signal.
