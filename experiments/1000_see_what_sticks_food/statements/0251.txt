Category: spice
Ingredient: turmeric
Preparation: ground
Symptom: fall frequency
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming ground turmeric worsens fall frequency in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Unknown
Confidence: 50
Rationale: The two original Alzheimer trial arms each reported one combined fall-or-dizziness event. Three additional dementia cases used turmeric powder in capsules and measured behavioral symptoms and caregiver distress without separate falls. Parkinson formulated-curcumin studies give composite motor or safety outcomes. A stopped multi-supplement arm supplies no attributable comparison. Different Huntington model diets produced opposing rotarod results and a heterogeneous Parkinson rodent synthesis favored longer rotarod performance. No family isolates culinary ground turmeric and Parkinson fall frequency, so the scorecard remains open. The full solid-lipid curcumin trial in 39 healthy adults gives inconclusive group contrasts for rapid-step time, step errors and walking speed. Cellular monoamine release and sleep-deprived rat maze performance supply neither fall counts nor a compatible exposure comparison. The formulation, endpoint and population limits remain material when both signs are considered. The 2018 curcumin pain-model paper provides a separate control comparison in healthy mice. Time to fall from a rotating rod, expressed as percent impairment, had t=.2988, df=8 and p>.05 after acute injected curcumin. This behavioral control is inconclusive for motor impairment and does not measure recurring clinical falls. The additional review concerns curcumin formulations in Alzheimer disease and sleep deprivation. Clinical falls after culinary ground turmeric remain unresolved.
