ID: 0072
Category: oil
Ingredient: olive oil
Preparation: extra-virgin
Symptom: constipation severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming extra-virgin olive oil reduces constipation severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: A double-blind randomized trial in 140 analyzed adults with functional constipation found lower four-week Rome III severity (-4.22, p < 0.001) and better Bristol stool form (+1.14, p < 0.001) with two tablespoons daily of raw extra-virgin versus refined olive oil, while excluding neurological disease, lacking an oil-free arm and omitting polyphenol assay. An ulcerative-colitis crossover abstract reports reduced GSRS constipation after extra-virgin olive oil, with full between-oil tables paywalled. In Parkinson disease, MEDI-PD's Mediterranean diet comparison was inconclusive (p = 0.600), the eight-person pilot was uncontrolled and multicomponent, and the KIM crossover encouraged olive oil in both phases; an epilepsy ketogenic cohort recorded 26.2% constipation without an oil-free control. The matched exposure and symptom contrast outside Parkinson disease supports only a minimal easing lean. The TANDIM IBS randomized diet comparison also found inconclusive GSRS constipation despite broader gastrointestinal improvement. It does not isolate olive oil.
