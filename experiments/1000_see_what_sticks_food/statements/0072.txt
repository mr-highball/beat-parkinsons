ID: 0072
Category: oil
Ingredient: olive oil
Preparation: extra-virgin
Symptom: constipation severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming extra-virgin olive oil reduces constipation severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: A randomized active-oil comparison in 140 adults with functional constipation favored raw extra-virgin over refined olive oil on constipation and stool form. It excluded neurological causes. The Albacete original shows a favorable association for virgin olive oil, contrary to a secondary review classification, with only 11 low consumers and no controlled Parkinson analysis. MEDI-PD and the independent Fernandez waitlist trial found inconclusive Parkinson whole-diet comparisons. Fernandez measured olive oil within dietary adherence and did not assign isolated oil or grade. The ulcerative-colitis crossover between-oil result and an isolated Parkinson trial remain open. The matched active-oil comparison supports a minimal easing lean. A synucleinopathy review explicitly lists extra-virgin olive oil as customary constipation advice without a trial at the time of publication. Its separate fermented milk comparison does not estimate olive oil. This provides no additional directional estimate. The two microbiome reviews describe the olive-oil food-frequency association and Mediterranean dietary context. They do not add an independent extra-virgin olive-oil constipation comparison to the existing clinical record. Publisher Figure 2 now confirms lower constipation severity after extra-virgin olive oil than after canola oil in the 32-person ulcerative colitis crossover (paired p<.001). This supplements the independent functional-constipation comparison and is eligible as a limited indirect easing signal because the exposure and symptom fit. Its magnitude, tolerability, period and order effects remain unresolved. The planned COLONIC trial is a separate registry record without outcomes. No isolated Parkinson estimate is available, so keep the minimal signal. The review separately discusses constipation in Parkinson disease and olive oil within a Mediterranean pattern. It supplies no comparison of extra-virgin olive oil for constipation. The earlier active-oil trial outside Parkinson disease remains the closest directional evidence. The 2022 gut-brain review places olive oil in a wider dietary pattern and supplies no grade-specific constipation estimate. The flavonoids review mentions olive oil within a dietary pattern. It adds no isolated extra-virgin olive oil constipation result beyond the earlier comparisons. The Parkinson phase Ib study recorded one constipation event in a three-person active formulation arm. Olive oil carried compounds in every arm, and grade and constipation severity were not compared. The autonomic review identifies its extra-virgin olive-oil dietary suggestion as untested. The earlier oil comparison remains the closest evidence. The Bahrami surgical trial author abstract gives a favorable bowel comparison for extra-virgin olive oil versus usual care, with no full estimates or stool-score definition. The NHANES association comes from a dietary composite and total fat ratio. The Fernandez randomized Parkinson whole-diet result was inconclusive between arms and its final abstract shares the preprint cohort. These sources do not elevate the minimal easing anchor from the functional-constipation comparison. Fieldwork from the Aydin region describes fig and olive oil used together for stomach discomfort, and the paper cites a combined use for constipation. The oil grade and symptom change are unavailable.
