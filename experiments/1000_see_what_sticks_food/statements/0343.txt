ID: 0343
Category: oil
Ingredient: olive oil
Preparation: cold-pressed
Symptom: anxiety severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming cold-pressed olive oil reduces anxiety severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: The Religa cross-sectional study linked higher olive-oil quantity to lower HADS anxiety outside Parkinson disease, with adjusted difference −1.86 points and 95% CI −3.54 to −0.18. Oil grade was unknown and the unadjusted item did not survive multiplicity correction. A cold-pressed rat-oil comparison had selected favorable behavior and inconclusive other maze outcomes. DieTBra did not isolate a favorable additive oil result and the overlapping TANDIM follow-up likewise provided no olive-oil-specific anxiety estimate. MEDI-PD is inconclusive for the oil. The newly identified hydroxytyrosol study concerns cultured retinal cells and contributes no symptom sign. The quantified human association retains a minimal easing tier and a direct matched Parkinson comparison remains open. The wine review names olive oil in a Mediterranean diet description without an independently assigned cold-pressed exposure or anxiety result. Olive oil appears in the earlier total-score food survey and in a Mediterranean pattern description. The surveys and review comparisons do not identify cold-pressed grade or an anxiety-specific effect. The cell study gives an olive-oil constituent context with separate laboratory endpoints. The COLONIC registry names extra-virgin oil and HADS anxiety without results. This adds neither an independent outcome nor a cold-pressed olive oil Parkinson anxiety contrast. A 2020 survey of older people at risk of dementia has a GAD-7 anxiety measure and an olive-oil item in the overall Mediterranean diet score. It gives no separately estimated cold-pressed olive-oil comparison. The instrument-level relationship does not change the current rating. Bradbury et al. observed within-group falls in perceived stress with olive oil. The olive-oil comparison with nonrandomized no treatment was inconclusive, as was fish oil versus olive oil. Grade and Parkinson anxiety remain open. The broader nutrients review combines these contrasts. The early-adversity study lists olive oil in the Mediterranean pattern and groups it with several other patterns. Its anxiety comparison follows adversity, without a separate cold-pressed oil or Parkinson result. Olive oil occurs in the Mediterranean diet discussion of a fasting review. The paper supplies no isolated cold-pressed exposure or oil-attributable Parkinson anxiety estimate. Olive oil occurs in the gut-brain review as part of a broader dietary pattern discussed in relation to mood. The article does not estimate anxiety by oil intake or grade in Parkinson disease. The 2022 gut-brain review discusses a Mediterranean pattern without a cold-pressed olive oil and anxiety contrast. Olive oil appears in dietary pattern background without a cold-pressed oil or Parkinson anxiety estimate. The Parkinson formulation study recorded one anxiety event in a small active arm and used olive oil across all arms. The olive oil grade and separate anxiety effect remain unclear. The autonomic review discusses the oil in constipation advice, leaving the existing anxiety evidence tier unchanged. A 90th-versus-10th-percentile comparison in UK Biobank gives incident anxiety HR 0.905 with FDR q=.020475 for olive oil. Oil grade is unreported, and the separate incident Parkinson estimate is HR 1.01 (p=.878). This supports exposure context, without adding a clinical anxiety-severity result in Parkinson disease.
