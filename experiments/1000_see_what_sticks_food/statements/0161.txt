Category: spice
Ingredient: cinnamon
Preparation: ground
Symptom: pain severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming ground cinnamon reduces pain severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Three independent randomized human trials outside Parkinson disease report lower visual-analogue pain with encapsulated oral cinnamon bark powder versus starch placebo in primary dysmenorrhea (n=114, 24-72 h), migraine (n=43 completers, p<.001), and rheumatoid arthritis (n=36, abstract p<.001), alongside a high-attrition multiple-sclerosis trial with baseline imbalance and endpoint switch (VAS vs registered PQAS). Competing controlled trials show null upper abdominal pain for cinnamon-sesame macerate versus sesame oil and inconsistent exercise-soreness findings, while the single Parkinson rehabilitation case combined cassia with multimodal therapy. One oral ground Ceylon cinnamon experiment in female rats found smaller bladder-distension reflexes with induced cystitis and larger responses in uninflamed bladders at the same higher dose. The mixed animal proxy supplies no transferable sign. Differences in culinary ground exposure, non-Parkinson pain etiologies, and reporting limits keep the favorable easing signal at minimal_signal.
