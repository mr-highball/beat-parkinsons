Ingredient: sichuan pepper
Preparation: ground
Symptom: fatigue severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming ground sichuan pepper reduces fatigue severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: One double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial in 82 healthy adults aged 30-55 (Kennedy 2019, PMID31835620, PMC6950039, NCT03673930) reports lower day-56 cognitive-task mental fatigue with 80 mg/day standardized Zanthoxylum armatum extract in MCT oil versus sunflower-oil placebo (p=.006), although pretreatment mental fatigue was already higher in the extract arm and primary registered endpoint was chronic secondary memory. Two healthy-gamer extract crossover registrations with fatigue outcomes (NCT07047105, NCT07752329) have no posted results. Standardized extract versus culinary ground Sichuan pepper, non-Parkinson healthy sample, and baseline imbalance keep the easing signal at minimal_signal. The primary day-56 analysis adjusts to each participant's day-1 pre-dose score, and Figure 8 has 39 analyzed per arm. Baseline imbalance remains a limitation. The multicomponent formula describes customary use for fatigue without isolating Zanthoxylum. The author abstract reviews polysaccharide fractions and lists fatigue among applications. No isolated ground culinary pepper outcome is reported. The 2026 paper measures extraction and laboratory material properties. Its mouse exercise-fatigue reference is a distinct study of leaf polysaccharide. The Kennedy 2019 supplement Tables S5–S6 give inconclusive acute task fatigue (p=.07) and day-56 pre-dose changes favoring extract during repetitions 2 and 3 with 39 per group. For repetition 2 the changes were −10.6 mm versus −1.31 mm, with SEM 3.10 mm in each arm. The article reports F(1,76)=8.01, p=.006 overall. Baseline ratings differed. The final post-dose time label is 6 h in Table S6 and 5 h in the article. These outcomes involve standardized Nepalese pepper extract in healthy adults.
