ID: 0502
Category: food
Ingredient: date
Preparation: dried
Symptom: pain severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming dried date reduces pain severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Unknown
Confidence: 50
Rationale: The 21-person whole dried Ajwa crossover reported near-identical abdominal pain diary means, and the pediatric whole-date and mouse comparisons did not clearly separate groups. Date syrups had favorable labor-pain observations with preparation, comparator and population limits. The 34-person date-seed exercise trial reported lower pain at 24 hours (3.35 vs 5.00 on a 0–10 scale, p<.001), with an inconclusive immediate contrast. Its companion biomarkers belong to the same cohort. Parkinson bar studies supply whole-product or composite outcomes without an attributable date-paste and pain comparison. Separate public accounts recall less abdominal pain or more discomfort after date fruit, without an established Parkinson diagnosis or controlled exposure. Extract results are confounded by sedation. No observed study direction transfers defensibly to whole dried-date intake and pain severity in Parkinson disease.
