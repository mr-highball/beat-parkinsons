ID: 0507
Category: spice
Ingredient: turmeric
Preparation: ground
Symptom: bradykinesia severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming ground turmeric reduces bradykinesia severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Multiple independent oral curcuminoid experiments in Parkinson-model rodents show improved bradykinesia and locomotor speed proxies versus lesioned controls: oral curcumin shortened pole-descent latency and raised open-field mean speed in MPTP mice (PMID37388445, p<.01 each), oral intragastric curcumin 160 mg/kg/day for two weeks improved open-field average speed, distance and rotarod latency in 6-OHDA rats (PMID37484231, PMC10360947, n=6/group; 40/80 mg/kg limited), middle-dose oral curcumin improved pole descent in MPTP mice (PMID36034698), oral curcumin improved pole descent and mobility time in rats (PMID40208367), oral demethoxycurcumin attenuated rotenone rat motor deficits (PMID29576695, PMC5858249), and curcumin nanoparticles enhanced MPTP mouse movement time and activity (PMID41878684, PMC13006185). In human Parkinson studies, nanomicelle curcumin showed a composite Part III time-by-treatment p=.04 without a separate bradykinesia item (PMID35636380), and a nonrandomized phospholipid-curcuminoid cohort noted slower OFF composite progression (+5 vs +11, PMID35556131). Conversely, a non-Parkinson curcumin walking trial gave numerically smaller 400-m gait-speed gain than placebo (+.014 vs +.103 m/s, NCT03085680) and young non-Parkinson mice fed isolated curcumin showed poorer skilled pellet reaching with null open-field movement (PMID24723215). Isolated curcuminoid extracts rather than culinary ground turmeric, rodent-only bradykinesia items, and competing non-PD gait/reaching data keep the easing signal at minimal_signal.
