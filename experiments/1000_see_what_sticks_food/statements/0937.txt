ID: 0937
Category: spice
Ingredient: ginger
Preparation: grated
Symptom: anxiety severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming grated ginger reduces anxiety severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Unknown
Confidence: 50
Rationale: The craniotomy supplement gives inconclusive patient anxiety VAS comparisons for ginger extract. A healthy-adult root powder comparison is inconclusive, and the SSRI adjunct abstract conflicts with itself about direction. The Ginger-PD registry has no standalone anxious-mood result. Simon's artificial permeability study and the newer injected 6-gingerol concentration study concern constituent passage or exposure. They do not measure anxiety. A firsthand report describes calming after grated ginger with uncertain preparation, timing and diagnosis. Rodent extract behavior supplies indirect evidence. The available comparisons leave the effect of grated ginger on diagnosed Parkinson anxiety open. The newer Jambi review attributes ginger to traditional blood-pressure use rather than anxiety and supplies no clinical Parkinson result. In the chemotherapy trial, all three HADS anxiety intervals cross zero. The separate migraine extract trial observed similar day-90 Beck means. These are original randomized comparisons of different preparations in other populations.
