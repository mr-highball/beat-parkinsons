Category: food
Ingredient: dark chocolate
Preparation: plain
Symptom: anxiety severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming plain dark chocolate worsens anxiety severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Two open-label randomized trials outside Parkinson disease report lower state anxiety after solid dark chocolate versus no snack before thyroid biopsy (n=99, STAI group-by-time p=.01) or mineral water in trainee nurses (n=47, 3 days), leaving expectancy effects unresolved. Competing randomized evidence includes a null acute state-anxiety trial in healthy adults (n=90), a small masked chronic-fatigue crossover without a direct between-treatment contrast (n=10), a brewed-cocoa crossover null versus water, an order-limited null in a 30-person matched cocoa-flavanol beverage crossover, and MEDI-PD restricting dark chocolate within whole-diet Parkinson counseling (HAM-A p=.745). A separate 30-person dark-chocolate cohort used baseline anxiety for stratification and reported only metabolic outcomes. The easing trials outside Parkinson support a minimal signal. In rats, cacao mass had a favorable acute avoidance result, a contrasting longer-duration trend and no change in escape. In healthy mice, isolated epicatechin shifted open-field exploration. The animal tasks and constituent do not resolve solid dark chocolate or clinical Parkinson anxiety.
