ID: 0503
Category: spice
Ingredient: nigella seed
Preparation: ground
Symptom: bradykinesia severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming ground nigella seed worsens bradykinesia severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Multiple independent controlled Parkinson-model rodent experiments report improved locomotor speed, open-field movement, or reduced drug-induced catalepsy and extrapyramidal movement signs with Nigella sativa seed extracts, seed oil, or thymoquinone: oral isolated thymoquinone increased rotenone-mouse open-field distance and movement speed (PMID40951472, n=3/arm; beam/wire inconclusive), isolated thymoquinone improved chlorpromazine-rat catalepsy and locomotion (PMID27180446), oral Nigella sativa oil 0.2 mL/rat reduced haloperidol-induced EPS movement disorders and oral dyskinesia (Malik 2016, PMID27752803; abstract-level depth), oral Nigella oil increased open-field activity in rotenone rats (DOI10.21608/ejh.2024.304554.2104; design discrepancies noted), and higher Nigella seed extract doses lowered drug-induced mouse catalepsy (DOI10.4236/wjns.2014.44035). Conversely, a single-plant hydroalcoholic Nigella seed extract haloperidol catalepsy experiment was inconclusive (PMID41438691). Animal-only models, oil/extract/constituent exposures rather than culinary ground nigella seed, small group sizes, and the competing null catalepsy study keep the easing signal contradicting the worsening hypothesis at minimal_signal.
