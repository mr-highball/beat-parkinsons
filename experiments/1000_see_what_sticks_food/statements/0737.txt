ID: 0737
Category: spice
Ingredient: cinnamon
Preparation: ground
Symptom: constipation severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming ground cinnamon reduces constipation severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Unknown
Confidence: 50
Rationale: A controlled ground-cinnamon crossover outside Parkinson disease had no clear daily stool-frequency contrast. Human cassia water-extract findings concern model-relevant transit during diarrhea. Extract experiments in constipated and diarrheal rats move pellet frequency in different directions under different conditions. The symptom-severity direction remains unresolved. The 2017 iRBD study used cinnamon as one odor in an aggregate smell-identification test. It assessed baseline constipation separately. The diagnostic setting supplies no constipation comparison attributed to cinnamon. In a randomized extract adjunct trial, the Table 2 constipation comparison was inconclusive (11 to 6 versus 18 to 15 of 49, p=.10), despite the abstract grouping it with significant results. A combined leaf tea reports qualitative bowel improvement without an attributable comparison. Koyna Valley traditions describe root paste for constipation and use ground bark for a different symptom.
