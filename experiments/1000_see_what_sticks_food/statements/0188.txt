ID: 0188
Category: food
Ingredient: fig
Preparation: raw
Symptom: drooling severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming raw fig reduces drooling severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Unknown
Confidence: 50
Rationale: A clinical case describes immediate drooling during a likely allergic reaction after fresh fig in one child, with corroborating fig sensitization but no oral challenge. The randomized drug-plus-syrup study reports drooling in 17, 15 and 5 of 40 children across the fig-and-almond, almond-and-raisin and placebo arms. Those event counts, acute swelling and different formulations provide adverse-reaction context. They do not estimate a change in Parkinson drooling severity. The clinical parkinsonism sources do not test raw fig. A six-patient levodopa crossover reports one better, one worse and four unchanged drooling scores under its add-on regimen. Japanese scale validation reports a combined saliva-and-drooling item separately from chewing and swallowing. Neither adds a fig estimate.
