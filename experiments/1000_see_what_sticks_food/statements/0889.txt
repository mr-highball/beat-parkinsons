ID: 0889
Category: spice
Ingredient: cinnamon
Preparation: ground
Symptom: walking slowness
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming ground cinnamon worsens walking slowness in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Three oral ground Cinnamomum verum experiments from one research program report improved rotarod, open-field movement, and shorter timed pole turning/descent in aged A53T (PMID32889602, n=5-6/group; 2025 correction replaced separate benzoate histology panels) and MPTP mouse Parkinson models (PMID24946862, PMID31119595). No human clinical gait-speed trial isolates ground cinnamon (Park 2026 OA15.04 reports intake behavior only). Same-program mouse agility proxies without clinical gait data sustain only a minimal_signal against worsening. The 2016 study reports better vertical-rod performance with oral Cinnamomum cassia bark extract than in haloperidol controls, each p<.05 for the two groups of seven. The extraction method, quantitative graph estimates and precise assignment timing remain unresolved. The model task addresses sensorimotor behavior. Ground culinary cinnamon, human Parkinson gait speed and walking slowness were outside the experiment. The cited Churihar original remains unauthenticated. The later Wang paper reports a null comparison for isolated cinnamaldehyde and haloperidol-induced mouse catalepsy. Conflicting venue trails and the unavailable numeric primary outcome remain open. The 2016 cassia-extract report is a different publication and cannot verify that comparison.
