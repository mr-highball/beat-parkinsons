ID: 0725
Category: spice
Ingredient: ginger
Preparation: grated
Symptom: pain severity
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming grated ginger reduces pain severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe True
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Multiple randomized human trials outside Parkinson disease report lower pain after oral ginger rhizome powder or standardized extract versus placebo, including 11-day raw and heat-treated ground ginger capsules for eccentric muscle pain (n=74 across two studies), menstrual pain powder capsules (n=120), acute migraine add-on extract with ketoprofen (n=60, p=.03), and steamed GGE03 extract in functional dyspepsia (p<.001) and knee osteoarthritis. Substantive competing randomized trials show null pain effects for powdered ginger in knee osteoarthritis, migraine prevention, post-hysterectomy recovery, exercise joint pain (p=.690), acute post-exercise crossover, and craniotomy care. No controlled Parkinson pain trial exists (recruiting Ginger-PD NCT07882108 measures ghrelin/gut markers); preparation, population, and null-trial heterogeneity keep the easing lean at minimal_signal. The Marquesan survey distinguishes the plant-inventory entry for culinary ginger from the shampoo-ginger use. The cosmetic documentation contributes no direction for grated ginger and Parkinson pain. The eugenol review lists ginger as a possible compound source. The studied purified molecule and behavioral animal outcomes add no ginger-specific Parkinson pain result. The ginger aging review points to separate knee osteoarthritis syntheses, animal and constituent studies. The newer Omani inventory describes ginger rhizome use for stomach ailments. These materials add no attributable grated-ginger Parkinson pain comparison, leaving earlier closer evidence as the basis of the lean. Another single-group study reports lower menstrual pain after a boiled ginger, lemongrass and date drink. Its timing, mixed composition and uncontrolled design do not give an independent grated ginger finding.
