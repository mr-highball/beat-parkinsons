ID: 0428
Category: oil
Ingredient: pumpkin seed oil
Preparation: toasted
Symptom: shortened stride severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming toasted pumpkin seed oil worsens shortened stride severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: Two Parkinson-relevant rodent experiments show longer measured stride length after oral pumpkin-seed preparations or a documented constituent: haloperidol-exposed rats given ascending methanolic Cucurbita pepo seed extract arms had figure-derived mean stride lengths of 6.4, 7.3 and 8.0 cm versus 5.7 cm in disease controls (PMID33759084, Figure 4; group counts and comparator footnotes unverified), and haloperidol-exposed mice (n=6/group) given higher oral isolated p-coumaric acid had longer day-21 stride than untreated controls (DOI10.1177/0976500X221150837, p<.05 and p<.01). Roasting-chemistry analysis confirms measurable p-coumaric acid (0.5-0.6 ug/g) and ferulic acid (0.9-1.8 ug/g) in 130-150 C roasted pressed pumpkin seed oil (DOI10.1002/ejlt.201700110) without establishing food-to-extract dose equivalence. Animal-only gait evidence, extract-versus-oil exposure gaps, and absence of human Parkinson testing keep the easing signal contradicting the worsening hypothesis at minimal_signal.
