Category: spice
Ingredient: sumac
Preparation: ground
Symptom: constipation severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming ground sumac worsens constipation severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Maybe False
Confidence: 50.005
Rationale: One randomized four-arm trial in 104 adults with functional dyspepsia (85 analyzed; Saravani 2025, PMID40365186, PMC12068504) reports greater week-8 constipation subscore improvement for aqueous Rhus coriaria (sumac) extract alone (n=24) and combined with eating-habit changes (n=25) versus omeprazole (n=25) in adjusted model 6 (p=.002 and p<.001) with zero adverse events, although unadjusted contrasts were unstated and effect magnitudes/confidence intervals were omitted from Table 6. Conversely, a preclinical mouse castor-oil diarrhea and rabbit jejunum study found crude sumac extract slowed induced diarrhea and relaxed intestinal contractions (DOI10.3329/bjp.v9i4.20879). Active omeprazole comparator, aqueous extract versus ground sumac, non-Parkinson dyspepsia cohort, and unreported effect sizes keep the easing lean contradicting the worsening hypothesis at minimal_signal.
