ID: 0325
Category: food
Ingredient: carrot
Preparation: roasted
Symptom: executive function impairment
Direction: reduces
Statement: Consuming roasted carrot reduces executive function impairment in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Unknown
Confidence: 50
Rationale: Carrot-juice controls had inconclusive Trail Making B change in older adults. A multicomponent capsule containing carrot had favorable within-period TESEN changes in healthy adults. The purple carrot extract model used an animal memory task. Roasted carrot and Parkinson executive impairment remain unresolved. The protocol verifies the executive-function measures planned for the existing CIRCUS trial family. No arm-level cognitive result or isolated roasted carrot exposure is reported. The 2026 CIRCUS report includes carrot in the common-produce diet with 39 adults participating and 36 completing. It reports lipid changes and no detected difference in office blood pressure or vascular measures. Neither the 2026 report nor the existing metabolomics abstract supplies the registered executive test results or an isolated roasted-carrot comparison. The repository abstract describes 15 participants divided among no fasting, water-only fasting and fasting with carrot juice plus water. The three Stroop tasks show no reported group main effect. The time-related changes include both fasting groups, with no usable independent carrot comparison. The rat study used carrot juice with water/feed and dopamine comparators. Its short discussion calls a faster return to the shock-associated compartment better memory. This interpretation and the apparent contrast need clarification. Passive avoidance is not a clinical executive-function measure.
