Category: oil
Ingredient: pumpkin seed oil
Preparation: cold-pressed
Symptom: pain severity
Direction: worsens
Statement: Consuming cold-pressed pumpkin seed oil worsens pain severity in people with Parkinson's disease.
Verdict: Unknown
Confidence: 50
Rationale: The oil add-on and oil-coupled ultrasound provide indirect pelvic-pain estimates, while the menopause joint-pain comparison was inconclusive. A recovered cancer-treatment trial reports improved aggregate oral symptoms after topical cold-pressed oil plus Solcoseryl. The pain item was unreported. The frozen Parkinson pain direction remains unresolved. The exact publisher and PDF locators are confirmed. The accessible original abstract reports a pain-domain advantage but no magnitude, verified processing grade or Parkinson comparison. The controlled pelvic-pain adjunct abstract gives overall response counts and qualitative pain-domain direction, without an inspectable pain table or verified pressing. The separate completed cold-pressed bladder study has a secondary combined symptom endpoint and no posted results. The original table gives week-12 between-arm joint pain p=.663, separately from within-arm change. Oil pressing is unspecified and the comparison concerns menopausal symptoms. The 42-participant three-group study reports mean pain decreases of 3.86 (SD 1.46), 1.29 (SD 1.86), and 0.57 (SD 0.94) on a 0–10 scale for high-dose extract, low-dose extract, and starch. The high-dose change comparison was reported p<.001 and low-dose versus starch p=.205. Baseline NRS means were 6.29, 4.93, and 3.86. No baseline-adjusted contrast, concealed allocation, or oil fraction was established. This was a seed extract in adolescents with premenstrual symptoms. The 2026 report may overlap. The separate oral oil adjunct still lacks obtainable numeric pain results and verified pressing grade.
