# P27 herb provider review, 2026-11-16

This receipt covers the 205 returned Europe PMC herb appearances (195 thyme and 10 bay) and three PubMed-only thyme appearances. Peer identity was compared within each mapped expression using PMID, not globally. The saved provider abstracts were inspected for source-specific setting and outcome, with manually entered rationales in herb-reasons.tsv; the FPC program joins these to exact memberships and validates all tuples. This is a scoped abstract screen, not a full-text or systematic review of every source. Six neuroprotection review/preprint contexts and the unreadable correction are pending.

2026-01-28: [Salem et al., DOI 10.3389/fphar.2025.1728908, PMID41684518](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1728908/full), Methods 2.7 and Results 3.1, reports 50 juvenile male rats assigned among five arms of propionic acid, isolated thymol 30 mg/kg, probiotic, combination or normal control. Y-maze spontaneous alternation is interpreted as attention and spatial memory; thymol increased the measure 35% relative to model controls, with six plotted animals per arm. The paper's other measures include open-field locomotion, conditioned avoidance and swimming. This is neither fresh thyme leaves nor a Parkinson trial. Authors cite the 2022 Abu-Elfotuh paper, but the model, treatment and designs describe distinct experiments.

2022-10-06: [Abu-Elfotuh et al., PMID36271575](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36271575/), abstract only: 40 male rat pups in five groups, MSG model and oral thymol 30 mg/kg/day, thymoquinone 10 mg/kg/day, both or controls, eight weeks. The authors report improvements in attention-related behavior against the model group without numerical task values in the abstract. [Corrigendum PMID37088669](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37088669/), DOI 10.1016/j.biopha.2023.114735, appeared April 21, 2023; public abstract/index does not say what it corrected. An author-upload listing exists at https://www.researchgate.net/publication/370192180 but the provider reports no preview. Exact correction and original numerical tables remain open. Do not treat the correction as a second study or infer its content.

The publisher routes https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S075333222201188X and https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0753332223005231 both returned HTTP 403 on the web provider. Equal allocation across the five groups is not expressly stated in the abstract.

Further 2026-11-16 correction attempts: the DOI route returned HTTP 403; ResearchGate's author-posted 'Read full-text' button returned HTTP 403 and its linked download route returned HTTP 404. The official PubMed correction record lacks an abstract. These are specific route failures rather than proof that no lawful copy exists.

Salem cites a separate 2022 original [PMID36559006, PMC9785377](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9785377/), DOI 10.3390/ph15121554. Its Methods 4.4.6 and Results describe oral isolated thymol 30 mg/kg/day before injected manganese for 35 days in rats. Figure 3 spontaneous Y-maze alternation was approximately 1.23-fold the manganese control, and the study calls the task spatial working memory; no dedicated attention assay, fresh thyme or clinical Parkinson participants. Official EFetch full XML saved as pmc9785377.xml. The initially requested PMC9786243 resolves to an unrelated citrus paper and is not this study.

PMID10617990 describes 15 dyspraxic children with pre-post improvement on manual dexterity after an oil mixture containing thyme, fish and evening primrose; no independent thyme effect or human attention comparison can be assigned. Three PubMed-only hits concern seabream immune feeding, topical eutectic formulations and antimicrobial oil review, respectively; they do not measure patient attention. The published bay-leaf inhalation vigilance study documented in primary-receipt.md was not one of the ten exact results of this particular focused expression and remains existing evidence.

The [2023 myrtenal review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10608190/) cites Fang et al., [PMID27761760](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27761760/), first public 2016-10-20, DOI 10.1007/s11011-016-9921-z: thymol 20 or 40 mg/kg in high-fat-diet mice improved reported Morris-water-maze performance versus the high-fat model, without numerical task estimates in its public abstract. It is spatial learning rather than a dedicated human attention test, and it is isolated compound. Original publisher tables and dose route require verification. The myrtenal preprint and 2023 review are one report family, not independent confirmations. Full text of the 2018 [spice review PMC6435951](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6435951/) is saved in neuroreviews.xml. Its thyme section cites a thyme extract rat anxiety maze study and thymol/other compound studies, but its reference 80 is the Deng antidepressant paper, despite being attached to a prior amyloid/scopolamine-cognition statement. That specific citation does not establish the asserted cognitive result. Additional review references remain open rather than presumed negative.
