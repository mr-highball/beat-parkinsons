# Food preparation and clinical symptom measures, November 15, 2026

## 0878, raw orange and constipation

A 221-person randomized study in healthy adults found more frequent and softer stools after processed orange pomace (p = .0281 and .04), with more gas and bloating. The registered constipation score has no separate numerical result in the accessible publication or official registry. The 91-person later crossover gave a stool-frequency interaction p = .46. For a different endpoint, at least one day without a bowel movement, Table S7 gives a product main effect of p = .004 and an interaction p = .40. The article gives p = .002 for the main effect. Table S7 begins with 38/89 and 32/86 people meeting the endpoint at baseline and ends with 33/89 and 26/84. The pomace baseline cells sum to 86 despite the column heading of 84. This is weak context about processed fiber. It supplies no convincing differential change in that crossover. Two acute trials compared fiber-matched whole orange and pomace products and measured glycemia only. A surgical preprint favored coffee over juice for time to first bowel movement and had no untreated arm. The Parkinson survey's opposite association groups all fresh fruit and pools missing responses with its constipation category. The raw orange hypothesis stays Unknown. ## Other food and symptom comparisons

- **0964, raw pineapple and urinary urgency.** An original Brazilian survey gives one pineapple consumer among eight women with mild and one among 12 with severe urinary-incontinence impact, pooling subtypes. A US urgency-incontinence analysis finds adjusted OR 1.29 (95% CI 1.03–1.62) for grouped high-acid foods. The public abstract leaves pineapple membership unverified. The closest prior pineapple-or-juice flare comparison was inconclusive and there is no isolated urgency episode measure.
