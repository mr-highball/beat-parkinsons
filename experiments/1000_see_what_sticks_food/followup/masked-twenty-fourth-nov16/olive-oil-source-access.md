## Relevant scope

- PMID 33173523 is the strongest exact in-scope contextual result. Full text is a 126-person MCI/SCD study during COVID quarantine. It includes an Apathy Evaluation Scale and the MEDAS Mediterranean diet screen, where olive-oil use is only one item. Table 4 for overall nonadherence vs apathy is inconclusive: unadjusted OR 3.02 (95% CI 0.86–10.63), p=.085; adjusted OR 2.76 (95% CI 0.83–9.21), p=.099. The prose calls the association significant, but those table estimates are not. The source neither isolates olive oil nor specifies refined oil and does not study Parkinson disease.
- Collection PMC10643713 has a separate PD mouse-model member using olive oil as a rotenone vehicle, with exercise/CO2 interventions and locomotion/coordination outcomes; no apathy and no refinement. Collection PMC10300527 has an extra-virgin olive-oil phenolics abstract in tumor-cell autophagy. PMC7545776 has an olive-oil vehicle/control in a mouse liver-fibrosis experiment while separate conference abstracts discuss apathy in Parkinson reward decisions. These are separate member/topic contexts, not an oil-apathy result.
- A separate, independent refined-oil human exposure was identified: AU-ROOAD, NCT03824197 / PMID 36501136 / PMCID PMC9736478. Twenty-five MCI participants completed six months of 30 mL/day refined olive oil (ROO n=12) or EVOO (n=13). ROO CDR improved within-arm, but between-oil differences were not significant; apathy was not measured and participants did not have PD.
- Other routes: PMID 35512773 measured apathy in PD in relation to broad dietary-pattern scores, not isolated olive-oil exposure; PMID 37997257 measured reported olive-oil/cooking-oil intake in PD and UPDRS/PD odds, but did not classify refinement or report apathy separately.

No exact trial or human effect of refined olive oil on apathy severity in Parkinson disease was found in these screens. The access gaps above are preserved as pending, not interpreted as negative findings.
