## Primary outcomes and registered intervention

The [Bedarf prebiotic pilot](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41531-025-00885-5), original text `research/2025-2029/Source-Text/PMC11968880.txt`, lists green beans as one option in a larger fiber-rich diet, alongside daily apples and lactulose. Ten of 11 PD–spouse pairs completed four weeks. Its published adherence measure uses apple consumption and overall fiber; individual green bean intake and cooking method are not given. The paper reports composite MDS-UPDRS III and gastrointestinal scores, not fatigue severity or fall frequency, and has no bean-only comparator. Its [official DRKS00034528 record](https://drks.de/search/en/trial/DRKS00034528) was first posted retrospectively 27 June 2024; it lists microbiome and metabolomic primary outcomes, and UPDRS III and gastrointestinal secondary outcomes, not a fall count or fatigue score. The 2024 preprint and 2025 paper are one trial family, not separate cohorts. The preprint URL https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.09.24313184v1 returned HTTP 403 in this replay; the published primary and registry were accessible. Existing dated entries: `research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Registry-Followup.md#german-registration-of-a-high-fiber-dietary-pilot` and `research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Green-Pinto-Tempeh-Garlic.md#green-bean-menu-option-and-parkinson-motor-scores`.

[Hutchins and Winham 2020](https://medwinpublishers.com/FSNT/FSNT16000211.pdf) compares daily canned, drained sliced French-style green beans with canned pinto beans in a randomized crossover for adults with type 2 diabetes, eight completing both phases. Registered NCT04003194 outcomes are glucose/HbA1c; the article additionally reports GI questionnaire results. It is neither a Parkinson study nor a fatigue or fall count. The official API route https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT04003194 was inaccessible via the web tool on this replay; `research/2015-2019/Source-Text/Trials/NCT04003194.txt` records prior official fields and first registration 1 July 2019.

The only close fatigue-title record, PMID 26998181, describes a mouse swimming test using a “green beans” beverage without establishing the edible pod exposure. It was **retracted** by PMID 28932324; the latter is the retraction of the same record, not another study. It is not evidence for the frozen claim. [Retraction notice](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28932324/). The 2013 chromatography paper PMID 23905090 compares plants and reports detection for fava; it does not establish the often-repeated high levodopa content for cooked green pods or a clinical fatigue/fall outcome. Existing assay chain `research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Green-Pinto-Tempeh-Garlic.md#green-bean-seed-assays-and-a-later-levodopa-citation` and `research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Green-Pinto-Tempeh-Garlic.md#levodopa-measured-in-common-bean-pods-and-seeds` distinguish fava, mature dry seeds and uncooked pod tissue.
