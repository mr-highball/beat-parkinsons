# Focused original outcome and study-family checks

## 0889 ground cinnamon and walking slowness

The 2016 study reports better vertical-rod performance with oral Cinnamomum cassia bark extract than in haloperidol controls, each p<.05 for the two groups of seven. The extraction method, quantitative graph estimates and precise assignment timing remain unresolved. The model task addresses sensorimotor behavior. Ground culinary cinnamon, human Parkinson gait speed and walking slowness were outside the experiment. The cited Churihar original remains unauthenticated. The later Wang paper reports a null comparison for isolated cinnamaldehyde and haloperidol-induced mouse catalepsy. Conflicting venue trails and the unavailable numeric primary outcome remain open. The 2016 cassia-extract report is a different publication and cannot verify that comparison. ## 0324 refined corn oil and shortened stride

The indexed publisher snippet corroborates 2.0 g/day total soy–corn capsules and 3.0 g/day maltodextrin placebo powder. The earlier original supplement establishes the 50:50 mixture. The official registry lists 3 g/day oil. The 10-week COPD comparison still supplies no accessible arm-level gait or balance values, refining grade, isolated corn-oil effect or stride-length estimate. The 2021 analysis pools baseline physical-function data across four COPD studies, including NCT03796455. It groups 78 participants by depression status. Walking distance and gait assessments are baseline comparisons, without an assigned oil contrast or a cohort-specific intervention denominator. This report is linked context and supplies no independent efficacy trial for the oil.
