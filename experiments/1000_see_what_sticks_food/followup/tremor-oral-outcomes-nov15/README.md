# Tremor, oral exposures and outcome measurement, November 15, 2026

## Brewed black tea and rest tremor

**0904 changed to Maybe True at 51.** In a six-week placebo-controlled Parkinson trial, isolated caffeine favored lower clinician-rated rest tremor at week three (−0.32 points, 95% CI −0.73 to 0.10) and week six (−0.28, 95% CI −0.83 to 0.27). The visits came from the same cohort. The earlier uncontrolled caffeine escalation reported an average short-term improvement and included participants who reported new or worse tremor. A longer caffeine trial found no clear individual daily-living motor-item differences. Its patient-reported tremor item cannot isolate rest tremor.

Recent original measurements confirm the presence and variability of caffeine in black tea. The Parkinson trial used 200 and 400 mg daily, above the amounts measured per serving in the tea composition study. A black-tea cohort reported lower total daily-living motor scores for some LRRK2 analyses and inconclusive idiopathic analyses, with no rest-tremor contrast. The two randomized time points and measured constituent are the basis for a minimal favorable inference. Uncertainty about dose, the rest of the tea mixture, and competing observations limit transfer to the frozen beverage claim. ## Related findings and open measurements

- **0381, hazelnut oil and visual hallucinations.** The 2023 controlled feeding pilot provides the first structured related assessment for this claim. Twelve older adults were randomized and ten completed. The test oil was entirely hazelnut oil, and the comparison-period blend also contained hazelnut oil. Screening covered depression and anxiety; the trial supplies no visual hallucination measure or adverse-event table. One unspecified medical withdrawal cannot be attributed to symptoms or a particular oil. Oil grade and effects in Parkinson disease remain open. The distinct later NCT06192407 trial was recruiting without results on the official registry recheck. [Original feeding study and catalog trail](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Hazelnut-Sunflower-Oils.md#hazelnut-oil-in-an-older-adult-working-memory-trial)
- **0520, ground cayenne and postural instability.** The conference program reports sway measurements from seven young adults after oral spice. The overall comparison was inconclusive and the two nominal comparisons could reflect order or practice. The study gives no product or controlled estimate attributable to ground cayenne. [Original proceedings extract](../../../../research/2025-2029/Source-Text/2025-Aylward-ACSM-Oral-Spice-Balance-Factual-Extract.txt)
- **0983, toasted Sichuan pepper and pain.** Fractions from dry-fried and raw huajiao reduced evoked mouse writhing compared with vehicle. The abstract does not confirm a direct processing contrast or the botanical voucher. A nine-person oral study measured mouth discomfort with several ingredients, and a randomized multi-herb gastrointestinal trial was inconclusive on its pain comparison. The evidence leaves whole toasted pepper and Parkinson pain unresolved. [Original Chinese abstract extract](../../../../research/2015-2019/Source-Text/2019-Bian-Huajiao-Pain-Factual-Extract.txt) · [Existing human results](../../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Sichuan-Pepper-Followup.md)
- **0723, raw apricot and OFF time.** One rat study prepared a methanolic extract from authenticated apricot kernels. It measured catalepsy and other animal motor tests. The human low-protein crossover measured daily OFF time in people with Parkinson disease, without a raw-apricot-specific comparison. Isolated caffeine in the 2017 trial gave no numerical handwriting item result.
