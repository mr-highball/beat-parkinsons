# Watermelon case reports: 0375 and 0931

Checked 2026-11-16. Neither record is a directly matched Parkinson food experiment or supports an effect direction.

## 0375: drooling during a suspected seed-allergy reaction

Kimberley Cousins, Scott Sicherer and Amanda Agyemang, **Anaphylaxis to watermelon seeds**, DOI 10.22541/au.166805825.55777232/v1, first publicly posted November 10, 2022. The author preprint is unreviewed, as explicitly printed in the [original PDF](https://d197for5662m48.cloudfront.net/documents/publicationstatus/93623/preprint_pdf/440d5aa127355bd36cce371d29ebe9d8.pdf). [Crossref exact metadata](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.22541%2Fau.166805825.55777232%2Fv1) confirms authors and date, without a published-version relationship; exact-title and all-author searches found no verified journal successor or revised attribution. This is a search result, not proof no later version exists.

The two-year-old girl experienced lip swelling and hives within minutes after a mixed honey snack containing watermelon seed paste, followed by vomiting, gasping and excess drooling. The paper reports previous tolerance of watermelon flesh, honey and fruit juices; the seed paste was new to her. Skin prick tests were positive to the snack and a commercially prepared roasted watermelon seed butter (wheal 5 mm and 6 mm respectively; nonstandardized testing); a nonallergic control tested negative. Seed-specific serum IgE was not commercially available, there was no oral food challenge, and the authors judged seed allergy plausible while acknowledging uncertainty and other ingredients. A later pumpkin-seed reaction was also reported. These are acute allergy-related symptoms in a young child, not Parkinson salivary dysfunction or a raw-watermelon-flesh comparison. No severity or frequency estimate for 0375 is transferable.

Searches for the exact title, three authors, DOI and version yielded this preprint and later citations but no verified same-author final publication. A separately indexed 2023 report about a boy with watermelon and pumpkin seed reactions is not this two-year-old female case; do not merge without independent evidence.

## 0931: watermelon in a dementia case with orthostatic blood pressure

Rima H. Jallad, Karen A. McWhorter, Jolene F. Siple and Jennifer S. Chui, **Derailed dementia: Alzheimer's disease versus posterior cortical atrophy**, PMID 25688631, DOI 10.1111/jgs.13265, [original full letter](https://agsjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jgs.13265), first online February 17, 2015, pages 418–419. A 71-year-old man with posterior cortical atrophy placed watermelon rather than tomato on a hamburger as an example of visuoperceptual difficulty. The case reports supine pressure 130/84 and standing 114/79 mmHg and a planned quetiapine taper because of orthostatic hypotension. It does not report whether or when he ate the watermelon relative to these observations, a food challenge, measured lightheadedness, or a Parkinson diagnosis. The later reported behavioral improvement followed a treatment plan including sertraline, not an assigned watermelon intervention. This is a contextual co-mention, not a directional food–orthostasis contrast.

Review depth: both original bodies were read; the first has preprint status, the second is the final journal letter. No shared or tracked files were changed.
