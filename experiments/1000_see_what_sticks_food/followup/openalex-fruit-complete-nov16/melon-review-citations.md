# Three melon–Parkinson review source checks

Checked 2026-11-16; these are three reviews, not primary trial records. None establishes a raw-watermelon intervention in people with Parkinson disease.

## Rank 3: Adediji and Oyetola, DOI 10.33003/sajols-2026-0402-46

Official journal metadata gives publication June 30, 2026. The journal website redirects .edu.ng to .org.ng; standard web reader failed on the redirected page, but local HTTPS returned HTTP 200 and the verified publisher galley https://saheljls.fudutsinma.org.ng/index.php/saheljls/article/download/753/657/1936 supplied the full 10-page PDF `citrulline.pdf`. Existing bundled `pypdfium2_cli extract-text` produced `citrulline.txt` with page markers. PDF abstract and sections identify L-citrulline as an isolated amino acid, mention watermelon as a source, and state direct PD-model evidence is limited and supporting data largely come from non-PD systems. A cited Gu 2023 paper is an intestinal cell bioavailability model for watermelon flesh, rind and skin; Haramipour 2022 is prenatal L-citrulline and newborn mouse reflexes; neither is Parkinson patients given watermelon. No culinary watermelon outcome or human Parkinson treatment estimate was identified. Public journal landing-page abstract differs in wording from downloaded galley abstract, so preserve this exact downloaded full version rather than imply they are identical. The botanical source and PD background are not an assigned human food experiment.

## Rank 9: DOI 10.47583/ijpsrr.2021.v70i02.037

Official DOI provides a nine-page 2021 review https://doi.org/10.47583/ijpsrr.2021.v70i02.037. On journal page 310 (PDF page 6), its Parkinson paragraph discusses isolated lycopene and cites reference 1, Chen et al. 2019 review PMID 30616078, rather than an independent watermelon trial. It also mentions a 5 mg/kg tau mouse experiment for Alzheimer disease, a distinct condition. The title-page PDF lists Kamboj, Nad, Tripathi and Konar; journal issue webpage also lists Kwatra and Rumao, so authorship differs between retrieved representations and needs independent bibliographic resolution before cataloguing. This is not an independent result from Chen or from an actual food trial.

## Rank 10: Chen et al. 2019, PMID 30616078, DOI 10.1016/j.biopha.2018.12.151

Publisher DOI and ScienceDirect full body returned HTTP 403 during checks. Identity-matched public CC BY-NC-ND full-text copy at https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331443523_A_review_for_the_pharmacological_effect_of_lycopene_in_central_nervous_system_disorders preserves pages 791–801 and full References. Neither is watermelon consumption or a patient study. Primary publisher preview https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S030439401500381X and PubMed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25980996/ identify oral lycopene 5, 10, 20 mg/kg/day and motor tasks including beam crossing and foot slips. It is already indexed at research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Tomato-Stride.md#isolated-lycopene-and-narrow-beam-crossing-in-mptp-mice and is related to claim 0246, but no raw watermelon or measured human bradykinesia. The 2021 lycopene review relies on this 2019 review, which cites the same 2015 study: avoid counting the three as independent experiments.
