## 0953, boiled barley and anxiety

The original frozen wording concerns worsening Parkinson anxiety. Araki and colleagues randomized healthy adults to boiled waxy barley or white rice and reported a more favorable tension–anxiety change with barley. Both final group medians were 43. The change analysis uses individual changes and has a different interpretation from the week-eight cross-section. The visible foods, eight postrandomization exclusions, multiple exploratory comparisons, unclear prespecification of the subscale contrast and absence of a Parkinson group sharply limit transfer. The earlier barley-flour crossover found no clear between-phase anxiety contrast and the roasted barley, milk and honey study has mixed within-period outcomes. The matching food preparation and measured human outcome support **Maybe False at 51**, a minimal lean against worsening. The Parkinson anxiety comparison remains open. ## 1000, steamed broccoli and falls

Complete ClinicalTrials.gov API searches returned two records for `sulforaphane Parkinson`, two for `broccoli Parkinson`, one for `glucoraphanin Parkinson`, one for `cruciferous Parkinson`, and four for `broccoli falls`, with overlap. NCT05084365 and NCT07360977 remain without posted results. The latter lists actual primary completion on October 14, 2026 and no dedicated falls endpoint. The Parkinson broccoli seed and mustard tea series used self-ratings. The older women's cohort pooled cruciferous vegetables and its endpoint was fall-related hospitalization. The Parkinson diet trial compared plant-rich diet counseling with usual care. These sources keep the steamed broccoli fall-frequency claim at **Unknown, 50**.
