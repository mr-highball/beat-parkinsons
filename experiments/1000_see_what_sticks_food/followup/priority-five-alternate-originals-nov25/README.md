## Asparagus and fatigue

Claim 0837 states that roasted asparagus worsens fatigue in people with Parkinson disease. The scorecard now records Maybe False at the minimal-signal anchor of 50.005. The original randomized crossover evaluated enzyme-treated common asparagus stem extract in 25 healthy people. A descriptive reading of the separate placebo and extract bar pairs implies about two POMS fatigue T points more reduction with extract after a mental challenge. The paper's significance marker compares the start and end of the extract period. An uncertainty interval for the difference in changes is unavailable.

The same plant species and stem tissue provide a limited exposure link to the frozen food. The culinary preparation, industrial extraction, study population and context of the fatigue rating each weaken transfer. Roasting changes composition in a study of discarded lower stems. Its measurements do not establish equivalence to the supplement. The 2026 review provides citation coverage and shares cohorts with the existing scorecard. It reports the significant within-period change as a placebo comparison. The verified trial record distinguishes the two and is the basis for the directional judgment.

Polygonatum and common asparagus are separate genera. One research text carries both PubMed and agricultural index keys for the Polygonatum review. The scoped source checks remain pending full-body inspection, and the review contributes no common-asparagus fatigue result.
