# Sunflower oil and executive function

Claim 0772 remains **Unknown (50)** under rubric version 1. The check covers 15 material families, including one family with uncertain independence. The original Europe PMC queue for 0772 now has 18 of 18 pairs checked at body context. Complementary searches and source follow-ups remain open.

- The DICe feasibility trial found faster switching on refined sunflower oil than on coconut oil in an exploratory comparison. Parkinson disease was excluded. The 302-person MEMO trial reported no clear executive-domain difference between high-oleic sunflower and active fish-oil comparators. The Jenkins trial did not distinguish allocated treatments for Trail Making or Serial Sevens. - Oral seed oil improved recognition memory in a small scopolamine mouse study. Students had better short-term recall after capsule use in an uncontrolled study. Ordinary mice on two very high-fat diets did worse in the radial maze. A separate group receiving sunflower oil as part of a saline and handling regimen made more working-memory errors at one assessed time point than untreated rats. The diet matrix, handling, measured tasks, preparation and population prevent an isolated effect estimate for the frozen claim.
