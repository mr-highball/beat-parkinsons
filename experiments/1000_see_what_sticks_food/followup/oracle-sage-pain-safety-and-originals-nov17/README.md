## Fresh sage and clinical pain

Two earlier placebo-controlled common-sage extract studies outside Parkinson disease report lower joint or muscle discomfort in menopause, with incomplete or inconsistent item estimates. A 2023 case reported abdominal pain of 6/10 after unusually high intake of common-sage plant material. The source leaves preparation and causal attribution uncertain. A small dried-leaf extract trial reported two abdominal pain events on extract and zero on placebo (P=.23), with no pain-severity comparison. Another cognitive trial combined two sage species and reported nine pooled pain or injury events without arm-specific counts for that category. The two classic extract studies describe induced pain behavior in animals. Claim 0025 retains **Maybe False at 51** for the worsening hypothesis. The weak clinical lean still comes from the two controlled joint and muscle discomfort results and has no verified fresh-leaf Parkinson pain comparison. All 147 literature appearances across three Europe PMC expressions and all 60 records from seven ClinicalTrials.gov pages were screened. The care bundle with sage tea measures postpartum breast pain, the mixed-spice trial plans an SF-36 pain domain, and the diabetes comparator includes sage among seven herbs and monitors adverse events. The official records supplied no posted results for these comparisons. The Polish sponsor describes a similar lactation-suppression project without a matching registry identifier or results, so the linkage remains provisional. The postoperative European registry remains open for actual arm-level data. Several translated patent hits refer to *Salvia plebeia*, a different species.
