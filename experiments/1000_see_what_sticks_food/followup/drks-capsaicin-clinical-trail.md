# DRKS00020893 capsaicin clinical trail

November 12, 2026. This follow-up concerns [0724, ground cayenne pepper and swallowing difficulty severity](../statements/0724.txt). The trial compares an inhaled cayenne extract aerosol with laryngeal contact by an endoscope. The registered primary outcome concerns immediate removal or reduction of pharyngeal residue. Recruitment required a positive aerosol-induced cough above the minimum peak flow and included idiopathic and atypical parkinsonian syndromes alongside other neurologic diagnoses. | 2024, investigator MAIK slides | Slide 55 provides pooled first-period counts and diagnosis footnotes. Cough responses were 20/20 and 1/20, and swallowing responses 18/20 and 1/20, respectively. The Parkinson-labeled counts were 4 and 5. The resulting Parkinson-labeled first-period cough counts are exactly 4/4 with aerosol and bounded at 0–1/5 with the endoscope. The corresponding swallowing bounds are 2–4/4 and 0–1/5. Slide 56 reports that 19/19 participants crossing to aerosol coughed and 16/19 showed a swallowing response. At least four of these 19 were among the five Parkinson-labeled participants, but neither the exact number nor their individual swallowing responses are given. The two switching to the endoscope showed neither cough nor swallowing response. Crossover depended on the first intervention and its response. Exact Parkinson-group swallowing response, patient-level diagnoses, severity-scale changes and later clinical outcomes such as aspiration pneumonia remain unresolved. The exact cough count follows from the full arm’s uniform response in a preselected aerosol-responsive population. Ground cayenne ingestion was outside the trial interventions.
