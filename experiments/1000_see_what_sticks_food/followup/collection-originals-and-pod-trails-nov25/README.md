# Five original collections and the bean-pod citation trail

The Round 1 audit recovered the identity-matched article body for five of 26 high-multiplicity archive groups. The original views were screened with the frozen ingredient names, preparation terms and verified constituent names. The result covers 99 exact claim–source pairs. Fifty-six are related food, constituent, formulation or sensory contexts and 43 are contextual exclusions. The other 21 archive routes remain outside this original-depth disposition set.

- ACNP 2024 has 304 numbered members in the archived P305–P608 collection. Kefir fractions and peptides are distinct laboratory exposures. Cinnamon scent was a reward cue in P470. Banana-flavored pellets were behavioral rewards in P368 and P371.
- ESHG posters distinguish olive oil in a lipid-metabolism challenge, sesame oil as an animal study vehicle, and caffeine in a metabolic phenotype test. The same body contains author names and study or instrument labels that do not denote food.
- The UEG 2018 poster collection reports caraway oil in a combination, several distinct coffee exposures, orange within a broad fruit group, and other food-adjacent contexts. The Böhn IBS trial, UEG P1704 and the final 2020 adherence publication share one trial family.
- The 2007 clinical microbiology supplement contains food-safety and laboratory ingredient observations. No culinary efficacy direction follows from those comparisons. Its later PMC posting date is distinct from the 2007 print publication.
- The 2009 encyclopedia section includes curcumin and other dietary constituent discussions. Its figure labels, color terms and reproductive egg references were screened separately.

The parent Böhn trial and the later diary analysis add grouped bowel, dietary and fatigue information. They do not quantify a raw-orange constipation or brewed-coffee fatigue comparison. The 2025 kefir abstract shares with P509 the kefir peptide identities, model and broad outcome stages. The two records share a possible publication lineage until independent data can be verified. The bean-pod citation trail adds a randomized oral glucose study, a historical review and a laboratory urinary-mechanism abstract.

Three source families were added to the relevant scorecards. Seventy-three claim scorecards were checked. Their published verdicts and anchors were preserved.
