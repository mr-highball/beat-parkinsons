# Ginger bowel endpoints and Parkinson trial diaries

Two controlled postoperative studies report an earlier first stool with ginger capsules. The first-stool comparison was secondary in both. A separate 160-person trial of ginger added to an enhanced-recovery pathway records ileus in 23 of 80 patients in each arm and similar time to first flatus. It lacked complete defecation data. The studies differ in surgical population, feeding, comparator and measured endpoints. Claim 0581 concerns grated ginger and chronic Parkinson constipation severity. [Cesarean original](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-018-25200-6) · [Placebo hysterectomy original](https://he02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/tjog/article/view/271390) · [Enhanced-recovery original](https://he02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/tjog/article/view/270744)

The PASS-GI original supplement supplies stool frequency, Bristol categories and adverse-event counts. This placebo trial tested DA-9701, derived from Corydalis tuber and Pharbitis seed. Its within-group comparisons leave the separate active-comparator MRI trial’s registered diary results open. The complete PASS-GI exposure and outcome check settles one original dried-date pain pair as an exposure exclusion. [PASS-GI original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7754502/) · [Original 2018 registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03022201)

The 14-person manometry original reports lower esophageal propagation velocity in the Results and Table 4. Discussion wording reverses that direction. The reported fixed-order water and ginger sequence and the absence of a stomach-emptying measurement distinguish this paper from the unresolved 14-person gastric citation. Different motion stimuli and populations yield different ginger nausea responses. Ten related assessments preserve these distinctions and the existing claim verdicts.
