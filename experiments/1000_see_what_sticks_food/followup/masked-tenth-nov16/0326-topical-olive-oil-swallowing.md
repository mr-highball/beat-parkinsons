# Topical olive-oil combinations and swallowing symptoms

## 2007-02-22 — Polypharmacy-associated xerostomia crossover

Ship and colleagues enrolled 40 adults with medication-associated dry mouth and 39 completed all visits. A randomized-sequence crossover compared one week of a branded olive oil, betaine and xylitol toothpaste, rinse, spray and gel regimen with one week of usual dry-mouth care. Figure 2 lists swallowing difficulty due to dryness as VAS item 2 and only oral dryness, tongue dryness and thirst as significantly different between regimens. The product regimen improved the aggregate eight-item VAS (P=.011). The authors enumerate antiparkinsonian drugs among eligible xerogenic medications. The article provides no Parkinson-specific swallowing result or specified oil grade. Wiley lists first publication on 22 February 2007 and journal issue publication in October 2007. The printed article gives 26 October 2006 as the acceptance date. PMID 17824884. [Publisher record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1365-2842.2006.01718.x) · [Original article PDF hosted by the product distributor](https://cdn.webshopapp.com/shops/246928/files/223226216/droge-mond-wetenschappelijk-onderzoek-xerostom-den.pdf)

## 2015-03-14 — Chewable capsule and subjective swallowing

In a randomized-sequence crossover, 34 adults with chronic dry mouth and measured hyposalivation used a 173-mg olive oil, betaine and xylitol suckable or chewable capsule and a 173-mg sorbitol tablet for one week each, separated by a seven-day washout. The aggregate xerostomia questionnaire means were 9.41 at baseline, 8.97 after control and 8.15 after active treatment. In the Results the active-versus-baseline aggregate change was significant (P=.0015). The abstract and conclusion describe improvement in swallowing and speech relative to control, but no separate item-level effect estimate or test is shown. Between-product unstimulated and stimulated salivary flow comparisons were nonsignificant (P=.88 and P=.53). Degree of blinding and baseline Parkinson status are not established in the methods reviewed. The capsule contains multiple active ingredients and does not specify the oil grade. Online publication 14 March 2015. PMID 26225058. DOI 10.1007/s12663-015-0752-y. [Original article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4511916/)

## 2016-11-25 — Post-radiotherapy dryness-related swallowing

Martín and colleagues recruited 40 patients with xerostomia after radiotherapy. Thirty-eight completed 14 days of olive oil, betaine and xylitol mouthwash, toothpaste, gel and spray. Final analysis included second-visit answers from both early discontinuations. Difficulty swallowing due to dryness was VAS item 2 and was among seven items reported to improve significantly in the paired comparison. Throat dryness was the sole nonsignificant VAS item. A participant with pre-existing complete aphagia discontinued after three days, reporting that the saliva produced exceeded what he could swallow. This was an uncontrolled before-and-after comparison with a multi-ingredient product. It did not measure an isolated extra-virgin olive oil or Parkinson dysphagia effect. First published 25 November 2016 and assigned to the 2017 issue. PMID 27920611. DOI 10.1016/j.rpor.2016.09.008. [Original article, Methods 4.3 and Results 5.2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5126143/)

## Claim 0326

These reports are related topical symptom outcomes. They do not identify dietary extra-virgin olive oil as the active exposure, and neither dryness-related questionnaire improvement nor a mechanically unsafe swallow measures an isolated Parkinson swallowing effect. The two randomized comparisons have no reported significant, separately quantified swallowing endpoint, while the uncontrolled cancer comparison has a favorable subjective swallowing signal. The existing experimental pharyngeal infusion and ALS pilot measure distinct outcomes under different conditions. Retain Unknown.
