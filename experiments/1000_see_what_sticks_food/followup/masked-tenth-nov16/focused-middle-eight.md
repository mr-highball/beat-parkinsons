## 0337 sumac and orthostatic lightheadedness: symptom-adjacent mixed intervention

Forouzanfar et al. first published 2022-11-30, PMID36505757, PMC9729036, DOI 10.1155/2022/6850342, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9729036/ ; official full article XML saved as PMC9729036.xml. Registered IRCT20190406043175N3; 182 outpatients with COVID-19 randomized to pomegranate juice 200 mL three times/day plus sumac 1.5 g twice/day plus standard drugs versus standard drugs, 178 completed (87 vs 91). Species/ground preparation not sufficiently identified in the accessible Methods. Table 4 reports nonspecific dizziness, not positional symptoms: men intervention 55/68 to 1/68 and control 33/52 to 3/51. Women intervention numerators 17/19 to 0/19; placebo baseline 23/39, after table prints zero yes and 3 no (100%), which is internally inconsistent with group size; active baseline percentage printed 8.5% for 17/19 also inconsistent. Both arms' pre/post symptom declines can reflect recovery from acute infection; baseline dizziness rates and sex distribution differ. No sumac-only effect or Parkinson orthostatic estimate, and table discrepancies preclude reliable pooled interpretation. It supplies related symptom context, not a defensible new direction. Compare existing Ardalani RSC Adv 2016 DOI 10.1039/C5RA22840A: sumac powder with captopril lowered resting BP versus placebo plus captopril, no standing symptom measurement; drowsiness narrative/table discrepancy is already documented in research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Sumac-Cardamom.md.
