## Claim judgments

- **0030, brewed green tea worsens olfactory impairment. Unknown at 50.** A human Parkinson cohort gave inconclusive, nonmonotonic estimates for binary hyposmia. A genetic proxy estimate was nominally favorable and did not survive multiplicity control. The 2010 and 2016 mouse experiments found olfactory nerve and epithelial lesions after concentrated green tea extract. They give neither an ordinary brewed-tea comparison nor odor performance. The 2026 combination study measured cell viability and biochemical markers and mentioned hyposmia in its introduction. - **0060, dried sage worsens insomnia. Maybe False at 51.** The registered student comparison assigned dormitory rooms and reported lower global PSQI with common sage tea than with a brown-sugar control. The table favors sage despite a baseline discrepancy with the prose. The registry and publication describe different sugar quantities, and the report gives no adjusted contrast for room clustering or responder count for the registry threshold. The 2026 insomnia pilot used active chamomile, reported inconclusive group comparisons, and has baseline and eligibility discrepancies. Menopause extracts supply another favorable sleep-quality signal. The 2019 Parkinson trial measured cognition and salivation and gave no insomnia result. The small improvement lean is opposite the frozen worsening premise. - **0072, extra-virgin olive oil reduces constipation. Maybe True at 53.** The published oil-grade comparison gives a 4.22-point adjusted favorable difference in a population outside Parkinson disease. The original registration shows a written dose revision within the same study. The dialysis trial had an inconclusive interaction against active mineral oil. The nursing-home study used topical oil and massage. The pediatric ketogenic program reported constipation during a whole diet with extra-virgin oil and no separate oil-free assignment.
