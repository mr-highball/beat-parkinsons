## 0439 refined soybean oil and swallowing difficulty

New candidate Yao et al., March 2024, PMID38494693, DOI 10.6133/apjcn.202403_33(1).0012, original https://apjcn.nhri.org.tw/server/APJCN/33/1/111.pdf . Randomized double-blind 12-week trial 62 girls aged 6–10 with obesity; 31 per group. Each green-tea polyphenol capsule contains 200 mg decaffeinated extract and 300 mg soybean oil; placebo substitutes soybean oil for the extract, so both arms received soybean oil. Daily intake diary covered swallowing adaptation. Authors report some participants thought capsules slightly large and had mild swallowing discomfort, without counts or arm allocation (p.113). The oil grade is not reported; swallowing capsule size rather than soybean oil may explain the complaint. No oil-free comparator, PD, or dysphagia severity contrast. Related exposure/delivery context only, no direction.

## 0406 brewed lemongrass and daytime sleepiness

Existing 1986 primary Leite et al. studied actual dried Cymbopogon citratus leaf tea and reported acute uncontrolled somnolence (5/11); matched sleep experiment https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0378874186900747 evaluated night sleep induction, quality, dreams and awakening, and abstract reports no differences versus placebo. It does not quantify daytime sleepiness scale, and treated groups were not the same as the acute safety set. Existing four-case PD aromatherapy oil study gives unexpected daytime sleep item unchanged; neither a tea comparison nor proof of no daytime effect. Review of source outcomes keeps somnolence and night sleep distinct.

## 0534 cold-pressed macadamia oil and executive function

Existing randomized RRIPP-3 NCT02908932 original https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/13/6/1854 used macadamia oil as active comparator to krill oil in army officers. Stroop and Symbol Digit treatment-by-time p=.4683 and .9121 in canonical assessment; exposure was capsules and pressing not verified. Neither comparison isolates oil versus no oil and no PD. Original trial uses multiple assessments and repeated occupational challenge; do not infer efficacy of oil from no detected between-arm difference.
