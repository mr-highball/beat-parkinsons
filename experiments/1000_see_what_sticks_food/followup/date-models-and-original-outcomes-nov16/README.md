## Dried dates and apathy or pain · 0758 and 0502

The Zangiabadi 2011 and Shabani 2013 articles both describe changes in open-field distance and rearing after fresh date extract in diabetic rats. They report different strains and comparison arms. The 2011 article gives inconsistent sham group counts and reports 38.50 and 51.36 m/s as a 133.4% reduction in one section. The means imply a 25% reduction, as the Discussion states. Shabani’s 2013 hot-plate contrast has inconsistent p thresholds in the abstract and full report. Its tail-flick caption also conflicts with the Methods on the route. Preserve the separate assays and discrepancies. [Original evidence summaries](../../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#fresh-date-fruit-extract-in-diabetic-neuropathy-models)

Publisher-deposited references from the El-Sayyad cerebellar review identify the Subash oxidative paper as the relevant date study at reference 118. The review body remains gated. The original oxidative paper documents behavioral testing before tissue collection and omits the behavioral data. It uses a closely matching extract diet, mouse groups and ethics approval to those of the 2015 behavioral article, while the feeding periods printed in the reports differ. The separate 2014 conference abstract is the same study as the 2015 behavioral article according to the authors. ## Fig, hallucination onset and rest tremor · 0175 and 0559

The 1989 Gibb and Byrne reports describe visual hallucinations and rest tremor in individual cases using clinical histories. Byrne’s original page scans confirm rest tremor in all six patients with typical parkinsonism and identify an earlier report of one case. The psychiatric summary groups five cases across several symptoms. In Ikeda’s 2016 study, 31 of 63 patients reported first-onset visual hallucinations at follow-up. Baseline fatigue was associated with first onset, while the direction stated for some subscores in the Results narrative conflicts with Table 3. The subtype counts in the Segura imaging study describe occurrence and not episode rate. These observations provide clinical definitions and confounders for follow-up of the frozen fig statements. [Early Lewy body case reports](../../../../research/1985-1989/Food-Experiment-Recovered-Parkinson-Clinical-Context.md#rest-tremor-and-visual-hallucinations-in-early-lewy-body-series) · [Parkinson incident-hallucination cohort](../../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Recovered-Parkinson-Clinical-Context.md#fatigue-and-first-visual-hallucinations-in-a-prospective-cohort)

The Charles Bonnet and restless-leg treatment cases record visual hallucinations in different diagnoses and exposure settings. Two more original author uploads were subsequently verified and archived: the Graham interview analysis pools hallucinosis and illusions, and the Stuerenburg letter reports medication intoxication. Their original queue decisions are ready for the next intake. ## Other original clinical outcomes

The camptocormia case describes qualitative rest tremor and severity of posture-dependent lumbar pain during clinical care. The affiliation uses Turkey as the place name. The Greene cohort measures timed walking, stride parameters and falls, rather than a sage effect. The G-CSF and computerized cognitive studies record distinct side effects or clinical outcomes and give no culinary intervention. Cochlear speech tests and Huntington exercise protocol observations require separating the measured outcome from background wording.
