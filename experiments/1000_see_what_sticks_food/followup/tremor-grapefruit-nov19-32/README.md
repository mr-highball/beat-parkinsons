## What the comparisons measured

The 2008 cabergoline pilot separately assessed UPDRS resting tremor in ten people with Parkinson disease. The public author abstract reports improvement. The 2006 clarithromycin interaction study found higher sampled cabergoline concentrations in seven patients and lower total motor scores in three of them. The text describes less resting tremor or rigidity without item-level results. A 2002 case report gives a similar interaction lead for itraconazole, with exposure and symptom details still limited to the accessible abstract. The 2005 Japanese paper's Figure 4 shows higher cabergoline concentrations after grapefruit **juice**, with no plotted clinical outcome or stated denominator. The same-year conference abstract reports a 1.7-fold sampled concentration difference in five people with Parkinson disease. Possible cohort reuse keeps both reports in one family. Separate crossover studies tested fresh fruit or pulp with cardiovascular drugs. The shared Parkinson diet lists grapefruit, with aggregate motor scores and no separate grapefruit comparison. These sources leave the raw-fruit exposure and clinical resting-tremor effect unconnected.
