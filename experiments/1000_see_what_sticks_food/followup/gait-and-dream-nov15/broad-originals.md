## 0642: turmeric extracts and walking

**PMID 34045905, 2021; DOI 10.2147/CPAA.S307464**, original https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8149286/ Table 2 and Methods. In nonarthritic adults with chronic knee pain, 53 per arm were randomized to 250 mg/day water-dispersible turmeric extract (150 mg curcuminoids) or placebo, 90 days. Table 2 reports 49 versus 47 completing. For the secondary 80-m fast-paced walk, change −11.0 ± 26.1 versus −6.8 ± 23.3 seconds, Mann–Whitney p=.0011; median changes −0.2 versus 0 seconds (IQR −2 to −0.06 versus −0.2 to 0.2). Baseline means 70.3 versus 69.2 s. Wide SD and rounded medians temper the result; this is not PD or grated rhizome. Abstract's p > .05 is attached to biomarkers and full body says no between-group biomarker differences; it is not the walk p-value. The 2023 corrigendum https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10314745/ corrects day-45 pain VAS change from −1.5 ±0.4 to −1.0 ±0.4, not the day-90 walking value.

**PMID 40361833, 2025-05-03; DOI 10.3390/healthcare13091055**, https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC12071995/fullTextXML , Table 4 and supplementation methods. Sedentary older adults in six randomized arms underwent 16 weeks: eccentric resistance training, maximal strength training, or no training, each with Cursol or placebo. Two 250 mg capsules/day; each contains 5.25 mg curcumin in a rhizome-extract formulation with calcium phosphate, polysorbate 80 and citric acid. Six-minute walk mean ± SD: eccentric+curcumin n16 605 ±62 to 661 ±36 m, eccentric+placebo n13 597 ±71 to 628 ±69 m, table group p=.002; maximal+curcumin n10 575 ±69 to 628 ±72 m, maximal+placebo n10 597 ±67 to 627 ±66 m, p=.036. No exercise: curcumin n15 599 ±98 to 595 ±94, placebo n17 549 ±108 to 545 ±110: both change −4 m on displayed means. Table reports group p=.002 despite 50 m baseline imbalance and identical displayed mean change; ANCOVA adjusts baseline but the report does not establish a clean beneficial stand-alone walking change. The paper's text says percent improvement and effect sizes were consistently larger but not significant, whereas its discussion and table assert significant paired exercise/curcumin comparisons: retain this internal wording inconsistency. No Parkinson or culinary grated exposure.
