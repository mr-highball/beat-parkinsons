# Gait, movement slowness, pain and daytime sleepiness, November 15, 2026

## Verdict changes

| Claim | Updated verdict | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0513, virgin coconut oil worsens daytime sleepiness | Maybe False at 51 | In the Parkinson baseline report, unspecified coconut-oil frequency has a weak inverse association with self-rated sleepiness. The separate Parkinson ketogenic diet trial names virgin coconut oil in a menu and gives a descriptive −0.45-point daytime-sleepiness contrast. Oil grade in the cohort, individual intake and the effect of oil within the diet remain unverified. |
| 0629, cold-pressed olive oil reduces bradykinesia | Maybe True at 51 | Unspecified olive-oil frequency has a weak inverse association with self-rated movement slowness in the Parkinson baseline graphic. The two rendered views are the same analysis. Older reports from the same project and controlled whole-diet outcomes do not give stand-alone replication for oil. The source has no confirmed cold-pressed exposure or examiner-scored bradykinesia result. |
| 0639, cold-pressed flaxseed oil reduces pain | Maybe True at 51 | Two single-blind arthritis studies from one investigator group report lower rest and movement pain with oral flaxseed oil. The reports have dose and numerical limitations, with possible study overlap. A separate small arthritis trial and a postoperative ACL trial give inconclusive pain comparisons. The pelvic-pain report alternates between flaxseed, oil and dried extract. Cold pressing and Parkinson pain remain untested. |
| 0642, grated turmeric worsens walking slowness | Maybe False at 51 | Two controlled extract trials outside Parkinson disease give favorable short-course walking speed or time under knee-related conditions. One report has conflicting printed effect numbers and the other has widely dispersed times. Other studies give inconclusive gait effects and the small C3 trial numerically favors placebo. Fresh grated turmeric is untested. |
