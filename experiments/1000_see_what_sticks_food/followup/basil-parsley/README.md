# Basil and parsley follow-up

Search date November 13, 2026 UTC. The frozen set contains 16 basil and 15 parsley statements. The [targeted plan](targeted-plan.tsv) maps all 31 IDs to search expressions and symptom families. The archived [species and ingredient aliases](terms/ingredients.tsv), [exposure expansions](terms/exposure-expansions.tsv) and [PubMed headings](terms/pubmed-mesh.tsv) preserve the input terms. The English, scientific and regional names cover culinary sweet basil and garden parsley. Holy basil, hairy basil, root, seed, powder, extract, tea, oil, headspace, odor tests and mixed meals were reviewed as separate exposure forms.

Native FPC tools retrieved PubMed disease queries of seven basil and 13 parsley results, 19 distinct PMIDs after overlap, and Europe PMC disease queries of 13 per herb. A separate ten-query Europe PMC replay retrieved every provider page, 210 appearances and 197 distinct IDs. The 100 MED IDs in that replay had PubMed abstract records retrieved in two EFetch batches and screened for exposure and outcome. The other 97 identifiers comprised 79 patents, 11 preprints, four AGR records, two PMC records and one CBA record. A nine-batch exact-ID follow-up retrieved 96 of these, with PPR926624 missing. Records screened by title and relevant abstracts included Chinese hemlock parsley patent formulations, and five related dyskinesia patent publications that use “basil” where the subject is the basal ganglia and the intervention is topiramate. Neither produced a culinary herb exposure. The disease searches matched dance, SALSA scales, Cistanche salsa, basilic vein and references to individual constituents or foods in a broader plant list. Animal or clinical follow-up also found studies that measured symptoms with a concentrated or differently identified plant preparation. Checking intervention appendices found basil and parsley in a Parkinson diet, with motor results available only as composite scores. The Parkinson olfaction paper recorded basil-item identification, without an ingestion test. [Date-bucketed Parkinson findings](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Basil-Parsley.md)

Seven year-bucketed notes summarize original work from 1999 through 2026. The pass archived six eligible PMC article texts, one CC BY publisher PDF rendered to plain text, one official registry record and one data extract in distilled text. It staged 39 related study-by-claim rows across 23 of the 31 claims. No source assessed the specified preparation, ingestion, Parkinson population and symptom endpoint together well enough to establish a direction. The search followed publisher, regional, university, registry, dataset, conference and author sources to recover comparators and preparations omitted from abstracts. Human findings include dried parsley tea as a trial comparator, an uncontrolled parsley sauce meal with both higher stool frequency and higher abdominal pain, and a dried parsley leaf and asparagus product with no clinically useful antihypertensive effect. One completed herbal sleep trial used a different basil species and further papers used blends. In healthy mice, concentrated oral sweet basil oil caused more rotarod falls at the highest tested level. The original workbook for another study showed better object recognition in aged animals given basil homogenate in oven-dried chow. Its favorable water-maze comparison was confined to a subset.
