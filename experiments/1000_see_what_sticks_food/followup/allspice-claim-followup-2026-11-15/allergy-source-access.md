# Allspice allergy original-source trail — 2026-11-15

## What primary records establish

The NLM PubMed EFetch record for PMID 35577235 identifies Regula, Edelman, Ferastraoaru, Ramesh and Hudes, “Severe allergic reaction to allspice, a hidden food allergen,” Annals of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology 129:241–242, DOI 10.1016/j.anai.2022.04.036. Europe PMC metadata gives first publication 2022-05-13; neither indexed record provides an abstract or patient details. Primary EFetch XML and Europe PMC metadata JSON are retained in the ignored allspice-allergy-source-replay build directory. [PubMed primary index](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35577235/)

The official 2015 ACAAI meeting program lists poster P114, “Severe Allergic Reaction to Allspice: Case Report,” by D. Edelman, D. Ferastraoaru and G. Hudes, New York. Original program PDF and text extraction are retained in that same build directory; the entry is on PDF page 104, text lines 6296–6298. The guide supplies only title/authors and no clinical abstract, patient age, test results, exposure format, neurological symptoms, or outcome. [Official ACAAI 2015 program](https://college.acaai.org/sites/default/files/2015ProgramGuidef.pdf)

The 2015 poster and 2022 paper have the same three authors and closely related titles, which support a plausible report lineage. Neither accessible primary record identifies the participant, so the same patient or cohort is unproven. No clinical features, ground-vs-whole preparation, exposure dose, hallucinations, handwriting outcome, or Parkinson status can be established from these primary records. They must not be counted as independent cases, nor conclusively merged.

It describes a 41-year-old and positive testing, but does not clearly map each field to a specific original source. Treat these as unverified retrieval clues only, not primary case facts, and do not infer whether it is the 2015 poster patient or whether omitted neurological events were assessed.
