# Falls, rigidity, balance and pain, November 15, 2026

## 0110 moved to Maybe False

More frequent coconut-oil use was weakly associated with less self-reported balance difficulty in the Parkinson-only baseline graphic in the 2024 MVP report. The heatmap has no published numeric coefficient or per-pair denominator, interval or adjusted balance result. The food-frequency field does not distinguish refining grade. A separate randomized multiple-sclerosis trial gave extra-virgin oil and EGCG together. Berg balance means were 49 to 52 on the combination and 43 to 43 on other fats and placebo. The original gives separate within-arm tests, an inconclusive perceived-balance result and a dose discrepancy with the official registration. Together these give a minimal qualitative lean against the claim that refined coconut oil worsens postural instability. The statement is graded **Maybe False at 51**. Preparation, co-intervention, population, outcome and confounding limit that inference. [Frozen statement](../../statements/0110.txt) · [Primary Parkinson figure and balance study](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Falls-Rigidity-and-Pain.md) · [Trial registration](../../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Falls-Rigidity-and-Pain.md#trial-registration-for-coconut-oil-and-epigallocatechin-gallate)

The 2026 preprint on the overlapping MVP cohort gives slower progression on total PRO-PD in a separate adjusted slope model. Baseline results were inconclusive and the slope analysis did not adjust for concurrent therapies or correct for multiple testing. Its body and Table 3 were verified. The exact Figure 6 estimate and interval remain open. It adds context on the overall score, with no new independent balance comparison.
