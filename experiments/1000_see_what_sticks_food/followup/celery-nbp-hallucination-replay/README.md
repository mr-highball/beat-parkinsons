## Parkinson patient sources

- Zhou 2019 PMID 30988766 (PMC6447885) randomized idiopathic PD to usual treatment with or without oral DL-NBP 200 mg three times daily for 24 weeks. Outcomes were UPDRS III, PSQI and PDQ-39, not hallucination items. The full Results describes one rash, one ALT increase and two mild gastrointestinal cases, while calling these three treatment-associated adverse events; the arithmetic may reflect categories rather than patient count. No hallucination-specific count was provided. The final evaluable groups in the existing catalog are 43 NBP and 60 control.
- Zhang 2019 PMID 31296990 (PMC6598748) randomized 60 people with PD dementia to donepezil alone or donepezil plus NBP, oxiracetam and Ginkgo. The diagnostic description lists visual hallucination as one possible supportive symptom; it gives no count, baseline hallucination rate, individual hallucination score or change. It reports liver-test abnormalities in 5/30 combination versus 4/30 controls; the text states no nervous-system adverse events, without item-level hallucination ascertainment. The multi-drug regimen prevents isolation of NBP.
- Zhao 2024 PMID 38463594 (PMC10918146) retrospectively compared NBP adjunct (76) with conventional care (50) in PD dementia. Table 7 lists total adverse reactions 5/76 versus 2/50 (p=.825), comprising dizziness, nausea and diarrhea; no hallucination category or hallucination endpoint is reported.
- 2026 PMID 42923556 (PMC13687748) retrospectively compared NBP plus donepezil (64) with donepezil (55) in PD with cognitive impairment; both received levodopa/benserazide. Table 6 gives 5/64 versus 9/55 adverse reactions (p=.149), consisting of diarrhea, dizziness, nausea/vomiting and dry mouth. The study excluded psychiatric conditions such as depression and schizophrenia; no visual-hallucination outcome is reported.

Four primary bodies were reviewed. ## Adjacent composite neuropsychiatric outcome

Gu 2024 studied Alzheimer disease with behavioral/psychological symptoms, not Parkinson disease. Eighty-four participants received donepezil and risperidone with or without oral NBP 0.2 g three times daily for three months. Its BEHAVE-AD explicitly includes hallucinations, but Table 2 reports only global scores: 11.21 ± 3.12 to 4.75 ± 2.35 with adjunct NBP versus 11.30 ± 2.87 to 6.06 ± 2.54 control. CNPI is likewise composite. Table 3 reports aggregate TESS scores, not hallucination-specific adverse events.
