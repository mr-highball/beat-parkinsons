# Archived citation trails and J-STAGE outcome searches

## Archived herb, spice, and oil citations

The November 12, 2026 audit used the 8,102-pair archived-text snapshot. The selection required `citation trail` triage, an herb, spice, or oil claim, exposure or related-exposure terms plus outcome terms in the archived text, and one of those sides missing from the indexed title and abstract. This gave 1,100 claim–source pairs across 304 source keys, including 247 herb, 460 spice, and 393 oil pairs. Every source title was screened. Sixteen records received further article-type, methods, results, or citation checks. The three original human analyses among these 16 addressed trauma genetics, depression genetics, and clinician interviews about Parkinson’s care. None estimated the effect of a frozen ingredient. The source ledger records archived paths and each source’s pair count. [Archived text snapshot](claim-local-text-screen.tsv) · [Source dispositions](local-citation-trail-dispositions.tsv)

Two secondary reports led to useful original clinical publications:

- The Parkinson’s oxylipin perspective pointed to Pomponi and colleagues’ randomized depression trial. After six months, 25% of participants assigned corn oil and 75% of those assigned DHA plus EPA had at least a 50% reduction on the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale. The corn oil grade was unspecified in accessible text, and there was no oil-free group. This records corn oil exposure and the measured endpoint for `0205`, with no isolated effect estimate. The same perspective led to a second flaxseed-derived omega-3 and vitamin E trial. It enrolled 40 people after the first 60-person study had published and measured total UPDRS and biomarkers. It gives no separate cold-pressed flaxseed oil or pain estimate for `0639`. [Archived perspective, Table 5](../../../research/2025-2029/Source-Text/PMC13271650.txt) · [Corn oil trial note](../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#corn-oil-as-the-comparison-in-a-parkinsons-depression-trial) · [Flaxseed trials](../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#flaxseed-derived-omega-3-plus-vitamin-e)
- The cannabidiol review identifies corn oil in three independent trials. Chagas and colleagues reported no clear difference in the PDQ-39 physical-discomfort domain between arms that shared the oil vehicle. De Faria and colleagues used the oil in a crossover measuring experimentally provoked tremor. De Almeida and colleagues used corn oil capsules as the placebo in a REM sleep behavior disorder trial, with Epworth daytime sleepiness among its secondary measures. The journal’s supplement remained inaccessible. In a later recheck, the investigator’s thesis Table 9 supplied arm-level Epworth scores. There was no statistically clear difference between the assigned arms at weeks 4, 8, or 12. These sources give indirect context for `0278`, `0438`, `0308`, and `0726`. [Archived cannabidiol review](../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC11524397.txt) · [2014 carrier trial](../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#corn-oil-carrier-in-the-2014-cannabidiol-trial) · [2020 and 2021 carrier trials](../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Oil-Followup.md#corn-oil-capsules-in-cannabidiol-trials-of-tremor-and-sleep)

A separate 2026 systematic review categorized pharmaceutical butylphthalide under *Apium graveolens*. Following the source identified the already cataloged 2019 trial of the isolated drug. The trial’s composite bradykinesia and rigidity and sleep quality outcomes concern that formulation. [Review, PMID 42489905](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42489905/) · [Earlier trial note](../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#pharmaceutical-butylphthalide-and-motor-symptoms)

## J-STAGE

The complementary J-STAGE WebAPI search covered 23 frozen ingredients with prior clinical or mechanistic leads or recognized oil formulations. It used 23 English and 37 Japanese abstract expressions and 12 Japanese full-text expressions for the seven near-candidate exposures. The queries used the documented article search service and preserved the query field, exact expression, retrieval time, HTTP and service codes, and returned counts. The provider reports zero hits as `ERR_001`. All 72 expressions reconciled, including 53 zero-hit searches. The 19 positive searches returned all 206 rows across 175 distinct article or collection identifiers. [Provider documentation](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/static/pages/JstageServices/TAB3/-char/en) · [Query ledger](jstage-herb-spice-oil-queries.tsv) · [Results and dispositions](jstage-herb-spice-oil-results.tsv)

A large portion of the results were issue-level proceedings or program entries. Targeted primary PDF checks examined seven relevant collections and four standalone articles. The checked collection hits included coconut oil in a nutrition program for hospital staff and in an esophageal cancer case, sesame oil in an oral-care service, olive oil as equipment lubricant, and lemongrass aroma in healthy volunteers. The remaining 121 distinct collections have title-level dispositions. Their constituent abstracts were not individually checked. The Global Index Medicus search endpoints returned HTTP 403 or an access challenge, so this pass reports no denominator for that provider. [WHO and PAHO description](https://www.paho.org/en/news/2-8-2019-technical-cooperation-biremepaho-who-global-index-medicus)

- Aoyama and colleagues’ chemistry article analyzed four essential oils. It led to a Kyorin University report with the same three-period design and the same pain estimates as the previously cataloged four-participant Parkinson series. These reports do not add an independent patient sample. A March 2025 Japanese nursing report remains accessible as bibliographic metadata, and the randomized UMIN record has no posted outcomes or a confirmed lemongrass formulation. [Existing study note](../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#lemongrass-essential-oil-and-chronic-parkinsons-pain) · [Chemistry article](https://doi.org/10.11425/sst.14.37) · [Kyorin project report](https://www.kyorin-u.ac.jp/univ/area/activities/archives/pdf/2023chikisogo_kiyou.pdf)
- Ogawa and Nishio’s 2017 report included one person with Parkinson’s among 13 tube-fed nursing-home residents. The intervention applied roasted or unroasted sesame oil with oral gauze, and the outcome measurements concerned oral hygiene. The authors did not give a Parkinson-specific comparison. [Study note](../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#sesame-oil-oral-swabs-in-nursing-home-dysphagia) · [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.11259/jsg.32.386)
- Kato and colleagues’ 1987 enteral feeding report included a patient with parkinsonian syndrome among 30 inpatients. The formula contained corn oil, and the report evaluated diarrhea and nutrition. Diagnosis, route, formula composition, and the measured outcomes limit relevance to the frozen corn oil claims. [Study note](../../../research/1985-1989/Food-and-Nutrition.md#1987--corn-oil-in-an-enteral-feeding-formula) · [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.11263/jsotp1982.6.92)
- A 2017 conference abstract compared 15 mL daily olive oil with coconut oil for bowel symptoms in 35 hospital staff. The authors reported no clear stool-outcome difference, with subjective improvement among roughly 60% of participants reporting constipation in each group. Parkinson’s nutrition cases elsewhere in the same proceedings describe different interventions. This gives indirect context for `0072`. [Study note](../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#olive-and-coconut-oil-for-bowel-symptoms-in-hospital-staff) · [Primary abstract P-1183](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jspen/32/supplement/32_695/_pdf/-char/ja)

The seven [claim mappings](local-citation-trail-claim-mappings.tsv) identify evidence for central review. The checked sources do not supply a directional estimate for a frozen ingredient, preparation, and symptom together.
