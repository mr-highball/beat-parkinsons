## Claim 0006

Three native Europe PMC expressions retrieved all 25 ranked appearances.
ClinicalTrials.gov returned six records, all screened. Japanese, Korean
and Chinese searches added distinct sources and followed the trial and
patent leads. One Chinese patent body remains open at indexed-abstract
depth. Public dates were taken from the original patent publications
when verified, since Europe PMC recorded several differently dated
filings. [Literature queries](focused-queries.tsv) ·
[Ranked screening](focused-results.tsv) ·
[Registry query](registry-queries.tsv) · [Registry decisions](registry-dispositions.tsv) ·
[Additional sources and open trails](clinical-access.tsv)

A Japanese patent reports 2.8 for both mixtures on its bowel-improvement
scale despite the accompanying claim of benefit. A Korean patent
reports better bowel-condition ratings for two preparations containing
perilla leaf alongside several other added extracts. Its base mixture
and rice comparison also differ, with only four healthy women per arm.
Neither identifies a fresh-shiso effect. [Original human comparisons](../../../../research/2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Shiso-Followup.md)

The randomized dried-leaf extract trial reported similar stool
frequency and constipation symptom changes. The record and paper name
different placebo ingredients. The athlete trial favored high-dose
Perilla seed oil on a selected minimum constipation score. Figure 4 and
the methods describe change signs differently, and the official
registration lists an olive-oil arm absent from the paper. The
separate perilla-and-ginger report gives a combined diarrhea and
constipation score with no control. [Dried-leaf study](../../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Shiso-Followup.md#dried-leaf-extract-and-constipation-measures-in-adults) ·
[Athlete trial and registration](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Shiso-Followup.md#perilla-seed-oil-and-constipation-ratings-in-athletes-and-sedentary-adults) ·
[Mixed-extract report](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Ginger-Constipation-Comparators.md#combined-perilla-and-ginger-extracts-in-functional-dyspepsia)

The official plant-powder record lacked an ingredient name. Its linked
publication identified bamboo. Two Hou Gu Mi Xi registrations describe
added perilla in narrative fields, and their original protocol tables
omit it. No item-level bowel results were available for either trial.
The 2017 Parkinson constipation formula has no Perilla in its original
supplement. The different 2026 Parkinson constipation report leaves
its formula and response calculation unresolved at abstract depth.
