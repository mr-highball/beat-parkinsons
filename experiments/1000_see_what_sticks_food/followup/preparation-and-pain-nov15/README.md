# Weak leans and preparation-specific evidence, November 15, 2026

## Verdict changes

- **0746, raw blueberries reduce constipation severity — Maybe True at 52.** The randomized crossover in 43 adults with functional gastrointestinal disorders compared freeze-dried whole blueberry powder with placebo. The GSRS constipation estimate was −0.3 points (95% CI −0.7 to +0.2, p = .19). The Bristol constipation category occurred in 2 of 43 after blueberry and 7 of 43 after placebo (p = .10 for the overall three-category comparison). Both outcomes came from the same participants. The severity score and stool direction support a small favorable lean. The interval spans both directions, processing differs, and the trial enrolled people without Parkinson disease. A separate trial recorded constipation as an adverse event in 1 of 15 blueberry recipients and 0 of 14 controls. A nine-week blueberry and grape seed extract trial alongside antibiotic triple therapy mentioned worsened constipation in some participants. The accessible abstract gives no arm counts or severity contrast. The new kombucha and infant reports add exposure context without an attributable fruit-specific constipation estimate. [Crossover original and Table 2](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Blueberry-Followup.md#blueberry-powder-and-constipation-in-functional-gastrointestinal-disorders) · [Older-adult adverse events](../../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#blueberry-powder-and-constipation-in-older-adults) · [Mixed-extract reports](../../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Blueberry-Followup.md#blueberry-and-grape-seed-extract-during-helicobacter-pylori-treatment)
- **0659, fresh summer savory reduces pain severity — Maybe True at 51.** Table 2 in a placebo-controlled knee osteoarthritis trial gives reported within-person changes of −2 ± 3 versus −0.5 ± 2 (between-group p = .38). Both groups have the same rounded baseline and end values, and the change is based on 39 of 60 randomized participants. The Methods and Discussion also conflict on capsule dose. A separate topical summer savory trial found greater improvement in oral mucositis pain with the extract gel. Oral aerial-part extracts reduced formalin-evoked pain in rodents, while thermal pain was inconclusive in that study and in a Parkinson rat model using isolated carvacrol. The oral leaf-powder contrast and related evidence support a minimal favorable inference. Fresh leaves and clinical Parkinson pain remain open.
