# Trout, sardines and shrimp evidence follow-up

The freeze contains thirteen claims across these foods. Ten entered this pass without an assessment. Each received an itemized outcome rationale and follow-up trail. Nine of the ten gained an assessment based on nearby human, animal, registry or clinical case evidence. Grilled shrimp and Parkinson visual hallucinations remains open without an eligible assessment. All thirteen verdicts remain Unknown.

## Search coverage

- Europe PMC disease expressions `R001–R006` used Latin and common names alongside Parkinson’s and related exposures. All nine returned pages were retrieved. Six expressions produced 381 result appearances.
- Europe PMC focused expressions `F001–F010` paired the food, compounds or allergens with each symptom family. Thirteen pages were retrieved, yielding 462 appearances. The disease and focused outputs contain 734 distinct source keys.
- Native PubMed ran six disease queries and retrieved 351 distinct PMIDs from 385 appearances. PubMed’s query translation initially rendered several non-Latin terms as an unintended literal `OR`. The ASCII and Latin follow-up is stored separately. Multilingual searches remain in the Europe PMC ledger.
- Native ClinicalTrials.gov retrieved every page for the exact food and Parkinson expression, three food-only expressions, and a Parkinson compound expression. Their respective results were 0, 20, 24, 44 and 6 study appearances. The official study API was checked for the individually reviewed records in `registry-trails.tsv`.
- Native Europe PMC citation traversal retrieved 818 forward or backward edges from 11 seed records across 24 pages. Candidate references, exact species names, separate symptom measures, and older unindexed reports were examined by the primary-source routes recorded in the dated notes and follow-up TSVs.

Direct-looking records included rainbow trout as a laboratory model or wild prey, shrimp as an MRI descriptor or part of a hallucination narrative, and sardines in pooled diet questionnaire options. These do not yield a food-to-symptom estimate. The locally archived primary studies include prescribed whole-fish meals, validated clinical measures, food-poisoning cases, and composite shrimp diets. Scope and inference are recorded with each claim.

## Findings across periods

The Parkinson fish-oil sleep study had inconclusive Epworth Sleepiness Scale and Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index results. Two small Parkinson depression studies reported some favorable clinician-rated outcomes after fish oil or marine fatty acids. The larger VITAL-DEP population trial found a higher rate of incident depression or relevant symptoms without a clear mean score difference. The foods, populations and endpoints remain distinct. [2009 sleep study](../../../../research/2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Trout-Sardine-Shrimp.md#fish-oil-and-daytime-sleepiness-in-parkinsons) · [2021 VITAL-DEP trial and Parkinson comparison links](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Trout-Sardine-Shrimp.md#marine-omega-3-capsules-and-incident-depression)

The preserved-sardine studies found dysarthria in a confirmed botulism outbreak and fatigue in a child with clinical botulism. Canned sardine feeding trials in older people and adults with diabetes measured metabolic outcomes. Exposure and symptom estimates differ across these reports. [Preserved sardine outbreak](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Trout-Sardine-Shrimp.md#dysarthria-in-a-preserved-sardine-outbreak) · [Canned sardine feeding trial](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Trout-Sardine-Shrimp.md#canned-sardine-feeding-in-older-adults)

Two forms of shell-derived chitosan had inconclusive human stool results. A later shrimp–krill gel experiment increased stool output in constipated mice. A fiber-only diet also increased stool output.
