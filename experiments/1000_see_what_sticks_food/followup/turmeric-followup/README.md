# Turmeric symptom evidence follow-up

This pass reviewed eight unassessed turmeric claims. Each has a dated local reference, assessment rows and an open search trail. All eight remain Unknown. The human studies addressed combined herbal formulas, curcumin formulations or related symptoms. The claims specify either ground or fresh grated turmeric and a particular Parkinson’s outcome.

## Search coverage

- The native Europe PMC disease search retrieved all 14 pages for eight expressions, with 640 record appearances. Nine focused expressions retrieved 141 appearances across nine pages. The combined ledger has 619 unique source keys.
- Native PubMed retrieved 191 distinct PMIDs across eight translated disease searches. Translation of non-Latin ingredient tokens inserted literal `OR` terms. Eight repaired ASCII and Latin expressions retrieved 500 distinct PMIDs from 630 appearances. The full multilingual terms remain in the Europe PMC ledger.
- ClinicalTrials.gov returned 2, 67 and 93 study appearances for the disease, whole botanical and extracted-constituent expressions. All pages were retrieved. The trial-level trail includes independently checked Iranian and Japanese registrations.
- Citation traversal retrieved 717 edges across 25 pages and 12 primary seeds, with all query rows completed. Publisher, university, regulator and registry sources supplied additional exact outcome checks. The [open trails](open-trails.tsv) record missing data, access limitations and follow-up sources.

## What the papers measured

A 2011 Parkinson study of a combined botanical granule reported a better secondary nonfluent speech rating. Its motor complications score contained no separate daily OFF estimate. Later nanomicelle and phospholipid curcumin reports supply aggregate outcomes or an examination during a medication OFF state. None of these sources gives a ground-turmeric effect on acoustic loudness, freezing episodes, fall frequency or daily OFF hours. The 2026 Japanese trial reported an exploratory eye-tracking attention result, while its primary composite memory and computerized attention outcomes were inconclusive. An older turmeric-with-breakfast study tested working memory. The curry cohort’s backward digit span score differed across intake groups, with no clear trajectory interaction.
