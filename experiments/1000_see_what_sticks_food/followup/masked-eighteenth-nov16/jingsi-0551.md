# Jing Si herbal tea: 0551 sleep follow-through

## Original 2024 controlled report

Liu CH et al., “Efficacy of Jing Si herbal tea in functional dyspepsia: A double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled study,” Journal of the Formosan Medical Association 124(10):918–923. PMID39256061, DOI 10.1016/j.jfma.2024.09.008; electronic publication 2024-09-10, issue 2025-10. Primary source indexed rendering https://tcmfaa.tzuchi.com.tw/home/images/files/TCMF-JCT/22_compressed.pdf and https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39256061/. The publisher PDF host timed out to local HTTP client; its public indexed rendering exposes Methods and Tables 1–2. No local PDF claimed recovered.

Methods identify NCT05948215. Twenty-six Rome IV functional-dyspepsia patients allocated 13 per arm to Jing Si eight-herb concentrate 15 mL/day or placebo for four weeks. Original baseline PSQI median (IQR) 14 (7.5–19) vs 11 (7–16), baseline between-group p=.472 (Table 1). Table 2 reports tea 14 (7.5–19) to 12 (6.5–17), p=.207, placebo 11 (7–16) to 10 (7.5–14.5), p=.141; these are within-arm pre/post p values, not treatment-by-time contrasts. No numerical adjusted between-arm change estimate or p-value for PSQI identified in accessible primary tables. Lower scores indicate better sleep quality; subtracting group medians is not a participant-level median change or randomized comparison. The global sleep-quality questionnaire is not insomnia-specific severity.

Primary original describes an eight-herb formula; registry names perilla leaves but does not establish source-leaf drying or amount in this trial. The separate 2022 product report https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9616512/ describes an aqueous concentrated product including P. frutescens leaves, but its batch and proportions cannot automatically be assigned to Liu’s trial. Seven other herbs and non-Parkinson dyspepsia population make attribution to oral dried shiso impossible. Do not infer a protective or null tested effect on frozen claim from non-significant within-arm p values.

The different IRB identifiers, condition, design, official dates and sample sizes support distinct registered protocols; do not assert person-level exclusivity without enrollment records. The 2022-11-10 protocol/consent for NCT05668104 https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/04/NCT05668104/ICF_003.pdf refers to an ongoing earlier FD study but does not establish individual overlap. PSQI endpoint is registered, not a posted outcome.
