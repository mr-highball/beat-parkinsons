## New primary controlled outcome: 0608 anise and insomnia
Simbar et al., published 29 November 2024, assigned volume 12 in 2026, Current Traditional Medicine, article e22150838313003, DOI 10.2174/0122150838313003240821091757. Publisher date https://www.eurekaselect.com/article/144661 ; indexed full primary https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S2215083826000044 . This was a triple-blinded randomized PMS study in college women, not Parkinson disease. Randomized 55 per arm; 32 and 35 completed. The intervention was oral 404 mg softgel containing oils of Pimpinella anisum, Oenothera biennis and Salvia sclarea; comparator was sunflower oil. Table 5 daily symptom insomnia scores baseline, cycle one, cycle two: combination 5.19±5.83, 3.56±3.79, 2.44±3.62; control 5.46±5.75, 2.86±3.62, 2.23±2.54. Table labels between-group P=.810, within-groups P=.572, time P<.001; authors report no significant group-by-time interaction. Fall in both arms cannot be attributed to anise, and the oil mixture is not ground anise seed. The publisher indexed page expressly identifies this as open access, but direct page access returned HTTP 403; findings above were read in indexed primary Table 5 rather than a downloaded body. Published item provides a relevant symptom but no independent anise contrast. Existing ground seed PMS study records insomnia in its instrument without numeric item data. Do not infer that lack of significance proves equality.

## New primary controlled functional outcome: 0668 refined peanut oil and falls
2026 peanut butter RCT, PMID 41632974, PMCID PMC12867520, DOI 10.1002/jcsm.70221, registry ACTRN12622001291774; primary https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12867520/. Older community participants at nutritional/muscle risk randomized peanut butter 43 g/day (n=60) or usual care (n=60) for six months. Adjusted five-times sit-to-stand change difference -1.23 seconds (95% CI -2.09 to -0.37); gait and most measures inconclusive. Peanut butter is not refined peanut oil and no PD population was studied. SARC-F contains a history-of-falls item but primary publication does not give falls item contrasts or prospectively measured fall frequency. Functional proxy cannot establish fall reduction or worsening.

## Other scope checks and open trails
0699: Existing 2022 post-COVID qualitative survey https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8886082/ reports altered odor perceptions of lentils/legumes in some participants; food is a smell stimulus, not an assigned intake intervention; no Parkinson or boiled-lentil effect on smell. 0692: Parkinson pomegranate intervention registry NCT05142085 concerns nanoemulsified seed oil, not raw arils; sleep sits in a composite without available item results. Mixed diet descriptions and non-PD juice do not establish arils or daytime sleepiness. 0548/0519: the previously reviewed Hegelmaier mixed-food protocol names chervil and lovage without individual consumption, isolated exposure or the target speech/anxiety estimate. 0552: original caraway gastrointestinal mixed formulations do not measure hand dexterity, nor establish ground-seed effect. 0664: McDonnell 2019 walnut-base aromatherapy was not consumed cold-pressed walnut oil; a PSQI night bathroom item is not a urinary urgency frequency endpoint. No direction is established for these claims by the identified contexts.

## Scope and limitations

This narrative preserves evidence rather than claiming complete literature coverage. The original-source priority-six assigned set is separately staged in binky/exact. No canonical edits.
