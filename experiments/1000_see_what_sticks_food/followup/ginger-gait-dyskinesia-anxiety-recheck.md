# Ginger, stride, dyskinesia and anxiety

On November 12, 2026, this follow-up tested the evidence for [0271](../statements/0271.txt), ground ginger worsening dyskinesia severity, [0566](../statements/0566.txt), ground ginger worsening shortened stride, and [0937](../statements/0937.txt), grated ginger reducing anxiety. The newly reviewed primary sources do not supply a directional estimate for the preparation, symptom and Parkinson’s population specified by any of the three statements.

## Primary outcome checks

| Source | Outcome and applicability |
| --- | --- |
| Koh et al., 2017, `10.7730/JSCM.2017.29.2.182` | Case 3 used two consecutive mixtures containing fresh and dried ginger. Instrumented stride was 11 ± 1 to 13 ± 2 cm at the same treadmill speed while receiving the first mixture. The case lists Parkinson’s disease as a rule-out and right basal ganglia infarct sequelae as the impression. Acupuncture, pharmacopuncture and medication changes occurred alongside the mixture. Case 5 had a reported Parkinson’s diagnosis and two stride measurements, both before the fresh-ginger formula. Case 3's UPDRS Part IV total moved from 2 to 0 and Case 5's stayed at 8. The report gives no dyskinesia subitem. [Dated evidence with primary pages](../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#ginger-containing-decoctions-in-a-korean-gait-case-series) |
| Lee et al., 2023, `10.22246/jikm.2023.44.4.774` | One Parkinson’s case began a fresh-ginger mixture after earlier stride improvements. Stride was 0.94 to 0.87 m in the final treatment interval, after declining before this formula began. Rigidity stayed at 7 in the same interval. This uncontrolled trajectory leaves the ginger effect unresolved. [Dated evidence with primary tables](../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#fresh-ginger-in-a-multiherb-regimen-with-measured-parkinson-gait) |

[Dated evidence](../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#dried-ginger-as-an-optional-component-of-a-parkinson-decoction) |

Its original text and potential cohort overlap remain unresolved. Counts by treatment group and a dyskinesia severity score are unavailable in the grant report. Its matching authors, intervention and reported outcome identify it as a companion to the journal article. ## Anxiety and formulation checks

- The Parkinson’s traditional-medicine review’s HAM-A trail leads to two Pingchan trials, a Zishenpingchan trial and Shudi Pingchan with brain stimulation. The primary formulas in the 2022 Pingchan, 2018 Zishenpingchan and Shudi Pingchan reports contain no culinary ginger. The 2021 Pingchan primary abstract gives no formula and the review's formula table also shows no culinary ginger. The Pingchan name `Jiang Can` refers to silkworm. `Curcuma` identifies a different genus from culinary ginger (`Zingiber`). [Review, Table 2 and section 3.3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10378741/) · [2022 Pingchan primary formula](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8914044/) · [2021 Pingchan primary abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.joim.2020.12.006) · [2018 Zishenpingchan primary formula](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6065246/) · [Shudi Pingchan primary report](https://doi.org/10.1142/S2575900020500068)
- Yuan et al., 2014, DOI `10.3969/j.issn.2095-6606.2014.06.006`, reports HAM-A/HARS and SAS measures for 40 patients with Parkinson’s and depression. The journal abstract names treatment strategies and gives no ingredient list. The direct full-text route and title, author and thesis searches yielded no readable primary formula. Ginger exposure remains unverified. [Dated evidence with primary abstract](../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#anxiety-measures-in-a-parkinson-and-depression-formula-trial)
- Existing rodent studies of gingerol- and shogaol-enriched extracts report anxiety-like behavior in spinal nerve injury models. The result supports a research lead for `0937` across preparations and populations. Screening recovered the 2011 mouse study and three records outside the target preparation and outcome: a permeability study (`31476432`), a spice review (`36438833`), and a dopamine-agonist adverse-effect review (`20359261`). The clinical-search query and multilingual routes documented in the prior audit covered separate material. This query counts title and abstract matches only. [Earlier clinical-search results](iran-ginger-and-saffron-publication-trails.md#human-ginger-and-combined-exposure-outcomes)

The Korean society lists Heo et al. 2022 and its indexed keywords include fresh ginger. Both the society issue and PDF viewer returned HTTP 200. The viewer directs to an empty document target. The primary abstract describes gait and voice changes during multimodal care, with diagnosis, measured stride and administered ginger exposure still unresolved. Koh’s related 2017 primary report was recovered independently. [Society issue](https://www.kmstroke.or.kr/kmstroke/02/04.php?language=kor&month=12&ref_uid=50&year=2022) · [PDF viewer](https://www.kmstroke.or.kr/kmstroke/02/pdf_viewer.htm?uid=280) · [Korean Traditional Knowledge Portal](https://koreantk.com/ktkp2014/thesis/thesis-view.view?ctrlNo=SHSGBS_2022_v23n1_13&tempLang=en)

Results from the registered ginger trials and any published item-level tables remain open leads. A total UPDRS or MDS-UPDRS score cannot recover stride length or dyskinesia severity. MDS-UPDRS Part IV time with dyskinesias and functional impact require separate results. A gait task, total locomotion, or an unbroken part score leaves the relevant symptom direction unresolved.
