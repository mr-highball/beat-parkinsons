## Herbs and carvacrol

Section 4.5 (lines 137–141 and Table 1 lines 326–328) names rosemary, basil and thyme as carvacrol sources. Reference 69 is Haddadi et al. The review describes mice, while the primary used male Wistar rats with 6-OHDA lesions and intraperitoneal isolated carvacrol. The rat study found improved passive-avoidance performance at 25 mg/kg. It reported no clear effect on apomorphine-induced rotation or thermal tail-flick hyperalgesia. These measurements concern memory, motor asymmetry and evoked withdrawal. Section 2 separately describes clinical depression, rigidity, apathy and postural instability. Reference 68 is an antibacterial review. Reference 67 is an essential-oil pharmacology review, searched through its abstract and bibliographic metadata. [Primary methods and Figures 2–4](https://www.scielo.br/j/anp/a/NFQdV3JLz748mSmpjVjLKHv/?format=pdf&lang=en) · [Carvacrol pharmacology abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19075694/) · [Author record for antibacterial review](https://ricerca.unich.it/handle/11564/695009).

Independent carvacrol experiments preserve different results. Lins et al. used reserpine and measured catalepsy and vacuous chewing movements, without restoring open-field locomotion. Manouchehrabadi et al. published after Ciulla and reported favorable catalepsy, pole and coordination measures in a distinct 6-OHDA cohort. These studies already have a catalog entry. Their separate experimental comparisons supply no dietary preparation estimate. [Lins primary report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29378222/) · [Manouchehrabadi primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31364033/) · [Local carvacrol notes](../../../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Spice-Followup.md#isolated-carvacrol-and-thymol-in-early-parkinson-models).

## Parsley and flavonoids

Section 4.17 (lines 247–265) gives parsley as one of many dietary flavonoid examples. The quercetin subsection is specifically situated in bee pollen and cites passive avoidance and water-maze findings in rodent models. References 121–122 are secondhand reviews. Reference 122's publisher-deposited bibliography indexes Patil et al.’s isolated apigenin/luteolin MPTP study at B46. Its motor tests were rotarod and open-field behavior. The journal retracted that paper on November 9, 2021, after the Ciulla review, citing reused figures. Its outcomes supply no positive evidence for parsley or a Parkinson symptom. The Sage full body for reference 122 was access-restricted. The deposited bibliography and retraction were accessible. [Cited review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1089/jmf.2017.4078) · [Deposited citation data](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1089/jmf.2017.4078) · [Original retraction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0028390821004317) · [Local prior retraction record](../../celery-leaf-dyskinesia/search-and-open-trails.md#claim-0002-evidence-search).

## Ginger and different motor endpoints

Section 4.14 (lines 219–227 and Table 1 lines 344–346) cites Park et al. for orally dosed isolated 6-shogaol in an MPTP model. The animal behavioral outcome was turn and descent time in a pole task. Cell and tissue findings concern neuroinflammation and dopaminergic injury. The paper supplies no measured muscle rigidity, levodopa-induced dyskinesia or pain. Ciulla's lipoic acid section separately discusses a levodopa-induced dyskinesia experiment in lesioned rats. Tail-flick in Ciulla is the carvacrol study. More recent ginger pain studies and oral zingerone experiments already have separate sources. ## Turmeric preparation and endpoints

Section 4.6 (lines 143–147 and Table 1 lines 329–331) distinguishes the rhizome as a spice from studied curcumin. Reference 71, Wang et al., summarizes 13 preclinical studies with mixed designs, low reporting quality and a measured locomotor lead. Its Table 3 lists favorable locomotor or coordination changes and the Schmitz et al. manganese cohort. The latter primary reports poor performance in the single-pellet reach task under curcumin or manganese and no neurochemical protection. The study differs from the homocysteine rat protocol, where injected curcumin was associated with better spontaneous movement. They address different rodent models and behavioral tests. None establishes the direction of human bradykinesia or muscle rigidity for a culinary preparation. Some older Chinese originals had only title and secondary table records. [Local Wang, Table 3](../../../../../research/2015-2019/Source-Text/PMC5561616.txt#L225) · [Primary Mansouri locomotor report](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12031-012-9727-3) · [Primary Schmitz report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24723215/) · [Schmitz university thesis](https://repositorio.ufsc.br/bitstream/handle/123456789/101095/316404.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1).

Reference 70, Mythri and Bharath, links the whole-turmeric study at its reference 14 and the diabetic hyperalgesia study at reference 86. The whole-spice exposure was boiled powder in drinking water before MPTP, with acute neuronal tissue outcomes after the toxin and a statement about no general change in animal activity before the injury. That report offers no symptom-specific movement or pain measure. The diabetes study did measure evoked thermal responses after oral isolated curcumin. It supplies a low-fit pain route for 0812 with no Parkinson population. The later clinically studied nanomicelles and phospholipid curcuminoids provide separate aggregate motor evidence with different preparations and limited item-level reporting. They postdate Ciulla and are absent from its citation route. ## Flaxseed combination and composite

Section 4.7 (lines 149–153 and Table 1 lines 332–334) lists the food sources and cites Taghizadeh et al. as its human original. The 60-person randomized study tested flaxseed-derived omega-3 with vitamin E versus placebo for 12 weeks. The original March 22, 2017 report gives a total-score change of −3.3 versus +4.4 (p=.02), and Table 3 adjusted changes of −3.4 versus +4.7 (p=.005). The methods cite the revised scale and describe four parts. Both tables label the published range 0–195, which does not match the full revised scale's 260-point summed maximum. The scale's exact implementation and raw items were unavailable. The cited revised scale includes pain and other sensations, rigidity and body bradykinesia. The study publishes no individual result for them. Power calculations used hs-CRP, and the trial supplies neither an oil-only arm nor the extraction method. These features preserve related roles for 0639, 0899 and 0913. [Publisher original](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0197018617300736) · [Accepted manuscript, Sections 2.2, 2.5–2.8 and Tables 2–3](https://magistralbr.caldic.com/storage/product-files/1634072890.pdf) · [Original cited scale](https://www.movementdisorders.org/MDS-Files1/Resources/PDFs/MDS-UPDRS.pdf) · [Local dated record](../../../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Flaxseed-Oil.md).

The separate Tamtaji et al. 40-person trial used the same combination in a later recruitment period and cites a different registration. Its favorable global result has the same attribution limitation. Its original body reports Part II and Part III comparisons rounded to p=.05, describes them as inconclusive and gives no item estimates. The 2021 Yoo cohort gives plasma ALA item-level associations with more favorable age-adjusted rigidity and weaker bradykinesia or sex-adjusted rigidity estimates. Circulating ALA and supplement comparisons address different exposures and estimands. These comparator records sit outside Ciulla's tested citation route. [Tamtaji accepted manuscript, Table 2](https://magistralbr.caldic.com/storage/product-files/1339864240.pdf) · [Yoo original supplement, Table S2](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art:10.1038%2Fs41598-021-92029-x/MediaObjects/41598_2021_92029_MOESM1_ESM.pdf).

## Watermelon and lycopene

Section 4.16 (lines 239–245) uses the plural **watermelons**, which did not trigger the original singular body flag for these rows. The actual experiments used oral isolated lycopene. Kaur et al. report a 39% increase in striatal acetylcholinesterase activity relative to rotenone-treated rats. The review leaves the measure behind that number unclear. Prema et al. report beam crossing, open-field activity and hanging in MPTP mice. Their Figure 1 suggests about 8.1 s untreated model and 6.2, 4.8 and 5.1 s in the three lycopene arms. The narrower test concerns coordination and the open field concerns spontaneous activity. Neither establishes a raw-watermelon clinical bradykinesia or apathy result. Apathy appears as clinical background in Section 2.
