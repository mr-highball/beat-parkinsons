# Clinical source routing and ingredient-name ambiguity

The existing classification can miss a clinical study when its abstract uses PD as shorthand for people with Parkinson disease. A native replay tested a fallback requiring explicit Parkinson context, a participant phrase and a nearby human study action. It preserved the original animal, laboratory, protocol and review routes. All 61,252 original claim–source pairs and their exact reviews, lexical flags and access fields were reconciled.

The trial changed the classification of 450 pairs across 342 source keys. It moved 417 open pairs between priorities without changing the queue membership. No pair moved into the archived highest priority. Three moved into the indexed higher priority, and all three used “to date” as a time expression. They describe a heat-pain fMRI study, an apathy scale validation and a 5-HTP comparison. A postmortem tissue study also received a clinical promotion because the abstract describes matching controls. The current production classifier was retained. The audit verified genuine human cohorts in several previously unclassified sources. This establishes a source-classification gap within the original searches. It does not establish that the change improves retrieval for the experiment's named exposures. The trial output also preserves the donor-tissue containment gap and the false date-fruit matches for a separate lexical test. Exact article review and the complete underlying queue remain available.
