# Edible-oil outcomes and Parkinson rehabilitation records

Five reviewed sources supplied 25 exact oil decisions. Agim and Cannon discuss oils as dietary fat sources and cite distinct interventions for mood and dyskinesia. The zinc and linoleic-acid Methods identify olive oil and DMSO as an injected model vehicle. The gut-microbiota review describes postural-instability phenotypes by bacterial profile and separately discusses olive phenols. The two later diet reviews cite Mischley’s cross-sectional baseline survey. Rechecking Table 2 gives adjusted total PRO-PD differences of −31.4 points per frequency step for olive oil and −20.2 for coconut oil, with neither oil grade nor individual motor-item estimates. The survey yielded five related claim memberships and represents one source cohort. The Rusch pilot’s constipation estimate comes from a whole diet in eight people and was already cataloged. A distinct randomized study’s between-group constipation comparison was inconclusive. Both results need to stay distinct from isolated extra-virgin olive oil. [Original pilot and later randomized report](../../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Oil-Followup.md#olive-oil-in-the-parkinson-constipation-pilot) The MEDI-PD assessment now points directly to the randomized 2024 report. Constipation score changes were −0.5 versus −0.8 (p=.60) for diet plus standard care and standard care. Twelve primary rehabilitation sources supplied 32 full-text contextual decisions for 22 sage and ten date pairs. One study measures pain under peer support. CO-OP plans fatigue and apathy measures, while its outcome paper describes them as baseline characterization. Separate traces confirm occupational therapy and a dance or music program as the actual interventions. These scoped reviews preserve clinical symptoms even where an ingredient label is publication metadata or a date phrase.
