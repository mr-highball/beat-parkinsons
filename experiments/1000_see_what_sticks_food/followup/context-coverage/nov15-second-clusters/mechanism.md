## Findings for publication

- **0359, grated turmeric and rigidity.** The directly cited Cui study is a related preclinical result. Figure 1C shows shorter bar and grid descent latency in rats pretreated with intragastric curcumin compared with rotenone-only rats, and both tests remained above vehicle-only controls. The tests measure delay leaving an imposed posture. They supply an indirect motor proxy for clinical rigidity. The study used isolated curcumin, a toxin model and pretreatment. It gives no separate estimate for passive muscle tone or a grated turmeric diet. The underlying 2015-12-08 literature entry already exists. The staged primary assessment and dated text supplement the exact review disposition. [Primary Methods and Results](https://www.spandidos-publications.com/10.3892/mmr.2015.4657) · [Figure 1](https://www.spandidos-publications.com/article_images/mmr/13/2/MMR-13-02-1381-g00.jpg)
- **0463, cold-pressed sunflower oil and rigidity.** The Nutrients table attributes an injection and its oil vehicle to the curcumin arm. The primary Methods show that curcumin went intragastrically, while sunflower seed oil carried the subcutaneous rotenone and was injected as vehicle in the control conditions. There was no isolated oil contrast among the rotenone-exposed animals, no oral oil exposure and no cold-pressed grade specification. [Review Table 1](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/18/3/439) · [Primary Methods](https://www.spandidos-publications.com/10.3892/mmr.2015.4657)
- **Nutrients review endpoint boundaries.** Khasnavis and Pahan report rotarod and open-field outcomes after cinnamon treatment in MPTP mice. The perphenazine catalepsy passage cited for rigidity concerns Nigella sativa, called black cumin, which is distinct from Cuminum cyminum. The nearby levodopa-induced dyskinesia result is from Shin and colleagues and uses abnormal involuntary movement scores after repeated levodopa in 6-OHDA rats. Table 1 misdescribes that reference as anxiolysis in MPTP mice. The ground cinnamon, ground ginger and ground Sichuan pepper dyskinesia pairs exclude that transferred outcome. [Cinnamon primary Methods and Figure 8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4167597/) · [Nigella primary](https://doi.org/10.4236/wjns.2014.44035) · [Gypenoside primary Methods](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12868-015-0163-5)
- **Ginger access.** The CP1 primary abstract confirms an oral mixed ginger and Cyperus rotundus extract in 6-OHDA rats and reports general motor and memory results. The publisher reserves the full body for access or purchase. A behavioral-methods review attributes apomorphine rotation and the Morris water maze to CP1. The exact exclusions for 0271 and 0626 describe what the open original Nutrients review and the CP1 abstract establish. Full primary assessment of any additional CP1 endpoints remains outstanding. [CP1 publisher abstract and access status](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1089/rej.2015.1729) · [Behavioral methods review, ref. 123](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3921/9/10/1007)
- **FXTAS exposure attribution.** All five sage pairs route to the research-funding acronym SAGE, with pain, parkinsonian rigidity, falls, fatigue and apathy handled in distinct clinical sections. Fatigue, for example, is a secondary outcome in Barbuto and colleagues' home-cycle versus balance trial for degenerative cerebellar ataxia. All seven olive oil pairs route to an ingredient of the MIND dietary pattern, discussed for cognition, while the claimed symptoms are considered in different treatment or descriptive contexts. The constipation and dysphagia sections explicitly cite extrapolated disease evidence, which the exact reviews examined for exposure as well as endpoint. [FXTAS review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13684911/) · [Barbuto primary fatigue results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12434599/)
- **Counterweight to the MIND narrative.** The FXTAS review cites Morris and colleagues' 2015 observational association between higher whole-pattern diet scores and slower cognitive decline in 960 older adults followed for a mean 4.7 years. Barnes and colleagues randomized 604 cognitively unimpaired older adults to MIND or control with mild caloric restriction in both arms. The intervention supplied extra-virgin olive oil, nuts and blueberries alongside dietary counseling. The three-year global cognition contrast was +0.035 standardized units (95% CI −0.022 to +0.092, p=.23), with no clear MRI difference. This tests whole-pattern assignment in a separate population. Neither study supplies an individual olive oil contrast for any of the seven exact symptom pairs. [2015 cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26086182/) · [2023 randomized report](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2302368)

Check the primary arm and measured outcome together before inheriting a review table's ingredient–symptom connection. Record proxy fit separately from preparation and motor context. Review tables can combine a true outcome with the wrong species, treatment, route or cohort even when each term appears in the article.

The primary Cui study lends indirect support to a curcumin pretreatment effect on catalepsy. Neither the Cui comparison nor the MIND results establishes a direction for the frozen food preparation and clinical symptom. The proposed additions support the existing Unknown verdicts.
