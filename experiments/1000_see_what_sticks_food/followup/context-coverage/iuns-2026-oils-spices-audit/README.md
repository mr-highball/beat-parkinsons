### Coconut oil and fatigue

OAP17_06 (layout line 28524, printed page 670, physical PDF page 671) reports 120 colorectal surgery patients randomized to an MCT–carbohydrate solution or carbohydrate solution within a perioperative protocol. The clinical study measured fatigue by VAS as a secondary outcome and reports lower scores with the MCT combination at preoperative and postoperative observations. The abstract does not specify an estimated fatigue contrast with uncertainty, the oil's botanical source or coconut grade. The population and acute perioperative endpoint differ from 0229's virgin coconut oil and fatigue in people with Parkinson disease. The accompanying 40-rat experiment is a separate arm within the abstract and provides no patient fatigue result. The exact IUNS pair can be related. A focused author/title search did not confirm a separate full report or trial registration for this cohort. [Cambridge original, OAP17_06](https://doi.org/10.1017/jns.2026.10126)

### Turmeric and pain

OAP36_02 begins at layout line 28754, printed page 675 and physical PDF page 676. It is the same NCT04946981 cohort as the original October 3, 2025 full article by Schönenberger and colleagues, PMID 41040018. A randomized crossover of 44 moderately active adults compared a bioavailable turmeric capsule formulation with placebo around downhill running. The primary 72-hour soreness AUC did not clearly differ. The post hoc contrast from maximum soreness to 72-hour recovery favored the formulation. The published analysis uses a sensitivity model without the planned pre-exercise covariate. Ground turmeric in Parkinson pain remains a different exposure and clinical outcome. The exact IUNS pair is related, with the proposed study-level record keyed once to the original article.
