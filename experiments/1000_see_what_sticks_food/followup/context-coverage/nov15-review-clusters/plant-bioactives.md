## Original reference checks

Review table 2 ref 60, Pathania et al. 2021 PMID 33882412, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33882412/ : in silico, human platelet assays and MPTP-treated mice received isolated hydroxytyrosol, a compound occurring in olive oil. Abstract reports motor impairment attenuated on catalepsy bar, narrow beam walk and open-field tests, plus biochemical outcomes. Whole olive oil was not the intervention, and no human bradykinesia or postural-instability result. Full article arm timing, dose specifics and adverse outcomes remain inaccessible in the abstract route, and the review table must not be read as though oil was administered; it places extra virgin olive oil in the source column for hydroxytyrosol.

Review ref 116, Anandhan et al. 2012 PMID 22138428, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22138428/ : purified theaflavin in MPTP/probenecid mouse model, with pathological and behavioral outcomes; black tea is botanical source, not administered brewed beverage. Abstract does not identify exact behavioral task or adverse results. Full body and precise dose/timing remain open. Review ref 117 is black-tea-derived compounds against amyloid and α-syn fibrils in laboratory, not patient motor scores.

Review ref 55, Yarim et al. online 2022 PMID 35698811, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35698811/ : mice received isolated apigenin in MPTP prophylaxis or post-exposure groups; abstract measures cytokines and tissue histopathology, not clinical rigidity, and parsley is a possible natural source, not the administered intervention. The abstract reports protocol in four groups (60 mice); its wording around administration route of all treatment components is not sufficiently clear to assign beyond explicit descriptions without full text. Separate quercetin-nanoparticle acrolein model [54] similarly does not administer raw onions.

Review ref 153, Huh et al. online 20 June 2023 PMID 37337830, original publisher https://www.biomolther.org/journal/download_pdf.php?doi=10.4062%2Fbiomolther.2023.098 : prevention-style mouse bacterial-injury model includes separate oral whole-plant-derived ginger extract and synthetic 6-shogaol arms. Fresh ginger heated 120°C two hours, dried, 70% ethanol reflux and lyophilized; 300 mg/kg/day extract vs 10 mg/kg/day 6-shogaol, begun one day prior to P. mirabilis exposure for 22 days. The 8-per-group motor figure reports open-field, pole and rotarod tests: extract group had more open-field movement, faster pole descent and longer rotarod vs bacterial group; 6-shogaol group showed faster pole descent, not a reported separate rigidity score. Report says five groups in prose but actually enumerates four; additional group not inferable. This is a heat-treated concentrated extract and a mouse model, not grated fresh ginger or measured clinical rigidity. Original is already documented in `research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Spice-Followup.md`. The review [153–156] groups diverse studies, including a nonmotor kidney injury paper [155], so do not assign all its claims to each study.

Rosemary mention at review line 166 is an isolated constituent, carnosic acid, and cell-protection mechanisms, not a fresh-leaf postural-balance trial. Food examples cauliflower, banana, tomato, onion and grapes are discussed generally as flavonoid sources; separate compounds/animal and cell models cannot establish individual culinary exposures. Anxiety is a general symptom/context and no implicated specific food anxiety intervention is shown in this review.
