## Corrected clinical Parkinson reports

Review citation 539, Ghodsi et al. 2022, PMID 35636380, registry IRCT20171123037600N1: 60 people randomized, oral nanomicellar curcumin 80 mg/day or placebo for 9 months, testing MDS-UPDRS and PDQ-39 each 3 months. Primary abstract reports no significant between-arm difference at any single visit; Part III group-by-time p=.04 but post hoc comparisons did not identify individual visit differences. Thus the review body claim that MDS-UPDRS significantly decreased at 90 days is not supported by its primary abstract. Its Table 3 labels the PD study under multiple sclerosis. Full primary tables were not accessible, and distinct bradykinesia and rigidity item results cannot be inferred. Existing curated primary note `research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#nanomicelle-curcumin-as-an-add-on`; primary abstract https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35636380/ and IRCT https://irct.ir/trial/28424 .

Review citation 538, Donadio et al. 2022, PMID 35556131, full original https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9210322/ , Table 2: nonrandomized comparison of 19 supplemented and 14 untreated analyzed PD patients (40 screened). Oral phospholipid curcumin formulation 1 g twice daily; commercial formulation standardized to 18–22% curcuminoids, so distinguish formulation mass from pure curcumin mass. Mean off-medication MDS-UPDRS motor change +5 versus +11; listed p values compare each arm with its own baseline. Participants selected different medication strategies, nonrandomized confounding, no individual rigidity or bradykinesia results. Original also presents adjusted regression; do not claim group equivalence or causal efficacy from these data. Source already curated in research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#phospholipid-curcumin-and-composite-outcomes.

Separate older 2019 Maghbooli IRCT20101209005352N2 https://journals.sbmu.ac.ir/neuroscience/article/download/26302/4 , 50 PD patients assigned nanomicellar curcumin 80 mg twice daily or placebo 3 months; fatigue severity effect p=.42 inconclusive while global sleep and QoL reported improved. Already curated in research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Iran-Registry-Followup.md, not a new cohort from a second report. Trial registration archive https://irct.ir/trial/5726 . Search led to an apparently stale secondary aggregator saying recruiting, but the primary 2019 report establishes publication; do not use aggregator status as current.

## Clinical depression and ingredient-specific context

Review citation 533, Lopresti/Drummond 2016 first online (2017 issue), original abstract https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27723543/ : 123 adults with major depression, four arms: placebo; curcumin extract 250 mg twice daily; curcumin extract 500 mg twice daily; curcumin extract 250 mg twice daily plus saffron 15 mg twice daily, 12 weeks. Combined active arms versus placebo showed IDS-SR30 depression p=.031 and STAI state p<.001, trait p=.001; no difference between active arms was found. The review Table 3 incorrectly pairs 500 mg twice daily with the combined saffron arm. Pooled results do not isolate saffron's effect; no Parkinson cohort or ground saffron comparison. The review quotes this study as ingredient-related context for 0063 but cannot establish that frozen claim.

Review citation 567, Saleh et al., 2025 primary https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12371096/: randomized 144 COVID outpatients to 500 mg turmeric pills, ginger pills or placebo three daily for five days; source calls them pills, and its methods do not establish whether whole powdered rhizome or extract. Primary outcomes CRP, ESR, LDH and white cell count, not Parkinson pain, rigidity or anxiety. The review's description as oral curcumin versus ginger extract should not silently override the original product uncertainty. Report says 43/43/42 completed, consistent with 128 total. No claim-level result.

Review citation 524 is sesame-oil lipid nanocarrier tested in human fibroblasts for psoriasis, not oral cold-pressed/toasted oil, Parkinson, anxiety or pain. Citation 298 olive-oil nanoemulsion and breast-cancer cell tests is not dietary olive-oil PD anxiety/bradykinesia. Citation 419 salmon-lecithin curcumin nanoliposomes and cancer cell viability are not grilled/poached salmon or PD; chicken embryo results are not poached chicken. Ginger family identity is not proof of a grated ginger intervention. Dried date appears as publication date metadata, not date-fruit exposure.
