This conceptual review concerns mechanisms hypothesized to prevent or delay neurodegeneration, but cites some actual clinical studies on symptoms. It introduces no new clinical trial. It proposes that EPA, DHA and astaxanthin might modify NRF2-related pathways, expressly acknowledging no direct clinical data for its specific enzyme hypothesis. Food-derived chemicals, whole dietary patterns and specific culinary preparations are not interchangeable. `exact-reviews.tsv` covers all 17 current priority-3 source pairs, with role proposals for the review itself, not verdicts.

## Original clinical citations and misdescription

Review ref 21 is Anwar 2024 secondary review, not the original cohort. Its claim that 1,053 patients took "supplements containing olive oil, wine, fresh fruit..." misrepresents the underlying food exposure. Original Mischley et al. 2017 https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2017/6405278 is a food-frequency survey in 1,053 self-reported PD patients. It reports associations of olive oil and coconut oil frequency with total PRO-PD severity adjusted for years since diagnosis, not randomized treatments or verified prospective individual symptom progression. Product pressing/refining and dyskinesia/anxiety/executive domain-specific contrasts are not provided. Dietary and supplement analyses are separate; do not treat cited foods as supplement ingredients. Existing primary archive should retain independent attribution.

Review says Mediterranean cognition randomized trial involved 40 people; primary Paknahad abstract https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32444045/ reports 80 randomized, 40 per group, 10-week intervention; combined dietary advice and executive-function domain measured by MoCA. Does not isolate walnut oil, flax oil, coconut oil or oil processing. Trial full methods/tables remain prior local access gap; existing context `followup/context-coverage/mediterranean-curcumin-audit-2026-11-15.md` notes other Paknahad cohort overlap remains unverified.

Review ref 25 original Rusch et al., 23 December 2021, NCT03851861 https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2021.794640/full : single-arm 5-week Mediterranean diet for 8 PD participants, recommends abundant olive oil among many diet components. GSRS constipation syndrome mean 2.25 to 1.54, paired p=.04; bowel movements/week and stool form did not differ significantly (p=.31 and .37). Eight household partners supplied baseline samples, not an intervention comparison arm. No separately assigned extra-virgin oil or oil-specific estimate; the mixed-diet association cannot be ascribed to olive oil. Prior literature already catalogs Rusch; review itself is related context only for 0072.

Review ref 22 Gao et al. 2007 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17991663/ tracks initially healthy participants and incident PD across dietary patterns, not symptom severity in people already diagnosed; cannot establish ingredient-specific symptom effects. Ref 38 Zali et al. 2024 randomized probiotics plus vitamin D against placebo; anxiety and GI scores are measured for the combined product, not an olive, coconut, walnut, soy, corn or flaxseed oil intervention.

## Source composition caveat

Table 1 reports paprika ALA as a percentage of fresh pericarp fatty acids for Jaranda/Jariza, not a percentage of the total paprika weight and not ground or smoked preparation. It cites [80], which is a 2022 curcumin/PD review and does not support the listed paprika figures. The figures appear in the independent Cholewski 2018 composition review https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6267444/ with its own reference 72; this source chain must be traced to the actual analytic paper before publishing composition numbers. Table values for olive, flax, soy, corn and walnut oils cite another nutritional-composition review and do not test neurologic symptoms. Astaxanthin or isolated EPA/DHA are not fish, oil or a patient symptom outcome.
