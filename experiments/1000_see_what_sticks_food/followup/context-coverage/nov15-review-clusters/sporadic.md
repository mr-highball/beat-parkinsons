# Sporadic Parkinson pathogenesis review

Dolgacheva and colleagues' review first published October 27, 2022. Its 23 original memberships were checked against lines 257 and 267 and the referenced bibliography. [Original archive](../../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC9657547.txt)

## Saffron trail

The list mentions saffron constituents and points to a separate review, PMID 35244296. It contains no measured effect of ground saffron on depression, rigidity or falls. The intermediate review's exact primary references remain access-limited. [Intermediate source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35244296/)

## Cinnamon trail

Cinnamon or cinnamaldehyde appears in the same secondary-reference passage. The article gives no assigned ground cinnamon intervention and no specific comparison of the frozen symptoms.

## Cumin identity

The named black cumin is Nigella sativa, associated with thymoquinone. The frozen culinary cumin is Cuminum cyminum. The intermediate source does not document the frozen toasted or ground cumin and symptom comparisons.

## Black pepper trail

The secondary reference mentions black pepper and isolated piperine. Complementary animal trials concern piperine and motor tasks or drug coadministration. The parent article does not connect an assigned cracked-pepper exposure to the frozen clinical symptoms. [Original piperine and levodopa study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10848080/)

## Turmeric forms

The review discusses isolated curcumin, a synthetic curcumin glucoside and experimental aggregation. The cited work did not use the frozen culinary turmeric preparations and symptom measurements. [Original synthetic-derivative report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22211690/)

## Blueberry model

The cited original used commercial blueberry juice and wheel running in lesioned rats. The relevant reported contrast is exercise-associated rotation behavior. Juice exposure and the measured task differ from a raw berry and clinical constipation comparison. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8935512/) · [Institutional original copy](https://uvadoc.uva.es/handle/10324/63897)

## Date context

The original has no verifiable Phoenix dactylifera dried date fruit and pain intervention. Publication or timing language is separate from the food exposure.
