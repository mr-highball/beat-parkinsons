## Corrections to review summaries

One review table described the 2021 MEDI-PD protocol as a completed trial with improved constipation. The 2024 results compared the Mediterranean diet plus usual care against usual care. In the intention-to-treat analysis, reported median constipation changes were −0.5 and −0.8 with p = .60 for the between-arm comparison. The older 2021 publication from another registration gave improvement within eight participants and did not randomize a constipation comparison. Neither comparison isolates an olive oil effect. [Protocol](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjopen-2021-053336) · [2024 original trial](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/16/17/2946) · [2021 pilot](https://doi.org/10.3389/fneur.2021.794640)

Fox's whole-diet MEDI association for anxiety was −0.739 with Holm-adjusted p = .10. The nominal p value of .01 repeated by the review does not carry that adjustment. The adjusted insomnia association was −1.07 with p = .02 for the whole diet. Survey items naming olive oil were analyzed against total PRO-PD. They do not give anxiety or insomnia outcomes by oil grade. [Original Table 2](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/14/23/5185) · [Oil item estimates](https://www.mdpi.com/article/10.3390/nu14235185/s1) · [Dated source context](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Oil-Followup.md#olive-oil-items-in-the-mvp-diet-pattern-analysis)

In Donadio, the published table analyzed 19 supplemented and 14 untreated participants. The untreated group chose usual medication adjustment, with no placebo assignment. The Methods report 21 and 19 initial participants, and the older catalog text used the latter control count incorrectly. Mean OFF examination scores increased by +5 in the supplement group and +11 in the untreated group. The table gives within-group changes on the composite, without separate rigidity or bradykinesia estimates. The study used phospholipid curcuminoids. [Original Tables 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9210322/) · [Corrected chronological catalog](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Evidence.md#phospholipid-curcumin-and-composite-outcomes)

A primary nanomicelle study reports a lower global PSQI in both treatment groups and a larger change with curcuminoids. A review used the word increase for the score. The primary table has no separate daytime sleepiness score. The accessible Ghodsi abstract and registry give aggregate scales without public item-level results. Paknahad severity and cognition reports came from the same research setting with closely similar baseline data and different trial accessions. Participant overlap remains unverified. The full Paknahad severity and Ghodsi reports are separate access gaps.
