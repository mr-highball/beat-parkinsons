# Facial expression, falls, voice and anxiety, November 15, 2026

## 0343 moved to Maybe True

The frozen statement says cold-pressed olive oil reduces anxiety severity in Parkinson disease. The 2024 MVP baseline graph gives a small inverse association between olive oil frequency and self-reported anxiety in people with Parkinson disease. The figure supplies no numerical coefficient or pairwise uncertainty, and the exposure field does not distinguish pressing method. In a separate convenience sample of 115 adults, higher reported olive oil quantity was associated with 1.86 points lower HADS anxiety after adjustment for age band, sex and education (95% CI −3.54 to −0.18, nominal p = .031). The unadjusted comparison's q value was .084, and the comparison for olive oil as the main cooking fat was inconclusive. The survey contains no Parkinson-specific or cold-pressed comparison. The matching direction across the two observational reports merits a **Maybe True at 51** judgment on the existing scale. The 12-week DieTBra trial did use cold-pressed extra-virgin oil. The oil-only arm's HADS anxiety prevalence declined within the arm, as did both other active groups. The published statistical critique says the between-arm post-treatment comparison favored traditional diet alone. The original outcome table and authors' reply remain inaccessible. The trial supplies no verified benefit attributable to the oil. The judgment rests on the convergence of observational directions and remains weak because grade, temporality and residual confounding are unresolved. The two related whole-oil and hydroxytyrosol rat studies were also screened.
