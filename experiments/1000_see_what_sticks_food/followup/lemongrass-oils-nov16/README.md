## Lemongrass and olive oil

The Grande-Terre survey documents two combined stress or insomnia reports using a bedtime lemongrass leaf infusion. The earlier volunteer trial investigated tea, sleep reports and dream recall. Neither source measured a minced-herb comparison in Parkinson insomnia or recorded witnessed dream enactment. The regional search records its Brazilian and Thai registry limits. ## Sunflower oil and depressive symptoms

Van de Rest examined an oral high-oleic sunflower comparator in older adults. Lucas examined a sunflower and fish-oil blend as an active comparator in women with psychological distress. Neither trial has an oil-free arm. In the Lucena inhalation trial, both randomized groups used the same sunflower carrier and one received lavender. The abstract's depression statistic is a within-lavender change. The sunflower oil grade and between-group estimate remain unverified. The earlier Nemets trial used olive or safflower oil, which resolves a later inconsistent attribution to sunflower. The Warren full methods remain open. ## Pumpkin seed oil, mood and orthostatic lightheadedness

The Gossell-Williams depression item is unchanged at zero with pumpkin oil. The wheat-germ-oil arm's median rose from zero to one. This is a postmenopausal item and the comparison leaves the effect on existing Parkinson depression unresolved. Karli's official supplement contains fatty-acid analysis. The registry specifies seated blood pressure and the clinical article's full estimates are inaccessible. The Maiya dissertation and Wong results tables remain open, as does reporting for the completed Caudill trial. ## Parsley, celery leaf and ginger

Four parsley expressions returned zero indexed records. The verified bedtime tea crossover measured morning refreshment against passionflower, with no daytime sleepiness instrument or Parkinson cohort. The celery search returned three appearances from one root-extract case and a review of the same case. The original abstract confirms mania with concurrent medications. Visual modality and dose remain unresolved from the body. Two independent thyroid reports concern dried leaves and a celery extract of unspecified plant part, respectively. Their outcomes do not estimate Parkinson visual hallucination frequency.
