# Selected original source groups

The intake used 189 exact frozen claim and provider-key pairs. This includes 52 earlier staged context pairs, 18 additional clinical pairs, five cases recovered from the earlier Alzheimer medication access gap, and 114 pairs from the ranked source-group pass. The 114-pair block was assigned by queued pair count in priorities one through four, then best priority and source key. It included the first ranked group with six pairs and 27 four-pair groups. Previously assigned source groups and two unresolved article identities were screened for overlap. Review reached full text for 110 of the 114 pairs and kept four saffron review entries pending. Moretti's publisher PDF confirms two Alzheimer medication regimens in 50 patients and no assigned culinary dates or sage. Its discussion mentions apathy and provides no separate arm-level apathy contrast. The complete NCBI records for a prosthesis-disuse case and an orthopedic fracture case add eight body reviews. The former examines gait and balance around prosthesis use. The latter reports surgical care and mentions Parkinson disease in a previously published case. The blonanserin extension lists dystonia in 12 of 106 adolescents with schizophrenia and excludes Parkinson disease. Its meals time medication administration. The ROMEMA protocol describes a medication study in mild cognitive impairment or Alzheimer dementia. Corn starch is a tablet excipient, and no corn-oil, dried-date or Parkinson symptom comparison is assigned. Another Parkinson study uses cinnamon, peach and apple as odor pens. An autonomic phenotype study asks participants to withhold coffee and tea before testing. The original roles preserve excipients, diagnostic stimuli, prior restrictions and clinical exposures in their respective study contexts. [ROMEMA protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10910786/) · [Odor testing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5115229/) · [Autonomic phenotyping](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38026514/)

An eight-participant Mediterranean-diet pilot reported lower constipation severity after broad diet counseling that included olive oil. Its single-arm design leaves an isolated culinary effect unresolved. A 139-participant Parkinson survey reports culinary differences by ethnicity and no clear nonmotor association for the studied diet factors. Other groups examined dietary education, probiotics and clinical comorbidities. These comparisons are linked to the recorded frozen exposure and symptom boundaries in the disposition rows.
