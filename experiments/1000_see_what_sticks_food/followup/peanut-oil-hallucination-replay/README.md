# Cold-pressed peanut oil and visual hallucinations · 0929

The sole original source key identifies the 1985 World Congress of Neurology abstract collection. Abstract 05.09.04 uses peanut oil as an injection vehicle in a hippocampal stress experiment. Abstract 13.09.06 separately reports visual hallucinations in people with Parkinson disease receiving medication. Reviewing the named presentations resolves the joint conference-level text match. The scanned PDF still has OCR limits. [Original scanned proceedings](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC7087691.1/PMC7087691.1.pdf)

The 1997 human oil challenge compared crude and refined oils in people with peanut allergy. Six reacted to crude oil, and none reacted to refined oil. The original crude oil pressing method and a hallucination result are unavailable. An earlier oral peanut-oil challenge likewise measured allergy. The ARISTOTLE food trial used peanut-oil-based manufactured control butter in healthy adults and reported stress and memory measures. ## Original pharmacy formulation and clinical exposure trail

The 2017 Transvaal leaflet names refined peanut oil as the sublingual vehicle and discusses hallucinations in its cannabis-specific warning. A clinician interview mentions Parkinson prescribing without identifying individual intake or reporting hallucinations. A similar sublingual formulation in an original dermatology case series gives no supplier or carrier. Product concentration alone leaves participant exposure unverified.
