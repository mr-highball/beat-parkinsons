## Anxiety evidence

Three Europe PMC queries retrieved ten ranked appearances. Five records
matched the ClinicalTrials.gov query. Complementary English, Spanish and
Portuguese web searches were used to follow originals and registries.
Those relevance-ranked results did not expose a complete count. [Queries](focused-queries.tsv) ·
[Literature dispositions](focused-results.tsv) · [Registry query](registry-queries.tsv) ·
[Registry dispositions](registry-dispositions.tsv)

An existing Parkinson survey reports oregano use in 29 of 139 people.
Its exploratory analyses use total HADS and NMSS and do not give an
oregano-specific anxiety estimate. The 2021 Parkinson trial of strained
dried sweet-marjoram tea reports total NMSS and Beck depression changes.
Anxiety is mentioned in the introduction. The accessible results omit a
separate anxiety comparison. The author-declared registry for the 2025
biomarker study has no registered anxiety outcome and lists 2022 conduct
dates, while the published article lists 2023–2024. The two publications
address distinct outcomes. Individual participant overlap remains open.

The rheumatoid arthritis trial's marjoram-containing massage blend favored
HADS against usual care. It showed no clear difference from almond-oil
massage, which itself favored anxiety versus usual care. The original
2026 effect table remains open. A completed stroke registry specifies
GAD-7 for topical sweet marjoram plus carrier massage and has no posted
outcomes. The oral six-spice registry plans SF-36 mental health and
excludes Parkinson disease. Its results are also unposted.

## Oracle lookup

The first search reached trial notes, while exact lookup missed the
older-format local records for NCT06693960 and NCT07123181. The catalog now
includes their current official records in its indexed registry format,
with the earlier extracts retained. The still-unsupported PACTR accession
was resolved through its verified official record and local catalog note.
