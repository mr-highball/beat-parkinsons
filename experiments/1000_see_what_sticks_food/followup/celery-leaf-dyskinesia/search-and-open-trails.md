# Claim 0002 evidence search

The frozen hypothesis asks whether eating fresh celery leaf worsens dyskinesia severity in people with Parkinson's. The evidence examined leaves the direction Unknown. The best-matched exposure reports measure plant chemistry and human circulating apigenin. The closest Parkinson studies use multicomponent meals or pharmaceutical butylphthalide. No verified source measured dyskinesia after the named culinary exposure.

## Native search receipts

- Local sources already covered leaf solvent fractions in ketamine sleep, hydroalcoholic leaf extract in hypertensive rats, orally administered whole dried celery extract in an MPTP mouse model, and a Parkinson vegetarian diet listing unspecified celery. The local 1987 human dietary-protein study describes changing levodopa response and dyskinesia during high- versus low-daytime-protein diets. That comparison does not identify a celery contribution.
- The Athena Europe PMC expression run dated 2026-11-15 UTC returned every page of all eight expressions. Counts were 0 exact celery-leaf plus explicit dyskinesia terminology, 1 ingredient plus Parkinson title/abstract, 1 ingredient plus levodopa-class terms, 4 butylphthalide plus named complications title/abstract, 12 broader butylphthalide outcome records, 0 flavonoid plus explicit dyskinesia, 0 multilingual exact-title cases, and 23 leaf/plant part and compound records. The 41 combined results include overlapping records. Exact queries and returned results are in europe-pmc/ingredient_queries.tsv and europe-pmc/ingredient_results.tsv. Raw/boiled leaves are explicitly separated from stalks in the original 2006 chemistry paper. The core also records a dried-leaf distillate that supplies a complementary measurement.
- The Athena ClinicalTrials.gov query retrieved 1 of 1 records across all pages. NCT07518810 assesses pharmaceutical butylphthalide in multiple system atrophy. The safety outcome and Parkinson-related search term do not make it a trial of fresh celery leaf or Parkinson dyskinesia. See registry/registry-query.tsv and registry/registry-exposure-candidates.tsv.

## Open trails

- Zhang Xiaping et al., Shanghai Medical & Pharmaceutical Journal 2011;32(6):274–276, is cited by a 2023 review as reporting a 1.5-point reduction in total UPDRS Part IV and changes in ON and OFF time after pharmaceutical butylphthalide. Exact item definitions and arm results are unavailable. The 2017 and 2023 citing bibliographies spell the second author's name differently (俞海涛 and 俞海泓). Searches by the title, both spellings, issue/pages, the publisher archive, Wanfang and Reference Network did not recover the original. A library copy with the complete Part IV and ON/OFF data is still valuable.
- ChiCTR1800017084 registers MDS-UPDRS Part IV as secondary. Han and colleagues' 2024 conference report appears to be the same trial but gives primary cognitive results only and broad statements on adverse events. Fuller publication, sponsor reporting or item-level data remain relevant. The treated entity is pharmaceutical butylphthalide.
- ChiCTR-IPR-16009395 (official URL showproj.html?proj=16021, HTTP 405 in direct calls) has a public mirror describing 72 planned participants and oral butylphthalide added to stable treatment including specified minimum levodopa. The mirrored endpoints are UPDRS Parts I–III combined plus wearable measurements. No authenticated results were recovered, so the mirror is a search lead.
- The original journal excerpt for Wang et al. 2019 (疑难病杂志 18(1):44–47) describes 86 participants and randomization to butylphthalide plus Madopar versus Madopar alone. Its opening describes dyskinesia as a general dopaminergic adverse effect. The reproduced report's results are behind an app sign-in. That phrase supplies no observed item-level result.
- Ni Li et al. 2026 (PMID 42923556) studied pharmaceutical NBP with donepezil in a retrospective comparison where both arms continued levodopa/benserazide. The original abstract lists UPDRS Parts II and III and says overall adverse-event incidence was comparable. The report's actual adverse-event categories were inaccessible in this search. NCBI and Europe PMC full-text representations returned an access challenge, rate limit, or unavailable record. Continue toward the full table before drawing symptom-specific conclusions.
- The 2008 and 2009 human celery-leaf and apigenin papers may overlap. Their reported cohort sizes differ while authorship, institution, protocol and exact values overlap. Their preparation data and drug-disposition endpoint can inform study identification, with no result for levodopa pharmacokinetics.
