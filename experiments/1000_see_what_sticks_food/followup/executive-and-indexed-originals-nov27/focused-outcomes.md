# Round 1 executive function, balance and mood follow-through

## 0949 frozen raspberry and executive function

The USDA Agricultural Research Service record for the 2026 journal article states that 36 participants completed both meal visits. The official registry still lists actual enrollment of 30, with its last update posted on July 14, 2020 and no results posted. The trial investigators' 2023 report names the registry and plots approximately 7.0 versus 9.9 total eight-box errors and 6.9 versus 9.5 between-search errors at six hours. The final article's abstract matches the investigators, meals, population and tasks. These records support one likely crossover family. The final group results, cognitive-analysis denominator and enrollment reconciliation remain open. The comparison used freeze-dried raspberry powder in older adults after a meal, so the existing minimal easing lean remains provisional for intact frozen raspberry and Parkinson executive impairment. ## 0019 toasted nigella and depressive symptoms

The preprint, protocol and 2022 outcome paper share IRCT20200411047027N1. The outcome report's Methods and Tables 1–2 confirm 20 analyzed in the cold-pressed nigella oil group and 21 in the paraffin oil group. HADS depression was prespecified. The inspected results and accessible protocol supplements supply no arm-level HADS data, and the outcome-paper denominators cannot resolve that gap. IRCT and WHO access checks did not provide a fresh registry record. ## 0471 cooked brown rice and postural instability

The EXPRESS comparison in prefrail and frail older adults assigned rice protein or whey alongside shared exercise. The public original abstract reports no physical-performance group difference. The registry prespecifies an SPPB total, which includes standing balance. The Oxford evidence table and a 2026 synthesis identify the same cohort without supplying the original standing-balance results. Exact primary group values, uncertainty and ingredient-specific transfer to cooked brown rice in Parkinson disease remain open. [Original DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/21551197.2021.1886222) · [Registry](https://anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=370522) · [Oxford evidence table](https://academic.oup.com/view-large/523175637)

## 0259 orange and falls

The 1984 publisher original and the 1986 Australian government report independently document orange juice in experimental masking drinks. Their analyses cannot isolate citrus or show that orange changes falls. The complete Wetherell review mentions that practice without naming the source in the relevant passage. Neither new original is a verified citation match for that review, so its exact source attribution remains queued.
