## 0929 historical originals

The 1947 original describes a child's apparent visual experience after topical exposure. It cites a contemporary arachis-oil formulation without verifying the case-specific batch. A separate original from 1950 explicitly names peanut oil as the injected drug carrier and records auditory and visual hallucinations during recurrent mania after active drug withdrawal. This is the same study and patient treatment context, with the active drug, route and psychiatric illness central to interpretation. The two new study rows are related observations with no oil-attributable or Parkinson comparison. ## 0635 regulatory and clinical source checks

The FDA drug case-ID and generic reaction queries returned 1/1 and 3/3 results with three distinct report keys across both queries. All list pomegranate as concomitant and code generic hallucination. The two 32-product records have duplicate flags and identical product and reaction sets. The oral product in the third record leaves the dose form and raw fruit exposure unspecified. Separate visual-specific drug and generic/visual food-event queries returned the provider's no-match response. The scope covers the listed search terms and datasets and does not establish no visual adverse events.
