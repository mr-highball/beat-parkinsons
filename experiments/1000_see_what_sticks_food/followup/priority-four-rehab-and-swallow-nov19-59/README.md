# Rehabilitation and swallowing source review

The 16-source set comprises all 69 queued claim–source pairs for the selected rehabilitation and swallowing studies and the two largest priority-four identifier discrepancies. The official PubMed records and original PubMed Central XML verify 13 full article bodies. The article methods, tables, captions and food terms were screened, with separate supplement access tracked. Twenty-six pairs reached full-text review. Twenty-five were contextual exclusions. One retained orange juice as a real ingredient in the [nurse-led dietary guidance](../../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Rehab-and-Orange-Context.md#orange-juice-in-nurse-led-dietary-guidance). Orange intake was neither separately assigned nor measured. The program measured drooling, with an adjusted difference of −2.34 points (95% CI −3.98 to −0.70). Falls were outside that comparison. Fig. labels and Raspberry Pi hardware were resolved against the original methods. The [swallowing studies](../../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Rehab-and-Orange-Context.md#swallowing-tests-texture-and-unrelated-treatment-comparisons) used barium with standardized food textures, distinct from sage intake.

The two PubMed expressions for raw oranges and fall outcomes and one ClinicalTrials.gov search exhausted their returned memberships. They identify a [citrus juice blood-calcium study](../../../../research/2015-2019/Source-Text/2017-PMID28802068-Citrus-Juice-and-Ionized-Calcium.txt) and orange juice shared by both arms in a [tremor challenge](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/Trials/NCT06403280.txt). The six newly archived rehabilitation registrations preserve actual arms, release dates and measured or planned outcomes. These observations leave the raw-orange fall hypothesis open.
