# Original source reviews and measured food exposure

The native queue replay verifies 394 full-text dispositions across seven claims, out of 408 assigned. The remaining 14 exact assignments retain original-access or complete-member holds.

The apple chewing study used 10 g peeled apple samples in 20 participants with Parkinson's and 20 matched controls. Its 2025 Table 2 reports chewing masseter activity of 74 versus 82 microvolts at the median (p=.74). The relative contraction measure was 82% versus 49% (p=.02). The separate workload was 26% versus 16% (p=.01). The 2023 Copenhagen paper reports other oral-function measures from the same participants. Neither paper assigns an apple-free group or estimates a separate resting-tremor effect. The 2018 celery study plots improvement in a separately assessed 0–5 rest-tremor score under a dried whole-plant solvent extract in mice. Figure 4a shows roughly 4.7 with vehicle and 2.1 with the highest extract group at day seven, with eight animals per group. This is an approximate figure reading. The extract measured apigenin and luteolin. Original fresh-petiole analysis detected both compounds using a fresh-weight denominator. A separate raw-stalk assay found 0.29 mg/kg 3-n-butylphthalide. The animal study did not measure that compound in the administered extract. The model points toward improvement with the extract. These sources leave the human raw-stalk direction unresolved because the active component and comparable exposure are unverified. [Animal source and figure](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12906-018-2166-0#Fig4) · [Fresh-petiole chemistry](../../../../research/2005-2009/Source-Text/2007-Vina-Chaves-Fresh-Celery-Petiole-Flavones-Extract.txt) · [Raw stalk and leaf phthalides](../../../../research/2005-2009/Source-Text/2006-Kurobayashi-Celery-Leaf-Phthalides.txt)

The peanut-oil retrieval authenticated two complete historical sources. Ebin's 1951 review cites a peanut-oil and wax drug suspension whose use was reported as unhelpful for tremor or rigidity. The underlying Bird and Meyers 1949 article remains at bibliographic depth. In the complete 1949 digest, the peanut-oil vehicle in a liver study and the mercurial rest-tremor cases occur in different reports. These comparisons give no isolated dietary-oil result.
