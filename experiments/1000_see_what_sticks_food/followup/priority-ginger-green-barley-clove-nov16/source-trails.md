# Source access and comparison details

## Ginger and anxiety

Simon PMID31476432 is a permeability assay, not a human anxiety trial. The official author abstract is available. The Semmelweis repository limits the full PDF to university IP. For PMID35345829, the original JATS body identifies curcumin from *Curcuma longa*, although the abstract calls it ginger. The body resolves the plant match for 0937. [Simon author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31476432/) · [Simon university record](https://repo.lib.semmelweis.hu/handle/123456789/8450) · [Curcumin original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8957039/)

The ginger adjunct trial’s public abstract gives conflicting directions. Publisher and author routes revealed no public numerical Hamilton Anxiety table. The posted IRCT protocol lists ginger powder plus SSRI and an anxiety outcome without results. Yuan’s 2014 Parkinson formula abstract documents HARS and SAS and a favorable reported direction, but gives no recipe or numerical result. The journal article route timed out, and cited thesis identity and formula remain unresolved. NCT07882108 already appears in the catalog and is recruiting. Its two ginger-extract doses are compared without a placebo assignment, and the exploratory MDS-UPDRS Parts I–IV give no posted item-level anxiety result. [Ataei publisher](https://www.benthamscience.com/article/135835) · [Ataei official registry](https://en.irct.ir/trial/53072) · [Yuan issue](https://xblc.bucm.edu.cn/zh/issue/2014/6/) · [Ginger-PD registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07882108)

The new Bano, Sharma and Fadaki records differ in extraction, doses, species and activity reporting. The focused searches also found PMID41632024 on oral extract in prenatal-valproate mice, and PMID34491647 on fresh ginger juice in a topical multi-ingredient IBS treatment with a reported quality-of-life anxiety item. Their original bodies and numerical results remain focused follow-through routes. [Bano primary report](https://www.nepjol.info/index.php/AJMS/article/view/34480/28861) · [Sharma primary article](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30084567/) · [Fadaki primary report](https://www.ijper.org/article/51/3s1/s159.pdf) · [Oral extract model](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41632024/) · [IBS trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34491647/)

## Green tea and fatigue

The Iowa State repository exposes the complete dissertation and derivative text. Chapter 5 Table 1 gives the same 14-person FSS summaries as the journal paper. The appendix gives demographic and medication details without paired FSS data. The linked 2013 conference abstract belongs to the same cohort. The UMIN000034209 registry still lists its four-condition trial as unpublished, with no study result fields populated. [Iowa State original dissertation](https://dr.lib.iastate.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/0f988737-be33-4064-837c-4029809bb93c/content) · [Green-tea registry](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000039000)

PMID42785532 lists isolated EGCG and a fly model. The abstract lists climbing and locomotion, while fatigue is in the traditional-use background. Publisher and matched SSRN submitted-version routes returned 403. The Europe PMC body endpoint returned 404, and the Elsevier API gave metadata. The associated SSRN title and authors match independently via Crossref. Preserve this as an open original body. ## Barley and clove

The barley-flour anxiety study concerns cooked flour in a talbina breakfast and comes from one proceedings and thesis family. The wheat, barley and rice listings in a Parkinson phytochemical review discuss food sources of ferulic acid, without food assignment. In the supplementary search, one Talbinah result refers to a digital platform, and another uses pearl-barley porridge as a shared carrier. The carrier does not estimate a barley effect. Full-body verification of the animal carrier study is a follow-through route. [Human crossover original abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6818840/) · [Barley carrier article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11504887/)

The older CCRAS annual report gives a null hot-water result in mice, with a printed dose of 1 mg/kg and no explicit powder route in that sentence. Its 1 g/kg oral observation was an inflammation comparison. The nearby acetic-acid writhing result concerns Jatamansi. The report’s preface is dated February 1998 while administrative text gives March 31, 1998 as the period end. Study and exact release dates remain uncertain. Separate clove oil papers from 2011 and 2015 used injections. The fissure cream abstract gives healing and pressure outcomes, leaving its clinical pain outcome open. The Oladele oral aqueous-extract download returned 404, so per-assay numbers and species remain to be checked. [Annual report, printed page 59](https://ccras.nic.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Annual-Reports-1997-1998.pdf) · [Fissure original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17573751/) · [Oral aqueous author abstract](https://doi.org/10.51244/IJRSI.2026.13020017)

The published Europe PMC receipts retain all query texts and recovered counts. Ginger category labels in the initial local search plan were corrected to the frozen spice category. Those additional interfaces supplied source leads and access checks without a stable complete-result count.
