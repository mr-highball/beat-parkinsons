## 2013 already catalogued case: additional full-text context

Gawrońska-Ukleja E et al., “Anaphylaxis after accidental ingestion of kiwi fruit,” Postepy Dermatol Alergol 30(3):192–194, PMID 24278073, DOI https://doi.org/10.5114/pdia.2013.35623, full original https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3834723/ and https://www.termedia.pl/Case-report-Anaphylaxis-after-accidental-ingestion-of-kiwi-fruit%2C7%2C20957%2C0%2C1.html . Publisher explicitly gives online date 2013-06-20; OpenAlex’s 2013-01-01 should not override it. In 2010 a 55-year-old male reported dyspnea, tongue numbness and swelling, dizziness, transient loss of consciousness and involuntary voiding after vanilla ice cream, attributed to a small amount of kiwi on a serving utensil. The inferred cross-contact was not measured. Later prick-by-prick testing with fresh kiwi was strongly positive and kiwi-specific IgE was elevated; headache, weakness, rash and dyspnea followed the test and were treated. The ingestion episode differs from the subsequent skin-test episode. No PD, orthostatic measurement, controlled food challenge or chronic symptom frequency is reported. Already catalogued as research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Kiwi-and-Caneberries.md#oral-allergy-cases-after-kiwifruit-and-raspberry with annotated source PMC3834723; do not create a duplicate accession. This is not a quantitative effect estimate.

## 2008 gold kiwi case accession candidate

Henríquez Santana A et al., “Anaphylaxis associated with the ingestion of Gold Kiwifruit,” Allergol Immunopathol 36(6):366–367, PMID 19150037, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/S0301-0546(08)75870-1, publisher original https://www.elsevier.es/en-revista-allergologia-et-immunopathologia-105-pdf-download-S0301054608758701 . Issue November–December 2008; no exact first-online date verified. A 35-year-old woman had throat itching, hives, dyspnea, uvular edema and measured hypotension after eating gold kiwi, treated with antihistamines, steroids and fluids; symptoms resolved within six hours. Specific IgE and skin test were positive; no oral provocation was performed. No quantitative BP, posture-specific testing or PD diagnosis. Gold cultivar is a distinct preparation from the frozen green/raw claim where applicable. This is a separate case from the 2013 contaminated-dessert report and the 2025 juice shock already catalogued.

Rank 13 Zhu et al. 2012 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22386357/ gives dizziness during anaphylactic shock after whole fresh kiwi and treatment; author abstract only. Printed values, original body and exact symptom timing remain open. PubMed also lists a subsequent comment (PMID 23380111), requiring review before a treatment conclusion; no medical treatment recommendation is inferred here. The broader pending list from the prior screen remains open, including anonymous collections and occurrence records; selected case upgrades do not close other records.


## 2010 — original additional kiwi case traced from allergy review

Gázquez et al., “Kounis Syndrome: Report of 5 Cases,” J Investig Allergol Clin Immunol 20(2):162–165, PMID https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20461972/ ; full journal PDF https://www.jiaci.org/issues/vol20issue2/10.pdf . Issue 2010; original submitted 2009-05-21, accepted 2009-07-16; exact first public date not verified. Keyword search did not locate an existing matching accession in the targeted local kiwi research, but a full catalog-wide dedup should precede integration.

Case 1, pp.162–163: 66-year-old male with known kiwi oral allergy developed urticaria, vomiting, chest pain and hypotension after a piece of kiwi; ECG abnormalities and raised tryptase occurred and a kiwi prick test was strongly positive. The paper does not state numerical blood pressure for this case, orthostatic position or Parkinson diagnosis. The report's 64/39 mm Hg belongs to case 2, a woman with ibuprofen-associated reaction, and must not be attributed to kiwi. Preparation and dose are incompletely specified; this was an acute allergic case, not ordinary kiwi effects. This patient is separate from the 2008 gold-kiwi, 2013 ice-cream and 2025 juice cases on age, sex, chronology and institutional details.

The 2023 kiwifruit-allergy review https://academic.oup.com/ijfst/article/58/12/6158/7807800, Table 2, led to this authenticated original and shows further allergy case trails; its aggregate percentages are of selected reports, not rates among all kiwi consumers. Pending 2003 Mempel PMID12789247 and 2007 Kerzl PMID17125821 concern likely linked immunotherapy history; primary full text not recovered and same-patient identity needs proof from the originals, so ranks 4 and 9 remain pending.
