## 0630 — raw arugula / olfactory impairment

[PMID 12137490](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12137490/) analyzes fresh Eruca sativa leaf headspace and odor-active compounds, not changes in olfactory ability after ingestion. [PMID 28946238](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28946238/) analyzes crushed Eruca and Diplotaxis volatiles; smelling a test substance is not ingestion affecting smell. No directional Parkinson comparison recovered. Continue original food-frequency and intervention records, distinguishing wild rocket species.

## 0597 — roasted butternut squash / voice volume

Butternut is Cucurbita moschata, not automatically the C. pepo seed preparations in other records ([species list](https://tind.wipo.int/nanna/record/57934/files/tg234_en.pdf?registerDownload=1&version=1&withMetadata=0&withWatermark=0)). No recovered primary compared roast flesh consumption with voice intensity in Parkinson patients. Hoarseness, sensory properties and general Cucurbita extracts do not establish loudness. Open trail: named meal records and acoustic speech endpoints.

## 0473 — dried summer savory / fatigue

2017 randomized dried Satureja hortensis leaf versus placebo evaluated cardiometabolic measures in metabolic syndrome, not fatigue: [IRCT2014012616372N1 and trial abstract](https://search.emarefa.net/en/detail/BIM-766370-the-effects-of-satureja-hortensis-l.-dried-leaves-on-serum-s). A distinct 2015 knee-osteoarthritis powder study measured pain, not fatigue ([original PDF](https://oaji.net/pdf.html?n=2016%2F2982-1455520556.pdf)). Neither justifies direction on fatigue. Keep S. hortensis distinct from S. khuzestanica and winter savory.

## 0427 — dried chives / shortened stride

Existing [chive outcome review](../../../followup/chive-outcomes/README.md) identifies chives in a multi-item Parkinson diet menu and aggregate motor reporting, not an individual chive assignment or stride result. New complementary search surfaced no dried chive/stride contrast. A researcher named Chivers is not a chive exposure; chive food assignment and stride-specific outcomes remain open.

## 0529 — toasted fennel seed / visual hallucination frequency

2002 proceedings DOI [10.1081/CLT-120005494](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1081/CLT-120005494), accessible as search-indexed individual abstracts but direct full-page fetch returned HTTP 403. Abstract 19 concerns star-anise tea, with auditory hallucinations in six, not fennel. Abstract 95 separately describes Datura-contaminated fennel tea: 15 symptomatic persons included blurred vision and other anticholinergic symptoms; it does not report visual hallucinations. Do not merge presentations or attribute contaminant effects to ordinary toasted fennel. [2011 fennel essential-oil case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21865126/) describes a seizure following a food with oil, not hallucination or toasted seed. Further original patient-level cases needed; no direction established.

## 0465 — grated nutmeg / freezing of gait

Existing [nutmeg pass](../../../followup/nutmeg-paprika/README.md) records several extract-model motor assays but no freezing episodes. [Hayfaa et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.2147/JPR.S45591), original PDF abstract and acute-toxicity section, reports unstable gait in mice after high doses of isolated seed alkaloid extract. It is not grated culinary nutmeg, Parkinson disease or measured freezing. [2010 inhaled essential-oil study](https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/11/11/4771) measured running-wheel activity in mice, not freezing or oral exposure. These do not supply a patient-level direction.

## 0579 — toasted hazelnut oil / daytime sleepiness

Existing [hazelnut oil pass](../../../followup/hazelnut-sunflower/README.md) describes controlled feeding including a fatigue-inertia outcome but no daytime sleepiness estimate. Fatigue inertia is not the Epworth sleepiness scale and oil roasting was not established. Whole-nut trials and non-hazelnut oil studies cannot identify toasted hazelnut oil effects. No new matched comparison recovered; seek product pressing and sleep-specific table or trial.

## 0480 — cold-pressed pumpkin seed oil / pain

Khalil et al. 2025, [DOI 10.21608/aimj.2025.446526](https://doi.org/10.21608/aimj.2025.446526), [indexed author abstract](https://openalex.org/works/W4414047556): 50 men with chronic pelvic pain were randomized equally to tadalafil or tadalafil plus a 1010 mg pumpkin-seed oil capsule for 12 weeks. The abstract reports an advantage in pain domain without numerical estimate, and clinically meaningful overall improvement 12/25 versus 11/25. Pressing method is unverified, and the publisher DOI body was inaccessible in this access pass. Do not recast abstract direction as a quantified pain effect or a Parkinson result. Existing pending assessment remains appropriate until original domain table and oil production are verified.

Gossell-Williams et al. 2011, [PMID 21545273](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21545273/), [original Table 3](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=d5cd5e8f2629f9463c6cf9a92731ecf39fcbbb2c&repid=rep1&type=pdf): 21 women assigned 2 g/day pumpkin oil and 14 wheat-germ-oil control; joint-pain median 2 to 1 in pumpkin group, 1.5 to 1 in control. Between-group endpoint p=.663; no demonstrated difference, exploratory single item, press method unspecified. It does not disprove a favorable effect or demonstrate it. Registry [NCT02119429](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02119429) identifies a separately registered premenopausal oil comparison, not automatically the 2011 postmenopausal report; this pass did not verify its results or pressing details.
