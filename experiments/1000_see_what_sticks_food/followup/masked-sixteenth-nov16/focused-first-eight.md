0500 fresh thyme urinary urgency: New Risi et al., 2017-12-31, PMID29473381 DOI 10.4081/aiua.2017.4.293 [original Table 1](https://www.pagepressjournals.org/aiua/article/download/aiua.2017.4.293/6904/33027). Forty-two nonconsecutive women with mild stress/mixed incontinence took one tablet/day of eight-herb product including thyme for two months, without placebo. Urgency intensity (PPIUS) mean 1.357±1.509 to 1.26±1.481 p=.705; 8 baseline PPIUS 4 retained that score; 5/17 with urgency improved and one worsened. Mixed product, unverified thyme species, part, amount and processing; no PD or urinary urgency frequency. No treatment effect on thyme isolated. Existing thyme essential-oil ex-vivo bladder experiments likewise do not establish clinical effect.

0443 cooked curry leaf rest tremor: Existing PMID32041265 Parkinson diet menu includes curry leaf with multicomponent UPDRS III; no per-item tremor, cooking or single-ingredient contrast. Original haloperidol/paraquat curry-leaf animal studies measure coordination and catalepsy or biochemical surrogates rather than a clinical rest-tremor estimate. No new qualifying effect.

0550 ground cardamom smell: 2021-12-31 Mukhtar et al. DOI10.36552/pjns.v25i4.617 [original Methods and Table 4](https://www.academia.edu/77124131/Anosmia_in_Parkinson_s_Disease_in_Pakistan_A_Matched_Case_Control_Study). Pakistan smell test used commercially available cardamom food essence, sniffed: 36/87 PD vs 49/58 controls correctly identified it (p<.001). Not a treatment or ingestion. In contrast Baert 2022 PMID35529474 recorded no clear case-control difference for identifying natural cardamom. Other in-catalog olfactory-training and testing sources use scent, not ground seed consumption. Related-only contextual assessment possible, no direction.
