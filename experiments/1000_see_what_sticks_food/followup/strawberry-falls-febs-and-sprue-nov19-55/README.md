# Strawberry falls, FEBS posters and historical sprue reports

The University of Padua holds all **372 pages** of the 2011 FEBS poster supplement. The first and last folios, matching DOI and selected member pages were verified. Native term review screened all 19 queued exposures in both raw-order and layout text. Thirty original claim–source pairs now have body-level decisions. Twenty-eight preserve ingredient, derivative or formulation context. Two resolve date and orange terminology. [Dated FEBS source text](../../../../research/2010-2014/Source-Text/2011-PMC7164111-FEBS-Poster-Food-and-Parkinson-Context.txt) · [Original pair decisions](dispositions.tsv) · [Native term counts](term-coverage.tsv).

The strawberry mentions come from a food-phenolics survey and an isolated ellagic acid experiment in cell lines. A separate Parkinson poster reports circulating biomarkers and supplies no dietary intervention. None of these members reports a raw strawberry and fall-frequency comparison. [Member notes](../../../../research/2010-2014/Source-Text/2011-PMC7164111-FEBS-Poster-Food-and-Parkinson-Context.txt) · [Supplement as deposited by the university](https://www.research.unipd.it/retrieve/e14fb267-9a44-3de1-e053-1705fe0ac030/Febs-2011.pdf).

The Dutch clinical literature gives firsthand reports of strawberry use for sprue. Van der Burg describes mixed berry regimens and possible irritation. The title leaf is dated 1880, although the bound volume is cataloged as 1881. Snijders describes three exposure periods in one patient with improving weight and stools during the longest period. He reports other dietary and medical changes, improvement that continued after strawberries ended, and irritation in some other recipients. A fracture during a transfer is recorded separately from the dietary observations. Slot mentions earlier strawberry use and describes a case whose fruit was not specified.
