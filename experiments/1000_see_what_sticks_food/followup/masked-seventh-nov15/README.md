## Grated turmeric and swallowing difficulty

Soni and colleagues randomized 60 people receiving postsurgical chemoradiotherapy for oral cancer. Their original abstract reports grade 3 dysphagia in 25% and 20% on the bio-enhanced oral turmeric arms versus 60% on placebo. A separate trial found mean Penetration-Aspiration Scale scores of 2.91 in both arms at baseline. After treatment, the mean remained 2.91 on nanocurcumin and was 3.72 on placebo among the 64 analyzed participants. Its May 2025 toxicity report, November 2025 swallowing report and 2026 congress abstract describe one cohort. The journal reports allocate 70 and analyze 64; the conference describes 64 as randomized. A separate oral fibrosis comparison gives 0/50 versus 4/50 with swallowing trouble at six months from 4/50 in both arms. Its inconsistent within-arm p-value and small counts make it weak secondary evidence.

The controlled outcomes give a weak favorable direction for the hypothesis, with large differences in preparation and the cause of swallowing difficulty. The Parkinson supplement registry gives regimen-level pill-swallowing difficulty but supplies no curcumin-specific count or severity result. The Soni arm table and independently accessible registrations for the two chemoradiotherapy cohorts remain open. A curcumin–piperine cancer trial has a reported swallowing score imbalance at baseline; its original comparison and model remain access work.
