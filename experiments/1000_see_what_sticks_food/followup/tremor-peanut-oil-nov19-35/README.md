## The recovered original Parkinson study

The 1949 Bird and Meyers paper reports lower tremor amplitude under each tested drug treatment. Frequency and occurrence were unchanged. The report presents no numerical amplitude estimate. The injected drug formulation contained peanut oil and wax. The comparison had no oil-only arm and did not test oral or cold-pressed oil. The 1951 Ebin review belongs to the same study family. The cited 1946 clinical case series associates the injected drug with relief of painful rigidity in some patients and reports no separately attributable tremor effect. The oral oil comparisons address outcomes such as allergic reactions, metabolism, cognition and general activity. In the blinded challenge among people with peanut allergy, 6 of 60 reacted to crude oil and zero reacted to refined oil in that sample. Processing details limit transfer to the exact cold-pressed product, and these are allergy outcomes. The motor models address mixed seed extracts, dietary lipid composition, or dyskinesia under an injected drug. The regional traditional-practice records describe cooking and topical use. They provide use history and no verified eyewitness observation of an effect on resting tremor.
