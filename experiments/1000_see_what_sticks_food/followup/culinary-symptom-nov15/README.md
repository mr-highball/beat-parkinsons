# Culinary preparations and measured symptoms

November 15, 2026. Two claims move from Unknown to Maybe at 51. The scores record minimal reviewer judgments based on transfer across preparations or populations.

## Weak directions

- **0133, brewed lemon balm reduces depressive symptom severity. Maybe True, 51.** Three independent placebo comparisons in people outside Parkinson disease favor the botanical in powdered capsules or extracts. Original angina Table 3 gives DASS depression means of 8.54 to 6.46 versus 6.92 to 6.71. The adjusted comparison favored the botanical, with close final means and no adjusted interval. Brew measurements establish a chemical bridge with large sample variation. The burn-patient tea table gives an ambiguous between-group contrast. In a different population, a freeze-dried aqueous extract left severe-depression counts unchanged while the placebo group started higher and fell from 10 to 6. Registered brewed tea versus hot water has unpublished results. - **0277, ground anise seed worsens fatigue. Maybe False, 51.** The ground-seed powder trial in functional dyspepsia favored the vitality domain. The separate IBS essential-oil trial favored lower tiredness on an item measured directly. At week four, values were 3.15 and 3.94 on a 0–10 scale with anise oil and placebo, respectively. The original table reports a three-arm p-value and the later review’s description conflicts with the table. The ground-seed trial supplies preparation fit and an indirect outcome. The oil trial supplies symptom fit and a different formulation. Both studied gastrointestinal populations. ## Reviewed claims that remain open

- **0006, fresh shiso and constipation.** Dried leaf extract had little observed difference from placebo in adult stool days and PAC-SYM. A separate seed-oil comparison favored selected constipation scores among athletes. An eight-person fixed-order oil pilot reported favorable within-phase measurements but lacked a randomized treatment comparison. [Dried leaf trial](../../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Shiso-Followup.md#dried-leaf-extract-and-constipation-measures-in-adults) · [Seed-oil trial](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Shiso-Followup.md#perilla-seed-oil-and-constipation-ratings-in-athletes-and-sedentary-adults)
- **0272, brewed black tea and walking slowness.** Observational contrasts for black tea and Parkinson motor score or black/oolong tea and gait quality could indicate a benefit, but neither measures a tea-specific change in Parkinson walking speed. The randomized caffeine gait-item interval includes both directions. [Parkinson tea cohort](../../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Black-Tea-Motor.md#black-tea-and-repeated-motor-composite-scores-in-lrrk2-parkinson-disease) · [Caffeine trial](../../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Black-Tea-Motor.md#randomized-caffeine-trial-and-separately-reported-gait-and-rest-tremor)
- **0774, ground nutmeg and insomnia.** One uncontrolled powder-in-milk case improved from 21 to 10 on the Regensburg scale by day 15 and reported daytime drowsiness early in treatment. A newer uncontrolled student study combined an infusion with sleep education. A separate uncontrolled report used the aril rather than ground seed. The findings do not isolate culinary ground nutmeg.
