# Chive preparation and symptom outcomes

The Parkinson menu study included chives in a multi-ingredient diet. Its motor exam included facial expression and its baseline assessment included therapy complications and stool form. The intervention results report a total motor score. A separate Parkinson protein-redistribution study recorded reduced OFF time among responders alongside more peak-dose dyskinesia in some patients. It did not identify a chive exposure. [Menu and outcomes](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Chive-Outcomes.md#facial-expression-in-the-chive-containing-parkinson-menu) · [Protein redistribution](../../../../research/1990-1994/Food-Experiment-Chive-Outcomes.md#protein-redistribution-and-peak-dose-dyskinesia)

A related *Allium* extract improved fecal output and intestinal transit in a constipation mouse model. Ferulic acid found in common chive tissue improved rotarod retention when administered as an isolated compound to a separate mouse cohort. The stress study used isolated ferulic acid and its supplement confirms elevated-plus-maze open arm entries among measured outcomes. Table S3 reports correlations with neurochemical measures and provides no between-arm estimate for that outcome.
