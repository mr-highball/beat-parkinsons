| Claim | Main evidence | Transfer judgment |

| 0768 · Steamed Swiss chard and olfactory impairment | Hegelmaier's vegetarian Parkinson diet lists Swiss chard in Appendix A alongside several dozen foods. It assessed composites and microbiota, with no olfaction endpoint. Molsberry's diet-pattern cohort measured hyposmia with the Brief Smell Identification Test in a subset of participants without diagnosed Parkinson disease. | This is an ingredient match without a symptom test and a symptom test without an isolated preparation. The GSA 2024 farmer's market abstract describes chard among leftover produce. Its collection places olfactory identification in a different ARIC analysis. Other sources use Chard as an author surname. |
| 0741 · Braised leek and apathy | The single Parkinson device case involved leek and bean sprouts in a jejunal phytobezoar. General Parkinson symptoms remained stable. Hegelmaier names leek without an individual food arm. The MVP dietary analysis shows lower self-rated lack of motivation at higher whole-diet scores, whereas the separate Oxford cohort's adjusted apathy associations were inconclusive. | Fox's sofrito item pools preparations, accepts tomato sauce as a positive, and gives an inconclusive comparison for total symptom burden (+45.0, nominal interval −11.6 to 102, adjusted p=.5). It does not establish leek consumption or symptom-specific apathy. Neither a device event nor a diet score gives a leek-specific direction. |
| 0575 · Fresh lemon balm and small handwriting | Two smaller Kennedy controlled healthy-adult trials test extract or dried leaf and computerized responses. Bortoletto's tincture study measures nocturnal temporal-muscle activity in children with bruxism. The 2020 program/body publication separates actual short-letter assessment from participant treatments. | Digit-symbol counts, attention speed, and bruxism do not estimate consistent or progressive small handwriting. The Calandra diagnostic comparison measures letters repeatedly and has no lemon balm exposure. |
| 0563 · Fresh tarragon and daytime sleepiness | Sayyah used injected concentrated essential oil and reported sedation/rotarod changes in mice. Kadatskaya's oral tincture increased early exploration and rearing, while the separate thesis context labels tincture sedative and points to drug-induced sleep studies with unresolved timing data. NCT02088203 completed the study of a soy-protein–Russian-tarragon complex in 2018, without posted outcomes. | Injection, concentrated aqueous-alcohol extract, motor suppression and sedative-induced sleep lack a reliable bridge to clinical daytime sleepiness after a fresh culinary leaf dose. The human protocol has general adverse-event monitoring and no registered sleepiness endpoint. |
| 0673 · Ground cayenne and reduced facial expressiveness | Topical capsaicin left spontaneous blink frequency inconclusive in five healthy volunteers and the source reports a focal eyelid dystonia case. A 2025 taste-disorder experiment used capsules filled with weighed culinary cayenne for whole-mouth pungency. The SALTVAPE protocol also uses a facial-expression picture scale alongside a cayenne preference item. | Human blink frequency, forced taste reactions and pictures selected to report liking are distinct from participant facial movement and hypomimia in Parkinson disease. No longitudinal or direct facial expression comparison after ingested ground cayenne was identified. |
| 0595 · Ground mustard seed and olfactory impairment | Jacquot et al. found improved immediate detection thresholds after nasal exposure to allyl isothiocyanate in healthy volunteers. Parkinson flavor-recognition tests use mustard as a stimulus, and Wright's 2021 oral mixed tea includes actual ground white mustard and reports no smell improvement. The 2024 report's illustrated participant scored zero for smell impairment throughout. | Nasal exposure to a constituent differs in route, concentration and outcome from eating ground mustard. The comparators in human food records either mix mustard with broccoli seed or use it as a stimulus. A single floor trajectory and absence of a tabulated smell result cannot resolve a directional effect. |
| 0714 · Cold-pressed walnut oil and daytime sleepiness | NCT04799821 reports an 8-week Epworth change of −1.37 points (95% CI −2.21 to −0.52) from baseline after eating 40 g whole walnuts daily in healthy young people. A mixed avocado/walnut/MCT placebo arm in fibromyalgia had three somnolence reports. McDonnell's bedtime control used walnut oil on a jojoba base alongside essential-oil aromatherapy in cardiac rehabilitation. | Whole walnuts and bedside exposure do not identify an oral cold-pressed-oil effect or an independent ingredient contrast. Tryptophan and melatonin measurements concern the whole nut. The bedside global PSQI difference does not supply a distinct daytime sleepiness result. |
| 0705 · Refined avocado oil and anxiety | The 40-person Rudisill acute trial compared CBD and mint-flavored avocado oil. The baseline-adjusted tranquility contrast was p=.85. The fibromyalgia mixed-oil placebo HADS-Anxiety change was +0.20 (−2.45 to 2.85), with no isolated oil arm. Mouse plus-maze results include an inconclusive direct cold-pressed-oil comparison in obesity and reduced open-arm behavior with reduced locomotion after maternal oil/pulp exposure. | VAMS calmness is a subjective immediate proxy. HADS-Anxiety is closer to the outcome but the oil is part of a blend and serving grade is unverified. The newly found ketogenic Parkinson report pairs a dietary-package PAS result with an interview mentioning either coconut or avocado oil. The interviews do not identify the oil exposure for the individuals contributing the estimate. |
