## 0709: 2014 celiac/parkinsonism case

Di Lazzaro et al, 2014-01-25 first online, Journal of Neurology 261:443–445, PMID24464413 DOI10.1007/s00415-014-7245-7. PubMed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24464413/ gives no abstract. Institutional partial original https://publires.unicatt.it/en/publications/dramatic-improvement-of-parkinsonian-symptoms-after-gluten-free-d-9/ reproduces opening of original: 75-year-old man, one year walking difficulty, instability and fatigability; hypomimia, bradykinesia, rigidity and postural instability; baseline UPDRS II 8 and III 19, previously no central nervous system drugs. Reproduction truncates before treatment, Table 1, and outcome; full publisher body and outcome table remain unverified. The cited title itself and 2018 review https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6115931/ describe improvement after gluten-free diet in biopsy-proven celiac disease, but a review-derived report is not a verified freezing-of-gait item or clinical estimate. No rye bread specifically, ingredient dose, gait-freezing assessment, medication course, temporal individual patient follow-up or re-challenge verified from the accessible original. Broad gluten elimination in a susceptible individual cannot identify effects of baked rye bread in Parkinson disease. A 2017 study by overlapping authors https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5425211/ compares celiac adults by gluten-free diet status for cortical excitability; it is not a documented follow-up of this patient or a freezing result. Author institution 2014 bibliography confirms citation only: https://www.unicampus.it/storage/45f949c3/UCBM_Annuario_Ricerca_2014.pdf .

## 0637: official terminated randomized trial, distinct 2019 observational study

Official DRKS00000268, registered 2010-01-19 https://drks.de/search/en/trial/DRKS00000268 , randomized 8-week Granu Fink femina versus placebo for female overactive bladder, three-day diary of urination, urgency and incontinence. Neurological illness excluded. Primary sponsor summary https://portal.dimdi.de/data/ctr/O-4035599-2-1-553048-20130403113852.pdf is saved as femina-sponsor-report-2012.pdf; report signed 2012-06-29, earliest public posting date unverified (URL suggests 2013 but does not establish it). Planned 100 subjects; only 7 active and 2 placebo treated; stopped due to slow recruitment, planned statistical analyses and efficacy results expressly not performed/presented. No adverse drug reactions observed in those nine, not a valid efficacy direction. Each oral capsule contained pumpkin seed oil 227.3 mg, aqueous fragrant sumac bark extract 56 mg, aqueous hop cone extract 18 mg, three capsules per day, with placebo. Botanical names in the sponsor report use Gewuerzsumach bark rather than Latin; 2019 product paper identifies same product bark as Rhus aromatica. Bark is not Rhus coriaria fruit culinary spice; no constituent-specific inference.

Gauruder-Burmester et al, 2019-07-01 online, Planta Med 85:1044–1053 PMID31261419 DOI10.1055/a-0946-2280, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31261419/ , independently describes a 12-week prospective noninterventional, uncontrolled same product study of 117 women. Daytime urination frequency improved in 77/99 patients by week 12; 0.9 to 0.4 mean leakages and 2.0 to 1.4 pads reported, but not an isolated urgency episode contrast. Same branded product does not make this a report of the 2010 randomized 9-patient cohort: design, timing, enrollment and duration differ. Lack of comparator and combined botanical species/form constrain causal and ingredient attribution.

Rhus coriaria (culinary sumac) fruit search retrieved laboratory work against uropathogenic bacteria (https://www.bibliomed.org/?mno=21031) and a secondary traditional-use review (https://www.ijpsjournal.com/article/Sumaq%20Rhus%20Coriaria%20A%20Comprehensive%20Review%20of%20the%20Culinary%20Delight%20and%20Medicinal%20Marvel%20in%20the%20Unani%20System%20of%20Medicine); neither provides a controlled fruit-specific urinary urgency outcome. No new direct Parkinson/ground fruit result verified. Assessment: 2010 pilot pending efficacy, 2019 product paper related only, no ingredient-specific directional estimate.
