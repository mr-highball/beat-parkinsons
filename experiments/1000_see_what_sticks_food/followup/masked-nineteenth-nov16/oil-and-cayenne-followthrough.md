# P19 oil and cayenne follow-through, 2026-11-15

## Sesame oral swishing and speech difficulty, claim 0697
Shrivastava et al., DOI 10.1590/pboci.2021.132, open original publisher collection 2021-10-29: https://www.scielo.br/j/pboci/a/fkJKytSzks83kFk4zNgmRDb/?lang=en (article Methods and Table 3). Original 62 oral submucous fibrosis patients, authors state equally randomized and no dropouts; active group oral swishing of Idhayam sesame oil for 10 minutes for 3 months, control group placebo capsules; both advised to stop causative chewing habits. Product name provided but paper does not state toasted processing or ingestion. Outcome was difficulty in speech visual-analog category, not blinded listener word intelligibility and not Parkinson disease.

Table 3 actual counts present/absent/reduced, in published order: baseline sesame 29/3/0 and control 24/2/0; 30 days 23/4/5 vs 20/3/3; 60 days 16/7/9 vs 15/6/5; 90 days 11/14/7 vs 13/7/8. Sesame counts sum 32 at every interval; control sums 26 through 60 days and 28 at 90 days, inconsistent with stated 31 per group and no dropout. The source provides no valid item-level between-arm contrast or account of these denominator inconsistencies; do not calculate inferred efficacy from these table counts. Swishing and placebo capsule are not matched procedures, and the outcome and condition differ. Original extracted PDF search omitted rows but SciELO HTML renders complete table; prior note is corrected by this primary full table.

## Suzuki salivary study, claim 0702
Suzuki et al., PMID22368259, DOI 10.1088/1752-7155/6/1/017106, first online 2012-02-27. Author original abstract https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22368259/ reports 42 randomized periodontal patients, L. salivarius WB21 oil drops vs placebo, days 1 and 15, stimulated salivary flow increased in both arms. Exact flow quantities and between-arm contrast not in abstract. Soybean oil and placebo composition are not named in the accessible original abstract, and refining is unverified. Independent reviews https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10445586/ and https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/files/134898405/ERAT_manuscript.pdf identify the probiotic preparation as soybean oil; review citing Suzuki via https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Comparison-of-BOP-reduction-over-15-days-by-oral-consumption-of-WB21-containing-oil-and_fig1_221864524 describes soybean oil alone in control. These are secondary attribution, not an original Methods verification. Both arms contain vehicle according to secondary source, so no soybean oil versus no-oil causal comparison. Stimulated flow is not Parkinson clinical drooling. Primary full methods remain open: publisher https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1752-7155/6/1/017106 returned HTTP 500 via direct retrieval; OpenAlex exact DOI returns OA false with no repository full-text location (suzuki-openalex.json). No author contact.

## Ground cayenne trial NCT03859583
Official https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT03859583 HTTP 200, raw nct.json and nct.headers. First posted 2019-03-01 after actual study start 2018-05-29; last updated 2019-03-01. Official current status UNKNOWN, last verified February 2019 last known recruiting; 2019-08 completion estimate, not actual. No posted registry results and no linked registry references. Protocol estimated 30 participants and labels randomized, participant-masked crossover; experimental meal includes four teaspoons cayenne powder in omelette, control without; outcomes substrate oxidation and energy expenditure, not PD dyskinesia.

Identifier-matched original thesis by Rafia Virk, George Mason University, Summer 2019, https://www.academia.edu/117372574/The_Acute_Effects_of_Consuming_Capsaicin_rich_Foods_on_Respiratory_Quotient_and_Energy_Expenditure_in_Healthy_Adults_A_Pilot_Study , full indexed original lines 289–299 and 309–318. Thesis explicitly cites NCT03859583; 10 healthy adults 21–42, control meal first and cayenne meal second, described non-randomized crossover, contradicting registry allocation field. Meal contained 8.20 g powdered cayenne (4 tsp; stated 20.4 mg capsaicin, McCormick), scrambled eggs, toast, orange juice and butter. Mean postprandial EE change 19±8 vs 25±11 kcal/2h, mean maximum EE change 31±10 vs 39±13 kcal/2h (paired p=.025); RQ mean p=.018 but the paired baseline-change comparison p=.484. Fixed treatment order and n=10 limit interpretation. This is a dietary exposure study and accessible results, not Parkinson disease or dyskinesia; registry status should remain as reported rather than relabel completed. Thesis title page is Summer 2019; third-party page labels 2020 and a later review cites it as 2020, so exact first-public release date unresolved. An official institutional full repository record was not located by title/author searches; do not infer first-public date from the semester.
