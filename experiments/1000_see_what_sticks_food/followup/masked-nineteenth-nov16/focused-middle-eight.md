## 0752 raw bell pepper / small handwriting
Prior primary PMID17341828 tests uncooked thawed California Wonder peppers in healthy adults for thermoregulation and autonomic responses; it does not test handwriting. Parkinson mixed-diet menus PMID30098269 contain red/green peppers, but no confirmed raw preparation or participant-specific intake and the Part II total does not identify handwriting item or writing size. PMID36501214 aggregates raw vegetables and handwriting/typing. Additional searches for bell pepper, capsicum, handwriting and micrographia identified no new isolated food-to-handwriting comparison. Neither direction established.

## 0719 cooked green peas / falls
Prior PMID30098269 supplies cooked pea-containing mixed recipes; one patient had a history of falls and midodrine changes but individual peas consumption and fall counts were not established. 2024 RCT PMID38999765/PMC11243455 studies pea protein versus whey along with exercise, muscle function rather than documented falls, not culinary peas. A contemporary pea-protein sarcopenia protocol is prospective and formulation differs. Do not convert proxies to falls direction.

## 0572 fresh tarragon / freezing
Existing PMID24074293 reports abnormal gait in acute toxicity of essential-oil injections; PMID36365307 oral isolated estragole did not report clear changes in selected gross motor measures. Neither scores Parkinson gait freezing or fresh tarragon intake. Complementary species-specific human and animal searches found no new measured fresh-leaf freezing comparison. Absence of a matching return is not evidence of no effect.

## 0557 dried celery leaf / daily OFF
Existing PMID30988766 describes oral synthetic butylphthalide and off-medication motor testing, not levodopa wearing-off diary hours; seed-derived pharmaceutical is not dried culinary leaf. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12906-018-2166-0 studies celery extracts in MPTP mice and does not measure patient OFF time. No new independent timed exposure/OFF comparison was identified.

## 0640 ground cayenne / dyskinesia
Published 2007-08-22 Morgese et al. DOI 10.1016/j.expneurol.2007.07.021, PMC2128772 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2128772/ uses levodopa-induced AIMs in 6-OHDA lesioned rats. Cannabinoid WIN reduced axial/limb/oral AIMs and the FAAH inhibitor URB597 reduced AIMs only with TRPV1 antagonist capsazepine. The original did not administer capsaicin; avoid attributing its benefit to cayenne despite that interpretation appearing in secondary literature. Lee et al. PMID16806299, first online 2006-06-30, DOI 10.1016/j.neuropharm.2006.04.016, is already catalogued for other claims: isolated capsaicin in reserpine and levodopa rats attenuated horizontal and vertical hyperactivity, but this is a movement proxy, not graded levodopa dyskinesia or oral ground cayenne. Prior 2013 PMID24140894 observes capsaicin blocked another drug's reduction of AIMs in mice; this is combination pharmacology, not standalone culinary intake. Conflicting models do not justify a culinary direction.

## 0566 ground ginger / shortened stride
New exact source PMID41568141, PMC12818266, DOI 10.1016/j.isci.2025.114489, first public 2025-12-18: oral gavage of nine-component Zhigancao decoction including Zingiber officinale rhizome/root, in MPTP mice. Table 1 lists 9 g ginger in the adult-equivalent formula; method boils, filters and concentrates the combined decoction. Figure 3L CatWalk stride length (n=6/group), visually checked fig3.jpg, shows MPTP loss relative to control and greater strides in medium and high dose mixed-decoction groups versus MPTP. No ginger-only or human comparison; cannot assign an effect to ground ginger. Existing Korean mixed-therapy cases remain confounded and prior IRCT20251028067794N1 trial of ginger powder remains pending.

Existing canonical registry NCT07882108, first posted 2026-10-20, https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT07882108 (raw ginger-pd.json): ongoing 12-week study of two ginger root extract doses in PD, RBD, and controls, no inactive control; exploratory MDS-UPDRS Parts I–IV, not a separately specified stride-length measure. Results not posted. Already catalogued for other claims, potential related pending link only.

## 0702 refined soybean oil / drooling
Suzuki et al., PMID22368259, DOI 10.1088/1752-7155/6/1/017106, first online 2012-02-27 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22368259/ randomized 42 periodontal patients to probiotic-containing oil drops or placebo for 15 days; abstract reports stimulated salivary flow increased in both groups. A secondary systematic review identifies soybean oil as the vehicle in both arms, but ingredient and refining in original full methods remain unverified. Flow is not drooling, population not PD, and oil-only versus no-oil effect cannot be isolated. Existing soy oil emulsion experiment PMID35809054 involved mouth swishing/spitting and did not estimate flow.

## 0697 toasted sesame oil / intelligibility
Shrivastava et al., 2021, DOI 10.1590/pboci.2021.132, https://revista.uepb.edu.br/PBOCI/article/download/765/450 studied 62 people with oral submucous fibrosis, randomized sesame oil oral swishing versus placebo capsules for three months. Speech-difficulty VAS category was reported improved after swishing; original full Table 3 subsequently recovered, with incompatible counts disclosed in oil-and-cayenne-followthrough.md. Swish not confirmed ingestion; toast/processing undocumented, listener intelligibility not measured, population not PD; exercise and cessation advice may confound. The direct PDF route gave HTTP 403. SciELO exposed the full original Table 3 in HTML. Existing Parkinson sesame-oil rectal combined regimen CTRI/2018/09/015592 does not supply oral edible oil or separate intelligibility.
