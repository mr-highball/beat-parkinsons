# Motor, apathy, falls and oil anxiety evidence, November 15, 2026

## Direction change

**0039, cold-pressed sesame oil worsens anxiety — Maybe False at 51.** The 2023 primary clinical abstract concludes that anxiety decreased for sesame oil compared with sunflower oil over 12 weeks in women with fatty liver disease. Sixty were randomized and 53 completed. Both oils were refined and odorless in matching companion reports. The 2025 article from the registered trial confirms unheated use. The abstract reports p = .011 without giving the contrast behind that value or the arm estimates. The matching registry provides metabolic outcomes and the link to the mood report remains inferred from design, setting and team. Acute injected oil had a different direction in rats. Oral intake and clinical anxiety make the human comparison closer to the frozen claim, with substantial limits from preparation, comparator and population. The 51 score is a qualitative evidence judgment. ## Comparisons that retain Unknown

- **0095, rolled oats and falls.** A 210-person hospital nutrition trial gave a first-fall HR of 0.41 (95% CI 0.19–0.86). Falls occurred in 10 versus 24 participants, with 16 versus 41 events. Fortified oatmeal was one hospital-menu component alongside dietary supplements, calcium, vitamin D and dietitian counseling. The comparison does not isolate rolled oats. - **0146, ground cinnamon and rigidity.** A rendered 2023 animal figure gave 21 seconds for leaf powder in hydrosol versus 26 for the rotenone model, with different statistical letters. In Lee's distinct 2026 prevention study, 23.7 versus 26.1 seconds with powder in water shared a letter and was inconclusive. Other leaf formulations had larger estimates. The January 2025 Wei clinical report randomized 60 participants and reports aggregate motor outcomes for an added formula. Its six tables are gated. These sources leave culinary bark and clinical rigidity without a separate estimate.
