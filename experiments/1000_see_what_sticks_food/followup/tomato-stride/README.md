# Tomato, lycopene, gait, and stride

The native search reconciled 175 appearances and 172 distinct source keys. Six registry records and all 232 citation endpoints were retrieved, with 11,020 citation edges before deduplication. The full Di Matteo chapter identifies freeze-dried tomato chow. Figure 2 reports less lesion-side dopamine depletion with Red Setter than with standard chow or High Carotene. The article examines striatal biochemistry. The separate step-length and same-foot stride results are in the clinical records below. The Nutri-Park paper labels its course-derived measure stride length. VIRTREAD measures maximum step length and finds inverse correlations with two circulating carotenoid peaks. Its standards were beta-carotene and lutein. PRIME explicitly specifies same-foot stride and collects dietary records during a whole-diet intervention. It has no posted results in the verified registry snapshot. Framingham models dietary lycopene against gait speed. Together these named outcomes guide follow-up, and cooked tomato and shorter stride in diagnosed Parkinson disease remain unresolved.
