## Claim 0060

The dormitory trial's student PSQI comparison remains the closest favorable whole-tea evidence. The printed sage baseline differs between the table and prose. Room allocation, unequal sugar, the registered control amount, and unavailable results for the PSQI threshold limit interpretation. No participant count for that threshold was reported. The 2026 pilot compared dried sage with active chamomile in adults with primary insomnia. Both groups' baseline ISI means fall below the stated eligibility range, and the day-14 and day-21 comparisons were inconclusive. The study describes follow-up timing inconsistently. The 2025 Thai pepper-leaf study cited by the sage paper used the same sample size and instruments but reports different investigators, settings and numeric results. Its official registration belongs to the pepper-leaf comparison. Participant overlap remains unverified. The additional older-adult randomized trial measured sleep following a combined aroma program against no intervention. The original names clary sage and musk sage as one ingredient. The abstract and Table 2 report different standardized estimates. The program does not isolate sage, and the intervention and population give no estimate for dried common sage eaten by people with Parkinson disease. Separate mixture trials plan PSQI and have no published outcome. The rodent morphine-dependence report supplies sedation context at abstract depth. The minimal lean from the student and menopause comparisons remains **Maybe False at 51** for the worsening hypothesis.
