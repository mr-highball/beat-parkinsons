## Original material and outcomes (1955-listed article)
Page 205: Japanese *Xanthoxylum piperitum* bark collected in Iwate and Fukushima, dried at room temperature away from sunlight, powdered and extracted with organic solvents to isolate toxic material. Trial observations compared bark, fruit and leaves and stated greatest activity was in bark, but the purification experiments used bark. Page 206: goldfish and frogs, bark extractions in 30%, 75%, 90% ethanol at 65 C; frog tests injected diluted emulsion intralymphatically; some exposures produced convulsions, respiratory arrest and death. On p.214 the author describes column-purified fraction as sanshotoxin, reports toxic response to intralymphatic exposure in frogs; non-toxic crystals isolated separately. These chemical/toxicological responses are neither whole-food ingestion nor measured Parkinson rigidity. Page 215 plate legend confirms bark source of the separately isolated crystal. No causal clinical direction may be inferred.

## 1956 companion II(A)
DOI 10.1292/jvms1939.18.171, J-STAGE issue 18(5):171–177, publisher issue date 1956-10-30, online 2008-02-13: https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/browse/jvms1939/18/5/_contents/-char/ja. First printed page says received 1956-12-25, again later than stated publication; exact chronology remains unresolved. `gotoh1956.pdf`, PDF pages 1–2 correspond printed pp.171–172. Prior extracted bark crude sanshotoxin was placed in the water or saline medium of fish and earthworms. Author describes earthworm initial increased activity followed by hypokinesia, akinesia and paralysis, and fish gill respiratory disturbance and convulsions. This is not mammalian rigidity or dietary intervention.

## 1960 III(B) full body joins preparation
DOI 10.1292/jvms1939.22.333, issue 22(6):333–340, 1960-12-25; already in research/1960-1964/Food-Experiment-Japanese-Pepper-Motor-Toxicology.md. `gotoh1960.pdf`, printed p.333: mice with intact brains or unilateral/bilateral decerebration, measured limb contraction, tone and movement by kymograph before and after prior crude sanshotoxin emulsion, injected intraperitoneally or applied locally to brain. Its references 1 and 2 link to the earlier preparation series; thus the crude material is bark-derived. Printed p.334 Table 1 shows no akinesia for its single intact 2.5 mg/10 g mouse, while the adjacent text and online abstract report significant akinesia at that dose. Do not collapse this contradiction. Drug-induced signs including convulsions are not a Parkinson patient comparison and no ground Sichuan culinary material was administered.
