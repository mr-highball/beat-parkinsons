## 0453 — cardamom and orthostatic symptoms

Al-Braik et al., 2011, PMID 21301765, original [Saudi Medical Journal PDF](https://smj.org.sa/content/smj/32/2/171.full.pdf), p. 174, Table 5. The visually checked table (`table5.png`, rendered from `al-braik-2011.pdf`) lists cardamom for case 27 with finger numbness. Low blood pressure (case 9), elevated blood pressure (case 17), and dizziness (case 13) are separate entries specifying multiple herbs; the table does not identify cardamom among their exposures. The survey reports 468 current herb users, 42 reporting reactions and 28 potential interactions in 26 patients. Concurrent medications, self-report and no comparator preclude attributing a blood-pressure or dizziness outcome to cardamom. No Parkinson population or orthostatic measurement was identified. This is exposure-context/exclusion evidence, not a directional claim assessment.

## 0431 — lemongrass and urinary symptoms

PMID 42980616, DOI [10.23736/S0026-4806.26.09937-4](https://doi.org/10.23736/S0026-4806.26.09937-4), first online 2026-11-04; [PubMed record and abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42980616/). The abstract describes 68 women with acute cystitis in nonrandomized groups: fosfomycin (24), standard mixed essential-oil preparation (21), and double-dose mixed preparation (23). Each capsule includes 40 mg Cymbopogon citratus oil alongside other botanical oils, not brewed leaf. Proportion reporting at most four voids/day changed from 25.0% to 70.8%, 19.0% to 85.7%, and 21.7% to 69.6% respectively. The reported p<0.001 is for within-group changes; it does not establish a between-arm contrast. Frequency is not a separate urgency measurement. No Parkinson participants or isolated lemongrass comparison are established by the abstract. The paper already has a related catalog entry in `research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Oil-Blend-Urinary-and-Texture.md`; no duplicate assessment is warranted. Full original body and urgency-specific tables were not recovered; an independent matched comparator was not verified. The DOI publisher route was inaccessible through the web retrieval; the accessible author abstract is the basis above.
