## Gafoor fruit-extract report
Already assessed in research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md. Exact record DOI 10.26463/rjps.10_2_3, Gafoor, Bhattacharjee, Mohammed Ali, Shabaraya, RGUHS Journal of Pharmaceutical Sciences 10(2), [author listing and abstract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366221659_Evaluation_of_Anti-Parkinson_Potential_of_Ficus_carica_Fruit_Extract). This listing says January 2020, while the catalog had been discussed as 2023: independent journal/first-public-date verification remains open. Abstract reports five rat groups including haloperidol, levodopa/carbidopa and oral ethanolic fruit extract 100 or 200 mg/kg, single and 14-day models; reports improved behavioral/motor coordination without identifiable numerical outcomes or rest-tremor instrument. Full text was unavailable at the listing, DOI resolve did not work in web route; exact dose control composition, raw starting material, n and methods remain open. The author's personal project listing mentions mice while the abstract says rats, so do not replace the original abstract's designation without original body.

The Singh/Verma 2025 bark phytosome report mentions fruit extract 100/200 mg/kg and catalepsy, but its reference 30 names Bagewadi and Khan 2015 Elaeocarpus ganitrus mice, not Gafoor; the source join and extrapolated numerical endpoints cannot be verified from that citation. It is not an independent fig fruit experiment. Review DOI 10.1007/s44187-024-00267-5 inconsistently labels Bhangale F. religiosa leaf extract as F. carica fruit in its table while correctly identifying F. religiosa in its narrative. Neither resolves a fruit-tremor test.
