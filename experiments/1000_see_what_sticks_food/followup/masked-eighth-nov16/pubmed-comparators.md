## Preemie Tots — canola comparator and daytime sleepiness

Boone et al. PMID35644107, PMCID PMC9516351.1, DOI 10.1016/j.earlhumdev.2022.105588, published 2022. Official NLM EFetch returned full author manuscript https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pmc&id=9516351 (saved PMC9516351-efetch.xml); Europe PMC fullTextXML returned HTTP 500 and PMC HTML returned CAPTCHA. NCT01683565 randomized 31 very preterm toddlers aged 18–38 months with autism symptoms to 90 days of fish/borage omega 3-6-9 or canola-oil placebo. Processing grade not specified. This secondary analysis was not prespecified although the sleep measures were in the protocol. Table 2, caregiver-rated Children's Sleep Habits Questionnaire daytime sleepiness: baseline 1.48 (SD .37) active vs 1.47 (.26) canola, final 1.46 (.37) active vs 1.36 (.26) canola; n baseline 15/16, final 12/15. Adjusted difference in change active minus placebo +0.06 (95% CI −0.13 to 0.25), d=.22, p=.55. This comparison is imprecise, and it compares two oils with no oil-free control, not a causal estimate for canola; toddlers, supplement delivery, unspecified refinement and caregiver sleep score differ from PD refined culinary oil. The same cohort's previously published 2018 primary results must not be counted as an independent trial. Original primary 2018 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6251698/ .

## Dretsch 2014 — corn oil ethyl ester comparator

PMID24690964, DOI 10.7205/MILMED-D-13-00395; official original abstract https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24690964/ and NLM EFetch saved PMID24690964.xml. Randomized double-blind 78 deployed adult soldiers (44 active, 34 placebo) for 60 days, active EPA+DHA 2.5 g/day versus corn-oil ethyl esters. Neurocognitive battery and psychological health indices showed no statistically significant between-group effects per abstract; it does not report a numerical attention-specific arm contrast or conventional food oil processing grade. Chemically ethyl-esterified oil is not established as refined edible corn oil. The abstract reports correlation between omega-3 index change and daytime sleepiness, not a corn-oil effect.
