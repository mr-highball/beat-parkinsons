# Claim 0290. Baked potato and swallowing difficulty severity

Retain Unknown, confidence 50. The frozen claim predicts that baked potato worsens swallowing difficulty severity in Parkinson disease. The input ledger for this review had 140 study-level rows, comprising 133 related, two inconclusive, and five pending. The high-priority original-source queue separately had two priority-four and ten priority-five exact pairs and 54 priority-six pairs. The latter remain outside this exact tranche. The eleven new body exclusions do not remove any previously assessed study or change a grade.

## Closest observations and directional judgment

- The dry-versus-moistened baked-potato caregiver account is the closest direction-bearing observation. A raw signal of greater difficulty with dry texture is limited to one person, the account supplies no repeatability or standardized outcome, and both sides are baked potato. A separate meal account combines baked potato with chili, a sneeze and coughing. The Indiana inspector confirms a diagnosed Parkinson resident ate baked potato, but the documented meal difficulty was attributed to chicken coating. No potato-specific outcome was reported. These observations leave baseline severity, exact food attribution and comparator unresolved. [Caregiver context](https://www.agingcare.com/questions/doing-more-harm-than-good-food-related-449251.htm?orderby=recent#1028342) [Official inspection, pp. 15–17](https://www.in.gov/health/reports/QAMIS/ltccr/5g6n11_2567.pdf#page=15)
- In Forsberg's one observed Parkinson case, boiled potatoes and oven-baked wedges occurred in separate, substantially different lunches. The baked wedges shared the 114 g served vegetable weight and were designed for multiple bites. The study observes eating behavior and gives no confirmed wedge ingestion amount or item-specific swallow endpoint. The 2022 thesis and 2024 article are one study. The article's age differs between Methods and Table 1. [Thesis, pp. 47–50](https://researchportal.hkr.se/ws/portalfiles/portal/45429347/PhD_Thesis_Sarah_Forsberg_2022.pdf#page=47) [Journal original](https://researchportal.hkr.se/ws/files/79817276/Sarah_Finger_Food_Meals_as_a_Means_of_Improving_Published.pdf#page=5)
- Cassani's Table 3 is a directionally compatible raw association in the frozen population. Participants already reporting swallowing disturbances reported higher intake of unspecified potatoes (67 ± 83 versus 50 ± 35 g/day; p=.002). The same group reported more purées and other easily modified foods. Existing dysphagia may change food choice, the potato cooking method is pooled, and the table compares reported intake according to existing symptoms. The fried item reports no significant group difference. This design does not identify exposure before symptom onset or isolate one preparation. [Cassani primary Table 3](https://cris.unibo.it/retrieve/handle/11585/670116/898507/CassaniE_JPARKRELDIS_2017_postprint.pdf#page=16)
- In the strongest named-potato clinical protocols, the Wisconsin team tested mashed potato and water in 12 people with Parkinson disease without dysphagia, but the pressure table pooled water only. The Pará team used milk-free mashed potato, water and cracker in 24 Parkinson patients and reported four with aspiration across the assessment, with no specific bolus attribution. The older North Carolina survey observes coughing/choking after consumption of chopped, buttered baked potato in a resident with other diagnoses. Another North Carolina case observes baked-potato flesh intake without difficulty outside Parkinson disease. These paired observational contexts provide neither a frequency estimate nor an effect in Parkinson disease. [Wisconsin abstract G3083](https://www.gastrojournal.org/article/S0016508598830576/pdf#page=2) [Pará primary](https://doi.org/10.25248/reas.e2929.2020) [Observed choking](https://info.ncdhhs.gov/dhsr/acls/star/docs/2023/20230307-110136.pdf#page=27) [Observed uneventful consumption](https://info.ncdhhs.gov/dhsr/acls/star/docs/2017/20171211-955352.pdf#page=2)
- A clinical food comparison can separate safety from efficiency. The Mataró study found lower penetration with pudding-viscosity contrast and increased some residue, and a 33-person 2025 Parkinson report found merged penetration/aspiration rates of 20/28 for water, 15/33 for vanilla pudding and 6/27 for dry biscuit, with different bolus-specific denominators. These do not establish that every drier solid worsens all swallowing outcomes. The apple-flavored saline/gum manometry study recorded higher values for some measures with thinner boluses and abnormalities for others with thicker boluses, with inconsistent mappings to self-report. PAS, choking, chewing force, residue, manometry and usual dietary texture have different meanings. [Mataró trial](https://doi.org/10.3390/brainsci10090609) [Mozzanica et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00455-025-10902-7) [Saleem et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00405-024-08774-y)

## Texture evidence and independent related-source proposals

IDDSI attaches the classification to food as served, including moisture, softness, particle size and separation of liquid. Level 6 adult pieces are no larger than 15 mm and have specified fork response. Level 7 regular and Level 7 easy to chew are different categories. Potato skin, size, foil versus uncovered baking, and gravy separation can each make a food label non-identifying. A passed texture test describes a sample; patient-level swallow safety and symptom severity require separate outcomes. The three new P5 menu or texture-paper entries were evaluated with this distinction. [IDDSI Level 6 audit tool and definitions](https://www.iddsi.org/images/Publications-Resources/AuditTools/English/audittooll6softandbitesized26jun2020.pdf)

- Ismael-Mohammed et al., first published July 2, 2026, studied food texture measurements and classification by 24 healthy participants. The boiled-potato sample failed the instrumental fork-pressure metric in Table 5 and passed the fork-cut metric (270.86 ± 33.53 g under the authors' 378 g cutoff) in Table 6. Healthy senior IDDSI ratings in Table 11 ranged from Levels 5–7. Bench methods and human perception characterize different aspects of divided foods. Neither captures a Parkinson patient swallowing an actual baked potato. Staged as related-methods candidate. The original is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. [Primary study, PMID 42392195](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13327767/)
- The April 7, 2020 sensory experiment compares five potato varieties across five cooking methods. The baked samples were foil-wrapped and oven-cooked at 218 °C. Six trained panelists rated sensory traits by consensus. Firm and creamy featured among descriptive traits across baked samples; no standardized dysphagia measure was taken. This is exact-exposure heterogeneity evidence, with no clinical claim fit. Staged as optional related-methods candidate. [Primary study, DOI 10.3390/foods9040451](https://www.mdpi.com/2304-8158/9/4/451)
- The confectionery RCT is an exact false association and supplies no new 0290 study. The two Parkinson patients were controls, fresh or baked potato was not tested, and the outcome phase assessed chewing, tongue pressure and bite force. The diet questionnaire article combined baked or mashed potato and estimated odds of Parkinson diagnosis, a different endpoint. The Korean food-frequency analysis used a steamed/grilled potato item and cross-sectional chewing status. None is a comparative swallowing treatment result. [Confectionery RCT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9588806/#tbl1) [Solanaceae study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4864980/#S2) [Korean survey](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7683204/#sec12)
