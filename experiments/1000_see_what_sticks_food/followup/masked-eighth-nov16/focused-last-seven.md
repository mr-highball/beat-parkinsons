## 0237 refined corn oil / attention
Carmichael et al. original PMID30080229 DOI10.1007/s12603-018-1028-2 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12880479/ (2018-04-11), Methods and Table 2: n10 cognitively healthy adults mean age67.3; blinded crossover 26 days per phase, 30-day washout. Placebo oil-in-water emulsion contained corn oil, matched taste/color/odor/texture to fish-oil, ginseng and tea catechin active drink; oil processing grade and quantity absent. Digit Symbol Substitution Test is stated attention/psychomotor: placebo pre 56.70±10.17, post 59.30±9.39, change +2.60±8.53, not significantly different from zero; active change +3.30±2.75; between phases not significant p>.05. No oil-free control, retest/practice plausible; no effect of corn oil itself or Parkinson estimated. Stroop placebo +4.80±7.22 vs active +6.60±8.71, likewise p>.05 between. This distinct original is a conservative related-assessment candidate with explicit causal and grade limitations.

Chang et al. 2019 attention trial https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-019-0633-0.pdf DOI10.1038/s41398-019-0633-0, NCT03542643, original Figure 1 and Methods pp3–4: comparator was 1.2 g/day soybean oil, NOT corn oil; 92 completers aged 6–18 with ADHD, EPA 1.2g/day vs soybean oil 12 weeks. CPT variability p=.041, commission errors p=.025 other direction, remaining attention measures largely inconclusive; irrelevant as corn exposure. A same-author conference abstract https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0889159118310912 explicitly names olive oil placebo and sample 105; do not assume same cohort or corn comparator. Prior corn-oil sustained-attention pediatric fMRI PMID20130094 already assessed.
