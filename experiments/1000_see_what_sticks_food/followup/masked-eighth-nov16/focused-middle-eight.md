## 0349 — raw strawberry and micrographia

Renoudet et al., 2012-06-25, PMID22846082, DOI 10.1089/jmf.2012.0060, PubMed original abstract https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22846082/: a neurologist reported apparent resolution of micrographia and several other manifestations six months after a single patient began a fisetin- and hexacosanol-rich combined diet. The abstract does not identify strawberry, its preparation, exact individual intake, comparator or independent strawberry effect. Publisher https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/jmf.2012.0060 exposes metadata and abstract but no verifiable full case text in this access route. The original body and actual food list remain pending; no raw-strawberry-specific attribution follows. Existing pending assessment is appropriate.

## 0236 — dried marjoram and walking slowness

Published randomized controlled trial PMID34455218 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34455218/ and publisher preview https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1353802021003072: 60 allocated, 51 completed; 30 days of infused 5 g dried leaves, filtered, compared with placebo in people receiving PD medications. Active arm mean total UPDRS III 18.76 ± 8.58 to 16.52 ± 7.96, within-arm p=.069; abstract says placebo scores did not improve, but gives no numeric between-arm gait-specific estimate. Filtered tea is not direct ingestion of dried herb. Individual item and placebo numerical table remain an access trail. The later same-center 2025 registered study PMID41052623/PACTR202205801626909 is already catalogued as a separate biomarker trial; its abstract lists oxidative markers and not walking, and its article/registry conduct dates conflict as documented in followup/open-trails.tsv.

## 0257 — ground mustard seed and dyskinesia

Wright 2024 DOI 10.18103/mra.v12i1.4955 https://esmed.org/MRA/mra/article/download/4955/99193547682/ Table 1: one illustrative self-rated dyskinesia value stayed 2 across weeks during multicomponent broccoli seed plus ground white mustard tea; no mustard-only control, blinded scoring, population-wide item contrast or culinary mustard exposure. The cohort was selected by baseline symptoms/adherence; no null of mustard effect follows. Existing local note research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md provides chronology and cohort-overlap uncertainty.

## 0274 — refined canola and daytime sleepiness

Phillips et al. 2018 PMID30098269, original https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.27390 and shopping/recipe supplement https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC6175383.1/MDS-33-1306-s002.pdf: assigned low-fat diet names canola oil, unspecified grade and individual use. Ketogenic versus low-fat group difference in mean change on daytime sleepiness item was -0.45 in favor of the ketogenic pattern, embedded in multi-food contrast; it cannot estimate the effect of canola or refining. Exact participant-level canola intake is unverified. The existing NCT03977558 shift-worker trial measures melatonin and BP, not named daytime sleepiness, and has no posted results in previous official snapshot. Publisher full article was inaccessible in web tool on this pass; local published note provides existing table interpretation.
