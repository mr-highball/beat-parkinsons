## Directional judgments

**0168, ground nutmeg worsens constipation severity.** In the 1975 report, stool frequency fell from 6.7 to 2.6 per day after a patient with medullary thyroid carcinoma switched from other antidiarrheal drugs to repeated high amounts of ground nutmeg. The patient had severe diarrhea. Independent animal extract experiments report reduced dry stool mass and shorter small-intestinal charcoal travel. One older aqueous-extract diarrhea experiment was inconclusive, and the 2012 charcoal report disagrees with itself about whether a challenge was used. A separate human constipation report is known through a secondary account and awaits its original letter. The weak grade rests on the verified bowel-output evidence, with substantial uncertainty about dose and transfer to Parkinson constipation. **0139, ground nigella seed worsens fatigue severity.** An open-label randomized acute-COVID trial recorded fatigue on day four in 12/30 after Nigella oil plus standard care and 25/30 with standard care alone. The baseline-adjusted OR was 0.15 (95% CI 0.04–0.48). The oil result points weakly against worsening. The more preparation-matched ground-seed trial gave mixed fatigue counts and no clear group effect. A combined Nigella and flax comparison in multiple sclerosis was also inconclusive. Animal extract work addresses performance during exercise. Oil, acute symptom presence and established Parkinson fatigue severity differ substantially.
