## Source corrections and distinctions

- The 2008 Bitar primary abstract assigns 30.97 mg/kg to carvacrol and 124 mg/kg to thymol in sunflower oil. The 2010 Herath thesis reverses those Bitar values in its literature column. Herath’s separately measured thresholds were 0.01 ppm thymol and 1.00 ppm carvacrol. The two measurements have different experimental contexts, and this catalog keeps each paper’s own values with its assay.
- Macari’s 2021 study abstract gives a general statement about no change in acceptability and includes the word thyme. The paper’s Methods and Table 2 identify the *Satureja hortensis* extract series. The 0.05% group tied the control for odor quality. The 0.10% and 0.20% groups gave lower odor-quality ratings with separate reported significance groups. This was a food-product sensory panel. It did not test treatment of the panelists’ sense of smell.
- A reservoir of potentially misleading neighboring records includes aroma chemistry of sprouts, an electronic nose experiment, a frog odorant receptor preparation, rat or pig odorant-binding protein experiments, winter and Khuzestani savory, blends described as savory and isolated carvacrol or thymol. Studies identified botanically as *S. hortensis* still had to specify preparation, route and measured outcome.
- The mucositis registry lists WHO oral ulcer assessment and Oucher pain. The reflux registry lists global reflux visual analog and frequency scales. The prior published reflux report gives no separate voice or swallowing result. The Stopcivir registration lists hospital stay, shortness of breath and oxygen saturation and no secondary outcome. The COVID-19 syrup is a multi-ingredient coexposure with no separable summer savory arm.
