## Boundaries and open primary routes

1. The archived Aoyama cohort of four people with Parkinson disease provides new PDQ-39 item-level results in this evidence packet. General speech, clarity of writing and fear of public falls stayed unchanged for all four. Selected walking items changed for some. One participant changed on an item that combines distressing dreams and hallucinations. These items measure neither voice volume nor letter size, observed falls, objectively challenged posture or witnessed dream enactment. They were assessed after oil inhalation or massage. The original did not identify the plant with an unambiguous scientific binomial in the consulted intervention methods. The 2025 Japanese composition analysis is a separate analytical source, and the March 2025 nursing report is still incomplete. [Primary speaking, writing and walking data, Table 15](https://www.mj-med-u-tokai.com/pdf/500303.pdf#page=13) · [March issue index](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=202502247924611955) · [Chemical composition article](https://doi.org/10.11425/sst.14.37)
2. The 1986 Brazilian study provides the strongest same-preparation sleep result. Its questionnaire covers dream recall and nightmares without recording enactment. The acute somnolence and polyuria complaints came from separate uncontrolled observations. The historical paper's journal body was readable in an indexed rendering although native PDF access showed a challenge. The Nigerian leaf-tea papers record 24-hour void counts, 12-hour urine volume, laboratory and seated pressure outcomes. Their identical dose groups, timing, named location and participant counts argue for likely overlap. The Thai government summary of the 64-person CKD project reports increased 24-hour volume by day 10 in the extract group, with no arm estimate printed. Neither volume nor void frequency quantifies sudden urgency.
