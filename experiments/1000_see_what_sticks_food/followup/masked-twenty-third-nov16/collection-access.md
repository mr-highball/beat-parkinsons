### 0823 — corn oil and olfactory impairment

The exact lexical candidate PMID 39113792 / PMCID PMC11303084 is Zhang et al. (2024), a mouse study of Rbm24/Notch1 signaling in Parkinson-associated olfactory dysfunction. Its full original text is locally available through the end. In Methods, tamoxifen for conditional knockout was dissolved in corn oil (20 mg/mL) and given intraperitoneally once daily for five days. This is a shared vehicle in a genetic manipulation, not dietary corn oil; the reported smell behavior is associated with Rbm24/Notch1 manipulations. No refined grade, corn-oil-only contrast, human cohort, or dietary oil exposure was reported. The match is lexical and population/outcome-adjacent, not an effect estimate for refined corn oil. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11303084/)

The separate local 2026 mouse olfactory-training result likewise used corn oil as a shared injectable vehicle in a 3-methylindole model, with buried-food-pellet latency as the olfactory behavior. It does not isolate oil. PMID 37908819 / PMCID PMC10613687 is another olfactory-method context: its human odor-identification experiment dissolves trimethylindole in corn oil for presentation in a felt-tip odor pen. This is a solvent exposure, not dietary oil; the oil's refining is unspecified. These exact queue candidates provide context around odor testing but no estimate of dietary refined corn oil's effect on olfactory impairment.

### 0898 — cooked oats and hand dexterity

The strongest close preparation result is Neelemaat et al. 2012, registered as NTR476 / ISRCTN29617677. It was a 210-person randomized trial in malnourished hospitalized adults aged at least 60. Fortified oatmeal was one item in an enriched meal package accompanied by oral supplements, calcium/vitamin D, and dietitian support. Over three months, 10 intervention participants versus 24 usual-care participants fell (16 vs 41 falls); the reported hazard ratio for first fall was 0.41 (95% CI 0.19–0.86). This is a package-level fall endpoint, not hand dexterity, and does not isolate oatmeal or establish the cooking details. The original Table 2 and trial identifiers are available in the local factual extract and primary report. [Original report](https://www.kenniscentrumondervoeding.nl/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Neelemaat_2012._JAGS.pdf)

Other nearby oat work reviewed locally includes rat oat/probiotic chow in a toxin model (no standalone oat arm or clinical manual test) and green oat extract supplements (different preparation). Search hits PMC11157553 (2024 stroke-conference abstracts) and PMID 40507038 (2025 microbiota/exercise review) are not direct dexterity evidence. No exact cooked-oat-versus-control manual dexterity estimate was located.
