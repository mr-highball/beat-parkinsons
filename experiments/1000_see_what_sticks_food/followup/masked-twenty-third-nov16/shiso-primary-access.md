## Nguyen 2024 PMID38224793: intranasal oil and social defeat

Authors Ly Thi Huong Nguyen, Nhi Phuc Khanh Nguyen, Khoa Nguyen Tran, Heung-Mook Shin and In-Jun Yang, DOI 10.1016/j.jep.2024.117775, Journal of Ethnopharmacology 324, 117775. The original author abstract identifies Perillae Folium as leaves and twigs, and describes intranasal essential oil in social-defeat mice. The anxiety-like results are increased center activity in an open-field test and open-arm activity in an elevated plus maze, while social interaction, tail suspension and forced swim are separate measures. The abstract does not disclose dose, n, group-specific means, uncertainty or statistical contrasts. The essential oil and intranasal delivery are not fresh shiso consumption or a Parkinson model. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38224793/

## Takeda 2002 and Tsuji 2008: separate measured outcomes

PMID11917505, Takeda et al., Japanese Journal of Psychopharmacology 22(1):15–22, February 2002: original author abstract reports oral aqueous leaf extract and separate rosmarinic acid experiments in mice using forced-swim immobility as an antidepressant-like screen. The abstract does not report a conditioned-fear or other anxiety-like outcome, even though anxiety appears in traditional-use background. Exact source https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11917505/ . No full paper accessed; keep its exact experimental comparisons open.

PMID12404088, Takeda et al., Psychopharmacology 164:233–235, 2002, DOI 10.1007/s00213-002-1253-5, is a distinct primary mouse experiment. Author abstract reports intraperitoneal purified rosmarinic acid 0.25–4 mg/kg caused a U-shaped reduction in conditioned-fear freezing; caffeic acid 1–8 mg/kg reduced it, without a change in spontaneous motor activity at effective doses. Freezing is a defensive rodent anxiety proxy, not a Parkinson gait-freezing outcome; isolated injected compounds are not consumed fresh leaves. Exact primary https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12404088/ ; Springer https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00213-002-1253-5 supplies abstract and subscription preview, no numeric group values or figures.

PMID18800619, Tsuji et al., Japanese-language 2008 review 28(4):159–167: its abstract explicitly reviews the above prior research and later mechanistic work; it is not an independent replicate or a primary clinical anxiety study. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18800619/ .

A separate accessible 2003 primary report by Nakazawa et al. https://bpb.pharm.or.jp/bpb/200304/b04_0474.pdf tests intraperitoneal isolated apigenin or TMCA in forced-swim immobility, not a conditioned-fear anxiety assay. Apigenin 12.5 and 25 mg/kg reduced immobility; 100 mg/kg returned to control, and TMCA did not significantly change it. Do not merge this distinct author group or depressive proxy with the 2002 conditioned-fear result.
