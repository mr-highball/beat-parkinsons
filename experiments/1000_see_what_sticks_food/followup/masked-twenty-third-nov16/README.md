## What the original evidence measures

- **0805, grated turmeric and attention.** The five-per-arm Yale result favors formulated curcumin on attention/vigilance descriptively, with p=.209. The primary article names the terminated registry and describes a different capsule regimen. The older-adult Trail A result favors a formulation, while Flanker and the separate repeated cognitive battery give inconclusive controlled differences. Acute digit vigilance is sensitive to adjustment. The closest eye-tracking result is exploratory and has a baseline imbalance. The accessible Parkinson trial results give no isolated attention response. The human direction across outcomes and preparations remains too inconsistent to grade grated turmeric as a weak lean. - **0901, raw carrot and olfaction.** The Korean survey separately models carrot frequency against rhinitis and rhinitis against self-reported olfactory impairment. The printed carrot point estimate is outside its confidence interval. The questionnaire and recall descriptions also differ. The original paper supplies no direct raw-carrot and smell estimate. - **0670, 0687 and 0671, culinary herbs and anxiety.** Boiled dill seed had favorable postpartum anxiety scores, and maternal dill extract moved offspring maze exploration in a favorable direction. The 2025 dill extract source has an inaccessible legend and an apparent inconsistency in the supplement, and its dose differs from an earlier organ study by overlapping authors. Cilantro leaf extract improved maze exploration, but Latha reports oral and injected administration in different sections. A separate human study used coriander seed capsules. The shiso oil abstract identifies nasal delivery to socially defeated mice. The separate earlier isolated-compound study used injection and measured defensive freezing. These routes, preparations and outcome settings leave the culinary Parkinson hypotheses unresolved.
