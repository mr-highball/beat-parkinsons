# 0901: Rhee et al. 2014 carrot, rhinitis and olfaction — original review 2026-11-16

Original Rhee CS, Wee JH, Ahn JC et al., "Prevalence, risk factors and comorbidities of allergic rhinitis in South Korea: The Fifth Korea National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey", American Journal of Rhinology & Allergy 28(2):e107–e114; DOI 10.2500/ajra.2014.28.4040, PMID24717946. Publisher identifies first online March 2014. Public full PDF https://sage.cnpereading.com/storage/sage/journal/article/AJR/2014/AJR_2014_28_2/unzip/10.2500_ajra.2014.28.4040.pdf, HTTP 200, 422996 bytes, retained in rhee2014.pdf; page2.png/page5.png are render checks. 2016 Japanese/Korean meeting p233 https://gakkai.co.jp/jkmeeting2016/jkjm2016_abstract-book.pdf reports overlapping survey and numbers and is not a distinct cohort.

KNHANES V-1 2010 cross-sectional population study, 2305 people >=10, three serum allergen-specific IgE assays. AR group required a positive allergen test and rhinitis symptom; healthy group lacked atopy and symptoms. Methods "Dietary Intake" p.e110: 58-food frequency questionnaire, 10-point frequency scale, groups below median vs at/above median. Figure 1 p.e108: carrot versus AR prevalence with multivariable logistic adjustment for sex, age, marital status, household size, stress, and eating-out frequency. No carrot raw/frozen/steamed form, amount, individual consumption verification, Parkinson disease cohort, or direct carrot intake–olfactory outcome model was found. Cross-sectional data cannot fix temporal order or causation.

Independent separate association in Table 3 p.e112: reported olfactory dysfunction in AR group 7.2% (SE 1.9) vs healthy group 1.8% (SE .5); adjusted AR vs healthy OR 4.88 (95% CI 1.88–12.66), adjustment as above. Olfactory loss assessed as subjective questionnaire, not objective olfactory test. It is not an estimate for carrots or a mediation analysis. Table 4 evaluates AR severity subclasses vs healthy, not carrot.

Original e111 textual numeric inconsistency is confirmed visually: frequent carrot intake association with AR printed OR 2.01, 95% CI 1.01–1.43, p=.10, while prose calls significant. A point estimate 2.01 cannot sit inside that stated interval. Figure 1 carrot plotted dot visually around 1.4 with upper confidence endpoint around 2.0, suggesting a possible transposition but exact value cannot be safely corrected without original data/erratum. Similar adjacent printed values (bread OR 2.68, CI 1.14–1.75; bean curd OR 2.82, CI 1.16–1.81) show the same issue. The public 2016 original meeting abstract repeats broad association without resolving numbers. Do not assign the carrot number, significance or olfactory direction as verified.

In paper limitations p.e112–113, authors identify self-report and recall bias; terminology says food frequency questionnaire then refers to 24-hour recall for frequency, so clarify against original KNHANES survey before stating time window. The paper's rhinitis correlation is an indirect comorbidity lead only, not defensible direct symptom effect.
