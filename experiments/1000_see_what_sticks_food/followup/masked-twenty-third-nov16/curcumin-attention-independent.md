Consulted primary bodies or original abstract as identified below. No exact fresh grated turmeric/Parkinson attention comparator recovered in this branch.

Donadio et al. 2022, PMID35556131, DOI 10.1093/jnen/nlac034, full primary https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9210322/ . Prospective, nonrandomized, supplemented 19 vs untreated 14 PD patients; 2 g/day curcumin-phospholipid product for 12 months, not food rhizome. MMSE was a screening/inclusion criterion, not a reported attention calculation subscore or longitudinal attention outcome. Table 2 NMSS total mean change -2 ± 0.6 vs +8 ± 7 (SE); table p-values are within-arm, not between-arm. NMSS is multisymptom composite; no numerical memory/attention item is supplied. Different levodopa rules and baseline differences limit comparison. The full source does not supply a Parkinson attention-specific response.

Ghodsi et al., online 2022-05-18, PMID35636380 DOI 10.1016/j.clineuro.2022.107300, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35636380/ . Original abstract: randomized idiopathic PD, 30 assigned per arm, oral 80 mg/day nanomicellar curcumin vs placebo for nine months; MDS-UPDRS and PDQ-39 totals. No mean total differences at any point; motor Part III trend p=.04 but no post-hoc time point difference. Abstract does not give Part I cognitive item, MMSE attention, or any separate attention test. Original full article/item tables not obtained on this route. Do not infer attention null from composite or absence of abstract reporting.

Santos-Parker et al. 2018 PMID29951592 DOI 10.3233/NHA-170029, full original https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6004902/ Table 3: healthy adults aged 45–74 randomized placebo n19 vs Longvida 2000 mg/day n20 for 12 weeks; NIH Flanker inhibitory control and attention mean ± SE placebo 8.3±0.1 to 8.5±0.1, curcumin 8.3±0.1 to 8.5±0.1; group-by-time p=.97 (common time p=.04). This is a directly measured attention-containing human task with no differential improvement, not evidence of worsening or proof of equivalence, and not PD or grated turmeric.

Small et al. 2018 original published PDF https://www.ajgponline.org/article/S1064748117305110/pdf Table 2 and Results; DOI 10.1016/j.jagp.2017.10.010. Forty nondemented participants randomized Theracurmin 90 mg curcumin twice daily n21 vs placebo n19 for 18 months. Secondary Trail Making A means (SD) 32.6 (9.3) to 24.9 (5.3) seconds vs 30.5 (8.3) to 28.4 (10.8); group-by-time F(1,37)=4.8, p=.04, effect size .67, direction faster with curcumin. This is an attention/psychomotor proxy, a secondary endpoint and distinct formulation, not PD/culinary use. Multiple outcome exploratory context and modest sample limit certainty.

Rainey-Smith et al. 2016 full original https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/british-journal-of-nutrition/article/curcumin-and-cognition-a-randomised-placebocontrolled-doubleblind-study-of-communitydwelling-older-adults/83419D0C8B37ACD8BBC7C27B91FBAF58 . Biocurcumax 500 mg three times daily for 12 months in cognitively healthy older adults; no between-group differences in change across reported cognitive task measures including attention, while MoCA interaction was driven by an interim placebo decline. Formulation and population differ; not proof of no effect in patients.

Adjudication: independent human formulations show favorable and no-difference attention-related outcomes; neither supports a confident graded magnitude or a directly measured food/PD effect. Donadio composite and Ghodsi abstract do not resolve attention direction. Existing favorable weak lean is not materially strengthened by this branch; the null comparison is a meaningful counterweight and zero findings must not be presented as a directional result. Open: obtain Ghodsi original item-level table or author manuscript; check any other explicitly oral PD study reporting MMSE attention/calculation separately.
