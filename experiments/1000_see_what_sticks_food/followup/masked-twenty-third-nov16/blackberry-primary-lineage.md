# 0876 Park/Magnusson blackberry cognition lineage — 2026-11-16

## Verified primary report

Poster 22, 2025 Linus Pauling Institute Diet and Optimum Health meeting, Catherine Park, Daphne Chiu, Lexa Lewis, Mahak Hosseinikia and colleagues, last author Kathy Magnusson, original https://lpiconference.org/2025-poster-abstracts/ and https://lpiconference.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/DOH-2025-poster-session-abstracts_V2.pdf p.22 (PDF page 22, zero-based p21). Twenty-nine adults at least 55, screened for age-related cognitive deficits but without dementia; 20 g acute dose, then 20 g freeze-dried blackberry powder in water twice daily 12 weeks; comparator plain water, outcome assessors blinded. NIH Toolbox fluid composite p=.04, total p=.0009, crystallized p=.02; depression score increased p=.047. The abstract does not specify the exact comparisons, numerical arm estimates, allocation method, participant blinding, group counts, multiple-testing corrections, acute outcomes, preparation cultivar or microbiome results. Notably it states no significant treatment effect on virtual Morris water maze memory and cognitive flexibility. Fluid composite includes flexibility alongside memory and processing; it is not an isolated executive-domain result, and unclear p-value contrast must not be made a proven between-arm effect. Small trial, unblinded water comparator, dried powder and non-Parkinson population limit transfer to frozen whole fruit and PD executive impairment.
