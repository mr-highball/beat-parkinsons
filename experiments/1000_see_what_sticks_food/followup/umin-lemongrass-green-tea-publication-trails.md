## UMIN000062783, aromatherapy massage for chronic Parkinson’s pain

- **Registry and history.** Both public versions returned HTTP 200 when the registry history form’s detail action was submitted with `recptno=R000065252` and `seq=1` or `seq=2`. The change list identifies the public disclosure date as the only update. The protocol names Izumi Aoyama at St. Luke’s International University, lists an open-label parallel comparison with usual care, and specifies the three-week change on the pain VAS. The secondary outcome field is blank. Eligibility names jojoba as a carrier oil and refers generically to essential oils. No species, sleep outcome, or results publication is given. The current record shows 28 enrolled and scheduled follow-up through September 30, 2026. The ministry mirror likewise gives the pain VAS and an empty secondary endpoint. - **Earlier clinical series.** Retrieved the journal PDF from the publisher’s bare hostname after the `www` hostname failed TLS validation. PDQ-39 item 30, unexpected daytime sleep, is `2/2/2/2` in person A and `0/0/0/0` in B, C and D (pre, lemongrass inhalation, jojoba massage, lemongrass massage). For nocturnal pain in Table 13, PDF p. 11, the pre-to-week-3 aroma massage scores are A `6→6`, B `8→2`, C `8→2`, D `6→6`. Nocturnal pain concerns pain severity and frequency. Item 33 combines distressing dreams and hallucinations. Neither is a measure of insomnia severity. The four-person cohort and three-condition crossover reported in 2025 are distinct from the 28-person 2026 parallel trial. - **Claim disposition.** Four stable unexpected-sleep responses offer limited indirect evidence for `0406`. The newer randomized trial still needs a public outcome report, oil identity and any separately measured sleep outcomes.

## UMIN000034209, high-theanine green tea and fatigue

- **Registry and history.** The English history list returned HTTP 200 in PowerShell. All four detail forms returned HTTP 200 when submitted to `ctr_e/ctr_view_his.cgi` with `recptno=R000039000` and `seq=1` through `4`. The original September 20, 2018 record and the March 22 and September 20, 2019 revisions retain the same double-blind four-condition crossover with 150 mL each of 4% or 2% green tea, water, and theanine water. Version 4 adds enrolled count 35 against the target 40. All four mark results unpublished. The registered fatigue outcome is 60 minutes after ingestion and includes emotion, IgA and corticosterone. The ministry mirror shows its own March 27, 2020 update date and the same crossover drinks and planned outcome. [Bilingual registry](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000039000) · [Version history](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_his_list.cgi?recptno=R000039000) · [Ministry mirror](https://rctportal.mhlw.go.jp/detail/um?trial_id=UMIN000034209)
- **Kyoto patent lead.** Paragraph 0062 of Kyoto Prefecture’s May 20, 2021 published patent attributes a report received March 25, 2019 to Eri Watanabe at Kyoto Prefectural University of Medicine and gives the exact Japanese registered title. Paragraphs 0063–0069 describe a three-arm parallel study of 105 healthy men and 200 mL tencha, caffeine water or water. Figure 8 plots work-task scores in a 54-person subset selected for similar first-eight-minute scores. Water–tencha task score comparisons at 40–48 and 50–58 minutes are labeled p = .038 and .012. Lower mental fatigue versus water appears in the prose without arm-level fatigue data, a usable effect estimate or uncertainty. The 2018 registration never listed the patent design in any of its four versions. The count, design, beverage volumes and comparators preclude using the patent as the results of the registered crossover without a verified explanation. The Japanese patent PDF SHA-256 is `4d70f8fcb8a690cb72169d671b13127f079d06db5f3eb45b36fa4183d05139c0`. A one-off visual render verified Figure 8. [Patent publication, pp. 10–11](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/e3/d8/4e/3e1403b0278c6e/JP2021073884A.pdf#page=10) · [Figure 8, p. 14](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/e3/d8/4e/3e1403b0278c6e/JP2021073884A.pdf#page=14) · [Dated patent note](../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#tencha-and-task-related-fatigue-in-a-patent-disclosure)
- **Investigator, funder and citation routes.** Searched `テアニン高含有緑茶の作業効率・疲労改善効果についての研究`, the English registry title, both registry numbers, `渡邉映理`, patent spelling `渡邊瑛理`, and the patent authors with `碾茶`, `暗算` and `カフェイン水`. J-GLOBAL lists Watanabe’s 2019–2020 tea beverage brain-function project and a 2020 high-quality matcha concentration and relaxation report. The Tea Industry Research Institute’s development sheet describes extract composition and sensory data. Kyoto Prefecture’s commissioning archive confirms later medical evaluation of matcha. No full research report with the four registered beverage arms and fatigue results surfaced from the researcher’s current university page, Kyoto Prefectural University of Medicine repository searches, or J-STAGE, CiNii and the prefectural archives. Watanabe’s 2013 paper used two beverages following a 120-minute task and is already cataloged. [J-GLOBAL researcher record](https://jglobal.jst.go.jp/detail?JGLOBAL_ID=201801003434477856) · [University faculty page](https://www.fuksi-kagk-u.ac.jp/faculty/health/watanabe-eri.html) · [Tea development sheet](https://www.pref.kyoto.jp/chaken/documents/tentyainryo.pdf) · [Kyoto commissioning record](https://www.pref.kyoto.jp/chaken/news/proposal.html) · [2013 primary article](https://doi.org/10.1625/jcam.10.9)
- **Claim disposition.** The patent’s qualitative mental-fatigue direction opposes `0948` indirectly. The population was healthy young men, the tea was a specified tencha beverage, and the endpoint was tied to a laboratory task. The 35-person crossover outcome and any association with fatigue severity in Parkinson’s remain unresolved.
