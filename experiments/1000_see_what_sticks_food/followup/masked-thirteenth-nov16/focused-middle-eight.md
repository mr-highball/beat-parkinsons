## 0446 ground culinary sumac and attention

Heuer et al. 20 October 2023, DOI 10.31989/ffhd.v13i10.1213, original https://ffhdj.com/index.php/ffhd/article/download/1213/1932/8012 and https://ffhdj.com/index.php/ffhd/article/view/1213 . Randomized double-blind n=80 healthy adults, 68 completers, oral 750 mg/day Galla Rhois extract vs cornstarch for six weeks. It is extract of insect-induced galls on Rhus javanica, not ground fruits of culinary Rhus coriaria; no PD. Table 3 adjusted ASRS scores: extract 35.14 (95% CI 33.39–36.89) vs placebo 38.73 (36.89–40.57), p=.006. Table 4 number-count accuracy and time p=.67 and .24, respectively; Tower of Hanoi time p=.03 with accuracy p=.09. Several measures, per-protocol analysis, incomplete reporting of each attention subscale; cannot infer a direction for the different plant/part food. Article abstract claims several SF-36 domains significant, but Table 5 identifies p=.033 for role emotional and p=.94 energy/fatigue, p=.30 emotional well-being, p=.42 pain: do not repeat broad abstract claim. Registry not located in paper on this pass.

## 0505 cold-pressed walnut oil and executive function

PMID29634989, Miller et al. 2018, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29634989/ is already catalogued for claims 0393 and 0655, but not 0505. Young healthy volunteers randomly received walnut-oil milkshake, cream milkshake or no added fat; fear-prediction acquisition/generalization/extinction differs from clinical executive-function impairment. First author conference original https://arph.nl/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/arph-conference-2015.pdf specifies 30 mL walnut oil or 85 mL cream; randomized study abstract describes faster extinction with walnut oil. Oil pressing grade and full primary table unresolved, no PD. Qualify as context or related with no executive-function claim, not a replacement for the already assessed cold-pressed mouse comparator.

## 0407 refined rice bran oil and apathy

Kiyokawa et al. 1995 primary https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/internalmedicine1992/34/5/34_5_380/_pdf pp.380–383: woman presented with acute lung injury/fat embolism after cosmetic injection of unidentified vegetable oil. Clinicians described consciousness as apathetic; fatty-acid profile resembled rice bran oil, not a confirmed identity. Processing, measured apathy scale, oral route and PD are absent; severe illness confounds the sign. This documents an extreme clinical exposure context, not a direction for refined food oil. Existing oral rice-bran-extract depression trial did not measure apathy.
