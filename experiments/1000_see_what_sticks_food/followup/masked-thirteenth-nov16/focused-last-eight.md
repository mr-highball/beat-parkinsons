# Focused evidence for claims 0537, 0501, 0421, 0390, 0449, 0430, 0528 and 0415

The literature expressions searched the ingredient, form and outcome before inspecting the assigned direction. These original-source follow-ups supplement the fully enumerated provider searches. No observed result supports a directional grade for the specified culinary exposure and Parkinson symptom.

## 0537 raw pomegranate arils and drooling

A pediatric whole-fruit experiment reports increased saliva during stimulation. It does not isolate arils or control for equally stimulated chewing. The 2009 extract rinse trial measures salivary biochemistry. The 1999 Parkinson study measures reduced resting saliva versus matched controls. The nanoemulsified seed oil registration is a separate preparation with no result located in its registry record. The possible 2026 program update and the 2023 conference mentions still require independent trial identity and outcomes. ## 0501 rolled oats and low vocal volume

The 2016 and 2020 clinical reports describe two distinct patients with acute dysphonia after oat milk. The 2013 poster is the first report of the 2016 participant. The clinical reports do not quantify loudness. Caustic ingestion lists oatmeal as a possible diluent and dysphonia among symptoms without a patient-specific linkage. The earlier Parkinson cohort reports aggregate symptoms with oatmeal, without separate acoustic outcomes. ## 0421 dried thyme and postural instability

A second model reports improved rotarod retention after injected thyme extract in ischemic rats. This aligns with some rodent motor tasks and adds no oral dried-leaf comparison or Parkinson balance measure. Existing related studies differ in motor task, route and model. ## 0390 dried lovage leaf and speech intelligibility

The previous food ledgers map mixed meals and composite Parkinson outcomes. The returned expressions did not identify a separately assigned Levisticum officinale leaf exposure with a speech intelligibility measure. Medicinal Ligusticum or Chuanxiong references require species verification before applying to culinary lovage. Keep the score and speech outcome separate.

## 0449 toasted nigella seed and freezing of gait

Available Nigella motor reports use seed oil or isolated constituents and global locomotor, catalepsy or exploratory motor tasks. A gait-freezing estimate requires observed or patient-reported freezing episodes. No timed or instrumented freezing endpoint was located for toasted Nigella sativa in the recorded search scope. Follow oil reports and their final registered study outcomes without assuming movement-score equivalence.

## 0430 ground sumac and bradykinesia

The donor fibroblast study supplies a Parkinson-derived model with Rhus coriaria extract. It cannot measure bradykinesia or oral exposure. The enrolled-human RCTs captured by related searches are cardiometabolic or oral uses. The Anacardium microcarpum chicken-slice source shares a plant family and a neurotoxin model with some sumac reports. It uses a different species and had no bradykinesia endpoint. [Parkinson-donor study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36361562/) · [Exact source receipt](current-original-body.tsv)

## 0528 toasted sesame oil and attention

A human sesamin-plus-astaxanthin trial reported processing and psychomotor speed differences. At 12 weeks, descriptive simple and complex attention changes did not favor the combination. The sesame presscake extract trial explicitly did not assess attention. In three rat vigilance reports, and in the Parkinson cannabinoid comparison already reviewed, sesame oil was a vehicle rather than an isolated active comparison. Isolated sesamol rat-pup results concern open field and Y-maze behavior. The original human oil preparation and attribution therefore remain unresolved. ## 0415 cold-pressed avocado oil and walking slowness

The existing cold-pressed mouse comparison uses exploration measures. Other ovarian or maternal and Huntington-model work measures movement of a different kind. Avocado-soy unsaponifiable trials use a separated combination rather than culinary cold-pressed oil. Their diagnosis-specific composite questionnaires cannot substitute for measured Parkinson walking speed. [Existing cold-pressed mouse study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35889863/)
