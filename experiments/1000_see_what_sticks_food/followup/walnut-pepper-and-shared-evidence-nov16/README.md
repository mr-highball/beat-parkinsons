## Cold-pressed walnut oil and dystonia

Ganji's cold-pressed walnut oil animal experiment reports lower neurological disease scores in induced mouse demyelination. Its scale measures weakness, paralysis and death. The mean's precise averaging window is unclear. The nine returned Europe PMC forward citations include one original walnut-oil neurological behavior study, the already cataloged rotenone fly-climbing experiment. A separate cell study measured viability and oxidative markers. None measured dystonia in people with Parkinson disease. [Mouse original and factual text](../../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Walnut-Oil-EAE.md) · [Citation results](citation-dispositions.tsv) · [Fly and cell originals](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Walnut-Oil-Replay.md)

The Tindall cardiovascular crossover compared whole walnuts with two walnut-free diets. Its menus identify flaxseed and safflower oils, with no walnut-oil treatment arm or clinical motor outcome. The original links NCT02210767. A secondary report lists NCT01235832, which is an avocado study. Four current records returned by a separate walnut-oil intervention search provide no listed dystonia outcome. One title names walnut oil while its assigned arms list whole nuts. ## Roasted bell pepper and apathy

Parkinson diet menus contained baked capsicum in both plans and did not publish an individual apathy-item comparison. An independent individualized plant-rich intervention reported apathy ratings with conflicting post-treatment comparisons and no identifiable bell-pepper consumption. A dietary crossover reported baseline apathy, and its companion interviews describe apathy as a pre-existing barrier, without published arm outcomes for the registered apathy scale. In 13 healthy men, isolated capsiate was tested for isometric muscle force and mechanical fatigue. The author abstract supplies no apathy or effort-investment measure and the institutional item provides no manuscript file. A separate cycling study reported an inconclusive comparison for willingness to invest effort. ## Cracked black pepper and pain

The original Bang article tested isolated oral piperine in inflammatory rat pain models. Higher-dose treatment increased tolerated pressure and reduced evoked vocalizations. The lower dose had little effect on the vocalization response. Corn-oil vehicle, dose, isolated exposure and an induced inflammatory model limit application to culinary cracked pepper and clinical Parkinson pain. ## Shared original sources

The shared-source review checked original article bodies and the exact food–outcome pair against each source. The pressure, neurodegeneration and diet reviews contain composition, vehicle or disease contexts with distinct intervention and symptom boundaries. The cold-pressed oil review's mustard-massage pain summary cites a mustard extract hydrogel laboratory article in its reference 72. The reference supplies no human massage comparison.
