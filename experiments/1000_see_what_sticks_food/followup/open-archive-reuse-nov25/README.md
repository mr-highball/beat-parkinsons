The four kinds of distinctions below illustrate why a source can matter without changing the result.

- **Cinnamon and smell.** Mice in a study of induced smell loss received training with four odors, including cinnamon. They found buried food faster at an interim test. The endpoint concerns recovery after the training package, with no separate oral cinnamon or Parkinson result. [Olfactory-training original](../../../../research/2015-2019/Source-Text/2019-PMC6767412-Round1-Original-Comparisons.txt)
- **Oil identity and route.** The Huperzia paper screened olive, soybean and sesame oils for solubility. Motor outcomes came from a different formulation. Two other rodent reports used sunflower oil as an injected vehicle for experiments on a decoction or marula oil. The animal outcomes do not isolate the culinary oils as interventions. [Huperzia formulation](../../../../research/2025-2029/Source-Text/2026-PMC13520518-Round1-Original-Comparisons.txt) · [Decoction](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2022-PMC9699733-Round1-Original-Comparisons.txt) · [Marula oil](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2023-PMC10729903-Round1-Original-Comparisons.txt)
- **Tea, turmeric and related compounds.** The nanoparticle review traces a black-tea extract experiment and separates curcumin formulations from ordinary ground turmeric. The rosmarinic acid review identifies separate sources on animal depression-like behavior, Melissa extract and spearmint tea for knee osteoarthritis. The study families retain their original endpoint boundaries. [Nanoparticle review](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2023-PMC10652730-Round1-Original-Comparisons.txt) · [Rosmarinic acid review](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2022-PMC8989115-Round1-Original-Comparisons.txt)
- **Parkinson mobility.** The Mobilise-D observational baseline report compared wearable gait outcomes and stratified observations by disease stage. It separates bout-dependent pace and stride outcomes from prospective falls measurement in the wider cohort. The queue's basil match is in a person's name in the publication metadata, with no herb exposure in the study design.
