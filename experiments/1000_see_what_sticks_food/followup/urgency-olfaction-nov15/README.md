# Ginger, sesame, olfaction and related outcomes, November 15, 2026

## Changes in direction

**0581, grated ginger and worsening constipation — Maybe False at 51.** Four separate controlled trials in adults with multiple sclerosis, hypothyroidism, occasional constipation or functional dyspepsia generally report symptom or stool-frequency changes favoring processed ginger. The multiple sclerosis trial reported a constipation-severity change of −24.15 points versus +11.39 on placebo (baseline-adjusted p<.001), with a baseline imbalance. Stool consistency was inconclusive in the occasional-constipation trial, and the report's extract content is inconsistent. Parkinson formula studies are mixed, and a migraine trial reported a single constipation event on extract. The official Ginger-PD study tests two active extract doses and has no posted results. The weak opposing judgment rests on repeated human symptom measurements and limited transfer to fresh grated ginger in Parkinson disease. [Earlier primary studies](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Ginger-Cinnamon-Bowel.md) · [Steamed extract and registered Parkinson study](../../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Ginger-Bowel.md)

**0404, cold-pressed sesame oil and worsening fatigue — Maybe False at 51.** The original active-comparator abstract favors sesame oil for fatigue in women following a calorie-restricted diet for fatty liver disease. It reports p=.002 in a sentence that does not define the fatigue contrast or give arm values. Same-cohort methods identify both oils as refined. The Parkinson cannabidiol report gave sesame oil to all patients and supplies no attributable oil effect despite reporting fatigue events. The weak opposing judgment depends on transfer across clinical population and oil processing.
