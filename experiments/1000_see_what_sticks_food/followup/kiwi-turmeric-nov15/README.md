# Cumin and specific motor outcomes, November 15, 2026

## 0560 moved to Maybe True

The frozen claim says ground cumin reduces constipation severity in Parkinson disease. An adult functional dyspepsia trial tested a crushed common-cumin mixture with fennel, ajwain, anise and sugar. The reference sachets contained oats, sugar and 10% of the active mixture. The adjusted constipation comparison favored the full mixture at week one (4.74 versus 7.98, p = .049). At week two the estimate remained in the same direction with greater uncertainty (4.77 versus 7.43, p = .152). The trial rated multiple symptoms and 55 of 67 randomized participants completed it. The comparator and trial allocation differ between the registry and paper, and the registry describes caraway where the paper identifies common cumin. The accessible registry does not list constipation among the prespecified symptom endpoints. These discrepancies reduce confidence. The published study does not assign cumin independently. [Original clinical trial, Methods and Table 2](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Cumin-and-Motor-Items.md#crushed-cumin-within-a-randomized-digestive-symptom-trial)

The earlier oral essential-oil extract study reported more frequent defecation and changes in stool consistency among participants with constipation-predominant IBS. That study used an uncontrolled pre/post design. A separate randomized postoperative extract study recorded first defecation about nine hours earlier, a recovery measure outside chronic Parkinson constipation. An aqueous extract increased wet stool in another small rat experiment, whereas a different extract experiment slowed intestinal charcoal passage in rats. The human comparisons give a minimal favorable lean, **Maybe True at 51**, with mixed preparations, possible context-dependent gut responses and no controlled outcome after culinary ground cumin alone in a Parkinson population.
