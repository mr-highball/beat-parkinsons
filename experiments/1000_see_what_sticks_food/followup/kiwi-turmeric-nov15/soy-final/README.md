## Chronological human oral-exposure examples

- 1993 [PMID 8231657](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8231657/): normolipidemic men ate several dietary fat mixtures including soybean oil; platelet and plasma fatty acids and thrombogenesis were measured, not levodopa or PD OFF.
- 1997 [PMID 9308326](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9308326/): healthy men consumed soybean oil then fish oil diets; blood lipids and oxidation, no PD OFF diary.
- 2003 [PMID 15011966](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15011966/): healthy men consumed diets prepared with red palm or soybean oil; serum lipids and carotenoids, no PD motor fluctuations.
- 2005 [PMID 15821181](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15821181/): elderly nursing-home residents randomized 2 g/day fish versus soy oil for heart-rate variability; no PD OFF measure or no-oil arm during supplementation.
- 2013 [PMID 23531328](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23531328/): pregnant women randomized fish oils versus soy-oil placebo for depression; no levodopa/OFF assessment.
- 2017 [PMID 28814399](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28814399/) reports soybean-oil salad dressing and micronutrient absorption; [PMID 28503188](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28503188/) studies soy-oil linoleic acid and lipid enzyme activity in healthy adults. Neither tests PD wearing off.
- 2017 [PMID 28121722](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28121722/): 272 healthy volunteers randomized EPA/DHA versus soybean-oil placebo for 18 weeks; negative affect, impulsivity and fMRI, not PD OFF. No oil-free comparator isolates soybean effect.
