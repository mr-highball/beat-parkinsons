# Brewed green tea and Parkinson fatigue publication trail

Checked November 12, 2026 for [claim 0948](../statements/0948.txt). The review followed the Chen pilot and Watanabe registration through investigator and coauthor searches, official institutional records, forward citations, and related Japanese and translated searches. The 2015 pilot remains the only located study that tested the frozen preparation and fatigue outcome in people with Parkinson’s. The pilot’s mean and median moved toward higher fatigue without a controlled estimate.

## Chen pilot and companion reports

- The April 2013 FASEB abstract describes 14 people with Parkinson’s aged 51–79, three cups of green tea daily for three months, and the same investigator team and oxidative stress findings as the 2015 paper. Its motor-subscore and MMSE results agree with the starred rows of the final Table 1. It supplies no fatigue value. The 2015 Results separately describe inconclusive total UPDRS and PDQ-39 scores. [2013 abstract](https://doi.org/10.1096/fasebj.27.1_supplement.368.1) · [2015 version of record](https://doi.org/10.4236/jbbs.2015.56020)
- The official Iowa State DSpace item exposes the full dissertation PDF and indexed plain-text bitstream through its ORIGINAL and TEXT bundles. Chapter 5 Table 1, printed p. 120, reports identical FSS means and ranges to the 2015 paper, with median values of 27 and 30.5. The fatigue row has no significance marker. ## UMIN and Kyoto leads

- Searches used both UMIN identifiers, the registered Japanese title, Eri Watanabe’s name as `渡邉映理` and `渡邊瑛理`, and tencha terms including `碾茶`, `暗算` and `カフェイン水`. The four versions of UMIN000034209 still show the 35-person four-condition crossover without posted outcomes. The 105-person patent experiment remains a separate design description with lower mental fatigue mentioned qualitatively and numeric p-values only for task performance. The new Kyoto Prefecture February 2020 meeting packet lists a tencha-development talk by patent co-inventor Kenji Haraguchi without cohort identifiers or fatigue results. ## Other human comparisons and outcome checks

- Unno et al. (2022) measured a fatigue VAS in 81 healthy adults receiving two powdered green tea formulations. The registered endpoint is sleep EEG and the published human Results and Table 6 supply no fatigue estimate. Kurosaka et al. (2025) report lower drowsiness but greater local pain or dullness from before to after the tea session in 22 healthy young men. Publisher Table S4 and the article’s Figure 4 give condition-specific observations and no between-condition change with uncertainty. The same-day condition order was fixed. [Unno study and licensed text](../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#powdered-green-tea-and-an-unreported-fatigue-measure) · [Kurosaka study and Table S4](../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Tea-Followup.md#commercial-green-tea-and-acute-task-fatigue-in-healthy-men)
- The 218-person Dalian Parkinson cohort recorded caffeinated tea, caffeinated coffee and FSS. The main tables and publisher Table S1 analyze intake and fatigue as separate predictors of survival. Table S1 supplies mortality hazard ratios by beverage intake. There is no beverage-to-fatigue estimate, separate green tea category, or identified brewing method. The data statement gives no public de-identified participant data. This check supplies no linked outcome for [0121](../statements/0121.txt). [Primary report](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12883-022-02899-5) · [Publisher Table S1](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1186%2Fs12883-022-02899-5/MediaObjects/12883_2022_2899_MOESM1_ESM.docx)
- The 2025 PPMI-Online and Fox Insight analysis estimates green tea and coffee associations with motor MDS-UPDRS Part II. Its beverage supplement also uses motor scores. The PPMI-Online form separately asks about caffeinated green tea and coffee intake and rates past-week fatigue on a 0–4 scale. Fox Insight has a caffeine exposure module and a separate 2019 fatigue survey with 1,647 responses in its Parkinson’s cohort. The instruments reviewed give no brewing method or matched sample count. Participant-level links require provider access agreements, and the retrieved reports supply no beverage-to-fatigue association.
