## Sunflower oil and swallowing

The 2020 Ludwar randomized crossover compared a cold-pressed sunflower rinse with mineral water in medication-related dry mouth. Eight of 24 participants reported easier swallowing after the oil period and two after water, p = .031. This was an unadjusted, secondary yes-or-no response. The between-treatment primary comparison for overall dryness was inconclusive at p = .067. Stimulated and unstimulated salivary flow changes both favored water, each p = .010. The oil came from roasted kernels and participants spat it out. They knew the product they used. The study supplies no objective swallowing result or Parkinson subgroup.

Separate 2018 and 2026 Parkinson surveys give a clinical bridge from dry-mouth symptoms to difficulty swallowing. In the 2026 survey, 111 people responded and swallowing difficulties were associated with worse questionnaire dryness, B = 0.41 (95% CI 0.04 to 0.78). A high-oleic sunflower comparator during head and neck cancer treatment provides occasional inability-to-swallow events as reasons for nonadherence. Those reason percentages give no severity change or arm incidence. The existing infant lipoid pneumonia cases distinguish silent aspiration events from the effect of oil on swallowing severity.

The patient-reported crossover gives a weak lean against the frozen worsening direction through oral contact and symptom perception. Refined culinary ingestion involves a different preparation and duration of contact. Physiological swallowing safety in Parkinson disease remains untested by this comparison. The 51% score is a research judgment about this hypothesis.
