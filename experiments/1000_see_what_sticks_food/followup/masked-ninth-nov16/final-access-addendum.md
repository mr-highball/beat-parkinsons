## 0293 peanut oil and smell

APOLLO final primary https://academic.oup.com/chemse/article/doi/10.1093/chemse/bjag001/8430749, PMID41555643, original protocol https://d-nb.info/1318769949/34, ISRCTN13142505. Both arms received intranasal peanut oil; active arm included vitamin A, placebo arm matched carrier, no no-oil or oral control. Protocol says two drops each nostril daily for 12 weeks; 2026 final article Table 3 gives placebo group (n15) mean TDI change +3.88 (SD 5.17), vitamin A group (n30) +2.85 (SD 7.19), adjusted difference 0.40 (95% CI −3.27 to 4.08), p=.825. This is not a randomized estimate of peanut oil effect. Neither full final article nor protocol identified refining method or manufacturer in accessible text; the base also contains small vitamin E per allergy exclusion. No defensible cold-pressed/refined designation or food-grade assumption. The existing assessment already uses registry key ISRCTN13142505, so do not duplicate results as another cohort.

## 0433 asparagus and swallowing

Rieker et al. 2004 primary German publisher record https://www.springermedizin.de/typ-i-und-typ-iv-sensibilisierung-auf-asparagus-officinalis/8023674 and indexed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15146878/ DOI10.1007/s00105-004-0717-4: 55-year-old cook with recurrent seasonal hand dermatitis reported breathlessness and swallowing difficulty after eating asparagus; prick-to-prick and patch tests showed type I and IV sensitization. Both German and English abstracts give no cooking form, dose, challenge timing or controlled rechallenge; publisher record exposes abstract, not full case body in access tried. Cooking and exact clinical timing remain unknown, and allergy cannot be generalized as ordinary-food PD treatment.

Distinct clinical texture-study lead: Okkels et al. 2018, Clinical Nutrition ESPEN 25:126–132, DOI10.1016/j.clnesp.2018.03.119; University of Copenhagen original abstract https://researchprofiles.ku.dk/en/publications/acceptance-of-texture-modified-in-between-meals-among-old-adults-/ says 30 adults ≥70 with dysphagia rated 20 texture-modified snacks for flavor and appearance. Original article table view https://www.scribd.com/document/585144873/okkels2018 lists asparagus-and-chicken soup, not standalone steamed asparagus. Outcomes are preference rather than measured swallowing change; mixed soup, no PD subgroup or ingredient contrast demonstrated. Citation and university metadata indicate 2018, NDL https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1360298764619118080 lists created 31 March, journal June; exact earliest online publication not independently verified. Treat as exposure context only.

Texture guidance from NSW Central Coast Local Health District https://www.cclhd.health.nsw.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/BestPracticeManualChapter21SwallowingandFood-TextureswithIDDSI.pdf distinguishes fibrous asparagus stalks from softer tips, but is practice guidance rather than a controlled bolus study. Searches `asparagus swallowing dysphagia bolus study texture`, `asparagus dysphagia fiberoptic randomized swallowing vegetable`, and exact German title plus cooked terms returned no qualifying measured-steamed-asparagus comparison on screened results; no exhaustive indexed count claimed.
