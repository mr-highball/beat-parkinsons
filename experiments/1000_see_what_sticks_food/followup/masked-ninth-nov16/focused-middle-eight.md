## 0326 extra-virgin olive oil and dysphagia: newly found trial family

Flood V, Vucic S, Bogaardt H, Menon P, 2017 28th International Symposium on ALS/MND original work-in-progress abstract CW-31, pp. 44–45 https://symposium.mndassociation.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/WIPS-final-141117.pdf. Study planned 45 people with ALS, randomized to laryngeal exercise alone, diet targeting 20% energy as extra-virgin olive oil alone, or combined exercise/diet, four weeks with swallowing assessment. At the 2017 presentation recruitment and assessment were underway; no outcomes reported. This is a directly named oil and dysphagia measure in ALS rather than PD, but an open trial, not a directional result.

Niven K et al., National Allied Health Conference 2019 https://nahc.com.au/a-multidisciplinary-approach-to-support-management-of-nutrition-swallowing-and-quality-of-life-in-patients-with-motor-neurone-disease/ ; independent government presentation list dating this abstract to 2019 https://www.wslhd.health.nsw.gov.au/ArticleDocuments/2556/Final_Allied%20Health%20Research%20Report%202020.pdf.aspx . Same investigators, three arms, four-week EVOO and/or exercises, preliminary n=14. Authors report combined diet/exercise had less swallowing burden/fatigue on Swal-QOL (54.0 vs 68.2, p=.017) with better communication and fewer swallowing symptoms compared with exercise or diet alone. The abstract is ambiguous about which values map to time point or group and whether all comparisons are pooled; it gives no separate diet-only effect. Combined intervention, tiny preliminary sample, imprecise comparison and ALS/PD transfer prevent attributing benefit to EVOO or drawing a confident claim direction.

Hammond R et al. 2019 oral abstract #325 https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1747-0080.12567, matching team/three arms/n=14: pooled diet-containing arms increased EVOO intake from 3.9% to 18.3% of energy; reported weight change +1.2 kg versus +0.1 kg exercise-only, p=.1. No separate swallowing outcome. These are companion reports of one trial, not independent replications. Search for official registration and full report using Flood, Vucic, Bogaardt, Niven, EVOO, exercise and ALS terms did not identify an unambiguous trial ID in this focused search. NSW current project page https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/wslhd/research/allied-health-research lists same investigators and intervention without results. Open: registration, full report, arm-level swallowing outcome, score orientation and adherence. No author contact.

Existing PMID28647435 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28647435/ cross-sectionally observed 37 versus 29 mL/day unspecified-grade olive oil among PD patients with versus without existing swallowing disturbance; this is association of reported intake with existing symptoms, not prospective or oil-grade-specific causal evidence. The new preliminary ALS exercise-plus-diet result cannot resolve this uncertainty.

## Ninth registry and PubMed source reconciliation

All are context-only for this group. In the snapshot only NCT04404166 has a posted-results flag, and it is post-stroke phone support, not food oil; protocol-level screen makes no numerical result claim.

TAPIR NCT06064266 matched primary report JanssenDuijghuijsen et al., 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.cdnut.2025.104564, PMC11908603 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11908603/ . Twenty healthy adults received an 8-day controlled diet, and day 9 high-fat shake included 95 g sunflower oil, almond milk and plant sterols. SIMBA is a 25 × 8 mm ingestible small-intestinal aspirate sampling capsule, not a drug tablet. Participants swallowed the capsules with water; outcomes concern sample retrieval, intestinal lipids and microbiota, not efficacy in dysphagia. The protocol excludes swallowing disorders. It is not a test of swallowing treatment or olive oil.

NCT07187739 allocates a Mediterranean pattern after STN stimulation in PD and encourages olive oil, but measures nutrition-related motor symptoms rather than prespecified isolated swallowing, no oil-only control and no results in snapshot. NCT07664527 rinses and gargles an EVOO-water mixture for removal of deposited inhaled medication, not oral oil swallow or dysphagia treatment. NCT05635929 and NCT07739771 are topical oil/betaine/xylitol oral-mucosal-care studies in oncology, not isolated food-grade EVOO/PD outcomes.

The completed PubMed retrieval contains 28 appearances and one No-overlap membership: PE015 rank 9 PMID33326697. pubmed-novel-screen.tsv covers that unique row. The abstract https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33326697/ concerns olive oil and other otic cerumenolytics for earwax, not swallowing or diet.

Potential independent mechanistic primary: Yahagi et al., published 2008-10-10, PMID18930115 DOI 10.1016/j.neulet.2008.10.017, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18930115/ . Human experiments used slow tube infusion of water, saline or unspecified-grade olive oil to tongue/pharynx and measured intervals of repeated voluntary swallows using submental EMG. The abstract reports pharyngeal water shortened intervals while oil and salt prolonged them; it does not provide numerical comparison, sample size or evidence of extra-virgin grade, normal ingestion or PD. This is a sensory stimulation experiment, not a clinical oil-swallowing treatment, and comparison of local stimuli cannot establish whether dietary EVOO worsens clinical dysphagia. The primary body and exact comparator/statistics remain open. Researcher grant report https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/file/KAKENHI-PROJECT-19791638/19791638seika.pdf confirms oral/pharyngeal sensory study context but has not been used for numerical inference. A related food-engineering paper PMID37316059 used vegetable puree containing 2.5% EVOO for rheology, without a patient swallowing outcome.

ALS Flood/Niven trial: repeat focused investigator, title and ACTRN searches yielded the previously staged 2017 protocol and 2019 preliminary conference reports but no verified registration ID or oil-only swallowing table. Continue to preserve this specific open route rather than infer unpublished results.
