# Peppermint ESS, spearmint, and bounded external follow-up

Checked 2026-11-15.

## Peppermint ESS original Table 2

Sinclair et al., PLOS One, 23 April 2026, PMID42024666, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0344538, https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0344538 ; original publisher-version PDF on author's institution https://knowledge.lancashire.ac.uk/id/eprint/58889/7/58889%20Sinclair%20et%20al.%20VOR.pdf Table 2 pp.8–9. NCT05561543; 40 adults with pre/stage-1 hypertension allocated peppermint oil 100 microliters/day or peppermint-flavored placebo for 20 days. ESS secondary endpoint, higher means more sleepiness. Means (SD) placebo 5.05 (2.96) baseline and 4.40 (2.84) day 20; peppermint 7.15 (3.41) baseline and 6.51 (2.69) day 20. Adjusted peppermint-minus-placebo difference +0.57, 95% CI -0.43 to +1.57, p=.257. Prespecified model adjusts baseline ESS; difference direction may not be inferred from raw post values due baseline imbalance. Non-PD; Mentha x piperita essential oil differs from M. spicata tea. It cannot establish a spearmint effect and confidence interval spans both signs.

## Spearmint sleepiness-related patent

WO2019016717A1 https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2019016717A1/en describes a small claimed 30-subject insomnia experiment, two groups of 15, 15 days, multispecies topical oil including commercially sourced Mentha spicata oil. Study says ESS measured at baseline and self-reported fresh or sleepy mornings; no group-specific ESS scores or uncertainty shown. Topical mixed formulation, not brewed spearmint or PD. Claimed sleep improvements are not an isolated spearmint result. Existing 0373 sleep-extract assessments remain closer to oral species exposure; targeted search did not recover a new oral M. spicata ESS comparison.
