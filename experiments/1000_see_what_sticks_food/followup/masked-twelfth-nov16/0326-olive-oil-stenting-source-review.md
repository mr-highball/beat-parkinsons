# 0326 — Olive oil as an adjunct to oesophageal stenting

## 2009 — Original stenting report

The September–October 2009 report by Dan Sabău and colleagues describes 142 patients treated for oesophageal stenosis from 1996 through 2008 across three Romanian surgical clinics. There were 87 malignant and 55 benign cases. Sixty of the malignant cases received the prosthesis. The primary Results section reports that postoperative feeding progressed from liquids to semi-solids and solids and began 8–12 hours after intervention. The published text gives the swallowing and refeeding context without identifying olive oil as a regimen ingredient. This historical cohort period precedes the dated 2015–2020 cohort below. [Publisher original text](https://www.revistachirurgia.ro/endoprotezarea-esofagiana-laparogastroscopica-prin-foraj-transtumoral-in-neoplasmele-esofagiene-si-esogastrice-depasite/) · [NLM PMID 19943552](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19943552/)

## 2018 — Technical report with postoperative olive-oil instructions

The January–February 2018 issue of Chirurgia published the technical report by Alexandru-Dan Sabău and colleagues, 113(1):137–143. It describes 162 cases with oesophageal neoplasm. On printed p. 141, the team describes recommending a teaspoon of grade-unspecified olive oil before meals plus a carbonated drink after meals to help keep the prosthesis clear. It cites the team's 2017 Romanian textbook chapter, pp. 264–274, for the recommendation. The chapter body remains unavailable. Oil uptake per patient, period of recruitment and oil-specific outcomes are unreported in the 2018 technical report. The paper gives received 30 December 2017 and accepted 2 February 2018. The March 5 Crossref deposit date is not an independently verified first public publication date. [Original journal PDF, printed p. 141](https://www.revistachirurgia.ro/pdfs/2018-1-137.pdf#page=5) · [Journal issue](https://www.revistachirurgia.ro/archive/2018/issue-1) · [NLM PMID 29509540](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29509540/)

## 2023-03-09 — Retrospective cohort with Ogilvie swallowing grades

Alexandra Delia Lupu-Petria and colleagues analyzed 63 of 72 patients with advanced oesophageal carcinoma presenting at Surgery II, Sibiu Emergency County Hospital, January 2015–December 2020. All 63 analyzed patients underwent oesophageal stenting. Section 2.4 describes a spoonful of grade-unspecified olive oil before meals and carbonated cola after meals when switching to a semi-solid diet. Table 1 records pre-stent Ogilvie grades 3 in 25 and 4 in 38, with post-stent grades 0 in 51 and 1 in 12. The abstract's 16.6% postoperative minimal dysphagia corresponds to 12 divided by the initial 72, whereas Table 1 uses the 63 analyzed patients (19.0%). The comparison is before versus after stenting, with bundled postoperative management and no oil-free comparator, grade-specific oil identification or Parkinson group. The recovery cannot be assigned to olive oil. The source is CC BY 4.0. [Publisher original](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9032/11/6/815) · [Original journal PDF copy, Methods and Table 1](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/f7a3/07fcf9c49f3cbf3459cf568ee10154594a69.pdf) · [NLM PMID 36981472](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36981472/)

The 2018 and 2023 papers share authors, technique and a postoperative rationale. The 2018 report does not provide a recruitment interval that can establish disjointness from the 2023 retrospective cohort, and the specific postoperative quantities differ. Use the 2018 report for technique provenance and one related study-family assessment keyed to the more informative 2023 cohort. This preserves the real oral exposure and measured dysphagia while keeping 0326 Unknown. The 2025 same-team comparison of stenting procedures is a separate source trail. The final original PDF does not mention olive oil, and it does not strengthen an oil effect. [Original 2025 comparison](https://www.chsjournal.org/CHSJ/papers/CHSJ.51.02.05.pdf)
