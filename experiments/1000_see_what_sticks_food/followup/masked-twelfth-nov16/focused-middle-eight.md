## 0437 boiled shrimp, speech intelligibility

Previously assessed 2025 shrimp scampi case (SHM abstract 0469) and 2019 angiostrongyliasis series. Official Danish infectious disease investigation https://en.ssi.dk/news/epi-news/2021/no-45---2021 describes one botulism patient with dysarthria and dysphagia following a shared meal of fish, shrimp and tuna mousse; shrimp cooking method is unresolved. ## 0386 dried rosemary, dream enactment

Bloomer et al. published 12 Oct 2016, DOI 10.4236/health.2016.813132, original University of Memphis PDF https://www.memphis.edu/nutraceutical/pdfs/7-2016-health-clock.pdf pp.1309–1315. Randomized double blind n=16 per group, CLOCK proprietary blend of rosemary and daylily vs rice bran capsules, 2 weeks, 2 week washout, 2 weeks at double dose. Bad dreams were one of several alternative eligibility criteria, not an independently reported outcome; Leeds sleep scales and self-assessed sleep quality showed no significant condition-by-time effect. No individual dream-event or REM behavior count, no rosemary-only comparison, no PD. No directional inference for 0386. Herb may be extract, not established dried culinary leaf. Ingredient supplier co-authors.

## 0400 ground cumin, urgency

Anjum et al. 13 Nov 2023, DOI 10.1002/cbdv.202301268, PMID37843082 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37843082/ : female rats with cyclophosphamide-induced cystitis; aqueous ethanol Cuminum cyminum seed extract 250/500 mg/kg reduced reported induced nociceptive behavior and bladder inflammation. Extract applied to isolated bladder strips at 3 mg/mL reduced carbachol-induced tone. The abstract does not establish route of administered extract, voiding frequency, subjective urgency, or human/PD. Original Wiley full text route https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/cbdv.202301268 remains to be checked for exact methods and quantitative cystometry. This is a related bladder mechanism, not human urgency or a ground-food efficacy finding.

## 0371 ground cinnamon, drooling

Already catalogued under 0375, Karami Nogourani et al. 11 Mar 2012 DOI 10.1155/2012/569327 PMID22505903, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22505903/ . Fifteen healthy students chewed five flavored gums and collected saliva. Original table https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=6b9320a9b355802c186a6e5d61ff092aa8ece1fb&repid=rep1&type=pdf reports cinnamon-flavored gum 0.79 (SD .33) before and 3.76 (1.51) mL/min at 3–6 minutes. Every flavor increased saliva; no unflavored chewing control; flavoring identity and ground cinnamon ingestion unverified. Saliva collection is neither oral escape nor a drooling scale; no PD. Reuse existing annotation with new flavor-specific sentence if desired, no claim-direction inference.
