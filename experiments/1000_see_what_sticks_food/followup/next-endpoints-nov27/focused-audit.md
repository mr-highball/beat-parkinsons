# Focused original contexts

## 0121 brewed coffee and fatigue

The original single-centre cross-sectional cohort's Table 2 reports FACIT fatigue 30.1 ± 10.2 among 67 habitual black-coffee users and 29.2 ± 11.6 among 19 nonusers, p=.745. Preparation and treatment assignment are unverified. The dialysis comparison does not establish equivalence or a Parkinson effect. The cohort already appears in the assessment ledger. The new exact catalog record exposes its identifiers and source depth. [Primary study](../../../../research/2010-2014/Source-Text/2014-PMID24895603-Black-Coffee-and-Dialysis-Fatigue.txt)

## 0324 refined corn oil and shortened stride

The original NUTRIMAL trial's adjusted gait-speed contrasts were +0.03 m/s (95% CI −0.02 to +0.08) and +0.04 (−0.01 to +0.09). The control oil is a mixture. Gait speed does not measure isolated stride length. The trial supplement lists 2 g of mixed 50:50 soy and corn oil with maltodextrin while the registry lists 3 g. The 10-week COPD comparison reports no accessible arm-specific gait values. No refined oil or isolated stride outcome was verified. The corn-oil and maltodextrin control had a within-arm +0.12 m/s gait-speed difference (95% CI 0.01 to 0.23) during rehabilitation. This does not establish an isolated oil or stride-length effect. Two rows for the same COPD publication were consolidated into one assessment and one scorecard family. ## 0487 refined grapeseed oil and insomnia

The 2020 dissertation and 2022 paper belong to the same randomized NCT02841618 comparison. The dissertation's Table 10 lists 10.1 g grapeseed oil per cookie compared with high-oleic safflower oil. Oil refining is unspecified and the source reports no sleep result. The different cookie registrations supply no reported insomnia estimate. The separate proanthocyanidin-extract study's original comparator insomnia table remains unavailable. ## 0203 dried parsley and pain

The 1804 journal refers to garden parsley roots for a historical pain indication. The exposure, population and outcome cannot establish a dried-parsley Parkinson effect.
