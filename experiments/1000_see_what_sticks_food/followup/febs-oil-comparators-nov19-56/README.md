# Corn and olive oil comparator follow-through

The [COPD publisher supplement](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2024-PMID39181037-COPD-Corn-Soy-Capsule-Comparison.txt) identifies the placebo as an equal-parts soybean and corn oil mixture. Its two depression contrasts have opposite point estimates and confidence intervals spanning zero. The registered trial reports multiple functional measures. The main paper's group-level gait and balance tables remain behind an access gate.

The [NUTRIMAL original trial](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2021-PMID33871558-NUTRIMAL-Gait-Balance-and-Mixed-Oil.txt) reports gait speed and standing balance in older adults. Its walking-speed differences relative to control were +0.03 m/s (95% CI −0.02 to 0.08) with protein and +0.04 m/s (−0.01 to 0.09) with protein and fish oil. Both the control and protein-only drinks contained high-oleic sunflower and corn oil. The [individual-response analysis](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2022-PMID35191215-NUTRIMAL-Individual-Response.txt) and [oxylipin paper](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2023-PMID36645979-NUTRIMAL-Oxylipins-and-Mobility.txt) do not add independent trials.

Claim **0546, refined corn oil and Parkinson falls, has a checked version 1 score of Unknown at 50**. ENRGISE recorded one serious fall in each of its corn-oil-only placebo and fish-oil-only groups, 1/71 and 1/109. In the Alzheimer trial, the pooled corn-or-soy comparator reported falls in 33/164 versus 42/238 with DHA. Those numerators represent people with reported falls and supply no recurrent Parkinson fall rate. The NUTRIMAL and COPD trials add functional comparisons or test descriptions under different mixed-oil interventions. The [corn oil laboratory papers](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2022-PMID36916049-Tahmasebi-Corn-Oil-Solvent.txt) and [olive oil liver and kidney reports](../../../../research/2015-2019/Source-Text/2015-Yilmaz-Ozden-Amaranthus-Olive-Oil-Hepatic-Vehicle.txt) use injected carriers and do not identify culinary oil effects. Their author and design overlap leaves some cohort links uncertain. The newer [constipation review](../../../../research/2025-2029/Source-Text/2026-PMID42459041-Neurodegenerative-Constipation-Ginger-Review.txt) and its [previously archived supplement](../../../../research/2025-2029/Source-Text/2026-Park-Constipation-Review-Supplement-Foshati-Extract.txt) point to the already assessed multiple sclerosis ginger-tablet trial. The original now has an abstract-context decision. The complete review remains an access lead. The broader PubMed falls expression returned 30 records. All 30 were screened. The [Smith trial](../../../../research/2015-2019/Source-Text/2015-PMID25994567-Smith-Corn-Oil-Muscle-Comparison.txt) reports muscle outcomes and an adverse-event table without a fall row. The [Weber study](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2021-PMID34890772-Weber-Corn-Oil-Rotarod-Vehicle.txt) measures rotarod falls or slips with corn oil shared across arms. The [Pitozzi paper](../../../../research/2010-2014/Source-Text/2010-PMID20070918-Pitozzi-Olive-Oil-Light-Dark-Box.txt) reports a longer light–dark latency with high-phenol olive oil in its results and figure while its abstract describes the opposite direction. This is aging-rat evidence. These source checks leave the corn-oil fall-frequency score and the existing olive-oil verdict unchanged. [Result membership and screening](pubmed-screen.tsv).
