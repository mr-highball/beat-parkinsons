# Ground mustard seed and visual hallucinations · 0817

A 2021 self-selected Parkinson report uses a tea prepared from broccoli seeds ground with a small proportion of white mustard seed. Its visible symptom sheet includes dreaming, mood and brain fog. The four dropped low-incidence items are unidentified. No mustard-only comparison or visual hallucination frequency result is reported. A separate 2020 first-person report permits mustard or daikon seed in a general extract recipe and does not verify the actual choice for a particular medication observation. ## Original outcome selection and comparator details

Wright's 2024 self-report study allowed participants to modify symptom-sheet items and selected records with at least two motor and two nonmotor baseline symptoms. The author excluded records with reported poor preparation adherence or irregular dosing. Neither displayed table reports hallucination frequency. The earlier author dossier repeats the eight-person study and leaves its four omitted low-incidence symptoms unnamed. Exact participant overlap between the 2021 and 2024 results and the original raw questionnaires remain open. A separate mixed Parkinson diet listed generic mustard without individual consumption or a ground-seed comparison. NCT05084365 concerns sulforaphane and specifies neither mustard in the formulation nor a hallucination result. The current record has unknown status and no posted results, and lists Parkinson in an inclusion line and in a general neurologic exclusion list. The 2024 clinical formulation study in people with suppressed HIV used mustard seed powder in both arms. Its results cannot isolate a mustard-specific effect.
