# Pumpkin seed oil endpoint follow-up

The November 15, 2026 UTC review focused on five pumpkin seed oil claims with no existing assessments and revisited the sleepiness and cold-pressed behavioral leads. Prior assessment and source ledgers remain part of the evidence. The frozen claims refer to consuming the specified culinary oil in people with Parkinson’s. None of the sources recovered here provides a direct comparison matching the frozen exposure and symptom.

Roasting and pressing describe different stages. Raczyk et al. independently verified cold pressing from both roasted and unroasted seeds, with different phytosterol, carotenoid, tocopherol, and oxidative stability measurements. Absence of a roasting description remains an exposure gap when an oil is reported as cold pressed.

## Claim screening

| Frozen claim | Outcome-level finding |
| --- | --- |
| 0106 · toasted oil worsens drooling | The 2017 human study measured recurrent oral ulcer counts, characteristics and healing under topical oils. Almohaimeed et al. measured salivary gland histology after oral ethanolic extract of fruit whose seeds and peel were removed. No candidate measured saliva output or clinical drooling from toasted oil. |
| 0310 · cold-pressed oil reduces dyskinesia | Hegelmaier lists pumpkin seed oil among the offered menu items in Parkinson disease. | 0330 · toasted oil worsens dream enactment frequency | Haghjoo et al. evaluated intranasal whole pumpkin with peel removed, in a sesame-oil base, in chronic insomnia and excluded Parkinson disease. ## Source access and lineage

- The original NLM diet supplement matches the publisher caption. Its plotted dotted and solid groups compare diet plus enema and diet alone: both diets could offer pumpkin oil and other oils. The attachment does not add actual ingredient intake, pressing or roasting data, or item outcomes. It closes the supplement check while leaving any separately archived data unverified.
- The original CC BY 2025 mung-bean paper identifies repeated heating during oil extraction and seven assigned groups with no oils-alone control. The wire-hanging supplement identifies group values for the combined arms. The accepted main article and supplementary data table label this a suspension outcome, a distinct measure from hand fine-motor skill. Source SHA-256 for the supplement is D389C8E6144DD98FAF4BF9A725AB706990A09596F8DDA2CDB709F82CE73F728D.
- PMID 39965508 exposure materials and Table 2 test names are visible in indexed publisher sections. The prior catalog note calls its colchicine challenge intracerebroventricular, but the recovered original abstract and indexed Methods do not verify the route. The related liver study PMID 40934545 independently specifies oral dosing with the same nominal amounts. Its route cannot backfill the brain paper and cohort overlap is unverified. Retain the prior route claim as unverified pending original experimental Methods.
- The 2026 paclitaxel and 2025 cuprizone reports were assessable at primary abstract level. Full paper methods and arm values were not recovered. Neither study was used to infer processing or effects on a clinical symptom.
- The existing 2009 rotenone paper measured tissue outcomes with commercial oral oil. Its design does not fill the dyskinesia gap. The 2021 and 2025 motor-model seed extracts cannot be treated as cold-pressed oil. Contextual evidence is represented in related assessments and unresolved hypotheses retain their Unknown status.
