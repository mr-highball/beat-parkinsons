On November 12, 2026, seven HAL title-and-abstract searches retrieved all 4,690 reported appearances across 4,459 distinct document IDs. The count includes 231 repeats across queries. HAL marked 4,301 distinct records as having an abstract. Fifteen HAL document IDs share a normalized DOI with the multilingual OpenAlex snapshot. A version or report can also match by study, author and title without sharing a DOI. The drinks query was unusually broad. Replaying its individual expressions with zero result rows showed that `thé` alone returned 4,426 records when paired with the Parkinson predicate, compared with 14 for coffee, nine for caffeine, six for tea, and three each for café and caféine. Yerba, mate, maté and matcha each returned zero in these individual probes. The single word `thé` appears to broaden the match through HAL’s text analysis. The narrower replay replaced it with French tea phrases and retrieved all 25 reported records. All 25 also appear in the original result set. The broad capture remains available for screening. The native collector writes all successful page responses to its output directory and validates the exact count, uniqueness within each query and the terminal cursor response. The query uses a page size of 50 and continues until HAL reports the end. Raw responses include the indexed abstracts where available. Keep them under the ignored build directory. The tracked TSVs contain the searchable bibliographic data and review results.

The offline mode reconstructed all 4,459 unique records with 4,301 indexed abstracts. Document ID, HAL ID, title, language and abstract availability reconciled against the retrieval table. Multiple indexed languages are retained when provided for the same abstract. An initial exact-word probe surfaced additional olfactory and animal-model reports among the broad records. Abstract availability and lexical matches are discovery measures. The priority ledger records the source checks.

The native index screen examined the title and available abstract of every record. It used the experiment terms, constituent and formulation aliases, French terms for all frozen ingredients and outcomes, and selected clinical endpoint variants in English. Matches in the title are recorded separately. Unqualified `date` and `fig` are excluded from this index pass to avoid ordinary dates and figure labels. After adding English olfaction variants, 84 records matched at least one ingredient or related term, and 1,111 matched at least one symptom term. Five records contained both terms for at least one frozen claim, giving six claim–record pairs. The contexts were a pesticide background mention, two olfactometer stimuli in one diagnostic study, the flaxseed mouse study, an incidence model using symptoms as covariates, and a country name. The per-claim table includes zero-match claims.
