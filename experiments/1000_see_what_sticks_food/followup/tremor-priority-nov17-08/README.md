# Cooked-meat and harmane evidence, with original fig reviews

A Parkinson case-control study found higher blood harmane in 113 cases than in 101 controls. The separate chart-defined tremor-predominant and gait-dominant subgroups had similar mean log blood measurements, 0.80 versus 0.72 in the source scale (p=.72). The subgroup comparison contains 35 and 13 cases and uses a motor phenotype rather than continuous rest-tremor severity. The study does not establish which foods contributed to the measured blood levels. [Original Parkinson study extract](../../../../research/2010-2014/Source-Text/2013-PMID24300779-Parkinson-Harmane-Extract.txt)

Essential-tremor studies tested whether general animal protein, meat types or cooking doneness could explain biomarker or clinical differences. One observed higher total meat intake in men only, with an inconclusive association with action-tremor scores within cases. The 2005 protein study and the independent 2020 Faroe study found no clear overall intake difference. The Faroe study found no difference after correction across 14 intake categories and no clear relationship between the biomarker and total meat. Those studies do not report baked-turkey effects on Parkinson resting tremor. Potential overlap within the earlier New York reports is recorded. Food chemistry papers report harmane in chicken and other meats, and several assays include turkey with other analytes or preparations. An authenticated quantitative baked-turkey harmane measurement and a Parkinson feeding comparison remain open. The grapefruit review followed clinical interaction and adjacent gastric-acid leads. A 1994 author abstract describes lemon juice with levodopa in ten participants with low gastric acid. It gives no numeric rest-tremor result. The distinct UMIN record studies withdrawal of pH-affecting medications, with blood levodopa as its outcome and no posted results. Earlier grapefruit-juice and cabergoline pharmacokinetics do not give a tremor comparison.
