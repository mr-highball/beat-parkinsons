# Spice screening of archive text outside the claim queries

The November 12, 2026 archive inventory contained 1,139 spice claim–PMCID pairs. Excluding the 92 exact keys already present in the first, protocol, oil, or preclinical disposition ledgers left 1,047 pairs across 434 sources. All 1,047 received a disposition. The inventory included 89 sources with unclassified titles and subjects, 60 citation trails with a body ingredient-name match, and 285 citation trails with related-only body matches. [Archive inventory](archive-body-outside-query.tsv) [Spice dispositions](archive-body-spice-dispositions.tsv)

The review grouped keys by PMCID and checked the local context around ingredient names, alternate names, or constituent matches. It prioritized the unclassified records, then citation trails with ingredient names. A native Free Pascal check reconciled every disposition key against the eligible set, rejected duplicate keys, and verified local file paths and recorded anchors. A source entry can account for multiple claim IDs. The screening uses the archived plain-text snapshot and the sources traced from it.

The screened set yielded no directly matched culinary preparation and clinical Parkinson symptom comparison. The new human contextual records were a saffron-extract combination chart review and a case that listed cassia as one component of routine treatment during multimodal rehabilitation. The new preclinical notes retain both positive model findings and null or unfavorable comparisons, including isolated zingerone and eugenol given after a lesion and isolated capsaicin used as a receptor probe. Some studies were available only as primary abstracts.
