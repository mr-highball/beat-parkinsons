# Avicenna cassia and smell: source trail, 2026-11-15

Sinaei and colleagues, Current Pharmaceutical Design 2017;23:3315–3321, PMID28302020, DOI 10.2174/1381612823666170316122949, list Cinnamomum cassia among plants discussed for smell loss in the Canon. The [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28302020/) does not identify the original section, preparation, route, dose or patient outcome. The publisher offers the body for purchase; the [author page](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315309836_Olfactory_Loss_Management_in_View_of_Avicenna_Focus_on_Neuroprotective_Plants) exposes an abstract, with no full text verified.

The [Arabic transcription of the Canon, Book III, Part 1](https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/القانون_في_الطب_-_الكتاب_الثالث_-_الجزء_الأول) contains Art V on the nose, Treatise I, chapter فصل في آفة الشمّ on smell impairment. I read the displayed chapter from its heading through the following epistaxis chapter. It discusses loss, weakness and alteration of smell and several historical nasal applications, and refers back to head-treatment chapters. The terms سليخة, دارصيني and قرفة do not appear in that chapter. This does not exclude a formulation in a referenced section, another edition or formulary. This is a community transcription, not a verified critical-edition facsimile.

The [Hamdard critical-Arabic-based English translation of Book II](https://virtualgarden.greenspell.org/content/files/2025/01/Avicenna-Canon-Book-II.pdf), Discourse II, section II, entry 35, Salikhah, cassia bark, printed pages 269–270, names Cinnamomum cassia as its modern editorial identification. Its complete entry was read: it describes aromatic bark and preparations for digestive and urinary indications but does not explicitly name loss of smell. Those other application routes cannot be transferred to the smell claim. The translation cautions that historical botanical identifications can be uncertain.

The same translation, entry 3, Dar sini, cinnamon, Cinnamomum zeylanicum, printed pages 204–205, describes powder and tablets with wine for preservation. This is a separate entry and its preservation statement is not a smell-loss treatment. The [Arabic Book II transcription](https://amir-saleh.com/books/ka%202.htm) independently contains the cassia entry; its source edition remains unverified.

Disposition: keep the review as a contextual secondary historical lead and its exact primary passage, formulation and route pending. The located olfactory chapter and separate cassia entry do not establish their asserted join. Historical instruction is not a measured clinical Parkinson comparison or evidence of benefit or worsening after eating ground cinnamon.
