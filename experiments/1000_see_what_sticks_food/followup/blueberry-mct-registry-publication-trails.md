## Wild blueberry powder, NCT07720869

- **Registry.** ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 returned HTTP 200 with `hasResults=false`, no references module and no document section. The record gives 24 enrolled and April 18, 2025 actual completion. The protocol’s secondary gait-variability measure spans step length, width and duration during normal, fast and dual-task walking. Total UPDRS-III on and off medication is planned. [Registry JSON](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT07720869) · [Local registry text](../../../research/2025-2029/Source-Text/Trials/NCT07720869.txt)
- **Outcome lead.** The January 15, 2026 LSU announcement credits Hondzinski’s lab with observing a possible slowing of decline in walking variability associated with fall risk after daily wild blueberry intake. The investigator, institution, exposure and outcome point to the registered study. The announcement gives no trial ID, comparison, arm sizes, estimate, uncertainty, walk condition or fall counts. The April 2025 LSU feature identifies Taylor Gauss’s involvement and describes the blueberry work as a project. The investigator’s CV identifies the matching funded project and a 2014 unfunded proposal with near-identical title. The proposal supplies no evidence of a second cohort. [January 2026 announcement](https://apa.lsu.edu/chse/news/2026/hondzinski-parkinsons-award.php) · [April 2025 project feature](https://www.lsu.edu/blog/2025/04/parkinsons-hondzinski.php) · [CV, funding pp. 15–16](https://www.lsu.edu/chse/kinesiology/files/curriculum-vitae/hondzinski-jan.pdf#page=15)
- **Author and conference routes.** Searched Hondzinski, Ingram, Champagne, Calegan, Kidder, Gauss and Addison with wild blueberry, gait variability, placebo or the exact title, including institutional sites, the Pennington collaborator and indexed Movement Disorder Society, ACSM and Society for Neuroscience materials. The 2025 Baton Rouge Society for Neuroscience poster 14, also listed in Hondzinski’s CV for NASPSPA, analyzes postural sway by Parkinson motor subtype in 20 participants. The author team and several motor measures overlap, but the poster supplies neither a fruit intervention nor a trial ID. Gauss’s 2026 LSU dissertation abstract concerns ankle stretching. LSU’s direct dissertation PDF endpoint returned HTTP 403. The July 2026 Louisiana Parkinson’s Conference program lists a general nonmedical-interventions session by Hondzinski and Gauss with no results attachment in the announcement. [2025 poster 14](https://faculty.lsu.edu/sfnbr/sfnbtr_annual_symposium_program_2025.pdf) · [Gauss dissertation](https://repository.lsu.edu/gradschool_dissertations/7030/) · [2026 conference program](https://www.lsu.edu/chse/news/2026/parkinsons-conference-2026.php)

Searches by the grant number `AWD-44934-1`, intervention and exact title surfaced protocol or project records. No quantitative clinical report was identified. ## MCT crossover, ISRCTN13221188

- **Registry.** The official WHO-format single-trial XML returned HTTP 200. `results_date_completed` is `31/12/2025`. The actual enrollment, results summary, results link, results posting date, first publication date, participant flow and outcome fields are empty. The registry PDF last-edit date is December 16, 2024. The record targets 36 participants. ISRCTN’s API documentation explains that completed status is generally date-derived and can be overridden. The third intervention lists ketogenic diet alone in the formal arm description, and ketogenic diet plus MCT oil in the plain-language text. [Official XML](https://www.isrctn.com/api/trial/ISRCTN13221188/format/who) · [Official registry PDF](https://www.isrctn.com/pdf/ISRCTN13221188) · [ISRCTN API documentation](https://www.isrctn.com/editorial/retrieveFile/81786542-9920-48a0-8fce-09f8428ab843/37855)
- **Investigators’ preliminary results.** The Colorado Parkinson Foundation asset is a 68-page PowerPoint export whose metadata dates creation and modification October 8, 2024. The served asset has a version timestamp of July 14, 2025 UTC. Its first public date is unverified. PDF p. 58 specifies coconut oil or MCT oil added to the usual diet and continued on the ketogenic diet. PDF p. 60 reports preliminary improvement in daily fatigue and sleep quality, less perceived cognitive fog, and improvements in tremor and mobility during the oil phases. There are no analyzed counts, oil identities or grades by participant, fatigue scale scores, ESS scores, executive subscales, phase-specific estimates or uncertainties. The deck presents the phases in a fixed sequence on p. 58, while the formal registry specifies randomized order. Page 61 summarizes the contrast using MCT oil. The study title, investigators, duration, phases and measures align. The slide layout was checked on a one-off Poppler render.
