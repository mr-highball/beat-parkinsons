# Ginger bowel outcomes and original clinical context

## Ginger and constipation, claim 0581

Two Japanese trials with the same named ginger extract enrolled different starting bowel patterns. The May 2023 trial enrolled healthy adults with three to five bowel movements per week. The sponsor reports more bowel movements with extract in week four. A reproduced notification summary gives 6.8 versus 5.2 weekly movements, with the original tables and exact analysis still unavailable. The separate September 2023 trial enrolled healthy adults with ten to twenty weekly bowel movements and recurring loose stools. The sponsor reports fewer bowel movements with extract in week four. The two reports leave their secondary constipation-scale results unverified. They give no common-cohort comparison of these directions and no Parkinson group. Claim 0581 remains **Maybe False at 52**. [Constipation-prone trial and registration](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Ginger-Constipation-Comparators.md#registered-ginger-extract-comparison-in-constipation-prone-adults) · [Loose-stool trial and registration](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Ginger-Constipation-Comparators.md#registered-ginger-extract-comparison-in-adults-with-frequent-loose-stools)

In a 40-person trial, the accessible abstract reports lower constipation severity in older adults receiving abdominal massage with ginger and lavender oils compared with no intervention. The registry first posted after reported completion. A matching doctoral thesis belongs to the same study family. The publication preview and registry give different study start dates. This comparison leaves the contribution of either oil and the effect of oral ginger unresolved. These include moxibustion or applied ginger juice in diarrhea-predominant IBS, and an Iranian study with ginger in both arms. ## Green tea citation and clinical symptom attribution

Kanehira’s original 2011 experiment tested an isolated fermented GABA beverage during arithmetic tasks. In the nine selected participants with chronic fatigue, the 50 mg comparison favored lower POMS fatigue scores. The linked 2014 Yoto study measured brewed tea separately. Kanehira supplies no brewed tea or Parkinson fatigue comparison, and claim 0948 remains **Maybe True at 51**. [Original GABA table and citation boundary](../../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Green-Tea-Outcomes.md#fermented-isolated-gaba-beverage-in-the-yoto-fatigue-citation-trail)

Abe’s 2016 original author abstract describes one patient who attributed pain to insects he perceived during visual hallucinations. Both symptoms subsided after gabapentin. This clinical history supplies separate pain and hallucination context for claims 0502 and 0175. Their frozen food comparisons remain **Unknown at 50**.
