# Vestibular study lineage and measurement

The 2024 ARO journal item is a title index. The recovered organizer abstract book supplies the original methods and preliminary results for SU212 on PDF pages 316–317. The presentation compares rotary vestibulo-ocular reflex gain at 0.32 Hz in 13 Parkinson participants with freezing and 20 without. It gives no numerical difference or adjusted estimate. The authors describe freezing provocation and clinical items used to classify the participants. [Verified original summary](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2024-PMID38308691-ARO-SU212-Vestibular-Freezing.txt)

NCT04768647 was first posted on 2021-02-24. The current registry reports 60 actual participants, completion on 2025-10-30, and no posted summary results. Its eligibility text preserves the 64 planned participants that appear in SU212. Shared investigators, settings, objectives and planned enrollment make this a likely linked study family. The abstract does not print the registration number. The registry describes an observational cohort and separate pilot device subset. Its randomized design fields do not make the preliminary comparison of existing freezing status randomized. [Local registry](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/Trials/NCT04768647.txt)

The 92-person Michigan Romberg study observes more frequent freezing among people who failed a sensory conflict task. Its printed Table 2 exposes a mismatch with the abstract percentage for the freezing subgroup. The 138-person Korean original has inconclusive head-impulse reflex associations. These observations concern different tests and populations. The Michigan studies' cohort overlap remains unresolved. None of these studies tests fresh lemon balm.
