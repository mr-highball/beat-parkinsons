# Date preparations and pain measurement

The original blinded date-seed trial clarifies the time course of muscle discomfort in physically active participants (17 completing in each group). Table 4 reports 5.06 versus 5.82 on the 0–10 pain scale at the end of the two-week intervention (p = .070), and 3.35 versus 5.00 at 24 hours (p < .001). The date-seed powder, exercise-induced outcome and participants differ from the frozen dried-date and Parkinson pain comparison. The registered protocol and later biomarker report share IRCT20150205020965N9. They are companion publications within one trial family. The public protocol supplies the code, while a separately accessible registry record with first posting date remains open. The 2004 Zaghloul date-flesh extract and 2012 date-extract jam experiments measure rat paw swelling. The 2015 aqueous-fruit comparison measures heat-evoked tail withdrawal, with convenience allocation and inconsistent outcome statistics. The spathe study injected an inflorescence-sheath extract and measured two separate pain behaviors. Traditional Iranian descriptions concern a spathe distillate, which remains a distinct oral-use lead.
