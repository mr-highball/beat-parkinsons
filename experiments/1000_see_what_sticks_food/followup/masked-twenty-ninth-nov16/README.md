## Claim comparisons

- **0968, dried tarragon and depressive symptoms.** The original Persian report confirms that dried leaf powder was extracted before oral gavage. Rats in two extract groups had lower forced-swim immobility under restraint, with similar total maze entries. A different oral extract study in mice found changes in social behavior, with inconclusive sucrose preference. An earlier tincture report described no forced-swim activity. The preclinical comparisons have limited transfer to eating dried tarragon or to mood symptoms in people with Parkinson. [Original tarragon outcome](../../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Tarragon-Savory-Oregano.md#dried-tarragon-leaf-extract-and-immobility-during-rat-restraint)
- **0997, ground anise and falls.** The human ground-anise trial tracked general adverse symptoms during acute COVID-19 and did not give a fall count. The vitamin D trial registers falls and lists aniseed ether in its placebo. The outcome publication remains open. A rat study's time to fall during rotarod testing measures a different outcome and used isolated anethole. [Trial adverse events](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Nigella-Anise.md#adverse-events-in-the-aniseed-powder-trial) · [Falls protocol](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Anise-Falls-Registry.md)
- **0978, fresh marjoram and visual hallucinations.** The 1970 ethnobotanical report describes alleged use without a verified event. The Parkinson dried-leaf tea pilot reports a nonmotor composite. Its separate hallucination result remains open. A later rat study observed general toxicity after oral extract and supplies no perceptual measurement. [Historical report](../../../../research/1970-1974/Food-Experiment-Marjoram-Hallucination-Reports.md) · [Parkinson tea study](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Marjoram-Olive-Outcomes.md#marjoram-pilot-and-coverage-of-individual-parkinson-symptoms) · [Original toxicity study](../../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Marjoram-Hallucination-Reports.md)
- **0958, fresh basil and shortened stride.** The 2009 and 2011 publications report better inclined-beam performance in mice receiving sweet basil extract before induced ischemia. The papers disagree on the planned occlusion duration, so shared animals are unresolved. A 2018 study used post-injury extract and a neurological severity score. Fresh culinary intake and measured stride length were outside the verified outcomes. [Pretreatment report](../../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Basil-Extract-Motor-Experiments.md) · [Post-injury report](../../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Basil-Extract-Motor-Experiments.md)
