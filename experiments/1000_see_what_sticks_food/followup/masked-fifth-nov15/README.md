## Directional findings

**0171, toasted fennel seed worsens fatigue severity.** In the 2003 randomized crossover, moderate or severe menstrual fatigue was less common during both fennel oil periods than placebo. The overall fatigue comparison was inconclusive at p=.08. The 2014 capsule trial gave lower self-rated weakness with fennel than no medication. Its abstract and result table disagree about the control end point. The 2007 placebo comparison gives p=.058 without fatigue values or direction in its accessible abstract. An additional 2003 fruit-oil trial lists fatigue as a descriptive symptom and supplies no fatigue-by-treatment estimate. These findings weakly oppose worsening after allowing for preparation, menstrual symptoms and the weakness measure. The Parkinson-specific fatigue estimate and the 2007 fatigue table stay open. [2003 crossover](../../../../research/2000-2004/Food-Experiment-Fennel-Fatigue.md) · [2007 original abstract](../../../../research/2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Fennel-Fatigue.md) · [2014 original weakness table](../../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Fennel-Fatigue.md)

**0180, ground nigella seed worsens insomnia severity.** Separate trial registrations identify the seven-day actigraphy and 90-day questionnaire comparisons from the same site and team. Their proprietary thymoquinone-rich seed oil favored several sleep measures against placebo. The seven-day paper's corrigendum corrects a placebo p-value. In a different postmenopausal trial, the original describes an oily seed extract with inconsistent dose descriptions. It gives an overall PSQI comparison p=.45 and reports different nigella values in the text and Table 2. The comparator trial and a more recent oil-versus-melatonin crossover qualify how far the branded-oil findings can transfer to culinary ground seed and Parkinson insomnia.
