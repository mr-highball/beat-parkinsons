# Round 1 publication integrity and original clinical outcomes

## Corn oil retraction provenance

The journal withdrew the Azhar psychological-distress trial after the editor identified errors in its data and analysis. The official notice was published online on July 17, 2026. The report and notice now have separate exact bibliographic records. The existing Round 1 assessments for corn oil and depressive symptoms (0205), daytime sleepiness (0308), and insomnia (0726) already kept the withdrawn results out of their verdicts. The trial registration remains protocol context, with a corn-oil control and no structured results. [Withdrawn report](../../../../research/2025-2029/Source-Text/2025-PMID41461240-Azhar-Omega-3-Corn-Oil-Retracted.txt) · [Publisher notice](../../../../research/2025-2029/Source-Text/2026-PMID42469048-Azhar-Corn-Oil-Study-Retraction.txt)

For 0308, the de Almeida thesis Table 9 compares cannabidiol against a corn-oil carrier in Parkinson disease with REM sleep behavior disorder. Epworth ratings differ at baseline and show uncertain between-group comparisons over follow-up. The design provides no corn-oil-versus-no-oil comparison. The distinct Dretsch ethyl-ester report still lacks a verified original arm-level Epworth table. [Thesis and cohort material](../../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Corn-Oil-Sleep-Registry-Retraction.md)

## 0117 ground Sichuan pepper and fatigue

The original Kennedy supplement gives an inconclusive acute treatment main effect (p=.07) and repetition-specific day-56 pre-dose changes, with 39 participants per group. A lower day-56 fatigue rating on cognitive tasks still supplies a provisional easing signal for the studied standardized Nepalese pepper extract. Baseline differences, a 5 h versus 6 h post-dose label discrepancy, and the healthy-adult cohort limit transfer to ground Sichuan pepper and Parkinson fatigue. The supplement is part of the existing trial family. [Primary supplement and numerical data](../../../../research/2015-2019/Source-Text/2019-PMID31835620-Nepalese-Pepper-Fatigue-Supplement.txt)

## 0502 dried date fruit and pain

A new 2024 original survey records customary use of date fruit for joint pain in Muzaffargarh District. Table 6 includes fresh fruit or infusion and gives no measured pain response or ingredient-specific participant count. The dried preparation and Parkinson pain outcome remain open.
