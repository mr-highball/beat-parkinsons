# Original strawberry search contexts

PMID 40847152 (DOI 10.1038/s41415-025-8814-0, PMCID PMC12373497) used “strawberry-like” to describe gingival enlargement in a discussion of granulomatosis with polyangiitis and related differential diagnosis. The British Dental Journal article discusses recognition of systemic oral signs. Its visible body does not introduce strawberry intake. [Original PMC text](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC12373497.1/PMC12373497.1.txt)

The VPS35 methods and discussion contain mStrawberry-tagged fluorescent constructs as confirmed by the archived human-author manuscript body. The protein-interaction study's SBNO2 paragraph names a fly gene homolog. Those two uses carry no fruit exposure. [VPS35](../../../../research/2010-2014/Source-Text/PMC4024763.txt) · [SBNO2](../../../../research/2010-2014/Source-Text/PMC3278837.txt)

The sulfur review's plant example states strawberry and maize did not show the accumulation seen in several other infected plants under the tested conditions. This is plant tissue, not human ingestion. The lupeol article identifies strawberry among possible dietary sources, but its model tests isolated lupeol; none of the animal outcomes is a strawberry comparison. [Sulfur review](../../../../research/2010-2014/Source-Text/PMC4170951.txt) · [Lupeol article](../../../../research/2025-2029/Source-Text/PMC12907166.txt)
