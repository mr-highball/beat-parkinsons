# Rosemary, saffron and shared original-source follow-through

Reviewed November 16, 2026 UTC for the original frozen experiment.

## Rosemary and depressive symptoms

Three Europe PMC expressions returned eight appearances. Each has a preparation-specific result disposition. The exact Parkinson-and-mood expression returned zero. The ClinicalTrials.gov search retrieved 15 of 15 records over two pages and produced two closely related records. MsFLASH includes rosemary extract in both capsule arms, so the PHQ-8 contrast concerns omega-3 assignment. The dementia pilot used rosemary essential oil among multiple odors and found no clear group difference on its depression scale. The recently cataloged massage and course-allocation studies report favorable comparisons under blended or inhaled exposure. The updated review abstract reports an inconclusive pooled depression comparison with high heterogeneity. Claim 0128 is Maybe False at 51, with the lean based on earlier dried oral rosemary trials in other populations. ## Saffron and the comparator outcomes

The Affron study compared a standardized extract with placebo in women reporting low mood and poor sleep. Adjusted depression improvement favored the extract while its sleep-disturbance comparison was inconclusive. Sleep-related impairment favored the extract on a different measure. The existing source text and one trial identity now cover both endpoints. A separate publicly posted perimenopause preprint reports a favorable fatigue comparison for a different standardized extract. The body of AGR:IND609044984 remains restricted. The existing Isfahan and Tehran studies are distinct Parkinson cohorts. The Isfahan fatigue outcome first appeared in the public registration after outcome manuscript receipt, leaving its original prespecification timing unresolved. ## Original source screening

The olfaction consensus and bioactives review were checked as full, identity-matched manuscripts. The consensus uses cinnamon, clove and other ingredients in odor contexts and reports a contraindication for peanut oil as a topical additive. The source trail recovered the Choi paper and its original report of a higher total smell score after training with five odors. The diagnosis, route and combined comparison leave the ground-cinnamon Parkinson claim open. Three additional full clinical articles supplied exact boundary checks for procedure-related neck pain, apathy, aspiration-related salivation and early Parkinson sleep outcomes.
