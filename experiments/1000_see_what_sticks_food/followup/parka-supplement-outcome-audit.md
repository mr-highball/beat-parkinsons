## Parkinson cohort outcomes

- The April 2–3, 2025 AD/PD poster SHIFT 01-102 explicitly identifies NCT04322461. All ten Parkinson participants received 24 g/day D-beta-hydroxybutyrate and 1 g/day nicotinamide riboside in two doses, with moderate supervised exercise three days per week for two months. The baseline assessment followed a single supplement dose and was compared with the post-intervention visit. The authors report CANTAB attention and psychomotor speed improved 17% (p < .05), global brain ketone uptake was 1.5-fold relative to the single-dose visit, and physical performance did not change. A June 13, 2025 institutional presentation listing identifies D-beta-hydroxybutyrate, nicotinamide riboside and exercise in the title. [AD/PD primary abstract, PDF p. 45](https://info.kenes.com/Flip/ADPD25_ADPD25%20-%20Orals%20on%20Demand%20-%2027%20March/PDF.pdf#page=45) [PIVOT presentation listing, p. 85](https://www.cdrv.ca/clients/SanteEstrie/Sous-sites/Centres_de_recherche/CDRV/Recherche/Publications-scientifiques/Publications_2025.pdf#page=85)
- The June 12, 2026 PIVOT abstract explicitly identifies the same accession, ten Parkinson participants, twice-daily beta-hydroxybutyrate salts, and the same exercise schedule. It omits nicotinamide riboside and gives no product name. It reports 16% attention and psychomotor improvement, 2.4-fold global brain ketone uptake versus the single-dose visit (p < .001), no change in global brain glucose uptake, and 52% and 53% increases in somatomotor and limbic functional connectivity (p = .005 and .013). The abstract gives no executive function-specific CANTAB result, cognitive subtest name, or separate estimates for attention and psychomotor speed. [Primary 2026 abstract](https://doi.org/10.17118/11143/25386) [Institutional PDF](https://usherbrooke.scholaris.ca/bitstreams/122648a6-8090-41fa-936a-f234ee7408c7/download) [Institutional plain text](https://usherbrooke.scholaris.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/71066af3-3200-4483-a640-52202df66b5a/content) [Dated 2026 assessment](../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-WHO-CTG-Followup.md#parka-ketone-salts-and-brain-connectivity-at-pivot-2026)

The clinical abstracts describe the same accession and ten-person Parkinson cohort. Their estimates of uptake and CANTAB change differ. Neither supplies arm-specific data for the remaining enrollees, a controlled exercise comparison, executive-domain scores or an isolated oil intervention. The latest reporting describes ketone salts. The April 2025 abstract documents nicotinamide riboside, and the 2026 omission leaves its status in that report unspecified. The abstract's 16% attention and psychomotor change offers no estimate for refined coconut oil and executive function. The institutional item's deposit license applies to Université de Sherbrooke dissemination, and no general reuse license is listed. Local records retain original summaries with source links.
