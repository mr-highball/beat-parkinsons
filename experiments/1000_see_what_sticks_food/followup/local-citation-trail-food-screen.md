# Archived food citation trails

The November 12, 2026 screen used the 8,502-row [archived text snapshot](claim-local-text-screen.tsv). It selected frozen food rows with `citation trail` triage, an exposure or related-exposure flag and an outcome flag in archived text, and either side missing from the indexed title and abstract. This yielded 671 distinct claim–source pairs across 399 archived articles. All 399 titles were read. The native Free Pascal exposure matcher returned 1,800 exposure-containing lines for 669 pairs, including 44 lines that matched exposure and outcome terms. Reading the remaining two texts identified cauliflower-like anatomy and cherry-red retinal spots. Eighty-one sources, representing 189 pairs, received a documented body-text, primary-article, or citation check. The other 318 received title-level dispositions. The ledger retains the claim ID, PMCID, archived path, and screening depth for every pair. ## Primary evidence and preparation checks

- A source on deep brain stimulation led to Robertson and colleagues’ Parkinson study, which used raw carrot in a jaw velocity task. Participants bit each piece and expelled it. In a separate source on orofacial function, the cited sweet-potato study used the same test food in treated and untreated macaques and evaluated chewing kinematics. These are adjacent to the carrot and sweet-potato claims by ingredient. [Carrot study](../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#raw-carrot-in-a-jaw-velocity-test) · [Sweet-potato study](../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#sweet-potato-as-a-mastication-test-bolus)
- The 2005 autopsy series included one person with Parkinson’s. The publisher’s Results preview assigns the grape case to an infant. Potato chips and banana appear in the pooled list, and the food in the Parkinson case remains unverified. The later Feola case series describes other foods and conditions. The 2015 survey cited in a Parkinson dysphagia and gastroparesis review studied 45 people with idiopathic or diabetic gastroparesis. Participants reported more upper gastrointestinal symptoms with oranges and broccoli, among other foods. These reports supply no estimate for the frozen Parkinson outcomes. [Autopsy series](../../../research/2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#foods-recovered-in-a-fatal-choking-autopsy-series) · [Feola primary report](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.legalmed.2023.102339) · [Gastroparesis survey](../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#food-tolerance-in-idiopathic-and-diabetic-gastroparesis)
- A probiotic review led back to the randomized Parkinson constipation trial in which both groups received orange-flavored sachets. The active product increased weekly bowel movements by 1.37 (95% CI 0.68–2.07) relative to placebo. The contrast concerns probiotic strains and fructo-oligosaccharide in a shared flavor. Another review cited a kefir-powder trial in osteoporosis that measured bone density and turnover, with no falls outcome. [Parkinson constipation trial](../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#orange-flavor-in-a-parkinsons-constipation-trial) · [Kefir trial](../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#kefir-powder-and-bone-outcomes-in-osteoporosis)
- The archived caffeine reviews cite the six-week encapsulated caffeine trial. The early Parkinson green-tea-polyphenol trial used a concentrated preparation and did not report a fatigue or smell result in the accessible summary. A complementary Japanese search found a five-person green tea versus water experiment after an acute fatigue task. Total subjective fatigue declined in the tea condition, with no reported significant beverage-by-time interaction. A later related registration had no posted results. In a separate randomized trial of 42 healthy young adults, matcha powder did not clearly change fatigue after a stress task. An Italian Parkinson dietary survey measured coffee and tea intake, but the published contrasts by diagnosis and constipation did not estimate beverage-specific symptom effects. [Capsule fatigue trial](../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Coffee-Followup.md#encapsulated-caffeine-and-fatigue-severity) · [Parkinson tea-polyphenol trial](../../../research/2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Tea-Followup.md) · [Japanese acute trial](../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#green-tea-after-a-laboratory-fatigue-task) · [Japanese registration](../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#high-theanine-green-tea-and-fatigue-registration) · [Matcha trial](../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#matcha-and-task-related-fatigue-in-young-adults) · [Italian survey](../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#coffee-tea-and-constipation-in-an-italian-dietary-survey)
- The carnosine and palmitoylethanolamide reviews identify chicken and tomato as food sources of compounds evaluated in Parkinson’s. The recovered full carnosine pilot gives a total UPDRS comparison and item-level motor results, including inconclusive hand and leg rigidity contrasts. It gives no fatigue result. The palmitoylethanolamide series used a sublingual ultramicronized formulation and appears in a 2014 congress abstract and a separate 2017 journal report. The public 2017 abstract supplies no individual fatigue or rigidity values. A separate salmon trail led to a soft-roe nucleoprotein diet in a mouse model that measured locomotion, with no grilled salmon or human bradykinesia result. [Carnosine pilot](../../../research/2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#carnosine-supplementation-alongside-levodopa) · [2014 palmitoylethanolamide congress abstract](../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#ultramicronized-palmitoylethanolamide-in-parkinsons-disease) · [2017 journal report](../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#2017-journal-report-of-sublingual-palmitoylethanolamide) · [Salmon roe study](../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#salmon-roe-nucleoprotein-and-locomotion-in-mice)

## Scope and access

For plausible matches, the review followed references and ran complementary searches by study identifiers, authors, ingredient names, and outcomes. The green tea extension included Japanese title and registry searches. The carnosine access follow-up located the author-shared 2008 paper, 2007 proceedings metadata, and overlapping-author Russian dissertations and patent. The autopsy access follow-up located the Adelaide repository record, which has no public article attachment, and the publisher’s Results preview. The publisher PDF and full case-level results remain unavailable. No newly verified primary result isolated a frozen food, culinary preparation, and symptom outcome in people with Parkinson’s. The previously cataloged espresso result remains the direct study identified for brewed coffee and fatigue.
