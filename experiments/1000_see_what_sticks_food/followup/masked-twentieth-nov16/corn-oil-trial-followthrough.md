## NCT03986307 / S61809 / Dalle et al.

Official current API https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT03986307 (HTTP 200; local NCT03986307.json) names institutional study identifier S61809, 23 actual enrollees, actual January 1–May 25 2019 completion, registered/first posted June 14 2019, after completion. Secondary outcome Sleep explicitly specifies the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index and change across 14 weeks. Omega-3 versus corn-oil capsules both accompany the same resistance exercise for final 12 weeks. The record reports no results or linked publications; last update 2019-06-14.

Primary paper Dalle et al., first available December 7 2020, PMID33284965, DOI 10.1093/gerona/glaa309, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7907485/ is matched directly by the Ethics Committee identifier S61809, 23 participants and the identical 14-week priming/exercise protocol. Primary body local PMC7907485.txt lines 72, 78, 82: healthy older adults 65–83, eight women, neurodegenerative disease excluded; corn placebo 3 x 1100 mg softgels/day, Vista-Life Pharma; processing/refining not identified. The article reports muscle, strength, inflammation and functional outcomes. Whole body and the original seven-page supplement https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC7907485.1/glaa309_suppl_supplementarty_material.pdf (HTTP 200 697926 bytes; local dalle-supp.pdf and PDFium full text dalle-supp.txt) contain no hits for sleep, sleepiness, PSQI or Pittsburgh. A separately reported PSQI result was not located through exact NCT, S61809 and author/instrument searches. The registered sleep result remains pending; it is not a null outcome and the trial is not a Parkinson intervention.

## NCT01216982 / Dretsch 2014 probable relationship

Official https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT01216982 (HTTP 200, local NCT01216982.json), first posted October 7 2010, last updated March 9 2011, currently unknown status, no posted results or linked publication. PI Daniel Johnston; soldiers 18–55; Lovaza EPA/DHA vs three 1g corn oil softgels daily for three months. Primary psychometric battery includes PSQI and Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS).

Dretsch et al. 2014 PMID24690964, DOI 10.7205/MILMED-D-13-00395, https://academic.oup.com/milmed/article-abstract/179/4/396/4160759, published April 1 2014, explicitly reports Johnston as coauthor, deployed Iraq soldiers 18–55, Lovaza EPA/DHA versus corn oil ethyl esters; 44 versus 34, measured baseline and 60 days. The official original abstract (NLM EFetch 24690964.xml, HTTP 200) says no significant treatment-group effects for psychological health and cognition; omega-3 index change had an inverse relation to daytime sleepiness, reported as r=0.30, p=.009. It does not give a PSQI or ESS between-arm numerical estimate. The main full paper remains publisher-gated; a separate 2021 sleep review (https://academic.oup.com/nutritionreviews/article/79/8/847/6056631) identifies PSQI and ESS but mixes its report denominator as n=106 against the original abstract randomized n=78. Avoid importing that denominator or a blood-index association as an effect of oil. The trial registry and paper are a strong plausible identity match, not definitively identifier-matched: registry says ordinary corn-oil capsules for 3 months; paper abstract says corn-oil ethyl esters and 60 days. No original registration number has been found in the available published abstract or original registry. Both sources already appear separately in the local research catalog, with linkage pending.
