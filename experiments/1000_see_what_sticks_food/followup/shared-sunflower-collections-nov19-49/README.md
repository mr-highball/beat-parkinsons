# Shared food-collection review

The source review resolves 93 original pairs across 42 frozen claims and ten articles or collections. Sixty-six retain food, ingredient or tested constituent context. Twenty-seven resolve mismatches such as the WHO SAGE study name, investigators named Melissa, database or publication dates, caption abbreviations and search-only references to cod liver oil. The 93 additional study assessments include 66 original context records and 27 companion-study assessments. [Original decisions](dispositions.tsv) · [Related assessments](related-assessments.tsv) · [Replay verification](replay-verification.txt).

- The 2009 and 2019 corn-oil depression trials included sertraline in both arms. The 2019 mean BDI-II comparison favored the corn comparator numerically, with an inconclusive arm difference. The 2020 strength study gave a different corn-arm change in the original abstract and the final report. Final isometric strength was +12.2% with omega-3 and −1.4% with corn oil, with arm interaction p=.015. The treatment, population and outcomes remain distinct from the clinical hypotheses. - The two versions of P233 are treated as one family. The primary egg-yolk study revised the reported counts of muscle proteins favoring that fraction relative to C4. A different challenge used hard-boiled eggs and labeled phosphatidylcholine and measured gut microbial metabolism. The cod study and registry describe the same crossover, with different reported overall enrollment and analysis populations. - The coconut-oil meal study measured an acute endotoxin response. The broccoli crossover and cooked tomato sauce study measured biomarkers. The obesity proceedings distinguish a coffee-and-tea intake change from a carbonated-drink sweetener association with fatigue. [Coconut comparison](../../../../research/2015-2019/Source-Text/2016-PMID27816052-Coconut-Oil-Meal-Endotoxin-Crossover.txt) · [Steamed broccoli](../../../../research/2010-2014/Source-Text/2010-PMID20713433-Steamed-Broccoli-and-DNA-Markers.txt) · [Cooked tomato](../../../../research/2000-2004/Source-Text/2001-PMID11752012-Cooked-Tomato-Sauce-and-Lycopene.txt).
- The boron paper's injected control comparison uses sunflower seed oil and saline. Shared significance letters in Figures 5–8 supply no resolved control difference for the marked motor comparisons. The abstract and methods conflict on the saline route. The separate oil-only catalepsy result remains unreported and executive function was not measured.
