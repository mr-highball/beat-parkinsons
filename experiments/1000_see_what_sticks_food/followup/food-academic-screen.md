# Food candidates in the Europe PMC ingredient search

This review covers the food rows labeled `human evidence candidate` or `protocol or registration` in the ingredient-first screening table. The native join found 176 candidate rows and one protocol, covering 177 distinct ingredient–source pairs, 155 distinct source keys, and 31 food ingredients. This filter is narrower than the earlier 213-key clinical shortlist assembled from titles, abstracts, and focused leads. [Ingredient screening table](academic-screen.tsv) · [Source dispositions](food-academic-dispositions.tsv) · [Earlier screening record](../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#screening-record-and-open-leads)

All 155 keys received a disposition. The 43 records taken beyond title and index screening include 10 checked against full text, 23 checked against abstracts, eight matched to existing catalog assessments, one checked against a secondary summary because the primary letter was unavailable, and one assessed from publication metadata. Both the abstracts and title can produce false positives. These records include a Parkinson’s muscle-strength protocol without a coffee intervention, diagnostic food odors and boluses, and EGG as an abbreviation for electrogastrography. The key count includes preprints that describe the same study as a later journal article. Their dispositions identify the overlap.

The outcome review found indirect context for `0121`, `0290`, `0832`, and `0833`. A standardized meal study evaluated how existing symptoms affected potato-salad and apple-puree intake. Two other studies used yogurt as a bolus during diagnostic swallowing exams. A coffee toxicity case involved exceptional consumption and concomitant medicines. The Lithuanian food-frequency study reported food-specific frequencies by diagnosis and symptom associations for the overall Mediterranean diet score. None of these comparisons supplied a new direction for a frozen food, preparation, and symptom claim. Two candidate articles had unresolved primary outcome access. The 2008 coffee case has no PubMed abstract or public publisher full text. An institutional bulletin provides an account of self-reported daily function without a separate fatigue result. The 1967 chlorphenoxamine-with-caffeine trial has no PubMed abstract or outcome tables available in this pass. Its drug combination is already a preparation mismatch. The Saudi fatigue article has a public abstract, with caffeine exposure categories and effect estimates still unavailable in the full tables. Publisher, title, DOI, and author searches supplied these access statuses.
