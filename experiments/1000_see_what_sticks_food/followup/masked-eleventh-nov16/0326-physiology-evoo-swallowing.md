# 2019-06-20 — Grade-specific olive-oil stimulation of the swallowing reflex in rats

At the 96th Annual Meeting of the Physiological Society of Japan, Izumi Ujihara, Suzuro Hitomi and Kentaro Ono reported acute pharyngolaryngeal infusion experiments in anesthetized adult male Wistar rats. Mylohyoid electromyography and visible laryngeal movement identified a swallow. The protocol assessed swallow number and time to first swallow. Direct pharyngolaryngeal infusion of extra-virgin olive oil elicited swallowing, while ordinary olive oil and mineral oil did not. Water elicited swallowing, as did mineral oil containing capsaicin or allyl isothiocyanate. The original reports neither the number of rats per oil stimulus nor an oil-specific numeric comparison. The authors point to TRPA1 stimulation as a plausible basis of the observed separation. This report has no dietary exposure, Parkinson model, aspiration outcome or symptom severity assessment. The publication date is 20 June 2019 and the result is from proceedings presentation 2P-487, on PDF page 272. DOI 10.1007/s12576-019-00673-w, PMID 31222665 identifies the whole proceedings. [Original proceedings, presentation 2P-487](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s12576-019-00673-w.pdf#page=272) · [Publisher identity](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12576-019-00673-w)

Related reports refine the transfer. The 2008 human experiment measured time between repeated voluntary swallows after generic olive-oil infusion into the pharynx. A 2006 meeting abstract by the same human laboratory applied generic olive oil to the anterior tongue and reported a weaker response than sodium salts in the slow-swallow subgroup, without reporting an oil-versus-water result. The experiments used distinct application sites and the cohort overlap is unverified. In a separate human chemesthesis experiment, a small sample rated real extra-virgin olive oil as irritating in the throat after swallowing. Pungency ratings do not show whether swallowing was quicker, more difficult or safer. The later electrophysiology and injury-model reports from the rat research group describe chloride effects or non-oil TRP agonists. They provide no replication of the grade comparison. Keep 0326 Unknown. The grade-specific result establishes the local-swallow reflex as a plausible route for further research. It is a direct animal reflex following mucosal application, whereas the frozen endpoint is severity of swallowing difficulty after eating EVOO in people with Parkinson disease. The controlled combination-product human data remain inconclusive and a diet-only swallowing result is unavailable from the ALS pilot.
