## Cardamom and pain

A randomized split-mouth extraction study compared a tablet containing cardamom and other plants with an active analgesic. At 8 hours, reported mean pain was 5.95 versus 5.14 (p = .383). At 72 hours it was 0.86 versus 1.18 (p = .525). The report contains a conflicting 8-hour mean in its prose. A separate bowel-syndrome study reports improvement on a pooled score with a five-plant product that includes ground cardamom. Neither study isolates cardamom or measures Parkinson pain. The injection studies concern animal nociception. Their design and one source's conflicting species description limit transfer. ## Olive oil and swallowing

Claim 0326 retains Unknown. The Sjögren trial's swallowing-difficulty score changed from 4.46 to 4.38 with the topical olive- and parsley-oil combination and from 4.73 to 4.46 in controls (between-change p = .57). The original article links the registration, which appeared after trial completion. Separately, local extra-virgin oil elicited swallowing in an anesthetized rat model where ordinary olive oil and mineral oil did not. The physiology abstract reports no numeric oil-specific estimate. Both study designs are indirect for dietary extra-virgin oil and clinical Parkinson dysphagia. The Spanish radiation report establishes a 2014 publication for a cohort that appears again in English in 2016. Count the two reports once.
