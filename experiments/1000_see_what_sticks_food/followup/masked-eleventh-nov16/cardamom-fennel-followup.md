## 2022 human tooth-extraction pain trial: cardamom as one ingredient

Shinde et al., online 25 June 2022, DOI 10.1007/s12663-022-01748-9, PMC9871148, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9871148/ . Original methods: randomized split-mouth 22 completed patients age 13–25, two premolar extraction sites allocated Analdent vs aceclofenac, tablets twice daily for three days; examiner, patients and analyst were described as blinded. Analdent includes 15 mg Elettaria cardamomum seed powder alongside clove powder 200 mg and four other specified plant powders per tablet. The composition text erroneously labels ginger powder as E. cardamomum as well; do not count this as extra cardamom. Trial cited CTRI/2020/03/024296 dated 27 March 2020, not independently retrieved here.

Table 3 pain VAS mean (SD) Analdent versus aceclofenac: 8 h 5.95 (3.11) vs 5.14 (3.04), p=.383; 24 h 3.50 (2.35) vs 3.23 (2.16), p=.690; 48 h 1.86 (1.78) vs 2.05 (1.65), p=.727; 72 h 0.86 (1.70) vs 1.18 (1.59), p=.525. The prose gives a conflicting aceclofenac 8 h mean 5.914; Tables 2 and 3 say 5.14. Change over time cannot isolate treatment from ordinary healing; no no-treatment group; lack of significant difference is not proof of equivalence. The active multi-ingredient medicine cannot identify the effect of cardamom, and acute dental pain in young patients is not Parkinson pain. Candidate related contextual source, not a directional estimate for 0363. Sponsor supplied product.

## 2020 conference primary, green cardamom

Habibiyan, Dehghani and Ahmadi, “Antinociceptive Effects of Green Cardamom,” OICLS-20 conference proceedings, printed pp. 111–114: https://greennetwork-events.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/ICLS20-CONFERENCE-BOOK.FINAL_compressed.pdf . Six groups of six male rats; hydroethanolic green cardamom fruit extract was injected intraperitoneally, with tail-flick assessed before and 20 minutes after. Doses 200 and 400 mg/kg had longer latency than control (reported p<.001); 100 did not, and 400 exceeded 200 (p<.001). Figure is graphical; no exact values inferred. One conclusion sentence says reduced pain threshold despite the reported longer withdrawal latency. Extract injection is neither oral ground cardamom nor a Parkinson pain outcome.

## 2019 journal precursor in the conference reference list

Rahmanian, Rahmanian and Jahromi, “Anti-nociceptive Effect of Hydro-alcoholic Extract of Cardamom Seed in Male Rat,” Journal of International Translational Medicine 7(1):5–8, March 2019, DOI 10.11910/2227-6394.2019.07.01.02. Indexed abstract https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333235095_Anti-nociceptive_Effect_of_Hydro-alcoholic_Extract_of_Cardamom_Seed_in_Male_Rat (search-indexed text; direct access HTTP 429 on this check). Abstract itself inconsistently calls animals mice despite title saying male rat. Intraperitoneal hydroalcoholic seed extract: reported antinociception at 200 and 300 mg/kg in tail-flick and formalin second phase, 300 in formalin first phase; salicylate comparator better on second phase. Authors describe formalin late phase as chronic pain, which is not a clinical chronic-pain cohort. Distinct authors and designs from 2020 meeting, but unverified original methods prevent an independence judgment. Seek full primary original before extracting animals, exact group sizes or coefficients.

## 2013 fennel report: motor construct boundary

Neetu Singh, Sunil Kumar and Rana, 30 September 2013, DOI 10.7324/JAPS.2013.3912, original PDF https://japsonline.com/admin/php/uploads/1050_pdf.pdf, pp. 66–69 methods and results. Market F. vulgare fruits shade dried, ground, Soxhlet methanol extracted (7.2% yield), not toasted culinary fennel. Oral extract 500 mg/kg daily for 7 days plus haloperidol injection, versus haloperidol alone, 6 mice/group; day 3,5,7 bar assay measured time retaining investigator-imposed forepaw position, with 200-second cutoff. Authors report less catalepsy in extract+haloperidol versus haloperidol on day 7, p<.001. This is imposed drug-induced catalepsy, not observed spontaneous dystonia, dystonic contractions, or clinical PD. Separate forced swim in rats measures immobility and norepinephrine mouse phase counts deaths; neither tests dystonia. Existing assessment already captures the relation; no new unsupported direction.
