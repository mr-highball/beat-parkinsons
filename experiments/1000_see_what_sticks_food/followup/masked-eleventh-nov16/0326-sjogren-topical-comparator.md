# 2018-12-17 — Topical olive-oil and parsley-oil products in primary Sjögren xerostomia

López-Pintor and colleagues randomized 28 patients with primary Sjögren syndrome and dry mouth, 14 per arm. Twenty-four patients completed 28 days and were analyzed (13 active, 11 control, all women). The test mouthwash and toothpaste contained unspecified-grade olive fruit oil, parsley seed oil, betaine, xylitol, allantoin and vitamin B5. The control products were similar in appearance and did not contain that group of ingredients. Participants and outcome examiners were masked. A company employee handled the randomization and prepared products. The report describes October 2016 to April 2017 recruitment and June to November 2017 treatment and follow-up. Wiley dates first publication to 17 December 2018. PubMed gives a later electronic date of 8 January 2019 and an April 2019 issue. PMID 30561129. [Publisher record](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/odi.13019) · [NLM record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30561129/) · [Original article, Sections 2.3–2.9 and Table 2](https://docta.ucm.es/bitstreams/ef26cef7-0aa8-4c53-bd41-9dec71ba445e/download) · [Author institution repository record](https://hdl.handle.net/20.500.14352/109298)

The swallowing item rates difficulty caused by dryness on a 0–10 cm visual analogue scale. Active mean was 4.46 cm at baseline and 4.38 cm at 28 days, with a mean improvement of 0.08 cm (SD 2.56, within-arm P=1.00). Control mean was 4.73 cm and 4.46 cm, with a mean improvement of 0.27 cm (SD 2.41, P=.68). The reported between-arm comparison of improvement was P=.57. The complete-case comparison is inconclusive for this multi-ingredient topical product and offers no isolated dietary EVOO or Parkinson swallowing estimate. The aggregate xerostomia score difference (P=.61) and the within-arm oral quality-of-life improvement (P=.04, between-arm P=.86) are different endpoints from swallowing. [Original article, Tables 2–3](https://docta.ucm.es/bitstreams/ef26cef7-0aa8-4c53-bd41-9dec71ba445e/download)

The article links NCT03611283 directly. The registry gives actual start 3 October 2016, primary completion 2 November 2017, and study completion 30 November 2017. It was first submitted 16 July 2018 and first posted 2 August 2018, after completion. The registry lists composite xerostomia and oral health quality of life assessments and carries no posted results. These dates do not prospectively establish a separately designated swallowing endpoint. [Registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03611283)
