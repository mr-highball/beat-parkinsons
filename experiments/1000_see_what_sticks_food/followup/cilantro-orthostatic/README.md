# Dried cilantro and orthostatic lightheadedness

Statement 0062 asks whether eating dried cilantro worsens orthostatic lightheadedness severity in Parkinson disease. The evidence currently leaves the direction unknown. The Parkinson dietary intervention names coriander among seasonings without recording its part, preparation or individual dose. It provides no standing outcome. Oral coriander seed powder trials measured general blood pressure in other patient populations. The identified leaf studies used extracts and measured urine output or isolated ACE. These results do not estimate an effect of eating dried cilantro on orthostatic lightheadedness. ## Source and endpoint checks

The 1986 human experiment reported no significant diuresis after boiled dried seed extract at its tested concentration. Its Figure 1 caption and Results give different concentrations for the comparative presentation. A 2019 experiment reported diuresis after oral extracts made from dried leaves in rats, without measuring blood pressure. Taken together, these reports preserve the route and plant-part distinction in the proposed renal mechanism. [1986 original assessment](../../../../research/1985-1989/Food-Experiment-Cilantro-Orthostatic.md#1986--boiled-dried-coriander-seed-control-in-a-human-diuresis-study) · [2019 original assessment](../../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Cilantro-Orthostatic.md#2019--oral-extracts-made-from-dried-cilantro-leaves-increased-rat-urine-output)

The Parkinson meal literature gives distinct outcomes under different protocols. The 1995 study found a larger supine systolic drop after a meal in people with Parkinson disease, while the separate standing and tilt measurements found a smaller orthostatic drop after lunch. The 2025 patient comparison measured larger postprandial drops in Parkinson disease than in multiple system atrophy, with patients seated and feet elevated. Neither study tested cilantro, and these results do not substitute for standing symptoms.
