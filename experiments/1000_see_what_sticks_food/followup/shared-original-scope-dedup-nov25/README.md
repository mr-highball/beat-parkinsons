## Clinical and cultural follow-through

- **Corn oil and rigidity.** The Finley feeding study included limb rigidity in the neurological examination and reported no neurological impairment in any diet group of healthy participants. The EFSA opinion reversed the fat labels for the 16 analyzed participants in one summary. Original Figure 1 shows 10 corn-oil and 7 cocoa-butter participants. Table 1 reports 10 and 6 for the tracer analysis. These counts describe one cohort with different denominators. The Parkinson rigidity, depression and pain claims remain open. [Feeding study](../../../../research/2000-2004/Source-Text/2003-PMID12949376-Finley-Corn-Oil-and-Neurologic-Examination.txt)
- **Tomato and motor outcomes.** Some gait measures improved after a tomato seed extract in the original mouse study. The authors measured gait separately from the clinical rigidity and executive function outcomes in the cooked tomato claims. The review and its underlying original count once in each affected scorecard. [Tomato seed experiment](../../../../research/2010-2014/Source-Text/2014-PMID24831121-Gokul-Tomato-Seed-Gait-and-Endpoint-Scope.txt)
- **Cinnamon and speech.** One conference case reported voice changes with several triggers, including oral or scented cinnamon, followed by later tolerance after vocal fold treatment. Independent oral-mucosal reports concern flavored mints, gum or candy. The Ninjin’yoeito source is a prospective open-label pilot. Its 12-component formula and combined oral outcome prevent attribution to cassia or to a speech intelligibility measure. Claim 0183 remains Unknown. - **Traditional voice remedies.** The West Bank survey identifies anise, ginger and spearmint among five leading recommendations for voice disorders. The abstract contains no patient outcome or Parkinson diagnosis. The remaining species require the full table.
