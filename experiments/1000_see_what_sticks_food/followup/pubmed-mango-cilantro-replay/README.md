# Repaired PubMed mango and cilantro result screen

The indexed PubMed searches ran on November 14, 2026 US Eastern (November 15 UTC). PR611 and PR612 returned 21 and 7 memberships, or 27 distinct mango PMIDs. PR620 through PR623 returned 9, 4, 5 and 26 memberships, or 40 distinct cilantro PMIDs. Every reported source ID was retrieved in a single ESearch request per expression. The exact outbound queries, NLM translations and PMID-level decisions are accompanied by the native receipts and `screen.tsv`. These targeted scopes correct the former runs in which the converter substituted a Parkinson clause for the initial ingredient clause. The screen covers all memberships in these six scopes, and reviews complete original bodies where available for the close or new human results. Literature or broad context outside these scopes is covered in the earlier dedicated search reports.

The strongest new mango chain came from PMID 36057629, retrieved in both mango expressions. The published protocol, registry, recovered publisher supplement, separately published feasibility pilot (PMID 40616143) and completed randomized results (PMID 40877839) establish that two of four available smoothie varieties contained mango and that clinical fatigue severity was measured. The published mixed product estimate was −0.1 (95% CI −0.6 to 0.4). The mixed fruit comparison does not give an isolated mango dose, uncooked mango intake, a Parkinson subgroup or an insomnia endpoint. The other mango records include the already cataloged 2012 nocturnal cramps case, drinks or supplement interventions with different preparation, prior extract trials and other biological or indexing contexts. The full 2024 Stadice body identifies a seven-day standardized leaf extract and cognition tests, and excludes sleep disorders. Closest new cilantro checks came from the orthostatic/postprandial expression. PMID 38926797 is a fasting hypoglycemic syncope case, with remote mixed-herb use and a closer omeprazole exposure favored by its authors. PMID 17182483 is a 2006 diabetes interview study that separately reports coriander use and aggregate complaints including dizziness. Its full publication body remains unresolved, leaving cross-tabulated adverse events unavailable. PMID 12078423 is a 2002 spice allergy series listing one coriander allergy, without an individual blood pressure, dizziness or preparation description in its accessible abstract. The 2014 primary abstract for PMID 24836119 specifies an aqueous leaf/stem extract, oral sucrose tests in rats and a yeast enzyme assay. Its use of the word “postprandial” pertains to glycaemia. The 2015 gentamicin study PMID 27486367 concerns shade-dried material subjected to solvent extraction, nephrotoxicity and laboratory measures in rats. Its Methods call the extract ethyl acetate, whereas other wording calls it ethanolic. Neither animal study estimates a hypotensive or standing response.
