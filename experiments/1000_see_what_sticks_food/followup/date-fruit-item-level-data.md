# Date fruit, pain and apathy dataset follow-up

The November 12, 2026 search followed Parkinson’s records and independent date-fruit datasets for [0502](../statements/0502.txt) and [0758](../statements/0758.txt). It verified a human Parkinson’s registration containing date paste and recovered the original abstract for its related pilot. It also recovered individual date-remedy and pain fields in a non-Parkinson survey. None of these records provides an estimate of how eating dried dates changes Parkinson’s pain or apathy severity.

## Parkinson trials and the NeuroFiber publication trail

ClinicalTrials.gov v2 was searched using `query.cond=Parkinson Disease` and seven separate quoted `query.intr` expressions. `Phoenix dactylifera`, `dried dates`, `Ajwa`, `Khassab`, and `Mazafati` each returned zero. `date fruit` and `date palm` each returned the same 20 records, with no continuation token. All 20 detail endpoints returned HTTP 200. A full protocol-text check for date fruit, palm, paste, flesh and seed, the botanical name, dried dates and the named cultivars found only `date paste` in `NCT07914387`. Its intervention name is Prebiotic fiber bar. The response and detail-check files are under ignored `build/date-fruit-items`. Europe PMC returned zero for `TITLE_ABS:("Phoenix dactylifera" OR "date fruit" OR "dried date" OR "dried dates" OR "date palm" OR ajwa OR mazafati OR khassab OR barhi OR "deglet noor") AND TITLE_ABS:parkinson*`. The title and abstract restriction explains why that query misses the bar’s excipient and publications about a pooled dried-fruit exposure. [ClinicalTrials.gov query](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies?query.cond=Parkinson%20Disease&query.intr=%22date%20fruit%22&countTotal=true&pageSize=100&fields=NCTId%2CBriefTitle%2CInterventionName) · [Earlier literature search](date-pain-apathy-primary-trail.md#archive-and-complementary-search)

## Date-specific outside Parkinson’s

- Tahir’s 2025 cross-sectional dysmenorrhea study supplies a participant-level supplement containing 202 responses. A native Free Pascal check decoded the XLSX worksheet and shared strings from the publisher’s file (MD5 `c21aed11b88e812ffb5eff65374650d3`). On sheet 1, field I value `1` selects 155 records reporting menstrual pain. The standalone date remedy is the trimmed, case-insensitive exact label `date` or `dates` in column W. The separately worded dates-in-milk entry does not occur in the 155 rows. The remedy groups and WaLIDD score (column O) sum to 24 and 160 versus 131 and 795. The count of highest pain-intensity code `3` (column L) is 11 versus 48, respectively. This cross-tabulation cannot establish remedy timing, treatment response, or the dried preparation. The observed difference may reflect participants with more pain choosing a remedy. The public supplement is linked and participant rows are absent from the catalog. [Dated analysis](../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Date-Pain-Followup.md#date-remedies-and-menstrual-pain-in-a-university-survey) · [Primary survey and linked data](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12905-025-03777-1)
- Takhrifa’s original questionnaire distinguishes dates from other dried fruit. Figure 1 reports results for ten foods and omits dates and dried fruit. The VAS estimate describes the rheumatology cohort as a whole. AlQuaiz’s original 2022 tables separately count date consumers and report several pain domains. The reported association for dates concerns appetite and weight-gain symptoms, without a pain estimate. [Dated rheumatology note](../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Date-Pain-Followup.md#dates-and-joint-symptoms-in-a-moroccan-rheumatology-survey) · [Dated premenstrual survey note](../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Date-Pain-Followup.md#individual-date-intake-and-premenstrual-symptom-domains)
