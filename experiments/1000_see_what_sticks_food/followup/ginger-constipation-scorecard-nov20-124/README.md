# Grated ginger and constipation scorecard

Claim 0581 predicts worse constipation. It retains Maybe False with the version 1 minimal-signal anchor of 50.005, replacing the legacy score of 52. Two small IBS trials isolated oral ginger capsules and reported lower constipation scores at follow-up. The analysis of a separate constipation domain was unadjusted in both. The same botanical rhizome and measured symptom give a basis for a weak transfer across dose form and clinical population. Neither trial enrolled people with Parkinson disease. [Al-Jassim original](https://journals.innovareacademics.in/index.php/ajpcr/article/view/30716) · [Rostamkhani original](https://jmp.ir/article-1-2297-en.pdf)

The point estimates and reporting constraints belong to their own comparisons. The overall IBS pilot was inconclusive. A migraine extract trial reported one constipation adverse event. Other ginger trials measured bowel symptoms in multiple sclerosis, hypothyroidism, functional dyspepsia or occasional constipation. The Parkinson registrations are still waiting for outcome reports. The Isfahan trial registers overall motor and quality-of-life outcomes, and the Cambridge study prioritizes ghrelin and compares two extract doses. [Cambridge registration](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07882108) · [Isfahan registration](https://irct.ir/trial/87235)

Four public discussions add perceived relief, no noticeable relief and worsening under different circumstances. The reports vary in formulation and include a multi-food smoothie, a period after stopping another product, and a reported response change after illness. The laboratory source cited by one thread measured contraction of isolated rat colon. The 19-family worksheet preserves these distinct comparisons and uncertainties.
