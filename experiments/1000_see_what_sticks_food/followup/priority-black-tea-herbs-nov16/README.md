## Claim judgments

- **0100, plain dark chocolate worsens anxiety. Maybe False at 52, previously 51.** In an independent 99-person randomized thyroid biopsy study, postprocedure STAI was 34.31 with dark chocolate and 38.89 without a snack. The article reports a group-by-time p = .01. Participants and the operator knew the assignments. A ten-person masked chronic-fatigue crossover gives favorable within-period HADS anxiety changes with high-cocoa chocolate and unfavorable changes with simulated chocolate, but lacks a paired treatment contrast. The earlier acute snack study was inconclusive. The brewed-cocoa study found a difference only for caffeinated cocoa compared with caffeine alone. The Parkinson crossover and rat study have no anxiety outcome. These measurements supply a slightly stronger weak lean toward improvement, with expectancy effects and clinical transfer unresolved. [Biopsy report](../../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Chocolate-Anxiety.md#dark-chocolate-and-measured-anxiety-after-thyroid-biopsy) · [Chronic-fatigue crossover](../../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Chocolate-Anxiety.md#high-cocoa-chocolate-and-anxiety-in-a-chronic-fatigue-crossover) · [Brewed cocoa](../../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Chocolate-Anxiety.md)
- **0081, extra-virgin olive oil worsens urinary urgency frequency. Unknown at 50.** The overactive-bladder trial assigned extra-virgin oil as a comparator alongside oxybutynin and ended without posted results. The saw-palmetto report describes an olive-oil placebo, while its own registry specifies polyethylene glycol-400. The report gives composite urinary scores and no separate urgency or void-frequency treatment effect. Parkinson dietary-pattern studies give broader urinary outcomes without isolating the oil. [Overactive-bladder registry](../../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Olive-Oil-Urinary-Comparators.md) · [Linked saw-palmetto records and Parkinson cohort](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Olive-Oil-Urinary-Comparators.md)
- **0082, brewed black tea reduces bradykinesia. Maybe True at 51.** An acute tea experiment in healthy rats reports more exploratory crossings, while the longer tea experiment gives an inconclusive crossing comparison. Neither tests bradykinesia. The earlier caffeine trial's clinical subscore and the competing longer and observational comparisons remain the basis of the weak constituent inference. [Original black-tea experiments](../../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Black-Tea-Animal-Motor.md) · [Primary caffeine trial](../../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Black-Tea-Motor.md#randomized-caffeine-trial-and-separately-reported-gait-and-rest-tremor)
- **0128, fresh rosemary worsens depressive symptoms. Maybe False at 51.** The primary dried-capsule and extract comparisons are already cataloged. The accessible 2026 review discusses different preparations and populations. A subscription rosmarinic-acid review remains at abstract depth. [Original plant studies](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#rosemary-capsules-in-major-depressive-disorder) · [Open review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00210-020-01935-w)
- **0133, brewed lemon balm reduces depressive symptoms. Maybe True at 51.** UMIN000055134 marks the brewed-tea crossover completed but lists no public results. The earlier active-comparator capsule trial reports a nonsignificant group comparison, with an unresolved numerical inconsistency between Results text and Figure 2. That inconsistency limits the use of its precise within-group estimate.
