# Shared caffeine survey in the beverage fatigue scorecards

The brewed-coffee and brewed-green-tea fatigue reviews used the same Alshimemeri survey and the same comparison. The two cards previously described its observed effect differently. Table 4 reports fatigue prevalence of 60.7% among frequent caffeine users and 62.5% among infrequent users (OR 0.94, 95% CI 0.37–2.35, p = 1.000). Both cards now record the same favorable point-estimate direction, its wide interval, and its source family. The survey grouped unspecified caffeine use and dichotomized the fatigue scale. It supplies no transferable direction for either beverage. [Cataloged original table details](../../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Food-Followup.md#reported-caffeine-intake-and-parkinsons-fatigue)

Coffee remains Maybe False for the hypothesis that it worsens fatigue, at the minimal signal tier. Its leading evidence is a filtered-coffee contrast for acute mental fatigue in healthy adults and a small, imprecise fatigue score contrast under isolated caffeine in Parkinson disease. Green tea remains Maybe True for the hypothesis that it worsens fatigue, at the limited clinical link tier. Its leading evidence is a 14-completer Parkinson pilot with higher fatigue scores after brewed green tea, without a control group. These distinct findings were assessed separately.
