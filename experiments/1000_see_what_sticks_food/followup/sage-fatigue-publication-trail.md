# Fresh sage and Parkinson’s fatigue publication trail

November 12, 2026. [Claim 0883](../statements/0883.txt) specifies fresh culinary sage and fatigue severity in Parkinson’s. The Newcastle study administered dried leaf capsules and reported cognition and salivation. The nearest recovered fatigue measures come from trials of sage extracts, essential oil or mixtures in participants without Parkinson’s. These sources leave the frozen direction unresolved. [2019 Newcastle grant report note](../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#sage-cognitive-trial-results-attachment) · [Fresh sage tip extract trial](../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#fresh-sage-tip-extract-and-menopausal-fatigue)

## Newcastle protocol, poster and manuscript

The official EU Clinical Trials Register lists June 26, 2007 as the database entry date and gives memory quality, memory speed and attention as primary endpoints in protocol field E.5.1. The public site launched in March 2011. A CDX check of the exact official protocol URL returned July 18, 2025 as its earliest indexed HTTP 200 capture, with a second capture in June 2026. Archive coverage does not establish when the protocol first appeared. The registry’s result page dates its only version to March 24, 2019. [Dated protocol note](../../../research/2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#sage-cognitive-trial-registration) · [Wayback capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20250718050933/https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/trial/2007-002899-34/GB) · [Official result history](https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/trial/2007-002899-34/results)

The five-page grant report, reference K-0613, was checked visually. Its SHA-256 is `025e438af2d978e6cf5c83f02e68eebb77df3ad408c7c5facddc9b249e996dc`. Page 2 refers to a study timetable for secondary outcomes that is absent from the five pages. Pages 2–4 report cognition, salivation and selected safety observations, with no named fatigue measure, arm-level fatigue estimate or adverse-event table. Page 1 mentions data analysis and poster preparation through October 2008. Page 5 describes an MRes project and a manuscript by R. Leung, D. Burn, R. Middleton, K. Wesnes and E. Perry scheduled for submission in March 2009. The summary’s comparison with vitamin E also differs from its description of the trial’s original placebo objective. [Primary grant report, pp. 1–5](https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/rest/download/result/attachment/2007-002899-34/1/26590)

The October 18, 2010 investigator account reports 25 patients and no overall cognitive advantage and cites the manuscript as in preparation. It calls the comparator placebo. The grant report names vitamin E as the replacement comparator and reports an individual working-memory composite favoring vitamin E. The two documents describe the same pilot, with different levels of detail. The individual composite result is distinct from an overall cognitive benefit. [2010 investigator account, sage section and reference 51](../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#investigator-account-of-the-newcastle-sage-pilot)

The manuscript title variants, grant number, trial accession and investigator combinations were checked through Europe PMC, PubMed-indexed results, Newcastle University’s research and thesis repositories, Parkinson’s UK, and targeted Movement Disorders and World Parkinson Congress indexing. ## Adverse-event reports

The official drug API returned three case IDs for exact `SAGE` and `FATIGUE`, two for the botanical name and fatigue, and zero for `MENOSAN` or `MENOFORCE` and fatigue. The exact and botanical searches overlap on one ID. The four unique case IDs resolve to one underlying German report through a shared worldwide identifier. The 2019 first- and third-quarter extracts confirm sage and fatigue co-listed in two original versions. The 2022 third-quarter extract shows all four IDs and links sage to nasopharyngitis. The event date was recorded as 2001, and a report version records manufacturer receipt in 2004. The official 2004 first-quarter archive contained no matching manufacturer identifier or sage drug name, so the earliest verified public sage-and-fatigue record is May 8, 2019. The dosage and preparation are unknown. Fatigue was recorded during a complex adverse event with multiple suspect drugs and without a listed Parkinson’s diagnosis. [2019 FDA releases](../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#sage-and-fatigue-in-fda-adverse-event-reports) · [2022 FDA and 2023 French agency findings](../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Herb-Spice-Followup.md#fda-and-french-sage-adverse-event-follow-up)

The food API returned 21 product-name rows for `sage`, none for `Salvia officinalis`, and two name matches with fatigue. In report `165773`, Sage is part of the brand of an ascorbic acid product. The second report, `103140`, lists Silver Sage T Thyro-Start as a concomitant product and Total Body Formula as suspect, with elevated selenium among the reported findings. These reports do not establish an exposure to fresh common sage.
