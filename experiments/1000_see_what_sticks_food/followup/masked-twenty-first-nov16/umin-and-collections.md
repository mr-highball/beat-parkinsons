## 0767 registry and source report

**Official registry, first disclosed 2024-02-22**: UMIN000053685, receipt R000060435, English https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/icdr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000060435 and Japanese https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000060435. Japanese Ministry portal separately reports registration 2024-02-22 and portal last updated 2024-04-03 https://rctportal.mhlw.go.jp/detail/um?trial_id=UMIN000053685. UMIN itself labels last modified 2024-02-22. Distinguish the portal's update from UMIN's own date.

The record reports protocol fixation 2017-02-17, IRB 2017-03-03, anticipated trial start 2017-03-14 and last follow-up 2017-08-16. Thus public disclosure followed the reported follow-up by more than six years; dates are those shown in the registry, not proof of precisely when enrollment actually began. Registry says recruitment completed, 46 enrolled; the results section says unpublished, analysis not completed, and has no results URL or results date. Its protocol URL field is blank. This stale registry status conflicts chronologically with the later journal report, but does not by itself establish a protocol violation or selective reporting. It lists healthy adults 40–74 and excludes sleep problems; randomized double-blind parallel design and two 8-week capsule arms. It names placebo but does not identify its material, amount, or refined status. Primary WMS-R memory; key secondary calculation performance, mental-function VAS and choline/LPC concentrations. It does not name sleep quality or daytime sleepiness separately among registered outcomes.

**Original report**, Tanaka-Kanegae, Kimura and Hamada, first online 2024-08-24, 2025 volume 76 pp 58–62, DOI 10.3164/jcbn.24-105, full publisher PDF https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jcbn/76/1/76_24-105/_pdf, verified first-online date https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/browse/jcbn/76/1/_contents/-char/en. Placebo was seven soft capsules containing 2.1 g soy oil per dose, consumed after breakfast and dinner for 8 weeks (4.2 g/day, 14 capsules/day); source names supplier Nisshin OilliO but does not specify refined status. Comparator was seven soy-derived lysolecithin capsules per dose containing 240 mg LPC, twice daily. Initial assignment 23 per arm; one placebo participant withdrew consent at two weeks; table gives n=22,23 and article confusingly says all participants were included in analysis, so avoid assuming whether withdrawal contributes to each timepoint beyond table denominator.

Sleep quality was a subjective 100 mm VAS, not an Epworth, Stanford or objective daytime sleepiness scale; placebo baseline 80.8 ± 3.2 and 8-week 80.7 ± 3.6, lysolecithin 76.5 ± 4.1 and 77.5 ± 3.8, respectively, mean ± SE. The article does not specify an untreated oil comparator, so within-arm change cannot isolate an oil effect. One participant complained of drowsiness in the lysolecithin group, not the soy-oil placebo group; the study physician did not attribute any adverse event to LLT. The setting is healthy volunteers, not PD, and sleep-quality differs from claim's daytime sleepiness. The reported subjective sleep item is broader than but not necessarily inconsistent with registered generic mental-function VAS; absent a posted protocol, prespecification cannot be established.
