# Chao 2025 primary and registry follow-through — 2026-11-16

## Verified article and limits
Chao Y, Tong X, Zhang B, Yang H et al., *Olfactory training improves cognition and modifies brain networks in type 2 diabetes patients with mild cognitive impairment: A 16-week randomised controlled trial*, Diabetes Obes Metab 27:7040–7051, first online 2025-09-05, PMID 40908892, DOI 10.1111/dom.70103. Author/publisher abstract https://dom-pubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/dom.70103 describes an open-label 60-participant 1:1 randomized trial: six odours rose, eucalyptus, lemon, clove, coffee and cinnamon, inhaled twice daily for six minutes, versus routine care. Baseline-to-16-week MoCA changed +1.9 versus +0.4; p=.001; partial eta-squared .190 with printed 95% confidence interval [0.04,0.35]. This interval is printed immediately after effect-size notation; it should not be represented as the confidence interval for a 1.5-point mean change. Executive function is described as improved but no test name, actual domain estimate, uncertainty or table appears in the accessible abstract. MoCA is global cognitive function, not an executive-only contrast. The intervention is bundled smell training, not oral ground clove or independent clove arm; T2D with mild cognitive impairment is not PD. Differential monitoring and open-label/no-training control allow expectation/practice differences. No trial-level direction can be attributed to clove alone.

Registration first public 2024-03-25, actual start 2024-01-01, actual completion 2025-05-15, last update 2025-07-11; 60 actual, parallel randomized open label; study identifier ZZ2024, Nanjing Drum Tower. Arms and same six scents, 6 minutes twice daily, 4 months and MoCA primary exactly correspond to published abstract. Registry controls maintained existing diet/lifestyle and glucose-lowering medications with calls about glycemia, versus training participants receiving twice-weekly calls assessing safety and adherence. Eligibility ages 40–75, T2D/MCI, stable treatment and right handed, with multiple psychiatric, neurological and nasal exclusions and avoidance of cognitive medications.
