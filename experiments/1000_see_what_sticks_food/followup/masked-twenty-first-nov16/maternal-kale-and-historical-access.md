## Original source and participant overlap
Ustun et al. 2022 PMID 36130610, DOI 10.1177/09567976221105460, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09567976221105460 tested maternal 400 mg encapsulated carrot or kale powder and fetal 4D ultrasound facial reactions at 32 and 36 weeks. These are flavor-induced reactions, not maternal or fetal olfactory impairment, and no roasting established. The open 2025 primary PMID 39892645, DOI 10.1016/j.appet.2025.107891, published online 2025-01-30, https://research.aston.ac.uk/files/200676845/Flavor_learning_and_memory_in_utero_as_assessed_through_the_changing_pattern_of_olfactory_responses.pdf explicitly says its neonatal follow-up builds on the prior fetal findings within the same cohort. At birth, 14 kale-exposed and 18 carrot-exposed infants were tested at 2–4 weeks using odorous powder on moistened swabs and neutral water. Prenatal exposure was maternal capsule, not neonatal kale meal. Reactions are hedonic facial responses rather than measured impairment; no Parkinson participants. This is a linked study family, not two independent intervention replications. A 2022 Durham interview https://dulib.blog/2022/11/14/open-access-week-2022-a-conversation-with-beyza-ustan/ confirms neonatal video analysis was still ongoing in November 2022. The 2025 paper is the best verifiable public result date for neonatal outcome; see whether exact paper already exists before cataloging.
