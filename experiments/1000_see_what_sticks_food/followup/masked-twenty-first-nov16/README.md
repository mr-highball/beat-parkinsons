## Closest primary comparisons

- **0648, ground clove and executive function.** A six-odor trial in diabetes with mild cognitive impairment reports MoCA change of +1.9 versus +0.4 (p=.001). Its author abstract describes executive-domain improvements without a numeric estimate for that domain. The 2024 NCT06327633 protocol is a probable study-family match. The two-dose caryophyllene study reports a within-arm cognitive-failures score change and has no placebo. Two separately registered protocols involve clove scent, other odors and a Trail Making task. These studies leave culinary ground clove and Parkinson executive function unresolved. - **0767, refined soybean oil and daytime sleepiness.** The healthy-volunteer placebo group reported sleep-quality VAS 80.8 to 80.7. The lysolecithin group reported 76.5 to 77.5 and the one drowsiness complaint. The study excludes sleep problems and supplies no clinical daytime sleepiness estimate or oil-free contrast. The UMIN record was first disclosed in 2024 and gives a 2017 last follow-up. [Published results and official registry](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Soybean-Oil-Endpoints.md#soybean-oil-comparator-and-subjective-sleep-quality)
- **0645 and 0793, smell outcomes.** Kwak Supplementary Table 4 gives correct spearmint identification of 53.8% in Parkinson disease and 76.9% in drug-induced parkinsonism (Holm-adjusted p = .721). The item denominator in the drug-induced group remains unclear. A separate nasal-polyps trial compared two active mint teas and reported no clear diary-smell difference. The prenatal kale-powder papers are one linked fetal and neonatal study family with facial-response endpoints. These scopes leave consumption-related olfactory impairment in Parkinson disease unresolved. It has no acoustic outcome or frozen preparation.
