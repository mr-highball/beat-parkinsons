# 0643 patent identity and evidence boundary — 2026-11-16

## Source identity and botanical resolution

Both retrieved records contain the same original Chinese medicinal name, **川芎** (Chuanxiong). Neither patent text says *Levisticum officinale* or specifies its leaves.

- **CN101664541A**, application CN200810051139, published 2010-03-10; grant **CN101664541B**, published 2013-04-24. The Chinese application text gives 川芎 as 8–20 weight parts within a nine-ingredient dysmenorrhea medicine; preparation extracts it together with four other herbs using ethanol. Literal text: [application](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN101664541A/zh) and [grant](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN101664541B/zh). The grant page’s machine-generated entity index includes an apparent “Levisticum officinale” tag, but that name does not occur in the reviewed Chinese formulation text. Do not use that automated annotation as a botanical identification.
- **CN101856398A**, application CN200910081762, published 2010-10-13; grant **CN101856398B**, published 2013-05-29. Its Chinese application identifies a formula of peony bark, 川芎 (22.4–44.8 parts), and borneol. The example processes 川芎 as pieces and water-extracts its volatile oil. Application text: [Chinese A publication](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN101856398A/zh); grant page: [B publication, translated view](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN101856398B/en).

The United States Pharmacopeia’s official monograph identifies **Sichuan Lovage Rhizome / Chuanxiong** as dried rhizome of *Ligusticum striatum* DC. (synonym *Ligusticum chuanxiong*), a different genus from *Levisticum*. [USP-NF preview, monograph citation and definition](https://doi.org/10.31003/USPNF_M10635_02_01). The 2020 Chinese Pharmacopoeia wording reproduced by ShennongAlpha likewise identifies 川芎 as dried rhizome of *Ligusticum chuanxiong* Hort. ([transcription and attribution to Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2020](https://shennongalpha.westlake.edu.cn/knowledge/nmm-00cg)). For the target species, Kew Plants of the World Online accepts *Levisticum officinale* W.D.J.Koch as a species in genus *Levisticum* ([Kew taxon](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:844187-1/general-information)). Thus the patent’s English common-name rendering “Szechuan/Sichuan lovage” is not the culinary lovage species in the frozen claim; its named drug part is a rhizome, not a leaf.

## Claimed indication versus evidence reported

- **CN101664541:** the claimed product is an oral, multi-herb remedy for cold-pattern dysmenorrhea. The application reports tests on oxytocin/prostaglandin-induced mice and rats, writhing behavior, PGE₂ and inflammation; it does not report a human clinical trial of this formula. Any broad reference to clinical treatment of dysmenorrhea in the background is not a study dataset. The test is on the whole combination and cannot isolate Chuanxiong.
- **CN101856398:** the patent claims a peony-bark/Chuanxiong/borneol formula for chest impediment/cardialgia and describes it as analgesic and antispasmodic. The grant’s example reports animal pharmacology (e.g., mouse writhing and tail-pain assays), while other test examples evaluate product chemistry/quality. The background makes a general “clinical practice” claim but gives no identifiable human cohort, protocol, or outcome results. This is not human evidence for Parkinson pain or for the isolated ingredient.

## Source boundaries and revised disposition

The original Chinese A-publication text and linked publication PDFs were retrievable through Google Patents and its `patentimages` host. The reviewed B-publication text for CN101664541 is Chinese; for CN101856398 the accessible grant rendering was English and explicitly machine-translated from Chinese. I did not retrieve the official CNIPA publication-system file or prosecution dossier, so the mirror’s text is the source boundary for the patent content. The pharmacopoeia identity is corroborated by the USP’s official monograph; the Chinese Pharmacopoeia 2020 text was accessed through a secondary transcription rather than the official book. Kew POWO supplies the distinct accepted target taxon.

The two `PAT:` appearances remain in the six-field screen as **related** (analgesic/traditional-use context) rather than pending: Chuanxiong identity is now resolved, but both records are mixtures involving another species and rhizome, and no human Parkinson pain outcome is reported. This resolves the lexical false friend without turning patent claims or animal results into clinical evidence.
