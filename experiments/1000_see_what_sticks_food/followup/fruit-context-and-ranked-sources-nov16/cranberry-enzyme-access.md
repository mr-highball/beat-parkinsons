## Cásedas et al. 2017 cranberry and bilberry juice

PMID 29038797, DOI 10.1039/C7FO01205E, first published September 20, 2017 per https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2017/fo/c7fo01205e/unauth. The publisher full PDF route redirected to an unauthenticated landing page in the web reader; the authoritative Spanish TESEO thesis repository https://aplicaciones.ciencia.gob.es/teseo-rest/api/documento/download/public/46646 supplied the complete doctoral thesis of first author Guillermo Cásedas, with the original full Food & Function paper reproduced at PDF pages 95–101. Its metadata https://produccioncientifica.ucm.es/documentos/617a27d277dc987a90427f98?lang=de gives defense March 13, 2020; this is not established as the thesis's first public deposit date. `casedas-thesis.pdf` and `casedas-thesis.txt` are the exact downloaded original and existing bundled pypdfium2_cli extraction.

Methods 2.1–2.2 identify commercial Rabenhorst cranberry and bilberry juices, donated by Natur Import; the juices were freeze-dried for laboratory assays. Cranberry is Vaccinium macrocarpon and the other juice is Vaccinium myrtillus (bilberry). Methods 2.6.2 (PDF p.97) places juice and MAO-A in a microplate with a solvent control and clorgyline comparator. This is an in-vitro enzyme test, not an administered-food trial. The text does not identify the MAO-A enzyme's species or tissue source; do not label it human adipose, brain or animal based on the title, assay target or separate publications.

Results 3.4 and Figure 4/Table 2 (PDF pp.99–100) report both juices inhibited MAO-A concentration-dependently and neither showed AChE activity. Prose gives IC50 223.96 µg/mL cranberry and 336.28 µg/mL bilberry, with clorgyline 0.02 µg/mL; Table 2 instead prints cranberry 0.1850 ± 0.04471 and bilberry 0.3336 ± 0.1219 under a µg/mL heading but individual rows show µg/mg. These values and units are internally discordant; do not harmonize or present a precise comparative estimate without correction. No clinical depressive-symptom score or oral intake outcome was measured.

## Distinct human adipose work is pomegranate

Les, Carpéné et al., **Pomegranate juice and its main polyphenols exhibit direct effects on amine oxidases from human adipose tissue and inhibit lipid metabolism in adipocytes**, DOI 10.1016/j.jff.2017.04.006, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1756464617301974. Crossref records June 2017 issue publication and April 9, 2017 metadata creation, but no exact first-online date; deposit date is not a public publication date. Publisher primary abstract and Methods/Results previews identify pomegranate juice, human adipose-tissue preparations and human/mouse adipocytes; no cranberry exposure and no mood measurement. It is a distinct coauthor paper, not evidence of cranberry being applied to human adipose tissue. Publisher preview also cites earlier pomegranate purified recombinant-enzyme work. No conjectural merge with the 2017 cranberry assay or 2019 chapter.

The same author's 2018 SH-SY5Y paper https://magistralbr.caldic.com/storage/product-files/331368443.pdf uses cranberry and bilberry juices in human-derived neuronal cells for oxidative outcomes; it is not a depression clinical study and is not the MAO-A experiment. Thesis clinical-trial mentions are literature background, not new patient outcomes. Exact chapter's original body, tissue specimens and source linkage remain unresolved; no inference of mood effect from enzyme inhibition.
