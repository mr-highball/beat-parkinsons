## Original candidate

Diane L. Elliot, Kerry S. Kuehl, Kim Dupree Jones and Kristen Dulacki, “Using an Eccentric Exercise–Testing Protocol to Assess the Beneficial Effects of Tart Cherry Juice in Fibromyalgia Patients.” Identified original complete body https://www.shorelinefruit.com/assets/docs/tart-cherry-juice-in-fibromyalgia-patients.pdf has running footer Integrative Medicine 9(5), October/November 2010, pp. 40–44. Independently cited issue 9(6), December 2010/January 2011, pp. 24–29: https://umb.herbalgram.org/media/2ghb2elh/419_021134-419-021134.pdf; https://www.sbu.se/contentassets/8137f518f409480794e4b5810557ce9b/bilaga-4-exkluderade-studier.pdf . The two issue/page citations cannot be confidently reconciled as different print versions from metadata alone; preserve both rather than silently assert a definitive first issue or date. Original PDF establishes 2010 as a public issue date, not an exact online date.

Methods pp. 40–41: 15 women with fibromyalgia enrolled; 14 analyzed, one withdrew due to gastrointestinal upset during cherry juice. Blinded, randomized two-period crossover: Montmorency tart-cherry concentrate, water and natural flavoring versus taste- and calorie-matched fruit punch. Participants were instructed to consume two 10.5-ounce bottles daily, morning and evening; day 10 exercise, four follow-up days, and one-week washout. Each cherry bottle provided the equivalent of 50–60 cherries; the 2026 review's one bottle per day differs from the original. Different arms were exercised in the two periods, an important design constraint. Mean age 51.3; not Parkinson disease; juice is not dried whole fruit.

Results pp. 42–43: no significant difference in pressure pain threshold or tolerance. Original overall VAS baseline-minus-day-one change reports control −3.9 ± 19.3 versus cherry −4.9 ± 16.3, p=.10; the paper also characterizes five selected participants as responders, an exploratory post-selection result. It reports no overall time-by-treatment effect for strength, day-one 26.5 ± 11.2 versus 22.4 ± 11.2 lb, p=.06. Original gives FIQ only at enrollment, and did not report a treatment comparison for fatigue. Exact score/sign and p should be quoted as printed, not repaired or reinterpreted as clear efficacy. A juice-exposed individual discontinued for GI upset.

## Same-cohort earlier report and open trails

Jones/Elliot/Kuehl/Dulacki conference abstract 2009 https://doi.org/10.1249/01.mss.0000353586.57232.6b, OpenAlex https://openalex.org/W2044042202, reports the same 14-participant crossover, author team and pain values and is plausibly the same cohort, not independent efficacy evidence. It reports four rather than five apparent responders and p=.09 rather than .10 for overall comparison; the 2010 full primary article has priority for its own data, but the discrepancies are not resolved. Full conference original access and any trial registry number remain unverified. Exact title/author/registry queries searched 2026-11-16; do not infer that no registration exists.
