# Repair audit of the peanut-oil PubMed searches

On November 15, 2026 UTC the repaired native PubMed tool retrieved 4, 2, 3, 7, 0, 12 and 4 records for PR531 through PR537, respectively. The 32 returned memberships represent 32 distinct PMIDs. A native Europe PMC query by exact source ID recovered all 32 metadata records across the six nonempty sets. Each PMID has a disposition based on the available primary material or its published metadata. The 1998 French oral challenge was found by following the pediatric allergy papers. PubMed omitted some non-English aliases from its displayed search translation, as preserved in the query receipt. The new records retain related exposure and outcome details.

- A dietary mouse comparison gave longer retention under the peanut-only diet on the low rotarod, whose behavioral interpretation included motivation or learning. The elevated test was inconclusive. The intervention changed both peanut and rapeseed amounts and did not measure handwriting. [Primary study note](../../../../research/1995-1999/Food-Experiment-Peanut-Oil-PubMed.md#dietary-peanut-oil-and-two-different-mouse-rotarod-tests)
- Pediatric oral and labial allergy investigations distinguished observed signs from pooled clinical descriptions. The 1998 cohort had 14 positives among 62 oral oil tests. The challenge oil grade and the pool of symptoms for each ingredient are uncertain, and no study measured speech intelligibility or voice intensity. Shared investigators and recruitment site leave participant overlap possible. [Challenge details](../../../../research/1995-1999/Food-Experiment-Peanut-Oil-PubMed.md#french-oral-peanut-oil-challenges-and-individual-reactions)
- An aged-rat experiment measured stimulated tongue muscle fatigue resistance against injected oil. A human volunteer study measured acute pain during tape removal with a topical oil condition. The latter reports an overall inconclusive pain comparison (p = .39) without separate oil effect. [Stimulated fatigue](../../../../research/2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Peanut-Oil-PubMed.md) · [Topical pain study](../../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Peanut-Oil-PubMed.md)
- Market groundnut oil was used in an animal arthritis experiment. Laboratory stripping and commercial refining are different processes. Pain itself was unmeasured. Another experiment used rectal oil as the comparison for an irritant and measured freezing and grimace. In the oral cannabinoid experiment the observed contrast concerns drug delivery. Its later human trial used lactose rather than the preclinical oil. [Groundnut and rectal vehicle notes](../../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Peanut-Oil-PubMed.md) · [Cannabinoid formulation](../../../../research/1995-1999/Food-Experiment-Peanut-Oil-PubMed.md#oral-cannabinoid-carrier-in-mouse-thermal-and-writhing-pain-models)

The repaired searches and source checks supplied no direct evidence for the nine previously audited Parkinson claims. In particular, the joint sleep, parasomnia and psychosis terms produced zero PubMed records, while the earlier primary sleep notes remain separately documented. Crossref preserves the complete primary abstract of the 1984 neuropathy paper. It describes stable or slightly increased stimulated muscle potentiation in the peanut-oil vehicle group and a variable decline after toxicant. The full body remains an access lead. [1984 source note](../../../../research/1980-1984/Food-Experiment-Peanut-Oil-PubMed.md) The 1997 and 1999 Toulouse tables and the 2011 taped-skin group summaries remain access gaps. A CC BY source text is available for the 2017 mouse grimace study.
