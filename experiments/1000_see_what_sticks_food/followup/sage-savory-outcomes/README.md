# Common sage and summer savory symptom searches

The November 13, 2026 follow-up screened common sage (*Salvia officinalis*) and summer savory (*Satureja hortensis*). Ten native Europe PMC disease, symptom and constituent expressions returned all 417 appearances across 330 source keys. Three separately translated disease expressions returned 42 Europe PMC appearances and 39 PubMed identifiers. The summer savory disease-only expressions returned zero in both providers. PubMed omitted several vernacular variants from its query translation. ClinicalTrials.gov returned four records for the Parkinson expression and 210 for the broader expression. The four Parkinson records concerned an observational technology study, rTMS, telehealth and DBS. The broader search included acronym collisions, a periodontitis gel, mouth rinses, menopause trials and cognitive studies in other populations. The native citation search reconciled 18 reference and citing-record requests for nine primary articles. It returned 651 citation edges. No edges were automatically routed for follow-up by combined ingredient and outcome wording. Manual screening of related titles recovered an independent prostate cancer sage trial and led to its original Methods, Results and registration. Other links concerned memory in diabetic animals, clinical populations without Parkinson, plant chemistry or isolated constituents. | Claims | Primary evidence checked and remaining gaps |
| --- | --- |
| Sage 0013, 0060, 0593 | The Menosan trial reports better perceived sleep quality and no clear dream recall change. The 2026 dried-leaf tea trial directly measured insomnia severity and had similar final scores in both active arms. Madaus described increased urge to urinate after a fresh triturate in an uncontrolled tolerability test. No identified source measures dream enactment or urinary urgency frequency after consuming ground sage in Parkinson. |
| Sage 0125, 0305, 0370, 0795, 0839, 0840, 0906, 0972 | The Newcastle pilot compared dried sage capsules and vitamin E in Parkinson, with memory and salivation findings. No reported result matches these outcomes. Footprint geometry after a peripheral nerve injury and recognition tests after lipopolysaccharide exposure provide separate animal evidence. An Alzheimer extract trial supplies composite cognition and agitation counts. Culinary sage odor chemistry studies measure properties of the stimulus. |
| Summer savory 0253, 0473, 0516, 0521, 0547, 0653, 0666, 0970 | The oral oil lead study found better rotarod retention and no clear open-field improvement. The cisplatin extract study and the dried infusion experiment had differing awake-activity results under different conditions. A Persian immobilization-stress abstract gives a forced-swim result, and an isolated injected constituent study gives a vacuous-chewing result. The cell-free assays measure amyloid beta aggregation and cholinesterase. The frozen symptoms remain untested with the named preparation and population. |
| Summer savory 0202, 0416, 0434, 0592 | Searches paired scientific and common names with speech intelligibility, hypophonia, handwriting, olfaction and hyposmia. Returned records included botanical, food chemistry, insect behavior and unrelated *Satureja* species. The available primary reports provide no measurements of these four outcomes after summer savory consumption. |

The broader sources include the completed OHSU Alzheimer sage trial without posted results and a recruiting cognitive trial in middle-aged adults. The Newcastle poster, item-level data and missing timetable remain open. The public date of the Newcastle registration itself is unverified.
