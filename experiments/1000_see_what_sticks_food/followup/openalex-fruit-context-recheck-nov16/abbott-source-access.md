Copy-ready source text: In a double-blind randomized crossover, nine highly trained young male water-polo players consumed 90 mL/day of Montmorency tart cherry concentrate diluted in water or a mixed lime, cranberry and raspberry cordial placebo for six days, with a five-week washout. The manufacturer-based anthocyanin concentrations were not analytically confirmed in study product or placebo. Participants rated total quality of recovery on waking from 6 (very poor) to 20 (very good) and rated muscle soreness; a simulated match on day 6 was designed to induce fatigue. The authors report no condition, time or interaction effects on recovery or soreness ratings (p > .05). This is a recovery rating, not a validated symptom fatigue score, and exposure and population differ from dried cherries in Parkinson disease.
