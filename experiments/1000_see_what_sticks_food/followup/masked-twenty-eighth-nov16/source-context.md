# Original reports and access checks

## Paprika index referral

The annual index lists paprika toxicity and biliary dyskinesia on separate pages. The complete seven-page Hunter article covers the paprika entry at printed page 509. Its subject is illness in a workforce splitting dried pepper fruit colonized by fungus. The author distinguishes illness in splitters from workers grinding or handling prepared pepper and regards fungal exposure as probable. The recovered article gives no oral paprika or Parkinson movement assessment. ## Source families and endpoint limits

- **0952.** The Karami reports share investigators, study size, mean age, products and collection intervals. The 2011 methods have a sex-count discrepancy. The 2012 report gives 0.85 to 4.03 mL/min with spearmint-flavored gum versus its own resting baseline, with an inconclusive six-minute flavor comparison. No independent replication is assumed. In a separate Parkinson study, chewing unspecified gum increased swallowing and shortened swallow latency. Saliva production and clearance can contribute differently to drooling. The registered mint-containing dry-mouth spray supplies no results. The separate nine-person supine protocol reported a fall of 3.0 mmHg from baseline. In the later almond-carrier-controlled nurse study, changes were −0.84 and −2.12 mmHg (p = .517). Standing symptoms and fresh leaf ingestion were outside these experiments.
