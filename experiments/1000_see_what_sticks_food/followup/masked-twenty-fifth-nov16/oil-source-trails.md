## Prioritized evidence leads

1. **Human PD diet survey, broad oil and symptom outcome (closest human neighboring signal).** Mischley et al., “Role of Diet and Nutritional Supplements in Parkinson’s Disease Progression,” analyzed baseline self-reported data from 1,053 people with self-reported idiopathic PD in a pragmatic observational cohort. Food-frequency items included coconut oil and safflower oil; primary endpoint was PRO-PD composite symptom severity, with scores adjusted for years since diagnosis, age, gender, and in a second model income. Table 2 reports coconut oil (1 tsp per unit frequency) predicted PRO-PD change −18.6 (SE 5.5), p=.001, 95% CI −29.3 to −7.8; adjusted −20.2 (SE 5.5), p<.000, CI −31.0 to −9.4. Safflower oil (1 tsp) was not associated: +0.7 (SE 6.9), p=.922, CI −12.8 to 14.2; adjusted +6.8 (SE 6.9), p=.325, CI −6.8 to 20.5. These are cross-sectional associations with an aggregate PD symptom score, not trials or outcomes for speech, drooling, dexterity, voice, constipation, or bradykinesia. Oil refinement is unspecified; the questionnaire appears to ask how often participants ate/used foods, so the 1 tsp is a modeled serving increment, not a specified administered dose. Citation: [full primary paper, Table 2 and methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5610862/).

2. **Voice endpoint in PD, whole-diet intervention only.** Koyuncu et al. randomized 74 PD participants reporting voice disorders to ketogenic vs regular diet for 3 months; 68 completed. Voice quality measured by Voice Handicap Index (VHI), not speech intelligibility. Mean VHI parameters improved in the ketogenic group (p<.001); regular diet group did not change significantly in published abstract/available table. The report does not attribute the diet to coconut oil, specify oil type/refining, or isolate any oil. This is a close endpoint bridge for 0963 but not evidence for refined coconut oil. [PubMed primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32892250/); DOI 10.1007/s13760-020-01486-0.

3. **Cold-pressed walnut oil in PD models (preclinical only).** Filaferro et al. used cold-pressed, unrefined *Juglans regia* oil from certified organic 2022 harvest, chemically characterized (roughly 69.4% PUFA). In rotenone-treated human HMC3 microglia and SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells, co-treatment improved viability and reduced ROS at selected concentration/time combinations; conditioned media from oil-treated microglia increased neuronal-cell survival. The companion 2024 *Drosophila* report found improved locomotor activity and reduced mortality in rotenone-exposed flies on a walnut-oil-enriched diet. Neither assesses human PD, bradykinesia, clinical movement slowness, or a clinical oil comparator. This is the closest ingredient/preparation/mechanistic neighbor for 0880, while still very indirect. [Full in-vitro primary paper](https://www.mdpi.com/1420-3049/29/23/5718); [Drosophila primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11397333/).

4. **Rice-bran oil model findings (formulation/model caveats).** Alotaibi et al. administered chamuangone-enriched rice-bran oil (1.97 mg/mL chamuangone) orally at 2.5, 5, and 7.5 mg/kg for 21 days in haloperidol-induced parkinsonian rats (n=6/group in table/figure notes). Stride length, grip-walk/climb timing, catalepsy, open-field locomotion, and biochemical markers were measured. At 3 h catalepsy means were 0.84, 0.62, 0.40 for the three enriched-oil groups vs 1.82 for haloperidol-only model control; at 1.5 h the low dose was 1.26 vs control 1.15 and the middle dose 1.14 (mixed timepoint pattern). This is an extract-in-oil formulation in a pharmacologic parkinsonism model, not plain refined dietary oil, and it does not assess speech/voice or human hand dexterity. [Original publisher PDF](https://www.itjfs.com/index.php/ijfs/en/article/download/2551/1123/15946); [institutional full-text record](https://www.um.edu.mt/library/oar/handle/123456789/122437). Related established catalog entry: `research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Soybean-Rice-Bran-Oils.md`.
