## Findings from primary sources

- **0950, plain kefir and fatigue.** One 24-person cancer-survivor exercise study reports 39.3% fatigue improvement with kefir. The abstract leaves the contrast behind p=.017 and the product flavor unspecified. The likely overlapping 19-person dissertation reports a Piper fatigue interaction. Added nutrition, expectations and nonrandomized allocation could explain part of the difference. A separate chemotherapy thesis found less physical symptom burden at cycle four, with tiredness one item and no separate estimate. Its Tables 4.3 and 4.4 conflict on the total symptom mean. An independent athlete crossover found that perceived fatigue improved with water and kefir. A likely matched powder registry and publication disagree on the preparation process and the report has two p values for treadmill endurance. The favorable cancer-fatigue observation remains too indirect for a lean on plain kefir in Parkinson disease. - **0734 and 0784, bay leaf and marjoram.** The higher-concentration bay decoction increased rat forced-swim immobility from 43.87 to 114.6 seconds while the abstract describes decreased immobility overall. Exploration and open-arm findings varied by concentration. The human tea study measured anxiety and stress without an apathy endpoint. Marjoram inhalation and neurofeedback studies report device or stress outcomes. A separate injected extract study found better mouse memory-task performance. The Parkinson tea trial reports total nonmotor outcomes without a separate attention estimate. - **0735, rosemary and voice volume.** A five-oil spray improved a pooled dominant throat symptom at 20 minutes (19/26 versus 14/34, p=.019), with an inconclusive three-day comparison. The control-arm label in the table conflicts with the reported cohort and percentages. No separate hoarseness result or acoustic voice level was reported. Original collection matches put Rosemary in author names and voice signs in other reports. - **0973 and 0963, safflower and coconut oils.** The registered synbiotic study records stool diaries and uses safflower oil in its comparator capsule. An oil-only pressure crossover mentions bloating without a constipation count; a separate movement trial reports one constipation event in each blinded period. The ketogenic Parkinson diet improved self-reported voice handicap without a stated coconut ingredient or intelligibility test. These comparisons retain useful preparation and measurement limits. These results give no comparable ground-spice estimate on the named Parkinson symptom.
