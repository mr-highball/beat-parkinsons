# Original clinical context for macadamia and ginger, November 15, 2026

## Macadamia hand outcomes

Two Japanese reports use macadamia nut oil as the carrier in separate topical mixed treatments. The 2022 cervical myelopathy case reports grip and a symptom rating that groups fine tasks with grip difficulty and sensory symptoms. The 2025 carpal tunnel case checks writing and pinch activities separately and describes changes qualitatively. Its quantitative right-grip change is 0.8 kg from the values given in Figure 1, while the discussion reports 0.9 kg. Neither case isolates the carrier, verifies cold pressing, or involves consuming the oil or a Parkinson diagnosis. Both are related evidence for claim 0921. ## Ginger clinical context

A 2018 Japanese proceedings abstract reports nocturnal abdominal pain resolving in one person with Parkinson disease after bedtime oral Dai-Kenchu-To. This is related evidence for claim 0725. The uncontrolled report gives neither a pain scale nor a separate result for its ginger component. A different 2005 series reported a descriptive decline in the Parkinson subgroup's mean colonic transit time, with statistical significance reported for the pooled Parkinson and multiple system atrophy sample. It administered 15 g per day divided over three times. A 2001 Sasaki publication with a constipation-specific title is a separate original-source trail with outcome text still unavailable. The 2001 and 2002 records may describe overlapping participants.
