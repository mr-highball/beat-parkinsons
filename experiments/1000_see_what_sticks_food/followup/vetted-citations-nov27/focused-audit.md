# Focused clinical results and scope

## Claim 0282, ground black pepper and insomnia

The Dey multidisciplinary ethnopharmacology source (PMID28017696, DOI 10.1016/j.jep.2016.12.040) is available at author-abstract depth and through its validated institutional identity. The exact dose, remedy and route table remains unavailable. The secondary reference describes a pepper and honey nasal remedy, which does not establish an observed change or ground pepper consumed orally.

The [official Brazilian registry](../../../../research/2025-2029/Source-Text/2026-RBR-5svckjw-Pepper-Aromatherapy-and-Sleep-Scope.txt) compares black pepper and Angelica essential oils by skin application and inhalation. It mentions sleep in the title, gives conflicting enrollment and duration values, and posts smoking results without an interpretable insomnia outcome. The previous leaf-tea comparison against chamomile describes day-14 ISI of 5.33 and 5.60 (p=.81) in a population without Parkinson disease. This imprecise active-comparator result does not establish equivalence, and leaves culinary peppercorn distinct. The [Lindheimer acute attention trial](../../../../research/2010-2014/Source-Text/2013-PMID23905652-Pepper-Rosemary-Attention-and-Fatigue.txt) contains attention and fatigue measures without sleep, and its oral fruit preparation differs from black-pepper essential oil.

## Claim 0357, cracked black pepper and attention

All seven pages of the original Lindheimer crossover were verified. Black pepper versus rice-flour placebo has differing descriptive reaction-time trajectories and statistically inconclusive condition-by-time tests. The notes preserve the actual primary-task means and the population and preparation mismatch. The archived original upgrades provenance for the existing family, without opening a separate independent trial.

## Claim 0487, refined grapeseed oil and insomnia

The original Terauchi trial family (UMIN000009353 / PMID24518152) randomized 96 menopausal women, with 91 completers, to 100 or 200 mg/day grape-seed proanthocyanidin extract tablets or placebo. The accessible original abstract gives a within-arm Athens Insomnia Scale change. Neither the abstract nor the 2018 secondary review provides a placebo-relative AIS estimate. The official original did not yield full tables. Extract and fixed culinary oil remain distinct.

As of November 27, 2026, NCT02841618 and NCT04677920 were active, not recruiting, without posted sleep results. NCT04648215 topical aromatherapy was terminated without results, and NCT04677946 remained not yet recruiting, with its oil identity unspecified. These are separate registration records. Exact oral oil sleep and Parkinson queries gave no attributable result or firsthand cultural report.

## Claim 0879, raw bell pepper and fatigue

UMIN000056794 reports 23 enrolled and has not posted everyday-fatigue VAS values after the eight-week purified paprika xanthophyll comparison. Its older governmental mirror lists the planned 30. Published short-term exercise fatigue values from the separate athlete comparison are distinct. Its Table 2 gives change −15.3 versus +2.0 mm immediately after exercise, p<.05 in 14 athletes. This does not identify an effect of raw sweet pepper on everyday Parkinson fatigue.

Three paprika/fatigue registry results were screened. NCT06612658 remains without results. The [cayenne add-on crossover](../../../../research/2015-2019/Source-Text/2018-NCT03678116-Cayenne-Blend-and-Mood-Scope.txt) registers an acute mood comparison but gives no posted result. The [single-group multi-ingredient study](../../../../research/2025-2029/Source-Text/2025-NCT06023082-Cayenne-Blend-Tiredness-Results.txt) reports favorable tiredness ratings without a control group; the mixed intervention and population prevent cayenne-specific attribution. The targeted local cultural and conversational routes had general culinary or smoke-related contexts without an observed raw sweet pepper fatigue response.
