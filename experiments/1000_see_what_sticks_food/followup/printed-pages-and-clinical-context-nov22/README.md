- **Wrist movement and rest tremor.** Van Hilten and colleagues monitored daily wrist activity in 65 people with idiopathic Parkinson disease and 68 controls. The methods describe exploratory grouping by resting tremor. The analyzed outcomes were activity and movement patterns, with no assigned fig exposure. [Actigraphy original](../../../../research/1990-1994/Source-Text/1993-PMID8350103-Original-Access-Gap.txt)
- **Chewing mechanics and pain.** Robertson and Hammerstad report mixed changes in jaw measures across Parkinson medication states, with pain and tenderness screened separately. Karlsson and colleagues used peanut chewing in both medication states and found medication-associated changes in jaw speed and displacement. The shared peanut task provides no ingredient effect estimate. Neither paper compared dried dates or measured a pain effect of eating dates. [1996 jaw study](../../../../research/1995-1999/Source-Text/1996-PMID8558149-Original-Access-Gap.txt) · [1992 chewing study](../../../../research/1990-1994/Source-Text/1992-PMID1316430-Original-Scope.txt)
- **Apathy.** The PPMI cognitive-subtype report records apathy in 23 of 50 participants in one Parkinson dementia cluster and 14 of 25 in the other, with p = 0.469. Those are observed subgroup characteristics, with no dried-date assignment. [Original methods and Table 2](../../../../research/2025-2029/Source-Text/2025-PMID40346094-Original-Scope.txt)
