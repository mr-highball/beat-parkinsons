## Primary follow-through: marjoram and insomnia

Chun et al. 2017, PMID 29151562, DOI 10.4040/jkan.2017.47.5.651, original full text https://jkan.or.kr/journal/view.php?number=2884 (Methods paragraphs 2–4, Results Table 4). Nonrandomized two-center study, women with >=6 months insomnia, excluded Parkinson and other neurological disease; 70 assigned and 62 analyzed. Four weekly 60-minute sleep education/exercise/meditation sessions plus an aroma necklace using 1:1 Origanum marjorana and Citrus sinensis essential oils, versus no intervention. The Oh 15-item sleep quality instrument (range 15–60, higher better) includes difficulty falling asleep and frequent waking; group differences in pre-minus-post score were -7.38 +/- 8.14 vs 0.97 +/- 4.25, t=-5.10, p<.001. It is impossible to isolate marjoram, and inhaled oils and educational cointerventions do not test dietary dried marjoram. The botanical name is as printed by authors; oral preparation not used.

PMID 28504569 original indexed abstract https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28504569/ identifies four arms including lavender/clary sage/marjoram essential oil blend and Pittsburgh sleep quality; abstract-level only here, no individual marjoram attribution. PMID 34031820 ethnobotanical title and abstract concern multiple herbs and indications; no assumed pairing without its original body. Patent BG107241U is an untested multiherb pillow concept.
