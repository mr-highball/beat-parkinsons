## Gastric emptying original: PMID 7939393

Konturek et al., July 1994, [PubMed original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7939393/), DOI 10.3109/00365529409092476. Ten young healthy subjects and ten duodenal-ulcer patients received a 500 mL standard meal without or with added 15% soybean oil, and separate tests involving placebo or 1,200 mg oral loxiglumide. Abstract reports baseline gastric-emptying half-times of 44 and 34 minutes in the respective groups and an approximate 50% longer emptying time after oil; antagonist pretreatment significantly reduced the oil-associated delay. It does not disclose exact added-oil-arm half-times, p-values, test order or randomization, dose timing relative to levodopa, meal composition, oil refining, or whether the 15% is volume of the original/final mixture. Do not infer 75 mL or a verified crossover design. It measured no levodopa, PD or diary OFF time; gastric emptying is an indirect bridge only. [Publisher PDF](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.3109/00365529409092476) returned HTTP 403 in the web provider, and publisher full-text endpoint was inaccessible; body and reference list remain open.

Competing context: [Tsai et al. PMID 10652918](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10652918/), 2000, 27 functional-dyspepsia patients received 10-g-fat solid meals with soybean oil or butter on separate days; gastric retention at 90 min was 0.600 ± 0.131 versus 0.648 ± 0.156, paired p>0.05. The abstract inconsistently labels one result RR99 although methods define RR90. No PD, levodopa, no-fat control or OFF diary. [Lawaetz et al. PMID 3555275](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3555275/), 1986, nine postvagotomy patients received soybean oil emulsion or water 20 minutes before a glucose meal; early emptying was reduced with emulsion, with variable symptoms by syndrome, no PD or levodopa.

Nearest identified PD timing primary [Contin et al. PMID 9696954](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9696954/), 1998, randomized crossover in eight levodopa-treated PD patients: a standard meal 30 min before controlled-release levodopa/carbidopa versus two hours afterward delayed absorption and motor response onset and curtailed response duration after the meal. The indexed abstract does not identify soybean oil and reports no 24-hour OFF diary. Meal timing and food composition are confounded with the oil hypothesis; this is not direct evidence of soy oil effect. An erratum is recorded in PubMed.
