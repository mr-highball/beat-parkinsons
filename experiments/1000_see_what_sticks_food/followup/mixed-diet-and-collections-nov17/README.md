## Canola and green tea in the mixed MS diet

The trial author's abstract reports a favorable Modified Fatigue Impact Scale comparison (p = .001). NICE gives a separate unadjusted 12-week comparison for 100 analyzed participants. The difference was −0.70 points (95% CI −5.34 to 3.94). The full primary table and analytic model remain unverified. NICE's extraction names canola oil as one cooking or dressing option and permits green or white tea. It lists spices generally and does not identify cinnamon, oil refining, tea brewing or measured ingredient intake. The earlier public registry specifies unblinded block allocation and 120 planned. These are different records for the same cohort and a whole-diet assignment outside Parkinson disease. ## Cinnamon formulations and fatigue

The 2019 trial assessed a distilled ajwain, Iranian borago and cinnamon mixture in 51 multiple-sclerosis completers. The group-by-time comparison favored the mixture, with an inconclusive separate group term and inconsistencies in the stated dropout count and uncertainty measures. The oral formulation was described as both a volume and capsules. A distinct endometriosis trial completed enrollment and prespecified both diary fatigue and the Fatigue Severity Scale for a six-herb capsule. It lists no numerical fatigue results, and its linked protocol reports a different duration from the current registration. Neither study isolates ground cinnamon. ## Clove pain and sunflower depression

The original eugenol experiment reports a longer thermal withdrawal latency on day five, with inconclusive mechanical results in a model that did not produce allodynia. The formulation differs from saline and does not match ground culinary clove. The separate sunflower follow-up confirmed that the fatty-liver study publisher exposes its abstract while restricting the arm-level depression table. Its publisher chronology places the article in May 2023. A mirror's March date precedes the publisher's April acceptance. The Australian comparator trial record still gives no measured results or actual enrollment and describes the design inconsistently across fields. The already cataloged van de Rest trial remains a distinct comparison without an oil-free arm.
