# Round 1 caffeine, black tea and celery review

The audited verdicts remain **Maybe True** for brewed black tea reducing rest tremor (0904), **Maybe False** for raw celery stalk worsening rest tremor (0281), and **Unknown** for brewed black tea worsening walking slowness (0272).

## Black tea and the measured outcomes

Postuma Table 2 gives distinct rest tremor, gait, fatigue, daytime somnolence and dyskinesia rows. The isolated-caffeine rest-tremor contrasts were −0.32 points at week three (95% interval −0.73 to +0.10) and −0.28 at week six (−0.83 to +0.27). Both favor less rest tremor numerically, with uncertainty that includes worsening. The trial used capsules and examined motor signs in the medication ON state. Its examiner gait item is distinct from walking velocity. Brewed tea contains measurable caffeine under controlled brewing conditions, with lower amounts per serving and a different matrix. This preserves the limited clinical link. ## Celery preparation and scope

The published mouse figure shows lower rest-tremor scores in the highest whole-plant extract arm versus the model vehicle at day seven, with eight mice in each group. The score is a separate video-rated model outcome. The controlled experiment supports a direction for the extract in this model. Fresh petioles and the experimental extract share measured compounds, while the compounds responsible for the result and equivalent exposure remain unresolved. The human pharmaceutical butylphthalide report has an inconsistent pooled tremor table and supplies no interpretable rest item. The celery-seed forum report gives no isolated direction. These observations support retaining the minimal signal and keeping human raw-stalk effects open.
