# Dietary originals and intervention context

The sixteen identity-matched originals account for all 72 selected claim–source pairs. PubMed Central XML supplied fifteen bodies. The published HTML supplied the full lifestyle article after its matching XML returned an abstract without a body. Review covered original methods and results, ingredient preparations, study units and source-family ties. Thirty pairs retain a related context and 42 are outside the exact question. These are source-level decisions. [Identities and review route](source-receipts.tsv) · [Original selection](selected-pairs.tsv) · [Pair decisions](dispositions.tsv)

The [soup feeding comparison](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC6956756.txt) found greater fecal mass and moisture in both rat groups given a soup mixture. The recipes included freeze-dried parsley and olive oil, and the assigned diets differed in corn oil, protein, starch and cellulose. The study supplies bowel context for claims 0050, 0072 and 0190 without isolating an ingredient or measuring clinical constipation severity. The [diet-and-lifestyle program](../../../../research/2015-2019/Source-Text/2016-Null-Kale-Lifestyle-Extract.txt) specifies many of the foods in its protocol. Its edited testimonies cover the combined diet, supplements, exercise and stress management program. Individual ingredient uptake and symptom changes were not paired.

The [NutriAct registration](../../../../research/2015-2019/Source-Text/Trials/DRKS00010049.txt) and [protocol](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/PMC7523819.txt) identify a planned GAD-7 measure, barley offered in usual care, and Parkinson disease as an eligibility exclusion. The [12-month report](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2021-PMID34656026-NutriAct-Adherence.txt) documents dietary adherence from the same trial and its author abstract gives no anxiety result. Three focused PubMed queries retrieved 15 memberships in total. The screened NutriAct members did not resolve GAD-7. The [asparagus crossover](../../../../research/2010-2014/Source-Text/2014-PMID25297618-Asparagus-Extract-and-Sleep.txt) lists physical fatigue among questionnaire items. The result section reports a significant difference for appetite and no significant difference on physical fatigue, without a numerical fatigue contrast. That adds contrary context to the prior healthy-adult extract literature. The [freeze-dried cherry formulation](../../../../research/2010-2014/Source-Text/PMC12878997.txt) and [tart juice concentrate](../../../../research/2010-2014/Source-Text/2011-PMID22038497-Tart-Cherry-Sleep.txt) were examined for sleep outcomes. Related product publications have unresolved participant overlap. None of the resolved sources changes a claim verdict.
