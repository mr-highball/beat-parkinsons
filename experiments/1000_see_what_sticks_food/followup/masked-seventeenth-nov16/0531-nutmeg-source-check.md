# 0531: nutmeg, daytime sleepiness and dose transfer

Initial scope ground nutmeg and daytime sleepiness; no ordinary-dose controlled Parkinson study recovered. The working premise is unqualified as to dose, but the experiment prescribes an ordinary culinary preparation. Judgment: retaining Unknown is defensible because the consistent human drowsiness signal occurs at intoxication doses; a 51% adverse lean would rely on including those excess exposures as representative of ordinary seasoning, a transfer not established by the reports. If the coordinator explicitly interprets the hypothesis as encompassing any quantity, a minimal adverse lean can be considered with that choice and the competing gaps stated.

- [Cushny 1908 original proceedings PDF](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/003591570800101313), pages 39–40, describes drowsiness and stupor in published poisoning cases, often after large amounts of grated or chopped nutmeg. Cushny states that Wallace collected the preceding published cases and performed animal experiments. This is a 1908 original meeting report **summarizing prior cases**, not a fresh prospective cohort; do not conflate it with the same-year short secondary medical-news note. It reports neither routine dietary doses nor Parkinson participants.
- [Roeters van Lennep et al. 2015 original clinical letter](https://www.njmonline.nl/article_ft.php?a=1536&d=1013&i=179), paragraphs 3–5, describes an otherwise healthy woman with confusion and drowsiness in the morning following accidental addition of more nutmeg than intended to her asparagus; alcohol was also consumed. The patient inferred the exposure explanation, no drug toxicology was performed, and there was no control or Parkinson assessment. This is a real food-use accident, but not a usual-serving comparison.
- [Tjondro 2010 university thesis abstract](https://repository.maranatha.edu/2268/1/0710074_Abstract_TOC.PDF), Indonesian and English abstracts, examined 30 healthy young women before and after nutmeg-fruit syrup. Addition-test score decreased 51.91 to 48.85 (p<.05), while vigilance test time 106.33 to 116.57 seconds was inconclusive (p>.05). No parallel control, verified seed inclusion, syrup concentration or sleepiness scale is given in the abstract; fruit syrup cannot be assumed to be ground seed. The body and recipe are open.
- Existing [Bleisch 2026 case series](../../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Nutmeg-Paprika.md) includes clinical somnolence after a deliberate high-dose exposure and other cases with varying symptoms; concurrent medicines and case selection limit causal inference for ordinary food intake. Existing inhaled essential-oil mouse wheel test is not self-reported sleepiness.

Queries 2026-11-15 included `"Cushny" 1908 "nutmeg" somnolence sleepiness case`, `"Cushny" "nutmeg" poisoning 1908 original`, `nutmeg accidental culinary ingestion somnolence case food preparation 2021`, `"nutmeg" inadvertent culinary dose drowsiness daytime case food`, and paired placebo/normal-dose searches for nutmeg and sleepiness. Ranked searches lack reproducible total counts; publisher primary PDF and clinical letter were read in full. No ordinary-dose conclusion should be inferred merely from missing records.
