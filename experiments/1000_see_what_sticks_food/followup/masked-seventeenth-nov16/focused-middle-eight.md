## New 2019 primary conference record — lentil-containing evening meal and next-morning sleepiness

Sher et al., World Sleep 2019, Board #091, [original poster abstract p. 934](https://worldsleepsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/WS19-Poster-abstracts-by-author.pdf#page=934). Twenty healthy adults (10 women, 10 men, ages 25–33) spent two nonconsecutive nights in a sleep laboratory. Two hours before bed, a 342-calorie meal containing lentils, feta, beet and other vegetables was compared with a 501-calorie hamburger and fries meal. Next-morning Epworth Sleepiness Scale comparison was t=0.76, p=.45; the abstract supplies no group mean, randomized-order statement, canning specification, separate lentil estimate or Parkinson cohort. The 2020 [Sleep abstract 0167](https://academic.oup.com/sleep/article-abstract/43/Supplement_1/A66/5847100) reports the same authors, sample, meals and statistics and is a repeat report, not an independent trial. This is related meal context for 0644, but the contrast cannot attribute an effect to lentils or establish equivalence. Source date is 2019; original full article or registration not verified in this limited follow-up.

## Eight claim-specific conclusions

- **0684 cooked lentils / facial expression:** [Hegelmaier 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7072275/) lists lentils in the multi-food Parkinson diet and reports motor totals, not the facial item or individual lentil consumption. [Bedarf 2025](../../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Lentil-Kidney-Bean.md) likewise bundles foods and does not provide facial score or preparation. Baroni's diet includes lentils and protein redistribution with no lentil-only contrast. No isolated directional evidence.
- **0644 canned lentils / daytime sleepiness:** Sher's 2019 meal study above is a new endpoint-specific but highly indirect context. Existing MIND trial combines legumes within a complete diet and reports nighttime sleep quality, not daytime sleepiness or canned lentil effects. Follow a complete trial manuscript, individual meal details or registered outcome if available.
- **0541 dried dill weed / fatigue:** [Peikert 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3975776/) found time to exhaustion in nine healthy adults was 77.4 ± 5.9 minutes under strained dill pickle brine, 77.4 ± 4.0 with hypertonic saline, and 75.7 ± 3.2 with water (p=.40); dill part and dose unknown, exertional performance differs from fatigue severity. A dysmenorrhea abstract attributes systemic-fatigue reduction to cumin, not a separately stated dill effect. Do not convert the latter into positive dill evidence.
- **0504 dried tarragon / hand dexterity:** [Ribnicky 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fct.2003.11.002) gave ethanolic Russian tarragon extract to rats; female low- and high-dose groups had reduced grip strength vs vehicle, but the intermediate dose did not and the authors discounted the nonmonotonic findings. Grip force is not manual dexterity; extract and species/population differ. A later muscle-atrophy patent uses a multi-extract blend; it cannot attribute outcomes to tarragon alone. Existing notes `research/2000-2004/Food-Experiment-Tarragon-Endpoint-Followup.md` and `research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Tarragon-Endpoint-Followup.md`.
- **0602 ground cardamom / rigidity:** Hegelmaier's [Parkinson diet](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7072275/) includes cardamom in a multi-food menu and reports total motor score, not a specific rigidity item or a cardamom-only comparison. Spice intake and exact form per patient are unverified. Existing related assessment in `research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Sumac-Cardamom.md`.
- **0531 ground nutmeg / daytime sleepiness:** Existing [2026 case-series note](../../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Nutmeg-Paprika.md) reports somnolence in an acute deliberate overdose with concurrent long-term psychoactive medications. Another 2010 inhaled essential-oil mouse wheel assay measures activity, not human daytime sleepiness. Overdose and inhalation do not establish direction for ordinary culinary intake in Parkinson disease.
- **0650 cold-pressed macadamia oil / speech intelligibility:** A [2015 case](../../../../research/2015-2019/Food-Experiment-Macadamia-Hazelnut-Followup.md) describes a child's acute hoarseness after chewing and spitting a kernel, with earlier moisturizer exposure to oil. No oil ingestion, pressing grade, formal listener intelligibility or Parkinson assessment. Existing oral oil trials do not establish a speech outcome.
- **0549 cold-pressed flaxseed oil / micrographia:** The [2007 patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20070004639A1/en) offers mixed oral/intravenous regimens and mentions small handwriting before treatment without a writing-size follow-up, an attributed oil dose or a valid control. Neither mixed-treatment symptom stories nor writing exercises establish a flax-oil effect.
