# Ground anise seed follow-up

The November 15, 2026 UTC review followed adverse-effect and autonomic leads for six previously unassessed ground-anise claims and checked nearby sleep, fatigue, and fall claims against the existing records. The frozen exposure is consumed ground Pimpinella anisum, called green anise, aniseed, or anisoon depending on the source. Star anise belongs to Illicium. Filtered aqueous or ethanol extracts, essential oil, isolated anethole, and combined products are separate exposures. None of the newly retrieved originals measured the frozen preparation and target symptom together in people with Parkinson’s.

The three French anise-flavored pastis records (4037446, 6476667 and 6526226) concern glycyrrhizin exposure, hypokalemia and, in some cases, myopathy or blood-pressure changes. The case labelled “aniseed aperitif” is an exposure naming trap. The ingestion trials and analyses involving Pimpinella were kept distinct from pure trans-anethole, compounds such as anethole dithiolethione, and anethole-rich oils of other plants.

## Ground-anise clinical study lineage

- The National Institute of Unani Medicine study was publicly listed March 24, 2023. A faculty record identifies Arfa Begum and the 2025 Sultana review cites the named 2023 RGUHS dissertation. Its table lists three capsules daily and marks adverse effects or ADR reporting as “Reported” without identifying a symptom. That cell cannot establish the occurrence of a particular adverse effect.
- The May 12, 2026 clinical abstract (PMID 42152642) says 4 g/day for eight weeks, weekly safety interviews, no adverse events, and reports composite outcomes. The October 9, 2026 CC BY companion (PMC13653034) gives three 667 mg capsules twice daily after mechanical milling of authenticated fruit. The second report explicitly cites the first for the 60-person randomization and uses the same intervention and measures. They form one trial dataset.
- The October report’s original NLM supplementary DOCX was retrieved and extracted with checked FPC 3.3.1. Table S1 identifies insomnia as an MKI symptom field, but reports no arm values. Table S2 labels models by trial group assignment, and Table S3 records missing-data handling. Model performance cannot determine the direction of an insomnia effect. The publisher says data are available on request.
- IRCT201103284317N4 belongs to a separate anise-extract hot flash study. The official 2011 record plans four weeks and 2009–2010 recruitment. The 2022 Zare paper cites that ID for eight weeks and 2018–2019 enrollment. The 2012 Qazvin paper describes four weeks in a 2009 cohort without confirming the exact code. The registration discrepancy should remain visible.

## Open trails

The exact symptom estimate from the NIUM trial, its first public registry record, and the 2023 dissertation remain open. The 2026 hot-flash trial DOI 10.1016/j.aimed.2026.100712 reports a combined Hot Flash Related Daily Interference score in indexed text. The full article, its sleep item result and trial identity still require verification. The 2023 Elsevier erratum DOI 10.1016/j.bbr.2023.114412 (PMID 37129969) is an accessible original metadata record with no abstract. The correction text and rotenone-anethole cohort details remain open. The 1994 newborn adverse case original, full 2019 cardiovascular routes, and prospective clinical speech, sleepiness, hallucination, dream enactment, handwriting, dexterity, and falls estimates are separate source trails.
