# Dietary-supplement citation follow-through

## Flaxseed and scale implementation

The 2017 Taghizadeh manuscript describes a randomized flaxseed-derived omega-3 and vitamin E combination. It reports a total change of −3.3 versus +4.4 and an adjusted comparison of −3.4 versus +4.7. Its printed 0–195 range and cited revised instrument differ. Values for pain, rigidity and bradykinesia are unreported. The assessment ledger references three claims to this single cohort, with no isolated or verified cold-pressed oil comparison. The 2018 Tamtaji trial is independent and reports selected subscale composites. ## Curcumin movement and pain comparisons

Mansouri's original Figure 1 reports greater open-field distance and speed after injected pretreatment relative to the injury-only group. Its vehicle and injury schedule descriptions conflict. Schmitz reports poorer skilled forepaw reaching for isolated dietary curcumin alone and for manganese-exposed groups. Beam walking and general activity comparisons were inconclusive. The Methods and figure disagree on sample notation. The early boiled whole-turmeric model assessed brain tissue and mentions an unshown general-activity observation. Sharma's original abstract reports evoked thermal pain in a diabetes model. The studied routes, outcomes and preparations differ, and these sources give no clinical direction for ground turmeric in Parkinson disease. ## Retraction and exposure wording

The original 2014 Patil paper, which Ciulla reaches through a secondary review, was retracted for figure reuse and manipulation. It supplies no retained evidence direction. Ciulla's watermelon context includes the literal plural, which the native singular flag missed. The linked animal experiments used isolated lycopene. The review's quoted 39% belongs to tissue acetylcholinesterase activity, with functional movement in a different model.
