# Fig menu, tea outcomes and original rest-tremor sources

A 16-person Parkinson study lists figs among the ingredients in a two-week mixed diet. Ten participants also received bowel cleansing. The article describes rest-tremor scoring within the motor examination. The original supplementary plot gives total motor scores immediately after the intervention, and the main figure gives one-year totals. Neither reports a separate rest-tremor outcome or a group assigned to figs. Two reviewed secondary papers lead to this same study, which supplies one new assessment for claim 0559. The grade remains Unknown. The original Singapore cohort associates black-tea intake with Parkinson incidence. Its diagnostic rest-tremor references do not constitute a symptom outcome. A small Indonesian isolated-caffeine trial reports positive total motor responder counts, with baseline imbalance and inconsistent adverse-event denominators. It measures rest tremor within the examination and gives no separate result. An original 1987 abstract reports no increase after an acute isolated caffeine dose. Its recorded assessment times differ between the PubMed and publisher displays, and the original body remains unverified. A Brazilian black-tea questionnaire study has preliminary prevalence and pesticide results without a verified black-tea rest-tremor analysis. The current minimal indirect lean for black tea remains Maybe True at 51. Original nutmeg sources include a French case with unsteady gait on examination and a separate Italian poison-center abstract without a documented tremor exam. The original French powder case with alcohol and medications and the Beckerman case remain open at full-text depth. These records address excess exposure and preserve the uncertainty for ordinary ground-nutmeg intake in Parkinson disease. Two olive-oil-containing diet supplements also lack a separate rest-tremor estimate.
