# Focused clinical and original-source follow-through

## 0983, toasted Sichuan pepper and pain

The [complete Yuan original](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2021-Yuan-Zanthoxylum-Patch-Rat-Migraine-Table.txt) documents a lower head-scratching count at 30–60 minutes with a topical extract in rats. It uses a different species, route and outcome proxy. The [Shao review](../../../../research/2020-2024/Source-Text/2024-Shao-Zanthoxylum-Volatile-Oil-Processing-Review.txt) interprets the separate 2019 Bian experiment without reporting an independently measured result.

Bian's [author abstract](https://ccj.pku.edu.cn/article/info?aid=230485909) orders the high and low values as raw volatile fractions, 19.50 ± 3.31 and 27.20 ± 3.91, and dry-fried fractions, 18.40 ± 3.10 and 21.10 ± 2.85. Blank control is 49.60 ± 7.62 and the positive comparison is 19.20 ± 3.79. These are evoked mouse writhing counts over 20 minutes. Comparisons against blank control are distinct from a direct processing contrast. The full methods, voucher and direct processing test remain unavailable. The existing minimal scorecard anchor rests on Bian's indirect evidence.

## 0480, cold-pressed pumpkin seed oil and pain

The [2011 pilot trial](../../../../research/2010-2014/Source-Text/2011-PMID21545273-Gossell-Williams-PSO-Postmenopausal-Pilot-RCT.txt) collected joint-pain scores from 18 pumpkin-oil and 13 wheat-germ-oil participants after allocating 21 and 14. The week-12 joint-pain comparison gives p=.663. The within-arm values of p=.030 and .801 do not constitute a between-arm change test. Pressing method is unreported, and the study does not address Parkinson pain.

The [2025 pelvic-pain adjunct study](../../../../research/2025-2029/Source-Text/2025-Khalil-Pumpkin-Oil-and-Tadalafil-Pelvic-Pain.txt) reports 12/25 responders on tadalafil and 11/25 on the combination and describes a qualitative pain-domain advantage for the combination. The accessible abstract omits quantitative domain values and oil processing. Publisher full-body attempts timed out, and targeted title and author discovery gave metadata or abstract copies. NCT06944392 remains completed without posted results and has a single oil group.

## 0722, dried cranberry and depressive symptoms

The [Gamus archive entry](../../../../research/2015-2019/Source-Text/2015-PMID25796668-Gamus-Cranberry-and-Depression-Contexts.txt) identifies Hebrew pages 9–15 in Harefuah and the English abstract on p. 70. The journal's official full-PDF link leads to an access page. The two abstracts separately assign cranberry to urinary infection prevention, acupuncture to depression and tai chi to Parkinson symptoms. Exact Hebrew and English title and institutional discovery did not establish the full body. The priority-three source remains open.
