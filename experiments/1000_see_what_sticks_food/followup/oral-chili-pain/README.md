# Oral red pepper and pain

The 2008 crossover compared ground red chili in a meal, chili in capsules and a standard meal. Both chili conditions increased acute abdominal pain and burning in the IBS-D sample. The 2014 six-week crossover found lower meal-provoked burning alongside an inconclusive peak pain comparison. The 2011 Italian trial's abstract emphasizes benefit, while the full diary comparison between groups was inconclusive and six active participants stopped because of pain on starting treatment. [Acute crossover](../../../../research/2005-2009/Food-Experiment-Oral-Chili-Pain.md) · [Chronic comparisons and lineage](../../../../research/2010-2014/Food-Experiment-Oral-Chili-Pain.md)

A separate early dyspepsia trial gave lower late pain scores with red pepper in capsules, with two active withdrawals for pain. The uncontrolled IBS pilot reported a within-participant decrease. The experiments distinguish acute symptom provocation, repeated exposure, population and delivery. Ground cayenne-specific Parkinson pain effects remain open.
