## Ground ginger and dyskinesia · 0271

An original Kyung Hee University patent reports lower abnormal involuntary movement area under the curve with dried ginger extract in levodopa-treated mice. Table 12 gives 144.00 ± 15.56 with extract and 276.75 ± 26.00 with levodopa alone, as mean ± SE. Isolated shogaol arms and an amantadine arm are distinct comparisons. The publication does not establish animal n, treatment route or masking in this example. Its 2025 Korean counterpart identifies a dried-rhizome ethanol extract and repeats the same experiment. Case lineage and experiment scope were checked before adding one study-family assessment. The reported effect favors less movement in the model. A human ground-ginger effect remains open. ## Grated ginger and constipation · 0581

A separate patent example gives fecal water of 69.54 ± 1.51% with isolated shogaol plus levodopa and 57.31 ± 2.46% with levodopa alone in a 6-OHDA model. The Korean sister document repeats the table. The related 2026 Kim paper uses a different model and measures bead expulsion. Overlap of animals or controls remains unverified. The clinical lean against worsening continues to rely on symptom comparisons from processed preparations and other populations. [Original results and scale source](../../../../research/2020-2024/Food-Experiment-Ginger-Outcome-Measures.md)

## Grated ginger and anxiety · 0937

The craniotomy report was already catalogued under TCTR20220124002. Its recovered supplemental Table 1 gives 2.00 versus 1.21 VAS on day one (p = .420) and 1.62 versus 0.71 on day two (p = .266), with 24 participants per arm. The per-visit comparisons remain inconclusive. The drink contains standardized extract, and patients were undergoing surgery. The supplement adds detail for the same cohort. [Original supplement and dated note](../../../../research/2025-2029/Food-Experiment-Ginger-Anxiety.md#ginger-extract-and-anxiety-in-a-blinded-craniotomy-comparison)

## Brewed green tea and fatigue · 0948

The original Japanese cultured-tea experiment compared fermented extract with corresponding unfermented tea extract in a healthy 16-person crossover. The figure shows a smaller rise in fatigue with fermented extract and provides no precise fatigue-specific comparison or significance mark. The printed p < .1 thresholds concern total mood disturbance or other mood items. Salivary markers were inconclusive. The acute healthy-adult results supply context for a different preparation. In the closest Parkinson brewed-tea study, mean fatigue rose 3.7 points in 14 completers without a control and the thesis leaves paired variance unresolved. That result still carries the minimal worsening lean. ## Ongoing trial measurements and original access

The official Cambridge Ginger-PD registry still compares two extract strengths and gives no results. It names MDS-UPDRS Parts I–IV and GIDS-PD as secondary or exploratory measures in addition to its ghrelin endpoints. Published instruments include MDS-UPDRS item 1.4 for anxious mood, items 4.1–4.2 for dyskinesia, and four GIDS-PD items on constipation. The original GIDS-PD has six-month recall. The registry gives a 12-week treatment schedule and leaves recall adaptation, separate item analyses and results open. The Iranian powder-tablet trial continues to report total MDS-UPDRS and no symptom-item results.
