## 0244 · Roasted shiitake and swallowing difficulty

The exact source queue for this claim is empty. Current PubMed ESearch returned five records for shiitake and swallowing, dysphagia, aspiration or choking. The matching IDs were 42603941, 42409513, 40947197, 34574299 and 33093939. Using Lentinula edodes instead returned PMID 41662656. Adding Parkinson disease and swallowing or dysphagia to shiitake returned zero. Restricting the dysphagia or choking search to 2025–2026 gave three already screened IDs. The ClinicalTrials.gov query for shiitake and swallowing or dysphagia returned zero. The materials and skin-care hits do not supply swallowing outcomes.

Original report PMID 33093939 supplies two downstream bowel-obstruction observations after pieces of shiitake. A separate 2017 series gives similar mechanical context. The 2010 O-220 meeting original lists one shiitake obstruction and five Parkinson cases without linking the food and diagnosis. The chicken–shiitake smoothie FEES crossover uses preparations with different ingredients and viscosities. Its favorable spillage component cannot isolate shiitake. The Japanese 2024 meal-related case report identifies Parkinson disease without naming shiitake. Contemporary Japanese and English searches and the archived elder-care account were checked for preparation and diagnosis. The elder-care account describes reported eating difficulty only.

The later publisher-listed Kameda chapter remains unavailable. Its ingredient, outcome and possible reuse of the earlier cohort are unverified. The exact and focused discovery checks meet the recorded review gates. The scorecard remains open at 50, and the published display status becomes Unknown (Researched).

## 0171 · Toasted fennel seed and fatigue

The existing Maybe False 50.005 score remains. Ghodsi's Table 1 compares weakness in dysmenorrhea using fennel soft capsules and a no-medication control. Motavalli reports 8/36 with fatigue in each arm and a comparator discrepancy across records. Delaram reports an aggregated symptom domain. The 2026 crossover publication mentions fatigue in background and reports pain outcomes.

PubMed's fennel, fatigue or weakness, and Parkinson query returned zero. A broader fatigue and trial query returned PMID 32421208, whose tiredness evidence is from pharmacological lactation treatments. ClinicalTrials.gov condition and intervention queries for fennel and Parkinson or fatigue, including Foeniculum vulgare, returned zero. The 2007 Torkzahrani abstract reports fatigue p=.058 from 46 extract and 44 placebo participants. The original arm values and sign remain unavailable after English and Persian title, author, journal issue and reference follow-through. Keep this material check open.

The October 1992 herbal manual lists wild fennel among intended remedies for general fatigue. It does not give a fatigue-specific plant part or preparation. The adjacent dried-seed infusion is described for digestive discomfort. Earliest public circulation of the dated edition is uncertain.

## 0325 · Roasted carrot and executive function

The CIRCUS protocol supplies the planned cognitive tasks and baseline characteristics. The primary paper and NCT03410342 describe one family. On November 27, 2026, the registry showed completed with no posted results. The 39 randomized in the article and 36 actual enrollment in the registry are retained separately. Isolated roasted-carrot executive outcomes remain unresolved. The published protocol includes carrots in two mixed-produce conditions, with varied preparations. The Cambridge accepted manuscript and official trial documents were checked for the planned measures. A November 2025 AHA abstract from the same registration reports a food-intake metabolite score. The official issue listing confirms the abstract identity and date. The indexed author abstract gives no cognitive result. Exact registration, investigator and institutional repository searches located no public arm-specific cognitive estimate.

The 2023 Taounate fieldwork adds documented uses of wild carrot for digestive and genitourinary conditions. The evidence preserves the parts discrepancy between the text and the figure and distinguishes the cognition citation in the literature table from the reported local practice. The claim remains Unknown and partial.
